Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Revenue and Customs (HMRC), online via GOV.UK and the HMRC portal; customs matters via the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) and National Clearance Hub.

E-government filing portal

A BVI Business Company (non-resident) must register for UK Corporation Tax within three months of commencing UK trade through a permanent establishment; notify HMRC online using the 'Register a new company for Corporation Tax' service (form CT603) to obtain a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) and Corporation Tax reference. If the BC makes taxable UK supplies, it must also register for VAT using form VAT1A (Non-Established Taxable Persons), supplying the Certificate of Incorporation, registered office address, directors' details, UK PE address, bank details, and anticipated turnover. Once approved, HMRC issues a VAT registration number; the entity should also obtain an EORI number for customs declarations if exporting.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 annual taxable turnover for UK-established businesses; nil threshold for non-established taxable persons making any UK taxable supply

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The supply of goods (including manufactured packaged rice/noodle products) is zero-rated for UK VAT where the goods are directly exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK, or supplied to a VAT-registered customer in Northern Ireland/EU under applicable distance-sale rules, and the exporter retains valid commercial, transport, and customs evidence proving removal from the UK within three months (or six months under approved arrangements). The exporter is entitled to deduct attributable input VAT and may claim repayment on each VAT return; HMRC normally pays claims within 30-45 days.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CDS) — electronic declaration accepted by UK Border/Customs as the departure lodgement.
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill — transport document evidencing the goods left the UK.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / payment confirmation (e.g., SWIFT MT103) — retained to evidence the export sale proceeds; HMRC has no statutory FX remittance deadline but payment proof is expected in audit.
  • Commercial invoice and packing list — cross-referencing the VAT invoice, buyer, commodity code and export terms.

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate. Making Tax Digital for VAT requires VAT-registered businesses to keep digital records and file VAT returns via HMRC-recognised APIs; e-invoicing is permitted but not mandatory; exports must be declared electronically via CDS.

HMRC may deny zero-rating and issue a VAT assessment under VATA 1994 s.73 where export evidence is missing/false, clawing back output VAT at 20% plus interest. Civil penalties under FA 2007 Sch. 24 apply: up to 30% for careless inaccuracy and up to 70%/100% for deliberate/concealed inaccuracy; dishonest evasion can attract penalties up to 100% of VAT under VATA 1994 s.60-67 and criminal prosecution under s.72(1). Retrospective clawbacks can cover a four-year or six-year assessment window.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full capital and current account convertibility; the remaining provisions of the Exchange Control Act 1947 were repealed in 1979, and no central bank approval is required for equity injections, profit remittance, loan repayments, or trade settlements. In practice, profit repatriation from a BVI-owned UK account is unrestricted and subject only to standard anti-money laundering (Proceeds of Crime Act 2002) and sanctions (Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018) reporting, plus HMRC transfer pricing and controlled-company reporting where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. UK law imposes no statutory timeline for repatriating or surrendering export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio into GBP. A BVI Business Company operating through a UK treasury account may retain foreign currency balances freely, provided that HMRC customs declarations, Common Reporting Standard information, and FCA-compliant transaction monitoring are satisfied.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority) in coordination with HM Treasury and the Financial Conduct Authority for banking conduct and foreign exchange supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Medium-to-High for FCA-authorised electronic money institutions and non-bank cross-border payment providers (e.g., Ebury, OFX, Currencycloud-based rails) that support BVI corporate legal persons in trade-based food manufacturing. Pure retail digital challenger banks (Revolut, Monese, Monzo Business) typically reject BVI-registered entities as a standing Client Onboarding policy. Legal constraints: an EMI may not grant credit or accept deposits, must safeguard client funds under the E-Money Regulations 2011, and remains fully subject to the FCA AML/CTF rules; a BVI company should expect heightened UBO (25%+ ownership) disclosure and certified corporate documentation before activation of any cross-border payment flow.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is via SWIFT MT103 corporate-to-bank payment from the BVI parent to the UK operational or capital escrow account, with the remittance narrative expressly stating 'Shareholder equity contribution / EQUI' and referencing the signed board resolution. The UK receiving bank is entitled to require: (a) a qualified electronic signature certificate on the board resolution, (b) certification of the beneficial ownership declaration, and (c) source-of-funds evidence for the first material tranche. No Bank of England or HM Treasury consent is required because the UK permits unrestricted capital inflow; cleared funds then settle over the Faster Payments Service (sub GBP 1 million) or CHAPS (same-day, high value) for domestic use. All payments above the firm's monitoring threshold (typically GBP 10,000) are subject to automated transaction monitoring and post-event CDD refresh.Outbound settlements for raw-material purchases and packaging suppliers use SWIFT MT103 for standard corporate payments, with clear invoice and contract references. Domestic supplier payments clear via CHAPS for high-value same-day settlement or Faster Payments for near-instant retail-level disbursement; euro disbursements clear through TARGET2, and Asian-corridor supplier payments clear via the FX corridor's correspondent network. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: UK applies OECD transfer-pricing principles with no bright-line statutory Local File threshold, but HMRC expects OECD-compliant Local File documentation where UK-controlled transactions exceed £1,000,000 in a period and the company is a large enterprise (not an SME: fewer than 250 employees and either turnover ≤£50m or balance sheet ≤£43m). Master File and Country-by-Country Report requirements arise when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds £750,000,000. A BVI BC with a UK PE must therefore prepare Local Files for cross-border transactions between the UK PE, BVI head-office and affiliates.

Economic substance scope: The UK has no standalone Economic Substance Regulation; instead HMRC applies the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR), Diverted Profits Tax (25%), and PE attribution rules to combat artificial avoidance of a UK taxable presence. As a BVI Business Company, the entity remains subject to the BVI International Tax Authority's Economic Substance Regulations: annual ESR notification is mandatory, and if the entity is tax resident in the UK it must evidence that status to be exempt from BVI substantive tests; if anyway it carries on a BVI relevant activity, it must satisfy headquarters/IP/financing substance requirements in BVI. The UK manufacturing PE must itself maintain management, staff, premises and operational decision-making in the UK.
