Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) is the sole competent national tax authority, administered through the GOV.UK unified portal (gov.uk), HMRC online services and the Government Gateway identity system. Corporation Tax operates via the Corporation Tax Online Service; VAT operates via VAT Online with mandatory Making Tax Digital (MTD) API filings. Customs and export control are executed by HMRC and Border Force through the Customs Declaration Service (CDS).

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company obtains UK identifiers only upon actual UK tax nexus. Step 1 - Companies House: if the overseas company establishes a UK branch or office, register the overseas company via Form OS IN01 within one month of opening the establishment (Companies Act 2006 s.1046). Step 2 - Corporation Tax: a non-resident company trading through a UK permanent establishment (PE) must notify HMRC under CTA 2010 s.42 within the statutory notification window (practically no later than 3 months after commencing PE trading), submit the non-resident company registration form in the CT41G series through HMRC online services, and obtain the 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) for CT600 compliance. Step 3 - VAT: a non-established taxable person (NETP) must register via the HMRC VAT1/NETP route once taxable turnover reaches the GBP 90,000 threshold; HMRC may require security or a UK tax representative under SI 1995/2518. Step 4 - PAYE: registration is compulsory where UK employees or UK-based directors are engaged (ITEPA 2003 s.684). Step 5 - if no UK PE, no UK-source income and no UK taxable supplies arise, no UTR/VAT registration is mandatory; the only residual exposure is the 20% UK withholding tax on UK-originated yearly interest and royalties (ITA 2007 s.874), and no UK-Cayman double taxation treaty is in force to reduce it.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope (non-resident case). For UK tax, the Cayman Exempted Company is a legal corporation and is never fiscally transparent; its residence status is determined by central management and control (CMC): if CMC is exercised in the UK, the company is UK-resident and taxed on worldwide profits at 19% (small profits rate up to GBP 50,000) to 25% (main rate above GBP 250,000) with marginal relief between the thresholds; if CMC remains in Cayman, only UK-PE-attributable profits and UK-source income are taxable. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope (non-resident case). For UK tax, the Cayman Exempted Company is a legal corporation and is never fiscally transparent; its residence status is determined by central management and control (CMC): if CMC is exercised in the UK, the company is UK-resident and taxed on worldwide profits at 19% (small profits rate up to GBP 50,000) to 25% (main rate above GBP 250,000) with marginal relief between the thresholds; if CMC remains in Cayman, only UK-PE-attributable profits and UK-source income are taxable. The Cayman income-tax-exemption certificate (Tax_Exempt_Zone) has no UK effect and cannot be used as a UK election.

Available election forms

The UK has no check-the-box entity classification election comparable to IRC s.7701; classification is statutory. The relevant mechanisms are: (i) Foreign Permanent Establishment (FPE) exemption election under CTA 2010 s.18A, available only to UK-resident companies and not to a non-resident Cayman entity; (ii) mandatory non-elective application of the UK anti-hybrid mismatch rules (TIOPA 2010 Part 6A) from 1 January 2017, which deny deductions or impose counteracting adjustments for hybrid mismatches involving the Cayman entity; (iii) Controlled Foreign Company elections (TIOPA 2010 ss.371BC-371BD) for exempt-period or full-tax treatment where UK-resident participators hold the Cayman company; (iv) VAT elections including voluntary registration below the threshold, VAT group election for connected UK entities, cash accounting scheme, annual accounting scheme, flat-rate scheme for qualifying businesses, and option to tax land; (v) automatic statutory close-company and personal-service attribution rules apply with no election available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 90,000 of taxable turnover in any rolling 12-month period, effective from 1 April 2024; deregistration threshold is GBP 88,000. Discretionary portfolio advisory services supplied by a non-UK-established supplier to overseas recipients fall outside the UK VAT net and do not count toward the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated under VATA 1994 s.30(6) and SI 1995/2518 reg.129 provided the goods are supplied to an overseas person, are exported from the UK (including the Isle of Man) within 3 months of the time of supply, and the supplier retains conclusive export evidence (customs declaration, transport documents and commercial invoices) in its VAT record. For the present multi-family-office mandate, the core supplies (discretionary portfolio advisory and management services to non-UK client families) are outside the scope of UK VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rule where the recipient belongs outside the UK, rather than zero-rated; no UK output tax is exigible, and UK input VAT recovery is available only through the refund route under VATA 1994 s.39 and the VAT Regulations Part XXII, subject to the partial-exemption rules for exempt financial services. There is no inward remittance certificate requirement in UK law; contract evidence and bank/FX remittance records fulfil the commercial substantiation function.

  • UK Customs Export Declaration (SAD/CDS) and, where applicable, completed Goods Vehicle Movement Service (GVMS) departure declarations
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill constituting transport evidence of physical export from the UK
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and purchase or contract documentation supporting the exported supply
  • Evidence of the overseas client's non-UK business establishment (contract, VAT or entity registration certificate, banking and FX remittance records) for offshore services

Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT is mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses: digital record-keeping, digital links, and quarterly VAT Returns must be submitted through HMRC-recognised MTD software using HMRC application programming interfaces (APIs). The UK does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or pre-approval stamping regime for invoices; e-invoicing (PEPPOL, EDI, PDF) is permitted voluntarily, and retention of source documents for six years remains compulsory. Late submissions are penalised through a points-based regime, and late payments attract statutory interest under the Finance Act 2009 regime.

HMRC may retrospectively re-rate exports as standard-rated where statutory export evidence is missing or defective, assessing the unpaid VAT with interest under VATA 1994 s.74. Civil penalties under Finance Act 2007 Sch.24 reach up to 100% of potential lost revenue (PLR); the assessment window is 4 years for ordinary errors, 6 years for careless inaccuracies, and 20 years for deliberate and concealed conduct (VATA 1994 s.73(6A)). Criminal prosecution under VATA 1994 s.72 is available for fraudulent evasion, carrying an unlimited fine and/or imprisonment for up to 7 years. In export clawback exercises HMRC aggregates cumulative mis-ratings, applies joint and several liability to directors and controlling participators, and imposes disclosure-based penalties where consideration is never received or export chains are contrived.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for GBP, USD, and KYD. UK abolished exchange controls in 1979, leaving current and capital accounts fully convertible. Cayman's Exchange Control Law grants exempted companies deemed exchange control permission to conduct business in any currency; profit remittance passes freely and without non-tax restrictions or approval hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Neither UK nor Cayman imposes statutory surrender timelines or mandatory conversion ratios. Export proceeds and operating income may be retained in any currency indefinitely; only counterparty sanctions screening and tax disclosure obligations apply.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (United Kingdom); Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (Cayman Islands); no dedicated FX control administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Moderate feasibility. FCA-authorised UK EMI accounts (e.g. Revolut Business, MultiPass, or similar) provide rapid virtual onboarding, API-based FX, and SWIFT/SEPA clearing, but are not substitutes for full-service relationship banking for discretionary MFO activity. Offshore EEA EMIs no longer benefit from passporting into the UK post-Brexit; they may only serve UK clients through a FCA-authorised UK entity or under the Temporary Permissions Regime (now largely expired). Legal constraints: EMIs cannot grant credit or accept third-party deposits; funds must be safeguarded in a ring-fenced pool; custody of client securities is not permitted.
