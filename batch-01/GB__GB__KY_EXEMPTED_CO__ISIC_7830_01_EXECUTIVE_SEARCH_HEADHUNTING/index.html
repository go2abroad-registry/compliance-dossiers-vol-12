E-government filing portal

As a Cayman Exempted Company without UK central management and control (non-UK resident), register with HMRC for Corporation Tax if a UK permanent establishment (PE) exists (e.g., UK headhunting office, employees, or dependent agent authority). File via the 'Register a non-UK company for Corporation Tax' service within 3 months of starting UK trading; HMRC issues a 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR). Register for VAT online at gov.uk/register-for-vat (for non-UK established persons: mandatory from the first UK taxable supply, with no turnover threshold). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The UK has no check-the-box regime. A Cayman Exempted Company is opaque and resident in the Cayman Islands if central management and control is not in the UK. UK tax on a UK PE arises by statute under CTA 2010 s5. Relevant elections: (i) treaty-based profit attribution election applying the OECD 'independent enterprise' / separate enterprise principle to the UK PE (TIOPA 2010); (ii) VAT cash accounting, annual accounting and Flat Rate Scheme elections, subject to conditions and exclusions for non-UK established persons; (iii) anti-hybrid adjustment elections under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A, which HMRC may accept where a Cayman entity finances UK operations because Cayman imposes no entity-level income tax. No election alters the legal tax-exempt status of the Cayman entity itself.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 (from 1 April 2024) for UK-established businesses; £0 (mandatory immediate registration) for non-UK established persons making any taxable supply in the UK. Voluntary registration is available from the first supply. B2B headhunting services to overseas customers are outside the scope of UK VAT and do not count toward the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under VATA 1994 Sch 8 and VAT Notice 703 when exported to a non-UK destination within 3 months of supply, with prescribed evidence retained. Services (core executive search): B2B supplies to a recipient belonging outside the UK are outside the scope of UK VAT under the place-of-supply rules (SI 1992/3121), legally equivalent to zero-rating for input tax deduction purposes. B2C supplies to non-UK customers remain standard-rated unless a specific exception applies; executive search services do not fall within Sch 8 Groups 15/16. Services directly related to exported goods or land outside the UK may qualify for separate zero-rating, but this is not the principal commercial model.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD) acknowledgment (only for physical goods; for executive search services: signed retainer agreement and completion reports evidencing the overseas customer and service delivery)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (only for physical goods; for services: meeting minutes, placement invoices, and time-and-effort records demonstrating actual performance)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT bank statement evidencing payment from the overseas customer (required for both goods and services to support zero-rating or outside-scope treatment)

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping in the UK. Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT mandates digital bookkeeping and submission through HMRC-recognised software using MTD APIs. The UK has adopted the PEPPOL network for public sector (B2G) e-invoicing: mandatory for central government from 2024, with phased expansion to all public buyers by 2027; in-scope suppliers must transmit invoices in a standardised machine-readable format via a certified PEPPOL Access Point. Private-sector B2B e-invoicing remains contractual.

HMRC can deny zero-rating or outside-scope status and recover unpaid VAT if export evidence is defective (VATA 1994 s30; Notice 703). Assessment windows: 2 years for return errors, 4 years for careless errors, and 20 years for deliberate or fraudulent conduct. Civil penalties under FA 2007 Sch 24 apply (up to 100% of VAT for deliberate inaccuracy with concealment), plus default surcharge and interest. Fraudulent evasion of VAT and dishonest zero-rating claims are criminal offences under VATA 1994 s72, punishable by fines and imprisonment (up to 7 years). Goods not physically exported within the required period trigger retrospective VAT clawback with interest from the time of supply. HMRC's specialist Exports and Evidence team can issue protective assessments and, in repeated failure cases, cancel VAT registration and deny input tax recovery.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The British pound sterling (GBP) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. The Exchange Control Act 1947 has been repealed, and no prior approval or mandatory FX conversion is required for profit remittance, capital repatriation, debt service, or dividend distributions. A Cayman Exempted Company with central management and control outside the UK is treated as non-resident for UK corporate tax, provided it does not carry on a trade through a UK permanent establishment. Retained search fees and placement commissions remitted to the Cayman entity are not subject to UK withholding tax, except where the payment is for UK domestic consulting services performed by a UK branch or agency. For FX conversion, UK banks and FCA-authorised payment institutions are fully licensed to execute spot and forward transactions for corporate clients; no central bank approval is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement in the United Kingdom. No mandatory timeline or conversion ratio applies to export or service receipts. The Cayman Exempted Company may retain any foreign currency receipts in offshore or onshore bank accounts, convert to GBP at its discretion, and deploy funds for working capital, disbursements, or profit extraction. All export-related service fees from executive search contracts may be settled through standard MT103 wire transfers, electronic money institutions, or trade finance instruments, subject only to AML/KYC verification and sanctions screening.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority) and HM Treasury. The United Kingdom does not have a dedicated foreign exchange administration; exchange control is not exercised by the Bank of England or HM Treasury.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: High for the low-risk executive search and headhunting commercial model. FCA-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut Ltd, Modulr, and Paysafe Financial Services Ltd offer remote onboarding, multi-currency IBANs, and B2B payment rails. Legal constraints: (i) Under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011, EMIs are not banks and client funds are safeguarded but not FSCS-protected; (ii) Most EMIs accept Cayman Exempted Companies only where the entity demonstrates clear tax residence and beneficial ownership transparency; (iii) EMIs may refuse to process retainer payments from clients in higher-risk jurisdictions without source-of-funds documentation; (iv) EMIs generally prohibit the use of accounts as a payment intermediary for third parties, so outbound disbursements must be for the entity's own invoices and trade; (v) Certain EMIs apply a 60–180-day KYC review period for offshore jurisdictions before releasing large FX conversions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Cayman Exempted Company can receive shareholder capital injections and intercompany loans through the SWIFT MT103 channel with explicit payment reference 'CAPITAL INJECTION' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN'. The UK receiving account must have an IBAN and a valid BIC. Banks require a board resolution, signed share subscription letters, and a certified copy of the shareholder loan agreement to distinguish equity from debt. Since the UK imposes no exchange control, no regulatory purpose code is mandatory, but a bank may ask for a BOP purpose classification such as 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' (A1) or 'Loans, Deposits and Other Investment – Intercompany Lending' (A3) for macroeconomic reporting. Verification will include sanctions screening, anti-money laundering review of the source-entity jurisdiction, and a legal opinion on the Cayman entity's powers.For international retainer fees, candidate success fees, invoice settlements, and vendor payments, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 (bank-to-bank customer transfer) using the beneficiary's BIC and IBAN. Within the UK, Faster Payments Service (FPS) provides near-instant GBP clearing for domestic vendor payments. For cross-border trade settlements in excess of bank reciprocal limits, Commercial Letters of Credit governed by ICC UCP 600 may be issued, but for professional services the documentary credit route is atypical and costly; a straightforward MT103 wire, SWIFT gpi, or an EMI-to-EMI bridge is commercially preferred. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% in Cayman Islands (no corporate income tax). If a UK permanent establishment is established, UK corporation tax applies at 25% (profits > £250,000) or 19% (profits ≤ £50,000), with marginal relief for profits between £50,000 and £250,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% – Cayman levies no dividend withholding tax, and the UK does not impose withholding tax on distributions made by a non-UK resident company. If the Cayman company becomes UK tax resident, UK dividend law also imposes no withholding on dividend distributions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No specific annual pound-sterling threshold triggers a mandatory Local File or Master File in the UK. HMRC requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for all cross-border transactions with associated enterprises. A Country-by-Country Report filing is mandatory only for multinational groups with consolidated revenue ≥ €750,000,000. For other taxpayers, upon a HMRC information notice under Schedule 36 FA 2008, documentation must be provided within 30 days; failure attracts penalties.

Economic substance scope: Cayman's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) impose a substance test on companies performing 'relevant activities' (banking, insurance, financing, leasing, headquarters, shipping, distribution, service centre, or IP holding). Executive search and headhunting is not a relevant activity; therefore, no ESR substance requirement arises, but the company must still file an annual Economic Substance Return and a Declaration of No Relevant Activity. If the company is managed and controlled from the UK, HMRC will regard it as UK tax resident and require substance and filings in the UK; the Cayman exemption is disregarded under anti-avoidance principles.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Failure to notify UK chargeability / Operating an unregistered UK PE

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty under FA 2008 Schedule 41 up to 100% of the tax liability; daily penalties for continuing failure; interest on unpaid tax. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion can follow where there is deliberate concealment, with unlimited fines and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed under joint and several liability provisions where the company fails to register, with HMRC seeking recovery of unpaid tax from any individual exercising control. Director disqualification under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986 applies for up to 15 years; travel bans and confiscation orders can be imposed under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002.
Transfer pricing mispricing between Cayman head office and UK PE

Statutory sanction: HMRC may adjust transfer prices under TIOPA 2010 Part 4, increasing taxable profits, and charge interest and penalties. A penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax arises under FA 2007 Schedule 24 where the document is inaccurate due to carelessness or deliberate error. Diverted Profits Tax (25%) may also be applied to contrived arrangements.

Director personal liability: Senior Accounting Officers (SAO) and finance directors can be fined up to £5,000 for failing to take reasonable steps to establish and maintain appropriate tax accounting systems. Directors can be made personally liable for penalties attributable to their own deliberate actions, and disqualification or imprisonment up to 2 years can arise for knowingly delivering false tax documents.
Sham economic substance / abuse of Cayman shell

Statutory sanction: HMRC can assert UK tax residence on the basis of central management and control, imposing corporation tax on worldwide profits, plus late payment interest and penalties of up to 100% of tax. The General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) can counteract abusive tax arrangements, and the DOTAS regime may impose disclosure penalties up to £600,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit the company to act as a sham may incur civil liability to creditors and shareholders. Under s.212 Insolvency Act 1986 and ss.993 Companies Act 2006, fraudulent trading gives rise to personal liability to contribute to company assets and criminal penalties including imprisonment up to 7 years and unlimited fines. Travel bans are routinely imposed to secure evidence and assets.
Anti-money laundering / Economic Crime Act violations

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017 and UK Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018; failure to submit a SAR is a criminal offence with imprisonment up to 2 years and an unlimited fine. The Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 introduces strict liability for senior managers in failing to prevent fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners can be prosecuted for money laundering if they knew or suspected the origin of funds; personal fines are unlimited, and imprisonment for up to 14 years applies under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002. Confiscation orders and asset freezes can be enforced against directors personally; director disqualification for serious AML breaches is mandatory.
