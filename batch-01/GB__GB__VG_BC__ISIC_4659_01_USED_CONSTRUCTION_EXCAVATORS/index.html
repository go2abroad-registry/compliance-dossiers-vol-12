Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) — GOV.UK HMRC portal (www.gov.uk/hmrc)

E-government filing portal

A BVI Business Company that is not UK tax resident and has no UK establishment is not ordinarily required to obtain a UK Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) or Corporation Tax registration. For UK VAT purposes, the company must apply directly to HMRC using Form VAT1 (or the VAT2 procedure for non-established taxable persons), providing evidence of identity, non-UK registration, principal place of business, and a UK VAT representative or tax representative where HMRC requires one. The company receives a 9-digit VAT registration number (with prefix GB) and must register before beginning taxable supplies if it will exceed the compulsory registration threshold or is a non-established business making any taxable supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for export applies to goods sold to a customer outside the United Kingdom, provided the goods are physically exported within a permitted time limit and the exporter retains commercial evidence of export (e.g., customs departure message, transport documents, shipment instructions, bank records). Services supplied to a business customer who belongs outside the UK may be outside the scope of UK VAT or zero-rated if they fall within the relevant place-of-supply rules, which is common for intermediaries in international wholesale trade. The used construction excavators transaction must be supported by a bona fide export chain and should include a declaration in the seller's VAT return and appropriate box treatment.

  • UK Customs Goods Export Declaration (SAD Form C88 via the Customs Declaration Service, CDS)
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill for ocean freight or equivalent transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign exchange receipt confirmation from the receiving bank
  • Commercial invoice and fully signed export contract
  • Transport evidence such as CMR (international road carriage) or Air Waybill, if applicable
  • Proof of payment matching the export invoice and customs value

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government clearance of individual invoices is in force. Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT requires digital record-keeping and submission of VAT returns through HMRC-recognised software using HMRC Application Programming Interfaces (APIs). E-invoicing is not mandatory under UK law; paper and PDF invoices remain valid, provided full VAT invoice content and preservation rules are met.

HMRC routinely scrutinises export zero-rating claims. If adequate evidence is not held at the time of supply or if the goods do not genuinely leave Great Britain or Northern Ireland, HMRC will disallow zero-rating and assess VAT at 20%, with late-payment interest. Civil penalties under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 range up to 100% of the VAT for deliberate inaccuracies, with higher penalties for concealment. Retrospective clawback can extend up to 4 years for careless errors, 6 years for deliberate errors, and 20 years in the most serious cases of fraud or deliberate suppression. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export evasion under VATA 1994 and Finance Act 2000 may additionally apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains a fully liberalised currency and capital account regime. Sterling, the US dollar, and the euro are freely convertible for both current and capital account transactions. There is no foreign exchange licensing, surrender requirement, or transaction-level approval for a non-resident BVI Business Company holding a UK corporate bank account. Profit remittance, dividend distributions, and shareholder loan repayments are unrestricted and may be converted into any permitted currency. The only hurdles are prudential: anti-money laundering vetting, sanctions screening, CRS/FATCA reporting, and bank-level source-of-funds verification, any of which can delay settlement but not legally block conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender rules or mandatory conversion ratios under UK law. A BVI Business Company is not required to convert export receipts from used construction excavator sales into GBP, nor to repatriate or deposit proceeds within any prescribed timeline. Receipts may be held in any freely convertible currency. HM Treasury does not impose export-proceeds realisation quotas, and the Bank of England has no balance-of-payments repatriation obligation for the account holder.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (with HM Treasury as the sovereign authority; no dedicated Foreign Exchange Administration)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium for this particular profile. FCA-authorised UK e-money institutions may open accounts for non-resident BVI companies if the beneficial owner and director meet enhanced due diligence, but many EMIs consider used heavy-machinery wholesale as a Medium-Risk Enhanced category requiring additional documentary burden. Offshore digital EMIs not licensed or authorised by the FCA cannot legally provide payment services in the UK; they may only act as unregulated technical services providers. Any EMI must be approved under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011 or the Payment Services Regulations 2017, and must not accept deposits or provide commercial lending. Practical viability is higher for pure settlement and FX conversion, but lower for custody balances, trade-credit, and documentary credits, which require a full licensed bank.
