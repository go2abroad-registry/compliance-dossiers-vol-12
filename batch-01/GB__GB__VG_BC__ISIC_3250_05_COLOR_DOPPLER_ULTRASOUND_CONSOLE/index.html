Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Revenue and Customs (HMRC); online portal: GOV.UK HMRC services via Government Gateway

E-government filing portal

For a BVI Business Company ('BC') commencing UK activities, first ascertain whether the entity has a UK permanent establishment or makes taxable supplies in the UK. For VAT, file Form VAT1 ('Application for VAT registration for businesses not established in the UK') with HMRC, accompanied by certificate of incorporation, registered office address, and bank details; upon approval HMRC issues a VAT registration number. Appoint a UK tax representative if HMRC requires it under Schedule 11 VATA 1994 unless a mutual assistance relief applies (the UK-BVI tax information exchange arrangement generally permits direct registration). For Corporation Tax, register through HMRC's Corporation Tax online registration service to obtain a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) and complete Form CT41G/CT41B as directed. For physical export of goods, obtain a GB EORI number and enrol for the Customs Declaration Service (CDS). All registrations must be kept current under the duty to notify HMRC of relevant changes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope (a non-UK resident BVI BC is within the UK corporation tax charge only on UK-source income and gains attributable to a UK permanent establishment; it does not benefit from BVI's internal tax exemption in the UK unless UK domestic residence and the CTA 2010 rules provide otherwise)

Available election forms

The UK does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' or elective pass-through regime. Entity treatment follows statutory residence (central management and control) and Corporation Tax Act 2010. Hybrid mismatch rules under Part 6A TIOPA 2010 apply asymmetrically with limited elective adjustments, but an ordinary BVI company is treated as an opaque corporation and cannot elect pass-through status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 taxable turnover in the UK over a rolling 12-month period (from 1 April 2024). Non-resident suppliers of goods into the UK may be subject to distance-selling thresholds; non-established suppliers making certain B2C or land-related supplies must register from the first supply irrespective of the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK within 3 months of the time of supply may be zero-rated, provided the supplier retains valid evidence of export and satisfies HMRC Notice 703. The medical Doppler ultrasound console must be consigned from the UK, cleared through customs (CDS export declaration), and delivered to a non-UK destination (e.g., BVI). Certain cross-border services are zero-rated when the customer belongs outside the UK under the general place-of-supply rule. All conditions under VATA 1994, Schedule 8 plus HMRC guidance must be met.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from the UK
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing the medical device
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 bank confirmation evidencing export consideration repatriation
  • Copy of export licence or medical device export authorisation (if applicable)

No real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory continuous transaction control stamp, and no pre-approval of invoices. Under Making Tax Digital (VAT), businesses above the threshold must keep digital records and file VAT returns through HMRC's API using MTD-compatible software. E-invoicing is voluntary and may be exchanged via the Peppol network; no digital stamping is mandated.

HMRC may assess underdeclared VAT for up to 4 years, or 20 years in cases of deliberate conduct and fraud, and may retrospectively claw back zero-export relief with interest. Penalties under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 can reach 100% of lost VAT for careless or deliberate errors. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom has no general exchange-control regime in effect. The Exchange Control Act 1947 was repealed by the Exchange Control (Repeal) Act 1979, and the UK has accepted IMF Article VIII convertibility obligations, meaning current account payments and capital transfers are freely convertible. A BVI Business Company may hold GBP, USD, EUR or multi-currency accounts, accept equity or shareholder-loan capital, pay suppliers abroad, and repatriate profits, dividends, interest, royalties, intercompany loan repayments or liquidation proceeds without obtaining central bank approval. Profit remittance from a UK bank account to a BVI parent or shareholder is not subject to withholding or to exchange-control permission. The only material hurdles are bank-level AML/KYC screening, OFSI sanctions compliance, CRS/FATCA reporting, and proof that the BVI entity is not a sham or a covertly UK-managed company triggering UK corporation tax.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. UK law imposes no mandatory surrender of export proceeds, no statutory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for receiving or repatriating foreign-currency receivables. The BVI Business Company may keep export sales proceeds in USD, EUR or other currency and may utilise offshore clearing accounts. The practical legal duties are evidentiary: under the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017, the account-holding bank must be able to see consistent trade documents for each inbound medical-ultrasound export payment, including commercial invoice, contract, packing list, transport document, customs declaration and matching SWIFT credit advice.

Central bank supervision: The Governor and Company of the Bank of England (Bank of England), the United Kingdom's central bank; HM Treasury is the sovereign fiscal authority and custodian of residual exchange-control powers, with the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) administering financial sanctions. No separate foreign exchange administration licence body exists for ordinary corporate treasury operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for an FCA-authorised UK Electronic Money Institution used as a secondary multi-currency receivables collection tool; Low for an offshore or unregulated EMI. An offshore EMI cannot lawfully issue e-money or provide payment services to a BVI Business Company in the UK without FCA authorisation or a UK-regulated agent model under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011 and the Payment Services Regulations 2017. Authorised UK EMIs offer faster digital onboarding and virtual IBANs but are not a substitute for Tier 1 relationship banking: they do not issue or advise commercial letters of credit, they are constrained from acting as escrow providers for LC proceeds, and many will decline BVI tax-exempt companies for high-value medical-device export flows. The BVI Business Company should therefore hold its primary treasury and trade-finance facility with a Tier 1 bank, using an EMI only for small-ticket receipts and incidental foreign-currency collections. EMIs must safeguard customer funds in segregated accounts and cannot deploy them as working capital.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inward capital injection should be routed as a SWIFT MT103 from the subscribing shareholder's bank to the BVI Business Company's UK Tier 1 bank account. The ordering customer must be the actual shareholder, the beneficiary must exactly match the BVI company name and account, and the remittance information should state 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION' or 'INTERCOMPANY SUBORDINATED LOAN' rather than an ambiguous reference. The receiving bank will require a pre-advice package: board resolution, subscription or loan agreement, UBO identity documents, source-of-funds statements and a group structure chart. The payment may be received in USD into a multi-currency or USD clearing account and subsequently converted to GBP. The UK imposes no exchange-control approval and no stamp duty on inward capital movement, but every node must complete OFSI sanctions screening and the bank may perform a call-back verification. For large transactions the file should include audited or reviewed financial statements and a bank reference from the remitting institution; any deviation from the stated shareholder should be documented with a legal assignment, not merely an instruction field.
