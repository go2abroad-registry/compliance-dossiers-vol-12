Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital or escrow deposit is required for a Cayman Exempted Company; nominal capital of USD 1,000 suffices for incorporation. For FCA authorisation as a payment institution under PSR 2017, the applicant must hold initial capital of at least EUR 125,000 (EUR 50,000 if acquiring only), being paid-up share capital and reserves. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) — operating via the GOV.UK online portal; corporation tax and VAT registrations are handled through the Government Gateway and HMRC's 'Register for Corporation Tax' and 'Register for VAT' digital services. Customs declarations are made through the Customs Declaration Service (CDS). HMRC is headquartered at 100 Parliament Street, London SW1A 2BQ.

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company that is not incorporated in the UK but carries on a trade in the UK through a permanent establishment (PE), or makes taxable supplies of goods/services in the UK, must register for UK tax identifiers. Step 1 — Corporation Tax: register for a 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) by submitting HMRC form CT41G (or the online 'Register an overseas company for Corporation Tax' service) within 3 months of starting to trade in the UK; a UK tax return (CT600) is required annually even where no UK tax is due. Step 2 — VAT: a non-established taxable person (NETP) must register for a GB VAT registration number from the first taxable supply in the UK (no £90,000 threshold applies to non-established businesses); registration is via HMRC form VAT1 (or the 'VATEC' non-established taxable person process, now delivered through HMRC online services); a UK tax representative may be required by HMRC (VATA 1994, Sch 1, para 10A) in cases of serious VAT non-compliance. Step 3 — Customs: obtain a GB EORI number via the HMRC online service for any import/export of goods. Step 4 — PAYE/employment taxes if the entity has UK employees. Step 5 — apply for the appropriate FCA authorisation (Authorised Payment Institution or Small Payment Institution under the Payment Services Regulations 2017) before handling funds; the FCA authorisation number is referenced in AML/tax filings. Where the entity operates a UK branch (PE), the overseas company branch must also be registered with Companies House under the Overseas Companies Regulations 2009 (Form OS BR1), and the registration number must be quoted alongside the UK UTR/VAT IDs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

The UK has no check-the-box election. A Cayman Exempted Company is treated as an opaque non-resident company under UK tax law and is chargeable to corporation tax only on profits attributable to a UK permanent establishment (CTA 2009, Part 4; TIOPA 2010 s 5). Available elections and filings: (i) the UK branch exemption election (CTA 2009 s 18A) is only available to UK-resident companies and therefore cannot be made by a Cayman entity for its UK PE; (ii) hybrid mismatch elections under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A (s 474) may be filed where double non-taxation arises from the Cayman entity's tax-exempt status in the Cayman Islands and a UK mismatch arrangement; (iii) for VAT, the entity may elect to use the VAT Mini One Stop Shop / One Stop Shop (MOSS/OSS) for B2C digital services, the annual accounting scheme, cash accounting scheme, or partial exemption special methods (e.g., bespoke capital goods method or standard partial exemption method under VATA 1994 s 26 and SI 1993/1741); (iv) an election under CTA 2009 s 196A to treat certain shares/derivatives for tax purposes; (v) where no UK PE exists but the company receives UK-source income subject to withholding tax, no UTR registration is mandatory but a claim to treaty relief may be made via HMRC's double taxation treaty claim form (UK-Cayman relations are governed only by a Tax Information Exchange Agreement and a Tax Cooperation Agreement, not a comprehensive double tax treaty, so most categories of UK-source income cannot be reduced below the statutory withholding rates).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 taxable turnover per rolling 12 months for UK-established businesses (from 1 April 2024); scheduled to rise to £100,000 on 1 April 2026. Separately, FCA-regulated payment institutions must also notify HMRC for Money Laundering Regulations supervision where they are not already supervised by FCA.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: exports of goods from the UK are zero-rated at 0% under VATA 1994 s 30 and Schedule 8, Group 6 (supply of goods exported by the supplier) and Group 7 (supply of goods which it is intended to export), provided the goods are physically removed from the UK within the prescribed time limits (usually within 3 months of supply) and HMRC evidence requirements are met (VAT Regulations 1995, Part XVI, regs 116-131). Services for export: financial/payment processing services supplied to non-UK customers are normally outside the scope of UK VAT (B2B place of supply = customer's country under VATA 1994 s 7A and the place-of-supply rules) rather than zero-rated, or are VAT-exempt where they fall within Sch 9 Group 5; the 0% rate applies to services directly related to exported goods (e.g., freight, insurance) under Sch 8 Group 7A. A Cayman Exempted Company acting as a payment gateway will typically charge no UK VAT on B2B fees to overseas merchants because the customer belongs outside the UK, but it must retain robust digital evidence of the customer's business establishment (e.g., merchant's VAT ID, EC sales list references or treaty certificates) to support the outside-of-scope/zero-rated treatment in the UK VAT return.

The UK does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system for VAT invoices. However, 'Making Tax Digital for VAT' (MTD) is mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses (from April 2022), requiring: (i) digital record-keeping in MTD-compatible software; (ii) submission of VAT returns directly from those digital records via HMRC's MTD Application Programming Interfaces (APIs); (iii) tri-annual digital links between software packages; and (iv) penalties based on points for late or non-compliant submissions (Finance Act 2021 s 108, points-based late-submission/late-payment penalty regime effective from 1 January 2023). There is no requirement for pre-clearance of each invoice; however, e-invoices must comply with the UK VAT invoice content rules (VAT Regulations 1995, regs 13-16) and, for cross-border services, the place-of-supply and reverse-charge provisions of VATA 1994 ss 7A, 8 and Schedule 4A. Payment gateway services ('transactions concerning payments and transfers') are exempt financial services under VATA 1994 Sch 9 Group 5, so a PSP will generally be a partially exempt business subject to the input tax recovery rules, the annual partial exemption adjustment, and the capital goods scheme (VAT Regulations 1995 Part XIV).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom operates a fully liberalised capital and current account regime; the Exchange Control Act 1947 has been repealed in its entirety, leaving no statutory exchange controls, conversion quotas, or pre-approval requirements for cross-border fund transfers. Both current account transactions (trade, fees, royalties) and capital account transactions (equity injection, loans, dividends) may be freely converted into GBP and remitted overseas. The only operative discipline is compliance with sanctions, PEP screening, and anti-money laundering reporting; profit repatriation from a UK bank account held by a Cayman exempted company is not subject to any timing, amount, or conversion ratio restriction, provided the beneficial owner and transaction origin are transparent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation for UK-domiciled or UK-account-holding entities to surrender export receipts, repatriate foreign currency, or convert export proceeds into GBP within a prescribed timeline. A Cayman exempted company operating as a payment services provider through UK clearing rails is equally exempt from any mandatory surrender. However, as a regulated activity, all inbound and outbound settlement flows must maintain a complete audit trail, and cross-border transaction data must be reported to HMRC under the UK Payment Services Regulations 2017 and, when applicable, the BoE's International Transactions Reporting System for statistical purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (BoE) acting as the central monetary authority; foreign exchange administration is effectively shared by HM Treasury, the Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA), and the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) for conduct, anti-money laundering, and sanctions compliance under the UK Money Laundering Regulations 2017 and OFSI sanctions framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low to Medium. UK-authorised electronic money institutions (e.g., Revolut Business, Tide, Currencycloud) generally require the customer to be a UK or EEA-incorporated trading entity with substantive local management; a pure Cayman exempted company without a UK branch and without FCA authorisation will typically be declined. Certain offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Leupay, Interpay) can onboard Cayman structures, but they are not direct members of UK Faster Payments or Bacs clearing systems and must route settlement through a UK sponsoring bank, which recreates the same KYC/AML burden. Therefore, a direct corporate banking relationship with a Tier 1 UK commercial bank remains the only viable long-term liquidity and clearing solution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from the Cayman exempted company's shareholder or parent to the UK operating account must be routed via a correspondent banking network using a valid SWIFT/BIC and MT103 message with complete remitter details. Recommended UK Balance of Payments purpose codes: '7.1 - Investment by non-resident in UK subsidiaries' for equity injections, or '6.1 - Loans from direct investor' if structured as intercompany debt. The remitter must attach a board resolution approving the injection, a capital call notice or valuation statement, and source-of-funds documentation evidencing the lawful origin of the transfer. Correspondent banks will require the ordering bank to be a non-OFAC-sanctioned institution with a clean reputation; for non-USD/GBP currencies, the pair is first converted in the London FX market via a market maker before crediting the UK GBP account.Disbursements from the UK treasury account should be executed through a multi-rail clearing matrix: (a) vendor and service payments settle via SWIFT MT103 over the correspondent network, using purpose code '2.0 - Trade / Other services'; (b) merchant payouts in GBP below £1 million per day utilise UK Faster Payments (FPS) for near-instant settlement; (c) same-day high-value GBP transfers use CHAPS for irrevocable settlement; (d) acquiring receivables from Visa/Mastercard settle via association rails (VisaNet and Debit Card Settlement) through a UK Principal Member bank, with daily net settlement into the operational account; and (e) trade transactions requiring document scrutiny are supported by irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit issued by the UK bank under UCP600, using MT700/MT701 messages.
