Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC); digital access via HMRC Online Services and Government Gateway; VAT and Corporation Tax portals at GOV.UK; anti-money-laundering supervision for Trust or Company Service Providers also sits with HMRC

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Cayman home status: the Exempted Company holds a Tax Exemption Undertaking issued by the Cayman Government pursuant to section 6 of the Cayman Companies Act (valid 20 years); this relieves only Cayman Islands taxes and has no effect on UK liabilities. Step 2 – UK establishment: if the company opens a UK branch, file Form OS IN01 with Companies House under the Overseas Companies Regulations 2009 (SI 2009/1801) within one month and obtain a UK Company Registration Number. Step 3 – Corporation Tax: a non-UK-resident company carrying on a trade through a UK permanent establishment must register with HMRC Corporation Tax within 3 months of commencing the trade; HMRC issues a Unique Taxpayer Reference; registration is effected by Form CT41G (non-resident) or the online 'register a non-UK resident company' service; residence is tested under CTA 2009 s.14 (central management and control) – a Cayman exempted company with UK-resident directors or UK-based strategic management will be treated as UK tax resident and taxed on worldwide profits. Step 4 – VAT: register on Form VAT1 (UK establishment) or VAT1F (non-established taxable person) via the HMRC VAT portal once UK taxable turnover reaches GBP 90,000, or from the first taxable supply if not UK-established; HMRC then issues the UK VAT Registration Number. Step 5 – AML supervision: as a commercial registered-agent business, apply online to HMRC for registration as a Trust or Company Service Provider under the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017 before performing such functions in the UK; HMRC issues an AML supervision reference number. Step 6 – Ongoing compliance: file CT600 and VAT returns digitally under Making Tax Digital, retain export and place-of-supply evidence, and maintain arm's-length transfer pricing records for any Cayman-UK intra-group arrangements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Cayman exempted company status per the Companies Act s.6 undertaking, exempting only Cayman taxes); for UK purposes the entity is an opaque, non-pass-through corporate; if central management and control is in the UK it is subject to Entity_Level_Taxation on worldwide profits, and if non-resident with a UK permanent establishment it is charged on a Territorial_Scope basis on UK-attributable profits only; the Cayman exemption does not displace UK tax where a UK nexus exists

Available election forms

UK law has no check-the-box elective classification; status is determined by statute and case law. Relevant elections and mechanisms: (i) VAT group election under VATA 1994 s.43A where a UK branch is included with other group companies; (ii) appointment of a VAT tax representative by a non-established taxable person under SI 2004/2852; (iii) option to tax land under Sch.10 VATA 1994, available but not relevant to pure secretarial services; (iv) UK One-Stop-Shop (OSS) non-UK scheme election for B2C digital and distance sales to EU consumers; (v) hybrid mismatch adjustment and neutralisation elections under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A, which operate only as anti-avoidance corrections; (vi) no double tax treaty exists between the UK and the Cayman Islands, so no treaty-based PE or residence tie-breaker election is available – only a Tax Information Exchange Agreement (TIEA, 2009) governs information exchange.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 90,000 annual UK taxable turnover (from 1 April 2024; deregistration threshold GBP 88,000; previously GBP 85,000); non-established taxable persons have no small-supplies threshold and must register from the first taxable supply in the UK, unless only zero-rated supplies are made

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: exports of goods removed from the UK to a destination outside the UK are zero-rated under VATA 1994 Sch.8 Group 13, subject to the supplier holding evidence that the goods left the UK within 3 months of the time of supply (VAT Notice 725); a valid EORI number and a Customs Declaration Service (CDS) export declaration are required. Services: corporate-secretarial and registered-agent services are B2B services under the general place-of-supply rule (VATA 1994 s.7A); the supply is taxed where the customer belongs, so services supplied to customers outside the UK (e.g., Cayman or other offshore entities) are outside the scope of UK VAT with a 0% effective result, while supplies to UK customers are standard-rated at 20%. The Cayman entity has no UK VAT charge on its offshore fee income; UK input VAT may be recovered either through UK VAT registration or, where no UK taxable supplies are made, through the non-EU '13th Directive' refund procedure under SI 1995/2518 Part XXII.

  • Customs Export Declaration – CDS export entry (EX1) showing 'Released' status, exporter EORI number, destination country and gross mass (goods)
  • Bill of Lading – or Master Air Waybill, CMR or Combined Transport Document evidencing physical departure from the UK (goods)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate – SWIFT MT103 or bank statement evidencing receipt of foreign-currency sales proceeds from the overseas customer
  • Commercial invoice showing the UK VAT number, zero-rated treatment and overseas customer details
  • Packing list (goods) and executed client engagement contract / service performance report (services) demonstrating the B2B nature and place of belonging of the recipient

No continuous transaction controls, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or pre-clearance e-invoicing regime operates in the UK. The mandatory digital layer is Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT: since 1 April 2022 all VAT-registered businesses must keep digital records and file VAT returns via MTD-compatible software connected to HMRC APIs. Peppol BIS 3.0 e-invoicing is voluntary; HMRC consulted on e-invoicing in 2023-2024 and has flagged a possible phased mandate in the late 2020s to early 2030s, but no legislative mandate exists at the effective date.

HMRC may deny zero-rating and assess VAT under s.73 VATA 1994 where the exporter fails to obtain or retain removal or place-of-supply evidence; assessment time limits are 4 years for innocent understatements, 6 years for carelessness and 20 years for deliberate error or fraud (s.77 VATA 1994). Penalties apply up to 100% of the VAT understated under FA 2007 Sch.24 and up to 100% for evasion under s.60 VATA 1994; criminal prosecution may be brought for cheating the public revenue. Input VAT credits can be retrospectively clawed back with interest under s.74 VATA 1994. UK law imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline for exports; the 180-day window is applied as the internal maximum to reconcile inward remittances and documentary evidence, which in practice aligns with HMRC's expectation of contemporaneous proof. Where UK activity is deliberately structured through the Cayman entity to avoid a taxable UK presence, Diverted Profits Tax at 25% plus interest and penalties up to 100% may be charged, and separate civil penalties apply under the MLR 2017 for unregistered TCSP activity.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains a fully liberalised current account and a broadly unrestricted capital account. No general exchange control or approval is required for profit remittance, dividend distribution, or return of capital by non-resident entities. UK-resident banks apply internal FX compliance screens, sanctions checks, and reverse-KYC on beneficial owners; any Cayman tax-exempt company must evidence economic substance if it claims non-UK tax residence, and must not rely on UK treaty benefits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion percentage, and no requirement to repatriate FX earnings in the UK. Exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts. A Cayman Exempted Company with UK banking must still complete BO/BP statistical reports if selected by HMRC/ONS, and must comply with HM Treasury sanctions screening on beneficial owners and counterparties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (BoE); HM Treasury retains sole authority over financial sanctions and certain exchange-control-vicinity restrictions. If the Cayman Exempted Company opens and maintains a Cayman Islands bank account, the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) supervises the licensed bank and local money transmission.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility, with legal constraint: an FCA-authorised Electronic Money Institution (EMI) may issue e-money, process SEPA/Faster Payments, and hold organisational funds, but may not provide deposit-taking facilities or corporate lending. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital subscriptions should be remitted via SWIFT MT103 from an established Cayman bank to the UK operating account. To minimise delays, state the beneficiary as the Cayman Exempted Company, reference the share subscription agreement in field 70 (Remittance Information), and, where the receiving bank uses ISO 20022 purpose codes, use a direct-investment or equity-capital-related purpose code consistent with Bank of England statistical reporting. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% main rate (profits above GBP 250,000); 19% small profits rate (profits up to GBP 50,000); marginal relief fraction 1/200 producing a 26.5% effective marginal rate on the GBP 50,000–GBP 250,000 band. A close investment-holding company is taxed at 25% flat on non-trading profits. A Cayman exempted company managed and controlled in the UK is UK tax resident under the De Beers doctrine and taxed on worldwide profits; if genuinely non-resident, UK tax is confined to trading profits attributable to a UK permanent establishment (CTA 2009 s. 5 and s. 19).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the UK does not impose withholding tax on dividend distributions by UK-resident companies (no ACT since 1999; the former 1/9th tax credit is not repayable). A Cayman exempted company has no Cayman withholding tax on dividends, so outbound distributions by the exempted vehicle, whether to UK, US or other shareholders, suffer no withholding at the corporate level; recipient individuals are taxed under UK dividend rates (8.75%, 33.75%, 39.35%) only as personal income.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Delivery deadline: 12 months after the end of the accounting period (e.g., AP ending 31 March 2025 is due by 31 March 2026). Payment deadline: 9 months and 1 day after the AP end for companies with taxable profits up to GBP 1.5 million; large companies (profits above GBP 1.5 million) pay four quarterly instalments, and very large companies (profits above GBP 20 million) use accelerated instalments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: UK CbCR and the OECD Master File/Local File obligation apply when the worldwide group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750 million (approximately GBP 645 million). For companies below that threshold, HMRC requires 'reasonable, contemporaneous' transfer pricing documentation — including a functional analysis and benchmark — for any controlled transaction where the 'potential advantage' to the UK exceeds GBP 1 million in a year (SME de minimis); 'large' UK companies (meeting at least two of: 250 employees, GBP 36 million turnover, GBP 18 million total assets) must document all controlled transactions, and failure to maintain adequate records attracts penalties of up to GBP 3,000 plus tax-geared penalties under Sch 24 FA 2007.

Economic substance scope: Cayman Islands Economic Substance Regulations 2018 (as amended): a Cayman exempted company carrying on a 'relevant activity' — including (i) headquarters business, (ii) distribution and service centre business, (iii) financing and leasing, (iv) IP holding or (v) pure equity holding — must file an annual Economic Substance Return with the Cayman Tax Information Authority and demonstrate adequate substance (directed and managed in Cayman, adequate employees, premises and operating expenditure proportionate to core income-generating activities). A UK-registered secretarial agent (ISIC 8299) is not per se a Cayman relevant activity; however, if the exempted company earns intra-group service fees or royalty-type returns, it is caught and must either meet the full economic substance test or file a 'nil relevant activity' return. UK counter-part: the Diverted Profits Tax (31% from 1 April 2023) imposes a charge where an offshore entity with insufficient substance obtains a UK tax advantage by avoiding a UK permanent establishment or by diverting UK profits to the Cayman vehicle; the UK's CFC charge (25%) applies to profits attributable to UK resident individuals or UK activities. Practical substance for the exempted company should include a Cayman registered office, physically resident directors (not merely nominee service), real board minutes and a UK filing of a PE return (CT600) where a UK branch exists.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Offshore Evasion and Failure to Correct (FTC)

Statutory sanction: FTC penalties up to 200% of the tax liability, plus a further 5%–10% asset-based penalty where the asset value is significant and a 2% per month penalty (capped at 10% of outstanding tax) for continued non-payment; tax-geared penalties of up to 100% of the additional tax (up to 200% for offshore matters) under Sch 24 FA 2007; publication of the company's name on HMRC's 'deliberate defaulters' list; Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 confiscation orders; daily late payment interest; criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion of corporation tax with an unlimited fine.

Director personal liability: Directors and the secretary can be prosecuted for 'cheating the public revenue' (common law) and for fraud by false representation (Fraud Act 2006) — terms of imprisonment up to 14 years; HMRC may issue Personal Liability Notices (PLN) transferring the company's unpaid tax to directors where the company is insolvent and the evasion was caused by their conduct; travel bans and director disqualification (Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986) for up to 15 years.
Shell Company / Abuse of Non-Residence and DPT

Statutory sanction: Diverted Profits Tax at 31% on the 'notional profits' of the UK PE, assessed via a DPT30/DPT1 notice with a 30-day filing deadline; penalty of up to GBP 10,000 plus daily amounts of GBP 600 for failure to file; additional CFC charge at 25%; Sch 24 penalties of up to 100% of the tax (200% for offshore cases); the company is exposed to estimated and 'doomsday' assessments issued by HMRC under TMA 1970 Sch 18.

Director personal liability: Where the company is a sham or the corporate veil is pierced for artifice (Prest v Petrodel Resources), directors may be personally liable for the company's tax debts; HMRC can apply to the court for a 'management disablement order' against directors; directors' disqualification up to 15 years for unfit conduct in operating a shell company; criminal liability for cheating the public revenue where they knowingly designed the artificial structure.
AML / Company Law Breach by Registered Agent

Statutory sanction: Unlimited fines on indictment and/or up to 2 years' imprisonment under the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017; civil penalties of up to GBP 5,000 per breach (first tier) and revocation of HMRC/SSB registration; striking-off of the UK agent company and prohibition from acting as director/secretary; economic crime legislation imposes asset freezes and confiscation under POCA 2002.

Director personal liability: Directors, the Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) and company secretaries are personally liable for AML offences committed with their consent or connivance (MLR 2017 reg 86); failure to disclose suspicious activity (POCA s. 330) carries up to 5 years' imprisonment; knowingly assisting money laundering (POCA s. 328) carries up to 14 years; fines are unlimited and personal disqualification from regulated work applies.
False CT600 Filing and Fraudulent Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Fraudulent evasion of corporation tax: unlimited fine and/or imprisonment up to 7 years (Fraud Act 2006 and FA 2000 s. 144); penalties of up to 100% for deliberate inaccuracy and up to 100%–200% for offshore-related inaccuracies; daily penalties of GBP 10 per day for record failures; publication of deliberate filers; for the corporate entity, the Criminal Finances Act 2017 s. 45 'failure to prevent facilitation of tax evasion' offence creates an unlimited fine where a person associated with the company facilitates tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors signing CT600s are held to have 'personally approved' the return; where false statements are made knowingly or recklessly, the director is criminally liable (misrepresentation under the Theft Act 1968 and Fraud Act 2006 s. 2) and can be personally fined and imprisoned; HMRC's 'senior accounting officer' regime requires the director to certify the adequacy of tax accounting arrangements, with penalties of GBP 5,000 for unreasonable failure; restitution and contribution orders may render the director jointly liable for the corporation tax shortfall, and a conviction can trigger travel restrictions and extradition procedures.
