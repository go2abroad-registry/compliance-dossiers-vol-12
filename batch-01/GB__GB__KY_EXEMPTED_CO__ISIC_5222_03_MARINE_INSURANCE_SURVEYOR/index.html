Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported by the supplier from the UK to a destination outside the UK are zero-rated if the supplier obtains and retains valid evidence of export within three months of the supply. Offshore services, including marine surveying and claims inspection services supplied to a business customer belonging outside the UK and directly connected with goods whose export or import is taking place, are likewise zero-rated if the place of supply is the customer's country. Where the service is not directly related to export or the recipient belongs in the UK, standard VAT or the reverse charge rules apply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CHIEF/CDS)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or other bank transfer evidence
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Proof of shipment such as a signed SAD Form (Single Administrative Document)

No real-time fiscal clearance requirement or central government e-invoicing clearance system exists in the UK. Making Tax Digital (MTD) mandates digital record keeping and quarterly submission of VAT returns to HMRC via recognised software and Application Programming Interfaces (APIs). E-invoicing is lawful without government digital stamping; however, certain business-to-government (B2G) procurements, such as NHS supply contracts, require electronic invoices that comply with the PEPPOL standard.

HMRC can impose penalty assessments under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 for careless or deliberate inaccuracies, issue assessments for unpaid VAT with interest, and invalidate zero-rating claims where export evidence is missing or fraudulent. Dishonest export zero-rating claims attract civil penalties up to 100% of the VAT plus interest, and criminal prosecution may be brought under the Customs and Excise Management Act 1979 and the Value Added Tax Act 1994, leading to restitution orders and imprisonment for acquisition of fraudulent VAT repayment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom has accepted Article VIII obligations of the IMF Articles of Agreement, providing unrestricted current account and capital account convertibility. Onboarding a Cayman exempted company does not require exchange control permission for dividend or profit remittance. However, payments into and out of the UK must satisfy the Money Laundering Regulations 2017, the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018, and any applicable OFSI licences. A UK branch of a Cayman company is taxable on UK-source profits, but remittance of branch profits to the Cayman parent is not subject to UK withholding tax. Distributions from a UK subsidiary to a Cayman parent are generally free of dividend withholding tax; interest and royalties remain subject to statutory withholding unless relief applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in the United Kingdom. Export proceeds may be retained freely in any currency in an authorised bank account, subject only to standard record-keeping, sanctions screening, and anti-money laundering obligations. No foreign currency repatriation declaration or central bank approval is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England and HM Treasury; the United Kingdom maintains no dedicated foreign exchange control authority because the sterling regime is fully liberalised. The Prudential Regulation Authority (a Bank of England division) supervises banking institutions, while the Financial Conduct Authority regulates payment services and anti-money laundering compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for established cross-border EMIs with multi-licensing and a genuine multi-jurisdictional client base; Low for UK-only digital banks. Legal constraints include FCA conduct-of-business rules requiring EMIs to perform full due diligence on non-resident corporate customers, and many UK-licensed EMIs refuse Cayman exempted companies because they require UK/EEA incorporation, a UK trading address, or a UK VAT registration number. International EMIs licensed in Lithuania, Malta, or Ireland may support a Cayman company subject to enhanced due diligence, higher transaction fees, and lower thresholds. A pragmatic solution is to hold a segregated client account at a UK sponsor bank or operate through a registered UK branch of the Cayman entity.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File is required where the company is not an SME, i.e. the company exceeds two of the following three UK thresholds: annual turnover £50 million, balance sheet total £43 million, or 250 employees. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are mandatory for multinational groups with total consolidated revenue of at least £750 million. All related party cross-border transactions must be documented and priced at arm's length.

Economic substance scope: Cayman Economic Substance Regulations apply to Cayman exempted companies carrying on relevant activities. Marine insurance surveying is generally outside the core insurance/reinsurance relevant activity unless it is integrated into an insurance business; if applicable, core income-generating activities must be performed in Cayman with adequate premises, employees, and board oversight. Independently, HMRC may apply UK permanent establishment anti-avoidance principles to assert a UK taxable presence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 100% of the lost tax, together with daily penalties and late-payment interest; criminal prosecution under the Finance Act 2000 and common law can result in unlimited fines and imprisonment for up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be made personally liable under personal liability notices, disqualified under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986, restrained by travel bans, and subjected to confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Cayman Economic Substance penalties of up to KYD $10,000 for first failure and up to KYD $20,000 plus 6 months imprisonment for continued default; HMRC may additionally apply Diverted Profits Tax at 20% and offshore asset or shell-entity penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who allowed the company to continue without substance can face director disqualification, joint and several liability for diverted profits, travel bans, and asset freezes in the context of civil fraud proceedings.
AML and Financial Crime Breach

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017, with unlimited monetary penalties for corporates; failure to report a suspicious transaction may constitute a money laundering offence punishable by up to 5 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors, Money Laundering Reporting Officers and compliance officers are personally exposed to prosecution, serious crime prevention orders, confiscation orders, and exclusion orders where they knowingly participate in financial crime.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: HMRC may adjust taxable profits and impose penalties under Schedule 18 Finance Act 1998, including penalties up to 100% of the attributable tax for deliberate inaccuracy; daily penalties up to £200 may be issued for non-compliance with information notices.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign inaccurate transfer pricing documentation or facilitate the evasion of TP adjustments may be held jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax, subject to wrongful trading claims in insolvency, and exposed to director disqualification.
