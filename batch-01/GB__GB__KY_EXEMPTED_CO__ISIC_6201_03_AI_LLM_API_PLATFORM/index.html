Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC); online portal: GOV.UK business tax services and Government Gateway (www.gov.uk/topic/business-tax; VAT registration at www.gov.uk/vat-registration; Corporation Tax at www.gov.uk/register-for-corporation-tax; MTD filings at tax.service.gov.uk). No sub-jurisdictional tax authority applies; the UK is a unitary national tax regime.

E-government filing portal

Step 0 — Nexus review: B2B supplies of AI/LLM API services to UK business customers fall within the UK customer's reverse charge and, absent a UK permanent establishment (PE), do not obligate the Cayman company to register with HMRC. Registration becomes compulsory when the entity has a UK PE (CTA 2009 s.19: fixed place of business, dependent agent, or UK staffed API-support/sales hub), supplies B2C digital services to UK consumers (GBP 0 registration threshold for non-established taxable persons), or imports/despatches physical goods into the UK. Step 1 — Register the non-UK (Cayman exempted) company for Corporation Tax within 3 months of commencing a UK trade via the HMRC 'non-UK company' digital route (Form CT41G); submit certified Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum & Articles, register of directors, and UK correspondence address or authorised tax agent; HMRC issues a 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR). Step 2 — Register for VAT online as a non-established taxable person (Form VAT1 via Government Gateway); no statutory value threshold applies; supply Cayman legal-entity due-diligence documents, expected UK taxable turnover, banking details, and appoint a UK tax representative if HMRC requires one. Step 3 — Obtain a GB VAT registration number, an EORI number if any physical UK import/export of goods occurs, and a PAYE reference if UK employees are engaged. Step 4 — Ensure adequate Cayman economic substance (staff, premises, direction, expenditure) for intangible-property receipts to avoid the UK Offshore Receipts of Intangible Property tax (ORIP, Finance Act 2019: 20% on relevant receipts from UK payers where substance in the low-tax jurisdiction is insufficient). Step 5 — Post-registration: file Corporation Tax and VAT via Making Tax Digital-compatible software, retain records for 6 years, and manage Corporation Tax marginal bands (19% small profits rate up to GBP 50,000; 25% main rate above GBP 250,000, with associated-company rules).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Cayman domicile — no income, corporation, or withholding tax in the Cayman Islands). For UK fiscal purposes the Cayman exempted company is an opaque, non-UK-resident company taxed at entity level only on UK PE-attributable profits (Corporation Tax 19%–25%) and on UK-source intangible-property receipts under ORIP (20% where substance is inadequate); it is not a pass-through entity and no check-the-box transparency applies in the UK.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under UK law; tax residence is determined by central management and control (case law: Wood v Holden), so a Cayman board managing and controlling the company from the UK would create UK residence. Hybrid mismatch rules (TIOPA 2010 Part 6A, OECD BEPS Action 2) are mandatory and carry no elective carve-out. VAT elections available to registrants: option to tax real estate, annual accounting (turnover not exceeding GBP 1.35m), cash accounting (turnover not exceeding GBP 1.35m), flat-rate scheme (turnover not exceeding GBP 150,000; narrow application to AI/data services), and margin schemes (not applicable to digital API supplies).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

UK-established persons: GBP 90,000 of taxable supplies in any rolling 12 months (effective 1 April 2024; previously GBP 85,000). Non-established taxable persons (including Cayman exempted companies): no threshold — registration is compulsory from the first UK taxable supply not already subject to the B2B reverse charge; B2C digital/e-services supplied by a non-UK supplier to UK consumers trigger VAT registration at GBP 0; UK B2B customers generally account for VAT under the reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods: 0% UK VAT (VATA 1994 s.30 and Items 1–3 Schedule 8) applies if the goods are removed from the UK by the supplier or on its behalf within 3 months of supply and within 6 months of sale/appropriation, supported by commercial evidence of export. Offshore services: B2B supplies of AI/LLM API services to customers belonging outside the UK are outside the scope of UK VAT (place of supply is the customer's country; reverse charge applies); B2C digital services to a non-UK consumer's usual residence are also outside UK VAT scope; there is no separate 'zero-rating' for services — the applicable treatment is the place-of-supply rule, evidenced by customer establishment, business status, and country records. Services physically performed wholly outside the UK (if any) may be zero-rated. The company must obtain and preserve dual evidence: (i) customer business/location evidence (VAT registration number, IP geolocation, billing address, executed contract), and (ii) for physical goods, the complete export documentation suite.

  • United Kingdom Customs Export Declaration (SAD C88/EX1) transmitted via the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) with Movement Reference Number (MRN) clearance evidence
  • Commercial invoice (final) stating the overseas buyer, Incoterms 2020, goods/service description, value, and AI/LLM API supply characterization
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical removal from the UK (required only for export of goods; not applicable to pure digital API services)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 (or equivalent foreign-currency receipt record) proving payment received from the overseas customer
  • Customer establishment and status evidence: overseas VAT/GST registration number or non-business consumer address, IP geolocation, billing address, and bank details
  • Executed API service agreement / licence schedule confirming place of supply, recipient establishment, and digital-service classification
  • Packing list and HMRC/CDS export status message for any bonded or physical export movements (where applicable)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-approval of invoices in the UK. Making Tax Digital for VAT (MTD) is mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses: digital records must be kept and VAT Returns submitted to HMRC exclusively through MTD-compatible software via HMRC Application Programming Interfaces (APIs). Invoices may be issued in paper, PDF, or any electronic format; PEPPOL-based e-invoicing is voluntary except for certain public-sector procurement.

HMRC may conduct post-clearance export audits and require production of the evidence bundle within 30 days; if zero-rating or outside-scope treatment is unsupported, HMRC issues an assessment clawing back VAT at 20% plus late-payment interest. Civil penalties: 30% (prompted disclosure) up to 100% (deliberate and concealed) of potential lost VAT under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007; customs declaration inaccuracies attract penalties under CEMA 1979. Criminal liability: fraudulent VAT evasion and dishonest evasion (VATA 1994 s.72; FA 2000 s.144) carry penalties and imprisonment up to 7 years; HMRC may extend assessment periods to 20 years in cases of fraud (default 4 or 6 years); mutual exchange of information with overseas tax authorities applies. Note: UK VAT legislation does not impose a statutory 180-day FX repatriation deadline for export zero-rating; the 180-day window in this dossier is the internal compliance and evidence-repatriation benchmark.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full capital and current account convertibility without general foreign exchange controls. Profit remittance, dividend distributions, capital repatriation, and loan repayments from UK-regulated bank accounts are generally unrestricted, subject to sanctions screening, anti-money laundering obligations, and statutory reporting under the Companies Act 2006 and the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018. A Cayman Exempted Company conducting AI/LLM API platform business through UK bank accounts may freely convert GBP to USD, EUR, or USDC via regulated payment institutions, but ensure no activity with sanctioned jurisdictions and maintain at least two weeks of source-of-funds documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies in the United Kingdom. Export receipts may be retained in any permitted currency. For statistical purposes, UK resident companies must report certain cross-border transactions through the Bank of England's International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) if the aggregate value exceeds GBP 50 million per calendar year; a Cayman exempted company without UK residence is not ordinarily a reporting entity, but the UK bank may require a self-designation form confirming non-resident status.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England and HM Treasury (Exchange Control is administered on a liberalised basis under the Currency Act 1982; no separate FX control authority exists)
