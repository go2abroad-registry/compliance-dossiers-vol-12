Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC); online services portal: HMRC Online Services / Government Gateway

E-government filing portal

For a non-UK resident foreign company (BVI Business Company) with a UK taxable presence, registration for Corporation Tax is mandatory if the company has a UK permanent establishment, carries on a UK trade, or derives UK-source income. The foreign entity must register via HMRC's 'Register a non-UK company for Corporation Tax' online service using the Government Gateway, providing company details, director information, registered/uK establishment address, and tax representative if appointed. HMRC will issue a Company Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) and Corporation Tax account. Separately, the entity must register for VAT if it makes taxable supplies in the UK; a non-established taxable person must register for VAT before making any taxable supplies, using form VAT1 or online VAT registration through the HMRC portal. If payroll employees exist in the UK, PAYE registration is also required; for excise or import supplies, an EORI number from HMRC is needed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The UK imposes entity-level Corporation Tax on companies, including non-UK resident companies operating through a UK permanent establishment; there is no UK 'check-the-box' election or hybrid entity classification election similar to the US. The BVI Business Company is not treated as pass-through under UK law, but the UK tax position is moderated by double tax treaty relief where applicable; the entity may elect for matters such as group relief, capital allowances pooling, the Patent Box regime, and may make an election under CTA 2009 / CTA 2010 provisions to align accounting periods. For VAT, the entity may elect to use the Flat Rate Scheme if eligible, annual accounting scheme, or cash accounting scheme, but no election alters the fundamental corporate tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 GBP cumulative taxable turnover for UK-established businesses over a rolling 12-month period; non-established (offshore) businesses have no registration threshold and must register from the first taxable supply made in the UK, subject to distance selling and overseas goods supply rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside the UK are eligible for zero-rating under section 30(6) VATA 1994 and VAT Notice 703, provided the seller retains valid commercial and official evidence of export within the statutory time limits (generally within 3 months of the time of supply). Goods must be physically exported from the UK to a non-UK destination by the seller or on the seller's behalf, and legal ownership must pass either before export or at the final destination. For B2B services performed for a non-UK business customer, the supply is usually outside the scope of UK VAT under the place-of-supply rules (section 7 VATA 1994) rather than zero-rated; specific zero-rating applies to certain international services listed in the VAT Act. The passive electronics inventory distribution model (ISIC 4652) will typically consist of tangible goods exports, requiring proof of physical removal and transfer of ownership to an overseas recipient.

  • Customs Export Declaration (National Export System entry or full UK Customs Declaration / SAD)
  • Commercial invoice
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR road consignment note
  • Packing list
  • Proof of receipt from overseas buyer or overseas warehouse
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (required for foreign exchange control and banking evidence; also used to prove proceeds repatriation where financing or regulatory approval requires it)

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate in the UK. Making Tax Digital (MTD) requires all VAT-registered businesses to maintain digital records and file VAT returns via HMRC-recognised software using API integration with HMRC. E-invoicing is permitted on a voluntary basis; e-invoices and associated digital evidence must be capable of being submitted to HMRC for audit. No mandatory API clearance or prior authorisation for invoices applies.

HMRC applies intensive scrutiny to export zero-rating claims; missing or unreliable evidence can result in the reclaim of input VAT being disallowed and VAT at 20% being assessed on the supply. Civil penalties for inaccuracies and dishonesty are up to 100% of the tax involved, with interest and late-payment surcharges. Deliberate evasion with fraudulent intent can lead to criminal investigation under CEMA 1979 and VATA 1994, with unlimited fines and imprisonment of up to 7 years; directors and tax representatives may be jointly liable for evasion in certain cases. Retrospective clawbacks extend to a 4-year assessment window (or 20 years for deliberate non-disclosure).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full current and capital account convertibility under the Currency Act 1983 and the Exchange Control Act 1947 (as repealed in 1979). No general restrictions apply to profit remittances, dividends, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds for non-resident corporate shareholders, provided transactions are conducted through U.K. regulated banks and comply with U.K. sanctions, anti-money laundering, and know-your-customer requirements. The U.K. does not impose exchange control approval gates on cross-border capital movements; however, payments to sanctioned jurisdictions or persons are blocked.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in the United Kingdom. Export proceeds may be held in any permissible currency in a U.K. bank account or offshore account, and no obligation exists to convert foreign currency into British pounds. The only relevant compliance condition is accurate declaration for HMRC tax and customs purposes and the maintenance of transfer pricing documentation where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority) and HM Treasury (Exchange Control Policy)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is High for U.K.-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011, and for U.K. registered payment institutions under the Payment Services Regulations 2017. Offshore digital EMIs without FCA authorisation or passporting rights have Low viability because they cannot offer sterling accounts or access U.K. clearing systems directly; any such provider must rely on a U.K. banking partner or an authorised EMI, and may be treated as a higher-risk third-party payment intermediary. Legal constraints also include safeguarding requirements, client money segregation rules, and FCA supervision for the U.K. entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder loans or equity injections from the BVI parent should be routed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the BVI bank or another international bank to the U.K. corporate operational account. The payment message must include a clear purpose code, such as 'LOAN' for intercompany loan disbursements, 'INVS' for equity capital, or the standard ISO 20022 Purpose Code 'INCA' for capital injection. Banks require the U.K. entity to provide the executed loan agreement, board resolutions approving the capital contribution, and evidence of no adverse sanctions exposure on the remitting party. For amounts exceeding EUR 10,000 or equivalent, enhanced due diligence is triggered under U.K. AML regulations; a formal source-of-funds report should accompany the transfer.Trade settlement should be executed using SWIFT MT103 for direct wire payments to suppliers, or via an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (documentary credit), particularly when the supplier requires pre-shipment payment security. U.K. banks use the CHAPS system for same-day sterling settlement, while euro-denominated trade payments are generally cleared through the Clearing House Automated Payment System (CHAPS) as euro correspondent or via TARGET2 using accounts held with U.K. euro clearers. For USD disbursements, payment rails may utilise the bank's U.S. correspondent network (Fedwire/CHIPS) or the Clearing House Interbank Payments System.
