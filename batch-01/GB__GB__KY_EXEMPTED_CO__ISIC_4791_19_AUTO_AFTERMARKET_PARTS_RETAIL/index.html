Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC); online portal: HMRC Online Services / Government Gateway (gov.uk); customs filing via Customs Declaration Service (CDS).

E-government filing portal

The Cayman Exempted Company, being non-UK incorporated and centrally managed and controlled outside the UK, must register as a Non-Established Taxable Person (NETP) for UK VAT using HMRC form VAT1A submitted online; a UK VAT registration number is issued from the first taxable supply in the UK (no turnover threshold applies). A Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) for corporation tax is required only if the company becomes chargeable through a UK permanent establishment; a CDF (Customer Duty Deferment) account and EORI number are required for customs declarations if the company exports goods in its own name. For online marketplace sales, the deemed-supplier rules may shift VAT registration duties onto the marketplace operator if goods are sold through a UK online marketplace.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No UK 'check-the-box' election exists. UK corporation tax follows statutory residence and permanent establishment principles; the Cayman exempted company is not transparent and remains tax resident in the Cayman Islands, bearing UK tax only on UK-source profits attributable to a UK PE. Elections available: optional VAT registration for the NETP (VATA 1994); VAT group registration under section 43A VATA 1994 where an overseas entity has a UK establishment; partial exemption special method approvals; Option to Tax on land for any UK property; and use of Postponed VAT Accounting (PVA) for import VAT. Hybrid mismatch rules (Part 6A CTA 2010) apply to direct tax only and do not alter the entity's non-transparent status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 85,000 annual taxable turnover for UK-established businesses. Non-established overseas sellers have a zero threshold: registration is mandatory from the first UK sale of goods that are subject to UK VAT, including distance sales of goods imported into or located in the UK. Post-Brexit rules (2021 VAT e-commerce package retained in domestic law) treat online marketplaces as deemed suppliers for imported goods consignments not exceeding GBP 135, with the actual seller remaining liable in other cases.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The online retail supply of automotive replacement parts and tuning accessories qualifies for 0% VAT where the goods are physically exported from the UK to any destination outside the United Kingdom (including EU member states after Brexit), are exported in the same state, and are exported within the required time limits of the supply. The seller must retain valid commercial and customs evidence, and the buyer must not be a UK VAT-registered person receiving goods in the UK. Genuine export using international courier or freight forwarder services, with the seller or the buyer in control of export, satisfies Schedule 8, Group 13 VATA 1994 and HMRC VAT Notice 703. The online marketplace deemed-supplier model does not preclude zero-rating if the underlying economic supply is an export sale, provided the marketplace transfers title and risk and export documentation is held by the entity that files the VAT return.

  • Customs export declaration lodged via HMRC Customs Declaration Service (CDS), including the Goods Movement Reference and 6-digit commodity code (8708 for motor vehicle parts)
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract identifying the buyer, description of goods, quantity, price, and INCOTERMS
  • Transport documentation evidencing physical departure from the UK: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or courier consignment note with tracking status and date of departure
  • Proof of export corroborating the transport document: port/airport departure records, export packing list, or carrier delivery confirmation from the destination country
  • Bank settlement or payment evidence showing foreign currency repatriation or payment from the overseas buyer; there is no UK statutory Inward Remittance Certificate requirement, and HMRC generally accepts payment evidence and bank statements
  • Retention of any e-commerce transaction log, order records, and IP/logistics evidence demonstrating shipment to an address outside the UK

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing clearance authority, or digital stamping regime is in force in the UK. Mandatory digital filing applies through Making Tax Digital (MTD): VAT returns must be submitted using HMRC-recognised software connected via HMRC APIs, with digital links maintained between accounting records and the nine-box VAT return; paper returns are no longer accepted for VAT-registered businesses. E-invoicing remains voluntary (Peppol-based B2B exchange is permitted but not required). All customs export declarations must be lodged electronically through the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) with optional pre-departure messaging; no fiscal printer or real-time stamping is required.

HMRC may assess undeclared VAT for up to 4 years, extended to 20 years for deliberate misdeclaration or evasion, and may issue penalty determinations up to 100% of the VAT for dishonest conduct (Finance Act 2007 Schedule 24 and Finance Act 2008 Schedule 41), plus interest. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims, including fabricated export evidence or goods that never left the UK, expose the company to retrospective VAT clawback, customs civil penalties, denial of zero-rating, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion of VAT; goods may be seized and forfeited, and dishonest officers may face personal liability. The seller must retain export evidence for at least 6 years; where proof of export is missing, the 0% rate is retrospectively denied. HMRC also scrutinises artificial FX repatriation delays and round-tripping structures; although UK VAT law does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline, failure to evidence payment within 180 days is treated as a red flag and may trigger a full export evidence review, with penalties and interest chargeable on any VAT found due.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: £1 million (for local file) and £50 million (for master file) based on turnover; SMEs (turnover <£10m, assets <£5m, employees <50) are exempt unless they meet specific conditions.

Economic substance scope: UK does not have Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) but applies Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules. A Cayman exempted company that is UK tax resident must demonstrate real economic substance in the UK (e.g., management, employees, assets) to avoid CFC attribution and anti-avoidance challenges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to 100% of tax understated; criminal prosecution with unlimited fines and imprisonment up to 7 years under the Fraud Act 2006 and TMA 1970

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participate; travel bans and asset freezes possible under Proceeds of Crime Act 2002; disqualification as director up to 15 years
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: CFC rules may attribute profits to UK shareholders; penalties for failure to file CFC returns; potential recharacterisation of transactions; fines up to £3,000 per return plus tax due

Director personal liability: Directors can be held jointly and severally for unpaid tax if they knowingly allow the company to be used as a shell; personal liability for penalties; potential extradition for serious tax fraud
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to £5,000 per breach; criminal penalties up to 2 years imprisonment and unlimited fines under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017; suspension of payment processing

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for AML failures if they consent or connive; fines and imprisonment; prohibition from acting as director; travel bans and asset seizure under POCA
