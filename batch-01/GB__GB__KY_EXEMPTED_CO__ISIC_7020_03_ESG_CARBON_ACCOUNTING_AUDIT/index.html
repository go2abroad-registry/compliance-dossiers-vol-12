Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Companies, Cayman Islands General Registry (Department of Commerce and Investment, Cayman Islands Government, Grand Cayman), with e-filing through the Cayman Integrated Registry System (CIRS).

Governing statute

Cayman Islands Companies Act (2023 Revision), a British Overseas Territory law under the sovereignty of the United Kingdom: exempted company incorporation regime (ss. 7–10), memorandum and articles of association (ss. 5–6), registered office obligations; supplemented by the Companies Management Act (2015 Revision), the Beneficial Ownership Transparency Act (2021 Revision), and the Economic Substance Act (2018 Revision).

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum and Articles of Association filed with the registration application (Form REG-1) under the Companies Act; the Registrar issues the Certificate of Incorporation upon approval.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 14 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital: the exempted company may incorporate with one fully paid subscriber share of no par or nominal value (typically US$1). No escrow, capital contribution, or bank deposit verification is required by the Cayman registry; the licensed registered office provider conducts subscriber due diligence only.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance from the Cayman Islands Fire Service (CFS) for the registered office premises, covering suppression, egress, and smoke detection per NFPA 101; structural load and occupancy safety certification issued by the building owner under the Cayman Islands Building Code; occupational health clearance under the Labour Act covering office first-aid provisions, fire marshal designation, and workstation ergonomics. No industrial civil-defence clearance applies given the absence of a physical plant.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital: the exempted company may incorporate with one fully paid subscriber share of no par or nominal value (typically US$1). No escrow, capital contribution, or bank deposit verification is required by the Cayman registry; the licensed registered office provider conducts subscriber due diligence only.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC), the national tax authority of the United Kingdom; primary digital portals are GOV.UK (www.gov.uk), HMRC Online Services, the Customs Declaration Service (CDS), and the Making Tax Digital (MTD) API framework.

E-government filing portal

The entity is a Cayman Exempted Company (a non-UK resident, opaque corporate body). UK registration obligations arise only upon the creation of a UK tax nexus. Step 1 (company law): if the company establishes a UK branch or place of business, file Form EE01 with Companies House under the Overseas Companies Regulations 2009 within one month, obtaining a UK Establishment (UKEST) number. Step 2 (corporation tax): if the company carries on a trade in the UK through a permanent establishment, notify HMRC through the online 'Register for Corporation Tax' service within three months of commencement; HMRC issues a 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) and an online corporation tax account. Step 3 (VAT): as a non-established taxable person (NETP), register for VAT by completing the online registration (form VAT1 equivalent) before making any taxable supply in the UK; HMRC issues a GB VAT registration number; HMRC may require appointment of a UK tax representative under VATA 1994 Schedule 11. Step 4 (customs): obtain a GB Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number (GB + 12 digits) before submitting export declarations through CDS. Step 5 (payroll and other taxes): register for PAYE and, if applicable, environmental or plastic packaging taxes only where a UK payroll or registrable activity exists. The Cayman Islands domestic TIN and tax-exempt status are not recognised for UK registration purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The UK has no check-the-box or form-based entity classification election; classification is determined by statute and legal form. A Cayman Exempted Company is always an opaque company chargeable to UK corporation tax at entity level on profits attributable to a UK permanent establishment (territorial scope for non-residents; its Cayman Tax_Exempt_Zone status is wholly disregarded by HMRC). Relevant elections include: (i) election under CTA 2009 s.18(3) for a non-UK resident company within the charge to corporation tax on a UK trade to be taxed by corporation tax on other UK-source income; (ii) treaty-based PE profit attribution under the OECD authorised approach, including mutual agreement procedure relief; (iii) VAT group registration election under VATA 1994 s.43, available where common control of 80% or more is shown and a UK establishment exists; (iv) option to tax land and buildings under Schedule 10 VATA 1994; (v) partial exemption special method election to apportion residual input tax; (vi) hybrid and other mismatch elections under CTA 2010 Part 6A, allowing an alternative tax treatment to neutralise a hybrid mismatch within the statutory response period; and (vii) advance pricing agreements and thin capitalisation agreements under TIOPA 2010. All such elections are time-limited HMRC notifications rather than entity classification opt-ins.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 taxable turnover in the trailing 12 months, or expected turnover within the next 30 days, for UK-established businesses. A non-established taxable person such as the Cayman Exempted Company has no turnover threshold: it must register for UK VAT from the first taxable supply made in the UK, unless a statutory exception applies for international services or distance selling rules. Registration is also compulsory for UK-established persons whose acquisitions of goods from abroad exceed £90,000 per annum.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating is available under VATA 1994 s.30(6) and the VAT Regulations 1995 where the taxable person exports goods to a destination outside the UK (including the EU) and, within three months of the time of supply, obtains and retains both official evidence (e.g., CDS export declaration output confirming physical departure) and commercial evidence (e.g., invoices, packing lists, insurance documents). The goods must be exported by or on behalf of the supplier and must physically leave the UK within the statutory window. Services: for ISIC 7020 ESG carbon footprint auditing and offset brokerage, the place of supply for B2B services is where the customer belongs, so supplies to non-UK business customers are outside the scope of UK VAT with reverse charge applying in the customer's jurisdiction; B2C services received outside the UK are likewise outside the scope. The supplier must hold two independent items of evidence of the customer's business status and country of establishment, and for services of the effective use and enjoyment by a non-UK recipient; absent such evidence, the supply is standard-rated at 20%.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

The UK does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime. Mandatory digital reporting applies through Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT: all VAT-registered entities must maintain digital records and file VAT returns via MTD-compatible software through HMRC APIs, with digital links between software components (mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses from April 2022). E-invoicing is voluntary and format-based (PEPPOL/EN 16931 is under active consultation for future mandate); paper and PDF invoices remain lawful, with a 30-day invoice issuance rule. Import and export declarations are required electronically through the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) API, with pre-lodgement of export declarations up to 30 days before departure and post-departure submission within 24 hours of export.

HMRC applies stringent audit scrutiny to export zero-rating claims. Official and commercial export evidence must be obtained within three months of the time of supply; failure triggers a retrospective clawback in which the supply is recharacterised as standard-rated, HMRC raises an assessment under VATA 1994 s.73 (normal four-year window, extended to six years for careless inaccuracies and twenty years for dishonest conduct), and statutory interest is charged on the underdeclared VAT. Civil penalties under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 range from 30% (careless) to 70% (deliberate) and 100% (deliberate and concealed) of the tax at risk; Schedule 41 Finance Act 2008 penalises failure to notify or register; default surcharge and late-filing penalties apply separately. Criminal liability arises under VATA 1994 s.72(1) for fraudulent evasion of VAT, carrying up to seven years' imprisonment and unlimited fines, and under the common law offence of cheating the public revenue. Where an exporter knew or should have known that the export chain was connected to fraudulent suppression of VAT (the Kittel/Trayman and Avery doctrine), HMRC may also deny input tax recovery and claim the VAT as lost. All customs and commercial export records, including inward remittance evidence, must be retained for at least six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under UK law, both current and capital account transactions are fully convertible. There are no exchange control restrictions applicable to resident or non-resident entities, including Cayman exempted companies maintaining UK bank accounts. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and loan repayments may be made freely in any currency without prior central bank approval. The UK subscribes to IMF Article VIII and OECD Codes of Liberalisation. The only constraints arise from HM Treasury financial sanctions (UK Sanctions List), anti-money laundering verification, and tax reporting obligations under the Common Reporting Standard (CRS) and FATCA. A Cayman exempted company should be able to convert GBP to USD or any other currency for operational needs, provided source of wealth documentation satisfies UK banks' internal enhanced due diligence policies. There are no repatriation hurdles, but if the Cayman entity is managed and controlled in the UK, it may become UK tax resident, which is a legal risk outside FX convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

UK law does not impose any export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements. Exporters and service providers, including ESG advisory and carbon credit brokers, may retain proceeds in foreign currency indefinitely. There is no statutory timeline for repatriation. The Cayman Islands similarly imposes no exchange surrender obligations. Such receipts are subject to standard tax reporting in their relevant jurisdiction. For UK branch or permanent establishment profits, transfer pricing and attribution rules apply, but not FX surrender.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority), with HM Treasury as the responsible authority for financial sanctions and residual exchange control matters

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium to Low. UK-licensed electronic money institutions (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business, Tide, Modulr) generally require the customer to be a UK-incorporated entity or to have a genuine UK trading presence. A Cayman exempted company, while permitted to open an account under UK AML regulations, will likely face enhanced due diligence, restrictions on the scope of multi-currency transactions, and mandatory requirement of a UK registered address or UK tax reference. Offshore EMIs that are not UK- or EEA-licensed are not viable for GBP clearing via Faster Payments or CHAPS and cannot provide UK sort codes. Legal constraint: under the Payment Services Regulations 2017, only UK or EEA-authorized payment institutions can provide payment accounts in the UK, and onboarding a Cayman entity may be treated as high-risk and require senior compliance approval.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from Cayman shareholders into a UK operating or settlement account should be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 with purpose codes such as 'A0100 – Investment in capital (equity) of the company' or 'A2000 – Loan repayment'. For a Cayman exempted company, the routing is normally from a Cayman bank to the UK bank through correspondent banks such as Bank of New York Mellon, JPMorgan, or Citi. Required verification includes the UK bank's internal beneficial ownership attestation, the underlying investment agreement or board resolution, and where the capital is used for carbon offset inventory, a commercial invoice or purchase contract. All inbound wires must pass HM Treasury sanctions screening and confirm the originator and beneficiary details exactly.For settlement of carbon credit purchases and offset project development services, UK GBP payments can be made via CHAPS using SWIFT MT103 for high-value time-critical payments, and via Faster Payments for low-value transactions. Commercial letters of credit (L/C) are permissible and advisable where counterparties require trade finance security, particularly in emerging markets. All outbound disbursements must carry an accurate payment purpose code (e.g., A0501 for goods traded, A1101 for business services) and maintain a full audit trail.
