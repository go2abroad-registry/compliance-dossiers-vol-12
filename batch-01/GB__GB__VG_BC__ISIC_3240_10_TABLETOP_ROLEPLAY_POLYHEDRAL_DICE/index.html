Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A non-resident BVI Business Company must apply to HMRC using form VAT1 (or VAT1GI for non-established taxable persons), appoint a UK tax representative where required, provide proof of economic activity, corporate registration documents, details of UK establishment or direct sales, and expected UK taxable turnover. HMRC issues a UK VAT registration number (GB prefix) and determines the effective date; registration may be mandatory from the date the threshold is exceeded or from the date it becomes foreseeable that the threshold will be exceeded. The entity must maintain digital records compatible with Making Tax Digital for VAT software and file returns via MTD-compatible APIs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Under UK domestic law no general check-the-box election exists; a BVI Business Company is a body corporate outside the UK corporate tax net unless centrally managed and controlled in the UK or carrying on a trade in the UK through a permanent establishment, resulting in UK corporation tax at 25% (main rate) or 19% (small profits rate) only on UK-source profits. The entity may elect to be treated as a non-resident company for corporation tax purposes if it is not UK resident; flat-rate VAT scheme and annual accounting scheme elections are available for qualifying VAT traders; partial exemption and option to tax elections apply to land and property but are irrelevant to dice casting exports.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 90,000 (annual taxable turnover); GBP 0 threshold for non-established businesses that make taxable supplies in the UK

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the direct export of goods sold to non-UK businesses or consumers and shipped outside the UK within the required time limits, and to services treated as supplied where the customer belongs outside the UK and the supply falls within the place-of-supply B2B rules. The BVI Business Company must hold valid commercial or official export evidence at the time VAT return is submitted; for goods, evidence must be obtained within 3 months of the supply; for services, the supplier must retain contracts, invoices, and evidence of the customer's overseas status and place of establishment. Missing evidence renders the supply taxable at the standard rate of 20%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (valued at commodity code 9503 or 9504 as applicable, referencing the GB Economic Operator Registration and Identification number)
  • Bill of Lading (for sea freight from UK port) or Air Waybill (for air freight from UK airport)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer evidence showing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice with explicit zero-rated VAT treatment and reason code for export
  • Packing list identifying shipped goods and shipping address outside the UK
  • Proof of buyer's non-UK residence and purchase order/contract

Real-time fiscal clearance is not mandated; however, HMRC requires Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT, mandating digital record keeping and submission of VAT returns via HMRC-recognised software using direct API integration from 1 May 2022 (fully digital for all VAT-registered businesses). There is no government-approved digital stamping or continuous transaction control; e-invoicing may be used voluntarily but must contain all statutory details and be machine-readable. HMRC operates a VAT penalties and interest regime for late submission and late payment.

HMRC may issue civil penalties of up to 100% of the VAT under-asserted for deliberate error, plus interest accrual; fraudulent misuse of zero-rating may trigger criminal prosecution under Finance Act 2003 and VAT evasion penalties including fines and imprisonment up to 7 years. Retrospective VAT assessments may be made up to 4 years for careless errors and up to 20 years for deliberate concealment, with repayment and interest clawback, suspension of VAT registration, and debarment from MTD benefits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility with no statutory approvals, quotas, licensing, or registration requirements for cross-border capital movements. The BVI Business Company (VG_BC) is free to convert GBP, USD, EUR, and all freely traded currencies without Bank of England or HM Treasury consent. Profit remittances, dividends, royalty payments, management fees, intercompany loans, and share-capital repatriation may flow into and out of the UK without exchange-control restriction. The only operative constraints are the Money Laundering Regulations 2017, the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018, and OFSI licensing requirements for any sanctioned counterparty, jurisdiction, or transaction. No surrender, conversion, or prior-approval hurdle applies to the epoxy-resin dice casting e-commerce business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory repatriation timeline, and no required conversion ratio for export receipts in the United Kingdom. Repeal of the Exchange Control Act 1947 by the Finance Act 1987 eliminated all residual surrender obligations. Receipts from Kickstarter/Stripe (USD), PayPal (multi-currency), and direct trade sales may be retained in any currency indefinitely. Prudent treasury policy nevertheless recommends same-day concentration of foreign-currency sales proceeds into the licensed UK operating account to optimise liquidity visibility, reduce FX exposure, and demonstrate transparent cash-flow mapping to the onboarding bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority); HM Treasury administers the Exchange Equalisation Account. The United Kingdom has no dedicated foreign-exchange administrative authority comparable to a capital-controls regulator, and exchange controls were fully abolished in 1979.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. British-regulated Electronic Money Institutions under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011 (for example Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Modulr) are legally permitted to open settlement, FX, and payment accounts for a BVI Business Company, subject to each EMI's internal client acceptance policy. However, many retail-facing EMIs categorically exclude BVI corporate clients or impose elongated UBO verification; Currencycloud and Airwallex are the most viable because they operate B2B cross-border treasury desks with established policies for BVI holding structures. The legal constraint is that EMIs are not deposit-taking banks: they cannot issue commercial documentary credits, do not hold direct CHAPS membership, and are not covered by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme. The EMI rail is therefore a supplementary sweep and FX aggregation channel, not the principal liquidity clearing backbone.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% main rate of UK Corporation Tax under Corporation Tax Act 2010 s.3 for taxable profits above £250,000; 19% Small Profits Rate for profits not exceeding £50,000; marginal relief applies between £50,000 and £250,000, producing an effective marginal rate of 26.5%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - the UK imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions paid by UK-resident companies to shareholders, including a BVI Business Company declaring dividends to its BVI parent or members; dividends are paid gross with no ITA 2007 s.874 or s.906 charge.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

CT600 must be filed within 12 months of the end of the chargeable accounting period (s.3 TMA 1970). Corporation tax is payable within 9 months and 1 day after the end of the accounting period for ordinary profits; large companies with taxable profits exceeding £1,500,000 must pay by Quarterly Instalment Payments under CTA 2010 s.59E.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory UK Local File and Master File obligations apply to 'large' enterprises, defined as companies with at least 250 employees and either turnover exceeding £26,000,000 or balance sheet assets exceeding £19,500,000; Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least €750,000,000 (approximately £657,000,000) in the preceding fiscal year. Smaller BVI BC operations remain subject to the arm's length standard under CTA 2010 Part 4, but contemporaneous documentation is only mandatory if HMRC issues a notice or a CbCR threshold is met.

Economic substance scope: As a BVI Business Company, VG_BC is within the scope of the BVI Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). If the company is tax resident in the UK by reason of central management and control, it is excluded from BVI substance obligations only upon filing the BVI annual return and demonstrating UK tax residence to the BVI International Tax Authority. UK law then requires real economic substance: premises, personnel, equipment, and entrepreneurial risk in the UK under CTA 2009 s.5 and OECD PE attribution; otherwise the UK GAAR, diverted profits tax at 25% for accounting periods commencing on or after 1 April 2023, CFC rules, and Pillar Two top-up tax considerations apply. A BVI shell with no BVI or UK substance that holds UK dice-manufacturing profits will be countered with full recharacterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Default

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 100% of the tax understated for deliberate and concealed behaviour under FA 2007 Schedule 24; daily penalties up to £60 per day after a failure notice; statutory interest at 7.75%; criminal prosecution under FA 2000 s.144 and TMA 1970 s.107; publication of deliberate tax defaulter details where the understated tax exceeds £25,000.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable under FA 2013 s.204 where assets are stripped to avoid collection; director disqualification up to 15 years under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986; asset freezing and confiscation orders under POCA 2002; imprisonment up to 7 years for cheating the public revenue.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: BVI ESR penalties of up to US$20,000 for first non-compliance, up to US$20,000 additional and possible imprisonment for continuing default; UK diverted profits tax at 25%; CFC charge under CTA 2010 Part 9A on attributable profits; GAAR counteraction with a 60% charge on counteracted advantages.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit revenue diversion face contribution orders under CDDA 1986 s.15A, personal penalties for false statements (TMA 1970 s.95), restraint orders, passport surrender and travel bans under TMA 1970 s.167 where unpaid tax exceeds £5,000 and departure from the UK is imminent, and disqualification of up to 15 years.
Anti-Money Laundering and Payment Rails Abuse

Statutory sanction: Offences under POCA 2002 s.327-329 carry up to 14 years' imprisonment and unlimited fines on indictment; breaches of the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds Regulations 2017 attract penalties up to £4,000,000 or 10% of turnover; account freezing orders and civil recovery orders under POCA 2002 Part 5; forfeiture of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally prosecutable as principals for concealing criminal property (s.327), failure to disclose (s.331), and tipping off (s.332); confiscation orders under POCA 2002 Part 2 can be enforced against personal assets; travel bans, restraint orders, and director disqualification for up to 15 years may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing and Sham Intercompany Pricing

Statutory sanction: HMRC adjustment to arm's length profits under CTA 2010 Part 4; penalties up to 100% of the additional tax where the inaccuracy is deliberate under FA 2007 Schedule 24; diverted profits tax at 25% on contrived arrangements lacking economic substance; late payment interest on the resulting tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign defective transfer pricing documentation or false balance sheets face personal penalties for dishonest records (TMA 1970 s.95), contribution orders under CDDA 1986, and, on company insolvency, personal liability for wrongful trading under Insolvency Act 1986 s.214 equal to the loss caused to creditors; criminal sanctions apply where documents are forged.
