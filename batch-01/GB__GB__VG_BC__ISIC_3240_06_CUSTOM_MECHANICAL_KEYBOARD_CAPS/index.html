Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC). Primary portals: GOV.UK / Government Gateway (https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration); UK customs via Customs Declaration Service (CDS) (https://www.gov.uk/guidance/customs-declaration-service); MTD VAT API endpoint: https://www.tax.service.gov.uk/organisations/vat.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Apply for a GB EORI (Economic Operators Registration and Identification) number via HMRC's online EORI application before any import or export. Step 2 - Register for UK VAT as a Non-Established Taxable Person (NETP) under VATA 1994 s3(2) using form VAT1 through the Government Gateway; HMRC requires: BVI certificate of incorporation, director details, anticipated UK taxable turnover, UK agent details, and, where goods are sold in consignments not exceeding £135 to UK consumers, confirmation of the deemed-supplier rule. HMRC issues a 9-digit VAT Registration Number (VRN). Step 3 - If the BVI entity becomes UK resident or acquires a UK permanent establishment, obtain a UK Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) via CT41G; a non-resident company with UK source income may register for Self Assessment on form SA1. Step 4 - Enrol for CDS through an approved software bridge or appoint a customs broker for export declarations. Step 5 - Register as an employer for PAYE if UK staff are engaged.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A BVI Business Company is a separate legal person chargeable to UK corporation tax at entity level on profits attributable to a UK permanent establishment (CTA 2009 s5, s19); without a UK PE, the non-resident company is taxable only on UK-source income. BVI domestic tax exemption does not extend to UK-source activities. For UK VAT, the BVI BC is a taxable person (VATA 1994 s92) with no pass-through eligibility.

Available election forms

The UK does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory mechanisms include: (i) foreign branch per-filings election under CTA 2009 s18A (irrevocable; allows branch profits to be excluded from UK charge where subject to foreign tax); (ii) VAT grouping election under VATA 1994 s43 (requires UK-established group members; a BVI parent alone cannot form a UK VAT group without a UK-established subgroup); (iii) option to tax land under VATA 1994 Sch 10 if a UK warehouse or fulfilment centre is acquired; (iv) hybrid and branch mismatch adjustments under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A, including potential HMRC designation of the BVI BC as a reverse hybrid where UK-resident participators hold interests; (v) CTA 2010 s939G notification rules for gains of foreign companies controlled from the UK; (vi) VAT annual accounting or cash accounting elections subject to turnover conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 annual taxable turnover for UK-established businesses (VATA 1994 s3; Notice 700/1). For Non-Established Taxable Persons: registration is mandatory if any taxable supply is made in the UK or where future taxable supplies foreseeably exceed the threshold within 30 days. For imported goods sold in consignments not exceeding £135 to UK consumers, the supplier is the deemed supplier (s7A VATA 1994; Notice 700/24) and must account for 20% UK VAT at the point of sale irrespective of the £90,000 threshold, unless an online marketplace is the deemed supplier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under VATA 1994 s30 and item 1, Group 1, Schedule 8 read with VAT Notice 703 where: (i) the supplier is a UK VAT-registered person or a non-established VAT-registered trader; (ii) the goods are physically exported from the UK within 3 months of the time of supply; (iii) the exporter retains valid commercial and customs evidence of export, including CDS export declaration and proof of departure from the UK customs territory; (iv) the goods are exported by the supplier, the overseas customer, or an approved third-party carrier/freight forwarder in the same state. For goods in consignments not exceeding £135 imported into the UK and sold to UK consumers, the deemed-supplier rule requires 20% VAT at point of sale and such supplies are NOT zero-rated. Offshore design and customisation services supplied to a non-UK B2B recipient are outside the scope of UK VAT under place-of-supply rules (Notice 741A). Evidence retention period is 6 years.

  • Commercial invoice (with HS code per UK Global Tariff, e.g., 3926.90 for plastics; INCO terms; valuation per D.V.1 basis)
  • Packing list with item-level weights and quantities
  • Bill of Lading (sea) / Air Waybill (air) / Post Office or courier export certificate (express) evidencing departure from GB territory

The UK has no real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing stamping, or sales-data reporting mandate for domestic VAT invoices. Mandatory obligations: (i) Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT - all VAT-registered businesses must maintain digital records and submit VAT returns via HMRC-recognised Functional Compatible Software through the MTD VAT API; (ii) e-invoicing is voluntary and may be exchanged via PEPPOL BIS 3.0 / UBL 2.1; HMRC does not pre-clear or cryptographically stamp invoices; (iii) goods exports require electronic customs declarations through the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) using CDS-approved software or a customs broker, producing a Movement Reference Number (MRN) and declaration acceptance; (iv) non-UK sellers using UK fulfilment warehouses must hold a GB EORI and lodge entry declarations via CDS; (v) Shopify Payments and Stripe are payment facilitators only and do not constitute HMRC-approved tax clearance gateways, but the merchant must configure VAT calculation and generate compliant VAT invoices for B2B buyers.

Export zero-rating is an HMRC high-risk review area. Where statutory export evidence is absent or defective, HMRC may raise a best-of-judgment assessment under VATA 1994 s73 (time limit: 4 years; extended to 20 years for deliberate conduct). Penalties: (i) inaccuracy penalty under FA 2007 Sch 24 up to 100% of potential lost revenue; (ii) dishonest evasion penalty under VATA 1994 s60/s61 up to 100% plus interest under s74; (iii) customs duty evasion under CEMA 1979 s170 with civil penalties and criminal prosecution; (iv) corporate criminal liability for facilitation of tax evasion by associated persons under the Criminal Finances Act 2017 with unlimited fines; (v) retrospective clawback arises if goods are returned to the UK within 12 months without import declaration, if CDS records departure but the destination jurisdiction confirms non-arrival (HMRC mutual-assistance exchanges under VAT 416), or if the 3-month departure rule is breached; in such cases zero-rating is reversed, output VAT is re-imposed, and interest runs from the original due date. HMRC also applies MTIC-style diversion analytics to cross-border sales; in B2C dropshipping where an online marketplace is the deemed supplier, the marketplace may be jointly liable for uncollected UK VAT unless the sales qualify for VAT-free export under s30.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains no exchange controls; GBP and all major currencies are fully convertible for current account and capital account transactions. A BVI Business Company operating through UK bank accounts may freely remit dividends, intercompany loans, or capital repatriation proceeds without special government approval, subject only to standard AML/KYC screening and sanctions compliance. No hurdles exist under UK regulations for profit remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory surrender or conversion timeline applies to export receipts in the UK. Exporters may retain foreign currency balances indefinitely in any authorized bank account. Only routine statistical reporting via the Bank of England Quarterly International Transactions Survey may apply, but there is no conversion ratio requirement.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Monetary Policy Committee) with HM Treasury for fiscal/regulatory oversight; Prudential Regulation Authority supervises banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility with legal boundaries. Authorised UK e-money institutions (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business, Currencycloud) offer fast multi-currency accounts, virtual IBANs, and direct clearing. However, FCA e-money licenses or equivalent EEA authorisation must be verifiable. BVI entities are generally accepted, but enhanced due diligence may be applied due to tax-exempt non-resident status; restrictions may arise if the BVI entity cannot demonstrate genuine economic substance or a clean source of funds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: £1,000,000 (for controlled transactions with related parties; SMEs with turnover below £200m and assets below £2bn are exempt from full documentation but must maintain contemporaneous records)

Economic substance scope: UK applies the 'central management and control' test for tax residency. A BVI company managed and controlled from the UK is deemed UK tax resident and subject to UK corporation tax on worldwide profits. Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are not directly applicable in the UK, but the UK's substance requirements are embedded in the residency test and anti-avoidance rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to 100% of tax understated, plus interest; criminal prosecution leading to fines and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participate in evasion; travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed under Proceeds of Crime Act.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits, recharacterisation of transactions, penalties up to 100% of tax avoided, and potential strike-off from UK register.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for unpaid tax if the company is a sham; disqualification as director for up to 15 years.
AML and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Fines up to £5,000 per breach, imprisonment up to 2 years for money laundering, and confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability if they consent or connive in money laundering; travel bans and extradition may apply.
