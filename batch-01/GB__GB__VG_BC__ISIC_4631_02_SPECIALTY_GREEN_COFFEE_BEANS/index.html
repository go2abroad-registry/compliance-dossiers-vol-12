Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC), United Kingdom Government. Operational digital portals: gov.uk tax account, Government Gateway, HMRC Online Services, Making Tax Digital (MTD) Application Programming Interfaces (APIs), and the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) which replaced CHIEF for all import/export declarations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Entity identity: the BVI Business Company (BC) must produce its BVI certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, and register any UK establishment, branch or place of business with Companies House (Form BR3/OS), obtaining a UK establishment number. Step 2 — Corporation Tax: if the BC is UK tax resident (central management and control exercised in the UK) or carries on a trade through a UK permanent establishment (PE) (e.g., UK warehousing, UK sales office, UK agent with authority to conclude contracts), the BC must register for Corporation Tax with HMRC by filing Form CT41G and will receive a 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR); CT returns (Form CT600) are due 12 months after the accounting period end. Step 3 — VAT: as a Non-Established Taxable Person (NETP), the BC has nil registration threshold and must register online via Government Gateway (VAT1-equivalent digital process) before making its first taxable supply in the UK, obtaining a 9-digit GB VAT registration number; HMRC may require appointment of a UK tax representative under VATA 1994 s.8A(2) and SI 1995/2518 reg. 212. Step 4 — Customs: the BC must obtain a UK EORI number (GB prefix) through HMRC CDS to clear green coffee bean imports and effect export declarations. Step 5 — Identity evidence bundle: passport/certificate of incorporation of legal owner, director register, beneficial ownership register, UK place-of-business evidence and a letter of engagement appointing a UK tax representative where so required.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation with Tax_Exempt_Zone overlay: the BVI BC is itself exempt from BVI corporate income tax on foreign-source and offshore business income (BVI Business Companies Act, 0% BVI tax on non-BVI-source income). For UK purposes, the BC is not automatically UK resident merely because it is BVI-incorporated; UK residence follows the case-law test of central management and control (CMC). Where CMC is exercised in the UK (UK resident directors, UK board meetings, UK decision-making) the BC is fully within the UK Corporation Tax charge on worldwide profits at 25% main rate (19% small profits rate for profits up to £50,000, marginal relief between £50,000 and £250,000, effective from 1 April 2023). Where CMC remains in BVI, the BC is non-UK-resident but still taxable on trading profits attributable to a UK PE (territorial scope: UK-source trading income only). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with Tax_Exempt_Zone overlay: the BVI BC is itself exempt from BVI corporate income tax on foreign-source and offshore business income (BVI Business Companies Act, 0% BVI tax on non-BVI-source income). For UK purposes, the BC is not automatically UK resident merely because it is BVI-incorporated; UK residence follows the case-law test of central management and control (CMC). Where CMC is exercised in the UK (UK resident directors, UK board meetings, UK decision-making) the BC is fully within the UK Corporation Tax charge on worldwide profits at 25% main rate (19% small profits rate for profits up to £50,000, marginal relief between £50,000 and £250,000, effective from 1 April 2023). Where CMC remains in BVI, the BC is non-UK-resident but still taxable on trading profits attributable to a UK PE (territorial scope: UK-source trading income only). Given the business scope (Green_Coffee_Import into the UK), creation of a UK PE is highly probable, so in practice the BC will file UK CT returns on PE-attributed profits, including its share of container coffee trading profit, with double tax relief for any BVI tax (nil in practice).

Available election forms

The UK has no check-the-box or general entity-classification election; status follows CMC case law and treaty tie-breakers. Available elections relevant to the structure include: (1) Postponed VAT Accounting (PVA) election under VATA 1994 s.32A and SI 1995/2518 reg. 40B, enabling import VAT on green coffee to be accounted for on the VAT return rather than paid at customs; (2) MTD functional-compatible software election and the 'VAT group' election under VATA 1994 s.43A for the BVI BC to be grouped with a UK subsidiary (if established), creating a single VAT registration and intra-group supplies outside the VAT net; (3) group relief joint elections under CTA 2010 Part 5 to surrender losses between the BC and UK-incorporated group members; (4) capital allowances pooling election under CAA 2001 s.198A for UK warehouse plant and machinery; (5) hybrid and hybrid mismatch elections under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A (BEPS Action 2) where the BVI BC's 0% BVI-tax status is used in structured payments generating a deduction-without-inclusion or dual-deduction mismatch in the UK; and (6) transfer-pricing 'authorised OECD approach' self-election documentation under TIOPA 2010 Part 4, including advance pricing agreement (APA) application with HMRC where green coffee intercompany margins require certainty.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 rolling 12-month taxable turnover for UK-established businesses (effective 1 April 2024, increased from £85,000); deregistration threshold £88,000. However, the BVI BC as a Non-Established Taxable Person (NETP) has a nil registration threshold: VAT registration is compulsory before the first taxable supply is made in the UK. Import VAT is payable on all commercial imports of green coffee beans into Great Britain at 20%, deferrable under the duty deferment account or postponed VAT accounting (PVA), unless an import exemption or customs special procedure (e.g., inward processing, customs warehousing) is used.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exports is available under VATA 1994 s.30(6) and SI 1995/2518 regs. 129-134 for a supply of goods which HMRC is satisfied have been exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK (including the BVI). The BVI BC's UK trade qualifies only if all of the following are satisfied: (i) the goods (green Arabica beans, HS 0901.11) physically leave the UK within 3 months of the time of supply (HMRC VAT Notice 703); (ii) the supplier holds both 'commercial evidence' (e.g., customers' order, invoice, insurance, payment SWIFT) and 'official evidence' (e.g., CDS export declaration with MRN, endorsed Bill of Lading) as prescribed by Notice 703 and regs. 129-134; (iii) the supplier is the exporter or holds a principal-to-principal export contract with the overseas buyer; and (iv) the beans are not subject to any subsequent UK supply or processing before export. Domestic supplies of green coffee beans within the UK are standard-rated at 20% (VATA 1994 Sch.8 Group 1 Part II exception for coffee and other beverages); only the export leg qualifies for 0%. The BC may also reclaim import VAT on its green coffee imports via PVA, and any residual input VAT (including customs-related fees) may be recovered through the VAT return, subject to the 4-year correction window (SI 1995/2518 reg. 37). Empty container return shipments and overseas freight services are outside the scope of UK VAT, while UK-origin freight is zero-rated where exported.

  • Bill of Lading (ocean containerised freight) or Air Waybill (where air-freighted) endorsed to the overseas consignee, evidencing the beans' removal from the UK
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 payment receipt evidencing receipt of sale proceeds from the BVI or other overseas buyer

There is no statutory real-time fiscal clearance or government e-invoicing clearance platform (unlike Italy's SDI or Spain's SII). However, Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT is mandatory for every VAT-registered business, requiring (i) fully functional MTD-compatible software that maintains prescribed digital VAT records (VATA 1994 Sch.11 para. 6A; SI 1995/2518 Part 5A), and (ii) submission of VAT return data to HMRC exclusively through HMRC's MTD APIs (JSON-based; no manual portal entry after the first non-digital return). HMRC operates an 'API-first' clearance model with real-time validation of return payloads and digital 'nudge' compliance risk scoring. All import/export declarations must be filed electronically through CDS as the national single window, generating a unique DUCR/MRN per consignment; HMRC-approved electronic invoicing (EDI or ETSI EN 319-compliant e-signatures) is accepted, but e-invoicing is voluntary. VAT invoices must be retained digitally for a minimum of 6 years, and HMRC has direct digital access for inspection under the MTD data retention rules.

HMRC applies a risk-based export-evidence verification programme (Notice 703 and the digital export checking pilot); where export evidence is absent or insufficient, the zero-rating is disallowed and HMRC may assess the VAT via a protective assessment under VATA 1994 s.73 within the statutory time limits (2 years for careless behaviour; 20 years for deliberate behaviour under VATA 1994 s.77). Penalties and clawback mechanisms include: (i) the civil penalty regime in Schedule 24 FA 2007, up to 100% of the VAT amount for deliberate inaccuracies, with reduction for unprompted disclosure; (ii) criminal prosecution under VATA 1994 s.72 for fraudulent evasion, attracting up to 7 years' imprisonment and unlimited fines; (iii) retrospective clawback of input VAT and deemed-supply assessments under VATA 1994 s.77 where import VAT was deducted and export zero-rating is later invalidated; (iv) missing-trader intra-community (MTIC)/carousel-fraud measures applying the Kittel (Case C-439/04) 'knowledge test', which can disallow input VAT recovery even where export evidence exists if the BC knew or should have known of VAT fraud in the coffee chain; (v) penalties under CEMA 1979 ss.167-168 and FA 2003 Sch.4 for false customs declarations; (vi) interest on late VAT under VATA 1994 s.74; and (vii) public naming under Schedule 32 FA 2008. While UK law imposes no statutory FX repatriation deadline, the 180-day benchmark is the standard documentary credit / open-account tenor; extended non-repatriation of export proceeds will trigger HMRC transfer-pricing review under TIOPA 2010 Part 4 and potential bad-debt relief reversal under VATA 1994 s.36 if payment remains overdue by more than 6 months. The complete evidence bundle and all records relevant to the export supply must be retained for at least 6 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility; no prior approval is required for forex transactions, dividend remittance, loan repayment, or profit repatriation for non-resident BVI companies. However, UK banks will apply their own internal FX limits and require supporting board resolutions and source-of-funds evidence. Profit remittance from a BVI company through a UK account is treated as an offshore treasury movement unless a UK permanent establishment is created; all inbound and outbound cash flows pass through the bank's AML transaction monitoring.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The United Kingdom imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender or conversion ratio. A BVI company importing green coffee beans into the UK and re-exporting into the EU or elsewhere may retain proceeds in any freely chosen currency. Reporting obligations are limited to HMRC Intrastat/EC Sales List for intra-EU movement (post-Brexit adjustments), the Bank of England's balance-of-payments survey if selected, and bank record-keeping thresholds under the MLR 2017.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England and HM Treasury; the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) regulates banking conduct and anti-money laundering (MLR 2017); HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) supervises trade data and customs compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High for collections, vendor payments, and FX — but limited for documentary letters of credit and large-value clearing. Root cause: Inconsistent income size vs small balance sheet / frequent large capital injections from BVI shareholder

Defense evidence: Capital injection board minute and shareholder resolution; a fully secured funding tree showing upstream BVI share capital flow; audited or review-level management accounts for the BVI entity (or a letter of confirmation under the International Business Companies Act); bank statement history of the BVI parent; standard CAT (continuous transaction accounting) evidence under the MLR 2017; a treasury policy document describing weekly settlement patterns and FX hedging rationale.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection from the BVI shareholder must be paid via a licensed BVI-regulated bank to a UK clearing bank account in the name of the BVI company. Use SWIFT GPI MT103 with field 70 containing 'Capital Increase' and the purpose code 'M100' or 'J125' (depending on the receiving bank's internal classification). The UK bank will require the absent UBO to have good underlying wealth; a certificate of incumbency, a board resolution, and the paying bank's AML/KYC letter should accompany the instructions. No UK or Bank of England approval is required for a BVI company to hold a sterling or US-dollar account in the UK, but the bank will ensure the funds are not from sanctioned jurisdictions and will subject the transaction to a source-of-funds check. To comply with counter-terrorist financing rules, the remitter must be exactly the BVI shareholder or a disclosed loan maker (no third-party unscreened routing).For green coffee container settlements, use the legacy SWIFT MT103 for prepayment and documentary collection (FOB or CIF under UCP600); for large repetitive FX exposures, use MT101 for liquidity centralisation or MT102/MB for bundled payments to multiple suppliers. For long-haul bulk container trade, the BVI company should issue a commercial import letter of credit at its UK issuing bank (SWIFT MT700, confirmed by a UK or EU bank, or by a local coffee-origin bank), ensuring the underlying documents include the bill of lading, phytosanitary certificate, Green Coffee Association arbitration clause where possible, and the original invoice. Alternative rails include Bankers' Acceptance (BA) for up to 180 days and supplier pre-export finance via the UK bank's structured commodity desk. All disbursements must be matched to the sanctioned trade contract in the bank's connected treasury workspace.Outright FX forwards (USD/GBP, GBP/EUR) traded with an FCA-regulated or European Economic Area-authorized counterparty, for settlement of LC cover and supplier accounts.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in emerging market currencies (e.g., USD/BRL for Brazilian Arabica, USD/VND for Vietnamese Robusta, USD/ETB for Ethiopian origin) — strictly for trade hedge against the green-coffee procurement corridor, entered into under a full ISDA 2020 or IFEMA 2013 master agreement.Cross-currency swaps (USD/GBP and EUR/GBP) with UK authorised swap dealers (bank or broker) under ISDA with zero-threshold collateral, limited to the term of a confirmed trade order or import documentary credit.OTC currency options (European-style vanilla) for protecting budgeted gross margins on quarter-end forward coffee contracts, requiring daily margin and cleared to FCA, where the BVI company has a non-processing-user exemption under EMIR.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High for collections, vendor payments, and FX — but limited for documentary letters of credit and large-value clearing. FCA-regulated e-money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex) can provide multi-currency rails and SWIFT-type transfers, yet they are not wholly owned clearing banks and will not act as issuing bank for import LCs. Recommended positioning: use an EMI for operational float, speed of reconciliation, and cost-efficient FX forwards, while maintaining a full corporate bank for LC issuance and structured trade finance.
