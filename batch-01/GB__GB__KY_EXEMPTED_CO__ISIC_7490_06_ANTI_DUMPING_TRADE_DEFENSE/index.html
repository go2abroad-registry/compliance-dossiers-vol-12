Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains fully liberalised current and capital account convertibility. The Exchange Control Act 1947 has been repealed and no post-Brexit FX restrictions were reimposed. Profit remittance from a Cayman Exempted Company with a UK banking relationship is unrestricted, and no boe approval is required for bringing in, converting, or repatriating GBP, USD, or EUR. The only constraints are bank-level AML/Source of Funds reviews and UK withholding tax rules relevant to interest, royalties, and payments connected with a UK permanent establishment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender regime exists in the UK. Export receipts, anti-dumping defence consultancy fee income, and trade remedy settlement proceeds may be retained in any currency, converted at market rates, and freely remitted offshore without a mandatory conversion timeline or conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (BoE) as central bank and monetary authority; the Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) and Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) as operational supervisors; HM Treasury retains statutory powers over financial sanctions and anti-money laundering policy.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for this low-risk, law-sector consultancy. FCA-authorised EMI/PSD2 institutions (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, ClearBank, Currencycloud) may accept a Cayman Exempted Company under the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017, but only where full economic substance documentation is provided. Legal constraints: UK EMI-PIs are restricted from issuing documentary Letters of Credit, committed FX credit lines, or margin loans, and most EMI onboarding policies reject tax-exempt offshore vehicles without a verifiable UK trade nexus. A UK EMI account is viable only as a secondary collection/payment clearing rail, not as the primary treasury account.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

United Kingdom corporation tax: 25% main rate from 1 April 2023; 19% small-profits rate for profits below £50,000; marginal relief applies between £50,000 and £250,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Cayman Exempted Companies are not subject to Cayman dividend withholding tax, and the UK does not impose withholding tax on dividend distributions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

File by the later of 12 months after the end of the accounting period or 3 months after HMRC issues a notice to deliver a return. Corporation tax payment is due 9 months and 1 day after the accounting period end for small companies; large companies pay quarterly instalments.
