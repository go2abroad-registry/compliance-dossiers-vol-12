Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC), United Kingdom; online portal: GOV.UK (https://www.gov.uk) and HMRC Online Services via Government Gateway; Companies House for UK establishment registration (OV/OS forms)

E-government filing portal

A non-UK Cayman Exempted Company generally has no automatic UK tax registration duty unless it (a) establishes a UK permanent establishment (PE), or (b) registers voluntarily for Value Added Tax. Step 1: Assess UK PE risk under CTA 2009 s.19 using OECD agency and fixed-place tests; for offshore BIM/CAD services, remote supply without UK office, staff, or dependent agent normally avoids a PE. Step 2: If a UK PE exists, register for Corporation Tax online at GOV.UK within 3 months of commencing UK trade; obtain a 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) and file annual CT600 returns. Step 3: If a UK establishment (a branch or place of business physically located in UK) is created, file Form OS IN01 with Companies House within 1 month and comply with Part 34 Companies Act 2006 accounts and director disclosure rules. Step 4: For VAT, register via HMRC Form VAT1 (online or by post) if taxable supplies exceed the £90,000 annual threshold or if voluntarily registering to recover input VAT; a non-UK established business applies to the 'Non-Established Taxable Person' (NETP) unit in Dudley; HMRC will issue a GB-prefixed 9-digit VAT registration number. Step 5: For UK payroll, register for PAYE if UK employees are engaged; obtain an employer PAYE reference. Step 6: Keep the Cayman corporate registry certificate, registered office details, and UK agent appointment letter on file for HMRC beneficial ownership checks under the Persons of Significant Control regime if any UK real estate or public procurement contract is held.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; the Cayman Exempted Company is a body corporate treated as tax resident in the Cayman Islands (no income tax) and is not eligible for UK pass-through treatment. For UK tax, the entity is non-UK resident unless central management and control is exercised in the UK; if so, UK worldwide taxation may apply. In the absence of a UK PE, the company is outside UK Corporation Tax on its offshore BIM/CAD income; if a UK PE exists, UK Corporation Tax applies at 19%-25% band rates on attributable UK-source trading profits. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the Cayman Exempted Company is a body corporate treated as tax resident in the Cayman Islands (no income tax) and is not eligible for UK pass-through treatment. For UK tax, the entity is non-UK resident unless central management and control is exercised in the UK; if so, UK worldwide taxation may apply. In the absence of a UK PE, the company is outside UK Corporation Tax on its offshore BIM/CAD income; if a UK PE exists, UK Corporation Tax applies at 19%-25% band rates on attributable UK-source trading profits. The UK applies a territorial scope for non-resident companies (only UK-trading income and UK land gains are taxed) and treats the entity as transparent for UK DPT purposes? No: DPT is a separate 25% tax on diverted profits where arrangements lack economic substance are designed to avoid a UK PE. The entity is not within a UK tax-exempt zone; its Cayman tax-exempt status is irrelevant to UK tax classification except under hybrid mismatch rules.

Available election forms

The UK does not operate a 'check-the-box' election. Available elections and reliefs include: (1) UK branch exemption election under CTA 2009 s.18A - not available to Cayman-resident companies because the UK-Cayman relationship has no full double taxation treaty qualifying the territory; (2) optional registration for VAT under VATA 1994 Sch.1 para.9 (voluntary registration) and deregistration under Sch.1 para.13; (3) MTD voluntary use for annual accounting and flat-rate scheme election for small businesses; (4) hybrid mismatch self-assessment and election under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A to avoid double counting of deductions or double deduction outcomes involving the Cayman tax-exempt entity; (5) transfer pricing and advance thin capitalisation agreement application with HMRC for cross-border intra-group services, if the Cayman entity transacts with UK affiliates; (6) no election is available to be taxed as a pass-through entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 (taxable turnover threshold effective 1 April 2024; deregistration threshold £88,000); however, non-UK established businesses supplying services to UK VAT-registered businesses under the reverse charge are not required to register solely for that supply (the UK customer self-accounts for VAT); registration may become mandatory if the non-UK business makes direct standard-rated UK supplies exceeding £90,000 or holds goods in the UK for sale, and voluntary registration is available for non-established taxable persons.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating under VATA 1994 s.30(6) and Sch.8 item 1 applies only to goods physically exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK within 3 months of the time of supply; the exporter must retain valid commercial evidence of export. Services: offshore BIM/CAD drafting and structural engineering consultancy services supplied to a business customer located outside the UK are 'outside the scope' of UK VAT under the general place-of-supply rule for B2B services (VATA 1994 s.7A; SI 1992/3121), so no UK VAT is chargeable; if the overseas customer is a UK VAT-registered business, the UK customer accounts for the reverse charge and the non-UK supplier does not charge output VAT; if the supply is to a UK consumer (B2C) the place of supply is the UK and 20% VAT applies, unless the services are electronically supplied or otherwise covered by special rules. Zero-rating of exports is not automatically available for services; instead, the 'outside scope' treatment is claimed by evidencing the customer's business status and non-UK establishment. No export licence is required for BIM/CAD software deliverables.

  • For goods exports: Customs Export Declaration (CDS/SAD EX1) with MRN; Bill of Lading or Air Waybill; commercial invoice; packing list; proof of departure (carrier's date-stamped receipt, shipping note, or port/airport confirmation); contract or purchase order; bank payment confirmation (SWIFT MT103) evidencing inward remittance in contract currency.
  • For service exports (offshore BIM/CAD): executed service agreement; detailed invoice and deliverable schedule; evidence of customer's non-UK business establishment (foreign VAT/goods registration number or certificate of incorporation); acceptance certificate or milestone sign-off from an authorised officer; timesheet and BIM model delivery logs; bank remittance advice showing actual receipt of payment from the customer's overseas account.
  • Supporting evidence for both: internal transportation records (for goods) and intellectual property/delivery metadata (for digital CAD files) to prove that the service was physically performed outside the UK or supplied to a non-UK recipient.

The UK has no real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing clearance system and no digital stamping requirement. Mandatory VAT digitalisation applies through Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT: all VAT-registered businesses must maintain digital VAT records and submit VAT returns using MTD-compatible software via the HMRC MTD Application Programming Interface (API v1.0). Invoices may be issued electronically in standard formats without government pre-clearance. Goods exported outside the UK must be declared electronically through the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) using an Export Summary Declaration or Full Declaration (SAD/EX1), and export evidence must be retained in accordance with HMRC Notice 703. There is no requirement for national tax authority approval of individual invoices or a 'stamping' process.

HMRC's discretionary anti-fraud powers allow past zero-rating claims to be denied retrospectively if prescribed export evidence is not held at the time of the claim or within the required retention period (HMRC Notice 703, s.10). Assessments may be raised for up to 4 years after the end of the accounting period for ordinary error, up to 20 years for deliberate non-disclosure. Civil penalties under Finance Act 2007 Sch.24 apply up to 100% of the potential lost revenue for inaccuracies caused by deliberate or reckless conduct; under Sch.10 penalties for late payment and VAT evasion. Criminal liability may attach under VATA 1994 s.72 (up to £20,000 fine and/or imprisonment up to 7 years). Interest under Finance Act 2009 s.101 is mandatory on under-declared VAT. HMRC also operates a specialist 'Fraud Evasion of VAT' and 'Cross-Border Trading' task force for similar UK and offshore companies; clawback may include repayment of all zero-rated input tax recovered together with statutory interest and a reduction in the entity's penalty mitigation for cooperation. For services, if the B2B place-of-supply relief is misapplied to an agent or intermediary, HMRC may impose the 20% VAT on the full value of the service and deny the foreign status of the recipient.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Cayman Islands exempted company operates with fully convertible GBP/USD/EUR current-account and capital-account flows. The UK removed exchange-control restrictions in 1979; the Cayman Islands does not impose approval requirements for ordinary trade payments, dividends, interest, royalties or service fees. The KYD is pegged to the USD at a fixed rate and freely convertible within the licensed banking system. There are no profit-remittance exchange-control hurdles; the only practical preconditions are KYC/EDD documentation, corroborated underlying CAD/BIM service contracts and tax/compliance filing evidence, as no withholding tax applies inside Cayman and the UK does not tax the Cayman foreign company unless central management and control is in the UK.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no mandatory export-proceeds surrender rules and no prescribed conversion ratio. Neither the Bank of England nor CIMA imposes a statutory timeline for converting USD/EUR export proceeds into GBP/KYD. Invoices may be issued in any agreed currency and proceeds retained in the settling bank account. Record-keeping for at least five years under the UK Money Laundering Regulations 2017 and the Cayman Proceeds of Crime Law remains mandatory to demonstrate the commercial authenticity of each cross-border receipt.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority) for UK monetary and banking supervision; Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) for Cayman Islands currency issuance, exchange-control exemptions and AML/ATF regulation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High for the specified digital rails (Wire_Transfer and Wise), subject to a clean Cayman KYC pack and low-risk BIM/CAD service description. Wise and comparable FCA-regulated electronic money institutions can open remotely, settle multi-currency balances and offer SWIFT correspondent rails; however, EMIs are constrained by the UK Electronic Money Regulations 2011: they cannot provide general overdrafts or credit, cannot issue or advise commercial letters of credit, and customer funds are safeguarded rather than FSCS-insured. Some EMIs will not onboard Cayman exempted companies; a referral letter from a licensed accountancy/tax advisor materially improves approval odds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (main rate for profits above £250,000; small profits rate 19% for profits at or below £50,000; marginal relief between £50,000 and £250,000)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% – the United Kingdom does not impose withholding tax on dividend distributions (neither UK-source dividends from a UK subsidiary, nor dividends paid by a Cayman exempted company out of offshore profits to any shareholder).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing deadline is 12 months after the end of the accounting period; corporation tax payment is due 9 months and 1 day after the period end for small/medium companies, with large companies paying by quarterly instalments. A non-resident company only files if it has a UK permanent establishment or other UK source taxable chargeability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: In GBP: UK Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) requires a Master File and Local File where the ultimate parent group has consolidated group revenue equal to or above £750 million (approximately €750 million). HMRC also applies a lower practical threshold for UK Local File preparation: any UK-resident company or UK permanent establishment (including a Cayman exempted company with a UK PE) reporting intra-group controlled transactions with aggregate value exceeding £1 million in an accounting period must maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation. If the group's UK turnover exceeds £200 million, HM Revenue & Customs expects a substantive Master File, a UK Local File and entity-specific operating model documentation, irrespective of the ESK threshold. A Cayman exempted company with no UK PE and only offshore CAD/BIM contracts typically falls below these filing thresholds but must still document the arm's length calculation of its service fee if a central management and control challenge is made.

Economic substance scope: The United Kingdom applies a comprehensive 'central management and control' test for tax residence and a separate permanent establishment doctrine for non-residents; it does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations domestically. However, the Cayman Islands Economic Substance Regulations (ESR, revised 2019) require all Cayman exempted companies and other resident entities that carry on relevant activities (banking, insurance, financing, distribution, IP holding, headquarters, shipping) to maintain substantial economic presence in the Cayman Islands in relation to that activity. Offshore Building Information Modeling (BIM) and Structural Drafting is not an ESR relevant activity as such, provided the company does not convert into an IP holding vehicle (IP royalties are a relevant activity). To avoid being classified as a UK-resident shell or a sham under UK anti-abuse doctrines, the Cayman entity should maintain: (i) Cayman-registered office, (ii) physically present authorised director or board minutes, (iii) substance decision-making records in Grand Cayman, and (iv) emergency fund of liquid assets.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

UK Tax Evasion / Failure to Charge Corporate Tax on a Deemed UK Permanent Establishment

Statutory sanction: Upon discovery, the Cayman company is assessed on UK PE profits at 25% (or 19% small profits rate) plus penalty under FA 2007 Sch. 24 of up to 100% of the additional tax for carelessness or deliberate error. Daily interest accrues at Bank of England base rate + 2.5%; criminal prosecution under TMA 1970 s.66 and CTA 2010 Pt 33 can result in unlimited fines and imprisonment up to 7 years for directors who orbital to evasion.

Director personal liability: FCDs and authorising directors who knowingly allowed the agency to create a UK PE are personally liable under TMA 1970 s.66; HMRC may issue a personal liability notice, impose travel restrictions for non-payment of over £10,000 of tax, and on conviction directors face criminal imprisonment, disqualification under CDDA 1986, and civil restitution orders requiring the repayment of the full tax plus interest.
Shell Company / Sham Substance Abuses

Statutory sanction: If treated as UK tax-resident, the company is taxable on its worldwide CAD/BIM profits (25% UK headline rate) with retrospective penalties under FA 2007 (up to 100%) and potential dissolvement for non-filing of CT600. Cayman Islands Companies Registrar may strike the company off for failure to maintain a registered office in the Islands; US and UK banking partners may terminate electronic money institution (EMI) bank accounts upon adverse public disclosure.

Director personal liability: Directors and ultimate beneficial owners are exposed to wrongful trading claims under UK Insolvency Act 1986 if the company trades while insolvent; the corporate veil may be pierced in a civil suit, leading to restitution of diverted profits and personal liability of up to £100,000 or an unlimited amount for director negligence. UK HMRC also holds directors personally accountable for unpaid tax where a Cayman shell was used as a façade.
AML / Money Laundering Violations of the Proceeds of Crime Act and Cayman AML Regulations

Statutory sanction: Cayman Islands Proceeds of Crime Law (2019 revision) imposes up to 15 years imprisonment and a fine of up to CI $500,000 per count for money laundering; the UK Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 independently imposes confinement for all conduct occurring in the UK PE. Failure to maintain AML procedures under Cayman AML Regs (App. VI) attracts a civil penalty assessment issued by the Cayman Financial Authority of up to CI $250,000 and revocation of the exempted company's MLRO registration.

Director personal liability: Directors, MLRO and anti-money laundering officers are personally liable for failure to report a suspicious transaction (s.330 POCA) with a maximum sentence of 5 years; personal asset freezing orders under POCA s.439-A are routinely sought. Travel bans are imposed by Cayman Courts where the director is currently on the island, with a monetary bond set for appearance.
Transfer Pricing Evasion and Improper Invoice Allocation

Statutory sanction: HMRC will adjust profits under CTA 2009 Pt 4 s.165/167 to reflect an arm's length fee, imposing additional corporation tax at 25% plus interest. A penalty under FA 2007 s.239 may reach 30% of the understated tax (or 70%-100% for deliberate concealment). In criminal cases of cross-border evasion, HMRC can obtain a worldwide freezing order sufficient to protect the extra tax, fines and costs.

Director personal liability: Finance directors and C-level officers who sign false TP certificates are personally assessable for penalties for reckless inaccuracy under FA 2007 s.100A (up to £5,000 per document). UK director disqualification regime applies for unfit conduct; and if a court orders company repayment of unpaid tax, the directors who orchestrated the offshore fee scheme may be compelled to contribute to the company's dividend reimbursement as restitution.
Unlawful Distribution and Dividends from Capital in a UK-Regarded Company

Statutory sanction: Under UK Companies Act 2006 s.847 and Cayman Companies Act s.37, any shareholder who knows the distribution is unlawful is liable to repay the full amount to the company; directors authorising ultra vires dividends incur civil penalties equal to the amount paid plus interest. If the dividend payment causes the company unable to pay corporation tax, HMRC applies the preference regime under Insolvency Act 2006 s.244-247.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable to restore the amount misapplied to the company, and in cases of fraudulent preference, traceable shares subject to a freezing injunction; UK travel prohibitions are enforceable until outstanding tax and distribution sums (in excess of £20,000) are paid; criminal imprisonment is available for directors of a struck-off company under the Fraud Act 2006 for making off without payment.
