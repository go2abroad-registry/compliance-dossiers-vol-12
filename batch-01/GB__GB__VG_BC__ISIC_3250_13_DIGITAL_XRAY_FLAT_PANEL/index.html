Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC), United Kingdom; primary digital portal: www.gov.uk (HMRC Online Services / Government Gateway for VAT and Corporation Tax returns; Customs Declaration Service (CDS) for import/export declarations).

E-government filing portal

The BVI Business Company (VG_BC), being a non-UK resident and non-established taxable person, must register for UK VAT before making its first taxable UK supply using HMRC's 'Register for VAT (non-UK)' online service (Form VAT1G). No GBP 90,000 turnover threshold applies to non-established foreign entities; registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply unless all supplies are zero-rated or subject to the reverse charge. HMRC issues a UK VAT registration number upon acceptance. For direct taxation, the BVI BC remains outside UK Corporation Tax unless it carries on a trade in the UK through a permanent establishment or receives UK-source income; if such UK taxable presence arises, it must register within 3 months via HMRC CT Online (Form CT41G) to obtain a 10-character Corporation Tax Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR). Concurrently, the entity must obtain a UK-issued Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number (GB prefix) through HMRC's online EORI service to make customs declarations, or appoint an indirect customs representative using Form C1179. UK withholding tax procedures under ITA 2007 apply to any UK-source interest, royalties, or rental income as relevant.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (opaque taxable company; the BVI BC is a separate juridical person liable to UK Corporation Tax under CTA 2010 s.5 only on profits attributable to a UK permanent establishment or UK-source income; its BVI home 'Tax_Exempt_Zone' status affords no UK tax exemption, and the UK does not apply US-style pass-through treatment).

Available election forms

The UK has no US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election; entity characterisation follows the BVI BC's incorporated legal form as an opaque company. Statutory elections available include: (i) voluntary VAT registration to recover input tax (VATA 1994 Sch 1 para 9); (ii) Non-Union One Stop Shop (OSS) election for distance sales of imported goods into the UK; (iii) hybrid mismatch elections under TIOPA 2010 ss.259A-259OE (including hybrid PE elections and hybrid instrument/entity recharacterisation elections) for arrangements within the UK's ATAD hybrid rules; and (iv) Advance Pricing Agreements (APA) and Mutual Agreement Procedure (MAP) elections under TIOPA 2010 Part 4, together with HMRC thin capitalisation advance clearances for cross-border funding arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 90,000 (rolling 12-month taxable turnover threshold for UK-established businesses from 1 April 2024; GBP 88,000 deregistration threshold). Non-established taxable persons (including the BVI BC) must register from the first taxable UK supply with no de minimis threshold, subject to reverse-charge rules for services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods (digital X-ray flat-panel DR detectors and OEM radiology hardware) applies under VATA 1994 s.30(6) and Sch 8 Group 0 Item 1, provided: (i) the goods physically leave the UK within 3 months of the time of supply (extendable to 6 months with HMRC approval); (ii) they are consigned to a destination outside the UK, including EU destinations, which since Brexit constitute true exports; (iii) the BVI BC or its authorised agent holds contemporaneous official and commercial evidence of export (customs export declaration and Movement Reference Number, Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, departure stamps, freight and insurance documents, contract and commercial invoice); and (iv) payment is evidenced by bank remittance/FX records. Related services rendered B2B to non-UK customers (installation, commissioning, software licensing, maintenance) fall outside the scope of UK VAT under the place-of-supply rules (VATA 1994 s.7A) and attract no UK VAT charge. Medical devices additionally require UKCA/CE conformity marking documentation and any applicable export licence to substantiate the zero-rating claim.

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping regime is in force in the UK; electronic invoicing (Peppol/XML) remains voluntary. Mandatory digital compliance is instead enforced through Making Tax Digital for VAT (MTD): every VAT-registered business must keep electronic records and submit VAT returns exclusively via MTD-compatible functional software using HMRC's Application Programming Interface (API), applicable to virtually all VAT-registered businesses since 1 November 2022 (with narrow exemptions). Customs declarations must be filed electronically through HMRC's Customs Declaration Service (CDS) via digital portal or API, either directly or through a customs broker.

HMRC may withdraw zero-rating and assess VAT under VATA 1994 s.73, with statutory interest under s.74, where export evidence is deficient or the 180-day FX repatriation deadline is missed. Penalties apply under FA 2007 Sch 24 (inaccurate returns: up to 100% of tax lost, reduced for disclosure) and FA 2008 Sch 41 (failure to notify: up to 100%), with assessment windows of up to 4 years, extended to 20 years in cases of fraud or an unregistered entity. Fraudulent export schemes, including missing-trader/carousel arrangements and knowingly false export declarations, constitute criminal offences under VATA 1994 s.72 and the Customs and Excise Management Act 1979, punishable by fines up to GBP 50,000 and/or up to 7 years' imprisonment, plus asset forfeiture under POCA 2002. Under the UK-adopted Kittel doctrine, a supplier who knew or should have known that an export transaction was connected to VAT fraud loses both zero-rating and input tax recovery and becomes jointly and severally liable for the unpaid VAT. The VG_BC must therefore maintain a complete due-diligence file (buyer KYC, transport trail, BOL/CMR authenticity checks, payment reconciliation) for a minimum of six years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom operates a full current-account and capital-account convertibility regime. All exchange controls under the Exchange Control Act 1947 were abolished in 1979; no UK governmental approval, licence, or registration is required for inward capital injection, dividend or profit remittance, intercompany lending, royalty streams, or trade payments in GBP or any foreign currency. The only substantive restrictions on cross-border flows are targeted financial sanctions under the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018 (including the Russia, Belarus, Iran, North Korea, Syria, and Venezuela regimes), the National Security and Investment Act 2021 (which may require mandatory notification for acquisitions of UK medical-device manufacturers in sensitive sectors, though greenfield subsidiary formation is exempt), and standard AML/CTF checks. Profit remittance from a UK branch or UK subsidiary of a BVI company is unrestricted, subject to Companies Act 2006 section 830 distributable-profits tests, solvency, and arm's-length transfer-pricing documentation for any intercompany charges.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The UK imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. A BVI exporter operating through UK rails may retain 100% of foreign-currency receivables indefinitely, hold balances in GBP, USD, EUR, or other currencies, and convert freely through the wholesale market. No central-bank reporting is required by the corporate, although the remitting UK bank is required to report balance-of-payments and international-transaction data to the Bank of England and the ONS when individual payments exceed the relevant statistical threshold, and must screen all transactions against OFSI consolidated sanctions lists.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England, acting jointly with HM Treasury and the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI); residual foreign-exchange licensing powers vest in HM Treasury under the Exchange Control (General Exemptions) Order 1979, but no active FX approval regime is enforced.
