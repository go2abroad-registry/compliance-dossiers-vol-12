Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC), 100 Parliament Street, London SW1A 2BQ, United Kingdom. Primary portal: HMRC Online Services via GOV.UK Government Gateway (https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/hm-revenue-customs). Corporate registry: Companies House (https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/companies-house). Customs declarations: HMRC Customs Declaration Service (CDS) via authorised software. Export controls: Export Control Joint Unit (ECJU), Department for Business and Trade.

E-government filing portal

A BVI Business Company (BC) has no automatic UK tax registration duty. Step 1 – Corporation Tax: register online via HMRC's 'Register for Corporation Tax' digital service only if UK central management and control (CMC) makes the BC UK tax-resident, or when it trades through a UK permanent establishment; registration is due within 3 months of the trigger; HMRC issues a 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) and Form CT41G notice to file. Step 2 – VAT: as a non-established taxable person (NETP), register when UK taxable turnover exceeds GBP 90,000 in a rolling 12-month period (or is expected to), or when the first taxable supply is made; apply via form VAT1 (NETP supplement) through the Government Gateway; HMRC issues a 9-digit GB VAT registration number (format GB 999 9999 99) and may require security or appointment of a UK VAT representative. Step 3 – Customs: apply for a GB Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number (GB plus 12 digits) to lodge CDS export declarations. Step 4 – PAYE registration only if the BC employs UK-resident staff. Step 5 – Enrol for Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT and file returns via HMRC-recognised API software.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope: the non-UK resident BVI BC is taxed solely on UK-source profits attributable to a UK permanent establishment; treated as an opaque company, not a pass-through. The BVI jurisdiction classifies the BC as Tax_Exempt_Zone, but the UK grants no automatic entity-level exemption. UK Controlled Foreign Company rules under TIOPA 2010 Part 9A may impute BVI profits to UK shareholders; Diverted Profits Tax at 25% may apply to UK-exerted activities lacking economic substance.

Available election forms

The UK has no US-style 'check-the-box' election. Available statutory elections: (1) CFC regime – election to exclude trading finance profits and claim partial exemptions under TIOPA 2010 ss.371BC and 371ID; (2) Hybrid mismatch rules under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A – no general opt-out, but structured adjustments including 'dual inclusion income' relief are determined; (3) VAT elections – Option to Tax (VATA 1994 Sch.10), Flat Rate Scheme, Cash Accounting, Annual Accounting; (4) No UK-BVI comprehensive double taxation agreement exists, so no treaty election is available; only the UK-BVI Tax Information Exchange Agreement applies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 90,000 (approximately USD 114,000) UK taxable turnover in any rolling 12-month period, effective 1 April 2024; deregistration threshold GBP 88,000; no threshold applies for a taxable person expecting to make taxable supplies in the UK within the next 30 days; distance-selling thresholds abolished for B2B supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

All-terrain military tactical vehicles and related defense hardware qualify for 0% VAT under VATA 1994 s.30 and Sch.7 (goods exported to a destination outside the UK) provided: (i) goods are physically removed from the UK within 3 months of the time of supply; (ii) the exporter holds valid proof of export as set out in VAT Notice 703; (iii) title has passed to the overseas buyer; (iv) a valid export licence is held under the Export Control Order 2008 for controlled military goods; (v) ancillary services (engineering, customisation, commissioning) zero-rate as exported services where the recipient belongs in a non-UK country and the benefit is received outside the UK (VATA 1994 s.7 and the Place of Supply of Services Order 1992). Input VAT on procurement of components may be reclaimed, and the zero rating generates an entitlement to repayment of input tax.

  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Airway Bill (air) or CMR consignment note (road) evidencing physical removal from the UK

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping mandate exists in the UK. Mandatory Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT requires all VAT-registered businesses (since 1 April 2022) to maintain digital records and file VAT returns using MTD-compatible software via HMRC APIs. E-invoicing remains voluntary; PEPPOL interoperability is supported for public procurement. Export declarations are submitted electronically to HMRC's Customs Declaration Service (CDS) and generate a Movement Reference Number (MRN).

HMRC may conduct post-export verification under VAT Notice 703/2 and, if export evidence or the 3-month removal rule is not satisfied, issue a VAT assessment under VATA 1994 s.73 restoring 20% output tax, plus interest under s.74. Civil penalties for inaccuracies under FA 2007 Sch.24 reach up to 100% of potential lost revenue depending on careless or deliberate concealed conduct. Fraudulent VAT evasion is a criminal offence under VATA 1994 s.72 and CEMA 1979, carrying up to 7 years' imprisonment and/or an unlimited fine; Missing Trader Intra-Community (MTIC) rules deny zero-rating where the exporter knew or ought to have known of tax fraud. Unlicensed export of military goods is punishable under the Export Control Act 2002 with up to 10 years' imprisonment and/or an unlimited fine. Assessment periods: generally 2 years after the accounting period end, extended to 20 years in cases of fraud or neglect (VATA 1994 s.77). Diverted Profits Tax at 25% and CFC attribution may additionally claw back untaxed UK-related profits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The UK applies full Article VIII convertibility obligations: both current and capital account transactions are freely convertible into GBP, USD, EUR and any other settlement currency. Non-resident BVI Business Companies may remit dividends, management fees, loan repayments, and liquidation proceeds without Bank of England approval. The only hurdles are prudential and integrity-driven: sanctions screening, anti-money laundering verification, taxation transparency (including CRS/FATCA), and defense-related export licensing. Profit repatriation may be delayed if the bank requests source-of-wealth or beneficial ownership evidence, but there is no statutory FX conversion requirement or quantitative restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No UK statutory requirement exists for surrender, mandatory conversion, or holding of export proceeds in domestic currency. Export receipts for armored tactical vehicle sales may be retained in any authorized currency. However, defense exports must comply with the Export Control Order 2008 (as amended) and ECJU licensing; proceeds must be matched to licensed shipments and documentary export evidence, and any bank facility relating to such proceeds is subject to enhanced due diligence.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority) in coordination with His Majesty's Treasury and the Financial Conduct Authority; there is no standalone Foreign Exchange Administration as the UK maintains a fully liberalized regime without exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability. Authorised UK Electronic Money Institutions (e.g. Airwallex, Currencycloud, Revolut Business) generally refuse or heavily restrict BVI BCs in defense hardware manufacturing. FCA-regulated EMI frameworks under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011 do not permit commercial bank-grade trade finance, large-value government wires, or revolving export credit for high-risk ISIC 3040 activities. Legal constraints include FCA perimeter guidance, sanctions screening, and ECJU restrictions on military goods. A full relation bank with active correspondent banking is mandatory for all serious defense contracts; EMIs may be used solely for incidental FX conversion or subcontractor payments under a segregated ancillary account.
