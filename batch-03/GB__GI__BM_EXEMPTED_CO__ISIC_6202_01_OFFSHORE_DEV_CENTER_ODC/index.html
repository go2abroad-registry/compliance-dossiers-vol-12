Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax) — Government of Gibraltar, Ministry of Finance; online portal: Tax Office e-Services (www.taxoffice.gi). Sovereign reference: HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) for UK extracerritorial matters; Bermuda side: Bermuda Registrar of Companies (www.roc.gov.bm).

E-government filing portal

Step 2 — Register the foreign company as carrying on business in Gibraltar with the Gibraltar Companies House Registry, providing the Bermuda certificate, a Gibraltar registered office, and the appointed local agent/representative. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope (Gibraltar): the non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company is chargeable to Gibraltar corporate income tax only on income arising in or derived from Gibraltar and on profits attributable to its Gibraltar permanent establishment (PE), normally at the standard rate of 12.5%; offshore software-development services rendered to non-resident customers are outside the territorial charge. Bermuda classification: Tax_Exempt_Zone (Exempted Company status).

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate US-style check-the-box elections; classification follows incorporation and PE rules. Available statutory elections: (i) group-consolidation election for Gibraltar-resident companies within a 75% group; (ii) controlled-foreign-company (CFC) territorial-exclusion election supported by an economic-substance memorandum; (iii) OECD Pillar Two election to apply the Income Inclusion Rule and the Gibraltar Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax (QDMTT) for multinational groups with EUR 750M or more consolidated revenue; (iv) transparent-taxation election where the activity is carried on through a Gibraltar partnership or a protected-cell company. No hybrid-mismatch election exists; Gibraltar applies the OECD hybrid-mismatch framework, and deduction-denial consequences follow automatically.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar imposes no VAT, GST, or sales and use tax; indirect taxation is confined to customs and excise duties on physical imports cleared through the Port of Gibraltar plus stamp duty on certain documents. UK VAT does not extend to Gibraltar. Supplies of offshore software services from Gibraltar to non-resident customers are outside the scope of any Gibraltar indirect tax, and Bermuda levies no indirect tax on the Exempted Company.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Gibraltar has no turnover-based indirect-tax registration threshold because no VAT, GST, or sales/use tax is imposed. A customs registration (Local Clearance Holder / Customs Warehouse) is required only when physical goods are imported into or exported from Gibraltar; pure digital services have no economic-threshold trigger.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in an indirect-tax sense: Gibraltar levies no VAT, so no export zero-rating claim, input-VAT refund, or exporter refund procedure exists. Relief is achieved at the income-tax level: exported offshore software-engineering and maintenance services (ISIC 6202) delivered from the Gibraltar-based Offshore Development Centre are excluded from Gibraltar corporate income tax when (i) the services are performed, the contract is concluded, and the benefit is derived outside Gibraltar; (ii) no Gibraltar PE, employee, or agent contracts with the non-resident customer or delivers the service inside Gibraltar; and (iii) the service fee is at arm's length and is received from the non-resident customer within the FX repatriation period.

  • Gibraltar customs export declaration (ESCOT electronic declaration) where physical media, servers, or hardware are shipped out of Gibraltar
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export movement from the Port of Gibraltar where physical goods are exported
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency account/bank statement evidencing receipt of the service fee from the non-resident customer within 180 days
  • Master Software Development Agreement, Statement of Work, and Delivery Acceptance Certificate executed with the offshore recipient
  • Gibraltar territorial-source analysis memorandum and OECD-compliant transfer-pricing documentation where the invoice is issued to a related overseas party
  • Bermuda Exempted Company annual government-fee filing receipt confirming continuation of exempt status

No electronic-invoicing approval, real-time fiscal clearance, API submission, or digital stamping mandate exists in Gibraltar. Commercial invoices must simply contain standard B2B details (issuer/recipient identity, service description, date, amount, and currency) and be retained for six years for income-tax inspection. Should the group elect to register for the EU VAT One-Stop-Shop (OSS) through an EU Member State to sell digital services into EU consumers, the e-invoicing and transaction-reporting rules of that Member State apply; such registration must not be transacted through Gibraltar because Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area.

Under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act and the Tax (Fraud and Evasion) provisions, the Commissioner may re-characterise a purported offshore-source claim, raise deeming assessments, and apply interest plus civil penalties of up to 200% of the tax undercharged for negligent or fraudulent returns. Fraudulent export or offshore-source exclusion claims are criminal offences punishable by unlimited fines and imprisonment for up to 7 years; retrospectively the Commissioner may claw back assessments for up to 21 years in cases of fraud or wilful default (6 years for ordinary errors). Failure to repatriate service fees within 180 days without a documented commercial justification rebuts the offshore-source exclusion and can expose the Gibraltar PE. On the Bermuda side, late filing of the Exempted Company annual government-fee return attracts penalties of up to USD 1,000 plus USD 200 per day of continued default under the Companies Act 1981.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 750,000,000 consolidated group annual revenue for mandatory Country-by-Country Report and Master File; a Local File must be prepared for any related-party transaction exceeding GIP 250,000 per accounting period, and in all cases where the Gibraltar PE's annual turnover exceeds GIP 1 million.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to a Bermuda exempted company that is tax resident in Gibraltar or has a Gibraltar PE undertaking a relevant activity. An Offshore Development Centre performing software engineering and maintenance is generally not a separately listed relevant activity unless it also holds or exploits intellectual property; however, where the ODC earns autonomous IP-related income or acts as a pure holding entity above the software business, full ESR reporting applies. In all cases, the company must demonstrate an adequate number of employed staff, physical premises, actual management and control in Gibraltar, and operating expenditure proportionate to the income booked, and must file an annual Economic Substance Return with the Registrar.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Concealment of Gibraltar PE Profits

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 200% of tax understated; daily late-payment surcharges; criminal fine of up to GIP 250,000 and imprisonment for up to 10 years on conviction on indictment; asset confiscation under the Proceeds of Crime Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause or permit a false return are joint and severally liable for the corporate tax, interest and penalties; corporate veil-piercing is available where the ODC is a mere facade; travel bans, freezing orders, and disqualification from acting as a director for up to 15 years may follow.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Value Extraction

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length re-assessment of the Gibraltar PE with additional corporation tax at 12.5%; interest at the statutory penalty rate; adjustment penalty of 50%–100% of the transfer pricing adjustment; secondary adjustment imposing a deemed dividend or branch reallocation.

Director personal liability: The Bermuda parent and Gibraltar branch directors may be held jointly liable for the additional tax where the pricing arrangement is a sham; personal liability extends to civil restitution and, in egregious valuations, criminal fraud charges.
Shell Company / Breach of Economic Substance Regulations

Statutory sanction: ESR penalty of up to GIP 100,000 per year in Gibraltar; strike-off of the branch or company from the register; automatic information exchange with HM Revenue & Customs and the Bermuda Registrar; in serious cases, assessment of all profits at 12.5% in Gibraltar.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for ESR penalties where they omit to take reasonable steps; disqualification orders and extradition risk applies where the shell structure is used to evade tax.
AML / Sanctions Infringement

Statutory sanction: AML fines up to GIP 2 million per breach; civil recovery and confiscation orders; criminal prosecution for money laundering with imprisonment up to 14 years; cross-border travel restrictions and asset freezing orders.

Director personal liability: Directors fall within the AML relevant persons definitions; personal criminal liability arises where they consent to, or fail to report, suspicious transactions; the corporate veil will not shield personal assets where unlawful intent is proven.
