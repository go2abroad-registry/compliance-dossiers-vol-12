Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office (Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar), operating via the Tax and Customs Portal: www.gibraltar.gov.gi/tax

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned ELP (Exempted Limited Partnership) must submit Form GELP-1 together with the Partnership Deed, Certificate of Registration issued by the Registrar of Companies, details of the general partner(s), and a statement of the registered office in Gibraltar. The application is filed with the Income Tax Office, after which a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (GTRN) is issued within 10 business days. Non-resident partners without Gibraltar assessable income need not file separately.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign-owned ELP (Exempted Limited Partnership) must submit Form GELP-1 together with the Partnership Deed, Certificate of Registration issued by the Registrar of Companies, details of the general partner(s), and a statement of the registered office in Gibraltar. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available. The ELP is statutorily fiscally transparent; each partner is separately assessed on its distributive share. A partner may elect for joint assessment of partnership profits under applicable provisions of the Income Tax Act 2010, but the entity itself has no tax classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Not Applicable – Gibraltar charges no VAT or GST; selective excise duties and customs import duties apply under the Customs and Excise Duties Act 2015.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – there is no general indirect tax or registration threshold. Excise duty registration is at a nil threshold for operators of bonded warehouses for dutiable goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

As Gibraltar has no VAT/GST, exported oat-milk products are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax. If any excise-duty-controlled ingredients are used, duty relief is available on export evidenced by a certified customs export declaration. No zero-rating claim is required under VAT law because no VAT is chargeable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar Customs Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from the receiving customer / bank
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (if preferential tariff claimed)

Not applicable – Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, API reporting, or digital stamping for VAT/GST purposes. Export commercial documents remain the standard evidence for customs and excise purposes.

For fraudulent claims of excise-duty relief on export, the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Authorities may issue a deemed assessment equal to 100% of the relieved duty, impose a pecuniary penalty of up to £10,000 per consignment, and institute criminal proceedings under the Customs and Excise Duties Act 2015. Where a false Inward Remittance Certificate is lodged, offenders are liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding level 5 on the statutory scale and/or imprisonment not exceeding two years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates an unrestricted current and capital account system. Both residents and non-resident exempted limited partnerships may hold, remit, and receive foreign currency in unlimited amounts without prior consent. Profit remittance is subject only to standard AML/KYC verification, not to exchange control or withholding licensing. The GIP/GBP peg provides a stable conversion anchor; any profit or capital can be converted into GBP, EUR, USD, or other freely tradable currencies using commercial bank rates at prevailing market spreads.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation exists. Export revenues from UHT oat milk and packaging products may be retained abroad, converted into any currency, or held in GIP/GBP accounts at Gibraltar-licensed banks. There is no required timeline for repatriation and no mandatory conversion ratio. This reflects the territory's strong OECD alignment with international capital-movement standards.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), acting under the Financial Services Act 2019, is the supervisory authority for banking and currency operations. The Government of Gibraltar's Ministry of Finance retains note-issuance authority under the Currency Notes Act 1950, with the Gibraltar pound (GIP) issued as the domestic conversion unit at a 1:1 peg to Bank of England sterling. HM Treasury and the Bank of England are the ultimate monetary and reserve authorities, with no separate exchange-control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low – no automatic passporting of UK or EU EMIs into Gibraltar after Brexit. Among cross-border digi-EMIs, only those holding a Gibraltar payment institution license under the Financial Services Act 2019 (e.g., a GFSC-incepted EMI) can provide accounts for the ELP. Otherwise, the ELP can use EMIs as secondary FX trade shops, but cannot rely on them for primary clearing because their compliance systems frequently reject offshore partnerships. Feasibility rating: 20/100.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Gibraltar's year of assessment runs 1 July to 30 June. The return is due by 30 November following the end of the year of assessment in which the ELP's accounting period ends. Example: an accounting period ending 31 December 2024 falls within the year of assessment 1 July 2024 – 30 June 2025 and must be filed by 30 November 2025.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 10,000,000 — practical Revenue benchmark for related-party turnover triggering mandatory contemporaneous OECD-aligned Local File and Master File. No formal published de minimis exists; the Commissioner expects proportionate documentation below this level where a large or connected controlling partner is present.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to geographically mobile relevant activities: banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, distribution and service centres, intellectual property, and holding companies. Aseptic beverage manufacturing (ISIC 1050) is not per se a designated relevant activity, but a UHT oat milk plant must still substantiate real control and employment in Gibraltar. An ELP that owns and operates the plant — with physical premises, production lines, quality-control staff, and procurement decisions taken in Gibraltar — satisfies ESR. An ELP that merely holds the plant while manufacturing is subcontracted to a UK affiliate or managed from the Cayman-linked group will be treated as a low-substance shell for UK and Gibraltar tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act 2010; financial penalties up to the amount of tax evaded plus interest and prosecution costs; best-of-judgment assessments with tax-geared penalties of up to 100% of the understated tax; forfeiture orders against proceeds of the unlawful scheme.

Director personal liability: The general partner of the ELP and any limited partner who participates in management are jointly and severally liable; the corporate veil is pierced, civil restitution is pursued against personal assets, and the Supreme Court of Gibraltar may impose travel bans and custodial sentences of up to 7 years for deliberate fraud.
Shell Company / Deficient Economic Substance (ESR)

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may disregard the ELP's structure, reallocate profits to the place of effective management, revoke the ELP's exempt status, and impose economic substance fines (fixed penalty plus daily penalty) under the Economic Substance Regulations; persistent failure leads to strike-off of the partnership.

Director personal liability: A general partner of a limited partnership has unlimited liability by law; shadow-managing directors face disqualification orders, personal civil liability for post-strike-off debts, and the UK and Gibraltar tax authorities will treat the ELP as a sham, exposing the UK parent to direct assessment.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Primary arm's length TP adjustment with compensatory interest; disallowance of excess related-party interest and royalty deductions; tax-geared penalties of up to 100% of the underpaid tax where carelessness or deliberate behaviour is established.

Director personal liability: The general partner and senior accounting officers who certify false transfer pricing declarations are personally exposed; where an artificial arrangement is adjudged a sham, the veil is pierced and civil liability attaches to all signatories; criminal money-laundering charges follow if the TP adjustment is shown to disguise the proceeds of tax evasion.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties under the Proceeds of Crime Act and AML legislation (substantial fines on indictment, confiscation of proceeds, and suspension or revocation of the ELP's registration with the Gibraltar Registry); the licensing of any E-money or payment intermediary used is at risk.

Director personal liability: The general partner, directors of any corporate general partner, and beneficial owners face freezing orders, disqualification, and imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering; the Companies Act disclosure regime makes concealment of beneficial ownership a strict-liability offence, and the corporate veil provides no shield for individuals knowingly moving the illicit proceeds.
