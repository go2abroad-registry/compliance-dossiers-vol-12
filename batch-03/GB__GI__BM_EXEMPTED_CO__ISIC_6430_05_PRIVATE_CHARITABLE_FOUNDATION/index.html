Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar | Portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (including a Bermuda Exempted Company) intending to carry on business or derive income in Gibraltar must apply for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) with the Income Tax Office. The application requires: (i) certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association; (ii) details of the registered office and local representative; (iii) a declaration of intended activities; (iv) a board resolution appointing the local agent; and (v) completed Form GIB-TIN/1. Upon approval, the TIN is issued within 10 business days. If the entity is a non-resident company with no Gibraltar-source income, registration may be optional but is recommended for status clarification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not apply US-style check-the-box elections. A company that meets the requirements may elect to be treated as a 'Qualifying Exempt Company' (transitional) or as a charity under the Charities Act 2004, which provides exemption from Gibraltar income tax. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; Gibraltar follows OECD hybrid mismatch rules without elective regimes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – Gibraltar does not levy an indirect tax on exports; therefore, the concept of zero-rating does not arise. Export of goods is subject to customs duties (if any) but not VAT. Offshore services provided by a tax-exempt entity are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation.

Since there is no VAT export zero-rating in Gibraltar, there can be no VAT clawback. However, any false declaration made to the Income Tax Office or Customs Department can result in penalties under the Income Tax Act (e.g., up to 200% of tax evaded) and criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion. Charitable exemptions are subject to post-registration audit; if the entity ceases to be charitable, exemptions are clawed back with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates a fully convertible currency regime. The Gibraltar pound is pegged 1:1 to the pound sterling and circulates interchangeably within Gibraltar, but sterling is the formal settlement currency for cross-border transactions. Capital account and current account convertibility are unrestricted; no prior governmental approval is required for inbound endowment funding, outbound grantmaking, profit remittance, or liquidation distributions. The United Kingdom's Exchange Control Act 1979 was long repealed, and Gibraltar has not re-imposed any exchange control rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or conversion requirement exists in Gibraltar. Entities are permitted to retain export receipts and all foreign currency earnings in their original denomination without a mandatory conversion timeline or ratio to GBP.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for sterling); Financial Services Commission of Gibraltar (regulatory and exchange administration authority)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate-to-Low feasibility. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not authorised by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission and cannot serve as the principal regulated banking rail for a Gibraltar-domiciled charitable endowment. A non-Gibraltar EMI may be used only as an auxiliary pre-funded disbursement wallet if a separate GFSC-licensed payment institution is engaged or if the EMI operates via a compliant Gibraltar branch. Primary cash and escrow accounts must remain at a fully regulated Gibraltar bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound endowment transfers must be effected by SWIFT MT103 from the donor's bank to the Gibraltar bank account using a clear remittance purpose code such as CHAR or a donor-to-charity contribution code. The payment field must state: ENDOWMENT CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION FOR SCIENTIFIC GRANT-MAKING FOUNDATION – NOT FOR TRADE OR SPECULATION. Each incoming transfer must be pre-notified to the compliance department with a source-of-funds declaration, supporting donor KYC documents, and a certified board resolution approving the inward capital injection. For GBP transfers, banks clear via UK Faster Payments, CHAPS, or SWIFT with a Gibraltar IBAN; for USD and EUR, obtain correspondent bank routing details through the Gibraltar clearing bank.Outbound grant disbursements and any trade-linked philanthropic settlements are processed using SWIFT MT103 instructions from the Gibraltar bank. For trade-related procurement or co-financing with grant counterparts, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit may be issued by the Gibraltar relationship bank and advised through a correspondent bank network. All outbound payments require a precise payment purpose code, a beneficiary legal entity identifier, prior matching against sanctions lists, and a post-settlement document archive. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar Transfer Pricing Rules (effective 1 January 2019, aligned with OECD BEPS): contemporaneous Local File documentation is mandatory where the aggregate annual value of cross-border controlled transactions exceeds GIP 1,000,000; a Master File is required where the multinational group's worldwide turnover reaches GIP 50,000,000; Country-by-Country reporting obligations apply to groups with consolidated revenue of at least GIP 750,000,000. Small and medium enterprises (fewer than 250 employees and turnover or balance-sheet total under GIP 43,000,000) may prepare a simplified Local File, subject to the UK-Gibraltar DTA mutual agreement procedure.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations 2019 (ESR) require every entity carrying on a 'relevant activity' — including investment fund management, financing and leasing, and pure equity holding company activities — to demonstrate: (i) direction and management exercised in Gibraltar, (ii) adequate physical premises, staff, and expenditure proportionate to the income-generating activity, and (iii) core income-generating activities performed in Gibraltar. A private charitable foundation managing endowment assets is classified under the investment fund and holding company categories; the entity must file an annual ESR return and maintain an economic substance file evidencing local board minutes, employment records, and expenditure for the charitable investment office.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Charitable Claims

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 200% of the tax understated; criminal fines up to GIP 500,000 and imprisonment up to seven years under the Income Tax Act 2010 and the Criminal Justice Act 2015; forfeiture of the charitable assets derived from the evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and the foundation's Bermuda and Gibraltar trustees are jointly and severally liable for restitution of diverted charitable funds; court-ordered reimbursement, disqualification as charity trustee for up to 15 years, travel bans pending criminal proceedings, and imprisonment for fraudulent misrepresentation.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR enforcement penalties: fixed penalty of GIP 120 and a daily penalty of GIP 120 per day for continued failure (maximum GIP 120,000); the Commissioner may strike the entity off the Gibraltar Companies Register, revoke the Bermuda exempted company's Gibraltar establishment license, and issue an ESG non-compliance notice to HMRC/UK authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal penalty notices, disqualification orders under the Companies Act 2014 for up to 10 years, and a travel ban preventing departure from Gibraltar until the substance or compliance record is regularised; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission may also order the removal of the licensed fiduciary.
AML and Proceeds of Crime Breach

Statutory sanction: Unlimited fines under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; custodial sentences up to ten years for money laundering and tipping-off offences; asset freezing orders over the endowment portfolio; revocation of the foundation's banking facilities and of the licensed Gibraltar trustee's AML registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and the foundation's compliance officer incur personal criminal liability for tipping-off; the court may order confiscation of personal assets, a travel ban, and director disqualification; repeated breaches trigger personal responsibility for restitution to the Charity Commission and potential extradition under UK-Gibraltar mutual legal assistance arrangements.
Charity Law Violation

Statutory sanction: Removal from the Charities Register; recovery orders compelling the return of misapplied assets; fines up to GIP 100,000; prohibition from receiving further grants or donations; and automatic loss of the charitable income tax exemption with retrospective assessment at 10% plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable to restore misapplied foundation funds; the Charities Commissioner may apply for an order disqualifying individuals from serving as charity officers for up to 15 years; travel bans and imprisonment up to two years apply for knowingly making a false declaration of charitable purpose.
