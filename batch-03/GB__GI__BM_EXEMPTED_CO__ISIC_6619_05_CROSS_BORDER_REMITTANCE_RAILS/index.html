Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (headed by the Commissioner of Income Tax); corporate tax filings are submitted via Income Tax Office e-forms through the official Gibraltar government portal at www.gibraltar.gov.gi.

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company establishing a Gibraltar branch must: (1) register as an oversea company under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 with the Registrar of Companies, filing certified constitutional documents, the Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, the Bermuda Certificate of Exemption, and a Gibraltar registered-office address; (2) submit the corporate income tax registration application to the Income Tax Office to obtain a Gibraltar Tax File Number (TFN), typically issued within 5-10 business days; (3) appoint a Gibraltar-resident tax agent or authorized representative; (4) register for PAYE and social insurance if employees are engaged; and (5) obtain the requisite license from the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019 before operating the cross-border remittance business. No minimum share capital is required.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST regime (Gibraltar is outside the UK and EU VAT territory); indirect taxation is limited to Gibraltar customs import duty on physical goods, excise duty on specified goods, and stamp duty on instruments. Cross-border remittance services are outside the scope of any Gibraltar indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST registration or turnover threshold exists in Gibraltar. Importers must obtain customs registration with HM Customs Gibraltar; no duty is levied on exported services, and stamp duty does not apply to remittance transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Gibraltar as no VAT/GST is imposed. If the group also maintains a UK VAT-registered branch, financial remittance services are VAT-exempt (VATA 1994, Sch.9, Group 5), not zero-rated; supplies to non-UK recipients may be outside the scope of UK VAT under B2B place-of-supply rules. Gibraltar customs duty relief on physically exported goods requires standard proof of export; exported services require no customs filing.

Gibraltar has no VAT export mechanism, so no VAT clawback arises locally. Penalties primarily attach to: (1) Income Tax Act 2010 - inaccurate returns attract penalties up to 100% of the tax undercharged plus interest, increasing to 200% for deliberate concealment, with criminal exposure for directors; (2) GFSC/AML - fines up to GIP 500,000 per contravention and license revocation; (3) UK VAT - erroneous zero-rating or input VAT recovery by a UK-connected entity triggers HMRC penalties up to 100% of the VAT plus default surcharge; (4) Bermuda - revocation of exempted status permits retrospective fee assessment and loss of the 0% tax guarantee; and (5) failure to repatriate foreign-currency receipts within 180 days flags a joint Income Tax Office/GFSC audit, permitting back-dated assessments of up to 6 years, unlimited for fraudulent conduct.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully and freely convertible in Gibraltar; the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is exchangeable at par with GBP, and there is no foreign-exchange licensing for current operations. Profit remittance from Gibraltar is unrestricted, with no withholding tax on dividends, interest, or royalties paid to non-residents; the practical hurdles are the GFSC fit-and-proper assessment of the payment institution, the requirement to evidence economic substance in Gibraltar, and the obligation to safeguard client funds separately. Bermuda exempted-company status does not confer automatic exchange-control relief in Gibraltar; conversion into GIP/GBP is executed at commercial bank spreads.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for the repatriation of service-export earnings; the remittance agency may retain foreign-currency receivables in a licensed nostro account provided the balances are reflected in the safeguarding ledger and regulatory returns. As a prudential expectation only, the GFSC expects foreign-currency receipts from money-transmission operations to be converted into GIP/GBP within ten business days of receipt where the corresponding payout corridor requires conversion, but this is not a statutory requirement.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the statutory financial-services and licensing authority; Bank of England as the monetary anchor, given the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at par to GBP; HM Treasury and the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) as the sanction and AML/CFT overlay. Gibraltar has no independent central bank or exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High only for UK-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) that have obtained GFSC passporting or notification rights under the post-Brexit UK-Gibraltar framework; Low for non-EEA offshore digital EMIs because Gibraltar's Payment Services Act 2019 and E-Money Regulations 2019 require a locally licensed intermediary or an authorised direct branch, and the GFSC will not accept generic EMI onboarding without a safeguarding account at a GFSC-licensed bank and a locally registered AML/CFT line. A Bermuda-incorporated exempted company cannot use an unlicensed offshore EMI as its sole settlement rail; the EMI operating into Gibraltar would require a Gibraltar registered office and a money-remittance or e-money licence if it services Gibraltar consumers.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder and equity injection from the Bermuda exempted company to the Gibraltar branch requires board resolutions of both entities, statutory filing of the overseas branch with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies on Form OSIR/BR1, and transmission via SWIFT MT103 using UK balance-of-payments purpose code B04 (Direct Investment / Equity). The remittance information field must name the payee as the Gibraltar Branch of the Bermuda Exempted Company; the MT103 must reference the GFSC licence number and the bank must receive pre-notification of the expected credit, certificate of incumbency, local director and ultimate-beneficial-owner KYC pack, and proof of minimum own funds of EUR 20,000 for money-remittance-only payment services or EUR 125,000 for broader payment-acceptance services. Any EUR-denominated injection above EUR 100,000 triggers automatic prudential reporting to the GFSC through the bank.A three-tier clearing architecture is used: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct beneficiary-account credit in major corridor currencies; (2) MT700 commercial letter of credit for documented import settlement where goods or freight documents are required, with non-recourse protections against the remittance licensee; (3) pre-funded foreign-currency nostro accounts at the Gibraltar clearing bank for local cash-pickup agents, topped up via MT202 or MT103 and settled same-day in GIP/GBP. Domestic Gibraltar payments settle over the UK Faster Payments Service via agency arrangements, while time-critical high-value GBP payments clear through CHAPS. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GBP 1,000,000 in aggregate cross-border related-party revenue or expense transactions

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance (Company Taxation) Regulations 2019 apply. A Bermuda Exempted Company carrying on payment transmission through a Gibraltar PE must demonstrate that core income-generating activities (trade finance, clearing, settlement, and AML compliance) are undertaken in Gibraltar by employees with suitable qualifications, supported by tangible premises, and that operational spending is proportionate to profits. Annual filing of an Economic Substance Return (ESR-2024) is mandatory; failure results in financial penalties, compulsion notices, and potential deregistration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Gibraltar Criminal Procedure (Money Laundering) Regulations and Income Tax Act 2010; fines up to 200% of the tax evaded and restitution plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate are personally liable for the unpaid tax via veil-piercing; may be disqualified from directorship for up to 10 years, restricted from leaving Gibraltar, and imprisoned for up to 7 years.
Shell Company / Inadequate Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Confiscatory fine of up to GBP 50,000 per enforcement period and a mandatory order to cease trading in Gibraltar; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission will revoke the remittance licence.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if the board fails to maintain adequate substance; the court can impose a travel ban, seize assets, and order disqualification from holding comparable offices in any British Overseas Territory.
AML and Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines not less than EUR 1,500,000 or 10% of gross annual turnover (whichever is greater) under the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (AML) Regulations; automatic licensing suspension and illegal-money seizure.

Director personal liability: Directors and MLROs face personal criminal exposure, asset freezing orders, and imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering offences; civil liability extends to restitution of laundered amounts plus punitive damages.
