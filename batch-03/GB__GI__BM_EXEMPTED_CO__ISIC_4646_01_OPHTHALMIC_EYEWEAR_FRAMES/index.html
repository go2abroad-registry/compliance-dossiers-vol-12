Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
HM Government of Gibraltar – Customs & Income Tax Department (Income Tax Office, ITO) under the Commissioner of Income Tax; e-filing portal: https://www.tax.gi. Customs processing is performed via the Gibraltar Customs Office and the UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS), as Gibraltar forms part of the UK customs territory.

        
E-government filing portal
1) Appoint a licensed Gibraltar corporate services provider to supply registered office/agent. 2) Register the Bermuda Exempted Company (ELF_CUSTOM_BM_EX) with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies as an External Company (branch) under the Companies Act 2014. 3) Submit Form G1 Application for Registration to the Income Tax Office to obtain the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN). 4) Obtain a GB EORI number through HMRC/CDS for customs purposes (mandatory for any goods movement in the UK/Gibraltar customs territory). 5) Open a Gibraltar EMI/bank account (Swift_TT, Platform_Pay) for permitted transactions while keeping offshore receivables in a non-Gibraltar account to preserve the remittance exemption.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone – The Bermuda Exempted Company is outside Bermuda taxation (exempted status under the Bermuda Companies Act 1981; Bermuda corporate income tax at 15% applies only to MNE groups with consolidated revenue exceeding €750/US$750 million from 1 January 2025). As managed and controlled from Gibraltar, it is within Gibraltar's territorial income tax charge, but the foreign-source wholesale profits from acetate eyeglass frame distribution are chargeable only if remitted to or received in Gibraltar. Unremitted offshore profits therefore benefit from a de facto zero-tax regime. Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Tax_Exempt_Zone – The Bermuda Exempted Company is outside Bermuda taxation (exempted status under the Bermuda Companies Act 1981; Bermuda corporate income tax at 15% applies only to MNE groups with consolidated revenue exceeding €750/US$750 million from 1 January 2025). As managed and controlled from Gibraltar, it is within Gibraltar's territorial income tax charge, but the foreign-source wholesale profits from acetate eyeglass frame distribution are chargeable only if remitted to or received in Gibraltar. Unremitted offshore profits therefore benefit from a de facto zero-tax regime. Any Gibraltar-source profit would be taxed at 10% standard corporate rate (12.5% for prescribed utilities/telecoms; 15% Pillar Two top-up for in-scope MNE groups).

        
Available election forms
Gibraltar applies no US-style check-the-box election. Principal statutory mechanisms: (i) participation exemption election for qualifying dividends and capital gains on shareholdings (elected annually in the corporate tax return); (ii) territorial/remittance basis election for foreign-source profits under the Income Tax Act 2010; (iii) OECD/EU hybrid mismatch rules (ATAD2) operate compulsorily – no unilateral hybrid election exists; an inclusion/non-deduction adjustment is mandatory for hybrid entity/instrument arrangements; (iv) the Bermuda Economic Substance (Companies) regime requires an annual economic substance statement, effectively an 'elective filing' confirming no qualifying activity is carried on in Bermuda.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
Gibraltar has no VAT or GST; the indirect tax system is the Import Duty regime under the Customs & Excise Ordinance, with Gibraltar comprising part of the UK customs territory for import/export declarations.

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
N/A – no VAT/GST registration threshold applies in Gibraltar (GIP/GBP). Import Duty is payable immediately on specified goods only (alcohol, tobacco, fuel, vehicles and certain luxury items); acetate eyeglass frames (HS 9003.19 / ISIC 4646) attract 0% duty. A GB EORI number is mandatory from the first customs declaration; there is no de minimis economic threshold for customs registration.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
No VAT zero-rating exists in Gibraltar because no VAT is levied. Instead, direct exports of acetate eyeglass frames by the Bermuda Exempted Company from the third-country manufacturer to the foreign end-customer are wholly outside Gibraltar's indirect tax net. To sustain tax-exempt-zone income treatment, the taxpayer must show that (i) sale negotiations and execution occurred outside Gibraltar, (ii) title and risk passed outside Gibraltar, and (iii) proceeds were never remitted to or received in Gibraltar. Documentary evidence of these elements constitutes the 'substantiation bundle' for both income tax and customs purposes.

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal e-invoicing, digital stamping or tax-authority API clearance. Mandatory electronic data exchange applies at the border via the UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS), ICS2 Entry Summary Declarations for EU-bound consignments, and the Goods Movement Reference (GMR) system for transit movements. Invoices, contracts and customs declarations must be retained in machine-readable format for at least 6 years and produced to the Income Tax Office or HMRC on inspection.

      
The Commissioner may raise assessments within 6 years of the return (unlimited in fraud/wilful default). Review focuses on central management and control in Gibraltar, source of the trading profits and the remittance test. If proceeds are remitted to a Gibraltar account, the previously exempt foreign-source profits become chargeable at 10%/12.5% with interest. Civil penalties for inaccurate returns reach up to 100% of the undercharged tax (increased to 150% where deliberate and concealed), with criminal prosecution and unlimited fines/imprisonment for fraudulent evasion. Under the Customs & Excise Ordinance, false export documentation triggers forfeiture of goods and penalty up to three times the value of the goods. The General Anti-Abuse Rule permits the income tax authorities to disregard artificial structures designed to avoid the remittance charge and claw back tax for up to 6 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Gibraltar Pound (GIP) is pegged at 1:1 to GBP within the UK currency area. Full current-account and capital-account convertibility applies without licensing or prior approval. Profit remittances by a Bermuda Exempted Company are not subject to FX controls or repatriation taxes; FATCA/CRS self-certification, audited accounts evidencing distributable reserves, and board resolutions are the operative documentary requirements. Capital inflows and outflows are de-restricted, although GFSC-regulated banks apply enhanced due diligence for offshore vehicles.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
None applicable. Neither the UK nor Gibraltar imposes statutory surrender timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation quotas on export receipts. EUR/USD receivables from the wholesale distribution of ophthalmic eyewear frames may be retained in foreign currency, netted, or converted at freely negotiated market rates.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for GBP and Gibraltar Pound parity) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the prudential regulator for Gibraltar-licensed banks; Gibraltar has no independent central bank and no discretionary exchange-control authority.

Gibraltar: as the jurisdiction of management and control, audit is mandatory for tax-resident companies unless small-company exemption applies (two of three: turnover <= GBP 10.2M, balance sheet <= GBP 5.1M, average employees <= 50) or micro-company exemption applies (turnover <= GBP 632,000, balance sheet <= GBP 316,000, employees <= 10); audit exemption is unavailable to public companies, credit institutions and regulated financial entities, and may be overridden by members holding at least 10% of shares.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar Local File is mandatory when the aggregate value of controlled transactions (goods, services, intangibles and interest) exceeds GIP 1,000,000 in a chargeable period; Master File is mandatory where group consolidated turnover exceeds GIP 50,000,000. Bermuda ESR uses a separate economic substance filing threshold, but does not set an equivalent GIP-denominated Local File threshold.

      
Economic substance scope: Bermuda's Economic Substance Regulations 2018 apply to every Bermuda exempted company carrying on a relevant activity, including wholesale distribution and service-centre activities. The company must demonstrate adequate physical presence, full board management in Bermuda, senior employees on island, and operating expenditure proportionate to the activity. Gibraltar applies its own economic substance code for Gibraltar tax-resident companies engaged in geographically mobile activities; a Bermuda exempted company cannot use Gibraltar activity to satisfy Bermuda ESR requirements, and vice versa.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and False Tax Return
        
Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Income Tax Act sanctions include, on summary conviction, a fine up to GIP 5,000 and imprisonment up to 6 months; on indictment, an unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 10 years. Civil assessments may recover underpaid tax plus an evasion penalty of 100% of the tax due.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause false returns to be submitted are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and interest; the court may issue travel bans, worldwide asset freezing orders, and disqualification from acting as a director for up to 15 years.

      

      
      

        Shell Company and Economic Substance Circumvention
        
Statutory sanction: Bermuda ESR penalties escalate from BMD 10,000 for first-year non-compliance to BMD 10,000 plus an additional BMD 100,000 for continued default, culminating in Registrar strike-off. Gibraltar may treat the company as tax resident and assess 10% tax on worldwide or Gibraltar-source profits, with daily filing penalties of GIP 10 and civil penalties up to 30% of unpaid tax.

        
Director personal liability: Directors may have the corporate veil pierced for abusing limited liability, leading to personal liability for unpaid taxes, disqualification under the Gibraltar Companies Act, global asset freezes and travel bans pending the final tax assessment.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure
        
Statutory sanction: Gibraltar may adjust taxable profits to the arm's length result and levy penalties of 35% of any additional tax for careless errors and 70% for deliberate errors. Failure to produce documentation attracts a GIP 50,000 fixed penalty plus GIP 1,000 per day of continued default, with possible simultaneous penalties in the counterpart jurisdiction.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly endorse artificially low transfer prices may be jointly liable for the tax deficiency; professional disciplinary bodies may recommend disqualification and personal restitution orders for negligently misleading financial statements.

      

      
      

        Anti-Money Laundering and Unlawful Capital Repatriation
        
Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 provides, on conviction on indictment, an unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 14 years. The Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit may impose regulatory penalties up to 10% of annual turnover or GIP 250,000; asset confiscation orders and banking transaction bans may also be made.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are personally criminally liable for laundering proceeds, failing to report suspicious activity, and tipping-off. Courts may make confiscation and compensation orders against directors personally, impose international travel bans, and disqualify them from holding any governance role in a regulated financial institution for up to 10 years.
