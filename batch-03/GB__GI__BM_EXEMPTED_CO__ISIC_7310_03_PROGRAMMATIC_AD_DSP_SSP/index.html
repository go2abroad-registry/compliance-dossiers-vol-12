Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office (ITO), HM Government of Gibraltar, Portland House, Glacis Road, Gibraltar. Official government tax portal: GOV.GI / Income Tax Office e-Tax Services (https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/tax).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Bermuda Exempted Company as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 2014 by filing certified constitutional documents, a registered-office address in Gibraltar, and the prescribed foreign-company registration form. Step 2: Obtain the Gibraltar Companies Registry number. Step 3: File the ITO Taxpayer Registration Form with the Income Tax Office within 30 days of company registration; no tax clearance certificate is required for foreign-company registration. Step 4: The ITO issues a Gibraltar TIN (T-number) which is the statutory taxpayer reference for income tax and social-competence purposes. Step 5: Upon filing the first annual return, the company receives either an assessed tax position or a nil/out-of-scope determination confirming non-residence and absence of Gibraltar-source chargeability.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - a Bermuda Exempted Company registered in Gibraltar solely as an overseas company is not tax resident in Gibraltar unless central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar. Under Gibraltar's territorial Income Tax Act 2010, profits not accruing in or derived from Gibraltar are outside the charge to Gibraltar corporate tax; in practice the entity has zero effective Gibraltar tax liability when all management and ad-exchange revenue generation occurs outside Gibraltar.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Gibraltar law. Gibraltar applies OECD BEPS-aligned territorial rules automatically: a foreign company may file a non-residence notification and submit evidence of foreign management and control; the Commissioner may issue a formal Determination of Non-Residence or Certificate of Tax Residence for banking and EMI onboarding. No entity-classification election is available other than the standard corporate status recognized under the Companies Act 2014.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

General Consumption Tax (GST) - enacted under the General Consumption Tax Act 2015 but implementation repeatedly deferred; currently not in force. Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area, imposes no VAT, and has no VAT registration category.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold currently applies because GST is not in force. If and when the General Consumption Tax Act 2015 becomes operational, compulsory registration will apply to businesses whose annual taxable turnover exceeds GBP 100,000 (Gibraltar pounds, pegged 1:1 to GBP).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

With GST not yet in force, there is no export charge and no refund mechanism. When GST becomes effective, goods physically exported from Gibraltar will be zero-rated upon presence of export evidence; services supplied to non-Gibraltar B2B recipients with place of supply outside Gibraltar, including programmatic DSP/SSP ad inventory fees and ad-exchange commissions, will be outside the scope of Gibraltar GST and require no zero-rating substantiation. If the entity is voluntarily UK-VAT-registered through a UK establishment, B2B e-advertising supplies to non-UK customers are outside the scope of UK VAT under place-of-supply rules, with inbound purchases from non-resident suppliers subject to UK reverse charge; geo-validation evidence (IP address, device ID, contract counterparty location) must be retained to support the place-of-supply position.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (filed through HM Customs & Excise using the UK CHIEF/CDS-equivalent procedure under the UK-Gibraltar customs arrangements, where goods movement is involved)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export from Gibraltar (goods transactions only)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice demonstrating receipt of export proceeds in the designated banking channel, alongside a commercial invoice or service contract

No electronic-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping mandate currently applies in Gibraltar. Gibraltar operates no tax-authority invoice-clearance platform; commercial invoicing follows ordinary accounting-law requirements under the Companies Act 2014 and the GST Act's deferred regulations.

Under the Income Tax Act 2010, false or fraudulent declarations attract additional assessments up to 100% of the tax undercharged plus interest, and criminal penalties for tax evasion. Gibraltar customs legislation permits seizure of goods and penalties of up to three times the goods' value for false export-zero-rating claims; if GST becomes effective, clawback provisions will mirror UK VAT jurisprudence on fraudulent zero-rating and missing-trader-type abuse. HMRC and Gibraltar maintain reciprocal information-exchange and mutual-assistance powers, and any export-refund fraud is treated as a serious money-laundering predicate crime under the Proceeds of Crime Act (Gibraltar), exposing directors to civil recovery proceedings and debarment from financial-sector EMI/bank relationships.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar has a fully liberalised currency and capital account. The Gibraltar pound is unconditionally convertible into UK sterling at par without any approvals, surrender requirements or profit-remittance restrictions. Both current account and capital transactions — including outbound dividends, loan repayments, royalties and shareholder distributions — may be freely executed without exchange-control authorization. The only applicable controls are prudential AML/CTF record-keeping and tax transparency reporting, which do not restrict convertibility or repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules or forced conversion timelines exist. Exporters and service suppliers, including licensed and unlicensed service businesses, may keep proceeds in any foreign currency for an unlimited period at any GFSC-licensed bank. However, treasury policy should retain underlying invoices, platform settlement reports, SWIFT confirmations and reconciliation files for at least six years to satisfy Gibraltar's AML/CTF and tax transparency obligations.

Central bank supervision: Government of Gibraltar / Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the sole banking regulator; currency issuance is delegated to the Currency Commissioner of the Government of Gibraltar, operating under a sterling-linked currency board system. There is no independent central bank; monetary anchor is the Bank of England via the 1:1 Gibraltar Pound (GIP) to Pound Sterling (GBP) peg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Gibraltar prohibits unlicensed electronic-money institutions (EMIs) from operating or providing payment services within its jurisdiction. An offshore digital EMI (e.g., non-GFSC/EEA-licensed Wise, Revolut, or Airwallex) cannot hold GIP-denominated ledgers or settle through the Gibraltar clearing system. A fully GFSC-licensed EMI, or an EEA EMI with passporting rights into Gibraltar, can be used only as an auxiliary liquidity rail; direct bank account access remains the preferred and legally safest route for escrow, payroll and publisher settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inward wires are executed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for cover payments) from the parent/beneficial owner's bank to the Gibraltar account, with the ISO 20022 purpose code 'TODA' (capital injection/other) or 'CI10' included in the remittance information fields. The sending bank must transmit unambiguously the originator name and address together with the beneficiary name and Gibraltar-based account IBAN to satisfy FATF Recommendation 16. Before credit, the receiving Gibraltar bank will require a board resolution approving the capital injection, a source-of-funds statement, and a certificate of incumbency confirming the signatories. To avoid GIP settlement friction, route via a UK clearing bank correspondent (e.g., Bank of England Settlement Account) or use a segregated sterling 'suspense' account maintained at the receiving bank.Operational settlements to publishers, data providers and technology vendors are cleared through SWIFT MT103 over the CHAPS, SEPA or Fedwire network, with each disbursement bearing the appropriate ISO 20022 purpose code (e.g., 'TECH' for technology services, 'SUPP' for supplier payments, 'ROYL' for royalty/license streams). For unusually large programmatic-ad commitments (typically above GBP 1 million per payment), treasury should consider a commercial letter of credit or standby letter of credit (SBLC) issued by a GFSC-licensed bank, which substitutes bank credit for platform counterparty risk and creates a cleared payment instruction against presentation of verified invoice and traffic-delivery attestation. Turnover: Mandatory independent audit applies where the entity is regulated by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission, is listed on a recognised stock exchange, or fails the Gibraltar small-company audit exemption. Exemption is available on meeting two of three thresholds: turnover not exceeding GIP 10.2 million, balance-sheet total not exceeding GIP 5.1 million, and no more than 50 average employees. A Bermuda Exempted Company may otherwise be audit-exempted by unanimous shareholder resolution and is not required to file audited financial statements publicly in Bermuda.
