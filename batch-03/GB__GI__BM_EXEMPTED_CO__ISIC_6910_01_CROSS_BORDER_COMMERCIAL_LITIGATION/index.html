Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi, Income Tax / e-Tax section). Supporting authorities: Companies House Gibraltar (overseas branch registration) and HM Government of Gibraltar Customs & Excise Department (customs/excise). UK HMRC has no jurisdiction in Gibraltar; no VAT authority exists because Gibraltar is outside the UK VAT territory and applies no VAT.

E-government filing portal

(1) Register the Bermuda Exempted Company as an overseas company with Companies House Gibraltar under the Companies Act 2014 (Cap. 47) within one month of establishing a place of business, filing apostilled Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation and Certificate of Good Standing, constitutional documents, Gibraltar registered-office details, and resident agent acceptance. (2) Submit the Companies House certificate with Form TR-E (External Company Tax Registration) to the Income Tax Office through a Gibraltar-based tax representative; the Commissioner issues a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) within 3-5 working days. No VAT ID is issued or required as Gibraltar has no VAT/GST. (3) Register for PAYE and Gibraltar Social Security if the branch employs local staff. (4) Obtain a customs importer/exporter code only when physical goods are moved; pure cross-border litigation and legal services do not trigger customs registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: the Bermuda Exempted Company is statutorily exempt from Bermuda income, capital gains, and withholding taxes. In Gibraltar it is taxed only on its territorial Gibraltar-source branch profits at the flat 12.5% corporate rate; foreign-source income, passive dividends, capital gains, and outbound interest/royalty/dividend payments remain outside Gibraltar taxation. No worldwide income taxation applies.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Relevant elections under the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended): (a) group-relief loss-surrender elections among 75% group companies; (b) capital-allowance pooling elections for plant and machinery; (c) election for the Gibraltar branch to be treated as a separate establishment with arm's-length allocation of Gibraltar-source profit; (d) formal declaration of central management and control (residence determination) to avoid dual-residency and hybrid mismatch treatment under Gibraltar's OECD-compliant hybrid mismatch rules; and (e) elections to apply the territorial source rules to bookkeeping/turnover allocation methodology where a mixed source of fee income exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales/use tax in Gibraltar. Indirect taxation is confined to customs import duties and excise duties on alcohol, tobacco, and fuel under the Customs Ordinance (Cap. 57), plus Stamp Duty on specified instruments. Gibraltar is outside the UK VAT territory and the EU VAT area; no UK VAT reverse-charge or EU cross-border VAT compliance applies to the Gibraltar establishment. Cross-border commercial litigation, arbitration, and asset-tracing services therefore attract zero indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable: no VAT/GST regime exists and no turnover threshold triggers indirect tax registration. Customs importer/exporter registration is required from the first physical importation (nil de minimis). Stamp duty is instrument-specific. For legal/litigation services, no indirect-tax registration is triggered at any turnover level; corporate tax registration instead attaches from commencement of the Gibraltar branch.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable: export 'zero-rating' is a VAT mechanism and Gibraltar levies no VAT. Cross-border legal services (multi-jurisdictional arbitration, commercial litigation, and asset tracing) supplied from a Gibraltar establishment, whether to UK, EU, or offshore clients, are outside the scope of any Gibraltar indirect tax; no registration, zero-rating claim, or refund mechanism is required. Where physical goods transit Gibraltar, customs import duty is chargeable only on entry for local consumption; re-exported or temporarily admitted goods benefit from duty relief subject to Customs Ordinance conditions.

  • Customs export/import declaration (electronic SAD-equivalent filed via licensed Gibraltar customs agents; required only for physical goods movements)
  • Commercial invoice (itemized fee note for litigation services or consignment invoice for goods)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of goods
  • Certificate of Origin (required only where preferential tariff treatment is claimed)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing foreign-currency receipt (retained as audit evidence of cross-border fee inflows supporting the 180-day FX repatriation policy)

Gibraltar imposes no real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance API, or digital stamping mandate. Invoices must contain full statutory particulars and be retained for six years under the Income Tax Act 2010. Goods declarations are filed electronically via the Gibraltar Customs electronic declaration platform through licensed customs agents under the UK-Gibraltar customs co-operation framework; services invoices require no pre-clearance or government approval.

Fraudulent evasion of customs duty and false export declarations are criminal offences under the Customs Ordinance (Cap. 57), attracting forfeiture of goods, monetary penalties up to treble the evaded duty, and imprisonment up to seven years. On the tax side, erroneous claims of territorial exemption, non-residence, or hybrid tax treatment are retrospectively reopened by the Commissioner for up to six years (twenty years where fraud or negligent misstatement is demonstrated), with statutory interest and penalties up to 100% of the under-assessed tax; directors of the Bermuda Exempted Company may incur personal liability for knowingly false returns. Failure to substantiate the 180-day FX repatriation of professional fee receipts can trigger recharacterisation of turnover, denial of territorial exclusions, and withdrawal of the branch's tax clearances.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility; Gibraltar maintains no exchange-control legislation, no prior-approval regime for capital movements, and no restriction on profit remittance. The GIP circulates at parity with GBP with full sterling backing. A Bermuda Exempted Company may repatriate fees and settlement proceeds to Bermuda or onward to ultimate beneficial owners free of withholding taxes (Gibraltar levies no tax on dividends, interest, royalties, or capital gains for exempt companies). Remittance friction arises only where a counterparty is OFSI/UN/EU sanctioned, requiring a GFSC/OFSI licence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation requirement for export proceeds. Legal-services export receipts under ISIC 6910 may be retained indefinitely in GBP, EUR, USD, or other permitted currencies without mandatory conversion into GIP.

Central bank supervision: Government of Gibraltar (Currency Notes Act 1934) as issuer of the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) at fixed 1:1 parity with the Pound Sterling; the Bank of England as the anchor sterling monetary authority and ultimate liquidity backstop via the UK clearing system; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the statutory banking, payments, and FX regulator under the Financial Services Act 2019 (Gibraltar); HM Treasury's Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) for sanctions-nexus FX controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material statutory limitation. UK-authorised EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex) can on-board a Gibraltar-registered entity, but post-Brexit they cannot passport payment services into Gibraltar without a GFSC-authorised branch or a Gibraltar payment-institution licence under the Financial Services Act 2019; GFSC-licensed EMIs are scarce and require a genuine local link (registered office, local agent, AML/CFT officer). Critically, IOLTA/client-money trust balances cannot legally reside at an unlicensed EMI; statutory trust protection requires a GFSC-regulated credit institution. EMIs are therefore viable only for operational petty cash and non-client fee flows, never as the primary treasury clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 via London correspondent clearing (CHAPS). For a Bermuda Exempted Company operating from Gibraltar, inbound remittances must carry an appropriate ISO 20022 external-purpose code: 'COMC' (Commercial Payment) with the client-matter reference in field 70 for fee and settlement inflows, or 'ECAP' (Equity Capital) supported by a board resolution and share-allotment ledger for capital injections. The beneficiary account must be pre-designated as either the IOLTA client-money trust account or the operational account. Verification steps: (1) OFSI/UN sanction-screening of the remitting bank and remitter; (2) SWIFT/IBAN validation; (3) pre-notification to the relationship manager for any deposit exceeding GBP 50,000; (4) retention of the source-of-funds declaration for litigation-financing inflows. GIP/GBP parity renders UK-sourced inbound flows FX-neutral.Clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for routine litigation disbursements - expert witness fees, court fees, settlement payments, and client refunds; (2) SWIFT MT700 Commercial Letters of Credit for escrowed settlement deposits and litigation-funding guarantees; (3) CHAPS for same-day GBP and SEPA Instant for EUR payments to EU counterparties. Disbursements exceeding GBP 10,000 require dual signature (managing partner and finance director) plus, where applicable, a court order or settlement agreement copy. IOLTA refunds must be effected as clean MT103 with reference 'CLIENT REFUND - [matter no.]' and never commingled with fee transfers. FX conversions for EUR/USD disbursements execute at market rate through the bank's dealing desk; no exchange-control approval is required.Outright deliverable FX forwards (tenor up to 24 months) in EUR and USD against GIP/GBP, transacted under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a GFSC-licensed or London-based investment bank.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in emerging-market currencies for litigation recoveries subject to local exchange controls, cash-settled in GBP or EUR.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) for arbitration awards or settlement receivables exceeding GBP 5 million, converting USD/EUR future inflows into GBP at a fixed rate across the claim lifecycle.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed within nine months after the end of the accounting period; for a company using a 31 December accounting period, the return is due no later than 30 September in the following calendar year. Where the accounting period is aligned with the Gibraltar year of assessment (1 July to 30 June), the filing due date is the following 31 March.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GBP 1,000,000 in aggregate annual controlled cross-border transactions; a separately prepared Local File is expected when the Gibraltar entity’s controlled operating revenue exceeds GBP 1,000,000, and a Master File is expected when the worldwide group turnover exceeds GBP 50,000,000. Even below these thresholds, all intercompany pricing must be demonstrably at arm’s length and evidence must be held contemporaneously.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations apply directly to a Bermuda Exempted Company that is tax resident in Gibraltar and that carries on relevant activities: banking, insurance, shipping, investment fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, IP holding, distribution and service centres, and pure equity holding. A cross-border commercial litigation chamber engaged in arbitration and asset tracing will not automatically fall within a relevant activity, but if it also provides management, administration, or distribution services to affiliates it must satisfy the core-income generation test in Gibraltar. The company must convene an adequate board of directors in Gibraltar, maintain records and premises proportionate to its activity, file an annual Economic Substance Return, and ensure that strategic decisions take place physically in Gibraltar. Failure to do so will render the entity a non-substantive shell and expose it to severe penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty equal to 100% of the tax understated; a fine on conviction up to GBP 25,000; and imprisonment for up to 2 years for officers found guilty on indictment. The tax itself remains payable with late-payment interest from the original due date.

Director personal liability: The Gibraltar courts may pierce the corporate veil where the exempted company is found to be a facade for tax fraud. Directors may be ordered personally to make restitution to the tax authority, face travel bans preventing departure from Gibraltar or the United Kingdom, be disqualified from acting as directors for up to 15 years, and on serious fraud an officer may be extradited under the UK-Gibraltar criminal law framework.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Economic Substance Regulations start at GBP 100,000 per relevant activity and increase for continued non-compliance. The Commissioner may apply to the Supreme Court for a daily default penalty of GBP 5,000. The company also risks having its Gibraltar tax residence status voided, its director disqualification, and its registration struck off for defect.

Director personal liability: Each director is personally liable for fines and penalties arising from a continuing serious default if they were aware of or participated in the substance failure. Directors may be made personally liable for the company’s additional tax charges, prohibited from using UK/Gibraltar banking facilities through flagging as a high-risk shell, and issued with financial restriction or travel restraint orders to contribute to the outstanding tax and penalty liabilities.
Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act and Crime (Money Laundering) Act, conviction on indictment for money laundering carries a maximum penalty of 14 years’ imprisonment and an unlimited fine. Licensing authorities, including the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission and the Gibraltar Bar Council, may withdraw the statutory license required under ISIC 6910, impose regulatory fines, and confiscate the laundered assets.

Director personal liability: Directors, partners, and the designated Money Laundering Reporting Officer are subject to personal criminal prosecution. They may be arrested, remanded in custody, served with a confiscation order upon civil recovery proceedings, banned from leaving Gibraltar, and prohibited from continuing legal practice. Even without conviction, directors may face civil liability for breach of fiduciary duty and restitution to the trust account beneficiaries.
