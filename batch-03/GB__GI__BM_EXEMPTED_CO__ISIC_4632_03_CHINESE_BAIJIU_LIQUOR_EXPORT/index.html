A Bermuda Exempted Company establishing a Gibraltar presence must: (1) incorporate under Bermuda's Companies Act 1981 and obtain its Tax Exemption Certificate (no Bermuda TIN is issued because Bermuda imposes no income tax); (2) within one month of establishing a place of business in Gibraltar, register as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Companies House under the Companies Act 2014; (3) submit the company tax registration application to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office to obtain the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number, declaring the seat of central management and control; (4) file the relevant self-assessment or non-residence notice to document the absence of a Gibraltar permanent establishment where non-resident treatment is claimed; (5) register in the Chrysalis customs system for import/export activity and apply for the Alcoholic Liquor Import/Export Licence under the Alcoholic Liquor Duties Ordinance; (6) if hiring Gibraltar staff, register as an employer for Gibraltar payroll withholding.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda Exempted Company protected by a Tax Exemption Certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, guaranteeing no Bermuda income, capital gains, or dividend taxes until 2035, with extension possible to 2050). Gibraltar limb: the company is treated as a non-resident foreign company taxed on the territorial basis (Gibraltar-source income only) at the flat 10% entity-level corporate rate; if central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, it becomes Gibraltar-resident and is taxed on worldwide profits at the same 10% rate, with foreign tax credit relief available.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no US-style check-the-box election; however, the following statutory elections are available: (i) Bermudian Tax Exemption Certificate election under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, locking in zero Bermuda taxation until 2035 (extendable to 2050); (ii) Gibraltar Conduit Company election under the Companies (Taxation of Conduit Companies) Act 2011, exempting qualifying dividends, interest, royalties, and capital gains derived from non-Gibraltar group companies; (iii) Gibraltar group-relief election under the Income Tax Act 2010 to surrender losses among 75%-owned group companies; (iv) adoption of the OECD authorised OECD approach (AOA) for attributing profits to a Gibraltar branch/permanent establishment of the Bermuda company; and (v) excise warehouse-keepers election to hold Chinese baijiu under duty suspension pending re-export. UK/Gibraltar anti-hybrid rules follow OECD BEPS Action 2 and may deny deductions in the absence of a valid corresponding election by the Bermuda entity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both current and capital account items are freely convertible. Gibraltar applies no exchange controls, no blocked capital accounts, and no prior approval for dividend or profit remittance by a Bermuda Exempted Company. The main substantive hurdle is compliance with the Gibraltar Economic Substance Act and demonstrable management and control in Gibraltar for any passive income or treasury function.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Exporters are not required to surrender export proceeds, convert them within a specified timeline, or hold any proportion in local currency. Proceeds may be retained in any freely convertible currency.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) for banking and AML supervision; currency issuance under the Government of Gibraltar Currency Notes Act (Gibraltar pound pegged 1:1 to sterling).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. EEA/UK-licensed digital EMIs may provide limited GBP/EUR/USD payment processing and virtual IBANs for a Bermuda Exempted Company, but they cannot issue commercial letters of credit, act as presenting banks for documentary collections, or support high-value CNY/CNH clearing without a Gibraltar payment services licence. EMIs remain viable only for low-value sales and as contingency rails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when Gibraltar related-party controlled transactions exceed GBP 500,000 in a fiscal year; Master File is mandatory when the consolidated worldwide group revenue exceeds GBP 10,000,000 and the Gibraltar entity engages in cross-border related-party transactions exceeding GBP 5,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Both Bermuda Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 2018 and Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations apply to distribution and service centre businesses. The baijiu export wholesale activity is a relevant activity: the company must demonstrate premises, qualified employees, annual expenditure, and core income-generating activities in Bermuda or Gibraltar. If substance is absent, the authority may deem onshore profits equal to 20% of operating costs, disallow treaty-like benefits, and impose daily penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Underdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 200% of the understated tax, interest at 1.5% per month, a fixed monetary penalty of GBP 50,000 per false return, and imprisonment for up to seven years under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act and criminal tax fraud provisions.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where signed returns are knowingly false: directors become personally liable for the company's unpaid tax on a civil restitution basis, may be disqualified for ten years, and face travel bans, asset freezes, and custodial sentences of up to seven years.
Sham / Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative fine up to GBP 100,000 for inadequate substance, daily penalty of GBP 10,000 for continued non-compliance, mandatory strike-off, and re-characterisation of profits as Gibraltar-source taxable at 12.5% with a 50% penalty gross-up.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally assessed for the substance shortfall; civil liability extends to the amount of the penalty, disqualification from company management for up to ten years, and a Gibraltar court order may impose an international travel ban pending verification of substance.
AML and Trade Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, fines up to GBP 500,000 and imprisonment up to fourteen years per count; the Financial Intelligence Unit may impose civil penalties up to GBP 250,000 plus asset confiscation orders.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to file Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) are criminally liable; travel bans are automatic during financial crime investigations, personal bank accounts can be frozen, and personal confiscation orders may be issued against illicit enrichment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may adjust the consideration to arm's length, assess additional tax at 12.5% plus interest at 1.5% per month, impose a 100% penalty on the underpayment, and levy a fixed GBP 50,000 penalty for each missing documentation file.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate transfer-pricing schedules bear joint and several liability for the lost tax; abusive manipulation of the arm's length standard can be prosecuted as tax evasion, resulting in imprisonment and a five-year travel ban pending the conclusion of criminal proceedings.
