Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar — Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), with the Gibraltar Companies Registry (operating under the Companies Act 2014) responsible for overseas-company branch registration of the Bermuda Exempted Company; HM Customs, Gibraltar, is the competent authority for import, export and all indirect-tax/customs controls, operating the ASYCUDA World electronic customs processing system; income tax returns and e-payments are filed through the Gibraltar Income Tax Online portal; HMRC of the United Kingdom is a separate counterparty relevant only if the entity later incurs UK VAT or UK withholding obligations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Gibraltar branch registration: file with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies a certified copy of the Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, current Certificate of Good Standing issued by the Bermuda Registrar, the Bermuda Memorandum of Association and Bye-Laws, particulars of directors, secretary and shareholders, a Gibraltar registered office and a locally resident licensed agent, together with the prescribed overseas-company branch application (Form FC-1 equivalent under Part XXII, Companies Act 2014); obtain the Gibraltar Companies Registry number and branch registration certificate. Step 2 — Tax ID application: within 30 days of commencing the taxable activity (manufacture and sale of X-ray flat-panel detectors), submit the statutory corporate registration/notification form prescribed by the Commissioner of Income Tax, attaching the branch certificate, constitutional documents, and the CRS/FATCA due-diligence pack for all ultimate beneficial owners; the Income Tax Office will then issue the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (GI-prefixed reference) and open the corporation-tax file for Gibraltar-source profits. Step 3 — Customs registration: obtain an Economic Operator Registration and Identification code (EORI-GI or equivalent importer/exporter reference) from HM Customs Gibraltar and enrol in ASYCUDA World before the first physical shipment. Step 4 — Economic Substance: complete the Gibraltar annual Economic Substance notification; although pure manufacturing may fall outside the listed relevant activities, the OEM's distribution and service-centre element must be assessed and evidenced with substance in Gibraltar. Step 5 — UK VAT registration (optional): if finished goods are stocked in or sold from the United Kingdom, apply separately to HMRC for a UK VAT number; this step is independent of the Gibraltar tax identity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda) / Territorial_Scope (Gibraltar). The Bermuda Exempted Company is tax-exempt in Bermuda (no corporate income tax; annual fee only), is a separate limited-liability legal person, is not a pass-through, and is permitted to be owned by non-residents. For Gibraltar Stage 3 purposes, the branch default classification is Territorial_Scope: the entity is chargeable to Gibraltar corporation tax at 12.5% only on profits 'accruing in or derived from Gibraltar' under the Income Tax Act 2010. Profits of the offshore O.E.M. manufacturing and export trade that are economically attributable to Bermuda or other non-Gibraltar functions and are supported by arm's-length transfer pricing remain outside the Gibraltar tax net; central management and control exercised in Gibraltar will make the branch resident for filing purposes but will not enlarge the tax base beyond Gibraltar territorial source.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a United States-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections and defensive filings for this structure include: (1) group relief election under sections 53–55, Income Tax Act 2010, to surrender losses between 75% UK/Gibraltar group companies; (2) transfer-pricing documentation election adopting an OECD-aligned profit split between the Bermuda head office and the Gibraltar branch; (3) capital-allowance elections on the vapour-deposition manufacturing equipment and industrial-building allowance on the Gibraltar production unit; (4) economic-substance conclusive-evidence election through appointment of a Gibraltar-resident director, physical premises, and meeting minutes evidencing management in Gibraltar; (5) hybrid-mismatch alignment statements under UK TIOPA 2010 Part 6A and corresponding Gibraltar anti-hybrid provisions, such that the Bermuda 0% layer and the Gibraltar 12.5% layer do not create a deduction/non-inclusion mismatch; and (6) residency disclaimer election where central management and control remains in Bermuda, displacing UK CFC and diverted-profits branch-mismatch challenges. No new 'exempt company' election exists for companies incorporated after the abolition of the Gibraltar exempt-company regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Customs Tariff and Excise Duties regime — a VAT/GST-free consumption system. Gibraltar has never imposed VAT, GST, or a general retail sales tax. Indirect taxation is limited to Import Duties charged at first entry of physical goods under the consolidated Gibraltar customs tariff, Excise Duties on alcohol, tobacco, and hydrocarbon oils, and a small number of local duties. The ISIC 3250 digital X-ray flat-panel detector line (CN/HS 9022.14) currently falls under a nil-duty tariff line, so the indirect-tax burden for the Bermuda Exempted Company's Gibraltar export trade is effectively zero.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in Gibraltar for VAT/GST because no such tax exists; therefore there is no economic-turnover threshold in Gibraltar pounds (GIP/GBP) triggering registration. Corporation-tax registration is required from the commencement of Gibraltar-source activity without any minimum turnover, and the customs EORI/importer-exporter registration is mandatory from the first commercial consignment with no de minimis threshold. For comparison, if the entity later holds inventory in the United Kingdom and registers for UK VAT, the HMRC registration threshold is GBP 90,000 of taxable supplies in a rolling 12-month period, but that threshold has no relevance to Gibraltar-source operations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating operates through two routes. Route 1 — Gibraltar: because Gibraltar has no VAT, finished X-ray flat-panel detectors exported from Gibraltar are outside any indirect tax charge, and Import Duty paid on components or capital goods can be refunded or suspended when those goods are exported from Gibraltar within the statutory re-export period under customs supervision. Route 2 — United Kingdom: if the Bermuda Exempted Company or its UK affiliate invoices the detectors from the UK, the supply is zero-rated at 0% under section 30(6) Value Added Tax Act 1994 and the VAT Regulations 1995 where: (a) the goods physically leave the UK within three months of the supply; (b) the supplier holds at least two independent export proofs (official customs evidence, authenticated international transport documents, or a combined commercial-evidence pack); and (c) the customer is located outside the UK. Offshore services supporting the hardware (R&D and IP licences) qualify for UK zero-rating only if supplied to a non-UK business customer and if they fall within Schedule 5 VATA 1994; services rendered between Bermuda and Gibraltar remain outside both the Gibraltar and the UK VAT nets, so no VAT refund mechanism is required in the zero-rate state.

  • Customs Export Declaration — Gibraltar ASYCUDA World electronic export declaration (SAD/Form C88 equivalent), or the UK Customs Export Declaration (CHIEF/CDS) where routed through the UK, stating CN code 9022.14 and the ISIC 3250 detector description
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / combined transport document) evidencing the physical export movement from Gibraltar or the UK and the final port of discharge
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank/FX evidence showing that export proceeds have been repatriated through a Gibraltar-regulated bank within 180 days of the shipment date (supported by the LC/SWIFT MT700 record where payment is by letter of credit)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List — itemising the X-ray flat-panel detectors, unit prices, currency, Incoterms, CE marking, and batch/serial references
  • Certificate of Origin or Preferential Origin Declaration — required where tariff preference is claimed at importation in the buyer's jurisdiction under a UK/EU trade arrangement
  • ISO 13485 / CE technical-file reference sheet — substantiating medical-device classification and the statutory licence required for the export

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate applies in Gibraltar because no VAT/GST is collected. HM Customs Gibraltar requires electronic pre-arrival and pre-departure declaration through ASYCUDA World for every import and export consignment, but invoices themselves are not submitted to any tax-authority clearance platform. If the company later becomes UK VAT registered, the UK Making Tax Digital (MTD) mandate will apply, requiring functional-compatible software to transmit VAT returns through HMRC APIs and to retain digital records for six years.

Abuse of export zero-rating or duty relief triggers retrospective clawbacks and layered penalties. HM Revenue & Customs will withdraw the 0% treatment, assess UK VAT at 20% on the supply, and add late-payment interest, default surcharge, and Schedule 24 penalties (up to 30% for careless inaccuracy, 70–100% for deliberate inaccuracy). HM Customs Gibraltar may assess uncollected duty, impose civil penalties of up to three times the value of the goods for false export or customs declarations, and confiscate the goods. Criminal consequences include prosecution for fraudulent evasion of VAT under section 72 VATA 1994 (up to seven years' imprisonment and unlimited fines), the UK Fraud Act 2006 (fraud by false representation), and the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (money laundering) where the inward remittance certificate, bill of lading, or LC documentation is fabricated. The UK diverted-profits tax (Chapter 4, Finance Act 2015) and UK CFC rules may additionally be applied to the Bermuda-Gibraltar booking structure where economic substance and the 180-day FX repatriation timeline cannot be verified.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both Gibraltar and Bermuda maintain fully convertible currencies: the Gibraltar pound is pegged 1:1 with sterling and the Bermuda dollar is pegged 1:1 with the US dollar. There are no current account or capital account exchange controls for non-resident-owned vehicles in either territory, provided the entity maintains the appropriate designation of non-residence. Dividend and profit remittance are unrestricted where the company has satisfied the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 solvency test and has retained substantive KYC/source-of-funds file evidence; Bermuda exempted companies are tax-exempt on foreign-source income and benefit from BMA general consents for capital movements. Practical hurdles are documentary and procedural rather than regulatory: banks will require endorsed board minutes, updated registers of members, and confirmation of economic substance filings before executing largescale dividend or intercompany remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Neither Gibraltar nor Bermuda imposes statutory export proceeds surrender rules; there is no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts, no prescribed inward remittance window, and no obligation to convert medical-device export collections into local currency. Export receipts may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, or GIP/GBP at the discretion of treasury. As a prudent treasury standard, all export collections generated from OEM digital X-ray flat-panel detector sales should be credited to the dedicated operational account within 30 days of invoice settlement and each receipt matched to the underlying sales contract, LC reference, or purchase order to support GFSC AML record-keeping and BMA Exchange Control audit trails.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar does not maintain an independent central bank; monetary and banking authority functions are discharged by Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act and by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the banking regulator, with monetary policy aligned to the Bank of England. For the Bermuda Exempted Company, the Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) is the designated foreign exchange control authority under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations, acting through its Exchange Control division.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility, subject to material legal constraints. UK Financial Conduct Authority-authorised electronic money institutions and payment institutions may serve Gibraltar corporate clients only through a Gibraltar-registered branch, a local subsidiary, or an approved agent, because automatic EU passporting ended for Gibraltar after Brexit. Lithuanian, Estonian and other non-EEA digital EMIs generally cannot hold Gibraltar operational accounts or clear medical-device trade payments without attaining a GFSC e-money or payment-service licence; for this reason offshore digital EMIs are unsuitable as the sole liquidity rail. A GFSC-licensed EMI or a UK-authorised EMI with Gibraltar presence may be used as a secondary collection sub-rail for low-value distributor receipts, while all LC-backed and large-ticket TT settlements must route through the domestic commercial bank clearing network.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended rail: SWIFT MT103 (or ISO 20022 pacs.008) from the non-resident shareholder bank to the Gibraltar capital escrow account, with the remittance field stating 'CAPITAL INJECTION / SHARE SUBSCRIPTION' and referencing the board resolution date; the transfer should be classified as 'Direct investment - equity capital' in accordance with BPM6 and the Bermuda Exempted Company's non-resident designation. The receiving Gibraltar bank will require advance delivery of the board minutes, the share subscription agreement, the updated register of members, and a certificate or acknowledgement evidencing reliance on BMA General Consent for the Bermuda leg; no statutory Gibraltar FX approval is required. The credit is released only after the Companies Act 2014 share-allotment filing is recorded and the bank has completed source-of-funds verification.TT via SWIFT MT103 is the primary clearing rail for outbound intercompany and supplier payments, complemented by commercial letters of credit for large pre-approved APAC component purchases. An LC is opened via SWIFT MT700 against the ratified purchase order, documents are presented under UCP 600, and settlement is effected via SWIFT MT400/MT202; intra-Gibraltar and UK-regional disbursements clear through CHAPS and FPS, while EUR payments route through TARGET2 via a UK or EU correspondent bank and USD payments clear through CHIPS/Fedwire via a New York correspondent. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies the Income Tax (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019, aligned with the OECD/G20 and EU Code of Conduct: every Gibraltar-resident company performing relevant activities (including distribution and service centres, financing, holding, and IP activities) must maintain board direction and control in Gibraltar, CIGA performed by sufficient local staff, a physical office, and proportionate operating expenditure. For the Bermuda Exempted Company used as an IP owner or OEM distributor of X-ray detectors, the doctrine requires an annual economic substance return and a 'directed and managed in Gibraltar' declaration; if the company instead claims Bermuda non-residence, it must show that board meetings, R&D decisions, and purchasing/sales contracts are concluded outside Gibraltar. Core income-generating activities for the IP box, such as R&D of amorphous-silicon/cesium-iodide arrays, patent protection, and brand exploitation, must be demonstrably located either in Bermuda (under Bermuda's Economic Substance Amendment Act 2023, requiring a Bermuda office, employees, and local CIGA) or in Gibraltar; failure results in assessment on deemed arm's-length profits and exclusion from the 0% exemption.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: On indictment under the Income Tax Act 2010 (Gibraltar) and Criminal Justice Act 2015: unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 7 years; summary conviction: fine up to GIP 7,000 and imprisonment up to 6 months; civil tax assessment of up to 200% of the evaded tax plus interest at 8% per annum; automatic revocation of any territorial exemption or tax residence certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors signatory to the false return are personally exposed to criminal sanctions and disqualification for up to 15 years under the Companies Act 2014; fraudulent-trading provisions permit the court to pierce the corporate veil and order directors to make civil restitution to the Treasury and creditors; courts may impose travel bans and surrender of passports while the investigation is pending.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019, the Commissioner can impose a financial penalty of GIP 100,000 for the first non-compliant year and GIP 200,000 per subsequent year, issue a 'direction' to re-file, and strike off the company under the Companies Act 2014; parallel Bermuda penalties under the Economic Substance Amendment Act 2023 reach USD 250,000 per year plus compulsory winding-up for recidivism.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for economic-substance penalties where they caused or knowingly permitted the failure; Bermuda-registered directors face personal declarations and possible asset-freezing orders; Gibraltar courts can issue travel bans under s. 104 Supreme Court Act 1972 to secure attendance, and persistent substance failure is treated as a ground for disqualification under Gibraltar financial-services legislation.
