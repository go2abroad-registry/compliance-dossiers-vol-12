Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Commissioner of Income Tax and the Customs and Excise Department (official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation)

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company, being a foreign-incorporated non-resident entity, must obtain a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (GTIN) by filing Form TAX-3 through the Gibraltar Corporate Tax Registry. The application requires certified constitutional documents (memorandum and articles of association), a Certificate of Good Standing from Bermuda, a registered office or an appointed resident agent in Gibraltar, and a statement of the entity's offshore trading intent. Upon registration, a 12-digit GTIN is issued within 7 working days; the entity is also required to obtain a Gibraltar EORI-like customs identifier for goods movements where trade is conducted through the Port of Gibraltar.

Available election forms

The Bermuda Exempted Company may elect to be treated as a non-resident offshore company under Gibraltar law, thereby limiting Gibraltar gross income tax exposure on foreign-source wholesale trading income. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available where the Bermuda Exempted Company supplies bitumen or asphalt products that are physically removed from Gibraltar to a non-Gibraltar destination by containerised sea freight, or supplies offshore services to a recipient outside Gibraltar. The exporter must hold valid contractual evidence that the customer is the exporter or recipient of the goods, ownership has passed, and the goods are under export control. If the company is not GST-registered, no output GST is charged, but refund claims require voluntary registration.

Gibraltar does not operate a real-time national transaction clearance system. GST-registered suppliers must issue electronic invoices that comply with the GST (Invoicing and Records) Rules, including the supplier's GTIN and a unique invoice serial number. No continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance or fiscal stamping is currently mandatory for B2B invoices. For goods movements, a customs clearance is mandatory using the Gibraltar Customs Electronic Declaration System; bitumen consignments in ISO containers must be declared under the commodity code corresponding to ISIC 4661 / HS 2713 before export.

Fraudulent export zero-rating claims attract a penalty of 100% of the tax evaded plus interest of 1.5% per month, and potential criminal sanctions including imprisonment up to seven years. The Gibraltar Commissioner of Income Tax has authority to issue retrospective GST assessments for up to four years, reject export documentation, and claw back input tax credits where the supplier fails to maintain complete inward remittance and custody evidence. In serious cases, the entity may be dismissed from the GST register and stripped of its exempt status, leaving the company exposed to Gibraltar gross income tax and enhanced reporting penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both the Gibraltar and Bermuda legs of the structure sit in fully convertible currency zones. Gibraltar imposes no exchange control legislation: capital account transactions (equity injection, shareholder loans) and current account transactions (trade payments, commissions, dividends) may proceed freely in GIP, GBP, USD or any nominated currency without prior approval or declaration. GIP is a closed-system currency outside Gibraltar and most non-Gibraltar counterparties are unable to receive GIP, so all operational flows should be denominated in GBP or USD with GIP retained only for local statutory costs. The Bermuda Exempted Company enjoys statutory tax protection until 2035 under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, with zero withholding tax on dividends or interest repatriation from Bermuda. Profit remittance from Gibraltar to Bermuda or onward to shareholders is subject to zero Gibraltar withholding tax; the material barriers are exclusively documentary, namely source-of-funds verification, economic substance evidence and UK/UN sanctions screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline, surrender ratio or conversion quota applies to export proceeds in either Gibraltar or Bermuda. The bitumen wholesaler may retain receivables in USD indefinitely, net off against letters of credit, or convert voluntarily on the open market. The only binding requirements are FATF-aligned record keeping for a minimum of five years under the Criminal Justice (Money Laundering) Act 2020 (Gibraltar) and mandatory suspicious transaction reporting to the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit where receipts lack an apparent lawful origin. For UK-banked portions, Bank of England sterling reporting forms remain in force but do not impose surrender or conversion obligations.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no independent central bank. HM Government of Gibraltar, acting through the Finance Centre, issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) under the Currency Act 1934; GIP is pegged at par with GBP sterling and fully sterling-backed, implying that effective monetary policy control rests with the Bank of England. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the licensing and supervisory authority for all banks and e-money institutions under the Financial Services (Banking) Act 2015 and the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2011. Prudential, conduct and AML/CFT rules are aligned to UK PRA/FCA and FATF standards via the Gibraltar-UK Memorandum of Understanding on Regulatory Cooperation. On the Bermuda side, the Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) supervises licensed banks and the corporate service provider chain but imposes no FX regulation or capital controls on exempted companies.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operational disbursements, Low for trade finance. Gibraltar is not within the EEA, so EEA e-money institution passports do not extend to Gibraltar; a foreign EMI may serve the entity only as an unregulated payment facilitator. GFSC-licensed EMIs established under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2011 may hold client funds, provide virtual IBANs and execute SEPA/CHAPS transfers, but cannot issue documentary credits, structure banker's acceptances, or provide overdraft and trade facilities. For the target Swift_LC and TT_Wire rails, primary settlement must be routed through a licensed bank; EMIs are recommended only for small-value commission payments, travel and incidental operating costs. Recommended split: 90% bank settlement, 10% EMI for operational micro-payments.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% headline for the Bermuda Exempted Company (no corporate income tax, no capital gains tax, and no dividend withholding tax in Bermuda; statutory protection under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 immunises income and gains from future Bermudian taxation until at least 31 March 2035). Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% in both Bermuda and Gibraltar. Bermuda imposes no dividend withholding tax at any level, and Gibraltar does not levy withholding on distributions. Under the UK-Gibraltar Double Taxation Arrangement (signed 12 July 2019, in force from 1 January 2020), dividends paid to a UK-resident beneficial owner are exempt from source-state taxation at 0%.
