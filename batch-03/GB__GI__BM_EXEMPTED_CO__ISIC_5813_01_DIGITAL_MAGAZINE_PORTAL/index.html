Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both Gibraltar and Bermuda operate full-convertibility regimes. The GIP is pegged at par with GBP and cleared digitally through UK payment infrastructure; the BMD is pegged at par with USD. Current-account transactions (digital subscription revenue, sponsored-media fees, advertising settlements) and capital-account transactions (share capital injection, intra-group loans, dividend repatriation, liquidation proceeds) are free of exchange-control permits, prior approval requirements, repatriation limits, or profit-remittance authorizations. The only practical hurdles are documentary and substance-based: the Bermuda Exempted Company must maintain its valid Certificate of Exemption under the Bermuda Companies Act 1981, and the Gibraltar branch must satisfy annual economic substance reporting under the Gibraltar Economic Substance (Companies) Act 2020, because any failure in tax-residency or substance posture will degrade bank relationship risk-rating even though it does not legally restrict currency convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Neither Gibraltar nor Bermuda imposes any statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or custodial deposit requirement. Revenue from the digital magazine portal received in USD (via AdSense Payout), EUR (via Stripe), GBP, or GIP may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency or multi-currency accounts and converted at free-market rates at the entity's discretion. GFSC-licensed banks submit aggregate cross-border flow statistical returns to the GFSC, but that is a data-reporting obligation only and never a conversion or surrender mandate.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory regulator for banking, e-money, and payment services in Gibraltar; the HM Government of Gibraltar, under the Currency Notes Act (Chapter 94), is the sole issuing authority of the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP pursuant to a 100% sterling backing arrangement; monetary policy for the GIP is anchored de facto by the Bank of England. The Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) remains the home-state regulator of the Bermuda Exempted Company in respect of licensing, exemption-certificate administration, and cross-border supervisory cooperation with the GFSC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

RATING: MEDIUM-HIGH where a Gibraltar substance vehicle exists; LOW if pursued without one. Legal constraints: (1) any EMI physically servicing Gibraltar requires a GFSC licence under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2019 and the Financial Services Act 2019; UK FCA-authorized EMIs and EU PSD2 EMIs cannot passport into Gibraltar post-Brexit, so a UK-sort-code or EU-IBAN fintech account is lawful for GBP/EUR clearing but does not constitute a Gibraltar-resident banking nexus for GFSC supervision; (2) Stripe does not list Gibraltar as a supported merchant incorporation country, therefore card acquiring should be routed through a UK-incorporated special-purpose subsidiary or a licensed payment facilitator (e.g., Paddle or Lemon Squeezy) that settles by SWIFT to the Gibraltar treasury account; AdSense payout is viable by direct wire transfer to any GFSC-regulated settlement IBAN; (3) pure Bermuda offshore topco structures are systematically declined by digital-only EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Currencycloud-type platforms) under transaction-monitoring red flags; an EMI relationship requires the Bermuda Exempted Company to evidence real operating substance in Gibraltar, including registered branch filing, Gibraltar-resident director, current tax filings, and published portal analytics; (4) recommended fintech architecture: a GFSC-licensed EMI providing segregated client-money accounts, virtual IBANs, and interbank FX pricing, acting as a banking-as-a-service layer on top of the GIB/TNB settlement account; (5) all EMI flows above EUR 10,000 per day must be supported by the underwriting file (invoices, contracts, payment evidence) to avoid payment-account freezes under the GFSC AML/CFT Regulations 2019.
