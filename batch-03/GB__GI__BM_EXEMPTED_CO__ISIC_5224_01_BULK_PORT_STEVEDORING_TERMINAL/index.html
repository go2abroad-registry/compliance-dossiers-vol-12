Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax); official e-portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/departments/tax

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the prescribed foreign-company application with the Gibraltar Companies House (Companies Act 2014), attaching a certified copy of the Bermuda certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, register of directors and secretary, and details of the Gibraltar registered office. Step 2: After issuance of the Gibraltar foreign-company registration number, apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number using the branch tax registration form, appoint a Gibraltar resident tax representative, and declare the commencement date of the Gibraltar permanent establishment. Step 3: The Commissioner issues the Tax Reference Number, which must be quoted on all local corporate filings and payments. A Bermuda exempt company has no Gibraltar tax exemption by virtue of its Bermuda status; it is taxable as a foreign company with a territorial scope of taxation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections are recognized under Gibraltar law. A Bermuda exempt company cannot elect into a Gibraltar Qualifying Company regime unless it re-domiciles as a Gibraltar-incorporated entity. The Gibraltar branch is taxed territorially on profits accrued in or derived from Gibraltar; no entity-classification election is available. Remittance of branch profits to Bermuda is not subject to Gibraltar withholding tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar levies no VAT or GST; the only indirect taxation consists of Customs and Excise Duties under the Customs Duties Ordinance and related excise legislation.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – Gibraltar has no VAT/GST registration threshold. Customs and excise registration is triggered only by the physical importation or exportation of dutiable goods; no economic turnover threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST, so there is no VAT export zero-rating or VAT refund mechanism. Stevedoring and cargo-handling services rendered in Gibraltar to ocean-going vessels are business profits within the territorial corporate tax net and are not taxable supplies for indirect tax purposes. No zero-rating election or certificate may be issued.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (where cargo physically moves through the customs territory)
  • Bill of Lading or Equivalent Sea Waybill
  • Port Authority Terminal Operation Record evidencing stevedoring services

Gibraltar does not impose real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API reporting, or digital stamping for B2B supplies. Business records must be preserved for seven years, and the Income Tax Office uses self-assessment with risk-based audit. Port stevedoring invoices must comply with ordinary Companies Act record-keeping requirements but are not required to be cleared through any fiscal device.

No VAT zero-rating claim is available and no VAT refund can be fraudulently obtained. If a taxpayer makes a false statement to the Customs Department to obtain a duty relief, refund, or exemption, the Customs Duties Ordinance permits retrospective assessment within six years, penalties of up to three times the evaded duty, and forfeiture of the goods. Forged export or port documents may constitute tax fraud and give rise to criminal liability. The Income Tax Office may disallow related deductions and impose late-payment interest and a penalty of up to 15% for negligent or fraudulent returns.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP is fully convertible into GBP at the statutory 1:1 peg and both currencies circulate freely in Gibraltar. The UK/Gibraltar regime imposes no exchange controls on current-account transactions (stevedoring service fees, port dues) or capital-account transactions (equity injection, intercompany debt, dividend repatriation) for non-resident offshore entities. Convertibility is subject only to standard commercial-bank AML/KYC documentation and sanctions screening; no government approval, rationing, or non-resident deposit restriction applies. International maritime counterparties settle in USD; the terminal should hold a multi-currency account (GBP, USD, EUR) to avoid forced conversion risk.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no export-proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no statutory repatriation window in Gibraltar or the United Kingdom. A stevedoring terminal may retain export proceeds in the original invoicing currency and convert at its commercial discretion; the only obligation is the GFSC-mandated transaction reporting threshold for large cross-border flows and compliance with the proceeds-of-crime regime.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no domestic central bank; the Bank of England anchors monetary conditions and the Gibraltar pound (GIP) circulates at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP, issued by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934. Banking regulation and licensing are administered by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019, with the UK Financial Conduct Authority retaining conduct-cooperation arrangements; no central-bank permission, FX registration, or exchange-control licence is required for a Bermuda exempted company operating a bulk-port stevedoring terminal.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary operating or escrow rails. Gibraltar-licensed e-money institutions and offshore digital EMIs operating under the GFSC e-money framework cannot access the Port Authority clearing network, face low transaction-velocity caps, and labour under correspondent-bank de-risking constraints. Legal restrictions include the prohibition on deposit-taking, exclusion from commercial-credit facilities, and the pooled safeguarding-account model that creates priority and waterfall credit risk for bulk payroll and dock-dues disbursements. An EMI is acceptable only as a satellite collections channel in segregated accounts with daily sweeps to a licensed tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 from the Bermuda parent or the UBO's corporate bank into the Gibraltar operational account, using the ISO 20022 purpose classification for an equity injection (bank-mapped code 'EQUI' or equivalent inward-direct-investment narrative) and a memo referencing the share subscription under the Bermuda Companies Act 1981. The receiving Gibraltar bank must receive pre-notification comprising the board resolution, share-subscription certificate, source-of-wealth file, and the consolidated group structure diagram; transactions above GBP/EUR 10,000 trigger statutory source-of-funds verification, and same-day value settlement is achievable where both sending and receiving institutions hold GBP nostro balances at the Bank of England.Trade settlement rails include SWIFT MT103 for stevedoring subcontractors, port-authority dock dues, and equipment suppliers; MT700/MT707 documentary letters of credit for crane, gantry, and cargo-handling equipment; CHAPS for same-day GBP/GIP settlement with the Port of Gibraltar; and USD nostro transfers via CHIPS or Fedwire for shipping-line invoice settlements. GIP is legal tender only within Gibraltar, so all external clearing is effected in GBP at the statutory 1:1 parity; the treasury structure must maintain a segregated GIP account for Port Authority clearing reconciliation and a GBP/USD operational account for international counterparties, with a documented RMA between the Gibraltar bank and the Bermudian parent bank.Outright deliverable FX forwards (1-24 month tenors) on USD/GBP, EUR/GBP, and EUR/USD with a GFSC-licensed counterparty, permissible as prudent corporate hedges of forecast stevedoring receivables without documentary proof of exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted freight-quote currencies such as ZAR, IDR, BRL, or NGN where the Gibraltar bank maintains the necessary correspondent lines, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with credit support annex.Cross-currency swaps (USD/GBP, EUR/GBP) to match fixed-interest equipment-finance obligations on terminal cranes, hoppers, and conveyor systems.GBP/USD and EUR/USD options, including participating forwards and structured collars, to protect tender-price exposure on port-concession renewals.Hedging executed only through GFSC-authorized investment firms under MiFID II third-country counterparty rules, with no speculative uncovered derivative positions permitted by the Bermuda board under the treasury policy.
