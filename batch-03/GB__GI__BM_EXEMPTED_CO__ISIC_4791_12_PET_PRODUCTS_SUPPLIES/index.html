Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office (official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation); HM Government of Gibraltar – Customs Division (official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/customs)

E-government filing portal

A foreign Bermuda Exempted Company must first determine whether it carries on a commercial presence in Gibraltar. If it establishes a branch, place of business or local activity, it must file Form CO-1 (Overseas Company Registration) with the Gibraltar Companies Registry under the Companies Act 2014, attaching a certified certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered-office consent, and identity evidence for directors and beneficial owners. Upon registration, the Income Tax Office issues a Gibraltar tax reference number (TRN). No VAT/GST identification is issued because Gibraltar does not levy VAT/GST. A company with no registered presence and no chargeable Gibraltar-source income is not required to obtain a tax identifier. For import/export of dutiable goods, the entity must obtain an EORI number from Gibraltar Customs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box classification is available in Gibraltar. Available statutory elections include: (i) election for non-resident status where central management and control is exercised outside Gibraltar, limiting tax chargeability to Gibraltar-source income; (ii) election under the Qualifying Company (HQ services) regime providing a fixed annual tax of GBP 30,000 in lieu of certain income taxation; and (iii) election under the Tax (Administration) (BEPS) Regulations 2016 for hybrid-mismatch reporting. Bermuda Exempted Company status itself does not require or permit a local entity-classification election in Bermuda or Gibraltar.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST in Gibraltar; indirect taxation consists solely of customs and excise duties on imported alcohol, tobacco, petroleum, and other specified goods

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for a general indirect tax. A customs EORI and import/excise duty registration are required only when importing dutiable goods. For UK VAT exposure in Great Britain, the separate UK VAT registration threshold is GBP 90,000 (GBP 85,000 before April 2025) on taxable turnover; online sales to UK consumers may also trigger UK VAT registration under the Post-Brexit import and remote services rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar's fiscal territory does not impose VAT, so no local zero-rating can be claimed. If the entity is registered for UK VAT, exports of pet products from the UK to a place outside the UK/EU may be zero-rated under the VAT Export Regulations 1995, provided the supplier obtains valid export evidence within three months and retains it for at least six years. Supplies made from Gibraltar to non-UK destinations are outside the scope of Gibraltar taxation and are not eligible for any VAT/GST refund because no VAT/GST is charged in Gibraltar.

  • Customs Export Declaration (UK Single Administrative Document / CDS output) or Gibraltar customs declaration
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill or courier consignment note evidencing export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/FX transfer confirmation showing repatriation of export proceeds

Gibraltar operates no real-time e-invoicing, digital stamping, or fiscal clearance mandate. Cross-border goods movements require electronic declaration via the Gibraltar Customs EDI system and, for UK/EU trade, the UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS); an ICS/ECS safety-and-security filing applies. No API-based revenue clearance requirement exists.

Under the UK VAT regime, missing or defective export evidence triggers VAT clawback, interest, a misdeclaration penalty up to 30% per section 63 of the VAT Act 1994, and potential penalties up to 100% for deliberate evasion; HMRC may also apply civil penalties under the Finance Act 2007 and pursue criminal prosecution. In Gibraltar, customs duty evasion creates liability for unpaid duty, a fine of up to three times the duty value, and forfeiture of goods under the Importation of Goods (Control) Act and the Customs Ordinance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital accounts; there is no Gibraltar exchange-control legislation restricting capital inflows, outbound trade payments, or profit remittances. The Bermuda Exempted Company is non-resident for Gibraltar tax purposes unless management and control are exercised in Gibraltar, so no withholding friction applies to dividends or sale proceeds. E-commerce receivables are collected via PSP schemes (Stripe/PayPal) rather than through a FX licence, which further reduces conversion friction. Residual impairments are confined to Enhanced Due Diligence on high-velocity retail settlement flows and annual Economic Substance (ES) filings proving commercial substance; no mandatory conversion of foreign currency earnings exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. The Bermuda Exempted Company may retain e-commerce sales receipts indefinitely in GBP, EUR, or USD, and Bermuda's Exchange Control regime likewise imposes no surrender obligation on a non-resident exempted company. Treasury policy should nevertheless adopt a disciplined conversion rule: transfer 65% of USD proceeds to GBP monthly to fund operating costs and retain the balance in USD for Asian supplier inventory payments.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the licensing and AML/CFT supervisor for all banks and EMIs in Gibraltar; the monetary framework is anchored to the Bank of England because the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934 and pegged at par with GBP; UK sanctions and financial crime legislation apply to Gibraltar as a British Overseas Territory.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High, subject to three binding constraints. First, select a GFSC-licensed Gibraltar EMI or a full licence-holding FCA UK EMI; appointed-representative shells will be rejected by the payment processors. Second, the PSP layer (Stripe/PayPal) must be contractually integrated with the EMI settlement account because certain EMIs decline high-velocity B2C retail flows. Third, the Bermuda offshore parentage will always trigger Enhanced Due Diligence, so the EMI must support API-driven KYC uploads, audit trail extraction, and multi-currency virtual IBANs. Recommended stack: Stripe/PayPal for card acquiring; a GFSC/UK dual-licensed EMI for concentration and rapid settlement; and Jyske Bank for the final treasury buffer.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GBP 1,000,000 for Local File; Master File required when the multinational group has consolidated annual revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to entities carrying on geographically mobile activities, including distribution and service centres. The pet-supplies e-commerce operation must demonstrate physical presence, locally employed qualified staff, core income-generating activities (CIGA) performed in Gibraltar, and annual ESR-SC reporting. Failure to meet ESR triggers financial penalties and possible cancellation of Gibraltar tax residence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Additional assessments of up to 200% of the evaded tax; criminal fines up to GBP 500,000; prosecution under the Income Tax (International Tax Compliance) Act.

Director personal liability: Directors, shadow directors and beneficial owners can be made personally liable for unpaid corporate tax and civil restitution via corporate veil piercing; travel bans and imprisonment of up to 5 years for fraudulent evasion.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR penalties from GBP 10,000 to GBP 750,000 per default; repeated violations lead to cancellation of tax residence certificate and compulsory strike-off under the Companies Act.

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified for up to 15 years and held personally liable for debts and taxes incurred when continuing to trade without economic substance or signing false ESR returns.
AML/CTF Breach

Statutory sanction: Penalties under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 up to GBP 10,000,000 or 30% of annual turnover, whichever is greater; account freezing and forfeiture of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Money Laundering Reporting Officers and directors face personal criminal fines, confiscation orders, and imprisonment of up to 14 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment; late payment interest; administrative penalties up to 100% of the under-declared additional tax; adverse ruling publication.

Director personal liability: CFOs and finance directors who certify false transfer pricing documentation may face negligence penalties up to GBP 50,000 and personal liability for restitution of diverted group profits.
