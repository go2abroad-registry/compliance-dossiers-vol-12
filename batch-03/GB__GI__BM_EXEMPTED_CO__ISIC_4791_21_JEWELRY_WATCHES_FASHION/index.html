Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' election. Available mechanisms: (a) Bermuda — election for the Exempted Company to be designated as a 'permit company' under the Bermuda Companies Act 1981, allowing limited domestic business activity without losing exempt status; (b) Gibraltar — election or factual filing to maintain non-resident status where central management and control is exercised outside Gibraltar, and the 'qualifying company' procedure for any Gibraltar-incorporated subsidiary; (c) hybrid mismatch — no elective domestic check-the-box election; Gibraltar applies the OECD hybrid and branch mismatch rules automatically under its domestic anti-hybrid legislation, while the UK parent regime (Corporation Tax Act 2010, s. 259) allows a hybridity notice only in a narrow foreign-branch mismatch case.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Customs and Excise Duty Regime (non-VAT jurisdiction; no general VAT, GST, or sales and use tax is levied within Gibraltar; indirect taxation is limited to import duties and excise duties on specified goods).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST and therefore no turnover-based indirect tax registration threshold; any business importing or exporting commercial goods must obtain an EORI number from HM Customs Gibraltar at a nil registration threshold, and operators handling excise goods (alcohol, tobacco, fuel) require a licence under the Excise Duty Ordinance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Strictly, there is no VAT zero-rating in Gibraltar. Export relief applies to goods physically leaving Gibraltar, placing the supply outside the scope of Gibraltar customs and excise duties. For jewelry, watches, and fashion items sold online and shipped to non-resident consumers, the exporter must evidence physical departure from Gibraltar, lodgement of a valid export declaration, and settlement of proceeds in a freely convertible currency. Where import duty was paid at the time of importation into Gibraltar, a drawback or remission is available only upon production of export evidence within the statutory time limits. UK import VAT may arise separately at the consignment's arrival in Great Britain and is administered by HMRC, independent of the Gibraltar regime.

  • Gibraltar export declaration (C88 equivalent) lodged through the electronic customs declaration gateway of HM Customs Gibraltar
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (original or signed shipped-on-board copy)
  • Commercial invoice and itemised packing list issued by the Bermuda Exempted Company
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank credit advice, or Stripe/PayPal settlement report evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the designated Gibraltar or Bermuda bank account
  • Bermuda Economic Substance (ES) annual declaration and annual exempted-company government fee receipt

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate is in force in Gibraltar. Customs declarations are submitted electronically in a machine-readable format approved by HM Customs Gibraltar through the Gibraltar electronic customs declaration gateway; e-commerce invoices and payment records for the fashion jewelry and watch business must be retained for six years under the Income Tax Act, but no government real-time API clearance or e-invoicing platform applies.

Fraudulent export zero-rating or export-relief claims are prosecuted under Gibraltar's customs and excise legislation: civil penalties of up to GBP 25,000 or three times the duty evaded, seizure and forfeiture of goods, vehicles, and associated digital or electronic-money assets; criminal liability carries up to seven years' imprisonment and an unlimited fine; any drawback or duty remission granted is retrospectively clawed back with a 100% surcharge. A Bermuda Exempted Company making false export declarations further risks penalties of USD 10,000 to USD 250,000 per annum under the Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018, potential strike-off, and retrospective reclassification as Gibraltar-resident where central management and control is shown to be exercised in Gibraltar, exposing deemed profits to 12.5% Gibraltar corporation tax plus interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full currency convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange control laws, mandatory approvals, or central bank transfer restrictions. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, intercompany loan repayments, and capital repatriation may be executed freely through the banking system, subject to standard anti-money laundering documentation such as board resolutions, source of funds evidence, and CRS/FATCA declarations. The Gibraltar pound is pegged at par to pound sterling and GBP/EUR/USD conversion occurs at commercial bank rates. As a Bermuda Exempted Company, no Gibraltar corporate income tax is levied, but any claim of non-Gibraltar tax residence must be supported by central management and control documentation and, if applicable, economic substance evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement exists in Gibraltar. Export receipts may be retained in original currency or converted at the exporter's discretion. There are no mandatory conversion ratios or repatriation timelines. Only general AML/CTF reporting thresholds, sanctions screening, and record-keeping obligations apply to trade-related inward payments.

Central bank supervision: HM Government of Gibraltar / HM Treasury (monetary authority) and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the integrated financial services regulator for banking, investment, and e-money activities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to High for regulated Gibraltar-licensed and UK-authorized e-money institutions, which can support marketplace payouts, virtual IBANs, and SEPA/Faster Payments. Low viability for unregulated offshore digital EMIs because they cannot lawfully hold client funds, issue payment instruments, or provide merchant settlement rails in Gibraltar without an FSC licence. Third-country EMI passporting into Gibraltar is restricted after Brexit, so a Gibraltar e-money licence or UK EMI authorisation is the legally preferred route.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be executed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the ultimate beneficial owner or parent entity in EUR, GBP, or USD. The wire reference must identify 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION - [ENTITY NAME]' and be supported by a current board resolution, share allotment letter, and, where relevant, an intercompany loan agreement. For payments from EU jurisdictions, SEPA Credit Transfer may be used for amounts below EUR 100,000, but the remitter must include the purpose code for direct investment or capital contribution. All within bank-to-bank sanctions screening and FATCA/CRS self-certification requirements.Trade settlements to suppliers and third-party vendors should use SWIFT MT103 with corresponding purpose code for goods and services. For jewellery and timepiece procurement, suppliers in Asia or high-risk jurisdictions may require an irrevocable, confirmed commercial letter of credit at sight, with payment via MT103/MT202 under the LC terms. Disbursements to e-commerce logistics providers and marketplaces may be executed through commercial payment processors, PayPal or Stripe, with funds swept daily into the Gibraltar operational account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar has no statutory transfer pricing documentation threshold for Local File/Master File; however, OECD-aligned documentation is recommended for controlled transactions exceeding GB£1,000,000. MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million are subject to Country-by-Country Reporting obligations.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Requirements) Regulations 2018 apply to Gibraltar tax-resident companies engaged in relevant activities, including distribution and services; an online jewelry e-commerce business is a 'relevant activity' requiring physical presence in Gibraltar (employees, premises, expenditure) proportionate to the level of such activity. Qualifying Companies claiming a 0% foreign-source rate must demonstrate that no economic substance is present in Gibraltar and that core income-generating activities take place in the jurisdiction of their actual management.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Concealed Online Sales

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under Gibraltar's Income Tax Act as extended by the Criminal Justice Act; liability to pay underdeclared tax, a tax-geared penalty of up to double the amount of tax evaded, and/or a fine of up to GB£100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly procure fraudulent returns face unlimited joint and several liability for the company's unpaid tax plus civil recovery; travel restrictions and a custodial sentence of up to 7 years may be imposed. The corporate veil is pierced for sham/fraud cases.
Shell Company / Insufficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The QC election is invalid; all foreign-source profits become taxable at 10%; in addition, the Income Tax Office may impose a penalty of GB£10,000 for failure to comply with the Economic Substance Regulations and issue a notice requiring remedial action within 60 days.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for penalties if the substance requirements are knowingly omitted; they can be disqualified as directors in Gibraltar for up to 15 years and be named in public notices; serious non-compliance attracts criminal sanctions.
AML/CTF Deficiencies in Jewellery Trade

Statutory sanction: Civil monetary penalties of up to GB£5 million or 10% of global turnover; criminal conviction can result in a fine and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; license termination and closure of the e-commerce payment processors.

Director personal liability: Directors are subject to individual civil penalties; where non-compliance is a deliberate act of money laundering facilitation, directors face prosecution with imprisonment up to 14 years, forfeiture of personal assets, and regulatory debarment from managing companies.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing of IP Royalties

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the controlled transaction; taxable profits increased; a transfer pricing penalty of 100% of the additional tax due on the adjustment plus interest at 8% per annum.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's length arrangements can be made personally liable for the increased tax and penalties if the company becomes insolvent; also possible criminal liability for tax evasion by intentionally mispricing.
