Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office (ITO), HM Government of Gibraltar; corporate tax filings and registration are administered through the Government of Gibraltar tax portal and Gibraltar Companies House (www.gibraltar.gov.gi).

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014, the company automatically receives a unique Gibraltar company registration number (GRN), which serves as its corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN). The registered agent files a tax notification form with the Income Tax Office within 30 days of incorporation; the ITO then issues the corporate Tax Reference and annual self-assessment instructions. A foreign-controlled company seeking offshore tax treatment must additionally submit a formal Declaration of Central Management and Control evidencing that strategic management occurs outside Gibraltar. No separate TIN application is required for non-resident companies unless the entity later registers for local payroll (employer PAYE), customs, or a local establishment.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Gibraltar applies a territorial tax system: a company managed and controlled outside Gibraltar (non-resident/offshore) is chargeable only on income arising within Gibraltar. Foreign-source revenue from the video-match app (virtual credit sales, subscriptions, advertising, Apple IAP, Paymentwall settlements) falls outside the Gibraltar tax net. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — Gibraltar applies a territorial tax system: a company managed and controlled outside Gibraltar (non-resident/offshore) is chargeable only on income arising within Gibraltar. Foreign-source revenue from the video-match app (virtual credit sales, subscriptions, advertising, Apple IAP, Paymentwall settlements) falls outside the Gibraltar tax net. No capital gains tax is levied, and no Gibraltar withholding tax applies to dividends paid to non-residents.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election regime. Available statutory mechanisms include: (i) elective non-residence treatment via evidence of central management and control abroad (board minutes, foreign meeting locations, key decision records); (ii) Group Relief election allowing surrender of losses within a 75% or more corporate group; (iii) small company fixed-rate assessment election for qualifying low-turnover companies; (iv) for in-scope MNE groups (consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750 million), election to apply Gibraltar's Qualified Domestic Top-Up Tax at 15% and the Income Inclusion Rule under Pillar Two; and (v) redomiciliation/continuation election under the Companies Act to migrate the company's corporate seat out of Gibraltar without liquidation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / GST / Sales Tax. Gibraltar is excluded from the UK VAT system and the EU VAT area. Indirect taxation is limited to Gibraltar Import Duties and Excise Duties on specific physical goods (tobacco, alcohol, hydrocarbons). Online digital services supplied by the company are not subject to any Gibraltar indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST registration threshold exists in Gibraltar because no such tax is levied. For customs purposes, a physical importer of dutiable goods (alcohol, tobacco, fuel) must register with the Gibraltar Customs Service from the first import (effective threshold: GBP 0). Purely digital turnover creates no customs or indirect-tax registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is not applicable as a VAT concept because Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST. All digital services supplied to non-resident users are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax (effectively 0%). Physical goods exported from Gibraltar are generally free of export duty, provided the goods physically depart and are supported by customs evidence. Input VAT refunds cannot be claimed in Gibraltar (no input VAT exists); any foreign VAT incurred (e.g., EU cloud hosting, SaaS procurement) must be reclaimed through the relevant foreign jurisdiction's recovery mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar Single Administrative Document / electronic export declaration) — required only for physical goods; digital-only operations rely on payment and transaction records
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (logistics provider certificate of export) — required for physical consignments; not required for pure digital B2C services
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/PSP settlement statement (SWIFT MT103, Apple IAP, Paymentwall payout statements) evidencing receipt of foreign currency from non-resident users
  • Transaction-level audit log with user geolocation (IP country), device metadata, and app-store settlement summaries linking each revenue stream to foreign markets

No mandate. Gibraltar does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoice clearance, digital stamping, or API-based tax reporting. Voluntary e-invoicing is commercially permissible. Physical imports/exports, when made, require electronic customs declarations via the Gibraltar Customs Service single window (compatible with UK CDS documentation formats), but this is a customs/border process and not a tax-invoicing obligation.

Gibraltar Income Tax Office may open audit inquiries within six years from the end of the chargeable period (unlimited in fraud cases). If the company fails to evidence non-resident management and control or foreign-source revenue, the ITO may recharacterise profits as Gibraltar-source and assess tax at the standard 12.5% corporate rate, plus late-payment interest. Under the Income Tax Act 2010, penalties for incorrect returns range from 50% (careless) to 100% of tax understated (deliberate with concealment); fraudulent claims attract criminal sanctions. No VAT clawback exists, but false customs export declarations are sanctioned under the Customs Ordinance through forfeiture of goods, fines up to GBP 5,000, and loss of duty-free export privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility with no exchange controls, fixed-peg GIP/GBP parity, and unrestricted profit repatriation by dividends, management fees, loan repayments, or liquidation distributions. The operating treasury route from a Gibraltar account to Bermuda or international counterparties is free of central-bank approval hurdles. On the Bermuda layer, the Exempted Company must be designated as non-resident for exchange-control purposes (a permitted-entity classification is typically granted by the BMA), which fully exempts USD/EUR digital-export proceeds from convertibility requirements and removes any remittance obstacle for shareholder distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Neither Gibraltar nor Bermuda imposes statutory export-proceeds surrender timelines or mandatory conversion ratios. Digital service revenues (ISIC 6311; Apple IAP and Paymentwall settlements) may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, or GBP, converted at market rates at the corporate treasury's discretion, and redeployed without central-bank reporting thresholds in Gibraltar. The corresponding Bermuda requirement is only an exchange-control non-resident status filing, ensuring that collections are not obliged to be sold to an authorised dealer; ordinary financial-return filings to the Bermuda Registrar remain the sole periodic compliance duty.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar does not maintain a domestic central bank. The Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) under the Currency Notes Act 1934 at a fixed 1:1 parity with the British pound; banking supervision is vested in the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC); and the Bank of England provides the underlying monetary framework. For the underlying Bermuda Exempted Company, the Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) administers the Exchange Control Act 1972 and regulates the exchange-control classification of the entity.

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low for cross-border fintech/EMI sponsorship. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 annual aggregate of controlled transactions triggers mandatory Local File; GIP 3,500,000 annual group turnover triggers a Master File; de minimis exemption below GIP 250,000 per related-party transaction; any cross-border transaction with a no-tax or exempted affiliate must be documented regardless of value.

Economic substance scope: Under Gibraltar's Companies (Economic Substance) Act and the International Tax Compliance (Economic Substance) Regulations, entities claiming the 0% exempt status must maintain adequate substance in Gibraltar for 'Geographically Mobile Business', including board meetings, qualified employees, physical assets, and core income-generating activities; a Bermuda Exempted Company conducting real-time video chat and social telephony must demonstrate real decision-making in Gibraltar or face denial of exemption, penalties up to GIP 100,000, and forced striking-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty equal to 200% of the undercharged tax; criminal conviction for evasion under the Income Tax Act carrying a fine of up to GIP 100,000 and imprisonment up to seven years; interest accrues at the standard late-payment rate.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute or conspire in the evasion are personally liable for the unpaid taxes through piercing the corporate veil; courts may impose asset freezing, travel bans, and imprisonment between 12 months and seven years; disqualification from being a director for up to 15 years.
Shell Company and Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Office levies penalties from GIP 10,000 to GIP 100,000 per infringement; the tax exemption is retroactively revoked, causing reassessment at 10% for six years, plus interest and penalties; applicable laws: Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2021.

Director personal liability: Directors and ultimate beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for back taxes and penalties; in severe substance failures, the court can grant a 'fraudulent trading' order making directors personally liable for all company debts under section 332 of the Companies Act 2014.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority makes an arm's-length adjustment increasing taxable income; penalty of 100% of any understated tax plus 2% monthly interest; separate documentation penalties up to GIP 25,000 for non-production of Local File and Master File.

Director personal liability: Finance directors and TP signatories face strict liability under the Transfer Pricing and Substance Regulations; personal penalties are imposed where misstatements are knowingly false, including personal fines up to GIP 50,000 and prohibition from holding financial management roles.
AML and Money Laundering Flags

Statutory sanction: Financial Intelligence Unit imposes penalties up to GIP 250,000 or 5% of annual turnover, whichever is higher; senior officers can be imprisoned for up to two years; the company's designated interested business licence may be revoked, blocking App Store collection.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for conspiracy to launder money; they may be subject to worldwide travel bans, asset seizure, and custodial sentences of up to 14 years; corporate veil-piercing applies where the company is an instrumentality for illicit funds.
Unauthorized Dividend Stripping and Illegal Distributions

Statutory sanction: Civil restitution order: directors must repay any distribution made from capital to the company; the distribution is disallowed for tax-exemption purposes, resulting in a 10% corporate tax charge and late-payment penalties; potential strike-off proceedings.

Director personal liability: Each director who voted for an unlawful dividend is jointly and severally liable to restore the amount to the company; fraudulent intended concealment may lead to extradition-eligible offenses and director disqualification for up to 15 years.
