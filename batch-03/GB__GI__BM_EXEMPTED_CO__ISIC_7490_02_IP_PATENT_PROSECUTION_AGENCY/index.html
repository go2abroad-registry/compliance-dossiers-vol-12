Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar: HM Government of Gibraltar, Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), Durbar House, 179 Queen's Road, Gibraltar; primary online portal is the 'One Gibraltar' digital services platform (portal.gibraltar.gov.gi) and e-filing via the Income Tax Office self-assessment system. Bermuda (incorporation state): Registrar of Companies, Ministry of Finance, and the Office of the Tax Commissioner for payroll tax purposes; no income tax return is required for a Bermuda Exempted Company.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Register the Bermuda Exempted Company as an External Company (overseas company) with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 2014, filing certified charter documents, a list of directors, and a Gibraltar registered office address for service of process. Step 2 — Apply to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) using the corporate registration form, attaching (a) the Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, (b) the Gibraltar External Company registration certificate, (c) particulars of directors and shareholders, and (d) a declaration of the place of central management and control; processing takes approximately 7–14 working days. Step 3 — Register the entity with the relevant Gibraltar professional licensing body for patent and trademark agency work (patent attorney/trademark agent licensing is a statutory prerequisite for the ISIC_7490_02 scope) and, if staff are engaged in Gibraltar, register as an employer for income tax (PAYE) and social insurance. Step 4 — File the Annual Declaration of Compliance with the Bermuda Registrar and pay the Bermuda annual government fee (currently BMD 3,820–4,590 depending on share capital) to maintain the exempted status; maintain a non-resident exclusion certificate under the Bermuda Economic Substance Act by evidencing tax residence and management in Gibraltar.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Bermuda Exempted Company is exempt from Bermuda corporate income tax, capital gains tax, and withholding tax (0% headline, subject to the new 15% Bermuda Corporate Income Tax for MNE groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million effective fiscal year 2025, which does not apply to a boutique patent agency). In Gibraltar, the classification depends on the seat of management: if central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, the company becomes a Gibraltar-resident company chargeable at 12.5% only on Gibraltar-source profits (territorial basis); income arising from patent prosecution work physically performed in Gibraltar is Gibraltar-source, while foreign-source income received outside Gibraltar remains outside the Gibraltar charge. The Exempted Company's structural prohibition on Bermuda local business is compatible, as all trading activity occurs through the Gibraltar operation.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box election. Available statutory mechanisms include: (a) voluntary election for the Commissioner of Income Tax to treat a non-Gibraltar-incorporated company as tax resident in Gibraltar based on central management and control; (b) group relief election enabling transfer of losses between 75% Gibraltar-resident group companies (Income Tax Act 2010, s.65); (c) patent box (IP Box) election applying a preferential effective rate (historically 2.5%) to qualifying patent income derived from IP developed in Gibraltar; (d) capital allowance elections for qualifying IP assets; and (e) automatic application of OECD hybrid mismatch rules to neutralize double non-taxation arising from payments between the Bermuda exempted company and the Gibraltar taxable branch — no election to disapply is permitted. No election is available to opt out of the standard 12.5% corporation tax rate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST applies in Gibraltar. Gibraltar is constitutionally outside the UK VAT area and, post-Brexit, outside the EU VAT regime; indirect taxation is limited to customs and excise duties on imported physical goods, stamp duty on real estate transfers and instruments, and hydrocarbon duty. Supplies of PCT patent prosecution and international trademark registration services are entirely outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax. Bermuda similarly imposes no VAT or GST, but levies customs duties on imported goods (average ad valorem 15%–30% depending on classification) and a payroll tax on Bermuda-employed remuneration, which is not triggered by the Gibraltar operating entity.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST regime exists in Gibraltar, therefore no economic turnover threshold triggers indirect tax registration. Importers of dutiable physical goods must obtain a customs registration and account for duties via the Gibraltar Customs Department, but this does not apply to a service-only patent agency. In Bermuda, payroll tax registration is required only where Bermuda remuneration exceeds BMD 1,000 per month; the Exempted Company with zero Bermuda employees has no registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST, cross-border supplies of patent prosecution and trademark registration services to clients in the USA, EU, WIPO, Singapore, and other foreign jurisdictions are simply outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax. No zero-rating claim, input VAT recovery mechanism, or refund procedure exists. Substantiation of the arm's-length and indirect-tax-free treatment requires the client engagement agreement, itemized fee schedules separating official filing fees (WIPO, USPTO, EPO, UKIPO) from agent professional fees, WIPO/PCT or Madrid System receipts, evidence of foreign payer status, and bank records showing cross-border inward remittance.

  • Not applicable to services — no customs export declaration is required for IP/patent prosecution supplies
  • Client engagement letter and power of attorney evidencing the patent/trademark agency mandate
  • Itemized invoices separating official filing fees (WIPO/USPTO/EPO/UKIPO) from agent professional fees
  • WIPO PCT or Madrid System receipt confirming international filing date and designated states
  • Inward remittance confirmation (SWIFT MT103) from the foreign client for each professional fee invoice
  • Gibraltar Tax Reference Number registration certificate and tax residence certificate
  • Bermuda Certificate of Exemption and annual Declaration of Compliance evidencing tax-exempt status
  • Transfer pricing documentation demonstrating arm's-length fees between the Bermuda entity and the Gibraltar operating presence

No real-time fiscal clearance, tax-authority API integration, or digital stamping requirement exists in Gibraltar. Electronic invoices are accepted for statutory record-keeping and must be retained for at least six years, showing the Gibraltar TRN, the supplier's name and address, a sequential number, and the client's name and jurisdiction of residence. Physical goods declarations are processed electronically through the ASYCUDA World customs clearance system, but this applies to merchandise logistics only and not to professional or IP services.

There is no statutory FX repatriation deadline in Gibraltar or Bermuda; the 180-day benchmark is applied as an administrative expectation consistent with OECD transfer pricing documentation standards and prudent treasury practice. Absent a VAT system, no export zero-rating clawback exists, but the following tax audit exposures apply: (a) Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 — a negligent or fraudulent tax return attracts civil penalties up to 100% of the tax understated plus interest at 8% per annum, with criminal liability on indictment (fine up to GBP 500,000 and/or imprisonment up to seven years); a discovery assessment may be raised within 21 years in cases of fraud or wilful default; (b) permanent establishment risk — if the Bermuda entity invoices for patent work performed in Gibraltar without a Gibraltar branch and arm's-length allocation, the Commissioner may deem a Gibraltar PE, reallocate 12.5% tax on Gibraltar-source profits, and impose penalties for failure to notify chargeability; (c) Gibraltar transfer pricing rules require contemporaneous documentation for cross-border related-party fee flows; non-compliance carries penalties of up to GBP 100,000; (d) mischaracterization of mark-ups on patent office fees as disimbursable at cost without substantiating WIPO receipts may be treated as undisclosed Gibraltar taxable turnover, giving rise to a 100% penalty plus interest; (e) Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2019 — if the entity cannot substantiate non-resident status, penalties start at USD 250,000 for the first year and USD 500,000 for each subsequent year, with possible strike-off; and (f) CRS/FATCA self-certification obligations apply to the professional services entity as a non-financial foreign entity, and incorrect filings attract Gibraltar penalties of up to GBP 50,000.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full current-account convertibility and de facto full capital-account convertibility. There is no foreign-exchange control law, no Central Bank approval gate for inbound investment, and no restriction on profit repatriation or dividend distribution. GIP is freely exchangeable into GBP at par through authorised Gibraltar banks, and balances may be held in GBP, USD, EUR, or any major currency. For the custom 'Bermuda Exempted Co' structure, legal and beneficial owners may repatriate retained earnings by ordinary board resolution and bank wire, subject only to standard KYC and source-of-funds verification at the paying bank. No withholding or supplementary remittance tax is levied by Gibraltar on distributions to non-resident shareholders of an exempted company. Practical hurdles are limited to bank clearing times (CHAPS/Fedwire/SEPA) and correspondent-bank due diligence on the underlying patent-prosecution fee flow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion applies to export receipts, including receipts from PCT patent prosecution, international trademark filing, or legal fee disbursements. Gibraltar does not impose an export-proceeds conversion ratio or obligatory holding period for foreign currency. The entity is free to retain all receipts in original currency, sell them to its bank on spot terms, or hedge them via permitted forward contracts. If receipts are in GIP, banks convert to GBP at par automatically at no net economic cost. The only administrative requirements are accounting substance records, transfer pricing documentation, and anti-money laundering transaction monitoring on high-frequency fee disbursements.

Central bank supervision: Government of Gibraltar - Financial Services Commission (banking supervisor and licensing authority) in coordination with HM Treasury, United Kingdom; Gibraltar has no autonomous central bank, and the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued by the Government of Gibraltar and pegged at 1:1 to GBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for operational payment rails, Low for capital escrow and client-monies. Wise (natively suggested by the business model) and similar UK-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may be used for low-value disbursements of filing fees, annuity payments, and vendor invoices in USD, EUR, and GBP. Legal constraints: EMIs are not deposit-taking banks, do not hold client money as a licensed bank, and cannot provide formal escrow accounts, dedicated account segregation for regulated legal-client funds, or direct clearing through CHAPS/Fedwire under payment-banking credentials. A cross-border digital EMI account may be onboarded remotely using passport and corporate registry, but the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission AML regime requires that client monies relating to patent prosecution remain in a bank-designated client account or in a licensed bank as principal. Therefore, the blueprint must use an EMI only as a funding/collection ancillary ledger, never as the custody rail for capital, settlement reserves, or lawyer-client trust moneys.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections shall be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the Bermuda parent account or shareholder account to a licensed Gibraltar bank account. For GBP, use CHAPS via the Gibraltar bank's UK settlement agent; for USD, use Fedwire or correspondent-bank internal book transfer; for EUR, use SEPA Instant or TARGET2. The wire must reference 'SHARE CAPITAL / SUBSCRIPTION' or 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' in field 70, accompanied by a board resolution approving the injection, a certified statement of the exempted-company no-par-value capital, and a Form from the corporate services provider confirming the capital is not loan proceeds. For the patent-prosecution agency model, recurring fee receipts from applicants are not capital injections; they should be marked as 'CLIENT FUNDS / FILING FEES' and, if aggregated, must be held in a segregated client account. Where a Wise payout is used for a client's refund, the money trail must reconcile exactly from the receiving bank, through the client ledger, to the designated applicant wallet.Primary clearing rail for official patent-office payments is SWIFT MT103 to WIPO, USPTO, EPO, UKIPO, and national IP-office beneficiaries; each wire includes the official application or international registration number in instruction fields. Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) are not the standard clearing rail for patent prosecution fees and are generally limited to high-value annuities or bulk litigation settlements, advised through a Gibraltar confirming bank. Secondary rails include: SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for EUIPO and EPO, US domestic ACH or Fedwire for USPTO direct-pay, and Wise for minor renewal fees in low-value currencies, subject to the licensee maintaining audit reconciliation between the client file and the Wise transaction ID. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Medium for operational payment rails, Low for capital escrow and client-monies. Wise (natively suggested by the business model) and similar UK-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may be used for low-value disbursements of filing fees, annuity payments, and vendor invoices in USD, EUR, and GBP. Legal constraints: EMIs are not deposit-taking banks, do not hold client money as a licensed bank, and cannot provide formal escrow accounts, dedicated account segregation for regulated legal-client funds, or direct clearing through CHAPS/Fedwire under payment-banking credentials. A cross-border digital EMI account may be onboarded remotely using passport and corporate registry, but the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission AML regime requires that client monies relating to patent prosecution remain in a bank-designated client account or in a licensed bank as principal. Therefore, the blueprint must use an EMI only as a funding/collection ancillary ledger, never as the custody rail for capital, settlement reserves, or lawyer-client trust moneys.
