Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (official e-Tax portal: https://www.tax.gov.gi)

E-government filing portal

For a Bermuda Exempted Company registering in Gibraltar (whether as an overseas company or upon continuation under the Companies Act 2014, Cap. 196): (1) file a registration/continuation application with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies, obtaining a Gibraltar company number; (2) appoint a Gibraltar-registered office and local agent as required; (3) within 30 days of commencement or registration, submit Form 1 (Business/Tax Registration) to the Income Tax Office to obtain a Tax Reference Number (TRN), providing constitutional documents, registered office details, director identities, and expected income sources; (4) where the SPV confines its business to passive holding of subsidiary shares, apply for classification as a non-taxable non-resident or claim the statutory participation exemption for foreign dividends and gains; (5) if importing or exporting goods, separately obtain a Gibraltar EORI number from Gibraltar Customs. All steps are processed online through Companies House Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office e-Tax portal.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — For a Bermuda Exempted Company registering in Gibraltar (whether as an overseas company or upon continuation under the Companies Act 2014, Cap. 196): (1) file a registration/continuation application with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies, obtaining a Gibraltar company number; (2) appoint a Gibraltar-registered office and local agent as required; (3) within 30 days of commencement or registration, submit Form 1 (Business/Tax Registration) to the Income Tax Office to obtain a Tax Reference Number (TRN), providing constitutional documents, registered office details, director identities, and expected income sources; (4) where the SPV confines its business to passive holding of subsidiary shares, apply for classification as a non-taxable non-resident or claim the statutory participation exemption for foreign dividends and gains; (5) if importing or exporting goods, separately obtain a Gibraltar EORI number from Gibraltar Customs. All steps are processed online through Companies House Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office e-Tax portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime and has no hybrid-mismatch election comparable to BEPS Action 2. Available statutory elections under the Income Tax Act 2010 include: (a) participation-exemption election for dividends and capital gains on qualifying subsidiary shareholdings (minimum 10% interest held for at least 12 months, subsidiary subject to a qualifying tax rate of at least 5%); (b) group loss-relief election between Gibraltar-resident companies in the same group; (c) election for the reduced economic-substance compliance regime for pure equity holding companies; (d) for US federal purposes only, a single-member SPV may make an IRS Form 8832 check-the-box election to be treated as a disregarded entity, but this has no effect under Gibraltar law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No_General_Indirect_Tax (Gibraltar does not levy VAT, GST, or a general sales and use tax; it applies selective import and excise duties under the Customs Tariff Ordinance to specified goods such as alcohol, tobacco, fuel, and environmental levies).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT/GST; there is no turnover threshold because Gibraltar has no general consumption tax. For goods imported into Gibraltar, any importer (other than in de minimis personal baggage cases) must be EORI-registered and file an electronic customs declaration; import duty arises on the customs value per the Gibraltar Customs Tariff, with no de minimis threshold for commercial consignments.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT, so a classic export zero-rating mechanism does not exist. Goods physically exported from Gibraltar are relieved from Gibraltar import and excise duties, provided the customs export declaration, commercial invoice, and proof of departure are produced. Services supplied by a pure equity holding SPV (for example, dividend collection, treasury management, or financing facilitation) are outside the Gibraltar indirect tax net entirely. For direct-tax purposes, the substantive 'zero-rate' analogue is the participation exemption: foreign-source dividends and capital gains from qualifying subsidiaries are exempt from Gibraltar income tax where the non-resident or participation-exemption conditions are met.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document) linked to the exporter's EORI number
  • Commercial Invoice (original signed copy)
  • Packing List (for physical goods consignments)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing actual departure from Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate proving receipt of export proceeds in the qualifying bank account within the 180-day period
  • Certificate of Origin (where preferential origin treatment is claimed)

No real-time fiscal clearance, electronic invoicing mandate, digital stamping, or government API clearance is in force for general business or holding companies. Customs import/export declarations must be submitted electronically to Gibraltar Customs using the Single Administrative Document and the EORI framework; commercial invoicing remains governed by ordinary business rules and no state-integrated e-invoicing platform is required.

False export declarations and fraudulent zero-rating or participation-exemption claims are audited retrospectively by the Commissioner of Income Tax and Gibraltar Customs. Under the Income Tax Act 2010, negligent or fraudulent returns attract civil penalties of up to 100% of the tax underpaid, plus interest; criminal prosecution can lead to fines and imprisonment. The Commissioner may reopen assessments for up to 6 years (unlimited/20 years where fraud or wilful default is established). Customs law provides for seizure, forfeiture, and monetary penalties of up to three times the duty evaded for misdeclared exports. A pure equity holding company failing the reduced substance test under the Economic Substance (Companies) Regulations 2019 faces financial penalties up to GBP 10,000 per failure and potential strike-off. Any participation exemption or non-resident status obtained on incorrect documentation is clawed back retroactively with interest from the date the dividend or gain arose.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound is fully convertible, and Gibraltar does not operate exchange controls or provide a foreign-exchange licensing authority for current-account or capital-account transactions. A pure equity holding SPV may freely receive share-subscription capital from non-resident owners, retain dividends, and remit profits, dividends, returns of capital, or intercompany repayments without prior regulatory approval. Practical restrictions arise only through bank-level compliance at onboarding: the GFSC's Anti-Money Laundering Guidance Notes 2023 and the Tax Information Exchange Act require banks to verify the TIN, tax residence, and economic substance of the SPV before committing to a treasury mandate. For profit remittances, banks may require a board resolution authorising the dividend, a certificate of solvency if applicable, the Gibraltar Income Tax exemption designation, and an Economic Substance Return evidencing that the entity is not a shell conduit. There is no statutory foreign-exchange surrender, no exchange-control permit, and no hurdles to converting GBP, EUR, or USD balances held in the approved multi-currency account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Gibraltar has no statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, or retention rules under a centralised exchange-control regime. The ISIC 6420 pure equity holding SPV has no export trading revenue in its passive shareholder scope. If the SPV were to maintain a controlled foreign subsidiary with export sales, the proceeds could remain in any freely usable currency; only IFRS translation and transfer-pricing documentation would apply. No statutory timeline or conversion ratio exists for notified export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) together with the Government of Gibraltar (Financial Secretary); there is no separate central bank. The GFSC is the principal financial-services regulator for banking and payment institutions in Gibraltar, while the Government of Gibraltar issues and administers the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at a fixed 1:1 parity with pound sterling (GBP). The Bank of England does not exercise direct regulatory or exchange-control authority over the Gibraltar market.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, usable as an auxiliary settlement corridor but not as a substitute for a licensed commercial bank. Offshore digital EMI solutions (UK or EU e-money institutions, payment-facilitation platforms) may provide FX-friendly virtual IBANs and euro/USD clearing rails for a Gibraltar-domiciled SPV; however, most EMIs apply enhanced AML restrictions to Bermuda exempted companies and Gibraltar-passported structures, refuse to act as a primary treasury account, and will require a source-of-funds pack and a substantive tax-ruling before onboarding. Statutes such as Gibraltar's Financial Services (Payment Services) Regulations 2018 and UK/EU E-Money Regulations do not authorise EMI deposits to constitute bank deposits, and EMI holding does not create evidence of economic substance. The SPV treasury mandate should therefore be built on a main GFSC-licensed bank line, with EMIs used only for routing non-preserved, small-value operational payments.
