Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate or continue the Bermuda Exempted Company into Gibraltar at the Gibraltar Companies House (Companies Act 2014) to obtain the Gibraltar Company Number. Step 2: File the prescribed tax registration application with the Income Tax Office within 30 days of commencing activity, alongside certified incorporation documents, memorandum and articles, registered-office consent, and director details, to obtain the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN/TIN). Step 3: Open a Customs Management System (CMS) operator account with HM Customs Gibraltar if the company physically imports or exports ECG monitor units. A non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company must register only if it establishes a Gibraltar permanent establishment or derives Gibraltar-source income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box or entity classification election exists in Gibraltar; the former exempt-company regime was abolished for new entrants in 2010. Statutory elections available: (1) group assessment election under the Income Tax Act 2010 to consolidate profits/losses of a 51%-owned group; (2) election to transfer capital allowances between associated companies; (3) joint assessment of connected entities with Commissioner approval. Hybrid mismatch rules transposing OECD BEPS Action 2 apply mandatorily, disallowing deduction/non-inclusion mismatches with no opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Purchase Tax - a customs import duty on tariff-listed goods; no VAT or GST system operates. Gibraltar is excluded from the EU/UK VAT area (Article 28, Directive 2006/112/EC, as retained post-Brexit).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold applies. Purchase tax is chargeable per importation of specific tariff lines irrespective of annual revenue. Every company carrying on business in Gibraltar must obtain income-tax registration regardless of turnover; indirect-tax registration is effected through the Customs Management System (CMS) operator account.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - Gibraltar has no VAT and therefore no 0% export rating, input-VAT credit, or refund mechanism. ISIC 3250 ECG patient monitors are outside the Gibraltar purchase-tax tariff; physical exports to non-Gibraltar destinations are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation and carry no indirect tax burden to recover.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (SAD/CMS electronic movement record)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (GB/GI preferential origin certificate, where claimed)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / TT wire settlement evidence
  • Medical Device Regulatory File (UKCA/CE technical documentation for the ECG patient monitor)

No mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API reporting, or digital stamping regime exists in Gibraltar. Conventional paper/PDF invoicing is sufficient; Gibraltar Customs receives import/export declarations through the CMS but does not require per-invoice real-time clearance.

HM Customs Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office may audit export declarations, shipping records, and FX repatriation within a six-year assessment window. Fraudulent or unsupported export documentation triggers retrospective clawback of all relieved amounts, monetary penalties up to 100% of tax undercharged (customs penalties may aggregate to multiples of duty evaded, plus forfeiture of goods), statutory interest, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at 1:1 to GBP and is freely convertible into GBP, EUR, USD, and other major currencies through GFSC-licensed banks. Gibraltar maintains a fully liberalised capital account with no foreign-exchange approval thresholds, conversion quotas, or Central Bank consent requirements for direct inward investment, trade payments, or dividend/profit remittance to the Bermudan parent. Remittance is unrestricted provided the entity maintains current filings with the Gibraltar Registrar under the Companies Act 2014 and the Companies (Substance Requirements) Act 2020, and banks receive audited financial statements, board resolutions, and the underlying remittance instruction to evidence a lawful source of funds. No Gibraltar withholding tax, exchange-control exit charge, or balance-of-payments registration applies to the Bermuda Exempted Company's outward profit or intercompany settlement flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable as Gibraltar imposes no export-proceeds surrender, mandatory repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio on export receipts. The ISIC 3250 ECG monitor exporter may retain USD, EUR, or GBP proceeds in a Gibraltar multi-currency account or sweep them to the Bermudan treasury via SWIFT. GFSC record-keeping rules nonetheless require each inward and outward remittance to be supported by the underlying commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, and CE/UKCA/Medical Device Regulation documentation; a minimum documentary retention period of five years applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary anchor for the sterling area) and H.M. Government of Gibraltar (issuer of Gibraltar pound notes under the Currency Notes Act 1934); prudential regulation and AML/CFT supervision of licensed financial institutions by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium viability with strict limitation: GFSC-licensed money-service providers and DLT/digital-wealth firms (e.g., Xapo Bank) or UK FCA-authorised EMIs (Wise Business, Revolut Business) can be used for low-value collection flows, payroll, and multi-currency wallet settlement with virtual IBANs; they cannot issue or receive documentary letters of credit, provide trade-finance advances, or act as the primary settlement rail for high-value ISIC 3250 serial device exports. Regulatory constraints: UK EMIs lost EU-27 passporting after Brexit, and Gibraltar's domestic regulated-activities regime under the Financial Services Act 2019 does not grant a full EMI licence equivalent to the EEA Electronic Money Institutions Directive; therefore, a Gibraltar Tier-1 licensed bank remains mandatory for LC and material SWIFT MT103 settlements.
