Partial, industry-specific determination: Bermuda requires the exempted company to maintain a registered office at a physical address in Bermuda (normally the licensed corporate services provider's premises); a purely digital mailbox with no physical situs is non-compliant with s. 11A of the Companies Act 1981 and the BMA's beneficial-ownership recordkeeping expectations. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Bermuda: no VAT/GST/sales tax. Gibraltar: no VAT/GST (a narrow 'purchases tax' on imported alcohol, tobacco and fuel is outside the software business scope). UK: HM Revenue & Customs VAT at 20% applies only when the Bermuda entity makes supplies with a UK place of supply (B2C digital services) or where a digital platform acts as merchant of record.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Bermuda: not applicable (no indirect tax). Gibraltar: not applicable (no VAT). UK: GBP 90,000 annual taxable turnover threshold for UK-established suppliers; for a non-established Bermuda entity making B2C digital supplies into the UK, registration is required from the first supply (GBP 0 effective threshold) via the UK OSS; B2B digital supplies to UK VAT-registered customers are outside UK VAT scope under the reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Bermuda/Gibraltar: neither jurisdiction operates a VAT/GST system, so no export zero-rating claim or refund procedure exists; software license and middleware service exports are inherently outside the scope of indirect tax. UK context: B2B supplies of the anti-cheat middleware to non-UK customers are 'outside the scope' of UK VAT under Section 7A VATA 1994 (place of supply of services), economically equivalent to zero-rating. Substantiation requires (i) contract evidence that the recipient is a business established outside the UK, (ii) a valid foreign VAT ID or tax residency certificate, (iii) the contract designating the place of supply, and (iv) auditable consideration records; proportionate UK input VAT recovery on local procurement is available only where the entity makes taxable supplies in the UK.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/C88) where physical goods are exported
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or Swift credit advice) evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Signed software license / anti-cheat middleware agreement identifying the non-UK recipient and place of supply
  • Foreign customer VAT registration number or tax residency certificate

Bermuda and Gibraltar impose no electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping mandates. Under UK VAT, a VAT invoice must be issued within 30 days of the tax point; no HMRC pre-clearance or fiscal stamping applies, but Making Tax Digital requires electronic record-keeping and quarterly API returns for UK-established businesses. OSS digital-service returns (if B2C sales arise) are filed quarterly online; Stripe 'merchant of record' terms should be documented to fix VAT liability, as the payment rail itself creates no fiscal clearance obligation.

Bermuda: no VAT exists to claw back; the principal exposure arises under the Economic Substance Act 2018, with penalties up to BMD 250,000 plus BMD 10,000 per day of continuing default, and potential strike-off for failure to demonstrate substance. Gibraltar: no VAT clawback; misapplied qualifying-activity claims invite retrospective assessment at the 10% standard rate, penalties up to 100% of tax underpaid, and director disqualification. UK: wrongful zero-rating/outside-the-scope claims expose the entity to HMRC assessments for up to 4 years (20 years for deliberate concealment), penalties up to 100% of the VAT loss, default interest, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The GIP is freely convertible into GBP at a fixed 1:1 peg with no exchange controls. Gibraltar operates an open capital account and an unrestricted current account, permitting full repatriation of dividends, royalties, branch profits, and liquidation proceeds from a Bermuda Exempted Company without central bank approval or tax clearance. Bermuda abolished exchange controls for exempted companies in 1993. The only practical limitations are GFSC/FATF-aligned AML/CFT screening, verifiable source of funds, and economic-substance documentation; no mandatory conversion or profit-remittance hurdle exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar imposes no export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. Receipts from the gaming anti-cheat engine (licence fees, subscriptions, integration services, Stripe settlements) may be retained indefinitely in GIP, GBP, EUR, or USD in multi-currency treasury accounts. Bermuda's exchange-control regulations do not apply to exempted companies, and there is no requirement to repatriate offshore earnings to either Bermuda or Gibraltar.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the competent licensing, prudential and AML/CFT authority for all banks, EMIs, and payment institutions in Gibraltar. There is no central bank in Gibraltar; the Government of Gibraltar, acting through the Currency Commissioner under the Currency Notes Act 1934, issues Gibraltar pound (GIP) notes fully backed by Bank of England sterling. The Bank of England provides the monetary policy anchor and ultimate liquidity support, while the GFSC supervises corporate treasury, FX, and clearing activities.

Medium. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity is injected via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 COV for high-value cover payments) from the shareholder's bank to the Capital Escrow Account at the Gibraltar bank, using ISO 20022 transaction purpose code 'EQUI' (Equity Capital) or 'CBLA' (Capital for Business Line); a shareholder loan uses 'LEND'. The remittance instruction must reference the board capital-call resolution and the company's Bermuda registry number. The receiving bank holds funds in a restricted escrow until Compliance completes source-of-funds sign-off, after which the balance is swept into the multi-currency operational treasury; USD legs may be converted through spot FX at the 1:1 GIP/GBP peg without conversion friction.Outbound settlements for the anti-cheat middleware business execute through: SWIFT MT103 for international licence fees, hosting and subcontractor payments; SWIFT MT202 COV for interbank nostro cover; irrevocable documentary letters of credit (UCP600) issued by the Gibraltar bank for major GPU and kernel-testing hardware procurement; CHAPS or Faster Payments for UK/GIP domestic disbursements and payroll; SEPA/TARGET2 for Eurozone vendor payments; and the Stripe acquirer net-settlement rail for card receipts, zero-balanced daily into the primary treasury account.Spot FX contracts for same-day conversion between GIP, GBP, EUR, and USDOutright FX forward contracts (one to twelve months) hedging order-backed licence and subscription revenuesCross-currency swaps (XCCY) between GIP/GBP and USD/EUR for balance-sheet hedging of the Bermudian capital baseFX zero-cost collars to cap conversion costs on multi-year gaming-operator receivables within a defined corridorNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are out-of-policy and unnecessary because a fully deliverable GIP/GBP market exists at the official 1:1 peg

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium. GFSC-, UK- or EEA-licensed EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may be used as secondary collection rails for a Bermuda Exempted Company, but they cannot serve as the primary treasury bank because they do not provide documentary credits, GIP/GBP peg custody, FX-forward lines, or clearing-eligible escrow. Legal constraints: EMIs classify a Bermudian offshore entity with high-velocity gaming-sector revenue as medium risk, imposing velocity caps, strict source-of-funds evidence, and restrictions on gambling-adjacent interchange. Funds must be swept daily to a licensed Gibraltar bank.
