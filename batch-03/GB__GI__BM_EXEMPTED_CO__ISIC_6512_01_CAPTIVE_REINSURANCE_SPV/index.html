Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Gibraltar Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar, 9-10 Prison Lane, Gibraltar GX11 1AA; corporate tax and General Consumption Tax administered through the Gibraltar Tax & Customs e-services portal (https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi); captive insurance licensing regulated by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (https://www.fsc.gi).

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda exempted company intending to operate as a captive reinsurance SPV in Gibraltar must: (1) obtain a restricted captive insurance licence from the GFSC under the Gibraltar Insurance Act; (2) register the branch or continuation with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies to obtain the Gibraltar Company/Branch Reference Number; (3) file Form T with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office within 30 days of commencing business to obtain a Gibraltar corporate Tax Identification Number, supported by a local registered office and a Gibraltar-resident manager; and (4) register for GCT where taxable supplies exceed the threshold, securing a separate GCT registration number. Non-resident ownership is unrestricted; no statutory minimum capital is imposed under general company law, but the GFSC imposes solvency capital requirements consistent with adapted Solvency II principles.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (a) the Bermuda Tax Exemption Certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 guaranteeing exemption from Bermuda taxes until 2035; (b) the Gibraltar 'qualifying company' election under the Income Tax Act 2010 restricting taxation to Gibraltar-source income where beneficial ownership is non-resident and management/control is outside Gibraltar; (c) the captive insurance special election permitting deduction of technical reserves and claims provisions; (d) the US 'check-the-box' election on Form 8832 to classify the single-member SPV as a disregarded entity for US federal tax purposes; and (e) UK/Gibraltar hybrid-mismatch transparency elections under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A to neutralise unintended double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 100,000 annual value of taxable supplies (GCT Act 2015, as amended); insurance and reinsurance premium income is an exempt supply, so the captive is not obliged to register unless it separately supplies taxable services such as fee-based risk-management or captive-management services; exempt status restricts input GCT recovery under partial exemption rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Reinsurance services supplied by the Gibraltar captive to a non-resident cedent or group parent qualify for zero-rating or are outside the scope of GCT provided the recipient belongs outside Gibraltar, the risk is underwritten offshore, and the benefit of the services is enjoyed outside Gibraltar; physical exports of goods are zero-rated upon proof of departure. Because insurance is an exempt supply, input tax recovery is generally denied unless the service is properly documented as an exported service; contemporaneous treaty, servicing, and premium-settlement evidence is mandatory.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/C88 lodged through the Gibraltar/UK customs electronic system) for any physical assets or collateral exported
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement out of Gibraltar where goods are involved
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing premium collection from the non-resident cedent
  • Executed reinsurance treaty and bordereaux evidencing offshore risk assumption
  • Tax residency certificate or non-resident status declaration of the cedent and beneficial owners
  • Completed GCT zero-rating schedule and quarterly returns filed with Gibraltar Tax & Customs

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or continuous transaction control mandate is in force in Gibraltar; GCT returns are filed electronically on a quarterly cycle through the Gibraltar Tax & Customs e-services portal; customs declarations for goods movement are lodged via the Gibraltar electronic customs system, with invoice trail and SWIFT/UETR payment evidence retained for audit purposes.

Gibraltar Tax & Customs may assess within the statutory time limits (generally 6 years, extended in the case of fraud or negligent conduct) and claw back incorrect zero-rating refunds with statutory interest; civil penalties of up to 100% of the tax evaded and criminal fines or imprisonment apply under the GCT Act 2015 and the Customs & Excise Act; the GFSC may revoke the captive licence and disqualify directors on fit-and-proper grounds; UK transfer-pricing and hybrid-mismatch adjustments may additionally trigger HMRC restitution and penalty action.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains open current and capital account convertibility. The Gibraltar pound is pegged at par to the British pound, and there are no exchange controls, no repatriation permits, and no restrictions on profit or dividend flows. A captive reinsurance SPV may freely receive capital in GBP, EUR, or USD and may remit claims, fronting fees, dividends, and reinsurance premiums subject only to standard banking due diligence, sanctions screening, and documented commercial purpose. Non-resident ownership does not trigger convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Gibraltar imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to deposit insurance or reinsurance premium income with the monetary authority. Reinsurance premium inflows are treated as services-sector income rather than merchandise export proceeds and are therefore outside the scope of any export-proceeds surrender framework. The bank will nevertheless conduct source-of-funds and sanctions verification on all inbound premium recoveries.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the competent supervisory and licensing authority for banks and captive insurers. There is no separate Gibraltar central bank; the Government of Gibraltar is responsible for Gibraltar pound note issuance, and the monetary anchor is the British pound under a currency-board-style arrangement, with HM Treasury and the Bank of England exercising broader UK monetary and sanctions functions applicable to Sterling clearing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Licensed electronic-money institutions can provide ancillary collection and disbursement rails for low-value premium payments, but they are not acceptable custodians of captive insurance reserves or minimum capital. An offshore digital EMI with no physical or regulated presence in Gibraltar will generally be refused by GFSC-supervised banks as a primary treasury counterparty. The captive must maintain statutory reserves and capital in a fully licensed Gibraltar or UK bank, with any EMI used only as a pass-through payment mechanism subject to contractual audit rights and transaction-level reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial equity injection from the Bermuda parent or ultimate group treasury should be routed by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfer or MT202 bank-to-bank cover, denominated in GBP, EUR, or USD, directly to the Gibraltar bank account. The remittance instruction must reference the company name, the board resolution authorising the capital injection, and the bank's purpose code for direct investment/equity capitalisation, which in practice corresponds to the IMF BPM6 direct investment equity category. The bank will verify the remitter's account title, legal existence, source of funds, and absence of sanctions exposure. For USD transfers, clearing will pass through the US correspondent system, so the payment path must respect OFAC sanctions and avoid any sanctioned or blocked financial institution.Outbound reinsurance claims, fronting fees, ceded premiums, and operating expenses should be settled by SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers, with MT202 interbank cover where needed. GBP payments should clear through CHAPS real-time gross settlement; EUR through TARGET2 or SEPA Credit Transfer via a correspondent; and USD through Fedwire via the US correspondent. Commercial letters of credit are not appropriate for reinsurance disbursements because reinsurance settlements are documentary indemnity payments rather than trade-in-goods transactions; a standby letter of credit may, however, support fronting arrangements where security is required. All outbound payments must pass sanctions screening and must not involve sanctioned jurisdictions, persons, or shipping entities.Outright FX spot and forward contracts maturity-matched to premium and claims currency exposureRolling and extendable FX forwards to hedge forecast reserve cash flowsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies lacking deliverable local market liquidity, where the underlying obligation is a genuine hedge and the counterparty is a regulated bankCross-currency swaps (CCS) for long-term liability matching between GIP/GBP, EUR, and USDPlain-vanilla currency options only where expressly documented as risk-reducing and approved under the captive's prudent risk-management policy

0% — Gibraltar law imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar-resident company to its Bermuda Exempted Company shareholder or any non-resident; distributions may be made gross.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Gibraltar Local File is required where controlled cross-border transactions exceed GBP 1,000,000 aggregate in a period (or GBP 250,000 for financing, services or intangible transactions); a Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds GBP 100,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting applies at the OECD threshold of EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Income Tax (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019, aligned with the EU Code of Conduct Group and OECD standards, classify captive insurance and reinsurance as a relevant activity requiring substantive presence and governance in Gibraltar: core income-generating activities (underwriting, reserving, risk assessment, claims management) must be undertaken by qualified local staff; board meetings must be physically held in Gibraltar with real decision-making; expenditure and headcount must be proportionate to the underwriting portfolio; full outsourcing defeats substance, exposes the captive to a UK permanent establishment, and permits HMRC automatic exchange of information under the Gibraltar-HMRC cooperation agreement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Under-declaration

Statutory sanction: Assessment on the true profits, interest at 0.5% monthly, penalties up to 200% of the evaded tax under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, licence revocation by the GFSC, and criminal prosecution before the Supreme Court of Gibraltar.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where directors sign false returns or cause the SPV to be used for tax evasion; directors and the authorised captive manager face civil restitution for unpaid tax, travel bans, disqualification from UK and Gibraltar directorships, and imprisonment of up to 7 years.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance penalties escalating from GBP 10,000 to GBP 250,000, strike-off from the Gibraltar Companies Register, GFSC licence revocation, automatic exchange of information with HMRC, and re-attribution of all Gibraltar profits to the UK under the CFC rules with up to a 25% UK CFC charge.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for substance penalties and any unpaid UK CFC tax attributed to the group; sham doctrine invalidates the corporate structure, exposing directors to disqualification orders, travel restrictions pending HMRC enquiry, and imprisonment for fraudulent incorporation.
Money Laundering and AML Framework Breach

Statutory sanction: Unlimited fines, forfeiture of the underwritten reserve assets, suspension of the insurance licence, and imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering offences; the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit may issue freezing and charging orders.

Director personal liability: The Money Laundering Reporting Officer and all directors with relevant knowledge are personally criminally liable; civil restitution of laundered funds is ordered against directors, travel bans are imposed pending trial, and imprisonment of up to 14 years applies to wilful participation.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and Dividend Disguise

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustments restating profits to arm's length, additional Gibraltar CIT at 10%, interest, a 50% penalty on the adjusted under-declared profits, and UK GAAR counteraction of 60% of the attributable tax benefit.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve sham fee arrangements are jointly liable for the adjusted tax, face civil restitution to the revenue authorities, are subject to travel bans during multi-jurisdictional tax investigations, and may be imprisoned for fraudulent evasion upon criminal conviction.
Regulatory Capital and Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: GFSC administrative fines of up to GBP 1,000,000, prohibition orders against the captive, suspension of new business, compulsory liquidation, and disqualification from acting in the Gibraltar insurance sector.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for regulatory restitution to policyholders, can be barred from Gibraltar and UK financial services roles, may be subject to travel bans pending insolvency proceedings, and face imprisonment for trading whilst insolvent.
