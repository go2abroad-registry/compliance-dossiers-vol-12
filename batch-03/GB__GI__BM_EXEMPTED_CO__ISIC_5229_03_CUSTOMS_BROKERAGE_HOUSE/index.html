Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar (British Overseas Territory): Government of Gibraltar - Income Tax Office (portal: https://www.income-tax.gi) for direct tax registration and Tax Reference Numbers; HM Customs Gibraltar / Customs and Excise Department (portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/customs) for VAT, excise and customs administration. Bermuda home jurisdiction: Bermuda Registrar of Companies and the Ministry of Finance (no income tax is levied on exempted companies).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Bermuda: incorporate the Exempted Company under the Companies Act 1981 with the Bermuda Registrar of Companies; obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and CRN, then apply for the Minister of Finance Certificate of Exemption; file the annual economic substance declaration and pay the annual company fee. No Bermuda TIN is issued because no income tax applies. Step 2 - Gibraltar branch: register the overseas company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry (Companies Act 2014, Part XX) as an external company; file certified constitutional documents and appoint a Gibraltar-resident agent authorised to accept service. Step 3 - direct tax: submit the company registration form to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office to obtain a Tax Reference Number (TRN); a non-resident company with a Gibraltar permanent establishment files returns only on PE-attributable profits. Step 4 - VAT: register with HM Customs Gibraltar using the Gibraltar VAT registration form once GIP 100,000 annual taxable turnover is exceeded (or voluntarily); a 'GI'-prefixed VAT reference number is issued and import VAT applies regardless of threshold. Step 5 - regulatory: obtain the Gibraltar customs brokerage licence and, given the medium-risk enhanced AML tier, complete the applicable AML/CDD registration under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and Gibraltar AML/CFT legislation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - the Bermuda Exempted Company is statutorily exempt from all Bermuda taxes (income, capital gains, withholding and stamp) under the Bermuda Companies Act 1981; for Gibraltar tax purposes it is an external/non-resident company taxed at the 12.5% standard corporation tax rate only on profits attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment or on profits where central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar.

Available election forms

Gibraltar follows the OECD hybrid mismatch rules and ATAD 1/2; no exempt-company election or hybrid mismatch election is available. Available elections: (1) an electable non-resident/branch treatment where central management and control is substantiated outside Gibraltar; (2) Gibraltar group relief elections between qualifying foreign group companies; and (3) a US shareholder-level check-the-box (Form 8832) classification for the Bermuda entity. The Bermuda exemption is automatic upon issuance of the Ministerial Certificate of Exemption and requires no annual tax election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 100,000 (Gibraltar pound, at par with GBP) annual taxable turnover in Gibraltar; registration is mandatory above the threshold and voluntary below it; import VAT is chargeable irrespective of the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to: (a) exports of goods physically removed from Gibraltar to a third country or special territory, evidenced by customs exit confirmation within 90 days of supply; (b) services directly connected with exported goods, including customs brokerage, handling, packing, freight and insurance, while the goods are under customs control; (c) international transport services; and (d) B2B services supplied to a non-Gibraltar VAT-registered recipient under the place-of-supply-of-recipient rules, provided the customer's VAT registration number is obtained and verified.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CDS/SAD Ex.1) bearing the Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list, together with the customs brokerage agreement or power of attorney
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Airway Bill (air) evidencing physical removal from Gibraltar
  • Customs authority proof of exit and the 'date of export' record
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank finality statement matching the export proceeds
  • Customer VAT registration evidence (for place-of-supply zero-rating of B2B services)

No continuous transaction control, real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory e-invoicing applies to domestic B2B VAT invoices in Gibraltar; electronic invoices are accepted. Licensed customs brokers must, however: (i) file all export/import declarations electronically through the Gibraltar Customs e-Declaration portal and the national single window, interoperable with HMRC CDS and the EU ICS2/MRN environment; (ii) transmit pre-arrival and exit pre-notification datasets via approved API/B2G interfaces; (iii) apply digital duty stamps to excise goods moving under duty suspension; and (iv) retain VAT invoices, customs declarations and export evidence for at least six years.

HM Customs Gibraltar may claw back any VAT zero-rated without the complete documentary bundle, together with statutory default interest and penalties of up to 100% of the VAT amount. Fraudulent zero-rating, false CDS declarations, circular transit arrangements or forged export evidence are criminal offences under the Value Added Tax Act 2021 and the Customs Management Act 2010, punishable by fines, director disqualification and imprisonment of up to 7 years; the customs brokerage licence may be suspended or revoked and the entity placed under enhanced audit status.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP is fully convertible and irrevocably pegged at par with GBP; GBP banknotes and coins are legal tender in Gibraltar and circulate interchangeably, resulting in a de facto zero convertibility barrier. The capital account is fully liberalised: there are no exchange controls, no restrictions on the repatriation of capital or profit, and Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, or branch remittances. Customs brokerage fees, bonded-transit settlements, and trade disbursements may be invoiced and settled in GIP, GBP, EUR, USD, or any freely convertible currency. The Bermuda Exempted Company, being designated non-resident for Bermuda exchange-control purposes, is free to operate foreign-currency bank accounts in Gibraltar without BMA approval; no current-account or capital-account constraints apply to the Gibraltar treasury.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation obligation for export or brokerage proceeds; all trade receipts may be retained in the currency of collection. Under the Bermuda Exchange Control Act 1972 and subsidiary regulations, a Bermuda Exempted Company that is designated non-resident for exchange control is exempt from all surrender and conversion duties, provided it does not transact business in Bermuda dollars or maintain in-island resident accounts. Consequently, export proceeds earned through the Gibraltar customs brokerage operation attract zero surrender or conversion exposure.

Central bank supervision: Financial Services Commission (Gibraltar) (GFSC) as the integrated financial regulator; the Government of Gibraltar, through the Gibraltar Currency Commission, as the statutory currency-issuing authority maintaining the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) at strict 1:1 parity with Pound Sterling, with backing sterling reserves held via the Bank of England; the Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) as the exchange-control authority applicable to the ultimate Bermuda Exempted Company under the Bermuda Exchange Control Act 1972.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High for operational transaction flows: Gibraltar-licensed electronic money institutions under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Regulations 2020, notably Transact Payments Ltd (TPL) and ANNA Money, can issue GI-prefixed IBANs, hold client funds in safeguarded accounts, and execute cross-border ACH/SEPA-format and SWIFT-adjacent API rails; Xapo Bank Ltd, a GFSC-licensed digital bank, additionally provides multi-currency wallets and FX execution. Low for fiduciary-grade treasury functions: EMIs are not full commercial banks and therefore cannot lawfully operate capital escrow accounts, issue documentary credits (MT700), provide customs-bond guarantees, or extend revolving credit; all escrow, LC, and guarantee instruments must remain with a Tier-1 GFSC-licensed bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% standard Gibraltar corporate income tax rate; a non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company with no Gibraltar permanent establishment is exempt on non-Gibraltar-source income and may effectively bear a 0% rate

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% – Gibraltar does not impose withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar-resident company to any shareholder, resident or non-resident; Bermuda exempt companies also pay distributions with no Bermuda dividend withholding tax

Return must be filed no later than 12 months after the end of the accounting period, or within 30 days of a written notice to file, whichever is later; self-assessed tax is due nine months after the end of the accounting period

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File and Transfer Pricing documentation is mandatory where aggregate controlled transactions (brokerage sub-commissions, management fees, recharges, loans and IP royalties) exceed £1,000,000 per annum; Master File is required where the worldwide group turnover exceeds £750,000,000 or group controlled transactions exceed EUR 10,000,000

Economic substance scope: Applicable. As a Bermuda Exempted Company, the entity falls within the Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018; customs brokerage and bonded transit management are treated as a 'distribution and service centre' relevant activity if services are provided to related parties, or as 'logistics' if the entity holds the underlying bonded transit contracts. The company must file an annual Economic Substance return with the Bermuda Registrar and maintain real physical presence in Bermuda. Separately, the Gibraltar Economic Substance (Companies and Partnerships) Act 2020 applies to Gibraltar tax-resident companies carrying out relevant activities: the brokerage must prove premises, full-time employees, board meetings, and core income-generating activity in the place of alleged tax residence. Outsourcing to a Gibraltar licensed customs agent does not satisfy the substance test unless the outsource arrangement is supervised and the entity retains operational control

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 200% of the tax evaded plus quarterly default interest at 1.5%; criminal fines at the discretion of the Gibraltar Supreme Court; confiscation of the proceeds of crime under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; loss of the customs brokerage licence

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the Bermuda resident secretary who 'knowingly concerned' in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; directors face imprisonment of up to 5 years, travel bans, surrender of passports, and disqualification from holding directorships for up to 15 years under the Gibraltar Companies Act
Transfer Pricing Misprizing / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Compulsory transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's length position; disallowance of the overstated deductions; penalty equal to 100% of the resulting tax differential, increasing to 150% for deliberate transfer pricing errors; interest accruing at 1.5% per quarter on the underpayment

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved the transfer pricing documentation and related-party invoices are personally liable for penalties if the documents are found to be knowingly false; the Financial Services Commission may impose a ban on serving as a director of any licensed customs brokerage; restitution orders may be issued in favour of the tax authority
Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018: initial penalty of BMD $10,000, increased to BMD $50,000 for continuing default, with a potential 5-year prison term for directors or officers; Gibraltar Economic Substance Act: financial penalty up to £250,000 and compulsory strike-off of the Gibraltar branch; automatic exchange of ESR information under the OECD/JITSIC arrangements

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for false ESR declarations and for the company's failure to maintain substance; enforcement includes travel bans, notification to the Bermuda and Gibraltar registries, disqualification orders, and imprisonment for up to 5 years for knowingly making a false declaration on the ESR return
Unlicensed Customs Brokerage and AML/CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to £500,000 imposed by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission; immediate revocation or suspension of the customs brokerage licence; forfeiture of brokerage fees derived from the unlawful activity; criminal prosecution under the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Regulations 2019 and the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015

Director personal liability: Directors and the nominated Money Laundering Reporting Officer are personally liable for systems-and-control failures, with fines up to £50,000 each; individuals convicted of money laundering face imprisonment of up to 14 years; directors may be subject to travel bans, asset freezing orders, and GFSC public censure
Disguised Profit Repatriation / Constructive Dividend

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the payment as a constructive dividend or taxable profit distribution; disallowance of the corresponding deduction; penalty of up to 100% of the amount mischaracterised; late-payment interest; automatic notification to the U.K. and Bermuda tax authorities under the exchange of information framework

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable to restore unlawful distributions to the company under the insolvency provisions of the Gibraltar Companies Act; civil restitution orders may be enforced against personal assets; travel bans and director disqualification orders of up to 15 years may be imposed, and criminal charges arise where the disguised repatriation is linked to defrauding creditors or tax evasion
