Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda exempt status preserved; in Gibraltar the company is treated as a non-resident foreign corporation with Territorial_Scope taxation limited to Gibraltar-source income; foreign-source subscription-box revenue is not taxed unless attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment or remitted to a Gibraltar branch).

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists under Gibraltar law; the Bermuda Exempted Company is a per se opaque corporation. Available mechanisms: (i) formal application to the Income Tax Office for a determination of non-residence / absence of permanent establishment; (ii) group-relief loss-transfer election between Gibraltar-resident group members; (iii) capital allowance pooling and de-minimis election; (iv) CRS/FATCA self-certification election classifying the entity as a Passive NFE (no Gibraltar financial account is opened unless an EMI/bank requests the certification).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and operates no VAT, GST, or general sales tax; indirect taxation is limited to customs/import duties (UK-aligned Gibraltar tariff) and excise duties on alcohol, tobacco, and mineral oils.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable: no VAT/GST registration threshold exists in Gibraltar. Customs: EORI registration is mandatory upon first import/export with no turnover threshold; excise license required for handling alcohol, tobacco, or fuel products.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - Gibraltar levies no VAT, so there is no 0% export VAT rate and no VAT zero-rating or refund claim. Subscription-box exports (ISIC 4791 recurring physical merchandise) dispatched from Gibraltar to UK/EU/third-country consumers are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax entirely. If goods were previously imported into Gibraltar under duty-relief (e.g., inward processing), the relief must be discharged by documented export to avoid customs duty clawback.

  • Customs Export Declaration - UK/Gibraltar SAD (C88) or CDS export entry
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (verifiable departure from Gibraltar/UK customs territory)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT credit confirmation matching export proceeds to the Bermuda Exempted Company's bank account
  • Commercial Invoice (itemized description, HS codes, Incoterms, declared value)
  • Packing List and Certificate of Origin (non-preferential; EUR.1 only if exported to the EU under an applicable preferential arrangement)

No real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate applies in Gibraltar. Commercial invoices must state issuer/recipient details, HS codes, quantity, and price per general commercial law, and must be retained for tax audit. Customs declarations are filed electronically through the UK/Gibraltar Customs Declaration Service (CDS) or compatible EDI, but there is no government pre-clearance or API stamping of sales invoices.

Gibraltar Income Tax Office may open assessments for 6 years (extendable to 21 years for fraud or negligent misstatement); artificial routing of subscription revenue through the Bermuda entity or mischaracterization of foreign-source income can trigger re-determination of Gibraltar residency or source, resulting in 12.5% corporate tax, interest, and penalties up to 100% of underpaid tax. Customs false declarations, mis-described goods, or improper discharge of inward-processing relief attract penalty of up to £5,000 per declaration, seizure/forfeiture of goods, and criminal liability for fraudulent evasion of duty. No VAT clawback exists because no VAT was levied in Gibraltar.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains no exchange-control restrictions; both the Gibraltar pound (GIP) and the Pound Sterling (GBP) are legal tender and circulate interchangeably within the jurisdiction. Capital account transactions (foreign direct investment, subscription for shares, intercompany loans) and current account transactions (trade settlements, profit remittances, dividends, royalty and fee payments) are freely convertible without central bank approval, surrender obligations, or quantitative limits. A Bermuda Exempted Company operating from Gibraltar may hold, denominate and settle balances freely in GBP, GIP, USD, EUR and other major currencies. Profit and dividend remittance to shareholders is unrestricted provided the company meets its local filing and taxation obligations; no withholding tax applies to dividend distributions in Gibraltar to non-resident shareholders. Practical hurdles are limited to banking counterparty due-diligence, sanctions screening and the requirement that the economic substance of the operation be demonstrable in Gibraltar to avoid classification as a shell or low-substance entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds in Gibraltar. Gibraltar law does not impose any requirement to convert, surrender, or remit export receipts within a specified period, nor does it prescribe the currency in which such proceeds must be held. Receipts from the subscription box merchandising business may be retained in the original currency of invoicing (typically USD and EUR via card acquirers) or converted into GBP/GIP at the corporate treasury's discretion. The only relevant obligations are commercial and AML-adjacent: full traceability of the underlying trade documentation, accurate accounting entries supporting the source of funds, and maintenance of adequate records for Gibraltar Income Tax Act assessment and GFSC-supervised anti-money-laundering reviews.

Central bank supervision: Government of Gibraltar (HM Government of Gibraltar) acting as the monetary and currency authority under the Currency Notes Act, issuing the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at a fixed 1:1 parity with the Pound Sterling; banking supervision, conduct-of-business regulation and licensing are vested in the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Banking Act. The Bank of England serves as the monetary authority for the United Kingdom itself and is not the statutory central bank for Gibraltar, although GIP is fully backed by, and freely convertible into, GBP sterling.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for the e-money institution (EMI) channel, subject to strict limits. Gibraltar is not a participant in the EEA single-market passport after Brexit, so a UK FCA-authorised EMI (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex) or a GFSC-licensed EMI must be used through the mutual recognition framework between the United Kingdom and Gibraltar. The EMI channel is viable as a payment concentration, multi-currency FX and low-value disbursement layering tool for the subscription box traffic fed by Stripe Billing and Authorize.Net, but it must not be used as the primary regulated treasury depository for capital injection or long-term liquidity. Legal constraints include: (i) EMIs are not banks and cannot hold deposits taking; funds must be safeguarded in the EMI's segregated trust/safeguarding accounts; (ii) GFSC-licensed banks frequently require the EMI to be a licensed entity with clear ownership and compliance status; (iii) the Bermuda Exempted Company must demonstrate that its management-and-control center (Gibraltar) matches the onboarding jurisdiction; (iv) sanctions and UBO transparency obligations apply fully to the EMI account; (v) a dedicated GFSC-licensed bank account must remain the anchor operating account to retain access to SWIFT, CHAPS, Faster Payments, and SEPA clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is executed via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's or parent treasury account to the Gibraltar bank account held in the name of the Bermuda Exempted Company, using the bank's SWIFT BIC and a purpose-code narrative (e.g., 'SUBSCRIPTION FOR SHARE CAPITAL / EQUITY CONTRIBUTION' and, where required, the Eurostat/OECD BoP code E510 covering equity capital), together with a unique beneficiary reference matching the share subscription agreement. For GBP-denominated injections sourced from a UK account, UK Faster Payments (up to GBP 1,000,000 per single payment) or CHAPS same-day system may be used; for EUR injection, SEPA Credit Transfer and TARGET2 are accepted without GFSC approval; for USD, the clearing leg clears via CHIPS/Fedwire through the Gibraltar bank's New York correspondent. Verification includes the incoming MT910 credit advice, matching of originator and beneficiary details, retrieval of the referencing agreement, sanctions/AML screening, and confirmation that proceeds are credited immediately without specialty FX conversion. Capital escrow balances should be converted into the functional base currency at the bank's dealing desk only upon explicit treasury instruction, using a defined dealing-cutoff to avoid unhedged float.Outbound trade disbursements for procurement of subscription box merchandise and logistics are settled through segregated clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for non-UK/non-EU suppliers and for USD-denominated settlements; (ii) UK Faster Payments and CHAPS for GBP payments to UK suppliers; (iii) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for EUR payments to EU suppliers; (iv) Commercial Letter of Credit (documentary credit) issued by SWIFT MT700 through the Gibraltar branch for high-value repeat bulk orders, with Incoterms 2020 (typically FCA, EXW, or DDP) stated in the underlying commercial contract. The company should establish a payment factory to batch recurring supplier payouts, apply zero-balance account sweeps from the operational account into a central concentration account, and use host-to-host MT101 files where the corporate treasury system is enabled. Payment instructions require consistent invoice references, VAT/GST treatment aligned to the destination jurisdiction (Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area for goods), and pre-validation against supplier master data and sanctions lists; unsettled or disputed invoices are to be held outside the automated cycle to prevent chargeback-to-bank reputational exposure.Deliverable FX Forward (spot-deferred) in GBP/USD and GBP/EUR with tenors of up to 12 months, executed with a GFSC-licensed bank, and backed by a documented underlying forecast of subscription receipts and supplier payables.FX Swap (swap of principal and interest at maturity) for short-term liquidity rebalancing between USD, EUR and GBP working capital accounts.Cross-Currency Swap (collateralised under an ISDA/CSA) for longer-dated treasury restructuring of capital balances, permitted only where the hedge is aligned to the Bermuda Exempted Company's actual cross-border asset and liability exposures.Vanilla FX Options and collar structures (zero-cost collars) used to cap adverse rate movements on committed supplier or acquisition cash flows, with a negative statement in the hedging policy prohibiting speculative uncovered options.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) is legally permissible but operationally unnecessary because GBP, EUR and USD are fully convertible in Gibraltar; NDFs are to be restricted to exceptional settlement legs involving a genuinely non-convertible currency and must receive prior senior treasury and compliance approval due to heightened counterparty and documentation risk.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High for the e-money institution (EMI) channel, subject to strict limits. Gibraltar is not a participant in the EEA single-market passport after Brexit, so a UK FCA-authorised EMI (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex) or a GFSC-licensed EMI must be used through the mutual recognition framework between the United Kingdom and Gibraltar. The EMI channel is viable as a payment concentration, multi-currency FX and low-value disbursement layering tool for the subscription box traffic fed by Stripe Billing and Authorize.Net, but it must not be used as the primary regulated treasury depository for capital injection or long-term liquidity. Legal constraints include: (i) EMIs are not banks and cannot hold deposits taking; funds must be safeguarded in the EMI's segregated trust/safeguarding accounts; (ii) GFSC-licensed banks frequently require the EMI to be a licensed entity with clear ownership and compliance status; (iii) the Bermuda Exempted Company must demonstrate that its management-and-control center (Gibraltar) matches the onboarding jurisdiction; (iv) sanctions and UBO transparency obligations apply fully to the EMI account; (v) a dedicated GFSC-licensed bank account must remain the anchor operating account to retain access to SWIFT, CHAPS, Faster Payments, and SEPA clearing.
