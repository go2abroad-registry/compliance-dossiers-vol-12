Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar Income Tax Office, operating under the Commissioner of Income Tax; self-service portal at https://portal.tax.gi (Gibraltar Revenue Services (GRS) e-filing system).

E-government filing portal

Foreign Bermuda Exempted Companies establishing economic substance in Gibraltar must: (1) apply for registration at Companies House Gibraltar under the Companies Act 2014, filing the constitutional documents and registered office address; (2) submit Form GIP-1 (Gibraltar Introduction of Profits) where applicable; (3) complete the Taxpayer Registration Application with GRS through a licensed Gibraltar tax representative or resident agent; (4) obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) from the Income Tax Office, typically issued within 10–15 working days; (5) register for employee payroll (PAYE) and social insurance (GSSP) with the Director of Employment if hiring Gibraltar-based staff; (6) maintain a registered office and resident company secretary; (7) file an annual return and obtain the Certificate of Good Standing. No prior approval from Bermuda authorities is required for a foreign-managed Exempted Company, but statutory books must remain available for inspection in Gibraltar.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda domicile, as a non-resident owned Exempted Company) combined with Entity_Level_Taxation in Gibraltar. A Bermuda Exempted Company that is managed and controlled from or carries on a trade through a permanent establishment in Gibraltar becomes Gibraltar tax resident and is subject to the 12.5% corporate income tax on Gibraltar-source and remittance-basis profits. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda domicile, as a non-resident owned Exempted Company) combined with Entity_Level_Taxation in Gibraltar. A Bermuda Exempted Company that is managed and controlled from or carries on a trade through a permanent establishment in Gibraltar becomes Gibraltar tax resident and is subject to the 12.5% corporate income tax on Gibraltar-source and remittance-basis profits. Territorial_Scope applies only to the Bermudian exempt status; Gibraltar follows the OECD 2014 substance-over-form framework and requires economic substance in the jurisdiction of management to avoid reclassification as a shell entity.

Available election forms

No Gibraltar 'check-the-box' or US-style entity classification election exists. Statutory elections available: (a) election under Section 15(3) Income Tax Act 2010 for a company to be treated as tax resident in Gibraltar on the basis of incorporation (for UK–Gibraltar dual residency purposes); (b) application for exemption from quarantine under the Income Tax (Exempt Companies) (Amendment) Regulations, applicable only to pre-2011 exempt certificates; (c) the OECD Hybrid Mismatch Rules (Income Tax (Hybrid Mismatch) Regulations 2021) require an elective notification to GRS for avoiding or substantiating an asymmetric treatment between Bermuda's full exemption and Gibraltar's 12.5% rate; (d) Group relief election under Section 47A for loss surrender among Gibraltar-resident group members. Bermuda does not impose tax on the Exempted Company, so no Bermuda tax election is required; however, a Gibraltar–Bermuda permanent establishment relief election may be lodged to mitigate double taxation on profits attributed to the Gibraltar branch.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales tax in Gibraltar. Gibraltar is a separate customs territory from the United Kingdom and the EU, operating a customs and excise duty regime under the Customs Duties Ordinance 1937 and the Goods (Importation and Exportation) Ordinance. A General Consumption Tax (GCT) has been proposed but not enacted.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. No general consumption tax registration threshold exists. However, importers of goods into Gibraltar must register with the Customs Department for a Customs Declarant Code (CDN) and obtain an Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) equivalent, required for any import customs declaration, regardless of turnover. Commercial service exports (executive search fees) do not trigger any indirect tax registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — Gibraltar has no VAT system. Cross-border executive search and headhunting services supplied by a Gibraltar-resident Bermuda Exempted Company to non-Gibraltar clients are 'neither taxed nor zero-rated', falling wholly outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation. Consequently, no input VAT credit or export refund mechanism exists. For direct tax purposes, the service is treated as exported and is exempt from Gibraltar corporate tax only to the extent the income is not attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment or management-and-control activities; if the contract, payment, and decision-making are located outside Gibraltar, the profits are outside Gibraltar's territorial charge. The client's country will normally apply a reverse-charge or self-assessment VAT mechanism on the imported executive search service.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate exists in Gibraltar for B2B services. Invoices must comply with Section 55 of the Income Tax Act 2010 substantiation requirements. Gibraltar customs clearance uses the electronic ASYCUDA World system for import/export declarations; duties on business samples and marketing collateral are payable at import unless covered by temporary importation provisions. There is no requirement to have invoices validated by the tax authority prior to issuance.

Although no VAT clawback applies (no VAT system), GRS Direct Tax Audit and the Customs Department scrutinise export substantiation for transfer pricing and diversion of Gibraltar-source profits. Penalties under the Income Tax Act 2010: (a) understatement of profits due to non-remitted export income — up to 100% of additional tax plus interest at 4% above the applicable base rate; (b) late economic substance filings — automatic administrative penalty of GBP 1,000 and potential strike-off; (c) fraudulent misdeclaration of place-of-supply to avoid tax — criminal prosecution under Section 92(1) with fines up to GBP 50,000 and up to 7 years' imprisonment for directors; (d) retroactive reassessment period extends to 6 years for non-negligent errors and 20 years for deliberate concealment (Sections 98–99). Failure to repatriate export earnings within the 180-day deadline without a commercially justified reason triggers re-characterisation of the amount as a director's loan or deemed distribution, attracting dividend withholding at 0% but additional assessment under CFC rules. To avoid clawback, the company must retain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation under the OECD TP Guidelines, including a functional analysis proving that all high-value headhunting decisions (candidate sourcing, negotiations, fee setting) are executed outside Gibraltar.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full and unrestricted convertibility on both capital and current account transactions; the Gibraltar Pound is exchangeable at par with GBP and circulates interchangeably with sterling. No exchange control law, no surrender duty, and no prior authorisation from the GFSC or the Currency Commission is required for inbound equity capital, intercompany funding, retainer fee receipts, or outbound profit remittances. Profit remittance hurdles are purely documentary: the Bermuda Exempted Company must evidence economic substance in Bermuda or Gibraltar (Bermuda Economic Substance Declaration Form ES-1, Gibraltar ES Certificate of Compliance), maintain a CRS/FATCA registration with a valid GI TIN, and present audited or reviewed financial statements confirming distributable reserves; Gibraltar levies no dividend withholding tax and no capital gains tax on non-resident shareholders, subject to compliance with the Income Tax Act 2010 (Gibraltar) and the UK-Gibraltar double taxation agreement provisions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Neither Gibraltar nor Bermuda imposes statutory export proceeds surrender timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation quotas. Executive search and headhunting services exported from the Gibraltar entity may be invoiced and collected in GBP, EUR, USD, or any freely convertible currency and held in the corresponding bank account without conversion obligation. For AML/CFT purposes, each export receipt must be matched to an underlying commercial contract and invoice, with the original SWIFT MT103 or equivalent payment instruction retained in the customer due diligence file.

Central bank supervision: Government of Gibraltar Currency Commission (official issuer of Gibraltar Pound GIP at 1:1 GBP parity); monetary policy conducted by the Bank of England; bank licensing and prudential supervision by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Restricted but usable as a secondary treasury rail only. UK-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business) are not passportable into Gibraltar post-Brexit, and a Gibraltar branch of a UK EMI would require a separate GFSC e-money authorisation under the Financial Services Act 2019. Wise B2B is feasible for low-value operational retainers, candidate expense reimbursement, and contractor disbursements below GBP 25,000 per transaction, provided the EMI performs its own KYC and the Gibraltar entity maintains a recognised banking fallback with a Tier 1 licensed credit institution. Fully digital onboarding with Wise or Currencycloud is viable for ancillary flows but the capital escrow account and all client-retainer settlement accounts must be maintained with a GFSC-licensed bank to preserve creditor protection and audit trail integrity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital is injected from the Bermuda Exempted Company's Bermuda domiciliary bank (e.g., Butterfield Bank Bermuda or HSBC Bermuda) via SWIFT MT103 to the Gibraltar International Bank GBP or EUR account. The remittance must include a purpose code mirroring the Bermuda Companies Act share premium contribution and the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 notice of allotment; beneficial owner reference must recite 'Capital Contribution - Allotment of Shares - [Company Name] GI TIN XXX'. The bank will require a board resolution of the Bermuda parent authorising the transfer, a certified copy of the share allotment return filed with the Gibraltar Registrar, and confirmation that no exchange control approval is required by the Gibraltar Currency Commission. For retainer-fee receivables from international clients, the same SWIFT rail is used with purpose code 'Payment for professional services - retainer invoice number' and must be pre-matched to the signed executive search agreement to satisfy source-of-funds validation. (1) SWIFT MT103 is used as the principal rail for international candidate signing bonuses, subcontractor headhunter commissions, and client-rebatable disbursements above GBP 25,000; standard settlement coincides with CHAPS (GBP), SEPA Instant (EUR), and Fedwire (USD) clearing windows. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Restricted but usable as a secondary treasury rail only. UK-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business) are not passportable into Gibraltar post-Brexit, and a Gibraltar branch of a UK EMI would require a separate GFSC e-money authorisation under the Financial Services Act 2019. Wise B2B is feasible for low-value operational retainers, candidate expense reimbursement, and contractor disbursements below GBP 25,000 per transaction, provided the EMI performs its own KYC and the Gibraltar entity maintains a recognised banking fallback with a Tier 1 licensed credit institution. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous Local File where gross revenue exceeds GBP 8,000,000 or where cross-border related-party transactions exceed GBP 500,000; a Master File is required where consolidated group revenue exceeds GBP 50,000,000; executive search retainers paid by UK parent-linked clients are within scope even below the revenue threshold where intercompany value exceeds GBP 500,000.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's Economic Substance (Company) Regulations 2019 and Bermuda's Economic Substance Regulations 2019 apply concurrently. Executive search is classified as a service business / headquarters relevant activity for ESR purposes. The company must annually demonstrate: (i) locally managed board meetings and strategy sessions in Gibraltar; (ii) a physical office and an adequate, proportionate workforce in Gibraltar; (iii) CIGA performed in Gibraltar (client sourcing, candidate identification, retainer negotiation, delivery of placement, invoicing); (iv) no outsourcing of CIGA to the Bermuda parent without documented commercial rationale; and (v) filing of the ESR declaration within six months of the financial year-end.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax understated plus daily default surcharges and interest at the statutory rate; criminal conviction on indictment carries an unlimited fine and imprisonment for up to seven years; forfeiture of the proceeds of the offence.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who directed or consented to the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the company's restitution; courts may issue worldwide asset freezing orders, travel bans, and disqualification orders of up to 15 years; convicted directors face imprisonment for up to seven years.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR penalty of up to GBP 100,000 plus additional daily penalties of up to GBP 10,000 per day of non-compliance; compulsory strike-off from the Gibraltar Companies Registry; automatic exchange of information with HMRC, Bermuda, and the OECD framework; termination of Bermuda exempted status.

Director personal liability: Personal liability for unpaid ESR penalties where the directors caused the non-compliance; disqualification from acting as a director for up to 10 years; liability for restoration costs following strike-off; exposure to fraud-based preference claims in an insolvent liquidation.
Anti-Money Laundering, Sanctions, and Statutory Licensing

Statutory sanction: Unlimited fine on conviction under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and sanctions regulations; additional regulator fines of up to GBP 1,000,000; revocation of the statutory licence; compliance notices and business suspension; restraint and confiscation orders against the firm.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability for failure to file suspicious-activity reports, with imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering; civil liability for regulatory fines where the board failed to install a compliant AML framework; travel restrictions and property restraints; disqualification from serving as a director for up to 10 years.
Double Taxation Arrangement Abuse and Synthetic Routing

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and reassessment at UK taxpayer level with penalties up to 100%; disallowance of Gibraltar nil-rate claims; exchange of information with HMRC under the DTA/TIEA network; OECD principal-purpose-test countermeasures.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve synthetic routing face personal restitution orders and potential liability under the UK Criminal Finances Act 2017 failure-to-prevent-facilitation-of-tax-evasion offence, carrying imprisonment up to seven years; civil recovery of double benefit plus interest; HMRC director-level penalty notices; travel bans pending cross-border investigation.
