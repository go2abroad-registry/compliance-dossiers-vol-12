Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, Gibraltar (Ministry of Finance); official portal: Gibraltar Government Tax & Customs Digital Gateway at https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company commencing any taxable activity in Gibraltar must apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) before making its first supply. The application requires a certified copy of the Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, constitutional documents, board resolution appointing managers, details of the Gibraltar registered or branch office, and the appointment of a Gibraltar tax representative. If the company is not centrally managed and controlled in Gibraltar and has no Gibraltar-source income, it may be issued a TIN for administrative purposes while preserving its tax-exempt status. For UK-bound e-commerce sales, the company must separately obtain a UK VAT registration number from HMRC (Form VAT1 or automatic registration when selling through a UK online marketplace), because no Gibraltar VAT number exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. The Bermuda Exempted Company relies on its Bermuda statutory exemption and on Gibraltar non-residence. Where management and control in Gibraltar is unavoidable, the company may elect, under the Income Tax Act 2015, to adopt a territorial basis of assessment so that only Gibraltar-source profits are chargeable at 12.5%; alternatively, for a qualifying internationally trading company, the Commissioner may allow a deemed total income basis at a fixed percentage, effectively preserving tax-exempt treatment. Hybrid and branch mismatch positions must be reported to the Gibraltar Commissioner under the OECD BEPS Action 2 implementing regulations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales-and-Use-Tax in Gibraltar; indirect taxation limited to customs and import duties on arrival

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for a Gibraltar indirect tax register; however, importers must register with HM Customs Gibraltar regardless of turnover. For direct-to-consumer sales into the UK exceeding £135 per consignment, the Bermuda Exempted Company is a non-established taxable person and must register with HMRC for UK VAT without any turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

As Gibraltar does not levy a VAT/GST, exports are outside the scope of Gibraltar taxation and no '0% export VAT refund' is available locally. Goods qualify for a 0% Gibraltar charge where they leave Gibraltar under a customs export declaration and are directly transported to a destination outside Gibraltar. Offshore services qualify for 0% if the customer is outside Gibraltar and the service is effectively used outside Gibraltar. For UK supplies, the entity may zero-rate only where it proves the goods have physically left the UK; if the sale is fulfilled from a UK warehouse, 20% UK VAT is chargeable without substantive zero-rating relief.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EMS or UK CDS Exit Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent Sea/Air Waybill for oversized home furniture)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or authenticated payment settlement record from Stripe or Affirm BNPL proving receipt of export proceeds)

Gibraltar has no enacted real-time e-invoicing or fiscal-clearing system. All imports and exports must be declared electronically via the Gibraltar Customs Single Administrative Document (SAD) system, which interfaces with the UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS) for goods leaving or entering the UK. Electronic invoices must be generated for each sale and retained for six years; UK-bound sales are subject to HMRC's digital record-keeping and 'Making Tax Digital' VAT rules, and future EU/UK continuous transaction control developments will mandate interoperable e-invoicing.

False claims for import duty relief in Gibraltar are punishable by forfeiture of the goods and a penalty not exceeding £3,000, with imprisonment up to two years for fraudulent evasion. In the UK, a careless or deliberate zero-rating claim attracts a clawback of output VAT plus interest, a penalty of 30% to 100% of the VAT understated, and, for marketplaces, joint and several liability. Failure to repatriate export receipts within 180 days may precipitate a UK CFC challenge if the Bermuda profits do not benefit from the excluded-qualifying-territory provisions, although the Exempted Company's statutory Bermuda exemption normally shields undistributed profits from that attack.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound is pegged at 1:1 to pound sterling under a full commodity-currency-board arrangement; current-account transactions, including trade payments, freight, logistics and e-commerce merchant settlements, are fully convertible with zero administrative FX approval. Capital-account operations, including equity injection into a Bermuda Exempted Company branch or subsidiary in Gibraltar, intercompany loans and liquidation remittances, are equally unrestricted and require no central-bank licence or foreign-investment clearance. Profit repatriation from Gibraltar to Bermuda, or onward to the ultimate UK-connected shareholders, is free of exchange control and is not subject to Gibraltar withholding tax on dividends or interest in respect of a tax-exempt Bermuda company that does not generate Gibraltar-source income. The only operative friction is banking KYC evidence substantiating the underlying commercial transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement applies to GIP or GBP export receipts in Gibraltar. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%, there is no prescribed remittance timeline and the Exempted Company may retain 100% of export proceeds in GIP, GBP, EUR or USD within its operational accounts without reporting to the GFSC. Beneficial ownership and economic-substance disclosure obligations under the Companies Act 2014 and Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations apply regardless of the absense of exchange-control surrender.

Central bank supervision: The Government of Gibraltar acts as monetary issuer of the Gibraltar pound (GIP) through a statutory currency-board reserve held in gold and bank deposits at the Bank of England; prudential and conduct supervision of banks and e-money institutions is exercised by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), with financial-crime and counter-terrorist-financing oversight performed by the Gibraltar Financial Investigation Unit (FIU) under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Gibraltar continues to accord recognition to EEA/UK-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex and Currencycloud under transitional provisions of the Gibraltar Financial Services Act 2019, enabling EMI accounts to be opened for a Gibraltar-established operation of the Bermuda Exempted Company; however, most cross-border EMIs decline to onboard offshore tax-exempt entities lacking a credible local presence, prohibit the passive receipt of merchant-clearing payouts from Affirm BNPL in some settlement currencies and cannot offer lending, deposit insurance (FSCS/GDFS) or trade-finance instruments. Legal constraint: an EMI is not a bank and cannot issue commercial documentary credits; all core treasury balances and any Letters of Credit must therefore sit with a Tier 1 licensed Gibraltar or UK bank. EMIs are suitable only for low-balance FX conversion and supplier disbursement running on a fully separated reconciliation ledger.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital is executed exclusively via SWIFT MT103 corporate credit from the UBO/shareholder's home bank to the Gibraltar bank, referencing a defined treasury purpose code such as 'equity capital injection', 'shareholder subordinated loan' or 'working capital top-up' with the composite legal-entity identifier GB__GI__BM_EXEMPTED_CO appended. The bank requires an accompanying board resolution, a share-register update or loan agreement, and a source-of-funds confirmation evidencing the bank account from which the remittance is dispatched. For EUR-denominated injections, the routing must use SEPA CT with the SSI of the Gibraltar receiving bank and an R-Invoicing reference; for USD, the instruction must include the US clearing correspondent and the beneficiary's full legal name to avoid OFAC name-screening delays. All inbound funds are automatically converted into GIP/GBP at the 1:1 currency-board parity without FX margin where settlement is made in sterling; conversion into EUR/USD may be executed at the bank's prevailing interbank-allied rate on a spot basis.Outbound supplier and freight settlements utilize SWIFT MT103 instructed through the Gibraltar bank's host-to-host channel for direct payments to furniture manufacturers, ocean carriers and warehousing providers. For import quantities exceeding GBP 100,000, a commercial documentary credit is recommended through Absa Bank Gibraltar or Banco de Sabadell via SWIFT MT700 with shipment-against-documents terms, including a full set of bills of lading, commercial invoice, packing list and beneficiary certificate aligned to Incoterms CIF or FOB. Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) serve as a cost-effective intermediate rail for established suppliers. Merchant-clearing liquidity from Stripe and Affirm is swept daily into the multi-currency settlement account through the aggregator's payout engine, segregated at legal-entity level, and cleared against the same MT103 trade corridor with dual approver control. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% in Bermuda: a Bermuda Exempted Company is outside the Bermuda income tax net and enjoys a tax-exempt certificate, notwithstanding the prospective 15% Bermuda Corporate Income Tax for in-scope MNE groups with EUR 750m+ consolidated revenue from fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025. Where Gibraltar central management and control is established, Gibraltar corporation tax applies at 10% on the first GBP 100,000 of qualifying profits and 12.5% on the balance.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% in Bermuda: the exempted company may distribute dividends freely to any shareholder without Bermuda withholding tax or information reporting. 0% in Gibraltar on distributions declared by a Gibraltar-resident company to its Bermuda Exempted Company shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Bermuda Country-by-Country Reporting threshold: consolidated group revenue exceeding BMD/EUR 750 million triggers mandatory CbCR. Bermuda's Tax Commissioner expects a full Local File and Master File aligned to OECD BEPS Action 13 for the in-scope furniture e-commerce supply chain; the Gibraltar Income Tax Office likewise requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where intercompany cross-border transactions exceed GBP 500,000 in a period.

Economic substance scope: Both the Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018 and the Gibraltar Qualifying Company Rules 2022 apply economic substance doctrine: online retail of oversized furniture is a 'distribution and service centre' relevant activity in Bermuda and requires real substance in any jurisdiction claiming tax residence. The entity must maintain premises, full-time personnel, board meetings, and core income-generating activity decision-making in the claimed residence, file ESR declarations in Bermuda, and pay Gibraltar tax on attributable profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through the Bermuda exempted-company pipe

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment by the Gibraltar Income Tax Office for up to 12 years, back interest, tax-geared penalties up to 100% of tax, and criminal prosecution under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 with fines and up to 12 months' imprisonment; the UK Criminal Finances Act 2017 failure-to-prevent-tax-evasion offence also applies to associated persons facilitating the evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who directed the sham are jointly and severally liable for restitution of the tax evaded, face UK and Gibraltar asset-freezing orders, travel bans, extradition, and imprisonment; disqualification from management for up to 15 years applies in both the UK and Gibraltar.
Shell company / absence of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Registrar penalties up to BMD 50,000 plus daily default fines and strike-off with asset forfeiture; Gibraltar withdrawal of the 10% Qualifying Company rate, re-assessment at 12.5% plus 100% penalties, and compulsory strike-off of the foreign company's Gibraltar branch.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for tax collection where the company is a mere façade; Bermuda and Gibraltar courts may order directors to restore unpaid taxes from personal assets, impose disqualification orders, issue travel bans, and extradite directors on fraud charges.
Transfer pricing manipulation / profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment by the Gibraltar Tax Commissioner, secondary transfer pricing adjustment with deemed interest, penalties of 50–100% of the additional tax, and diverted-profits tax exposure in the UK where customer-facing functions are UK-based.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false or incomplete transfer pricing documentation incur civil fines and personal liability for restitution of abated profits; criminal sanctions apply for document falsification, and courts may freeze personal bank accounts and impose travel bans pending resolution.
AML / money laundering through payment rails

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission fines up to GBP 500,000, revocation of the EMI and e-money agent relationships, reporting to the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit, Proceeds of Crime Act prosecution with unlimited fines and up to 14 years' imprisonment per laundering transaction, and Stripe and Affirm permanent account termination.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal criminal liability for failure to report suspicious activity, confiscation orders and travel bans under the Proceeds of Crime Act, director disqualification, and exposure to extradition requests for overseas predicate offences.
False filings and fraud on fiscal authorities

Statutory sanction: Bermuda: fines up to BMD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for false statutory declarations; Gibraltar: false statement penalties, restoration assessments, automatic forfeiture of reliefs, and prosecution for cheating the public revenue.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal conviction of the signing directors, joint and several civil restitution, immediate travel bans and passport surrender, disqualification from acting as directors, and extradition to the UK or Bermuda on fraud or perjury charges.
