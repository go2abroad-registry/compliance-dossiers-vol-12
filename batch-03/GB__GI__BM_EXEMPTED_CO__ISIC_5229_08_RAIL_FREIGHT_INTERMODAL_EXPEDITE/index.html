Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office of Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar (official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax), acting through the Commissioner of Income Tax for direct taxation, and HM Customs Gibraltar (Borders & Customs Agency) for customs duties; HMRC of the United Kingdom retains residual competence for any GB EORI, UK VAT grouping or GMP-related filing.

E-government filing portal

Stepwise procedure for the Bermuda Exempted Company: (1) file constitutional documents (Certificate of Exemption, Memorandum and Articles of Association) with the Gibraltar Companies Registry and appoint a Gibraltar registered agent with a local office; (2) lodge Form IT-REG-FE (Non-Resident/Rock Exempted Entity Registration) with the Income Tax Office, declaring no Gibraltar-source income or a Gibraltar permanent establishment; (3) obtain the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (GTRN) and, where workers are engaged, register for Payroll Tax and social insurance; (4) apply to HM Customs Gibraltar for a Customs Business Identifier (customs declarant code) required for any freight transiting Gibraltar; (5) complete the annual Economic Substance return under the Economic Substance (Companies and Provision of Information) Regulations via the Gibraltar Compliance portal. All filings are made electronically; physical attendance is not required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - the Bermuda Exempted Company is a pure offshore vehicle exempt from Bermuda income, capital, dividend and withholding taxes under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act, and in Gibraltar is taxed on a strict territorial basis at the standard corporate income tax rate of 10% only upon profits locally sourced through a Gibraltar permanent establishment; international Eurasian block-train freight forwarding income sourced outside Gibraltar remains outside the charge, and no branch remittance or secondary withholding tax applies.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or UK s.12A hybrid-mismatch election is available in either Bermuda or Gibraltar; instead, the entity may elect to: (i) secure and extend a Bermuda Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act assurance (commonly grandfathered to 2035, with long-horizon assurances to 2056 available on satisfaction of substance conditions); (ii) elect Gibraltar 'qualifying eligible company' status for the Economic Substance regime in order to obtain a rebuttable presumption of compliance; (iii) adopt capital-allowance pooling and loss-surrender elections under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act. Where the rail-freight treasury function creates hybrid financial instruments, OECD BEPS Action 2 anti-hybrid results are respected via the UK and Gibraltar domestic rules, but neither territory grants a double-count deduction election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Customs-Duties-Only System (no VAT/GST) - Gibraltar is outside the European Union and outside the UK VAT charging area and has never enacted a VAT or GST law; indirect taxation comprises customs import duty on goods entering Gibraltar, excise duties on alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oils and motor vehicles, and ad valorem stamp and probate duties; freight-forwarding services themselves are not subject to any Gibraltar indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory economic-turnover registration threshold applies in Gibraltar because no VAT/GST exists; any person, whatever the value of the first consignment, must hold a Customs Business Identifier with HM Customs Gibraltar. For any separately maintained UK VAT registration of the Exempted Company, the statutory compulsory registration threshold is GBP 90,000 of taxable turnover in any 12-month period (from 1 April 2024), whereas a non-established taxable person making taxable supplies in the UK is compulsory registered from the first supply unless a Treasury direction to the contrary applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because Gibraltar has no VAT, the classic 0% export rating is applied only where the Bermuda Exempted Company maintains a UK VAT registration: supplies of exported goods and international freight transport services (including container block-train carriage commencing or ending in a third country) are zero-rated under Schedule 8, Groups 7 and 13 of the UK VAT Act 1994 upon proof that the goods were removed from the UK within 90 days and retained export evidence; ancillary loading, handling and storage services performed in connection with cross-border rail movement are zero-rated as services related to international goods transit. At the Gibraltar customs level, physical exports are relieved from import duty under outward-processing, re-export and free-zone reliefs, with any duty charged at import refundable upon demonstrated re-exportation; input VAT recovery is obtainable by a non-established taxable person through a UK VAT 65 return if the underlying cost is incurred for taxable export-making activities.

Gibraltar imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction control, no digital stamping and no fiscal API mandate; electronic invoicing is optional. Export entries crossing the UK customs frontier must, however, be transmitted electronically through the UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS) by a national agent holding CDS access, while rail movements on the Eurasian corridor are evidenced by electronic CIM/SMGS rail consignment notes and intermodal terminal freight manifests; digital invoicing may be used for SWIFT/Letter-of-Credit settlement but is not fiscally certified.

The Gibraltar Commissioner of Income Tax may raise assessments within six years of the end of the year of assessment, and HM Customs Gibraltar may conduct duty audits for customs purposes without statutory time limit where fraud or neglect is shown; fraudulent export claims, artificial transit documentation and circular cargo movements expose the Exempted Company to customs duty penalties of up to 200% of the evaded duty and confiscation of the goods, plus criminal liability before the Magistrates' Court; in the UK, HMRC may claw back zero-rating, disallow input VAT recovery, issue best-of-judgment assessments under Schedule 24 to the Finance Act 2007 with 30%-100% penalty inaccuracies, and initiate criminal proceedings for evasion carrying imprisonment; retrospective clawback under the Civil Penalty Directive and MTIC (missing trader intra-community) anti-fraud rules may additionally apply where the Eurasian corridor freight is supported by circular invoices.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates a currency-board system pegging the Gibraltar pound at 1:1 to the pound sterling. The UK Exchange Control Act 1947 was repealed and no exchange-control restrictions survive. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible. Dividends, capital repatriation, loan repayments, and profit remittances of financial and trading businesses may proceed without exchange-control approval. The only practical hurdles are bank-level KYC, sanctions, and AML screening. A Bermuda Exempted Company in Gibraltar is fully free to hold, convert, and remit operating income in any major currency; no Gibraltar Tax Office consent is required for external transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion timeline, or conversion ratio exists under Gibraltar law for export proceeds. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency indefinitely. For banks, export or freight-forwarding proceeds are reconciled against CIM/railway consignment notes, bills of lading, air waybills, invoices, customs declarations, and intermodal transport contracts. Internal bank reporting may require a GBP-equivalent valuation for statistical and regulatory purposes, but this is not a surrender requirement and does not restrict the company's use of proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (United Kingdom central bank and ultimate monetary authority) in coordination with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission, which licenses and supervises banks operating in Gibraltar and administers the Gibraltar pound currency-board arrangement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMI accounts, for example those provided by UK FCA-authorised electronic money institutions such as Revolut Business or Wise Business, are generally not acceptable as the sole or primary treasury rail for a Gibraltar commercial entity unless the EMI holds a Gibraltar electronic-money licence or an applicable passporting arrangement under the Gibraltar Financial Services (Electronic Money) Regulations. These platforms are suitable only for low-value ancillary collections and may not issue commercial letters of credit, open structured trade finance lines, or provide the full SWIFT/CHAPS clearing and physical treasury infrastructure required for ISIC 5229 rail freight intermodal forwarding. Legal constraints also include lower transaction thresholds, separate KYC risk appetite, and the need for a recognised Gibraltar nexus.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or intercompany capital should be routed via SWIFT MT103 for non-GBP currency or via CHAPS for sterling payments. The payment fields must identify the ordering institutional investor or parent company (Field 50K), the Gibraltar beneficiary bank BIC (Field 57A), the beneficiary account (Field 59), and a remittance instruction (Field 70) containing the bank-approved purpose code, commonly 'EQUI' for equity capital injection or 'INTR' for intercompany funding. The receiving bank will require a notarised board resolution approving the capital contribution, a source-of-funds statement, evidence of beneficial ownership, and a share subscription or shareholder loan agreement. No Gibraltar exchange-control approval is required, although the bank may apply enhanced due diligence for amounts above EUR 10,000 equivalent.Outbound trade settlement is executed through SWIFT MT103/TT transfers for invoice payments to rail operators, terminal handlers, freight forwarders, and logistics service providers. For larger capacity or equipment acquisitions, the company should use commercial documentary letters of credit subject to UCP 600, communicated by SWIFT MT700/MT707 and advised/confirmed by a reputable clearing bank. All disbursements must be supported by the underlying CIM consignment note, rail/road intermodal contract, invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and customs documents. SWIFT GPI tracking is recommended to reduce intermediary delays. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% Gibraltar Corporation Tax; a 15% OECD Pillar Two top-up applies only if the consolidated global revenue of the Bermuda/Gibraltar group reaches €750 million.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% statutorily; Gibraltar does not impose withholding tax on dividend distributions to resident or non-resident shareholders. Dividends paid by the Bermuda Exempted Company to ultimate shareholders are likewise exempt from Bermuda withholding tax.

The CIT1 is due no later than 4 calendar months after the close of the accounting period; for a 31 December year-end, filing and full payment must be completed by 30 April of the following year. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when Gibraltar cross-border controlled transactions with the Bermuda parent or sister entities exceed G£500,000 per accounting period. Master File is required where the MNE group's consolidated turnover exceeds G£50,000,000; country-by-country reporting is triggered at the €750,000,000 consolidated revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Rules transpose OECD and EU standards: every Gibraltar tax-resident or registered non-resident company must complete an annual Economic Substance Return and demonstrate adequate substance in the relevant core income-generating activities of freight forwarding—strategic management, contracting, cargo consolidation, and dispatch control—through board meetings in Gibraltar, physical premises, and full-time qualified staff. A Bermuda Exempted Company relying on the 0% foreign-source exemption must evidence that these functions are not performed in Bermuda as mere paper shell activities; otherwise the Commissioner will re-characterize the profits as Gibraltar-source and apply 12.5% plus penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / International Tax Arbitrage

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 and Criminal Justice (International Cooperation) Act: additional assessment of up to 200% of the evaded tax, a fine of up to G£50,000, and imprisonment of up to 3 years per offense for cheating the public revenue; cancellation of the Qualifying Company election and disqualification of the Gibraltar fiduciary.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize the misdeclaration are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the full tax understatement plus interest; courts may issue travel bans under the Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters framework when the director is a non-resident holding UK/EU ties, impose asset freezing orders, and order director disqualification of up to 15 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Rules: fixed penalty of G£10,000 per year of breach plus a continuing penalty of G£1,000 per day; the Registrar can strike off the company under the Companies Act 2014 and restrict the licensed corporate service provider from accepting new appointments.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: nominee directors and the Gibraltar licensed fiduciary are jointly liable for unpaid taxes and costs; false declarations under oath attract up to 5 years' imprisonment, freezing of the director's personal assets, and loss of professional licensing.
AML / Sanctions Breach (Eurasia Rail Corridor)

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar): money laundering offense punishable by imprisonment of up to 14 years and unlimited fines; asset forfeiture of the cargo value and freight proceeds; revocation of the company's banking access, including Swift termination and loss of the Letter of Credit facility.

Director personal liability: Directors and the AML compliance officer face criminal conviction for consent failures and can be ordered by the Gibraltar Supreme Court to make restitution to the liquidator; travel bans and disclosure orders are standard, and repeated breaches result in permanent exclusion from Gibraltar financial services employment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment of up to 100% of the recharacterized profit, a penalty of up to 40% of the corrected tax liability, interest at the statutory rate, and a mandatory full transfer pricing audit of all prior open years; loss of the Qualifying Company exemption certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors signing inaccurate transfer pricing documentation are exposed to civil restitution for the group's tax leakage and criminal fines of up to G£25,000; repeated failure triggers the auditor's license revocation and personal disqualification from serving as a director of any Gibraltar company.
