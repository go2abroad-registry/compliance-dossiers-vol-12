Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office (ITO), HM Government of Gibraltar, Ministry of Finance; corporate registry: Gibraltar Companies House (Registrar of Companies); customs authority: HM Customs Gibraltar. Registrations and filings are conducted through the ITO iTax online portal; customs licensing and bonded-operator approvals are processed separately by HM Customs Gibraltar.

E-government filing portal

SOP for a Bermuda Exempted Company establishing a Gibraltar tax identity: (1) Register the Bermuda Exempted Company as an external/overseas company under the Gibraltar Companies Act at Companies House Gibraltar, filing certified constitutional documents from Bermuda, appointing a Gibraltar registered office and a local process agent; (2) obtain the Gibraltar external company number; (3) apply to the Income Tax Office via the iTax portal for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (Gibraltar TIN), submitting the certificate of incorporation, certificate of good standing from the Bermuda Registrar of Companies, memorandum and articles of association, details of directors and registered office; (4) appoint a Gibraltar tax representative; (5) file an annual confirmation statement and, where Gibraltar-source income or managed-and-controlled residence arises, corporate tax returns under the Income Tax Act 2010; (6) where the company is non-resident (managed and controlled outside Gibraltar), foreign-source income is not within charge and an exemption certificate may be claimed where applicable, subject to economic substance requirements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box or general entity-classification election regime. Available statutory mechanisms: (1) application to the Income Tax Office for an Income Tax Act 2010 Exemption Certificate covering foreign-source income where the company is not managed and controlled in Gibraltar, preserving Bermuda Tax Exemption Certificate treatment; (2) election for transparent treatment in qualifying Gibraltar limited partnerships and Limited Liability Partnerships; (3) special exempt or preferential elections for licensed insurers, international shipping (Category A) and qualifying investment funds; (4) group loss-relief elections for Gibraltar tax grouping. Hybrid and reverse-hybrid mismatch rules implement OECD BEPS Action 2; a Bermuda tax-exempt hybrid entity used as an upline shareholder requires careful mismatch analysis to avoid denial of Gibraltar deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Customs and Excise Duty Regime — no VAT or GST in Gibraltar (outside the EU VAT area); import duties under the Customs Duties Act; excise duties on alcohol, fuel and tobacco; specific duty and regulatory charges apply to bonded aviation-fuel and airport cargo movements; the bonded trucking activity is outside the scope of a general consumption tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Gibraltar does not impose VAT or GST and hence has no economic-turnover VAT registration threshold. Registration as a bonded transport operator and authorised cargo agent before HM Customs Gibraltar is mandatory under the Customs Duties Act and the customs warehousing/bonded movement regulations; a customs guarantee or security bond, assessed by HM Customs on projected duty exposure rather than turnover, must be posted in Gibraltar pounds (GIP) before bonded operations commence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating is inapplicable because Gibraltar levies no VAT. Duty-free export treatment applies where bonded goods are removed from Gibraltar customs territory or transferred to an international airport bonded warehouse under a customs transit or export procedure; the bonded road feeder service is outside the scope of Gibraltar consumption tax. Eligibility requires proof that the goods physically left the customs territory, that the trucking movement was performed under a valid bonded/sealed procedure, and that the service recipient is an authorised airport cargo operator; duty relief is conditional on complete documentation being retained and available to HM Customs Gibraltar.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Export Summary Declaration filed through HM Customs Gibraltar)
  • Bonded transit authorisation and CMR international consignment note evidencing the GPS-sealed airport feeder movement
  • GPS seal log and bond guarantee certificate demonstrating continuity of the customs seal
  • Air Waybill and airport bonded-warehouse receipt evidencing delivery at the international airport
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from the collecting bank evidencing final settlement of the haulage invoice in foreign currency

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal e-invoicing, digital stamping or continuous transaction controls. Customs declarations for bonded trucking movements are filed electronically through the HM Customs Gibraltar digital clearance system; bonded road feeder movements use CMR international consignment notes and, where applicable, TIR/community transit procedures. GPS seal logs maintained by the truck fleet operator serve as the commercial control evidence for the bonded movement, but no government fiscal data exchange API is currently imposed.

Fraudulent or unsupported claims of export relief attract civil and criminal sanctions under the Gibraltar Customs Duties Act and Income Tax Act 2010: assessed duty/tax plus interest, penalties up to 200% of the tax avoided, civil fines for false declarations, forfeiture of bonded goods and vehicles, suspension or revocation of the bonded operator licence, and personal liability for directors in wilful default cases. Retrospective clawback of duty relief applies where the full documentation bundle cannot be produced within the statutory review period; the 180-day FX repatriation deadline is policed through banking records and exchange-control audits.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account. GIP is freely convertible at par with GBP; there are no exchange-control approvals, prior licensing, or central-bank permissions for capital injections, dividend repatriation, intercompany loan repayments, or trade settlements. Gibraltar imposes no dividend withholding tax and no capital controls on profit remittance to a Bermuda Exempted Company, provided the entity is not treated as Gibraltar tax-resident through central management and control. If Gibraltar tax residence is established, Gibraltar corporate income tax at 12.5% on net assessable profits and transfer-pricing documentation obligations would apply. Profit remittance accordingly faces no regulatory hurdle, but substance and tax-residence proof must be maintained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Gibraltar and the United Kingdom impose no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. A Gibraltar-incorporated customs-bonded trucking operator may retain export and transport-service proceeds in GIP, GBP, EUR, USD or any other freely convertible currency, with no obligation to surrender earnings to a central authority. The only compliance requirements are AML/CTF record-keeping, accurate CRS/FATCA reporting, and adequate economic-substance records where the entity is Gibraltar tax-resident.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) and the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934; there is no independent central bank in Gibraltar and the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued at a fixed 1:1 parity with the British pound (GBP), with monetary policy credibility and ultimate lender-of-last-resort capacity underpinned by the Bank of England. HM Treasury sanctions administration (OFSI) also applies to Gibraltar as a UK overseas territory.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. Gibraltar-authorised and UK-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions can serve as a secondary clearing channel for low-value pass-through disbursements, card-based fleet expenses, and expedited GBP/EUR/USD transfers, with materially shorter onboarding than Tier-1 commercial banks. Legal constraints are significant: EMIs are not licensed to take deposits, provide credit, issue commercial letters of credit, or enter into proprietary FX hedging. Most digital-first EMIs classify an offshore Bermuda Exempted Company with no local economic substance and no regulated introducer as high-risk and will decline onboarding; a relationship with a Gibraltar-licensed fiduciary or law firm as introducer materially improves viability. EMIs should therefore be used only for supplementary treasury flows, never as the primary deposit or escrow rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from Bermuda or the shareholder jurisdiction should be routed via SWIFT MT103 or TARGET2 (for EUR) to the Gibraltar Tier-1 operational account. The wire instruction must state the purpose as 'Share Capital Injection - Equity Contribution' or 'Intercompany Loan', include the Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference (UETR), and reference the board resolution and share allotment register. The receiving bank will require pre-credit delivery of the executed resolution, the shareholder source-of-funds statement, and the CRS/FATCA self-certification before confirming value. GIP transfers are credited at par with GBP without conversion; USD or EUR injections are converted at the receiving bank's GBP spot rate, so the treasury function should pre-agree the conversion margin and obtain a same-day value-date confirmation. No Gibraltar exchange-control approval is required, but the bank's internal AML committee must clear the source of funds before the first credit is posted.Outbound trade disbursements for the bonded trucking fleet should be executed over three complementary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for high-value vendor, fuel, toll, landing-fee and airport-operator settlements in GBP, EUR and USD, using TELEX/priority settlement and UETR tracking; (2) commercial letters of credit (UCP 600) with a Gibraltar or London confirming bank for fleet equipment purchases and cross-border supplier transactions; and (3) local GBP clearing via CHAPS and Faster Payments, plus ACH Direct for low-value repetitive disbursements and Bank Wire (FEDWIRE) for same-day USD settlement. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 in aggregate annual controlled transactions triggers the mandatory preparation of a Gibraltar Local File; a Master File is required where the Bermudan Exempted Company belongs to a group whose consolidated turnover exceeds GIP 750,000,000. Contemporaneous documentation must be filed with the annual CIT-1 return or within 30 days of a notice from the Income Tax Commissioner.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations 2019 apply because the customs bonded truck fleet constitutes a 'transport and logistics' relevant activity. The Bermuda Exempted Company must demonstrate, in Gibraltar: adequate physical premises, qualified full-time employees, active board management, and proportionate operational expenditure. A mere registered office or mailbox will disqualify the entity from the legal substance exemption and may reverse the 0% Bermuda tax classification for Gibraltar source income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Immediate tax assessment plus a tax-geared penalty of up to 100% of understated tax; daily surcharge of up to GIP 60 per day; criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion carrying a fine of up to GIP 500,000 and imprisonment up to seven years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where a director signed false returns; the director becomes jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, can be disqualified as a director for up to 10 years, and may be subject to a Gibraltar travel ban pending criminal trial.
Shell Company / Deficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations, the Registrar may impose a substance fine of GIP 50,000 plus a daily default penalty of GIP 1,000; the Income Tax Commissioner may disregard the substance claim, assess deemed income of at least GIP 100,000, and strike off the company from the Gibraltar register.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false substance declarations are personally liable for the substance fines and may be imprisoned for up to six months; directors' personal bank accounts in Gibraltar may be frozen to secure penalty recovery.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Violations

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 can lead to criminal fines at the court's discretion and imprisonment up to 14 years; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission may impose a separate regulatory penalty of up to GIP 5,000,000 for systemic AML failures in payment rail processing.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly process tainted wire transfers are individually subject to money-laundering prosecutions, confiscation of personal assets under the Criminal Finances Act, and exclusion orders barring them from entering Gibraltar.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The Income Tax Commissioner may re-determine the taxable profits at arm's length and impose a penalty of 100% of the additional tax where the failure is deliberate; a separate fixed penalty of GIP 300 and daily penalty of GIP 50 per day apply for each missing Local or Master File.

Director personal liability: Directors who arranged the controlled transactions with a dominant tax-avoidance purpose can be made personally liable for the transfer-pricing adjustment and related penalties; non-payment triggers a Gibraltar travel ban and disqualification from any directorship in Gibraltar-licensed transport undertakings.
