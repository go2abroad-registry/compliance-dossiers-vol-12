Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar is a British Overseas Territory with fiscal autonomy. Direct taxes are administered by the Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax), official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax-office. Indirect taxes (VAT and customs) are administered by HM Customs & Excise Department (Commissioner of Customs and VAT), official portal: https://www.customs.gov.gi. Governing statutes: Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended) and Value Added Tax Act 2019 (Gibraltar).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the foreign Bermuda Exempted Company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies either as an oversea company establishing a branch/place of business or by incorporating a Gibraltar subsidiary/branch vehicle. Step 2: Apply to the Income Tax Office for the Gibraltar corporate TIN by submitting the branch/corporate registration form, certified copies of the Bermuda certificate of incorporation, the Bermuda Tax Exemption Certificate, constitutional documents, board resolutions, audited financials, and evidence that central management and control remain outside Gibraltar for non-resident classification. Step 3: Apply to HM Customs & Excise Department for VAT registration when taxable turnover exceeds GIP 150,000, or before the first taxable supply if the supplier is not established in Gibraltar; voluntary VAT registration is permitted. Step 4: Appoint a Gibraltar-resident tax representative and provide local registered address, bank details, and forecast turnover. Step 5: Receive the Gibraltar VAT identification number (GI prefix) and the Income Tax TIN, then file annual corporate tax returns and periodic VAT returns through the official electronic portals.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda Exempted Company holding a Tax Exemption Certificate issued under the Exempted Companies Act 1981; however, Gibraltar does not recognise the Bermuda tax exemption and will treat the entity as a non-resident company subject to entity-level territorial taxation of Gibraltar-source profits only, at the Gibraltar corporation tax rate of 12.5%; no pass-through status is available in Gibraltar).

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no US-style check-the-box or pass-through election. Available statutory elections: (1) election to be treated as ordinarily resident in Gibraltar where central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar; (2) voluntary VAT registration election under the Value Added Tax Act 2019 for sub-threshold taxable persons; (3) group registration and group relief elections under the Income Tax Act 2010; (4) group reconstruction and loss surrender elections for qualifying Gibraltar groups; (5) renewal of the Bermuda Tax Exemption Certificate as a statutory annual election; (6) documentation and disclosure elections addressing OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch outcomes, including alignment of disregarded franchise payment positions to prevent double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 150,000 (Gibraltar Pounds, pegged 1:1 to GBP) of taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period; nil threshold for non-established foreign suppliers, who must register before making their first taxable supply in Gibraltar; voluntary registration available for below-threshold businesses.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for: (a) goods physically exported from Gibraltar to destinations outside Gibraltar, including packaged take-away food consignments exported by the supplier, whereas dine-in Malatang self-serve servings remain standard-rated; (b) zero-rated food categories listed in the Gibraltar VAT schedule, notably cold self-serve food for off-premises consumption when not supplied in the course of catering; (c) services directly connected to exported goods; and (d) franchise master-licence royalties, brand licence fees, and management charges supplied to non-Gibraltar franchisees, which are outside the scope of Gibraltar VAT (or zero-rated) where the effective use and enjoyment of the intellectual property occurs outside Gibraltar. All claims require documented proof of export, destination, counterparty status, and payment.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (Electronic SAD/C88 or equivalent lodged through the Gibraltar customs electronic portal)
  • Commercial Invoice and signed Export Contract
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR note, or courier Proof of Delivery with destination-country customs stamps
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Franchise royalty computation, IP licence agreement, and effective-use-and-enjoyment evidence

No real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory B2B or B2G electronic invoicing, no continuous transaction controls, and no digital stamping regime is currently in force in Gibraltar. Taxpayers must issue VAT-compliant invoices within statutory deadlines, retain records (digital or paper) for six years, and file VAT returns electronically with HM Customs & Excise Department. UK Making Tax Digital obligations do not attach to Gibraltar-only VAT registrations but will apply if the enterprise separately holds a UK VAT registration.

HM Customs & Excise Department may open audits within six years, extended where fraud or wilful default is evidenced, and may issue best-judgment assessments retrospectively clawing back zero-rated VAT, interest, and penalties. Civil penalties reach up to 100% of the VAT evaded for fraudulent evasion, with fixed penalties for failure to obtain or retain export evidence; criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment of up to seven years, unlimited fines, director disqualification, and seizure of exported goods. The burden is on the supplier to prove physical export and repatriation of proceeds within the obligatory 180-day period; any false or fabricated export documentation is treated as fraudulent evasion and invalidates all refund claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar applies no separate foreign-exchange-control regime for current or capital account transactions. Sterling, US dollars, and euros may be freely received, held and remitted without central-bank approval. Capital account transactions and profit remittances are unrestricted for a non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company, provided that the underlying transaction does not touch sanctioned jurisdictions or Parties and that bank-level compliance receives complete source-of-funds evidence. The practical hurdles are anti-money-laundering due diligence and economic-substance documentation; corporate payments into mainland China must also comply with PRC SAFE administration purpose codes and the foreign beneficiary's withholding/currency declaration, but no Gibraltar-side surrender or conversion approval applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio exist in Gibraltar for export proceeds, royalty receipts, or franchise fees. The Exchange Control Act 1947 as historically applied to Gibraltar has not been used to require conversion into Sterling; entities may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts in original or freely convertible currencies. Where the receivable is sourced from mainland China, the Chinese paying entity remains subject to PRC exchange-control verification at the point of remittance, but the Gibraltar receiving account has no surrender obligation and no deadline beyond ordinary bank credit validation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Sterling monetary authority), H.M. Treasury (UK financial sanctions and exchange-control policy), and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (licensing, AML/CFT and banking supervision in Gibraltar).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for a Bermuda Exempted Company operating in Gibraltar. A UK or EEA electronic-money institution is not passport-provisionable into Gibraltar after Brexit; offering e-money or payment-account services inside Gibraltar without a Gibraltar Financial Services Commission licence would contravene Gibraltar's Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act and Payment Services Act. An offshore EMI may be used only as an ancillary collection/disbursement sub-ledger outside the Gibraltar licensed perimeter and must never be presented to franchisees as a bank account. The capital escrow and main operating treasury should be held with a GFSC-licensed bank; any digital EMI funds must be segregated and legally incapable of being used for lending.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% (flat rate applicable to Gibraltar tax-resident companies and to Gibraltar permanent establishments of non-resident persons under the Income Tax Act 2010; the Bermuda Exempted Company head office remains exempt in Bermuda on its foreign-source income).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar does not impose a withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar tax-resident company or by the Gibraltar permanent establishment of a non-resident company. No advance corporation tax or dividend withholding mechanism exists under the Income Tax Act 2010.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 100,000 de minimis compliance threshold for related-party cross-border transactions (royalty, management fee or license payments) used as the internal documentation trigger; Country-by-Country reporting and notification apply only to MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Fully applicable. Gibraltar's Economic Substance Regulations require any tax-resident company or Gibraltar PE carrying on a relevant activity — including franchise licensing, distribution, holding of IP, and intra-group financing — to demonstrate management and control in Gibraltar, adequate physical premises, proportionate full-time employees and local expenditure. A Bermuda Exempted Company managed from Gibraltar or trading through a Gibraltar PE must hold board meetings in Gibraltar, maintain local books and payroll, and keep core income-generating activities within the jurisdiction. Failure to satisfy the test allows the GRA to reallocate the Bermuda entity's profits into the taxable Gibraltar branch and to apply sanction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Offshore Artificial Structure

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2010 (Gibraltar), a penalty of up to 100% of the tax undercharged, plus interest at 10% per annum, civil recovery proceedings in the Supreme Court of Gibraltar, public restitution orders and strike-off by the Registrar.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Bermuda Exempted Company face civil restitution, disqualification under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2006, court-imposed travel bans and imprisonment of up to 7 years on indictment for fraudulent evasion of tax.
Economic Substance Failure / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations of up to GIP 100,000, direction to file corrective returns, reallocation of all royalty income into the taxable Gibraltar branch, and removal of the company from the Gibraltar register upon persistent non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors incur personal financial penalties for failure to make economic substance filings; continued violations lead to disqualification orders, freezing injunctions over overseas assets and potential extradition proceedings where dishonesty is proven.
Unlicensed Food Establishment / Consumer Health Violation

Statutory sanction: Immediate closure notice by the environmental health authority, fines of up to GIP 20,000 per day of unlicensed trading, revocation of ancillary licences and civil damages to franchisees for misrepresentation.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for enforcement costs; wilful non-compliance can result in 12 months' imprisonment, permanent prohibition from acting as directors and restitution orders for consumer losses.
Transfer Pricing And AML / Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to taxable profits, a penalty of up to 50% of the adjustment plus interest, mandatory reporting to the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission, confiscation of prohibited payments and adverse public registry notation.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable under Gibraltar anti-money laundering and sanctions legislation, with travel restrictions, employment prohibitions, personal fines and imprisonment of up to 7 years under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar).
