Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office, Director of Income Tax, 1 Levanter House, Engineer Lane, Gibraltar; official e-service portal accessible through https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax-office. For import/export and goods clearance: Gibraltar HM Customs, HM Port of Gibraltar.

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company carrying on dangerous-goods freight forwarding from Gibraltar or with a Gibraltar permanent establishment must: (1) engage a Gibraltar-resident company secretary and registered office; (2) file branch registration (where applicable) with the Gibraltar Companies Registry; (3) notify the Income Tax Office within 30 days of commencing taxable activity using the authorized corporate tax registration form and obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TIN); (4) register with HM Customs as an economic operator/customs declarant where goods are imported, exported, or transhipped through the Port of Gibraltar; (5) obtain the requisite licensed freight-forwarder permit and any dangerous-goods / hazardous-cargo transport licence from the relevant Gibraltar licensing authority. There is no Gibraltar VAT identification number because Gibraltar does not operate a VAT/GST regime.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The Bermuda Exempted Company enjoys automatic Tax_Exempt_Zone status in Bermuda and no check-the-box election is available there. For Gibraltar tax purposes, a foreign entity may not elect its classification; if management and control are not exercised in Gibraltar it is treated as non-resident and taxed only on Gibraltar-source income, whereas if management and control are exercised in Gibraltar it is treated as Gibraltar-resident and taxed on worldwide income at the applicable Gibraltar corporate tax rate. There are no US-style check-the-box, fiscal-transparent, or hybrid-mismatch elections in Gibraltar for a Bermuda Exempted Company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Customs and Excise Duties (no VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax regime exists)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for a general indirect tax. No Gibraltar monetary turnover threshold triggers indirect tax registration; HM Customs registration is mandatory for any person importing, exporting, or handling controlled/dutiable goods, regardless of turnover. A taxable presence in Gibraltar for income tax purposes is determined by permanent establishment or source of income, not by turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST, and therefore there is no VAT zero-rating refund mechanism. Goods physically exported from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar customs/consumption duties where the export declaration is accepted by HM Customs and the goods are removed from Gibraltar within the prescribed period. Offshore freight-forwarding services supplied from Gibraltar are generally outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax. A Bermuda Exempted Company claiming duty-free export treatment must hold sufficient evidence that the dangerous goods were in free circulation and physically departed Gibraltar.

  • Validated Customs Export Declaration (electronic single administrative declaration submitted to HM Customs)
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and Incoterms evidence reflecting the export sale
  • Dangerous Goods Transport Document, including IMDG/IATA Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods where applicable
  • Approved dangerous-goods packing/container/packing certificate under IMDG Code or IATA DGR
  • Relevant Gibraltar import/export licence or permit for regulated hazardous chemicals
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT receipt evidencing settlement of the export proceeds

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time electronic fiscal invoicing, API-based tax clearance, or digital stamping. Customs declarations must be lodged electronically through the Gibraltar/EU customs procedures applicable to Gibraltar, with dangerous-goods consignments accompanied by electronic dangerous-goods notifications and IMDG/IATA/ADR compliant transport documentation. Invoices and supporting records must be retained for at least six years.

HM Customs may conduct post-clearance verification of export entries, and the Income Tax Office may audit underlying turnover and profit attribution for up to six years (indefinitely in cases of fraud). False export declarations or unsupported duty-free export claims can result in forfeiture of goods, monetary penalties up to the value of the duty evaded, claw-back of any tax/duty relief with interest, and criminal sanctions including fines and imprisonment under Gibraltar customs and income tax legislation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates a fully convertible current and capital account: no exchange controls, no prior approval for dividend or profit remittances, no restrictions on intercompany funding, and no distinction between resident and non-resident accounts for tax or FX purposes; GIP/GBP parity removes bespoke sterling conversion friction. Bermuda, as the entity's home regime, treats a Bermuda Exempted Company as non-resident when designated by the BMA, thereby exempting all foreign-currency receipts, capital injections, and profit distributions from surrender, exchange-control consents, or reporting thresholds, provided the company does not trade with Bermuda residents and maintains its records in a non-resident-eligible currency (typically USD, GBP, or EUR). Remittances from the Gibraltar operating branch to the Bermuda parent and onward to a UK beneficial owner face no statutory royalty, dividend withholding, or remittance tax in either Gibraltar or Bermuda, subject only to UK anti-avoidance and CFC rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar: no statutory surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion of export receipts, and no deadline for repatriating freight-revenue proceeds; currency of retention is unrestricted. Bermuda: a non-resident-designated exempted company is exempt from the BMA's surrender requirements applicable to resident companies; export proceeds from DG freight forwarding on international routes must simply be booked through a licensed BMA-recognized or GFSC-licensed bank and, where receipts are received in US dollars, frameworks must provide for settlement in FX or maintain the USD ledger position without mandatory local conversion. The UK, as ultimate sovereign state, imposes no exchange-control regime affecting Gibraltar or Bermuda trade receivables.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no central bank of its own; banking supervision and AML/CFT oversight sit with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019, while currency issuance rests with the Government of Gibraltar pursuant to the Currency Notes Act 1934, with the Gibraltar pound (GIP) pegged at par to GBP and Bank of England banknotes legal tender. For the Bermuda-incorporated entity, the Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) is the exchange-control and monetary authority under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Bermuda Monetary Authority Act 1969, and must issue a non-resident designation for the exempted company to achieve full offshore convertibility.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-to-Medium for primary relationship; Medium as a supplemental disbursement rail. Gibraltar-licensed and UK/FCA-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, or Modulr can offer multi-currency IBANs and cheaper SWIFT-equivalent rails, but most core EMIs decline ISIC 5229 dangerous-goods freight forwarders at onboarding or cap monthly throughput due to high-risk NACE correlation. A GFSC-licensed payment services provider with an explicit trade-supply-chain vertical, or a UK EMI that accepts freight forwarders (e.g., a platform specialized in logistics accounts), may be viable as a sub-limit settlement account only, never as the sole treasury vehicle, because EMIs lack credit, LC issuance, and correspondent-bank depth needed for DG forwarding guarantees and documentary collections.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound protocol: the Bermuda exempted company, as a non-resident, injects equity or subordinated shareholder loans from its Bermuda treasury account or from the UK parent via SWIFT MT103; wire instructions must state purpose code 'Capital Subscription' or 'Intercompany Loan' (where applicable) to avoid misclassification; a board resolution approving the capital injection, a signed loan agreement (if debt), and a certificate of non-resident designation must be lodged with the GFSC-regulated bank. Funds are routed through a Tier-1 correspondent (e.g., through the Bank of England infrastructure and CHAPS for GBP, or via CBRFSP/SEPA and SWIFT for EUR/USD) and credited to the Gibraltar operational account; no exchange-control approval is required, but all inbound wires above USD 50,000 are screened against Gibraltar sanctions and AML filters, and beneficial-owner verification is repeated on each injection.Trade disbursements for DG forwarding settlements are executed via three prioritized clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 single credit transfers for freight payments, customs duties, and port charges, with structured remittance information encoding the invoice reference, bill-of-lading number, and ISIC 5229 operational code to facilitate counterparty reconciliation; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700/MT707) issued by the GFSC-licensed bank or a UK confirming bank for high-value inbound cargo purchases where the supplier requires documentary assurance, with DG certificates and packing-list terms embedded as required documents; and (3) bank-guaranteed payment undertakings (e.g., 'payable at sight via MT799/MT760') for bond-required lanes. For intra-SEPA and GBP-denominated payments, Gibraltar-issued IBANs clear through the pan-European clearing system and CHAPS respectively, while USD disbursements route through a New York correspondent with Fedwire/CHIPS finality; all outbound disbursements must carry an explicit purpose code matching the nature of the underlying DG trade to avoid false-flag transaction monitoring.Outright FX Forwards for GIP/GBP at 1:1 parity arbitrage and for USD/EUR-based freight receivables against GBP operating costs, executed with GFSC-licensed or UK Investment Firm-regulated counterparties under ISDA-CSA documentationNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for emerging-market currency exposures (e.g., USD/TRY, USD/CNH, USD/INR) encountered on Asia-to-Europe dangerous-goods lanes, where cash settlement in GIP/GBP is performed without physical deliveryCross-Currency Swaps (XCCY) for swapping EUR nominal freight revenue streams into GBP to match operational liabilities, permissible under Gibraltar MiFID-style rules and requiring no exchange-control approvalEuropean-Style FX Options for hedging catastrophic volatility on large USD-denominated DG contracts, permitted where the option premium is booked at fair value and the counterparty holds an appropriate regulatory license

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation is mandatory where the aggregate value of controlled transactions with associated enterprises exceeds GIP 1,000,000 per chargeable period. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) is required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000. Documentation must be filed or produced within 30 days of a formal notice from the Commissioner.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies OECD-aligned economic substance requirements under the Income Tax Act 2010 and the EU Code of Conduct Group commitments. A tax-resident company must demonstrate adequate premises, employees, and active management presence in Gibraltar proportionate to its income-generating activities. For the ISIC 5229 dangerous goods freight forwarding business, substance is evidenced by: a locally licensed operating office, the statutory Dangerous Goods (DG) safety and hazardous-cargo permits, qualified logistics/HSEQ personnel employed in Gibraltar, board meetings held in Gibraltar, and operational control of invoices, contracts, bank accounts and DG compliance files. A shell arrangement with no local operational footprint will fail the substance doctrine, notwithstanding the Bermuda exempted company status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration of Management and Control

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties of up to 100% of the tax understated plus monthly interest at 1.5%; on criminal conviction for tax fraud, an unlimited fine and imprisonment of up to 7 years. The Commissioner may also issue a determination and commence distress action against company assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns or direct the concealment of Gibraltar-source profits may be jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall; personal civil recovery orders, travel restrictions, and disqualification as a director for up to 15 years under the Companies Act 2014; imprisonment for criminal tax fraud.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial or revocation of the Tax Residency Certificate, retrospective withdrawal of tax residence benefits, reassessment at 10% on deemed economic profits, mandatory strike-off and dissolution under the Companies Act, referral to the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit for AML investigation, and inclusion of Gibraltar on adverse EU/OECD listings

Director personal liability: Directors and ultimate beneficial owners face personal liability for unpaid taxes on wind-up; civil penalties for reckless trading; asset freezing orders; travel bans pending tax and AML investigations; personal criminal fines and imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering predicated on tax evasion.
AML / CFT Breach in Dangerous Goods Trade

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties imposed by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) of up to GIP 2,000,000 or higher for serious AML failures; suspension or revocation of the DG forwarding statutory license; criminal prosecution for money laundering with unlimited fines and imprisonment of up to 14 years; forfeiture of all assets connected to the offending transactions

Director personal liability: Directors and the Gibraltar Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) can be prosecuted personally for failure to report suspicious transactions; personal criminal liability with imprisonment up to 14 years; civil restitution of unlawfully obtained proceeds; orders for disqualification from managing companies and travel bans pending extradition or trial.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment by the Commissioner with tax charged at 10% on the adjusted profits; penalties of up to 100% of the additional tax assessed plus interest at 1.5% per month; potential secondary adjustment and disallowance of excessive interest deductions under thin capitalization rules

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's-length intercompany contracts may be held liable for the additional tax and penalties; the court may order personal contribution to the company's tax liabilities as a fraudulent trading remedy; director disqualification orders of up to 15 years may be imposed.
