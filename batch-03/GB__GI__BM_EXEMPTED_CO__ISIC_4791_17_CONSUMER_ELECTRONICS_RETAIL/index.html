Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar: HM Government of Gibraltar - Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax, tax portal: ito.gov.gi) and HM Customs Gibraltar (Customs House); UK (market/import VAT nexus): HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC), UK VAT Registration Portal via gov.uk.

E-government filing portal

A foreign Bermuda Exempted Co must: (1) appoint a Gibraltar-resident company secretary and maintain a Gibraltar registered office; (2) register at Companies House Gibraltar (CSIG) as an oversea company / branch under the Gibraltar Companies Act to obtain the company registration number used as the base Tax Reference; (3) submit the prescribed notification form with the Income Tax Office, together with the Bermuda Certificate of Exemption, constitutional documents and central-management-and-control declaration, to obtain the Gibraltar TRN; (4) comply with annual income tax return filing (territorial basis, 12.5% rate) if any Gibraltar-source trade/profits arise; (5) register separately with HM Customs Gibraltar for imports/exports only if goods physically move through Gibraltar; (6) if the entity makes UK taxable supplies of goods situated in the UK at point of sale, it must register for UK VAT with HMRC using form VAT1 / overseas-seller route regardless of turnover.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (the Bermuda Exempted Company is exempt from Bermuda income tax under its Certificate of Exemption; in Gibraltar it is a non-resident foreign company taxable only on a territorial basis for Gibraltar-source profits; it is not a pass-through entity and is not automatically subject to UK corporation tax or Gibraltar entity-level tax absent a permanent establishment / trade carried on in jurisdiction).

Available election forms

Bermuda: annual renewal election for the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act (EUTPA) certificate to lock in zero Bermuda tax. Gibraltar: no check-the-box regime after abolition of the old exempt-company status (2010); however, an eligible foreign-incorporated entity may make a non-residence declaration with the Income Tax Office to confirm central management and control outside Gibraltar; Gibraltar Companies Act s.71(1) election is available for wholly foreign-owned non-trading companies to be treated as non-resident; ATAD anti-hybrid notification/election requirements (Gibraltar Income Tax (Anti-hybrid) Rules) apply to any hybrid instruments or reverse hybrid mismatch outcomes; UK CFC small profits exemptions may be claimed where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar: no VAT, GST or general sales-use tax; indirect taxation limited to customs/excise duties (Import Duties Act, Excise Ordinance) and minor taxes (insurance premium tax, environmental levies). UK (GB) market nexus: Value Added Tax (VAT) at 20% standard rate on supplies/imports of goods located in the UK at time of sale.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: N/A (no VAT/GST system; no turnover threshold; customs registration only when goods are imported/exported through Gibraltar). UK nexus: GBP 85,000 general UK VAT registration threshold (rolling 12 months); however, for a non-UK established (overseas) seller supplying goods situated in the UK at time of sale, registration is mandatory from the first supply (no threshold); for imports of consignments up to GBP 135 sold via online marketplaces, the online marketplace (not Stripe/PayPal as payment providers) is deemed to supply and must account for UK import VAT at point of sale; UK import VAT and customs duty registration are triggered by physical importation of the goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

UK VAT export zero-rating (VATA 1994 s.30(6)) requires: (i) the supplier must be a UK VAT-registered taxable person; (ii) goods are physically exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK (EU, third countries, or Gibraltar) within three months of the supply; (iii) the supplier obtains and retains prescribed export evidence (customs MRN, shipping documentation evidencing departure, customer details); and (iv) title and risk pass to the overseas customer outside the UK. Offshore B2B services supplied to non-UK business customers are generally outside the scope of UK VAT / subject to reverse charge rather than subject to VAT; zero-rating or refund substantiation is only available where the Bermuda Exempted Co is itself a UK VAT-registered person with a UK establishment; a purely offshore seller without registration has no UK VAT to refund, though UK import VAT paid on cross-border low-value consignments may be reclaimed only via registered/importer-of-record arrangements.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD Form C88 / Goods Export Declaration with Movement Reference Number (MRN) via HMRC Customs Declaration Service CDS)
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical export from UK and destination outside the UK
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Settlement Records (SWIFT MT103 or Stripe/PayPal payout statements) evidencing export sales proceeds and FX repatriation
  • Commercial Invoice (with Incoterms, consignee and goods description)
  • Packing List
  • Proof of Export / Carrier Receipt (POD) confirming goods left the UK
  • Payment gateway transaction logs (Stripe/PayPal) and customer order records

Gibraltar: no e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate; bookkeeping and invoicing records must be retained for at least six years for Income Tax Office/customs audit. UK: HM Revenue and Customs Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT requires VAT-registered businesses to maintain digital records and file VAT returns via MTD-compatible software; the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) is the mandatory electronic platform for UK export/import declarations, replacing CHIEF; no QR code / government fiscal stamp applies to standard commercial e-invoicing.

Export zero-rating claims are strictly audited by HMRC; the supplier bears the burden of proof. Inadequate, forged or inconsistent documentation triggers retrospective VAT clawback assessments for up to 4 years (extended to 20 years where fraud or negligence is proven), plus interest. Accuracy-related penalties under FA 2007 Schedule 24 may reach 100% of the potential lost revenue and are increased for deliberate concealment; dishonest evasion attracts criminal prosecution under CEMA 1979 with unlimited fines and up to 7 years' imprisonment. On the Gibraltar side, the Income Tax Office may raise discovery assessments (Income Tax Act s.50), apply late-filing and negligence penalties (up to GBP 3,000 plus 100% of tax), and HM Customs Gibraltar may forfeit goods/conveyances and levy penalties of up to treble the goods' value. Where online marketplaces are used, HMRC may hold the marketplace jointly and severally liable for unpaid VAT of overseas sellers under VATA 1994 s.77A / FA 2019 s.44, and the Bermuda exempt status does not shield the underlying economic activity from UK/Gibraltar indirect tax audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains a fully open capital and current account with no foreign-exchange controls. GIP is freely convertible at par into GBP, and banks transact in GBP, EUR, USD, and major trade currencies without restriction. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, redemption of share capital, and intra-group payments from the Bermuda Exempted Company can be repatriated to any jurisdiction without prior central-bank or GFSC approval. The only practical hurdles are bank-level source-of-funds review, correspondent-bank compliance, and standard documentation, rather than any statutory convertibility limit. The entity may freely hold EUR and USD settlement balances from Stripe and PayPal without a surrender obligation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement on export proceeds or cross-border sales receipts. The consumer-electronics e-commerce entity may retain its USD, EUR, and GBP collections indefinitely in its Gibraltar treasury accounts. The only applicable requirement is an internal, risk-based AML obligation to maintain traceable sales records, e-commerce platform settlement reports, and supplier documentation sufficient to evidence the underlying commercial transactions.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory banking and payments regulator; there is no independent central bank. Currency issuance is undertaken by HM Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act, and the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at par 1:1 to the pound sterling (GBP). Monetary and FX policy effectively tracks the Bank of England sterling framework, while banking and AML supervision sits with the GFSC under the Financial Services Act 2019.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium as a settlement conduit, Low as the primary treasury concentration rail. Post-Brexit, UK-authorized electronic money institutions (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise) do not passport into Gibraltar, and Gibraltar is not an EU/EEA member. Offshore EMIs licensed in Lithuania, Ireland, Malta, or the UK may onboard a Bermuda Exempted Company operating through Gibraltar under their own remote-KYC acceptance criteria, but they typically reject high-value capital injections, impose restrictive card-not-present flow monitoring, and cannot issue or clear GIP. A GFSC-licensed payment service provider under the Financial Services Act 2019 is not a full EMI license; therefore, a UK or EU EMI is viable only as an inbound collections sweep (receiving Stripe/PayPal payouts) into the GFSC-regulated concentration bank. Legal constraint: no passporting right exists for UK/EU EMIs into Gibraltar; onboarding is on a per-client, peril-to-risk basis.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital is injected via SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.009 into the Gibraltar Capital Escrow Account. Field 70 must state the fund-movement purpose using the ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' (Equity) or 'FSEQ' together with the narrative 'SHAREHOLDER CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION TO BERMUDA EXEMPTED CO'. The receiving bank requires a board resolution authorizing the share allotment, the Bermuda share register, source-of-funds evidence from the shareholder (bank statements, net-worth certificate, or audited group financials), and an undertaking that funds will remain unpurchased until AML clearance. No central-bank or exchange-control filing is required in Gibraltar. After the source-of-funds review is completed, the treasury transfers the funds from the Capital Escrow Account to the Operational Account via internal journal referencing the capital-apportionment ledger.Supplier settlements for consumer-electronics inventory are executed through SWIFT GPI MT103 transfers from the Gibraltar concentration account, using a USD correspondent bank (e.g., JPMorgan or Citibank) or through HSBC/Standard Chartered for CNY and offshore RMB clearing via CIPS where suppliers accept CNH. For high-value recurring production batches, the Gibraltar bank may issue a commercial letter of credit (sight or 60-day usance) in favor of the Hong Kong or Shenzhen beneficiary, supported by commercial invoice, packing list, airway bill or bill of lading, and inspection certificate. EUR consumer refunds and VAT payments are cleared through a SEPA-capable European correspondent account of the Gibraltar bank; GBP refunds are sent via UK Faster Payments through a UK clearing partner. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium as a settlement conduit, Low as the primary treasury concentration rail. Post-Brexit, UK-authorized electronic money institutions (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise) do not passport into Gibraltar, and Gibraltar is not an EU/EEA member. Offshore EMIs licensed in Lithuania, Ireland, Malta, or the UK may onboard a Bermuda Exempted Company operating through Gibraltar under their own remote-KYC acceptance criteria, but they typically reject high-value capital injections, impose restrictive card-not-present flow monitoring, and cannot issue or clear GIP. A GFSC-licensed payment service provider under the Financial Services Act 2019 is not a full EMI license; therefore, a UK or EU EMI is viable only as an inbound collections sweep (receiving Stripe/PayPal payouts) into the GFSC-regulated concentration bank. Legal constraint: no passporting right exists for UK/EU EMIs into Gibraltar; onboarding is on a per-client, peril-to-risk basis.
