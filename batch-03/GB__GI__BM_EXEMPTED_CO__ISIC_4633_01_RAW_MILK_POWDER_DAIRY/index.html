Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is freely convertible and pegged at par to GBP; GIP and GBP banknotes circulate interchangeably within Gibraltar, and there are no exchange controls on current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittance for a non-resident exempted company is unrestricted, subject only to CRS/FATCA reporting, anti-money-laundering evidentiary requirements, and corporate solvency tests. On the Bermuda side, the entity's designation as 'non-resident' under the BMA General Permission disapplies the requirement to convert foreign currency receipts into domestic currency and permits unrestricted retention, borrowing, and dividend distribution in any approved currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Gibraltar for either resident or non-resident commercial entities; exporters may retain receivables in any freely convertible currency. For Bermuda Exchange-Control purposes, the exempted company's non-resident classification fully exempts it from the historical resident-company obligation to sell foreign currency (typically within approximately one month of receipt) to an authorized dealer, so the statutory timeline is not triggered for this vehicle.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar does not operate a domestic central bank; monetary authority functions for the sterling zone are exercised by HM Treasury and the Bank of England, while prudential banking supervision is delegated to the Financial Services Commission (Gibraltar). Exchange-control administration over the Bermuda Exempted Company itself is vested in the Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and the Exchange Control Regulations 1973.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for pre-funded collection floats, FX conversion, and operational petty-cash management; Low and not viable for trade-finance instruments. Legally and commercially, PSD/e-money licensed digital EMIs cannot issue or confirm documentary Letters of Credit, cannot route MT103 documentary trade settlements with full SWIFT UETR integrity, and generally decline Bermuda Exempted Companies with nominee-director structures, offshore holding matrices, or agri-commodity invoice chains due to automated AML filters. Such EMIs should therefore be used only as ancillary receivables channels, never as the primary clearing rail.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

10% (single flat corporate rate under the Income Tax Act 2010, as amended by the Income Tax (Amendment) Act 2011; no progressive brackets apply to companies)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. Gibraltar does not impose withholding tax, income tax, or remittance charge on dividends distributed by a Gibraltar-resident company; a Bermuda Exempted Company receiving profit from its Gibraltar trading vehicle is paid free of Gibraltar withholding under the territorial exemption for foreign income.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return and self-assessment payment are due within 12 months after the end of the chargeable period or accounting period (e.g. a company with a 31 December year-end must file by 31 December of the following calendar year); no extension is available without formal notice from the Commissioner.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due within 12 months after the end of the accounting period, concurrent with the CT-1 filing window; no grace period for foreign-incorporated Bermuda Exempted Companies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 2,000,000 (Gibraltar Pound, pegged 1:1 to GBP). A Local File is mandatory for a Gibraltar-resident company or permanent establishment whose aggregate controlled transactions (purchases, sales, commissions, financing) with related parties exceed GIP 2,000,000 in a chargeable period; a Master File is required when consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000; Country-by-Country notification applies at the same EUR 750,000,000 consolidated revenue threshold. Entities below these thresholds must still retain a contemporaneous transfer pricing study where any transaction category exceeds GIP 500,000 to support the Box 4 territorial exclusion.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations 2019 (as amended), consistent with the OECD Inclusive Framework and the EU listing process: a relevant entity carrying on 'Distribution and Service Centre' activity must maintain adequate employee headcount, premises, and annual expenditure in Gibraltar, and hold board meetings physically in Gibraltar at a frequency proportional to the volume of milk powder trading. The Bermuda Exempted Company wrapper does not displace ESR obligations: where central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, the company is a Gibraltar tax resident relevant entity and must satisfy the full economic substance test; failure converts the 0% territorial exclusion into a 10% tax assessment, with penalties and automatic exchange of information exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Cheating the Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2010 (Gibraltar), the Commissioner may raise assessments on a just and reasonable basis, add interest at 8.5% per annum, and impose penalties up to 100% of the tax undercharged with no reduction for deliberate concealment. Criminal prosecution under the Crimes Act 2011 (Gibraltar) for cheating the revenue carries up to seven years' imprisonment and unlimited fines; confiscation orders are available under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 to recover the value of the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Where the company is an instrument of fraud, the court pierces the corporate veil: directors and the beneficial owner become jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax; the Supreme Court of Gibraltar may grant worldwide freezing orders, travel bans, and passport surrender; a finding of fraudulent trading under section 514 of the Companies Act 2014 exposes directors to personal civil liability for the company's debts and automatic disqualification for up to 15 years.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Breach

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may re-characterise the entire group margin as Gibraltar-source profit chargeable at 10%, impose ESR penalties of GIP 1,000 plus GIP 500 per day up to GIP 25,000, refer the entity to the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission for regulatory censure, strike the company off under the Companies Act 2014, and exchange the substance file with over 40 jurisdictions under the OECD Order. Treaty relief under the UK-Gibraltar DTA is denied where treaty shopping is established.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a false ESR-1 or fail to ensure substance face personal criminal fines and imprisonment of up to two years; in liquidation, the court may require directors to contribute to the company's assets for wrongful trading under section 513 of the Companies Act 2014; disqualified directors of a shell cannot hold office in any regulated company for 15 years; travel restrictions are available where outstanding assessments exceed GIP 100,000.
AML, Sanctions, and Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar) criminalises the possession or transfer of criminal property: a body corporate is liable to an unlimited fine; individuals face up to 14 years' imprisonment for money laundering and up to 10 years for failure to disclose suspicious transactions. The Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act (Gibraltar) enables asset freezing, account forfeiture, and revocation of banking facilities; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission may impose administrative fines of up to 10% of group turnover and suspend the entity's licence under the AML Ordinance 2011.

Director personal liability: Directors, the MLRO, and the compliance officer incur personal liability where they know or suspect laundering and fail to file a Suspicious Activity Report within the statutory timeframe; civil recovery proceedings may pursue personal assets where the corporate veil is pierced for sham transactions; criminal travel bans, passport surrender, and extradition under the UK-Gibraltar mutual legal assistance arrangements are available; directors are exposed to secondary liability for the full value of laundered funds plus costs.
