Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP is pegged at 1:1 to GBP and both GIP and sterling circulate freely as legal tender in Gibraltar. The United Kingdom abolished exchange controls in 1979, and Gibraltar and Bermuda operate fully liberalised current-account and capital-account regimes. SaaS subscription income invoiced through Stripe Billing in USD, GBP, or EUR is freely convertible and may be held in multi-currency or base-currency accounts; there is no central-bank approval requirement for profit remittance, intercompany loan repayment, or shareholder distributions. Bermuda's currency (BMD) is pegged at 1:1 to the US dollar with no surrender, capital-export, or convertibility restrictions applicable to an Exempted Company.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation applies to digital-service export proceeds in Gibraltar, the United Kingdom, or Bermuda. Proceeds from subscription-based email-newsletter infrastructure and hosting services may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts. The sole compliance obligations are (i) GFSC anti-money-laundering record-keeping for transactions at or above the EUR 10,000 equivalent reporting threshold, (ii) Bank of England external-statistics transaction reporting performed by the banking counterparty, and (iii) Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018 annual filings confirming that the Exempted Company carries on relevant activity with adequate substance outside Bermuda.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (London) as the sovereign monetary and exchange-control authority for pound sterling; Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the statutory banking and payment-system regulator under the Financial Services Act 2019 (Gibraltar); Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) as the home-territory regulator of the Bermuda Exempted Company; Government of Gibraltar Currency Office (Commissioner of Currency) acting as the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) currency-board issuer at a fixed 1:1 GBP parity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for the collection tier and Medium for the primary operating tier. FCA-authorised UK EMIs (Wise Business, Currencycloud/Klarna, Airwallex, Modulr, PPS) and GFSC-licensed Gibraltar EMIs may accept the Bermuda Exempted Company provided the natural-person directors and >25% UBOs pass enhanced CDD and the Bermuda Tax Exemption Certificate is presented. Legal constraints: post-Brexit EEA passporting of UK-authorised EMIs has ceased, but Gibraltar retains qualifying-territory status under FSMA 2000, allowing a Gibraltar-licensed bank or EMI to service UK clients; the Bermuda Exempted Company itself is outside EEA/UK authorisation, so EMIs must remain strictly collection-and-disbursement gateways and must not be treated as the liquidity-reserve tier. Any arrangement causing the EMI to hold third-party card-transaction funds on the company's own account would trigger a GFSC payment-services licensing analysis that the low-risk SaaS model must avoid.

Late filing exposes the company to a fixed penalty of up to GIP 1,000 plus a daily default penalty of GIP 60 per day for continuing non-compliance; negligent or fraudulent returns attract a tax-geared penalty of up to 100% of the under-assessed tax with interest, and deliberate tax evasion is a criminal offence carrying fines up to GIP 10,000 and custodial sanctions for responsible officers.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 in aggregate cross-border controlled transactions in an accounting period triggers the mandatory preparation of a Local File; a Master File is required where the multinational group prepares consolidated accounts with total turnover of at least GIP 750,000,000, with Country-by-Country reporting imposed on the Gibraltar ultimate parent entity at the same threshold.

Economic substance scope: The Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018 applies to the Bermuda Exempted Company, which will be classified for the email infrastructure business as an intellectual property business or a distribution and service centre depending on the final allocation of core income generating activities; the company must maintain a physical office, qualified employees, and board meetings in Bermuda proportionate to those activities and must file an annual Economic Substance Return with the Registrar. The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations equally apply if the company is Gibraltar tax resident, and the UK-Bermuda competent authority exchange provisions prevent the company from obtaining treaty benefits without a demonstrated alignment of substance and activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Return Filing

Statutory sanction: Tax-geared penalty of up to 100% of the under-assessed tax, statutory interest, criminal fines up to GIP 10,000 per offence, and imprisonment at the discretion of the Gibraltar courts for wilful evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or authorise a false return are jointly and severally liable for the civil tax-geared penalty, may be disqualified from acting as directors, and can be subjected to court-ordered travel restrictions and custodial sentences under the Proceeds of Crime Act.
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda economic substance penalties of USD 10,000 rising to USD 250,000, and up to USD 500,000 for intellectual property activity; the Gibraltar Commissioner may raise a best-of-judgment assessment on worldwide profits at 12.5%; information is exchangeable with the UK under the Bermuda-UK exchange of information arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for the accuracy of the Economic Substance Declaration, civil recovery of unpaid Gibraltar tax if the company is struck off, and criminal sanctions for knowingly filing false substance declarations.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to GIP 500,000 per breach under Gibraltar AML legislation, freezing of corporate accounts by the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit, and fines up to USD 25,000 against the Bermuda corporate service provider under the Beneficial Ownership Act 2017.

Director personal liability: Directors and the licensed Bermuda registered agent may be individually fined and imprisoned for up to 5 years for money laundering facilitation; the Gibraltar courts may impose personal travel bans during investigation.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment at 12.5% with late-payment interest, tax-geared penalty up to 100% of the additional tax, and fixed penalties of GIP 1,000 plus GIP 60 per day where the Local File is not produced during audit.

Director personal liability: The director who signs the transfer pricing declaration is jointly and severally liable for the resulting adjustment, and deliberate falsification of the schedule constitutes a criminal offence leading to director disqualification and custodial penalties.
Failure to File Gibraltar Return and Unauthorised Trading Through a Permanent Establishment

Statutory sanction: Best-of-judgment assessment by the Commissioner, full recovery of tax, fixed and daily default penalties, distress enforcement against Gibraltar assets, and referral for strike-off of the local deregistration.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable following an application to the Gibraltar court where the company is dissolved, and the transfer of assets to a successor entity without settling the permanent establishment tax is actionable as a fraudulent disposition.
