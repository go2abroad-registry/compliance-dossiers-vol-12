Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with Territorial_Scope in Gibraltar; Tax_Exempt_Zone in Bermuda. The BM Exempted Co is a non-resident foreign corporation for Gibraltar tax purposes and is assessable to Gibraltar corporation tax at 12.5% only on profits arising in or derived from Gibraltar through its branch/permanent establishment. The Bermuda-incorporated parent remains exempt from Bermuda tax on offshore income under Bermuda's Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 2014 and Companies Act 1981. No pass-through or look-through treatment applies; the entity is a separate juridical person. If central management and control is moved to Gibraltar, Gibraltar residence could expose worldwide profits to Gibraltar corporation tax, subject to the UK-Gibraltar Double Taxation Arrangement.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no US-style check-the-box classification election and has not enacted hybrid mismatch elective rules. Statutory electable mechanisms relevant to ISIC 7020 advisory income include: (a) election of a designated accounting period for tax-return purposes; (b) group loss-surrender election under the Income Tax Act 2010 for Gibraltar groups; (c) management expenses election for holding/investment companies; (d) participation exemption treatment under the Companies (Participation Exemption) Regulations 2011, which applies on satisfaction of the ordinary-taxation and shareholding conditions, with no formal election required; (e) the Gibraltar IP Box election (Income Tax (Intellectual Property Regime) Regulations 2015) granting an 80% deduction on qualifying patent/know-how income to an effective 2.5% corporation tax rate; and (f) where dual residency is asserted, invocation of the tie-breaker and mutual-agreement procedures under the UK-Gibraltar DTA (2003). No countervailing hybrid mismatch election is available; BEPS Action 2 recommendations are not separately domesticated.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax applies in Gibraltar. Indirect taxation is limited to Gibraltar Customs Duty (import tariffs under the UK-Gibraltar customs union), Excise Duty on alcohol, tobacco and fuel, and Stamp Duty on instruments relating to Gibraltar real property and certain transactions. Gibraltar is expressly outside the UK VAT system and was excluded from the EU VAT common system.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; there is no VAT registration threshold in Gibraltar. Businesses importing controlled or dutiable goods must be licensed by the Gibraltar Customs Service, and an Excise Licence is required for alcohol, tobacco and fuel operators; thresholds are licence-based rather than turnover-based. Stamp Duty arises on the execution of specified instruments. Local currency: Gibraltar Pound (GIP), pegged at 1:1 to GBP.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable; Gibraltar levies no VAT, therefore no zero-rating claim or VAT-refund mechanism exists. For customs purposes, goods exported from Gibraltar attract no Gibraltar duty. For direct-tax purposes, fee income from international tax structuring and transfer-pricing advisory services (ISIC 7020) rendered to non-Gibraltar clients is excluded from the Gibraltar tax charge when the services are performed outside Gibraltar (foreign-source income), whereas services physically performed by the Gibraltar branch remain assessable on territorial profits at 12.5%. Import duty relief may be available only for qualifying goods subsequently re-exported.

  • Customs Export Declaration (required only for tangible exports or re-shipments; not applicable to pure cross-border service revenue streams)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (evidence of physical movement where goods accompany the services; digital delivery logs and server records are used for remote advisory deliverables)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103/MT202 credit advices and bank statements evidencing receipt of fees from abroad, substantiating foreign-source classification)

Gibraltar has no real-time fiscal clearance regime, no B2B or B2C electronic-mandate requirement, and no digital stamping platform. Tax invoices must nevertheless bear full statutory particulars (supplier legal name and TRN, purchaser, description, date, amount) and records must be retained for six years. Customs declarations for the import and export of goods are lodged electronically via the Gibraltar Customs electronic system, including transit declarations under the UK-Gibraltar customs union; cross-border service evidence uses bank-wire and credit-card acquiring records as audit trail.

Mischaracterisation of Gibraltar-source advisory profits as foreign-source, or artificial round-tripping of fees through the Bermuda parent, falls within the Gibraltar GAAR (Income Tax Act 2010 s. 40A, tax arrangement lacking genuine commercial substance). Incorrect or fraudulent returns attract penalties of up to 100% of the tax liability plus statutory interest under s. 97, with a 20-year discovery window for fraud and a 6-year window for negligent misstatement. Deliberate failure to register as a foreign company additionally incurs fines under the Companies Act (Cap. 100). Misuse of the participation exemption triggers retrospective clawback of the exempt income plus surcharges; any UK-Gibraltar DTA relief claimed on false documentation is void ab initio with restitution of tax and costs.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The GIP benefits from full de jure and de facto current-account and capital-account convertibility because the currency-board arrangement with the Bank of England backs every issued GIP with GBP reserves at a one-to-one ratio; Gibraltar applies no foreign-exchange surrender requirements, and there are no restrictions on profit remittances, dividend distributions, interest, or royalties paid by a Gibraltar-established operation to its Bermuda Exempted Company shareholder, provided the paying entity demonstrates economic substance and complies with Gibraltar's taxation and CRS/GAAR reporting regimes; the Bermuda Exempted Company itself engages no statutory convertibility restrictions when holding multi-currency accounts (GBP, EUR, USD) through Gibraltar clearing participants.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Gibraltar or the United Kingdom to export receipts or fee income earned by the tax-structuring consultancy, because both jurisdictions repealed their post-war exchange-control statutes and maintain fully liberalised capital movements; 0% of foreign-currency export proceeds must be surrendered to the central authority; the only practical discipline is bank-led: receipts in non-GBP currencies may be automatically converted to GIP/GBP unless the customer instructs a foreign-currency account to be held, and such retention requires the bank to evidence an underlying commercial need (e.g., future USD/EUR disbursements) for the continued holding of the foreign balance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (HM Treasury, United Kingdom) is the ultimate monetary authority for the Gibraltar Pound (GIP), which is issued by the Government of Gibraltar Currency Unit under a fixed parity of 1 GIP = 1 GBP; prudential and financial-conduct supervision of banks, EMIs, and FX intermediaries in Gibraltar is vested in the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Ordinance 2018 (as amended); residual UK exchange-control authority under the Exchange Control Act 1947 is inoperative and has been substantially repealed in both the United Kingdom and Gibraltar.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share-capital injections from the Bermuda Exempted Company's shareholders or parent treasury are routed as SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bank-to-bank cover) to the Gibraltar bank account with beneficiary detail 'Bermuda Exempted Company — Gibraltar Branch, Capital Escrow Sub-Account'; the remittance must carry a narrative statement 'UNPAID SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / CAPITAL INJECTION' with reference to the Bermuda Companies Act 1981 section under which shares are allotted; the beneficiary bank will request a board resolution authorising the account and identifying signatories, a shareholder resolution approving the capital call, and a share-allotment register update; where funds exceed GBP 100,000 equivalent, the bank requires full source-of-funds documentation, and for USD wires routed via the US clearing network an OFAC confirmation is required; because Gibraltar applies no exchange control, the funds may be credited directly in original currency to a multi-currency account without mandatory conversion, and the account should be coded as 'Capital Escrow Account' to prevent operational drawdown from co-mingling the capital base.Operational disbursements (advisory subcontractors, regulatory fees, rent, professional-indemnity insurance, and tax payments to the Gibraltar income-tax authorities) are cleared via SWIFT MT103 for international payments with purpose narrative 'SERVICE FEES / OPERATING EXPENSES'; domestic GBP payments to Gibraltar suppliers use CHAPS (same-day) or Faster Payments via the bank's UK clearing participation; EUR disbursements use SEPA Credit Transfer with the beneficiary's IBAN under Gibraltar's SEPA participation; Commercial Letters of Credit are ordinarily unnecessary for consultancy services, but standby LCs or bank guarantees may be issued as performance security where the advisory contract requires them, with underlying settlement remaining a wire in final funds; the disbursement schema should require dual authorisation, a whitelist of approved counterparties, and periodic reconciliation against the capital escrow ledger to preserve the escrow classification.Deliverable foreign exchange forwards (FXA) with tenors of up to 12 months for GIP/GBP versus EUR and USD exposures, executed under an ISDA/FX Master Agreement or the bank's standard terms with a GFSC- or FCA-licensed counterparty and requiring documented underlying transaction exposureCross-currency interest rate swaps (CIRS) for medium-term USD/EUR balance-sheet hedging of capital flows, subject to ISDA Master plus Credit Support Annex and a board-authorised treasury mandateVanilla FX options and zero-cost collars purchased on a hedge-only basis, limited to the amount of forecast fee invoices or budgeted capital disbursements, with no speculative open positions permitted under the Bermuda Exempted Company's treasury policyNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not generally permissible or market-available for GIP/GBP because the currency-board arrangement guarantees full convertibility; NDFs would be permitted only if the bank confirms a specific exotic-currency receivable and a licensed counterparty offers the instrument

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-LOW as a supplementary rail only. UK-FCA authorised EMI platforms and multi-currency treasury providers generally decline a Bermuda Exempted Company as a CDD/UBO-obscure offshore corporate, and none offers GIP-denominated accounts or direct Gibraltar connectivity under SEPA participation; Revolut Business and Wise Business may accept after provision of a full Bermuda Beneficial Ownership Register extract, Certificate of Incumbency, and evidence of Gibraltar substance, but EMI pricing and virtual-IBAN availability do not support capital-escrow or high-value cross-border injection flows; the EMI rail should be limited to receivable collection and disbursement of sub-threshold operational invoices, with SWIFT clearing through a licensed bank retained as the primary rail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar has no separate statutory transfer pricing filing threshold; from a UK perspective, a local file and master file are expected where connected-party transactions exceed GBP 1,000,000 in aggregate in the accounting period, and Country-by-Country Reporting applies only to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda Economic Substance Regulations 2018 apply to all exempted undertakings by requiring an annual declaration; if the entity undertakes a relevant activity such as headquarters, financing, leasing, or intellectual property holding, it must demonstrate adequate premises, full-time employees, and core income-generating activity in Bermuda. Gibraltar's economic substance rules similarly require resident companies carrying on relevant activities to maintain local management, staff, and expenditure; a non-resident exempted company avoids ESR filing only if it can prove central management and control outside Gibraltar and the UK.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Sham Transactions

Statutory sanction: UK HMRC may recharacterise the arrangement under principles of sham and Ramsay, assess all fees as profits of the UK trading company, impose tax at up to 25% plus interest, and apply penalties of up to 200% of the tax evaded; Gibraltar may withdraw exempt status and impose equivalent penalties under the Income Tax Act 2010.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced; directors face civil restitution orders for diverted profits, travel bans, director disqualification for up to 15 years, and imprisonment for up to 7 years for cheating the public revenue.
Misrepresentation of Tax Residence

Statutory sanction: HMRC may treat the company as UK-resident under UK case law, assess UK corporation tax on worldwide profits, and impose penalties for incorrect tax returns of up to 100% of tax; the Bermuda and Gibraltar registries may strike off the entity for making false filings.

Director personal liability: Directors who make false residence declarations are personally liable to penalties for negligent or fraudulent behaviour, may be ordered to pay civil restitution, face travel restrictions pending tax investigation, and can receive custodial sentences for perjury or fraudulent evasion.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Violation of Bermuda Economic Substance Regulations 2018 or Gibraltar economic substance code attracts fines of up to BMD/GBP 10,000 per infringement, daily default fines, cancellation of exemption benefits, and compulsory striking off or deregistration; financial information may be exchanged with HMRC under the exchange of information framework.

Director personal liability: Directors and corporate officers face personal financial penalties, disqualification, travel bans, and, in egregious cases, imprisonment for failing to ensure the company maintains real economic substance.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to GBP 500,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act and Bermuda Beneficial Ownership Act; additional penalties for each unregistered beneficial owner and for general AML compliance failures; assets may be frozen and confiscated.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable where the offence is committed with their consent, connivance, or neglect; they may face personal criminal fines, seizure of personal assets, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing and Country-by-Country Reporting Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: HMRC may make transfer pricing adjustments under TIOPA 2010 Part 4, increasing taxable profits and tax charges; penalties for inaccurate returns up to 100% of the additional tax; CbC filing failures attract penalties of GBP 3,000 per schedule plus GBP 500 per day for continued default.

Director personal liability: Officers knowingly concerned in CbC non-compliance are jointly and severally liable for civil penalties; the court may order disgorgement of benefits, director disqualification, travel bans, and imprisonment for deliberate concealment.
