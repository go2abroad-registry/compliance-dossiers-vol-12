Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar: Income Tax Office, HM Customs and Revenue Department, HM Government of Gibraltar (portal: www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation); Bermuda: Accountant General and Registrar of Companies, Ministry of Finance, HM Government of Bermuda (portal: www.gov.bm); United Kingdom (cross-border nexus only): HM Revenue and Customs (portal: www.gov.uk/hmrc).

E-government filing portal

For the Bermuda Exempted Company: incorporate under the Companies Act 1981 with the Bermuda Registrar of Companies, then apply for a Tax Assurance Certificate (TAC) under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, which guarantees exemption from all Bermuda income, capital gains, and withholding taxes through at least 2035. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar has no VAT or GST; the only local indirect tax is the Gibraltar Import Duty 'Prestation' (standard flat rate of 8% on most physical imports, with reduced and zero rates for socially important goods). Digital health and fitness app services supplied from Gibraltar are outside the scope of any Gibraltar indirect tax. Cross-border sales to UK and EU consumers are collected and remitted by Apple and Google as deemed suppliers under the marketplace rules: UK VAT at 20% and EU destination-country VAT at applicable standard rates.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP/GBP: no turnover threshold applies in Gibraltar for digital services because no VAT/GST exists; the Prestation import duty registration is triggered by the first importation of physical goods, with no de minimis threshold. UK: the deemed-supplier rules require Apple/Google to register and account for UK VAT from the first B2C sale to a UK consumer regardless of the app developer's turnover. EU: the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) special scheme applies to cross-border B2C digital services, with an EUR 10,000 annual intra-EU threshold below which the supplier may charge home-country VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar is outside the UK VAT area, so no Gibraltar VAT zero-rating exists and no Gibraltar VAT is chargeable on services. UK zero-rating for exported goods under VAT Act 1994, Schedule 8, Group 7 applies only to supplies made by UK-established suppliers where goods are physically removed from the UK; it does not apply to a Bermuda/Gibraltar app supplier. For B2B cross-border digital supplies, the place of supply is the customer's jurisdiction with the recipient accountable under reverse charge. For B2C supplies through the App Store or Play Store, the marketplace operator is the deemed supplier and must charge and remit UK/EU VAT, while the Bermuda/Gibraltar developer has no substantive VAT registration obligation. Any claim of export/outside-scope treatment must be substantiated with evidence of the customer's establishment, VAT registration numbers where applicable, IP and user-log metadata, and transaction banking records.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document) evidencing physical removal
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export from Gibraltar
  • Commercial Invoice and underlying export or digital supply contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 receipt evidencing foreign exchange repatriation

Gibraltar: no statutory e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate; customs declarations are filed electronically through the Gibraltar Customs system. UK: HMRC Making Tax Digital (MTD) mandates digital record-keeping and API-based VAT submission; no real-time invoice clearance is required. EU: Council Directive (EU) 2023/2842 introduces mandatory e-invoicing for intra-EU B2B supplies from 2028, but this does not apply to Gibraltar.

Deliberate or careless export zero-rating misdeclarations in the UK may trigger retrospective VAT assessments, interest, and penalties of up to 100% of the tax, with criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion. In Gibraltar, false customs or tax declarations attract penalties up to GIP 5,000 or treble the evaded duty, forfeiture and confiscation of goods and proceeds, and criminal liability under the Customs Tariff Ordinance and the Criminal Code. The Bermuda/Gibraltar group may also face clawback of any refund obtained on the basis of improperly substantiated export documentation, together with professional regulatory sanctions on the advising tax agents.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at 1:1 to the pound sterling (GBP), which circulates interchangeably alongside the GIP. There are no exchange controls applicable to capital or current account transactions for non-resident corporate shareholders. Profit remittance, dividend repatriation, and intercompany loan repayments are freely permitted. Hurdles are limited to standard know-your-customer and anti-money-laundering verification. The offshore Bermuda Exempted Company enjoys non-resident status for FX purposes and no restriction on foreign ownership of accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters and digital service providers may retain receipts in any freely convertible currency (USD, EUR, GBP, GIP). There is no obligation to repatriate or liquidate inbound export proceeds into GIP. The only practical compliance gateway is the provision of a valid economic substance declaration and transaction-level audit trail for tax residence verification.

Central bank supervision: Government of Gibraltar via the Financial Services Commission (GFSC) in consultation with UK HM Treasury; no independent central bank. Monetary issuance and currency back to the GBP are administered under the Government of Gibraltar's Currency Notes Act. Bank supervision and foreign exchange dealing regulation rest with the GFSC under the Financial Services Act 2019.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating is Medium but legally constrained. Gibraltar is not part of the EU/EEA; UK FCA e-money institution authorisation does not passport into Gibraltar. A separate e-money licence from the GFSC is required under the Financial Services Act. Offshore digital EMIs established in the UK, Ireland, or Lithuania may offer ancillary multi-currency IBANs but must not provide core settlement rails without a Gibraltar-authorised agent or holding sub-ledger. For low-risk health fitness app payment flows, a GFSC-licensed EMI acting as a distributive ledger technology payment service provider is viable, but settlement should be anchored through a tier-1 Gibraltar clearing bank to minimise counterparty risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires inbound funds to be transmitted via the SWIFT MT103 with OBI (Ordering Bank Information) and a purpose code consistent with 'capital contribution' (e.g., UK purpose code 'FCO' or Gibraltar-specific 'inward equity participation'). The transfer must originate from the shareholder's verified bank account, and the receiving Gibraltar account must be pre-nominated as a capital escrow or operational account. Third-party and crypto-fiat transfers are prohibited. A full documentary package, including the signed injection instrument and the board resolution, must be held in the statutory records. No Gibraltar exchange control authorisation is required, but the bank will require a negative currency control confirmation and anti-money-laundering screening.Outbound trade disbursements are executed through SWIFT MT103 for cross-border wire payments and MT202 for interbank cover payments where a commercial letter of credit (LC) is used. For EU counterparties and euro-denominated settlements, TARGET-2 and SEPA Instant Credit Transfer are available through the correspondent network. GBP settlements utilise CHAPS and Faster Payments, while USD trade settlements clear through either Fedwire or the Clearing House Interbank Payments System (CHIPS). Gibraltar's clearing rails allow the issuance and confirmation of documentary letters of credit under UCP 600, with stand-by credits under ISP98. For recurring app-payout and intellectual-property licensing fees, the recommended lane is SWIFT MT103 with ACH or SEPA Batch for volume efficiency, subject to an approved disbursement matrix.Outright FX Forwards (GIP/USD, GBP/USD, EUR/USD) with GFSC-licensed counterpartiesNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for emerging-market currencies not freely deliverable in GibraltarCross-Currency Swaps (GBP vs USD and EUR vs USD) with a maturity of up to 12 monthsFX Option Contracts (capped collars or zero-premium structures) for downside protection on USD revenue repatriation

If no Gibraltar-source profit exists, enter zero and file a nil return with supporting computation; late filing of a nil return still attracts penalties. Late filing: fixed penalty of GIP 100, increased by GIP 100 for each month of continued default up to a maximum of GIP 1,000; failure to pay assessed tax on time: interest at the statutory rate plus a late payment surcharge; fraudulent or negligent returns: penalty up to 100% of the tax undercharged, with criminal prosecution available (maximum imprisonment 7 years).
Schedule S-TP1 - Transfer pricing disclosure and controlled transaction schedule Discloses cross-border related-party transactions (intercompany app licence fees, management fees, loans, service charges) and the arm's-length method adopted. Non-resident filers with controlled transactions exceeding the statutory GIP threshold must complete the schedule and retain the contemporaneous Local File for inspection by the Commissioner. Late filing: fixed penalty of GIP 100, increased by GIP 100 for each month of continued default up to a maximum of GIP 1,000; failure to pay assessed tax on time: interest at the statutory rate plus a late payment surcharge; fraudulent or negligent returns: penalty up to 100% of the tax undercharged, with criminal prosecution available (maximum imprisonment 7 years).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where annual cross-border controlled transactions exceed GIP 1,000,000; Master File: required where the group's consolidated annual revenue equals or exceeds GIP 850,000,000 (equivalent of EUR 750,000,000 triggering Country-by-Country Reporting); below the threshold, taxpayers must still retain commensurate arm's-length evidence.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Company) Regulations 2019 apply to every company tax resident in Gibraltar that carries on a 'relevant activity', being banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, distribution and service centres, intellectual property holding, and pure holding-company business. A Bermuda exempt company whose central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, or that maintains a Gibraltar branch booking app-licensing or distribution income, must demonstrate for each relevant activity: (a) board meetings held in Gibraltar with qualified quorum and minutes recording strategic decisions; (b) an adequate number of full-time employees in Gibraltar proportionate to the app business; (c) adequate operating expenditure in Gibraltar proportionate to the income; and (d) core income-generating activities (software development, data hosting, user tracking, contract negotiation) physically performed in Gibraltar. Pure equity-holding companies enjoy a reduced substance standard requiring only compliance with statutory filing and adequate human resources for holding activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Assessment of the tax undercharged plus statutory interest; civil penalty up to 100% of the under-declared tax; on indictment, an unlimited fine and imprisonment of up to 7 years; confiscation of the proceeds of crime may be ordered by the courts.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling officers who sign or approve a false return are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the unpaid corporate tax; conviction triggers director disqualification of up to 15 years and a travel ban preventing departure from Gibraltar pending criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Non-compliance with the Economic Substance (Company) Regulations 2019: financial penalty up to GIP 100,000 for a first offence and up to GIP 300,000 for a repeat offence; continuing default may lead to strike-off of the company by the Companies Registry; any tax advantage derived from the artificial arrangement is reversed with interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise the evasion of substance obligations are disqualified from acting as directors for up to 5 years, are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate penalties, and may be held personally liable for the company's tax debts where the entity is struck off.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may adjust the controlled transaction under the arm's-length principle, re-characterise the arrangement, and raise an assessment on the additional profit at 10%; a penalty of up to 20% of the adjusted tax applies for failure to maintain documentation, and late-payment interest accrues from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve intercompany pricing without a benchmarking study may be personally liable for penalties attributable to the adjustment; habitually reckless conduct constitutes professional negligence and may result in disqualification orders and civil restitution orders.
AML / Beneficial Ownership (BORA) Breach

Statutory sanction: Failure to file or maintain accurate returns: fine up to GIP 500 per day for continuing default; knowingly or recklessly providing false beneficial ownership information: fine up to GIP 10,000 and/or up to 2 years' imprisonment under the Beneficial Ownership (Access etc.) Act 2017; parallel enforcement action may be taken under the AML (Amendment) Regulations 2019.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Gibraltar-resident registered agent/nominee are criminally liable; the court may impose travel bans, freeze bank accounts, and issue confiscation orders; a director convicted may be debarred from serving as a director of any Gibraltar company for up to 10 years.
