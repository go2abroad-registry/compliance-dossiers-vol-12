Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office (Gibraltar), with corporate registration at Companies House Gibraltar; the Bermuda Exempted Company is simultaneously regulated by the Bermuda Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 1981 and holds a Certificate of Exemption. No income or profits tax is imposed at the Bermuda level (zero-rate corporate tax jurisdiction), so the operative tax registration nexus for this structured trade is Gibraltar.

E-government filing portal

Stage 1 – Bermuda: file the exempted company incorporation and annual exemption declaration with the Bermuda Registrar of Companies and pay the annual government fee by 30 November; no Bermudian income tax registration or tax return filing is required because Bermuda imposes no corporate income tax on profits. Stage 2 – Gibraltar: register the Bermudian entity as an overseas company at Companies House Gibraltar (notice of place of business / branch), obtain the Gibraltar Companies House number, then file a company registration application with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN); submit certified copies of the Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Exemption, memorandum and articles of association, a Gibraltar registered office address (service-provider office acceptable), and the intended date of commencement of trade. Stage 3 – Economic substance: identify the relevant activity (for EMR SaaS, the qualifying activity is Intellectual Property and/or Distribution and Service Centre) and file the Gibraltar annual economic substance return with the Income Tax Office. Stage 4 – UK VAT (if required): where B2C digital supplies are made to UK consumers, apply to HMRC under the Non-Established Taxable Person (NETP) procedure to obtain a UK VAT number; B2B supplies to UK VAT-registered medical clinics are subject to reverse charge and do not require supplier registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Bermuda or Gibraltar law; entity classification follows domestic corporate law, under which a Bermuda Exempted Company is an opaque limited-liability corporation. Available statutory elections and alignment mechanisms include: (i) election/designation as a Bermuda Exempted Company with an exemption permit; (ii) election under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act to compute capital allowances on qualifying plant, machinery and intellectual property, including the Gibraltar IP regime with a reduced effective rate on qualifying IP income under the modified nexus approach; (iii) voluntary election to treat the Gibraltar activity as a taxable permanent establishment of the Bermudian entity where this facilitates treaty access or substantiates economic substance; (iv) UK anti-hybrid notifications under CTA 2010 Part 6A / TIOPA 2010 to align the UK, Gibraltar and Bermuda tax characterisations and prevent deduction disallowance or double-dip mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT or GST applies in Gibraltar, which is outside the EU VAT area and applies only import duties and excise taxes to physical goods; UK VAT (Value Added Tax) at 20% is the relevant indirect tax for digital services supplied to UK consumers, while B2B supplies of EMR SaaS to UK VAT-registered medical clinics are outside the scope of UK VAT and subject to the customer's reverse charge.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: no VAT registration threshold is applicable because no VAT system exists; import duties arise regardless of turnover. UK: GBP 85,000 taxable turnover threshold applies to UK-established businesses, but a non-established supplier of digital/electronic services to UK consumers has no threshold and must register with HMRC from the first sale; B2B digital services to a UK VAT-registered customer are reverse-charged without supplier VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT, so no Gibraltar export zero-rating exists and no refund mechanism is engaged for exported services. For UK VAT purposes, zero-rating of physical goods exported from the UK is available only where the supplier holds commercial export evidence (customs declaration and bill of lading/air waybill) obtained within three months and retained for six years. The medical-clinic EMR SaaS subscription is a cross-border digital service: B2B supplies to UK VAT-registered clinics are outside the scope of UK VAT (reverse charge), and B2C digital supplies are taxed at the place of consumption, so no export zero-rating claim is made for the software itself; any hardware bundled with the service could be separately zero-rated subject to the same evidence rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (UK SAD C88 / CDS Goods Departure Message; for the pure SaaS service no customs declaration is required, but it is mandatory for any physical hardware exports supporting the EMR installation)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or CMR consignment note for land freight) as proof of physical departure from the UK/Gibraltar export territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/statement confirmation evidencing receipt of the subscription consideration from the UK medical clinic customer
  • Commercial invoice and executed SaaS/subscription agreement with the UK clinic setting out the supply nature and the zero-rated or reverse-charged VAT treatment
  • Digital service delivery evidence (login credentials, cloud service logs, data-centre delivery receipt or go-live certificate) demonstrating that the cross-border service was actually rendered

Gibraltar imposes no fiscal e-invoicing clearance, no government digital stamping, no API real-time clearance, and no electronic invoicing mandate; conventional commercial e-invoicing is accepted. If the Bermudian/Gibraltar operator registers for UK VAT, HMRC requires Making Tax Digital (MTD) compliance, namely digital record-keeping and VAT return submission via MTD-compatible software over HMRC APIs; no real-time clearance or stamping applies. For UK public-sector e-invoicing, the PEPPOL network is required, but it is not relevant to private medical-clinic SaaS sales.

Gibraltar: the Income Tax Office may raise assessments, amend returns and impose interest plus penalties of up to 100% of the tax under-charged for negligent or fraudulent conduct; assessment time limits are generally six years, extended to 21 years for fraud or wilful default. UK: HMRC audits zero-rating via post-clearance checks under VAT Notice 703 and may retrospectively claw back wrongly claimed zero-rating with interest; civil penalties under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 reach 100% of the potential lost VAT for deliberate and concealed inaccuracy, and criminal prosecution for VAT evasion (including fraudulent export schemes) carries imprisonment of up to seven years with unlimited fines; HMRC assessments may be made within four years for careless errors and up to 20 years for deliberate concealment. For the Bermuda Exempted Company, abusive use of the exemption status could result in cancellation of the Certificate of Exemption and fines under Bermuda's Companies Act 1981, while artificial Gibraltar/Bermuda routing of UK-derived SaaS profits may additionally be counteracted by the UK diverted profits tax (25%) or anti-hybrid mismatch rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP is pegged at par to GBP and is fully convertible; neither Gibraltar nor Bermuda maintains exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. Medical-clinic EMR SaaS subscription receipts in GBP, EUR or USD may be freely received, retained and converted offshore. Capital account transactions (inbound share capital, intercompany loans, profit distributions to the Bermuda parent) are unrestricted and require no prior central bank approval. Profit remittance from Gibraltar to Bermuda is subject only to standard transfer-pricing documentation for the Gibraltar branch or permanent establishment and to anti-money-laundering record-keeping; Bermuda levies no withholding or remittance tax.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. No statutory timeline for repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio apply in Gibraltar or Bermuda. SaaS export receipts may be held indefinitely in any freely convertible currency. The only affirmative obligations are GFSC anti-money-laundering record retention (five years) and accurate reflection of all receivables in the audited financial statements filed with the Gibraltar Companies Registry.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the integrated financial regulator for banks and electronic money institutions; the Bank of England as the ultimate monetary authority for the Gibraltar pound (GIP), which is issued by the Government of Gibraltar through the Commissioner of Currency. For any Bermuda-resident banking or fiduciary arrangements, the Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) applies separately; however, the operational treasury nexus of this structure is Gibraltar, so GFSC rules govern the account relationships and FX activity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility. UK-regulated EMIs (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex) will generally onboard a Bermuda Exempted Company operating from Gibraltar, but only after enhanced due diligence requiring full UBO disclosure, evidence of economic substance in Gibraltar and a primary transactional account with a GFSC-regulated bank. Gibraltar-licensed EMIs are permitted under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act, yet no Gibraltar EMI currently accepts an offshore tax-exempt vehicle without a domestic banking anchor. EMIs should therefore be used exclusively as low-value collection gateways for SaaS receipts and never as primary treasury reservoirs.
