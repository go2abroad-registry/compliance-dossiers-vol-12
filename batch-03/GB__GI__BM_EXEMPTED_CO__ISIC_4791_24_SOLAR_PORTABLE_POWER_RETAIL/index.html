Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office of Gibraltar (HM Government of Gibraltar), official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation; HM Customs Gibraltar for indirect tax and customs clearances.

E-government filing portal

The Bermuda Exempted Company must first register as an external company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under Part XXII of the Companies Act 2014. After obtaining the external registration number, it must file the prescribed non-resident tax registration form with the Income Tax Office, attaching the Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, certificate of good standing, memorandum and articles, evidence of registered office in Bermuda, and particulars of any Gibraltar registered office and tax representative. The Income Tax Office issues a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (GIN). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda Exempted Company): The entity is a separate legal corporation, not a pass-through entity. Bermuda does not tax exempted companies. For Gibraltar tax purposes, absent central management and control in Gibraltar and absent a Gibraltar permanent establishment, the entity is non-resident and remains outside Gibraltar's corporation tax net. All offshore e-commerce sales income is therefore untaxed. If a taxable permanent establishment were created in Gibraltar, Gibraltar Income Tax at the flat corporate rate of 12.5% would apply to Gibraltar-source profits.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not maintain a check-the-box entity classification election. A foreign corporation cannot elect to be treated as a disregarded or transparent entity. Gibraltar's hybrid mismatch rules, implementing OECD BEPS Action 2 via the Income Tax (Hybrid and Other Mismatches) Regulations 2019, allow elective adjustments in respect of hybrid financial instruments and reverse-hybrid entities, including a dual-inclusion income offset election. Such elections affect the quantum or timing of relief but do not alter the entity's fundamental tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

3%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar Purchase Tax: no turnover registration threshold; Purchase Tax and customs duties are triggered at importation, and any importer, including a non-resident e-commerce seller using a Gibraltar warehouse, must obtain a Customs Identification Number. UK VAT: an overseas seller must register for UK VAT when UK taxable distance sales exceed GBP 90,000 in a rolling 12-month period (post-1 April 2024 threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To claim 0% VAT on exports from the UK, the exporter must be a UK VAT-registered taxable person; the goods must be exported to a non-UK destination, with export evidence obtained within 3 months and retained for 6 years; the sale must be to an overseas customer and the goods must not return to the UK. Directly connected delivery services may also be zero-rated. For Gibraltar Purchase Tax, goods exported from Gibraltar are outside the Purchase Tax charge, and Purchase Tax paid on importation may be refunded if the exporter proves physical exportation within the prescribed customs window and that the goods were not consumed or sold locally.

Gibraltar: no real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no electronic stamping, and no statutory e-invoicing requirement. Importers submit declarations via the Gibraltar Electronic Customs System using SAD or ENS filings. If the entity registers for UK VAT, it must file VAT returns through HMRC Making Tax Digital-compatible software; HMRC does not mandate real-time e-invoicing.

Fraudulent export zero-rating claims are treated as deliberate inaccuracy, triggering penalty assessments up to 100% of the tax evaded, interest, and criminal prosecution. Under UK VAT, VATA 1994 Section 72 provides imprisonment up to 7 years and unlimited fines; HMRC may assess retrospectively for up to 4 years, extended to 20 years for fraud or concealment. In Gibraltar, HM Customs may claw back Purchase Tax refunded, impose civil penalties and penal assessments, seize goods, and prosecute false declaration offences under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar is a free-convertibility offshore special territory. Current-account receipts and capital-account transfers are freely convertible between GIP/GBP, USD and EUR without exchange-control approval. Profit remittances by a non-resident Bermuda exempted company through a Gibraltar treasury account are unrestricted in law, subject only to financial sanctions, AML/CFT documentation, correspondent-bank policy and the final-UBO assessment by the receiving bank. There is no dual exchange-rate mechanism and no requirement to convert foreign-currency receipts into local currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender period and no mandatory conversion ratio apply under United Kingdom or Gibraltar exchange-control rules. Export and online sales proceeds may be retained indefinitely in the original currency and may be applied directly to supplier settlements or converted at market rates. No 30, 60 or 90-day surrender timeline is imposed; statistical reporting may be required but does not restrict treasury management.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England as monetary anchor for GBP and GIP; HM Government of Gibraltar Ministry of Finance as currency-issuing authority for the Gibraltar pound; Gibraltar Financial Services Commission as prudential and AML/CFT regulator for licensed banks and payment institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Moderate-high as a secondary treasury rail for FX clearing, supplier disbursements and marketplace payouts, but low as the sole concentration bank. Legal constraints: (i) any EMI must be authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority in the UK or the GFSC in Gibraltar under the applicable electronic-money and payment-services framework; (ii) unlicensed offshore EMIs may not lawfully provide payment or e-money services to a Gibraltar-connect treasury function; (iii) funds held with an EMI are not covered by a statutory deposit-protection scheme; (iv) the EMI's distance-onboarding process will still require certified UBO data, payment-volume history and AML source-of-funds evidence; (v) capital escrow and tax reserves should remain with a fully licensed bank rather than an EMI.

The receiving bank must verify that the beneficiary IBAN and legal entity name exactly match the operational account opening documents.
