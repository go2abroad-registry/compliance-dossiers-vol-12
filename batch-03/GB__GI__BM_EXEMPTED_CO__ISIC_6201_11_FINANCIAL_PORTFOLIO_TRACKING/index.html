Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Companies, Companies House Gibraltar, HM Government of Gibraltar (e-Registry online portal)

Governing statute

Companies Act 2014 (Gibraltar), ss. 1, 5, 8-14 (incorporation, filing, certificate of incorporation), s. 33 (private company limited by shares); Beneficial Ownership (Access and Safeguards) Act 2017, s. 3; Income Tax Act 2010, ss. 17, 40 (corporate charge to tax and exemption for non-resident managed companies). Constitutive features mirror the Bermuda Exempted Company model: no minimum capital, non-resident ownership permitted, and tax-exempt classification.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form GI-INC-01 'Application for Registration' together with the Memorandum and Articles of Association, the Statement of Capital, and the Declaration of Compliance under s. 9(2) of the Companies Act 2014

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital (minimum_capital_requirement: false); the company must allot at least one ordinary share of no par value. No escrow account requirement exists under Gibraltar law.

Funding & unlock path: No factory, therefore no fire-safety registration or structural load inspection is required. The registered office must hold a valid landlord's certificate confirming authorized premises use. Hosting data centers must maintain ISO 27001:2022 and SOC 2 Type II certifications; remote workforce operates under a clean-desk policy and cybersecurity clearance protocols.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (ITO), headed by the Commissioner of Income Tax, under the Ministry of Finance. Operational portal: https://www.tax.gi; electronic filing: https://www.tax.gi/self-service. Physical import duties and excise are administered by HM Customs Gibraltar, https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/customs. Although the sovereign ISO envelope is GB, Gibraltar is a separate British Overseas Territory with an autonomous tax administration; HMRC has no jurisdiction over Gibraltar-resident companies.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Continue the Bermuda Exempted Company into Gibraltar under Section 130 of the Companies Act 2014, obtaining a Gibraltar companies registry number and a certificate of continuance. Step 2: Lodge the Application for Tax Registration – Company (Form COMP-TR) with the Income Tax Office within 21 days of continuance or commencement of business, attaching the certificate of continuance, constitutional documents, registered office consent, officer declarations and, where applicable, the Bermuda tax exemption certificate showing the entity's exempted status under the Bermuda Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act. Step 3: The ITO issues a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) recorded in the CRS/FATCA tax file; the entity is tagged as a Tax-Exempt Zone non-resident filer with no chargeable assessment until insular-source activity is commenced.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — Step 1: Continue the Bermuda Exempted Company into Gibraltar under Section 130 of the Companies Act 2014, obtaining a Gibraltar companies registry number and a certificate of continuance. Step 2: Lodge the Application for Tax Registration – Company (Form COMP-TR) with the Income Tax Office within 21 days of continuance or commencement of business, attaching the certificate of continuance, constitutional documents, registered office consent, officer declarations and, where applicable, the Bermuda tax exemption certificate showing the entity's exempted status under the Bermuda Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box election. Available mechanisms include: (i) a central management and control residence determination under Section 5 of the Income Tax Act 2010, allowing a foreign-incorporated entity to elect a non-resident tax file where board control is exercised outside Gibraltar; (ii) continuance or redomiciliation under Companies Act 2014 Section 130, which fixes the entity's tax domicile for Gibraltar purposes; (iii) group relief elections for qualifying 75% affiliates under Section 46 of the Income Tax Act 2010; and (iv) entity-classification alignment statements under the Income Tax (Hybrid Mismatch) Regulations 2019, which neutralise deduction and monetary mismatches arising where Bermuda classifies the Exempted Company as non-resident and non-transparent while Gibraltar would otherwise classify the same entity as transparent or opaque.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST or sales-and-use tax regime applies in Gibraltar. Gibraltar is outside the UK VAT and EU VAT territories; indirect taxation is limited to customs import duties aligned with the Common Customs Tariff on physical goods and excise duties on alcohol, tobacco and hydrocarbon oils, administered by HM Customs Gibraltar.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT or GST because Gibraltar operates no VAT/GST registration threshold. An importers' and receivers' registration obligation arises at the point dutiable goods are entered into Gibraltar; there is no minimum monetary exemption for excisable goods under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar imposes no VAT, so no statutory 0% export VAT rate exists to claim for fintech software exports or offshore services. Export treatment operates at the direct-tax level: subscription and portfolio-tracking revenue arising from non-Gibraltar counterparties is excluded from chargeable profits under the territoriality principle, provided the invoicing entity maintains a Territorial Source Statement and bank evidence that the consideration arises outside Gibraltar and is not attributed to a permanent establishment in Gibraltar.

  • N/A for pure cloud software exports under ISIC 6201; no physical customs declaration or Bill of Lading is mandatory. The ITO may require a service invoice ledger, executed client agreements, and a board-approved source examination statement.
  • Where physical hardware is embedded or delivered, the mandatory bundle is: Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD entry lodged through the HM Customs e-portal)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, bank SWIFT MT103 advice, or foreign bank statement evidencing receipt of consideration from outside Gibraltar
  • Technical specification and software licence schedule describing the cloud-based portfolio-tracking service

No electronic invoicing clearance, real-time fiscal stamping or API-based continuous transaction control mandate applies to domestic supplies or digital services. Customs import clearance is effected by electronic Single Administrative Document (SAD) filings through the HM Customs e-portal. Stripe and Apple IAP sales invoices are not subject to Gibraltar fiscal e-invoicing certification.

The Income Tax Office applies a 180-day accrual standard: export consideration not repatriated within 180 days of the invoice date may be re-characterised as imputed Gibraltar-source financing income and assessed at the standard corporate rate (10% to 12.5%). Fraudulent export attribution or fabricated foreign contracts attract retrospective clawback of all exempted profit, an accuracy-related penalty up to 200% of the undercharged tax, an extended ten-year assessment window for fraudulent conduct, and criminal exposure under Section 105 of the Income Tax Act 2010. For physical goods, false export declarations are penalised at three times the duty evaded and the goods are liable to forfeiture under the Customs Duties Ordinance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar pound is pegged 1:1 to GBP and freely convertible; the territory maintains no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. There are no profit remittance restrictions or licences for dividend payments to non-resident shareholders; remittance may be executed via commercial banks without central bank approval, subject solely to AML/KYC and tax transparency filings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar and UK apply a fully liberalised exchange-control-free regime; exporters are not obliged to surrender foreign currency receipts to the central bank or convert them within a fixed timeline. The company may retain 100% of export proceeds in designated foreign currency accounts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (UK monetary authority) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as Gibraltar's banking supervisor; no Gibraltar central bank exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for licence-backed EMIs; Low for unlicensed offshore digital EMIs. A Bermuda Exempted Company may use an EMI that holds a Gibraltar financial services licence (e.g., as an authorised electronic money institution) under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2019. UK or EU EMIs do not passport into Gibraltar post-Brexit; segregation of client funds and safeguarding requirements must be proven.
