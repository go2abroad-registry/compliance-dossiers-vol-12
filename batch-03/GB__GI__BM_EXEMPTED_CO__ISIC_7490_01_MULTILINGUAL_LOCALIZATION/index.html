Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Gibraltar Tax & Customs Department, HM Government of Gibraltar (official portal: gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation; corporate filings are made through the Gibraltar Companies Registry and the Income Tax Office).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Apply to the Registrar of Companies (Companies House Gibraltar) for either (a) continuation of the Bermuda Exempted Company into Gibraltar under Part XXII of the Companies Act 2014, or (b) registration as an overseas company (branch) under s.363 of that Act. Step 2: Obtain the Gibraltar Company Number from the Companies Registry. Step 3: Within 30 days of the registration certificate, file the Initial Tax Notification with the Commissioner of Income Tax to procure the Gibraltar Taxpayer Reference Number (GTRN), which is the statutory tax identification number. Step 4: If employees are engaged in Gibraltar, register for PAYE income tax collection and Social Insurance with the Department of Social Security. Step 5: For physical media shipments, open a customs account with HM Customs Gibraltar; no VAT registration ID exists or is issuable because Gibraltar has no VAT. Step 6: File annual self-assessed Gross Income Tax (GIT) returns at the 10% standard corporate rate and Companies Act annual returns within the statutory filing window (typically 12 months after the accounting period end).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope. A Bermuda Exempted Company continued into Gibraltar becomes an ordinary resident Gibraltar company taxable on worldwide profits at 10% GIT; if instead the Bermuda company merely registers a Gibraltar branch (external company), only Gibraltar-source profits are within the charge. The Bermuda 'Tax_Exempt_Zone' character ceases on continuation; the former Gibraltar Qualifying Company 0% tax regime was abolished effective 2011.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not provide a US-style check-the-box pass-through election. Statutory elections under the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended) include: (i) group relief/loss surrender election between the Bermuda parent and the Gibraltar entity at ≥75% common ownership; (ii) capital allowance pooling and short-life asset elections; (iii) the option to apply transfer pricing documentation prepared under OECD Guidance to minimize adjustment disputes. Hybrid mismatch election: not locally available; the UK's Hybrid and Miscellaneous Mismatch Rules (TIOPA 2010 Part 6A) police mismatches arising among UK, Gibraltar and Bermuda entities because Bermuda levies no corporate income tax and Gibraltar is a low-tax jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST – Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area (Protocol 3 to the 1972 Act of Accession) and imposes no general sales or use tax. The residual indirect taxes are import customs duties, excise duties (alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oils), stamp duties, and insurance premium duty.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A – no VAT registration threshold exists. Customs registration and a first goods declaration are required at the time of the first physical import/export; there is no turnover-based economic threshold. By reference only, the UK VAT registration threshold of GBP 85,000 does not apply to Gibraltar.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Strictly N/A because Gibraltar has no VAT: exported translation, dubbing and localization services are outside the scope of any Gibraltar indirect tax and therefore attract an effective 0% rate. To preserve the de facto zero rate and the Bermuda parent's exempt status, the Gibraltar entity must (i) contract with non-Gibraltar principals, (ii) retain transfer pricing documentation for intercompany service fees, and (iii) evidence the foreign destination of deliverables. For physical media (e.g., encoded masters), Gibraltar Customs grants export clearance with no export duty where the goods are declared for export and physically leave Gibraltar.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD export entry) lodged electronically with HM Customs Gibraltar
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from Gibraltar (for physical media consignments)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank credit advice) evidencing receipt of export proceeds in a Gibraltar or Bermuda bank account
  • Commercial invoice describing the localization services and confirming that title and IP transfer outside Gibraltar

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime operates in Gibraltar. Invoices may be issued in paper or electronic PDF and must identify the supplier GTRN and describe the multilingual localization services. Customs declarations (Single Administrative Document) are lodged electronically through licensed customs software or agents.

HM Customs Gibraltar and the Commissioner of Income Tax may open audits and reopen assessments for six years (unlimited in cases of fraud). Unsupported export documentation triggers retrospective clawback of the de facto zero rate, restatement of the export revenue as taxable GIT at 10%, plus interest at 8% per annum. Penalties: late filing GBP 100 per return escalating; incorrect returns up to 200% of the tax underpaid under the Income Tax Act 2010; fraudulent evasion is a criminal offence carrying a fine of up to GBP 1,000,000 and/or 7 years' imprisonment. Customs offences additionally expose goods to forfeiture and penalties up to treble the duty avoided. A UK ultimate parent, if any, is also exposed to controlled foreign company attribution under TIOPA 2010 Part 9A if the Gibraltar profit lacks economic substance.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility. No exchange-control approval is required in Gibraltar or the UK for foreign-currency receipts, capital injections, profit distributions, or repatriation. GIP circulates at par with GBP; the UK monetary framework prevents any separate GIP exchange-rate regime. Bermuda's Exchange Control Act 1972 treats an exempted company designated 'non-resident' as fully external, allowing unrestricted foreign-currency accounts, share capital subscription, dividend remittance, and loan servicing. Statutory hurdles: (i) Bermuda economic-substance declaration must evidence tax residence in Gibraltar (or notification of residence in another jurisdiction); (ii) FATCA/CRS self-certification (Form W-8BEN-E / CRS SI) with a Gibraltar tax-residence TIN to avoid 30% US backup withholding on US-sourced localization revenues; (iii) no Gibraltar or Bermuda withholding taxes apply to profit remittance to shareholders.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None imposed. Gibraltar, the UK, and Bermuda (for non-resident designated exempted companies) impose no mandatory surrender of hard-currency export proceeds, no minimum conversion ratio into local currency, and no statutory repatriation timeline for intangible-service export receipts. Translation and dubbing revenues may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, or GBP in the Gibraltar account; Bermuda's six-month export collection rule under the Exchange Control Act 1972 applies only to goods and to resident accounts and is therefore inapplicable to ISIC 7490 services.

Central bank supervision: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) - exchange-control authority over the Bermuda Exempted Company; Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) - host-state regulator of licensed banks and EMIs; HM Treasury and the Bank of England - sovereign monetary framework; Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Currency) - issuer of the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at a 1:1 fixed peg with GBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: A- (High, conditionally). Wise B2B and Payoneer are acceptable secondary rails for multi-currency collection and mass contractor disbursement, but neither may function as the sole settlement bank. Hard preconditions: (i) the Bermuda Exempted Company must first register with the Gibraltar Registry as an overseas company under the Companies Act 2014 and obtain a Gibraltar registered address to qualify for Wise/Payoneer KYC; (ii) the full UBO chain must be disclosed and FATCA/CRS forms (W-8BEN-E) completed; (iii) USD receiving accounts at Payoneer/Wise require a US EIN (SS-4 application) to avoid Form 1099-K misreporting and withholding; (iv) the primary SWIFT account at a tier-1 Gibraltar bank must remain the master settlement account. Legal constraints: Wise and Payoneer are FCA/Irish-regulated electronic money institutions, not deposit-taking banks, so balances above the GBP 85,000 pass-through guarantee threshold must be swept to the primary bank; neither EMI offers a dedicated IBAN to the Bermuda legal entity without prior Gibraltar registration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Primary route: UBO's home bank → SWIFT MT103 → tier-1 Gibraltar bank account titled in the Bermuda Exempted Company (registered as a Gibraltar overseas company). Wire compliance: Field 50K (ordering customer) must match remitter KYC identity; Field 59 (beneficiary) must carry the company's full legal name and Gibraltar registered address; Field 70 should read 'SHARE CAPITAL INJECTION PER BOARD RESOLUTION [ref]' or 'SUBORDINATED LOAN PER LOAN AGREEMENT [ref]'. No domestic purpose-code registry exists in Gibraltar or the UK; the Field 70 narrative serves as the de facto purpose code, supplemented by the EU/UK Travel Rule (Regulation 2015/847) requiring remitter and beneficiary identity detail. No prior Gibraltar or UK host-state approval is required; remitter-side outward-remittance clearances may apply (e.g., RBI Form A2 for India, PBOC ODI procedural filings for China, BNM capital-transaction documentation for Malaysia). If booked as share capital, update the Bermuda share register annually; if booked as an intercompany loan, retain a signed, arm's-length loan agreement to satisfy CRS and transfer-pricing documentation. EMI-sourced inflows from Wise/Payoneer are acceptable after presenting the platform settlement statement as source of funds; the beneficiary bank will also match the remitter name against the UBO KYC file.Clearing rail matrix: (1) Supplier and payroll settlements via SWIFT MT103 from GIB or Banco Sabadell, plus SEPA Instant or UK Faster Payments for local-currency disbursement; (2) mass international freelancer payouts via Wise B2B batch API or Payoneer mass-payout feed, each transaction carrying the commercial invoice reference and screened against OFAC/EU/UN sanctions lists; (3) large-retainer secured-settlement arrangements may use commercial letters of credit (MT700) with a confirming or negotiating bank in Gibraltar, followed by MT103 settlement on presentation; documentary LC is not statutorily required for ISIC 7490 services but may be requested by enterprise clients; (4) intercompany funding to Bermuda or group entities via MT103/MT202 with travel-rule-compliant remitter and beneficiary details.Vanilla deliverable FX forwards (up to 12 months; EUR/USD, USD/GBP, GBP/EUR) booked through Banco Sabadell Treasury or Butterfield Markets to lock conversion rates on large contracted receivables.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted or thinly traded currencies (USD/CNY, USD/INR, USD/BRL, USD/NGN) to hedge localization invoices quoted in exotic currencies without requiring onshore local-currency settlement.Cross-currency basis swaps (EUR/USD, USD/GBP; 1-3 years) for structural working-capital exposure when revenue currency differs materially from the payment currency; requires ISDA/CSA documentation, a board derivatives mandate under Bermuda company law, and EMIR transaction reporting where the company exceeds the financial-counterparty threshold.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility: A- (High, conditionally). Wise B2B and Payoneer are acceptable secondary rails for multi-currency collection and mass contractor disbursement, but neither may function as the sole settlement bank. Hard preconditions: (i) the Bermuda Exempted Company must first register with the Gibraltar Registry as an overseas company under the Companies Act 2014 and obtain a Gibraltar registered address to qualify for Wise/Payoneer KYC; (ii) the full UBO chain must be disclosed and FATCA/CRS forms (W-8BEN-E) completed; (iii) USD receiving accounts at Payoneer/Wise require a US EIN (SS-4 application) to avoid Form 1099-K misreporting and withholding; (iv) the primary SWIFT account at a tier-1 Gibraltar bank must remain the master settlement account. Legal constraints: Wise and Payoneer are FCA/Irish-regulated electronic money institutions, not deposit-taking banks, so balances above the GBP 85,000 pass-through guarantee threshold must be swept to the primary bank; neither EMI offers a dedicated IBAN to the Bermuda legal entity without prior Gibraltar registration.
