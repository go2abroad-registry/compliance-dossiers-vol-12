Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (ITO), Ministry of Finance; official eFiling portal: https://www.ito.gi

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (including a Bermuda Exempted Company) with a taxable presence in Gibraltar must apply for a Gibraltar Tax Number (GTN) via Form 11 on the ITO eFiling portal. Required submissions include: certified copy of the certificate of incorporation, notarised board resolution appointing a Gibraltar tax representative, evidence of a registered or physical office address, and identification of ultimate beneficial owners. Where the entity is a non-resident exempt company, it may instead apply for a Certificate of Non-Residence under Section 7 of the Income Tax Act; this obviates a GTN, provided no economic activity is managed in Gibraltar.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign entity (including a Bermuda Exempted Company) with a taxable presence in Gibraltar must apply for a Gibraltar Tax Number (GTN) via Form 11 on the ITO eFiling portal. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box election. Entity classification follows the Income Tax Act 2010. The company may elect for territorial taxation on profits as a 'qualifying company' (12.5% rate) or may elect for non-resident tax-exempt status if wholly non-resident controlled. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are overridden by the Taxation (Hybrid Misdistribution) Regulations 2019, which follow OECD BEPS Action 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST system in Gibraltar; indirect taxation is limited to import duties, excise duties on alcohol, tobacco and hydrocarbon oils, and a consumption-based tax on certain imported services via the Official Controls Regulations.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT (no VAT regime). For customs purposes, any commercial importation requires registration with H.M. Customs Gibraltar as a registered importer/exporter; no monetary threshold applies. Relevant currency: GBP.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – because Gibraltar does not levy VAT or GST, exports are not zero-rated. Goods exported from Gibraltar are outside the scope of any indirect tax. Relief from import duty on goods temporarily imported or held for re-export is available under the Inward Processing Relief (IPR) regime, subject to H.M. Customs authorisation and a re-export period not exceeding 12 months.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CUS-EXP-01 filed via ACCS)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from a licensed Gibraltar bank (SWIFT MT103) evidencing payment receipt within 180 days
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (only for preferential tariff claims)

No real-time fiscal clearance or e-invoicing mandate exists. Import and export declarations are submitted electronically via the Gibraltar Automated Customs Clearance System (ACCS). No digital stamping or cryptographic invoicing requirement is imposed.

Misuse of the non-resident exempt status or submission of false export declarations triggers retrospective assessment for up to six years under the Income Tax Act 2010, with interest at 6% per annum and civil penalties up to 200% of the avoided tax. Customs infractions attract penalties of up to £5,000 per declaration plus repayment of any waived duty, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is fully convertible and is legally maintained at strict parity with pound sterling (GBP). Gibraltar applies no exchange-control restrictions on either current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, intra-group loan repayments, liquidation proceeds and passive electronic-inventory sale proceeds may be freely repatriated to the Bermuda ultimate beneficial owners without central-bank consent. The only material hurdles are GFSC AML/CFT record-keeping obligations, UK/UN/EU/OFC sanctions screening, and standard corporate verification by the paying bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender regime. There is no mandatory conversion timeline and no prescribed conversion ratio for export receipts arising from the distribution of multilayer ceramic capacitors and resistors. The Bermuda Exempted Company may retain, convert or remit all hard-currency receivables (USD, EUR, GBP, GIP) in full, provided the underlying contracts, customs declarations and SWIFT receipts are retained for five years for GFSC anti-money-laundering review.

Central bank supervision: HM Government of Gibraltar, Office of the Commissioner of Currency, acting in coordination with the Bank of England on sterling parity; prudential banking and payment-services licensing is administered by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium with material legal constraints. UK FCA-authorised electronic-money institutions such as Revolut, Wise and Currencycloud no longer enjoy passporting rights into Gibraltar following the UK's withdrawal from the EU. They may provide GBP and EUR virtual IBANs and payment-processing rails only through agency or correspondent arrangements with a GFSC-licensed bank. An offshore EMI cannot act as the primary custody bank, as the GFSC requires deposit-taking and settlement activity to be undertaken by a locally licensed credit institution. EMIs may therefore be used solely as payment-composition or last-mile disbursement tools, never for capital escrow or long-term treasury concentration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or MT202 (bank-to-bank cover) from the Bermuda parent or beneficial-owner account to the beneficiary GIP or GBP account held with Gibraltar International Bank Limited or Jyske Bank (Gibraltar) Limited. The purpose-code field must identify a permissible capital-account transaction, using ISO 20022 purpose codes such as 'DGCE' (direct capital expenditure) or 'EQUI' (equity credit), complemented by the free-text remark 'Equity injection to Gibraltar-exempt Bermuda Exempted Company; no exchange-control consent required'. The mandatory verification pack comprises: signed board resolution, share subscription agreement, source-of-funds bank statements, and a legal opinion from Bermudian counsel confirming the exempted-company status and lawful capitalisation. There is no surrender, approval, or registration ratio applicable.Trade disbursements settle predominantly through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for open-account purchases and standby documentary Letters of Credit (MT700/MT799) for EU supplier settlements in EUR. Where fit-for-purpose, documentary collections (MT400) may be used for reputationally difficult resale corridors. Gibraltar's clearing house nets GIP at parity with GBP, and the tier-1 banks participate in the Bank of England's CHAPS system for same-day GBP liquidity, permitting seamless conversion of GIP-denominated treasury balances into EUR or USD settlement legs.
