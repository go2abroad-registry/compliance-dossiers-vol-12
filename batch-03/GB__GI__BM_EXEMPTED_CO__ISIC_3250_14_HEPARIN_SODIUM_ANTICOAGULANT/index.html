Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office (ITO), HM Government of Gibraltar — St Jago's Arcade, 315 Main Street, Gibraltar GX11 1AA; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/departments/income-tax-office. Counterpart for goods: HM Customs Gibraltar (Customs and Excise Division) with its electronic clearance portal for import/export declarations.

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company with activity in or from Gibraltar must, unless expressly non-resident, register with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office as a non-resident company. Step 1: Obtain a Gibraltar registered office and file the foreign-company particulars with the Gibraltar Companies Registry. Step 2: Submit Form G/REG-1 (Company Registration Application) with the ITO, attaching certified copies of the Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, constitutional documents, directors' register, and a declaration of effective management and control. Step 3: Appoint a Gibraltar-resident tax representative and, for physical goods operations, register with HM Customs Gibraltar to obtain a customs operator code. Step 4: Upon approval, the ITO issues the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN), which is required for opening bank accounts, lodging customs declarations, and employer PAYE/social insurance registration where staff are engaged.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box or US-style hybrid entity election. Available elections under the Income Tax Act 2010 include: (a) 'Qualifying Company' status for government-authorised/licensed activities, producing a 2% effective tax rate on qualifying trading profits; (b) grandfathered 'Tax-Exempt Company' treatment for entities incorporated before 31 December 2010; (c) an election to treat a foreign-incorporated entity as non-resident where central management and control are established outside Gibraltar, leaving foreign-source profits outside the charge; and (d) elections under the UK-Gibraltar Taxation Agreement to allocate taxing rights. For a Bermuda Exempted Company, the default position is exemption from Gibraltar tax on all income and profits, with an optional election to assume Gibraltar tax residence where substance or banking requirements make that preferable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar levies no VAT and no general sales/GST system; the applicable indirect levies are Import Duties, Excise Duties, and Stamp Duties. The UK VAT Act does not extend to Gibraltar.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — there is no VAT/GST registration threshold in Gibraltar because no such tax exists. Registration with HM Customs Gibraltar (customs operator code) is mandatory from the first commercial import or export of goods, without a de minimis turnover threshold; excise-controlled premises require separate licensing before first use.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because Gibraltar imposes no VAT or sales tax, exports of heparin sodium anticoagulant API are not 'zero-rated' but are classified as 'outside the scope' of Gibraltar indirect tax, yielding a 0% charge. Eligibility is established by: (i) physical export of the goods from Gibraltar under a valid customs export declaration; (ii) sale to a buyer resident outside Gibraltar; (iii) passing of legal title and risk outside Gibraltar; and (iv) compliance with end-use/outward-processing arrangements in relation to imported crude heparin inputs so that customs duty relief on export is preserved. There is no input VAT refund mechanism as no input VAT is payable; duty relief, not VAT refund, is the sole recovery pathway for import duties paid at entry.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD / customs entry) with unique consignment reference
  • Commercial invoice stating HS code 3001.20 (heparin sodium API), quantity, unit price, and Incoterms
  • Packing list and weight/volume certificate
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical despatch from Gibraltar
  • Certificate of Origin (for preferential duty claims in the destination country)
  • Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product (CPP) issued by the Gibraltar Medicines Inspectorate / UK MHRA
  • Export licence and controlled-drug or precursor declaration for heparin derivatives, where applicable
  • Inward/outward processing endorsement or end-use authorisation for crude heparin inputs
  • FX remittance certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation evidencing settlement of the export receivable

There is no real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, B2B clearance API, or digital stamping mandate for ordinary commercial exports. Electronic customs declarations are compulsory and must be lodged through the Gibraltar Customs electronic clearance platform (single administrative declaration). Pharmaceutical consignments additionally require electronic submission of export licensing and precursor/controlled-drug declarations to the Gibraltar health authorities before customs release. No continuous transaction control system, e-invoice VAT reporting, or SAF-T/CTOS-equivalent mandate applies.

HM Customs Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office conduct joint audits of export claims and FX repatriation. False or fraudulent export declarations attract penalties under the Customs Tariff Ordinance and the Income Tax Act 2010: forfeiture of goods, monetary penalties up to £3,000 plus 100% of the evaded duty, retrospective clawback of customs duty relief with no limitation period in fraud cases, and imprisonment of up to 14 years for customs-related offences. Non-repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days is treated as a deemed Gibraltar-source profit, giving rise to tax assessment at the applicable corporate rate plus a negligence penalty of up to 100% of the tax charge and monthly compounded interest at 1.5% on the outstanding amount.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom and Gibraltar maintain fully liberalised current and capital account convertibility following the repeal of the Exchange Control Act 1947 and the absence of any foreign exchange control law in Gibraltar. There are no general restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, or repatriation of invested capital by a Bermuda Exempted Company operating through Gibraltar. Practical hurdles remain at the bank counterparty level: non-resident offshore entities are subject to enhanced due diligence, source-of-funds verification, CRS/FATCA self-certification, and economic-substance questionnaires. Remittances routed through Gibraltar are not dependent on central bank approval, but banks may delay transfers until the underlying trade documents, board resolutions, and corporate beneficial-ownership declarations are complete. Capital account flows may attract Bank of England reporting requirements when settlement occurs through UK clearers, and any transaction involving sanctioned jurisdictions or persons will be blocked under the UK Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or prescribed repatriation timelines in either the United Kingdom or Gibraltar. Export receipts may be retained in USD, EUR, or GBP at the commercial bank's discretion. The only applicable obligations are transactional: all export-related wire receipts above 10,000 EUR require accurate originator and beneficiary information; receipts connected to heparin-related trade must be supported by a copy of the export invoice and bill of lading to satisfy GFSC-sanctioned AML procedures. No portion of the export sale is required to be converted into Gibraltar pounds or GBP.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (sterling monetary authority) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the integrated banking and AML/CFT regulator for Gibraltar; the Gibraltar pound is issued by the Government of Gibraltar and pegged at par to GBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium feasibility. A Bermuda Exempted Company without local substance cannot rely on an offshore digital EMI licence as a permitted primary banking channel. Gibraltar law under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act and the Payment Services Act requires authorisation from the GFSC; a foreign fintech EMI licence is not automatically passported into Gibraltar. A licensed Gibraltar EMI or UK-authorized EMI registered with the GFSC can be used for ancillary operational expenses, but for heparin trade settlement, collection of export proceeds, and documentary credit operations, a licensed tier-1 bank is mandatory. Using an unregistered offshore EMI would create an AML red flag and materially jeopardise the entity's bankability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity or subordinated-loan injections from shareholders should be transacted exclusively through SWIFT MT103 wires from a recognised bank. The wire beneficiary must be the Gibraltar operational bank account, and the settlement instruction must include the corporate legal name, the bank account number, the IBAN, and the BIC of the receiving branch. For UK/Gibraltar filing purposes, the inbound credit must be tagged with the purpose code for 'Capital Injection, Shareholder Loan' or 'General Corporate Business' as required by the bank's regulatory reporting rules; the sender must include a capital-injection resolution and source-of-funds statement for amounts above GBP 50,000. Where the capital is denominated in USD or EUR, conversion into GIP/GBP should be executed at spot through the receiving bank; the bank will request a final board-approved exchange instruction and a copy of the share-purchase or subscription agreement. No central bank approval is required, but the inbound transfer must pass sanctions screening and be free of any links to assigned risk countries.For the active purchase of low-molecular-weight heparin crude and fractionation inputs, the primary clearing rails are: (1) SWIFT MT103 wire for standard supplier settlements and pre-payments when the supplier is deemed low-risk; (2) documentary Letters of Credit issued by SWIFT MT700 or MT707, with presentation documents negotiated under UCP 600, for high-value purchase contracts; (3) SWIFT MT798 and MT799 messages for LC amendments and bank-to-bank guarantees. Export proceeds should be mandated to the Gibraltar account using the customer's MT103 instructions with the invoice reference and Incoterms stated in the transport documents. For each disbursement, the bank will require the invoice, packing list, bill of lading or air waybill, and a signed board resolution when the payment exceeds the delegated authority threshold. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 15,000,000 group turnover and/or mandatory local file where controlled transactions in relation to crude heparin procurement exceed GIP 2,000,000; master file required where consolidated turnover exceeds GIP 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's Economic Substance Regulations (2018, as amended) apply to any tax-resident company (including a Bermuda Exempted Company managed from Gibraltar or a branch) carrying on a 'relevant activity' such as distribution and service centre operations. The heparin extraction entity must demonstrate: a registered office in Gibraltar; board meetings and strategic decision-making in Gibraltar; a physical operational presence (laboratory or manufacturing premises) with local employees; and adequate income-generating expenditure; otherwise the Commissioner may re-characterise profits, impose penalties up to GIP 100,000, or strike off the company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act, a fine of up to 100% of the tax evaded, interest at 8% per annum, and imprisonment for up to 7 years; the company may also be stripped of its exempt-company certificate with retroactive loss of 0% treatment.

Director personal liability: Where directors signed or knowingly procured the fraudulent return, the corporate veil will be pierced; they are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax. The Supreme Court may issue a travel ban, a property freezing order, a civil restitution order for the tax plus penalties, and a custodial sentence of up to 7 years (or 14 years under the Proceeds of Crime Act if proceeds were laundered).
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner adjusts the chargeable profit to reflect arm's length pricing and may impose a fixed penalty of GIP 10,000 per unfiled document; if the adjustment exceeds 20% of declared profit, an additional penalty of 100% of the tax underpaid.

Director personal liability: Directors who refused to cooperate with the Commissioner may be personally charged for the increased tax if the company is de facto a façade; disqualification as a director for up to 15 years and, on continuing default, imprisonment for a maximum of 3 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Regulations: penalty of GIP 100,000 for the first year of substance failure, GIP 200,000 for subsequent years; automatic revocation of the exempt-company status and re-assessment of all historical profits at 12.5%; ultimately, strike-off and dissolution under the Companies Act.

Director personal liability: Civil liability for all taxes recomputed from inception, plus an order for costs; the Gibraltar courts will 'pierce the corporate veil' when the company is found to be a mere alter ego, making directors personally liable for judgment debts; a director or shadow director may be banned from operating any company in Gibraltar for 10 years and denied work permits.
AML / Sanctions / Counter-Financial-Terrorism Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act and Sanctions Regulations, unlimited fines and/or imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering; facilitation of trade sanctions attracts fines of up to the greater of GIP 3,000,000 or 50% of the transaction value.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal prosecution even if they acted with gross negligence; the court may impose a travel ban, a worldwide asset freezing order, and personal restitution orders; any claim that the entity was a 'Bermuda Exempted Company' will not shield directors from liability where the company was used as a conduit and a 100% shareholder-owned vehicle.
