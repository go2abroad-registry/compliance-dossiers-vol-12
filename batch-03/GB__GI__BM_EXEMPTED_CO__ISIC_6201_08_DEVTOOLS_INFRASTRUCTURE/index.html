Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar: Gibraltar Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax) — official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi. Bermuda: Office of the Tax Commissioner (Corporate Income Tax) and the Registrar of Companies (Economic Substance Declaration) — portal: https://www.bermudataxhub.gov.bm. United Kingdom: HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC), VAT Registration Service — portal: https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Bermuda (home state): the Bermuda Exempted Company's identity is its Registrar of Companies file number; annual returns and the Economic Substance Declaration are filed through the Registrar's online portal, and, where the entity is part of an in-scope multinational group (consolidated global revenue exceeding EUR 750 million in at least one of the two preceding financial years), registration with the Office of the Tax Commissioner for a Bermuda Corporate Income Tax TIN is mandatory. Step 2 — Gibraltar (place of management/source of income): a Bermuda Exempted Company that establishes a permanent establishment or derives Gibraltar-source income must submit the prescribed application to the Commissioner of Income Tax for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN), attaching its memorandum and articles of association, Certificate of Incumbency, and director identity documents; registration is required within 30 days of commencing activity. Step 3 — UK VAT (market state): digital B2C supplies to UK consumers require HMRC registration as a non-established taxable person through the Government Gateway immediately upon first supply (no GBP 85,000 threshold), supported by proof of the Bermuda registration, director due-diligence evidence, and appointment of a UK tax representative if HMRC requires one post-Brexit.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda Exempted Company status — no Bermuda corporate income tax outside the 2025 Corporate Income Tax Act scope); Entity_Level_Taxation (Gibraltar permanent-establishment profits taxed at 12.5% standard corporation tax, with a 15% Pillar Two top-up for in-scope MNE groups from 2025); Territorial_Scope (Gibraltar taxes PE-attributed and local-source profits only; foreign-sourced passive income is excluded under the Income Tax Act)

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Bermuda, Gibraltar, or the UK. Bermuda: an in-scope exempted company may elect for CIT filing under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (effective 2025) or, outside that scope, may elect to be treated as a pure equity holding entity for Economic Substance Act reporting. Gibraltar: election for branch profit attribution under the Authorised OECD Approach is available; a foreign company may elect to become Gibraltar-resident where central management and control is established. UK: a non-established supplier may elect to operate through a UK tax representative (VATA 1994 s.48A); hybrid mismatch elections under TIOPA 2010 apply to Gibraltar-classified disregarded entities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

UK: GBP 85,000 annual taxable-turnover threshold for businesses established in the UK (VATA 1994 Sch. 1); non-established taxable persons, including a Bermuda Exempted Company supplying digital services from Gibraltar, must register for UK VAT immediately on the first supply to a UK consumer — no monetary threshold applies (HMRC VAT Notice 700/1 and the VAT place-of-supply rules for electronically supplied services). Gibraltar: no VAT registration threshold because no VAT is levied. Bermuda: no sales-tax registration threshold because no sales tax is levied.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical goods exported from the UK or Gibraltar may be zero-rated only where VATA 1994 s.30(6) and the VAT Export Regulations (SI 1995/2518 reg. 134) are satisfied: the goods must physically leave the territory within three months of the supply, and the supplier must hold valid export evidence in its records. Digital services (developer tools, database and API monitoring) do not qualify for zero-rating: B2B supplies are outside the scope of UK VAT under the place-of-supply reverse-charge rules; B2C supplies are taxable in the country of consumer residence (UK 20%, EU via OSS, or nil where no UK/EU consumer nexus). Bermuda and Gibraltar levy no VAT and therefore have no zero-rating or refund mechanism; input VAT incurred on UK/EU costs is recovered only through UK VAT returns or EU OSS refund procedures.

  • Customs Export Declaration (UK Customs Declaration Service CDS export entry or Gibraltar Customs export declaration) for physical goods; for digital services, a service contract, invoice, and performance/access logs replace the customs declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of exported goods from UK/Gibraltar within the three-month export window
  • Commercial Invoice and payment evidence, and, where applicable, an Inward Remittance Certificate or bank-account statement evidencing FX repatriation of export proceeds to the Bermuda Exempted Company's account
  • VAT records (MTD digital records, EC Sales List, OSS returns) and the Bermuda Economic Substance file documenting the underlying business activity

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, API stamping, or government pre-clearance e-invoicing regime exists in the UK, Gibraltar, or Bermuda. The UK mandates Making Tax Digital for VAT: every VAT-registered business must keep digital records and file returns via HMRC-approved API software (MTD-compliant); e-invoicing follows Peppol interoperability standards only for UK public-sector supply. Gibraltar and Bermuda impose no e-invoicing or fiscal-clearance mandate.

UK: fraudulent export zero-rating or missing-trader fraud triggers assessments and penalties up to 100% of evaded VAT (VATA 1994 s.60-s.61; FA 2007 Sch. 24), interest, criminal prosecution (VATA 1994 s.72; CEMA 1979) with imprisonment up to 7 years, retrospective 20-year assessment for deliberate non-disclosure, and joint-and-several-liability notices against contrived supply chains. Gibraltar: false statements and incorrect returns attract penalties up to 100% of the tax plus fines under the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended). Bermuda: failure to meet economic substance or CIT obligations attracts civil penalties, sanctions, and deregistration risk under the Economic Substance Act 2018 and Corporate Income Tax Act 2023, including clawback of the exempted tax status upon deregistration. Where a non-resident trader files a VAT credit without matching export evidence, the credit is denied, VAT is reassessed with interest from the original claim period, and an evasion penalty is imposed.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility: GIP meets de facto Article VIII obligations through the GBP peg, and both current-account (trade, SaaS subscription revenue, licence fees) and capital-account (intercompany loans, equity injections, dividend repatriation, liquidation proceeds) movements are unrestricted without prior central-bank approval. Practical hurdles are limited to bank-level AML/EDD evidence and CRS/ONS statistical purpose-code reporting. Repatriation of profit from a Bermuda Exempted Company itself is free of withholding in Bermuda, and Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividends, interest or royalties paid to non-residents. Residual risk: USD settlement/liquidity is provided through UK correspondent banks, so transfers touching sanctioned or grey-listed jurisdictions trigger enhanced due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar applies no export-proceeds surrender regime, no statutory conversion timeline, and no minimum mandatory conversion ratio. Export and subscription receipts denominated in USD, EUR or other currencies may be retained indefinitely in foreign currency; conversion to GIP/GBP is solely at the treasury manager's discretion. Only statistical reporting of the purpose code is required for Bank of England/GFSC balance-of-payments data.

Central bank supervision: Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the integrated banking and FX regulator of Gibraltar; the Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) under the Currency Notes Ordinance. There is no independent monetary policy: the Bank of England is the external monetary anchor and GIP is legally pegged at par (1:1) to GBP. No separate exchange-control authority imposes prior approval on FX transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium. Stripe Billing is viable as the primary merchant-acquiring rail for a Gibraltar/Bermuda Exempted Co cloud-subscription business and supports settlement in GIP/GBP and USD. Mercury ACH is NOT viable for the Gibraltar entity: Mercury requires a US domestic entity, a US EIN, and a US bank-facility signer. Compliance route: incorporate a Delaware C-Corp subsidiary as the US sales/merchant vehicle for Mercury ACH and Stripe's US rails, with dividends repatriated via MT103. Alternative EMIs that accept Gibraltar-incorporated companies are Wise Business and Currencycloud, offering USD/GBP/EUR local clearing (ACH — meaning ACH with US agent banks — SEPA via UK-owned IBANs, CHAPS). Critical legal constraint: EMIs are not deposit-taking institutions and are outside the Gibraltar Deposit Guarantee Scheme; daily sweeps of EMI balances to a Tier 1 GFSC-licensed bank are mandated by the treasury policy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity or subordinated debt via SWIFT MT103 (field 50K, 56A/57A, 59) from the Bermuda parent or UBO to the GIP-denominated account at the Gibraltar Tier 1 bank. For balance-of-payments statistical compliance use the ONS/Pink Book purpose code '3300/3310' (direct-investment equity injection) in field 70, with narrative 'ISSUANCE OF SHARES AND SHARE PREMIUM' or 'SHAREHOLDER SUBORDINATED LOAN'. Verification bundle: board resolution authorising the capital call; updated register of members; source-of-funds evidence (bank statements of the remitting account, dividend vouchers, prior-year accounts); CRS/BOE reporting acknowledgement; and a sanctions-screening clearance confirmation. Insist on the bank's use of field 70 purpose codes and retain MT103 confirmation with UETR for all cross-border flows; sweep USD balances to GBP/GIP only via the bank's published FX desk to avoid uneven intermediary spreads.Outright FX forwards (USD/GBP and USD/EUR) with tenors up to 24 months, documented under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or a short-form bank bilateral confirmation; permitted because GIP/GBP parity eliminates domestic FX risk and the USD/GIP exposure is linear and settable in the interbank market.FX swaps (spot/forward and forward/forward) used for liquidity-scheduling of Stripe USD settlements into GBP operating liquidity while preserving USD capex reserves.Foreign-currency options — collars, EU-style vanilla calls/puts and knock-in/knock-out barriers on USD/GBP — permitted under GFSC commercial-banking rules provided the Board approves a written hedging policy and the instruments hedge underlying cloud-revenue exposures.Cross-currency swaps (USD/GBP, USD/EUR) available where debt facilities are raised in a different currency than the entity's revenue base; requires ISDA CSA or credit-support annex and posting of eligible collateral with a UK bank counterparty.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permitted only for settlements in restricted currencies (e.g., BRL, INR, ZAR) received via merchant or trading platforms where deliverable liquidity is absent; NDFs on GIP/GBP are unnecessary and prohibited by policy given the fixed peg.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium. Stripe Billing is viable as the primary merchant-acquiring rail for a Gibraltar/Bermuda Exempted Co cloud-subscription business and supports settlement in GIP/GBP and USD. Mercury ACH is NOT viable for the Gibraltar entity: Mercury requires a US domestic entity, a US EIN, and a US bank-facility signer. Compliance route: incorporate a Delaware C-Corp subsidiary as the US sales/merchant vehicle for Mercury ACH and Stripe's US rails, with dividends repatriated via MT103. Alternative EMIs that accept Gibraltar-incorporated companies are Wise Business and Currencycloud, offering USD/GBP/EUR local clearing (ACH — meaning ACH with US agent banks — SEPA via UK-owned IBANs, CHAPS). Critical legal constraint: EMIs are not deposit-taking institutions and are outside the Gibraltar Deposit Guarantee Scheme; daily sweeps of EMI balances to a Tier 1 GFSC-licensed bank are mandated by the treasury policy.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% Bermuda corporate income tax (CIT) on the Bermuda Exempted Company; Gibraltar CIT of 12.5% applies only if central management and control is in Gibraltar or a Gibraltar permanent establishment (PE) arises, which is avoidable in a properly structured arrangement.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on dividends paid by the Bermuda Exempted Company under Bermuda law; 0% on Gibraltar-source dividends under Gibraltar law; the UK-Gibraltar Double Taxation Agreement (2019, in force 2022) contains no source-state dividend withholding in Gibraltar, leaving taxation to the UK residence state.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No codified Bermuda statutory threshold; OECD BEPS Action 13 Country-by-Country Reporting applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Bermuda and Gibraltar administrative practice requires Local File/Master File preparation where aggregate cross-border related-party transactions (IP royalties, intercompany management fees, financing charges) exceed BMD 500,000 (approximately GIP 500,000) per financial year.

Economic substance scope: The Bermuda Economic Substance (Requirements) Act 2018 (as amended) is fully applicable. The Exempted Company must either (i) demonstrate adequate physical premises, full-time employees, operating expenditure, and locally held board meetings in Bermuda for each relevant activity, or (ii) invoke the Non-Resident Entity carve-out by proving tax residence in Gibraltar/UK and providing the respective TRC to the Bermuda Registrar. The DevOps/API monitoring business (subscription and metered API revenue) is normally categorised as 'Holding Entity' if passive, or as 'Headquarters/Distribution and Service Centre' if it performs customer contracting, pricing, and IP management. 'Intellectual Property Business' and 'Financing and Leasing' categories receive the highest scrutiny from the Bermuda ES Review Team and UK HMRC information-exchange requests. Failure to meet the substance test results in penalty notices, automatic information exchange with the UK, and reassessment of profits.
