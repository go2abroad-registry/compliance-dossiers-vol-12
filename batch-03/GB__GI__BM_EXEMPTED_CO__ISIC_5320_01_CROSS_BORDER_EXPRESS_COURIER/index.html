Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, Government of Gibraltar (6-10 Parliament Lane, Gibraltar; online portal: https://www.ito.gi)

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company that is not incorporated in Gibraltar must first register as an overseas company under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 (Form OC-CR via Companies House Gibraltar). Within 30 days of commencing any taxable activity, the company must file a Notification of Taxability (Form NT) with the Income Tax Office, attaching a certified copy of the Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, a Certificate of Good Standing from the Bermuda Registrar, the Gibraltar overseas company registration certificate, and particulars of directors. Upon acceptance, a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) is issued. Annual Corporation Tax return form CT-1 must thereafter be filed irrespective of profitability. If the company does not have central management and control in Gibraltar and derives no Gibraltar-source income, it may apply for an advance non-resident clearance certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not employ US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections. Available elections and clearances include: (i) an application under Section 8(1) of the Income Tax Act 2010 to be treated as a non-resident company where central management and control is outside Gibraltar; (ii) an election for remitted-basis taxation for certain transitional cases; (iii) a formal application for Advance Tax Clearance from the Commissioner of Income Tax confirming that the Bermuda Exempted Company qualifies for an exemption from Gibraltar corporation tax on its foreign-sourced income; and (iv) designation as a 'not ordinarily resident' company for relief from tax on overseas income under the Income Tax (Persons Not Ordinarily Resident) Regulations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No General VAT/GST – Import Duties and Excise Duties Only

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – Gibraltar does not operate a VAT/GST system. There is no turnover-based registration threshold. Import duties are payable at the point of entry under the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Act, subject to the criteria for free circulation and re-export. The de minimis threshold for import documentation is £135 for consignments ceasing to be subject to customs control.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Gibraltar has no zero-rated VAT regime. Exports of goods from Gibraltar to non-Gibraltar destinations are outside the scope of Gibraltar consumption taxation and are neither subject to VAT nor eligible for a VAT refund. Where duty has been paid on imported goods that are subsequently re-exported from Gibraltar in an unaltered state (or after certain processing under supervised customs procedures), a drawback of customs duty may be claimed under the Gibraltar Customs Regulations. Such a drawback is conditional on actual re-exportation evidenced by customs endorsement.

  • Gibraltar Single Administrative Document (SAD) export entry (Surface Long Room Customs File reference)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Master Air Waybill or Bill of Lading evidencing physical export
  • Customs endorsement certificate for duty drawback / re-export
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank payment confirmation for the export proceeds
  • Courier consignment manifest including receiver's proof of delivery

No real-time B2B fiscal invoicing or digital stamping mandate applies. For import/export movement, GB-GI customs clearances are handled through the UK Customs Handling of Import and Export Freight (CHIEF) system or the new Single Customs Platform (SCP) with electronic submission of the Single Administrative Document (SAD). B2B invoices must comply with the Gibraltar Value Added Tax (Invoicing) Regulations only to the extent they evidence intra-Gibraltar customs entries; no government-issued QR code or real-time tax authority API certification is required.

Gibraltar's Customs and Excise Department, acting jointly with the Income Tax Office, may conduct retrospective audits of export documentation for up to 6 years (or without time limit where fraud is alleged). A false or inflated claim for duty drawback or export exemption triggers: (i) repayment of any duty refunded plus statutory interest; (ii) a penalty not exceeding 200% of the duty evaded; (iii) forfeiture of the goods and any related vehicles; and (iv) criminal liability subject to a fine of up to £250,000 and/or imprisonment up to 7 years for directors and senior officers. For corporate tax non-compliance connected to export income, the Income Tax Office may also claw back any exemption and levy a penalty of 100% of the tax unpaid.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is freely convertible into GBP at par for settlement, although GIP banknotes are legal tender only in Gibraltar. Capital-account movements — share capital injections by the Bermuda Exempted Company, intercompany loans, dividend repatriation, liquidation proceeds and repayment of shareholder debt — are not subject to exchange-control approval or volume quotas. Current-account payments for cross-border express courier services, including freight charges, customs duties and supplier disbursements, are unrestricted. Practical convertibility hurdles sit in AML/EDD documentation, enhanced source-of-funds verification for the Bermuda parent, and settlement inefficiency where GIP is accepted; SWIFT GBP/EUR/USD settlement is therefore the recommended treasury route.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or prescribed repatriation window for export and cross-border service proceeds. Receipts earned by the Bermuda Exempted Company from international time-definite courier activity may be retained in USD, EUR or GBP in external and on-island accounts. However, GFSC and banking counterparties will expect board-approved treasury policy and substance documentation demonstrating that currency retention is commercially justified rather than a tax-evasion artefact.

Central bank supervision: Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934, acting with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the banking and financial-services regulator; monetary policy is anchored by the Bank of England through the Gibraltar pound’s statutory 1:1 peg to pound sterling. There is no autonomous local central bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High. Gibraltar maintains an Electronic Money Institution licensing regime under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Regulations 2011, as amended, administered by the GFSC. UK and EEA EMI passports do not automatically extend to Gibraltar post-Brexit; a digital EMi may onboard only if separately GFSC-authorised or operating through a permitted Gibraltarian branch. For the Bermuda Exempted Company, a GFSC-authorised EMI is a viable payment-rail layer for client card-acquiring and ACH-type flows, but the main treasury balance must remain in a fully licensed commercial bank to avoid unregulated shadow-banking treatment and to preserve FSCS-independent cross-border clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from the Bermuda parent or shareholder into the Gibraltar operating account should be routed via SWIFT MT103 or, for two-legged correspondent transfers, MT202, using the ISO 20022 purpose code ‘EQUI’ for equity subscription or ‘LOAN’ for intercompany debt. The bank must receive the executed board resolution, subscription agreement or intercompany loan agreement, a funds-source statement, and a legal opinion from Bermuda counsel confirming the Exempted Company’s good standing. Pre-advise the receiving GFSC-licensed bank at least two business days before large value transfers and submit funds-received declarations to satisfy Gibraltar AML/CTF data expectations; avoid split-transaction structuring and ensure end-beneficiary labels match the account-holder legal name exactly.Outbound courier and trade disbursements should be executed by SWIFT MT103 for standard supplier payments with STP-quality beneficiary details, and by SWIFT MT700/MT710 where a confirmed commercial letter of credit is required under UCP600 with transport documents or courier receipts. For UK GBP legs, CHAPS or Faster Payment Service is appropriate; for euro-denominated supplier payments, SEPA Credit Transfer is the preferred clearing rail; ACH-Direct and credit-card POS rails are limited to client refunds and merchant settlement and must never be used for cross-border freight or customs disbursements. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Gibraltar year of assessment runs 1 July to 30 June; a company tax return must be delivered not later than 4 months after the end of the year of assessment, i.e., by 31 October following the tax year-end, for the basis period ending in that year of assessment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where the aggregate value of Gibraltar-resident controlled transactions with associated enterprises exceeds GBP 500,000 per chargeable period. Master File: required for multinational groups whose ultimate parent controls a group meeting the consolidated-revenue threshold of EUR 750 million. Country-by-Country Report: same EUR 750 million group-revenue threshold, filed in Gibraltar and exchanged automatically under the OECD CbCR framework; documentation must be maintained in English for the statutory retention period.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's economic substance regime, enacted through the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended) and the Economic Substance Regulations, requires resident companies carrying on relevant activities — including distribution and service-centre activity applicable to cross-border express courier operations — to maintain in Gibraltar a physical premises, locally employed and qualified staff, equipment and assets proportionate to the activity, locally exercised core income-generating functions, and board meetings and decisions conducted in Gibraltar. A Bermuda Exempted Company managed and controlled from Gibraltar is treated as Gibraltar tax resident; the Commissioner of Income Tax and the Registrar of Companies may require sworn declarations, issue compliance and penalty notices, and exchange information with HMRC under the UK-Gibraltar Exchange of Information Arrangement. Substance failure leads to financial penalties, strike-off, denial of treaty/TIEA benefits, and recharacterisation of the corporate structure as a shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / deliberate suppression of Gibraltar-source profits

Statutory sanction: Best of Judgment assessment at 12.5% with interest; civil penalty up to 100% of the understated tax, mitigated only by unprompted voluntary disclosure; criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion of income tax punishable by an unlimited fine and imprisonment not exceeding 7 years on indictment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or cause the filing of a false return are jointly and severally liable for the penalty, may be disqualified for up to 15 years, and, where investigation is active, face departure prohibition orders, asset-freezing injunctions, civil restitution of diverted courier revenues, and imprisonment.
Shell company / economic substance failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Regulations, the Commissioner issues a compliance notice followed by a penalty notice of up to GBP 10,000 plus continuing daily default penalties; the Registrar may strike off the company; information is automatically exchanged with the UK and TIEA partners, triggering foreign reassessments and withdrawal of treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are personally liable for daily default penalties; the court may pierce the corporate veil and require restitution of fee income earned through the conduit; travel bans and asset orders may attach where substance-failure findings are linked to cross-border tax evasion.
Transfer pricing manipulation / disguised profit extraction

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner adjusts the controlled transactions to the arm's length price under OECD-aligned rules, assesses additional corporation tax at 12.5%, adds late-payment interest, and imposes penalties up to 100% of the adjustment; general anti-avoidance provisions may recharacterise the arrangement in egregious cases.

Director personal liability: Finance and group directors who approved the non-arm's length intercompany pricing face joint and several personal liability for deficiencies where the company is insolvent or the arrangement amounts to fraudulent trading; freezing injunctions over personal assets and travel bans are available during cross-border collection and investigation.
AML violation / unlicensed postal and payment activity

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed express-postal operation attracts Gibraltar Regulatory Authority enforcement measures, suspension or revocation of the licence, and fines; commingled client funds may constitute unauthorised money transmission requiring a Gibraltar Financial Services Commission licence; the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (as amended) criminalises money laundering with an unlimited fine and imprisonment not exceeding 14 years, and the GFSC may impose administrative fines up to GBP 1,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors and the appointed compliance officer face individual criminal prosecution for AML failures; personal confiscation orders may be made against assets; travel bans may be imposed pending sentencing; the Bermuda exempted status will not shield a director once the Gibraltar activity is found to constitute a permanent establishment or place of effective management.
