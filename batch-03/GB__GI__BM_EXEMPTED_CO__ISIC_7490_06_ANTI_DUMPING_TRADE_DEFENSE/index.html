Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone – The Bermuda Exempted Company is exempt from Bermuda tax on non-Bermuda-source income; in Gibraltar it is classified as a non-resident foreign company chargeable only on profits attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment (territorial scope). Entity-level taxation applies where a Gibraltar nexus exists; no pass-through treatment is available.

Available election forms

Bermuda-only elections: (i) exempted-company election under the Exempted Companies Act 1981 and (ii) ESA 2019 economic-substance notification. Gibraltar: no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists; available administrative mechanisms are an advance non-residence / no-PE ruling from the Commissioner of Income Tax and an election to compute Gibraltar tax on a territorial branch-accounting basis where a PE is present. US-style hybrid elections are not recognised.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / GST / Sales-and-Use-Tax in either Bermuda or Gibraltar; only limited Gibraltar customs/excise duties (hydrocarbon oils, alcohol, tobacco, vehicles) and Gibraltar Insurance Premium Tax for regulated insurance lines. Professional consulting services are entirely outside any indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None: no VAT/GST turnover threshold applies. Goods importers must obtain a Gibraltar Customs registration/declarant code irrespective of value; service suppliers (ISIC 7490 anti-dumping trade-defence consulting) have no indirect-tax registration obligation in Bermuda or Gibraltar.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Intrinsic 0% effect: because no Gibraltar VAT/GST exists, all qualifying goods exports and offshore services are automatically outside the scope of any indirect tax. To preserve the tax-neutral character of the Bermuda Exempted Company the taxpayer must evidence: (i) services are supplied to non-Gibraltar-resident clients; (ii) work is performed outside Gibraltar by non-Gibraltar personnel; (iii) no Gibraltar PE or fixed base is maintained; (iv) full Bermuda economic substance is retained under ESA 2019; and (v) the commercial invoice and engagement contract identify the cross-border supply. For transiting goods, customs duty relief applies under free-zone/transit provisions; no VAT zero-rating claim is possible or required.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (SAD/CDS electronic acceptance)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 credit advice) evidencing export proceeds repatriation to the Gibraltar bank account

None: Gibraltar imposes no mandatory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or B2B API clearance. Professional services invoices are self-issued without government pre-clearance. Electronic customs declarations in SAD/CDS format apply only to physical goods movements, lodged through licensed customs brokers, and constitute duty control rather than indirect-tax fiscal clearance.

No export VAT zero-rating fraud exposure exists because there is no VAT system; however: (i) false customs declarations attract penalties of up to 100% of duty evaded, goods seizure, and smuggling offences under the Customs Duties Ordinance; (ii) the Gibraltar Commissioner may recharacterise purported offshore services as a Gibraltar PE or Gibraltar-source income, imposing 10% corporation tax plus interest and late-payment penalties of up to 10% of the tax; (iii) Bermuda ESA 2019 non-compliance attracts fines of up to USD 250,000 and up to 5% of relevant income, with possible revocation of the Tax Exemption Certificate; (iv) UK/Gibraltar GAAR may apply to artificial arrangements, resulting in backdated assessments, recharacterisation, and director disqualification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar and the United Kingdom maintain fully liberalised capital and current-account regimes; no exchange controls, prior approval, or surrender obligations apply to a Bermuda Exempted Company performing anti-dumping defense consulting. The GIP is freely convertible and legally guaranteed at parity with GBP, and USD/EUR liquidity is available via London correspondent clearing. Profit remittance and dividends to Bermuda are unrestricted and subject to no Gibraltar withholding tax; the only material constraint is tax residence: a BEC managed and controlled in Gibraltar is treated as resident and taxed at 12.5% (10% for regulated financial services) on Gibraltar-source income, while foreign-source fee income is taxable only when received in or remitted to Gibraltar. This territorial charge, not any FX restriction, is the principal repatriation planning cost.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion, or receivables repatriation timeline exists in Gibraltar or the United Kingdom for ISIC 7490 trade-remedy defense services. All foreign-currency fee receipts, success fees, and cost reimbursements may be retained in designated multi-currency accounts, converted at commercial rates, or applied directly to outbound disbursements. The only mandatory controls are transaction-documentation and monitoring: the GFSC-licensed bank must hold engagement contracts, invoices, and the transaction purpose for every payment; receipts above GBP 10,000 trigger standard customer due diligence; receipts above GBP 100,000 additionally require source-of-funds certification and a Transfer of Funds (FTR) record for the GFSC AML reporting systems.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the integrated regulator for banking, payments, and AML/CFT supervision; the Government of Gibraltar (Currency Commission) issues the Gibraltar Pound under the Currency Notes Act 1934; the Bank of England anchors monetary stability through the fixed 1:1 GIP/GBP parity; HM Treasury and OFSI administer the UK sanctions designations that bind Gibraltar under the Overseas Territories framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Post-Brexit, UK FCA-licensed EMIs and EEA-authorised payment institutions cannot passport into Gibraltar, and Gibraltar licences granted under the Financial Services Act 2019 (payment services) are not reciprocally recognised in the UK/EEA, so offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Revolut Business are usable only as secondary disbursement rails. Most digital EMIs routinely decline a Bermuda Exempted Company at onboarding or require a regulated Gibraltar TCSP introducer and a twelve-month operating history, because GFSC AML guidance requires the EMI to verify beneficial ownership and management location on the same evidentiary standard as a licensed bank. Recommended architecture: maintain Banco Santander or Gibraltar International Bank as the primary clearing and escrow bank, and use an EMI only for low-value disbursements such as refunds and per-diem expenses, with a daily sweep to the primary account; the EMI must never hold client escrow funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM. Post-Brexit, UK FCA-licensed EMIs and EEA-authorised payment institutions cannot passport into Gibraltar, and Gibraltar licences granted under the Financial Services Act 2019 (payment services) are not reciprocally recognised in the UK/EEA, so offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Revolut Business are usable only as secondary disbursement rails. Most digital EMIs routinely decline a Bermuda Exempted Company at onboarding or require a regulated Gibraltar TCSP introducer and a twelve-month operating history, because GFSC AML guidance requires the EMI to verify beneficial ownership and management location on the same evidentiary standard as a licensed bank. Recommended architecture: maintain Banco Santander or Gibraltar International Bank as the primary clearing and escrow bank, and use an EMI only for low-value disbursements such as refunds and per-diem expenses, with a daily sweep to the primary account; the EMI must never hold client escrow funds.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% standard corporation tax rate under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act (Cap. 100), as amended by the Income Tax (Amendment) Act 2024.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% – Gibraltar levies no withholding tax on dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders, regardless of the recipient's residence.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 – contemporaneous Local File is mandatory when annual aggregate cross-border related-party transactions (goods, services, royalties, financing) exceed GIP 1,000,000. Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds GIP/EUR 750 million, aligning with OECD Action 13 Country-by-Country thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar enacted Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) effective 1 January 2020, fully aligned with the EU Code of Conduct Group and the OECD Forum on Harmful Tax Practices. A Bermuda Exempted Company registered or managed in Gibraltar and carrying on the relevant activity of 'professional services – trade remedy defense' must maintain a location in Gibraltar (dedicated office), full-time qualified local employees who conduct the core income-generating activities (case strategy, submission drafting to anti-dumping authorities, hearings preparation), and operating expenses proportionate to turnover. The entity must file an annual Economic Substance Return (ESR-1) within 12 months of the fiscal year end. A non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company without Gibraltar presence is exempt from ESR, but then must prove foreign tax residence and cannot claim Gibraltar territorial exclusion.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 100% of the tax undercharged; default interest at the prescribed Gibraltar rate; criminal conviction under the Crimes Act 2011 §136 – imprisonment for up to 7 years and/or unlimited fine; forfeiture of proceeds under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil to hold directors fully and jointly liable for all corporate unpaid taxes and penalties where the company is a 'mere façade'; directors face travel bans under the Gibraltar Court (Travel Restrictions) Regulations, disqualification from directorship for 10 years, restitution orders over personal pensions, and imprisonment up to 7 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Regulations: first non-compliance penalty of £50,000; second consecutive breach penalty of £100,000; where substance is wholly absent, the Income Tax Commissioner is empowered to levy a 15% 'substance top-up tax' on the company's entire adjusted profit; the Registrar may strike off the company and refer directors for criminal filing of false declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the top-up tax and ESR penalties if they signed the false declaration; judicial veil-piercing orders require directors to reimburse the company for penalties; individual travel restrictions may be imposed pending repayment; disqualification for up to five years under the Companies Act 2014.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profits to the arm's length figure; penalty of 35% of the resulting additional tax payable; separate documentation penalty of GIP 10,000 per schedule per period; interest on adjustment assessed from the original filing date; publication of the adjustment notice in the Gibraltar Gazette for persistent offenders.

Director personal liability: The Finance Act 2020 imposes a personal penalty of up to GIP 50,000 on any director who negligently authorises false transfer pricing documentation; directors may be made jointly and severally liable for the adjusted corporate tax if the company is dissolved to avoid payment; bank account freezing orders may be issued against the director's personal assets where the transfer-pricing adjustment exceeds 50% of gross revenues.
AML and International Sanctions

Statutory sanction: AML fines up to GIP 500,000 per breach under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; preferential drain on assets through forfeiture orders; sanctions evasion is a strict-liability criminal offense: on conviction, imprisonment up to 7 years and an unlimited fine; the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2025 empowers the Supreme Court to wind up the company.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or recklessly ignored the compliance failure incur personal fines of up to £1,000,000; personal bank accounts and passports may be seized; foreign travel bans and extradition to the UK may be ordered; conviction for money laundering can result in 14 years' imprisonment in Gibraltar, with any periods reduced by time spent in pre-trial detention.
