Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda Exempted Company statutorily exempt from Bermuda taxation until 2035 under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966). For Gibraltar purposes the entity is a foreign company subject to Territorial_Scope: Gibraltar-source trade profits are taxable at 12.5%, while offshore service income from data labeling and RLHF curation performed outside Gibraltar falls outside the Gibraltar income tax charge.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (1) group relief election on a 75% group basis under the Income Tax Act 2010; (2) capital allowance pooling and balancing-charge elections; (3) branch profit territorial-computation election for a non-resident company carrying on a Gibraltar trade; (4) under Bermuda law, the election for a non-resident exempted company to file the Economic Substance Act Form ES/R designating its tax-resident jurisdiction (e.g., Gibraltar) and evidencing core income-generating activities; (5) dual-residence tie-breaker application under the UK-Gibraltar Double Taxation Agreement. Hybrid mismatch: the Income Tax (Hybrid and Mismatch Arrangements) Regulations 2020 neutralize deductible hybrid mismatches; no elective step-up is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar does not operate VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax. The only indirect taxes are Import Duty and Excise Duty under the Customs Ordinance 1962 and the Excise Duties Act 2014, levied primarily on alcohol, tobacco, fuel and motor vehicles. AI data-labeling and AI-training BPO services are outside the scope of any Gibraltar indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - there is no VAT/GST registration threshold in Gibraltar due to the absence of VAT/GST. For import duty, any entity importing physical goods into Gibraltar must hold a customs registration and file an entry per consignment; duty is assessed ad valorem on the CIF import value, generally 0% for digital/BPO equipment categories, with excise rates on restricted goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - there is no VAT/GST regime in Gibraltar and therefore no 0% export VAT rating or refund mechanism to claim. For corporate income-tax purposes, an effective 0% tax result on cross-border AI data-labeling and RLHF export services is achieved where the Bermuda Exempted Company demonstrates that central management and control is located outside Gibraltar, or that the services are wholly performed outside Gibraltar by a foreign permanent establishment, so that profits are not 'accruing in or derived from Gibraltar'. If any part of the services is performed in Gibraltar, a Gibraltar-source profit apportionment is required and the 12.5% corporate rate applies to that part.

Invoices need only comply with general commercial law and must be retained for six years for income-tax verification. The Gibraltar Companies House e-services and Income Tax Office online forms support filing but not transaction-level electronic clearance.

No VAT export zero-rating exists in Gibraltar, so no VAT clawback applies. Penalties arise under parallel regimes: (1) Income Tax Act 2010 - fraudulent or incomplete returns attract penalties up to 100% of undercharged tax plus default interest; (2) Customs Ordinance 1962 - false export declarations trigger forfeiture of goods and penal duties up to three times the duty evaded; (3) as a Bermuda Exempted Company, the Economic Substance (Bermuda) Act 2018 imposes administrative penalties up to BMD 250,000 and possible restoration or revocation of the exemption certificate for missing substance filings; (4) under the UK-Gibraltar DTA and exchange-of-information mechanisms, retrospective reclassification of tax residence (Bermuda vs Gibraltar) is possible where central management and control is actually exercised in Gibraltar, leading to 12.5% CIT assessments and late-payment interest. Gibraltar has no exchange-control law, but the 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation evidence standard is applied by banks and auditors to substantiate the offshore-source income exclusion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account. Gibraltar and the United Kingdom have had no exchange controls since 1979, and no prior-authorisation or surrender regime applies to a Gibraltar-resident branch. The Gibraltar Pound is freely convertible into GBP at par and into any other currency through the commercial banking system; unrestricted foreign-currency (USD/EUR) operational accounts are permitted. Profit and capital remittance from a Gibraltar operation to the Bermuda parent is unrestricted, is not subject to any exchange-control approval, and incurs no dividend withholding tax. The only practical impediments are the 12.5% Gibraltar corporation tax on a Gibraltar tax-resident company, the annual audit and filing obligations, and the requirement that each inbound/outbound payment carry a truthful balance-of-payments purpose narrative.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Neither Gibraltar nor the United Kingdom imposes a statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export or services-consideration receipts. A Bermuda Exempted Company designated non-resident for Bermuda Exchange Control Act 1972 purposes is likewise free of Bermuda surrender rules. The sole compliance burden is statistical: the receiving bank must classify payments under the Gibraltar/UK international transaction reporting system (ITRS) balance-of-payments categories, and false or misleading purpose codes are offences under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (sole monetary authority of the sterling zone and ultimate issuer of the reserve sterling that backs Gibraltar Pound note issue); Government of Gibraltar Commissioner of Currency (issues GIP banknotes and coins at a fixed 1:1 peg against Bank of England notes); Financial Services Commission of Gibraltar (banking and AML/CFT supervision); Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit (suspicious transaction reporting); Bermuda Monetary Authority (exchange control administration in the entity's jurisdiction of incorporation); HM Treasury Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI), whose sanctions regime is mirrored by the applicable Gibraltar Sanctions Regulations.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% Bermuda corporate income tax for a Bermuda Exempted Company; a newly enacted Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act applies a 15% rate only to constituent entities of in-scope multinational groups with global revenue above EUR 750 million, which is not the assumed base case for a standalone AI data-labelling BPO.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% under Bermuda law on dividends paid by a Bermuda Exempted Company; UK domestic dividend withholding is also 0%, and Gibraltar does not impose a withholding tax on distributions to shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: BMD/USD 850 million consolidated group revenue triggers Bermuda country-by-country reporting notification and ESR-related obligations; there is no Bermuda local-file or master-file monetary threshold because no corporate income tax applies. If the same group has a UK or Gibraltar taxable presence, HMRC generally requires local file/master file documentation for large or medium enterprises and controlled transactions exceeding £1 million; Gibraltar follows OECD transfer-pricing documentation principles.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda's Economic Substance Regulations 2018 require all exempted companies carrying on relevant activities to be managed and directed in Bermuda, maintain adequate full-time employees, physical premises, and operating expenditure, and perform their core income-generating activity in Bermuda. An AI data-labelling BPO is a service business relevant activity when the entity provides services to connected persons; if it serves unrelated clients, the company must still maintain documentary evidence showing that the Bermuda entity is not a shell. Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations may also apply to any Gibraltar tax-resident entity carrying on relevant activities, creating overlapping compliance obligations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Bermuda penalties of up to BMD 50,000 and imprisonment up to five years; UK HMRC civil penalties of up to 100% of the tax understated; criminal tax evasion sanctions and an unlimited fine under the Criminal Finances Act 2017 for failure to prevent tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns can be personally prosecuted, disqualified for up to 15 years, subjected to travel bans, asset freezes and confiscation orders, and ordered to make civil restitution to the tax authority.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda ESR penalties of BMD 10,000 per month after default, escalating financial penalties, automatic exchange of information with HMRC, deregistration, and potential recharacterisation of the entity as UK resident with corporation tax assessed at 19% to 25% on worldwide profits.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors and beneficial owners personally liable for unpaid UK corporation tax, impose director disqualification, restrict travel, and order imprisonment for contempt or fraudulent evasion.
Money Laundering / AML

Statutory sanction: Bermuda's Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 imposes penalties up to 14 years' imprisonment and unlimited fines for money laundering; AML/CFT code violations can result in civil penalties up to BMD 1 million; unlicensed money service activity is a separate criminal offence in the UK and Gibraltar.

Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable where AML offences are committed with their consent or connivance; they may face travel bans, personal confiscation orders, director disqualification, and civil recovery proceedings for assets derived from unlawful conduct.
Transfer Pricing / UK Permanent Establishment

Statutory sanction: HMRC may impose transfer-pricing adjustments, charge interest, and assess penalties up to 100% of any understated tax; the company may be denied treaty benefits under the principal purpose test, face CbCR penalties, and be subject to UK corporation tax on attributable profits.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability for wrongful trading if the group becomes insolvent following tax adjustments; HMRC can obtain court orders for director penalties, impose travel restrictions during serious non-payment, and pursue custodial sentences in cases of deliberate tax evasion.
