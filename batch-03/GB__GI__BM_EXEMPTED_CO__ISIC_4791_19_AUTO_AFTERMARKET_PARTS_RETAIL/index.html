Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Gibraltar classification: non-resident offshore company; the Bermuda Exempted Company is subject to territorial taxation only on Gibraltar-source income. It is not subject to Gibraltar income tax on worldwide profits and is not a pass-through entity; any Gibraltar-source corporate profit is taxed at entity level at the standard company rate of 10%).

Available election forms

No check-the-box or general pass-through election exists in Gibraltar. The Bermuda Exempted Company is a juridical corporation and cannot elect transparent treatment. Under Bermuda law, it holds and maintains a certificate of exemption under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, securing tax-exempt status in Bermuda. For hybrid mismatch purposes, the UK TIOPA 2010 Part 6A anti-hybrid rules may apply to the cross-border structure, but Gibraltar does not provide a unilateral hybrid classification election; treatment follows the entity's legal constitution and actual management and control.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar: no VAT/GST/sales-and-use tax; limited indirect taxes consist mainly of customs import duties, excise duties on specified goods (alcohol, tobacco, petroleum), and stamp/registration duties. Online retail sales of automotive aftermarket parts from Gibraltar therefore carry no Gibraltar indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; Gibraltar imposes no turnover-based indirect-tax registration threshold because it has no general consumption tax. If the seller holds inventory in the UK or otherwise exceeds UK VAT registration thresholds, UK VAT registration may be required (standard UK VAT threshold GBP 90,000, or lower distance-selling thresholds).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

No Gibraltar VAT is levied, so there is no origin VAT output tax to zero-rate; exports from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax provided the goods physically leave Gibraltar and documentary evidence of export is retained. If the same Bermuda Exempted Company supplies goods from UK inventory, UK VAT zero-rating under VATA 1994 s.30 applies to direct exports to customers outside the UK, subject to export within the statutory time limit (normally three months) and retention of full export evidence. Offshore digital or ancillary services supplied to non-UK/non-Gibraltar business customers may be outside the scope of UK VAT under the place-of-supply rules.

  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank receipt/SWIFT MT103 evidencing payment and repatriation of export proceeds
  • Certificate of origin or preferential origin documentation where a trade agreement preference is claimed

None in Gibraltar; no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, API clearance, or mandatory e-invoicing regime applies. Commercial invoices and customs declarations must be retained for customs and tax verification. If the entity registers for UK VAT, UK HMRC Making Tax Digital requires digital VAT record-keeping and compatible software, but no live fiscal invoice clearance is required.

Under Gibraltar customs legislation, false or misleading export declarations attract monetary penalties, seizure/forfeiture of goods, and potentially imprisonment up to 2 years for deliberate evasion. Where UK VAT zero-rating is claimed, HMRC may retrospectively claw back output VAT plus interest, assess civil penalties up to 100% of the tax for careless or deliberate inaccuracies, and pursue criminal penalties of up to 7 years imprisonment for fraudulent evasion or fraudulent zero-rating claims. The retrospective assessment window is normally 6 years, extended to 20 years in cases of fraud or deliberate non-disclosure.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar applies full current and capital account convertibility with no central-planning exchange controls. GIP is freely convertible to GBP at par; bank accounts may be held in GIP, GBP, EUR, USD, and other major currencies. Profit remittance from a non-resident exempt company is unrestricted and not subject to withholding, but remitting banks require underlying trade or equity documentation for FX evaluation and AML source-of-funds checks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation. Export receipts from the online auto-parts retail trade may be retained indefinitely in their original currency. Mandatory conversion ratio is 0%; no timeline applies in Gibraltar. If proceeds are sent from third-party marketplaces, the bank will need merchant settlement reports to identify trade as opposed to fee flows.

Central bank supervision: Government of Gibraltar – Currency Commissioner (sole issuer of Gibraltar pound); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as banking and exchange-control administrator; HM Treasury/Bank of England provides sterling monetary backstop because GIP is pegged 1:1 to GBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as primary clearing rail. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Payoneer) are not Gibraltar-licensed credit institutions and therefore cannot open domestic GIP settlement accounts or issue commercial letters of credit. After Brexit they have lost EU passporting rights into Gibraltar; they can act only as collection/payout agents through a licensed Gibraltar bank.
