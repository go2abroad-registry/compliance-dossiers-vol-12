Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity (Bermuda Exempted Company) that acquires a permanent establishment in Gibraltar or derives Gibraltar-source income must submit the Registration for Tax form (REG-TAX) to the Commissioner of Income Tax within 30 days of commencement. Required documents: certified Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, constitutional documents (Memorandum and Articles), identity of ultimate beneficial owners, and details of registered office in Gibraltar. The Commissioner issues a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (GTRN). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (the Bermuda Exempted Company is exempt from Bermuda income tax until 2035 under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act; in Gibraltar, absent a permanent establishment or effectively connected Gibraltar-source income, it remains outside the net of Gibraltar corporation tax; if central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, it may become tax resident and then subject to Gibraltar's territorial 10% corporate income tax on locally sourced profits).

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Entity classification follows the legal form under the Gibraltar Companies Act. A foreign exempted company may elect, by written notification to the Commissioner, to be treated as non-resident for Gibraltar tax where the central management and control is maintained outside Gibraltar. Alternatively, it may elect to become Gibraltar resident by adopting a resolution of the board and appointing Gibraltar directors/registered office, thereby importing the territorial tax regime. Further, the entity can apply to Bermuda's Ministry of Finance for the statutory Tax Exemption Certificate, guaranteeing the continued tax-exempt treatment in Bermuda and creating hybrid-mismatch protection in cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Purchase Tax (Gibraltar indirect duty – not VAT/GST)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable (Gibraltar has no VAT/GST). Any person importing goods or conducting taxable transactions in respect of goods subject to Purchase Tax must register with HM Customs Gibraltar as a business; there is no turnover exemption threshold. For export-only businesses, registration for customs purposes is mandatory even with zero economic threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of commercial goods, including organic dried goji berries, from Gibraltar to destinations outside Gibraltar are zero-rated for Purchase Tax provided: (i) the exporter is the declarant on the SAD and holds a valid customs registration; (ii) the goods physically leave Gibraltar within 90 days of customs release; (iii) there is no local sale or consumption; and (iv) supporting evidence of exportation is preserved. Offshore services (e.g., brokerage, logistics) provided to non-Gibraltar recipients are also outside the territorial scope of Gibraltar taxation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document - SAD)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (inbound transfer / SWIFT confirmation evidencing payment settlement)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (for preferential tariff treatment or Bermuda destination compliance)
  • Phytosanitary Certificate and Export Health Certificate for dried botanical products

Gibraltar does not impose a real-time e-invoicing clearance or fiscal stamping system. Export declarations must be lodged electronically using the Gibraltar Customs Electronic Declaration System (CEDS) through the Single Administrative Document (SAD). For high-volume exporters, the Authorised Economic Operator (AEO) status may be used to expedite clearance. Digital documentation, including e-signatures on export journey data and electronic bills of lading, are accepted on submission of the SAD.

Gibraltar Customs and the Income Tax Office carry out joint post-clearance audit reviews of zero-rated exports. If a taxpayer fails to substantiate physical export or to provide proof that the foreign sale proceeds were repatriated within the 180-day deadline, the zero-rating is revoked and the Purchase Tax becomes assessable with default interest. Penalties include a 25% surcharge on under-declared duty, penalties up to 100% of the omitted tax, and, for fraudulent claims, criminal prosecution leading to imprisonment and retrospective clawback of the full tax plus interest for up to 6 years from the end of the accounting period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full and unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account. GIP is convertible 1:1 into Bank of England sterling without central-bank approval; USD, EUR, CHF, JPY, and CNH are freely convertible through GFSC-licensed banks at commercial interbank rates; no capital controls, investment screens, profit-remittance quotas, conversion taxes, or repatriation waiting periods apply to a Bermuda Exempted Company operating in Gibraltar. Profit remittance is constrained only by private-law and fiscal formalities: (i) a dividend declaration must satisfy the Bermuda Companies Act 1981 solvency test and be supported by IFRS/Gibraltar-GAAP audited financial statements; (ii) remittances above USD 100,000 equivalent should cite the appropriate cross-border payment purpose code to avoid triggering a suspicious-activity report; and (iii) CRS/FATCA reporting of each natural-person ultimate beneficial owner is mandatory. For dried-goji and herbal-botanical export receipts, conversion of USD/EUR into GIP/GBP is offered transparently with no fixed margin cap under GFSC conduct of business rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rule exists. Gibraltar and UK exchange-control law impose no repatriation timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to sell export proceeds to the central monetary authority; the applicable conversion ratio is 0% and the permitted retention period is unlimited. Export proceeds may be held indefinitely in the invoiced currency (USD, EUR, or GBP) in any GFSC-regulated bank or, subject to board treasury policy, within the fintech payment layer. For an ISIC 4631 dried-goji and herbal-botanicals wholesaler, the onboarding bank will expect documentary substantiation of export transactions (signed sales contracts, bills of lading, phytosanitary certificates, and proof of delivery) both to preserve the Low_Risk_Standard AML classification and to evidence the economic substance required for distribution activities under Gibraltar law.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary anchor for the Gibraltar pound, which is issued by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934 at strict parity with sterling) and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), established under the Financial Services Commission Act 2007, as the integrated supervisory and licensing authority over banking, payments, and foreign-exchange activity. There is no separate Gibraltar central bank or foreign-exchange administration; the United Kingdom Exchange Control Act 1947, as extended to Gibraltar, was repealed in 1979, and Gibraltar has not re-enacted an exchange-control regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: High (7/10) as a fast settlement and FX-conversion layer; Low (2/10) as the sole depository for capital or trade surplus. Legal constraints: (i) UK FCA-licensed e-money institutions (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Currencycloud) may onboard a Gibraltar corporate on commercial terms without passporting into Gibraltar, and no regulatory prohibition prevents a Gibraltar entity from maintaining such an account remotely; (ii) EU/EEA EMIs may service the entity only if their internal onboarding permits non-resident corporate clients from British Overseas Territories; (iii) e-money funds are not bank deposits and are not covered by the Gibraltar Depositor Compensation Scheme or the UK Financial Services Compensation Scheme, so balances must be swept daily to a GFSC-licensed bank; (iv) the Bermuda Exempted Company must supply full CDD and a constitutional pack to the EMI, including a CRS self-certification, certificate of incumbency, and cancellation-of-bearer-shares evidence; and (v) the EMI layer is valid for supplier payments below USD 50,000 per transaction and for FX conversion, but GFSC supervisory practice requires open-account settlement of wholesale herbal invoices above that threshold to be executed over bank SWIFT rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Primary routing is via SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) denominated in GIP or GBP from the shareholder's bank to the Gibraltar operating account, executed with a UETR, ISO 20022 structured remittance, and, for UK-based remitters, the Bank of England ITRS purpose code 7001 (direct investment - equity capital); intercompany shareholder loans use purpose code 7003 (other capital). For same-day GBP, instruct the remitter to use CHAPS with the beneficiary bank's SWIFT BIC and GIP/GBP account number; no IBAN is required outside the EEA. For USD capital, route by Fedwire or CHIPS to the Gibraltar bank's US correspondent and forward an MT103 cover with the identical transaction reference; for EUR, route via SEPA/TARGET2 and provide an IBAN if the beneficiary account is European-cleared. The verification bundle must include a board resolution authorising the capital increase, the Bermuda share-register entry, MT103 confirmations from both sending and receiving banks, IFRS evidence of the capital booking, a source-of-funds statement supported by the shareholder's audited accounts or disposal contract, and a no-objection letter from the corporate services provider confirming the funds are free of lien or encumbrance.Tier 1: SWIFT MT103 with UETR and structured remittance data for supplier payments up to USD 100,000 equivalent. Tier 2: irrevocable, documentary, confirmed at-sight commercial letter of credit under UCP600 ICC Publication 600, executed through the SWIFT MT700-MT400-MT754 sequence, for large bulk goji, lycium, and herbal FCL shipments; this also protects the open-account risk of the cross-border distribution chain. Tier 3: MT202 COV (cover-only payment) where the beneficiary bank requires a separate chain-cover clearing instruction. USD creditor disbursements clear through the US correspondent (Fedwire/CHIPS), EUR through TARGET2, and GBP through CHAPS. Every disbursement must attach the underlying invoice, letter-of-credit document package, or purchase-order evidence in the bank's payment matrix; no third-party settlements, no cash advances to suppliers, and no crypto-denominated settlement are permitted.
