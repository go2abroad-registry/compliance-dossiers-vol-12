Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Ministry of Finance), together with Gibraltar Companies House, Gibraltar Economic Substance Office, and Gibraltar Customs and Excise Department; official portal: www.gibraltar.gov.gi

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company establishing presence in Gibraltar must: (1) appoint a licensed Gibraltar registered agent; (2) file particulars with Gibraltar Companies Registry as an overseas company and obtain a Companies House number; (3) register with the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number if it will derive Gibraltar-source income or maintain a permanent establishment in Gibraltar; (4) obtain a customs and excise registration number only if importing goods into Gibraltar; no VAT registration exists because Gibraltar has no VAT. A non-resident company without a permanent establishment may alternatively file a nil return or formal non-residence notification to avoid an unnecessary tax reference.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company establishing presence in Gibraltar must: (1) appoint a licensed Gibraltar registered agent; (2) file particulars with Gibraltar Companies Registry as an overseas company and obtain a Companies House number; (3) register with the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number if it will derive Gibraltar-source income or maintain a permanent establishment in Gibraltar; (4) obtain a customs and excise registration number only if importing goods into Gibraltar; no VAT registration exists because Gibraltar has no VAT. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections. The entity may, by board minute and substantive conduct, elect its seat of central management and control to remain outside Gibraltar, preserving non-resident territorial-scope treatment. If central management and control is exercised from Gibraltar or a permanent establishment exists, Gibraltar corporate tax at 12.5% will apply to Gibraltar-source and attributable income. The entity may also elect to comply with Bermuda and Gibraltar economic substance legislation to preserve exempt status; no further statutory tax-election form exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or general sales-and-use tax in Gibraltar; limited customs duty, excise duty, and stamp duty apply to goods and specified documents

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Gibraltar has no VAT/GST registration threshold. Registration with Gibraltar Customs and Excise is separately required for importers of goods and for excise-bearing commodities, with no turnover de minimis exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because Gibraltar does not levy VAT or GST on supplies or exports. Portfolio advisory and multi-family office service fees have no Gibraltar indirect-tax zero-rating claim. Goods physically exported from Gibraltar are outside the import-duty and excise charge subject to lodgement of an export declaration; services are outside the customs duty base entirely.

  • Commercial invoice or detailed fee schedule
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing departure from Gibraltar
  • Packing list and, where applicable, certificate of origin or export licence
  • Bank inward remittance certificate or treasury settlement evidence showing export proceeds received
  • Insurance certificate and freight records substantiating the physical export movement

None. Gibraltar does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, continuous-control API validation, or digital stamping. Taxpayers must retain invoices and accounting records for at least six years. Customs entries are filed electronically through the Gibraltar customs declaration system, but that is not an e-invoicing clearance mandate.

No VAT zero-rating refund or clawback mechanism exists. However, false customs declarations, misuse of import/excise reliefs, or export drawback fraud are prosecuted under the Customs and Excise Act and may result in forfeiture of goods and conveyances, recovery of up to three times the duty evaded, financial penalties, and imprisonment. If MFO management fees or export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day evidence window, Gibraltar tax and AML authorities may treat the income as undisclosed, giving rise to income tax, interest, penalties of up to 100% of the tax due, and anti-money-laundering enforcement, in addition to any Bermuda economic substance or regulatory sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies to both current and capital account transactions; Gibraltar maintains no exchange control legislation. GIP/GBP parity removes conversion friction for UK-origin capital. Profit remittance, shareholder loan repayments, management fees, and redemption proceeds flow freely to non-resident beneficial owners without central bank authorisation. The only operative constraints are bank-level source-of-funds verification, CRS/FATCA reporting, and GFSC AML/CFT obligations for the licensed Multi-Family Office.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable to MFO service receipts. Gibraltar imposes no mandatory surrender obligation, no statutory deposit timeline, and no conversion ratio for inbound receipts or earned fee income. Foreign currency (GBP, EUR, USD) may be retained in original denomination indefinitely. The sole requirement is internal transaction monitoring to detect and report suspicious inflows under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar).

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no independent central bank. The Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) under the Currency Notes Act 1934, pegged at 1:1 to GBP with Bank of England notes and coinage as legal tender. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the integrated financial regulator and AML/CFT supervisor; the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit (GFIU) receives suspicious activity reports; HM Treasury (United Kingdom) retains constitutional oversight of monetary stability and emergency liquidity for the Overseas Territory.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: LOW. Gibraltar has no domestic EMI licensing regime under the Financial Services Act 2019; UK FCA-authorised EMIs have no automatic passport into Gibraltar post-Brexit, and non-EEA offshore EMIs (Lithuania, Malta, Cyprus) routinely decline Bermuda-exempted discretionary MFO structures due to the High_Risk_Special_License classification under ISIC 6430. A GFSC-licensed trust and company service provider relationship, plus a sponsored onboarding at a Tier-1 Gibraltar bank, is the only workable route; white-label API banking through a GFSC-licensed bank may emulate EMI functionality without an EMI licence.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 (aggregate cross-border controlled transactions in an accounting period). A Master File is required where the consolidated group turnover exceeds GIP 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance (Company) Regulations 2019 require companies carrying on 'relevant activities' (including fund management, holding company activities, headquarters, services and financing) to demonstrate economic presence in Gibraltar. A Multi-Family Office providing discretionary portfolio advisory will be treated as a fund management or service centre activity. Such a company must hold board meetings in Gibraltar, have an adequate number of qualified full-time employees (generally at least 3), incur adequate operational expenditure (minimum GIP 250,000 for financial services), maintain a physical premises in Gibraltar, and file an annual Economic Substance Report (ESR-1) with the Companies Registry and the Commissioner. For a Bermuda Exempted Company managed and controlled from Gibraltar, these substance requirements apply equally; pure tax-exempt status in Bermuda does not exempt it from Gibraltar ESR and CIT obligations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction: a fine of up to GIP 5,000 and/or imprisonment up to 6 months. On indictment: a fine of up to GIP 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. The Commissioner may additionally demand twice the tax evaded and interest at 8% per annum. For false returns, a civil penalty of up to 100% of the understated tax applies.

Director personal liability: Directors who are knowingly concerned in evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, penalties and interest. The Supreme Court may issue a travel ban and order surrender of passports. Directors can be personally imprisoned for up to 5 years and disqualified from acting as directors for up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ESR non-compliance attracts an initial penalty of GIP 2,000, plus GIP 500 per month of continued breach, escalating to GIP 10,000 for aggravated cases. The Registrar may strike the company off the Gibraltar Companies Registry, dissolve the entity, and invalidate the Bermuda exemption certificate. Continued non-compliance may lead to automatic winding-up and disqualification orders.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for ESR penalties where the company is insolvent. A travel ban and exit restriction may be imposed by the Supreme Court pending payment. Disqualification from acting as a director for up to 15 years can be ordered; contempt of court for failure to comply with an ESR investigation carries a sentence of up to 3 years' imprisonment.
Unauthorized Financial Services and AML Breach (Multi-Family Office)

Statutory sanction: Criminal offence under section 118 of the Financial Services Act: upon conviction, a fine of up to GIP 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to 10 years. The GFSC may revoke any existing licences, issue public censure, impose supervisory penalties up to GIP 500,000, and order restitution to affected clients. AML offences carry separate fines up to GIP 250,000 per individual and imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are personally liable for AML penalties and may be ordered to compensate affected clients. The GFSC can impose a personal ban of up to 15 years on acting as a director of any regulated entity. Travel bans are routinely issued to prevent flight pending prosecution; in egregious cases, seizure or surrender of passports is ordered and extradition to the UK or Bermuda may be pursued.
