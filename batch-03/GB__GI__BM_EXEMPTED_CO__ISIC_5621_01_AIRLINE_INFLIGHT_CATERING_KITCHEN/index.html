Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

UK: Her Majesty's Revenue & Customs (HMRC), Government Gateway portal. Gibraltar: Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar; online portal 'MyTax Gibraltar' (https://mytax.gi).

E-government filing portal

Step 2: Appoint a Gibraltar-resident process agent and any licensed corporate service provider required for the bonded catering licence. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Bermuda Exempted Company is exempt from Bermuda income tax and is an opaque limited company. In Gibraltar, no blanket exemption applies; as a non-resident foreign company it is within Territorial_Scope and is assessable only on Gibraltar-source income and profits attributable to the Gibraltar permanent establishment. The bonded catering kitchen, if managed and controlled in Gibraltar, gives rise to a taxable PE for Gibraltar income tax purposes, but the Bermudian Tax_Exempt_Zone classification is not disturbed in Bermuda.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box election. A foreign Bermuda Exempted Company may file a written representation with the Income Tax Office to be assessed on a non-resident, PE-only territorial basis and to remain outside Gibraltar tax residence if central management and control is not exercised in Gibraltar. If central management and control is in Gibraltar, tax residence is automatic and cannot be negated by election. For US ultimate beneficial owners, the Bermuda Exempted Company is an eligible entity and may file IRS Form 8832 to elect classification as corporation, partnership, or disregarded entity; that US election does not alter Gibraltar, Bermuda, or UK tax classification. Gibraltar's hybrid mismatch rules apply only where the entity is fiscally transparent in Bermuda and creates a deduction/non-inclusion mismatch; this does not apply to a standard opaque Bermuda Exempted Company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

VAT/GST/Sales-and-Use Tax classification: Gibraltar levies no local VAT, GST, or general sales tax; indirect taxation consists of Gibraltar customs and excise duty, with export and international-aircraft catering supplies effectively zero-rated at 0%.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in Gibraltar because no VAT/GST regime exists. A bonded catering licence and customs bonded warehouse registration are mandatory from the first bonded import or stock movement, with no economic turnover threshold. The UK VAT registration threshold of £90,000 applies only if taxable supplies are separately made within the United Kingdom; zero-rated export supplies from the airport kitchen do not count toward that threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating applies where the bonded in-flight catering kitchen supplies meals, beverages, and catering consumables to a qualifying airline for consumption aboard an aircraft operating an international flight departing from the charging jurisdiction. The supply must be made under a written airline catering contract, delivered directly to the aircraft or ramp-side bonded area, supported by flight number and aircraft registration, and remain under customs/excise suspension at the time of delivery. Supplies to domestic-only flights, non-qualifying local operators, or goods released for free circulation before delivery do not qualify for zero rating or duty-free treatment.

  • Customs export declaration or bonded catering withdrawal/export entry filed with Gibraltar Customs or the applicable UK/Gibraltar border-export system
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing carriage by the international aircraft
  • Signed airline catering contract and aircraft/ramp delivery docket
  • Inward remittance certificate or equivalent bank/FX confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency within the repatriation deadline
  • Bonded warehouse movement and stock reconciliation report evidencing the meals uplifted

Gibraltar does not impose a mandatory electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance regime. The Gibraltar Customs and Excise office requires monthly electronic movement and inventory records for the bonded kitchen. Invoices may be issued electronically in PDF/EDI format without digital stamping. If UK export zero-rating is claimed, HMRC digital service channels and standard zero-rating invoice codes apply; no physical digital tax stamp is required for airline catering goods.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to retrospective audit for up to 6 years, extended to 10 years where deliberate concealment or fraud is identified. If zero-rating is disallowed or the 180-day foreign currency repatriation condition is not satisfied, the tax authority may claw back all exempted or zero-rated advantages, reassess full customs/excise duty and VAT-equivalent amounts, and charge statutory interest. Penalties may be up to 100% of the tax evaded. Fraudulent customs declarations, forged airline delivery receipts, or false export claims also expose the company, its directors, and ultimate beneficial owners to civil penalties, revocation of the bonded kitchen licence, cancellation of warehouse status, and criminal prosecution under Gibraltar customs law and applicable UK revenue statutes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility exists on both current account and capital account. Gibraltar has no exchange-control permissions or capital-transfer restrictions; GIP is freely convertible into GBP at par, and GBP notes and coins circulate alongside GIP. Profit remittances and dividend distributions to the Bermuda shareholder may be made without prior GFSC or HM Treasury approval. The only practical hurdles are documentary: audited financial statements, board resolutions, CRS/FATCA self-certifications, sanctions screening, and source-of-funds verification. The Bermuda exempted company must additionally demonstrate that the bank flows arise from a genuine aviation-catering business and not from tax-motivated pass-through.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio apply in Gibraltar. Export and trade receipts may be retained in the invoiced currency (typically GBP, EUR or USD) and need not be surrendered to any central bank or converted into GIP. For a Bermuda exempted company, invoices issued by the Bermuda entity are not Gibraltar-source export proceeds; nevertheless, funds received in Gibraltar must be supported by the underlying catering contracts and reported to the receiving bank under standard AML/CFT and FATCA obligations.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) – the statutory regulator for banking, payments and foreign exchange activity in Gibraltar; there is no separate central bank or foreign-exchange administration. Sovereign monetary anchoring is provided by the Bank of England (United Kingdom) under the Gibraltar pound's 1:1 sterling peg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for routine treasury and FX conversion; Low for structured capital escrow or high-value aviation settlement. A UK or EEA digital EMI cannot rely on an EU/EEA passport into Gibraltar post-Brexit and must hold a GFSC payment-service or e-money licence or operate through a licensed Gibraltar sponsor. Most offshore digital EMIs will reject a Bermuda exempted company lacking local substance unless a GFSC-regulated corporate service provider acts as introducer and the company maintains a physical registered office in Gibraltar.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% flat Gibraltar corporate income tax rate applied to chargeable profits attributable to the Gibraltar permanent establishment (PE); profits of the Bermuda Exempted Company not attributable to the Gibraltar PE remain outside the Gibraltar tax charge and are exempt at 0% in Bermuda under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 (exemption certificate valid to 2050).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividend distributions or on profit remittances from a Gibraltar PE to its foreign head office; dividends paid by the Bermuda exempted company to its shareholders are likewise free of Bermuda and Gibraltar tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local currency: Gibraltar Pound (GIP, pegged 1:1 to GBP). Local File is mandatory where the aggregate value of controlled cross-border transactions exceeds GIP/EUR 8.5 million in the accounting period, or where controlled intangible-related transactions exceed GIP/EUR 1 million; a Master File is required where the worldwide group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 750 million; Country-by-Country reporting applies at the same EUR 750 million threshold. All in-scope taxpayers must complete the annual Transfer Pricing Certificate within the Gibraltar Company Tax Return regardless of threshold.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's economic substance requirements (implemented under the Income Tax Act 2010 and the Economic Substance Regulations following the EU Code of Conduct Group process) apply to relevant activities including headquarters, distribution and service centres and IP holding. The inflight catering kitchen operates as an active operational business with local premises, staff, equipment and decision-making in Gibraltar, thereby satisfying substance organically. The Bermuda exempted company must avoid classification as an IP-holding or pure holding vehicle: any proprietary recipes, airline brand licences or logistics software should be held under commercially justified arrangements with board oversight in Bermuda, not in Gibraltar. Non-compliance with the substance notification and documentation rules triggers an initial penalty of GIP 10,000, escalating penalties for continued default, and ultimately strike-off of the Gibraltar registration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil assessment with tax-geared penalties of up to 100% of the undercharged tax for deliberate concealment, plus interest; criminal prosecution under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 carrying an unlimited fine and imprisonment of up to 7 years; seizure of bonded goods and revocation of airside catering licences.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause, connive in, or consent to the filing of a false return are personally liable for penalties; criminal conviction triggers director disqualification for up to 15 years, personal restitution orders for the tax loss, and confiscation proceedings under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment re-attributing profits to the Gibraltar PE at 12.5%, late-payment interest, negligence and deliberate-inaccuracy penalties up to 100% of the adjustment; automatic exchange of Country-by-Country and CRS information with Bermuda and UK authorities; potential UK CFC attribution.

Director personal liability: Directors approving non-arm's length pricing without contemporaneous benchmarking expose themselves to negligence determinations and personal penalty orders; in egregious cases, civil liability for fraudulent trading under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 imposes a personal contribution order for the company's unpaid tax liabilities.
Shell Company and Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the company as Gibraltar tax-resident under the management-and-control test, assessment on worldwide profits at 12.5%, economic substance penalties starting at GIP 10,000, strike-off of the Gibraltar branch, and adverse reporting to the Bermuda Registrar and UK HMRC.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for the company's tax where the shell entity cannot pay, under trust-recovery provisions; court-ordered travel bans and financial restriction orders can be imposed in enforcement proceedings, and disqualification orders of up to 15 years apply for acting as a de facto director of a sham entity.
AML Deficiencies and Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Confiscation orders and criminal fines under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar); fines up to GIP 10 million for AML breaches by supervised firms; suspension or revocation of the airport and customs bonded licences; criminal liability for money laundering carrying up to 14 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors, senior officers and the Money Laundering Reporting Officer face personal criminal liability for money laundering, fraudulent trading, and failure to submit SARs; personal confiscation orders may attach to tainted assets, and courts may impose travel bans and passport surrender pending investigation.
