Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, HM Government of Gibraltar — Income Tax Office (Gibraltar e-Tax portal: tax.gov.gi). Overseas-company registration is administered by the Gibraltar Companies Registry (part of the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission); customs and Purchase Tax are administered by HM Customs Gibraltar, Customs and Excise Division.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Overseas company registration: within one month of establishing a place of business in Gibraltar, the Bermuda Exempted Company must deliver to the Registrar of Companies a certified copy of its Bermuda certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, a list of directors and secretary, and particulars of a Gibraltar-resident authorised person empowered to accept service of process; a Gibraltar registered office address is mandatory. Step 2 — Income tax registration: file a Notice of Commencement of Business with the Income Tax Office, elect a financial year-end (typically 31 December or 31 March), and obtain the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (statutory TIN), which consolidates corporation tax, payroll tax and social insurance. Step 3 — Employer registration: if the Gibraltar branch employs staff, register for Payroll Tax and Social Insurance deductions. Step 4 — Customs registration: a Customs Trader Code is required only if the company imports or manufactures excise goods; no VAT ID is issued because Gibraltar does not levy VAT. Step 5 — Keep the Bermuda Tax Assurance Certificate (Economic Substance Act 2018) on file to substantiate the entity's exempt status in Bermuda and to support the territorial-source analysis in Gibraltar.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — the Bermuda Exempted Company is a non-resident corporation for Gibraltar purposes and is chargeable to Gibraltar corporation tax only on the profits of a Gibraltar permanent establishment/branch and on Gibraltar-source income. It is not a pass-through; it is taxed at entity level on branch profits at the Gibraltar corporate rates (10% up to £350,000; 19% on £350,000–£500,000; 26% above £500,000). The entity simultaneously retains Tax-Exempt status in Bermuda (no Bermuda income, capital gains or distribution tax).

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a 'check-the-box' or hybrid pass-through election. Available statutory elections include: (1) election of a chargeability/accounting date other than the default under the Income Tax Act 2010; (2) group relief and consortium relief surrender/claim elections between the Gibraltar branch and 75%-related Gibraltar-resident companies; (3) an election to compute branch taxable profits on an arm's-length attributable basis rather than apportioning worldwide profits; (4) tonnage-tax election for qualifying ship operators (not relevant to lobbying activities); and (5) application of the limited U.K.-Gibraltar arrangement for relief from double taxation on dual-source income. Cross-border hybrid mismatches involving the Bermuda and Gibraltar tax treatments must be documented under Gibraltar's mandatory disclosure rules (DAC6-style regime).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Purchase Tax (limited excise-style tax) with customs import/export duties — no VAT and no GST. The Purchase Tax regime applies to a closed list of goods delivered or consumed in Gibraltar (hydrocarbon oil, alcohol, tobacco, etc.); services, including government-affairs and lobbying advisory services, are entirely outside Gibraltar's indirect tax charge.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST registration threshold exists in Gibraltar. Purchase Tax registration (Customs Trader Code) is required for any entity importing or manufacturing listed excise goods regardless of value. For income tax, there is no de minimis threshold: an overseas company carrying on any trade in Gibraltar must register and file with the Income Tax Office, and the Commissioner may assess on an estimated minimum-profit basis where no return is filed. All thresholds and consequences are denominated in pounds sterling (GBP).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in VAT terms because Gibraltar has no VAT. Exports of goods from Gibraltar are outside the charge to Purchase Tax, and export of services (trade-policy analysis and lobbying) is outside Gibraltar's indirect tax net entirely. For corporate income tax, the branch may exclude from Gibraltar charge profits attributable to services performed and managed outside Gibraltar under the territorial source rules, provided the contract, performance records, personnel location, bank receipt evidence and 'economic employer' analysis demonstrate a foreign source. Refund relief is limited to customs duty relief/drawback for qualifying re-exported goods through HM Customs Gibraltar; there is no input-VAT refund mechanism.

None. Gibraltar imposes no e-invoicing mandate, no real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction controls (CTC), and no digital stamping. Corporate, payroll and social-insurance returns are filed electronically through the Government of Gibraltar e-Tax portal; customs declarations are submitted through HM Customs Gibraltar's electronic declaration platform. Conventional paper or electronic invoices with standard commercial particulars are accepted for tax record purposes.

HM Customs Gibraltar may seize goods and impose forfeiture plus monetary penalties (up to the value of the goods) and criminal sanctions for false export declarations. Under the Income Tax Act 2010, incorrect or fraudulent returns expose the taxpayer to an extended-period assessment, statutory interest, and a penalty of up to 100% of the tax undercharged; late or non-registration carries continuing monthly penalties. Where the Bermuda exempted structure is used to divert Gibraltar-source lobbying fees offshore (e.g., invoicing through Bermuda without substance), the Commissioner will re-attribute the income to the Gibraltar branch on a territorial-source restatement, claw back the tax advantage, impose the 100% penalty, and may initiate criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion; DAC6-style mandatory disclosure obligations apply to the cross-border arrangement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The GIP is fully convertible into GBP at par, and Gibraltar imposes no exchange-control restrictions on current-account services or capital-account movements. Retainer fees generated from the ISIC 7020 government-affairs advisory trade may lawfully be collected in GIP, GBP, EUR or USD, freely converted, reinvested or repatriated without FX licensing. Profit remittances and dividend distributions from the Bermuda Exempted Company are not subject to Gibraltar withholding tax, nor are they chargeable to Bermuda tax, provided the company holds a current Bermuda Tax Exemption Certificate and files annual attestations. Practical conversion frictions are limited to documentary hurdles: banks will demand CRS/FATCA self-certifications, a declaration that the company is neither managed-and-controlled in Bermuda nor tax-resident there, and, because of the lobbying nexus, a negative PEP and sanctions certification prior to any sizeable capital conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Gibraltar enforces no export-proceeds surrender regime and prescribes neither a mandatory conversion ratio nor a statutory repatriation timeline for service-export receipts. Proceeds from cross-border advisory retainers may be held indefinitely in the settlement currency, deployed intra-group via intercompany loan or equity, or remitted to ultimate shareholders, subject only to standard AML transaction monitoring and the six-year record-retention obligations arising under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar).

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar maintains no independent central bank. The Government of Gibraltar, under the Currency Notes Act 1934, issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at legal parity of 1:1 with the British pound. Prudential supervision, AML/CFT oversight and conduct regulation over all authorised banks, foreign-exchange dealers and payment institutions reside with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), which aligns its policy with the UK Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018 and OFSI guidance. Monetary conditions are anchored de facto by the Bank of England given the currency peg, making the GFSC the operative treasury gatekeeper for licensing, sanctions screening and capital-movement verification.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Limited viability (Low-to-Medium). UK FCA-authorised cross-border EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Payoneer and iCard may be used only for ancillary treasury functions such as low-value FX conversion and global payout to sub-consultants. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital must arrive by SWIFT MT103 from a regulated credit institution in the remitter's jurisdiction. Shareholder capital injections must use IMF BPM6 purpose code D.3 (Direct Investment - Equity Capital) and reference the governing corporate resolution; cross-border retainer income must be coded as business and management consulting and public-relations services under the same BPM6 classification. On receipt, the Gibraltar bank will screen the wire against OFSI, EU and UN consolidated sanctions lists, verify the payee's Legal Entity Identifier and corroborate the source of funds for every credit above the equivalent of EUR 10,000. GIP-denominated credits settle at par with GBP; EUR and USD positions clear through the bank's correspondent network and are swept under treasury policy into a segregated money-market or custody account, with all receipts reconciled monthly and reported under the CRS/FATCA cross-border framework inherited from the UK.The primary outbound rail is SWIFT MT103 for international consultant retainers, subscription data services and subcontractor disbursements, supplemented by SWIFT MT202 COV where correspondent-cover separation is required. For Eurozone suppliers the treasury should use SEPA Credit Transfers with IBAN validation; for UK and Gibraltar domestic obligations, GBP Faster Payments and CHAPS are the standard rails. Commercial letters of credit are not appropriate for advisory service disbursements and should be limited to contingency procurement of event infrastructure and multilingual broadcasting, issued on ICC UCP 600 terms and supported by performance guarantees under ICC URDG 758. Treasury governance should impose dual-signature authority above material-value thresholds, maintain a sole multi-currency concentration account at the GFSC-licensed bank, and run daily automated sweeping to operating sub-accounts to preserve liquidity and auditability.Deliverable FX forwards with tenors up to 24 months executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a GFSC-licensed investment firm or authorised credit institution.Cross-currency swaps (EUR/USD/GBP/CHF) for structural mitigation of multi-currency retainer liabilities and intercompany loan servicing.Plain-vanilla European-style FX options, both calls and puts, used to set budget floors on EUR/GBP and USD/EUR exposures.FX swaps (tom-next and spot-next) for rolling intraday and working-capital liquidity management across GIP/GBP, EUR and USD.Non-deliverable forwards restricted strictly to illiquid emerging-market currencies (for example offshore CNY, INR or NGN) where an onshore physical forward cannot be executed; GIP/GBP/EUR/USD are fully deliverable and do not require NDF pricing.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Limited viability (Low-to-Medium). UK FCA-authorised cross-border EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Payoneer and iCard may be used only for ancillary treasury functions such as low-value FX conversion and global payout to sub-consultants. They are not accepted by the Gibraltar statutory licensing framework as the principal operational bank, and most reputable EMIs decline onboarding of a Bermuda Exempted Company with non-resident ownership once the declared purpose is government-affairs lobbying, owing to political-exposure risk, correspondent-bank de-risking and the tax-exempt offshore status of the applicant. A GFSC-licensed commercial bank is therefore mandatory for the primary treasury account, the capital escrow arrangement and the licence-required settlement rail.
