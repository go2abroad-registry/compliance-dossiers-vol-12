Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), Ministry of Fiscal Affairs; portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation. Corporate registry: Gibraltar Companies House (Registrar of Companies). Customs/indirect-tax authority: HM Customs & Excise Department, Gibraltar, https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/customs. For Bermuda domestic tax-exempt status: Bermuda Registrar of Companies and the Ministry of Finance (Economic Substance filings), portal: https://www.bermudabusinessregistry.com.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Engage a Gibraltar registered agent, secure a registered office, and file the prescribed Foreign Company Registration application with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 2014 within one month of establishing a place of business, thereby obtaining a Gibraltar Company Number. Step 2: Within 30 days of commencing a tax presence, file the Income Tax Office Company Registration Application (prescribed form, accompanied by the Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, constitutional documents, shareholder/director registers and registered office consent) to procure the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (GTRN), which is the sole statutory corporate tax identifier. Step 5: No separate VAT/TIN registration is required or available because neither Gibraltar nor Bermuda operates VAT/GST; the GTRN and Bermuda Company Number cover national tax identity purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (the Bermuda Exempted Company is statutorily tax-exempt in Bermuda under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 until 2035 and has no Bermuda taxable income. For Gibraltar source exposure, the entity is a non-resident foreign company subject to Entity_Level_Taxation in respect only of profits attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment, at the corporate rate of 10% on assessable profit up to £10 million and 12.5% above £10 million; profits from foreign clients without a Gibraltar PE fall within Territorial_Scope exclusion. The entity is not Pass_Through_Eligible; it remains a taxable legal person in both Bermuda and Gibraltar).

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not provide a US-style check-the-box or fiscal-transparency election. Available elections/treatments: (1) election to be assessed under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 as a non-resident company relying on the UK-Gibraltar Double Taxation Arrangement 1952 where management and control remains outside Gibraltar; (2) historically Qualifying Company status (closed to new entrants since 31 December 2010; grandfathering only, of no value to a new PropTech SaaS); (3) election for economic-substance compliance location: the company may elect to attribute the relevant activity to the Gibraltar PE and file the Bermuda Economic Substance Declaration reflecting substance performed in Gibraltar; (4) hybrid mismatch election: none beneficial — Gibraltar implements the OECD hybrid-mismatch rules, disallowing double-deduction structures, and the Sail-backed Bermuda entity cannot make a transparency election for Gibraltar or UK tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Nil – Gibraltar does not operate a VAT or GST. Gibraltar indirect taxation is limited to customs import duties, excise duties on alcohol/tobacco/fuel, and international services no consumption tax. Bermuda equally imposes no VAT, GST, or general sales tax on goods or SaaS revenues. If SaaS is supplied to UK non-business consumers, UK VAT at the 20% standard rate arises under the UK VAT (Place of Supply of Services) Rules 1992 and Part 1 of Schedule 3A Value Added Tax Act 1994, because Gibraltar is treated as a non-UK territory for VAT; compliance is through the HMRC VAT Non-Union Mini One Stop Shop portal for digital services.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A – No indirect-tax registration threshold applies in Gibraltar or Bermuda because no VAT/GST regime exists; no turnover trigger for consumption tax registration is prescribed by law. For UK VAT, a mandatory registration threshold of £85,000 (GBP) of UK taxable supplies of digital services in a rolling 12-month period applies, and a simplified VAT on cross-border e-services registration may be used for supplies below that limit.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

No export zero-rating claim is available or necessary because Gibraltar and Bermuda do not impose VAT/GST on exports; the pure SaaS cloud subscription to overseas customers is outside the scope of any Gibraltar indirect tax and generates no negative VAT or refund right. For UK VAT purposes, where the SaaS is supplied from Gibraltar to non-UK overseas customers, the supply is 'outside the scope' rather than zero-rated and no refund of input tax is attributable. Physical IoT hardware/devices occasionally deployed with the PropTech platform (sensors, gateways) are exported under the UK-Gibraltar Customs Trade Partnership and may be exported free of export charges provided they are not in free circulation in the UK; documentary proof of export is required only for customs-duty purposes and does not create any VAT refund mechanism in Gibraltar.

  • UK-Gibraltar Customs Trade Partnership electronic Export Declaration (Exports System via Customs Declaration Service CDS) – required only where tangible IoT hardware is physically exported from Gibraltar
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing movement of any tangible goods; for pure SaaS, a software delivery certificate (activation log and server access record, issued through API) replaces the transport document as the export substantiator
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103/MT950 bank statement from the Gibraltar-licensed bank or UK EMI evidencing receipt of export consideration in hard currency within the 180-day repatriation benchmark

Gibraltar imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, digital stamping, or e-invoicing validation mandate for B2B or B2C SaaS invoices; commercial invoices suffice, preserved for six years. Bermuda likewise has no e-invoicing clearance system. If the Gibraltar entity elects voluntary UK VAT registration to serve UK B2C customers, it must file quarterly Making Tax Digital (MTD) VAT returns through HMRC-compatible software using a functional compatible bridging API to https://www.tax.service.gov.uk, but it does not require HMRC pre-clearance stamping of individual e-invoices, and only standard MTD digital record-keeping applies.

Although Gibraltar lacks a zero-rating refund mechanism to be falsely claimed, mis-invoicing foreign customers to avoid UK VAT where the supply is UK-consumed triggers HMRC penalties under VATA 1994 Schedule 24 of up to 100% of evaded VAT plus interest, extending to criminal conviction for cheating the public revenue. Gibraltar Income Tax Office audit powers permit assessments within six years, and up to twenty-one years on the basis of fraud or deliberate neglect; civil penalties of up to 100% of the undercharged tax, plus fines up to £10,000 for non-production of documents, are statutorily available. Customs misdeclarations of tangible exports attract forfeiture of the goods, an amount equal to the unpaid customs duty, a civil penalty of up to 100% of duty, and retrospective withdrawal of export facilitation within four years; fraudulent export claims constitute a customs crime triable before the Supreme Court of Gibraltar with imprisonment of up to seven years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

UK and Gibraltar maintain unrestricted current- and capital-account convertibility with no exchange control legislation. GIP is freely convertible only via licensed banks at the fixed Sterling parity. Bermuda operates the Exchange Control Act 1972; the Exempted Company must obtain and maintain BMA 'Non-Resident' designation to be treated outside the exchange-control net, eliminating prior-permission requirements for inbound equity, intercompany loans, and profit repatriation. No Gibraltar withholding tax applies to dividends or interest remitted to the Bermuda parent, and the Bermuda exempt company benefits from full tax-exempt status. SaaS subscription receivables may be retained in any currency without conversion obligation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender, conversion ratio, or statutory retention timeline applies to export receipts or SaaS revenue in Gibraltar or the United Kingdom. For Bermuda, the Exchange Control Regulations historically require resident entities to offer foreign-currency export proceeds to an authorized dealer within six months of receipt; however, a BMA-designated non-resident Exempted Company is wholly exempt from such offer-for-sale and conversion duties. The PropTech platform may retain and net USD, GBP, and EUR treasury balances freely.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary anchor for Stering area; Gibraltar Pound issued by the Government of Gibraltar at 1:1 GBP peg); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the statutory prudential and AML/CFT supervisor for licensed banks, EMIs, and payment institutions; Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) as the exchange-control authority for the Bermuda Exempted Company; HMRC/FCA rules apply to any UK-resident payment corridors.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (Bermuda Exempted Company statutory rate); 12.5% Gibraltar territorial corporate tax rate applies only if the company is Gibraltar-resident or earns Gibraltar-source trading profit.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%; Gibraltar does not levy dividend withholding tax and Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by an Exempted Company.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GBP 10,000,000 group turnover for Master File; GBP 1,000,000 aggregated related-party transactions for Local File; all controlled transactions are subject to the arm's length principle regardless of threshold.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's Economic Substance Regulations and Bermuda's Economic Substance Act 2018 require adequate substance for entities conducting relevant activities. A PropTech real estate SaaS platform deriving IP royalties or financing income in a no-tax environment must demonstrate local decision-making, qualified staff, and physical presence. Pure equity holding and low-tier IP holding are subject to reduced substance requirements, but a sham or letterbox arrangement cannot rely on the 0% rate and will be recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 7 years for tax evasion; tax-geared penalty up to 200% of evaded tax; daily default fines of GBP 300; asset freezing and confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate are personally liable for evaded tax and penalties; the corporate veil will be pierced; travel bans and passport seizure may be imposed; civil restitution orders require repayment of hidden profits; director disqualification up to 15 years and custodial sentence are available.
Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation as UK tax resident under CTA 2009 s.14 and UK corporation tax at 19%; CFC charge and GAAR counteraction; Gibraltar registry strike-off for non-compliance; criminal fine up to GBP 5,000 for failure to maintain accounting records.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes where the company is a sham; liability for fraudulent trading under the Companies Act; civil recovery from directors' personal assets; disqualification and imprisonment up to 10 years for continuing to trade abusively after strike-off.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Missing Documentation

Statutory sanction: Profits adjusted to arm's length; additional tax and interest at 8%; penalty up to 100% of the additional tax; documentation penalties up to GBP 30,000; UK HMRC may allocate profits under Part 4 TIOPA and impose a penalty up to 100% for careless or deliberate transfer pricing error.

Director personal liability: A director who signs false transfer pricing declarations faces a personal tax-geared penalty and liability for 50% of the underpaid tax; financing deductions may be denied; director disqualification and criminal prosecution for knowingly misleading tax authorities.
AML / CDD Breach and Unauthorised Payment Activity

Statutory sanction: Unlimited financial penalties under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act and AML Regulations 2019; GFSC penalty up to GBP 2 million or 10% of turnover; criminal money laundering conviction with imprisonment up to 14 years; freezing orders and confiscation of assets; EU/UK payment services regulatory fines if unauthorized activities fall within FSMA 2000.

Director personal liability: Directors and the AML/MLRO Officer are personally liable for failure to file suspicious activity reports; civil restitution of misappropriated client funds; travel bans during investigation; director disqualification and theft or fraud charges where deposits are diverted for personal benefit.
