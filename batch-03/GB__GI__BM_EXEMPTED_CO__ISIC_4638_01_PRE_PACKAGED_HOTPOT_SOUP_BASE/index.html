Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office (Income Tax Department), HM Government of Gibraltar, operated under the Commissioner of Income Tax; official portal and e-filing gateway at https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation. Customs and indirect-tax competence is vested in HM Customs of Gibraltar (Customs Division, Gibraltar), which administers import/export declarations and purchase-tax/customs duties.

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company (a non-resident entity for Gibraltar tax purposes) must obtain a Gibraltar Taxpayer Reference Number (TRN) from the Income Tax Office before commencing any Gibraltar-source trade. Procedure: (i) if establishing a branch or registered office, register as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry; (ii) submit the standard taxpayer-application form together with the certified Bermuda certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents (memorandum and articles of association), evidence of the registered agent and directors in Bermuda, and a written description of the export-distribution trade; (iii) obtain a HM Customs trader reference (importer/exporter code) for all shipments; (iv) file annual corporate tax returns, including nil returns, electronically through the self-assessment gateway. Where the Bermuda entity derives no Gibraltar-source profits, the return confirms a zero tax charge and preserves the administrative good standing of the TRN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box classification and no hybrid-transparency election; a non-resident legal entity is classified according to its constitutive law. The Bermuda Exempted Company may file the relevant undertaking and obtain a Certificate of Exemption (tax-assurance letter) from the Bermuda Minister of Finance under sections 80-85 of the Exempted Companies Act 1981, confirming statutory exemption from all Bermuda income and capital taxes, while remaining outside Gibraltar taxation on non-Gibraltar-source profits. Within Gibraltar, the only available corporate elections are group-loss surrender among 75% group companies under the Income Tax (Groups) provisions and transitional capital-allowance elections; no hybrid-mismatch, pass-through, or 'transparent entity' election is applicable to a non-resident corporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Purchase Tax / Customs Duties (transactional import-duty regime under the Customs Ordinance; no general VAT or GST is in force as Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT territory; a UK-aligned VAT regime is legislatively contemplated but not yet operational).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold in Gibraltar pounds (GIP) applies. Purchase tax is a transactional import duty, so registration is mandatory for every importer/exporter with HM Customs regardless of turnover. Income-tax registration is triggered by commencement of a Gibraltar trade rather than by any monetary threshold; the Bermuda company's offshore re-export trade (goods not sold, landed, or delivered within Gibraltar) creates only an administrative nil-return obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export transactions are outside the scope of Gibraltar Purchase Tax, and no duty charge arises if: (i) the pre-packaged spicy tallow hotpot soup base bricks physically leave Gibraltar for a destination outside Gibraltar (EU, UK, or third country); (ii) the exporter holds a valid HM Customs trader reference and files an electronic export declaration with MRN within 90 days of the related import entry; (iii) the goods are classified under HS 2103.90 (sauces, preparations for sauces, mixed condiments) or under HS 1601/1602 where meat/tallow content predominates, with the appropriate food-safety and animal-origin documentation; and (iv) arm's-length pricing is maintained so that no diversion to domestic consumption is imputed. Where the goods transit or are re-exported from customs warehousing, the purchase tax is suspended, and foodstuff tariff lines generally carry a 0% charge on any local import clearance, thereby eliminating refund exposure.

  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill evidencing date of shipment

Gibraltar does not currently impose real-time continuous transaction controls, fiscal stamping, API-based clearance, or mandatory e-invoicing for B2B sales. All customs import/export entries must be filed electronically through the Gibraltar/HM Customs declaration environment (SAD/electronic data interchange), with each consignment assigned a Movement Reference Number (MRN) before release. Goods routed through the Freeport or under customs warehousing are subject to a fully electronic authorization and duty-suspension procedure. Upon entry into force of the planned UK-aligned VAT regime, electronic invoicing and structured digital reporting are expected to follow UK Making Tax Digital standards.

HM Customs of Gibraltar may conduct post-clearance audits for up to three years from the date of export declaration, with no time limit in cases of fraud or negligence. False or fraudulent export declarations render the goods liable to forfeiture and expose the exporter to customs penalties of up to three times the value of the goods and/or imprisonment, together with withdrawal of the trader reference and mandatory guarantees for future shipments. On the tax side, the Income Tax Office may reopen assessments within six years of the relevant chargeable period (unlimited in fraud or negligent-misstatement cases), impose civil penalties of up to 100% of the tax lost plus statutory interest, and retrospectively claw back any improperly claimed zero-rating or refund advantage, with penal interest continuing from the date of the original refund.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Fully liberalised. The GIP is convertible at par to GBP without restriction, and intra-system transfers between GIP and GBP are treated as domestic clearing. There is no exchange-control statute in force: the UK Exchange Control Act 1947 was repealed in 1979, and Gibraltar has enacted no successor control regime. Both current-account flows (trade receivables, supplier payments, freight) and capital-account flows (shareholder equity contributions, intercompany loans, dividend and profit remittances to the Bermuda parent) are unrestricted and require no GFSC or BoE prior approval. Profit repatriation is free of any Gibraltar withholding tax; the only bank-side requirement is a correctly purpose-coded SWIFT instruction and standard corporate authorisation documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio and no repatriation-or-holding deadline for export proceeds derived from the export distribution of pre-packaged hotpot soup base. Receipts may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, GBP or other freely convertible currencies. The sole compliance obligation is the record-keeping and suspicious-activity-reporting duty under the Financial Services (Money Laundering) Act 2015 and the AML Regulations 2019, which requires the retention of ownership-chain and transaction evidence for a minimum of five years after the last trade settlement.

Central bank supervision: Monetary sovereignty rests with the Bank of England (BoE), which anchors the British pound sterling; the Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Currency) issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) licenses and supervises all banking, investment-instrument and foreign-exchange service providers under the Financial Services Act 2019 and additionally functions as the financial-intelligence authority for AML/CTF. For a Bermuda Exempted Company operating on a UK/Gibraltar cross-border distribution platform, the operative prudential and conduct regulators are the GFSC for the licensed bank relationship and the BoE/Prudential Regulation Authority only by virtue of the correspondent UK clearing chain.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must be routed via a bank-to-bank SWIFT MT103 from the Bermuda shareholder's principal bank to the Gibraltar operational account. The wire must carry purpose code 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION' (BoP item A1, shares and other equity) or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN - TRADE WORKING CAPITAL' (BoP item D3, intercompany lending), together with the beneficiary account name, the Gibraltar customer reference and the reason phrase 'capital injection for export distribution of pre-packed spicy tallow hotpot soup base (ISIC 4638)'. The receiving bank screens against OFAC, EU and UN consolidated sanctions lists; any transfer exceeding EUR 10,000 is captured under the cross-border payment reporting provisions of the AML Regulations 2019. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No general statutory filing threshold exists in Gibraltar; however, the Gibraltar Income Tax (Transfer Pricing) Rules require contemporaneous Local File documentation when the aggregate value of controlled transactions of the Gibraltar PE exceeds GIP 1,000,000 per annum, and a Master File when the multinational group consolidated revenue exceeds GIP 50,000,000. In Bermuda, the Exempted Company must maintain transfer pricing substance records to support the nil-tax certificate if it applies for a certificate of exemption.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar ESR applies: the company must demonstrate core income-generating activities (warehousing, logistics, contract negotiation, invoicing for hotpot base export distribution) are performed, directed, or managed in Gibraltar with adequate premises, employees, and expenditure. Bermuda ESR likewise requires the Exempted Company to file an annual economic substance declaration and, if not conducted in Bermuda, to demonstrate a lower-level substance filing in Bermuda. Failure to satisfy ESR permits the tax authority to de-emphasise the zero-rate status and recharacterise the substance of the structure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Tax-geared penalty up to 100% of the understated tax, daily surcharge of GIP 100 for continued default, statutory interest, and criminal conviction with fines at the discretion of the Gibraltar court.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the unpaid tax where they signed or caused the fraudulent return; criminal conviction may result in imprisonment up to 7 years, travel bans, publication of director disqualification, and a personal pecuniary penalty.
Shell Company and Abuse of Exempted Status

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may disregard the corporate form, re-determine Gibraltar tax residence on the basis of central management and control, assess 12.5% on worldwide trading profits, impose a penalty of 150% of the additional tax, and strike the company off the Gibraltar register.

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified from acting as directors for up to 15 years, held personally liable for the company's assessed taxes where assets have been dissipated, and subject to a travel restriction order pending settlement of the liabilities.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Mandatory transfer pricing adjustment, additional corporation tax at 12.5%, interest at the statutory rate, and a tax-geared penalty of 100% of the understated tax; the taxpayer carries the burden of disproving deliberate undervaluation.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the mispricing with knowledge are jointly and severally liable for the adjustment; persistent conduct triggers personal fines of up to GIP 100,000 and disqualification for insolvency or regulatory breaches.
AML and Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty up to GIP 250,000 and daily default penalties of up to GIP 5,000 per day; the company may be compulsorily struck off; the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit may impose additional regulatory sanctions under the AML framework.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines up to GIP 200,000, disgorgement of profits, a prohibition order restricting future directorships, and exposure to money-laundering charges where the false substance declaration facilitated the movement of criminal property.
