Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar Income Tax Office (official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/; registration and forms via https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax). For the Bermuda exempted company's underlying incorporation and tax exemption certificate, the competent authority is the Bermuda Registrar of Companies and the Bermuda Ministry of Finance.

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda exempted company does not obtain a Bermuda TIN for income tax because Bermuda levies no income, capital gains, or withholding tax. To the extent the entity becomes tax resident or establishes a permanent establishment in Gibraltar, it must submit a Gibraltar Taxpayer Registration Application (Form TRN) to the Income Tax Office within 30 days of commencing taxable activities, accompanied by certified constitutional documents, board resolutions, and evidence of management and control. No VAT-registration procedure applies because Gibraltar has no VAT. For CRS/FATCA compliance, the fund must obtain a Global Intermediary Identification Number (GIIN) through the U.S. IRS FATCA portal and register with the Gibraltar Competent Authority for automatic exchange of information.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available under Bermuda or Gibraltar law for an exempted company; the entity is a per se non-transparent corporation. The fund may make an irrevocable election to be treated as an alternative investment fund under the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission, but this does not alter tax classification. If the vehicle instead used a Gibraltar LLP, an elective transparent classification would be available under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act. UK hybrid mismatch rules (TIOPA 2010 Part 6TA) apply to UK-resident investors, requiring the entity to be treated as opaque for UK purposes unless an equivalent transparency election is available under UK law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax; Gibraltar levies only customs and excise duties and stamp duty; Bermuda levies no VAT but applies customs duties and payroll tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no statutory turnover threshold exists because Gibraltar and Bermuda do not operate VAT/GST regimes. Any physical importation into Gibraltar requires customs registration under the Customs Ordinance without reference to economic turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — since Gibraltar and Bermuda do not impose VAT/GST, export zero-rating is absent. Services exported from Gibraltar are outside the scope of any indirect tax, and digital asset transactions involving crypto-assets are generally not subject to VAT in Gibraltar; no zero-rating claim or refund mechanism is available.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / electronic customs export application through the Gibraltar Customs system)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement out of Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank FX confirmation evidencing repatriation of sales proceeds

No real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate exists in either Gibraltar or Bermuda. Invoices must be retained for at least 6 years, and the Gibraltar Income Tax Office may require digital records in proprietary file formats; there is no API clearance or digital stamping requirement.

Although no VAT zero-rating clawback exists, fraudulent customs declarations or misrepresentation of export shipments attract fines under Gibraltar's Customs Ordinance and the UK's Customs (Amendment) Regulations, up to the greater of £3,000 or 200% of the duty evaded, plus retrospective assessment and seizure of goods. In Bermuda, false export declarations are penalised with a fine up to 3 times the value of the goods and revocation of any customs registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar applies a fully liberalised exchange-control regime inherited from the UK Exchange Control (Repeal) Act 1979. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible without currency surrender, licensing, or conversion restrictions in Gibraltarian or United Kingdom statute. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are permitted without statutory withholding or positive convertibility clearance; however, payments can be rejected or temporally blocked where their source of funds or underlying crypto flows fail enhanced due diligence under the Criminal Justice Act 2015 (Gibraltar), Sanctions Orders, or GFSC advisory anti-money laundering and countering-the-financing-of-terrorism rules. Dividend and redemption distributions must be accompanied by audited financial statements, corporate beneficial-ownership declarations, and FATCA/CRS confirmation to clear institutional shotgun and correspondent bank controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules in Gibraltar or the United Kingdom. For a Gibraltar-domiciled crypto venture fund, receipts arising from the sale of digital assets, exchange execution, yield generation, and OTC liquidation are considered export proceeds and may be retained indefinitely in any currency without mandatory conversion. No mandatory timeline for surrender exists, and the fund may lawfully hold USD, EUR, GBP, USDC, or other stablecoin balances directly with licensed counterparties. Nonetheless, all receipts must be traceable via audited on-chain reconciliations for GFSC and bank AML compliance, and any repatriation to a foreign parent Bermuda exempted company must satisfy the parent company's own source-of-funds and tax reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (United Kingdom central bank) acting as the monetary authority for Gibraltar as a British Overseas Territory, with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) serving as the local licensing, regulatory, and foreign-exchange supervisory body for deposit-taking institutions and crypto-asset firms.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium. Post-Brexit, EEA electronic-money institutions are not provided passporting rights into Gibraltar; any EMI soliciting payments from, or holding accounts for, a Gibraltar entity must hold a GFSC Electronic Money Institution licence under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2015. Mainstream offshore digital EMIs (Revolut, Wise, Monese, Nimbl, or similar) maintain near-universal prohibited-business-product clauses against crypto hedge funds, multi-party treasury settlement, or digital asset clearance; hence seamless onboarding with such platforms is blocked. A dedicated GFSC-licensed local EMI or a white-label Gibraltar-licensed payments partner could potentially provide API-driven fixture rails after formal regulatory concurrence, but should be considered only as a supplementary convenience layer, never as the primary liquidity clearing source.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound investor capital is wired via SWIFT MT103 from investor traditional bank account(s) directly to the Gibraltar bank’s segregated Capital Escrow Account, using high-precision purpose code 6011 — Investment in Financial Assets, with a concise beneficiary reference “INVESTMENT CONTRIBUTION — DIGITAL VAULT SUBSCRIPTION” and mandatory inclusion of the fund-level LEI, the investor’s individual or entity passport/ID/LLP number, and a copy of the full subscription agreement. Pre-verified identification of the wire remitter is enforced by the receiving GFSC bank before funds are converted to the operational clearing account. This wire must not be initiated directly from another crypto exchange or an EMI without a mirroring ownership proof, as the CCAs and enhanced due diligence will likely deem such an incoming transfer unreconcilable. For stablecoin parallel subscriptions, inbound is limited to Fireblocks-verified whitelisted addresses with converted fiat confirmation from licensed GFSC OTC partner, accompanied by a well-formed source-of-wealth file.Fiat disbursements for exchanges, custodians, and OTC desks settle over SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 6021 (Portfolio Investment Payment) and expected beneficiary account field containing the external exchange corporate merchant name and LEI, where available. Payment orders are queued only after a three-party authorisation workflow and transaction attestation from the fund’s compliance officer. Digital asset disbursements for redemptions and trade settlement use Fireblocks API vaults with threshold multi-sig policy to approved institutional exchange APIs (Coinbase Prime, Bitstamp Institutional, Kraken, and Paxos-produced settlement rails) and to licensed OTC desk addresses. Commercial documentary letters of credit (Commercial L/C) remain lawful and viable for dual-natured token purchases, structured trade finance contracts involving carbon credits, or hybrid physical-plus-digital exchange; in such transactions the issuing bank requires full inspection certificate, bearing proof-of-title, insurer’s note, and approved ICSTA arbitration clause. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Expect contemporaneous Master File and Local File documentation where the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750 million (OECD/G20 Inclusive Framework threshold) or where the Gibraltar entity's cross-border related-party transactions exceed GBP 1 million (or local currency equivalent) in any category; transactions below these thresholds require proportionate, simplified arm's length records substantiating pricing of management fees, treasury arrangements, and crypto-asset transactions between the Bermuda Exempted Company and the Gibraltar investment manager

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies OECD-compliant substance expectations: a Gibraltar-resident company, including a foreign-incorporated entity managed from Gibraltar, must demonstrate physical premises, full-time employees, and effective decision-making in Gibraltar. For this crypto fund, substance is evidenced by the GFSC DLT-provider licence, licensed investment manager and MLRO, board minutes held in Gibraltar, and local custody/reconciliation functions through Fireblocks. Concurrently, Bermuda-side economic substance must be maintained if the company claims Bermudian tax residence; absence of either-side substance exposes the structure to recharacterisation, denial of treaty benefits, and treatment as a shell or conduit under Gibraltar anti-avoidance rules and OECD standards.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Deliberate Under-Declaration of Crypto Gains

Statutory sanction: Tax-geared penalty of up to 100% of the under-declared tax, interest on late-paid tax, daily default penalties, and criminal conviction carrying an unlimited fine and/or up to seven years' imprisonment

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the submission of a false return face criminal prosecution, civil restitution of evaded tax, asset freezing orders, travel bans pending proceedings, disqualification orders, and potential imprisonment
Shell Company / Sham Substance Structure

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the company as Gibraltar-resident with assessments on the full Gibraltar-source trading profit; penalties for failure to notify chargeability of up to 100% of the tax; interest; possible strike-off or deregistration; denial of treaty benefits pursuant to Gibraltar GAAR and investor-state anti-abuse rules

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties where the company is treated as a sham or façade; travel restrictions and civil recovery orders may be imposed; false declarations of residence may lead to criminal prosecution for fraud
AML / VASP Licensing Failure (High-Risk Special Licence)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines of unlimited amount for serious contraventions; criminal liability for unlicensed activity with imprisonment of up to two years; revocation of the DLT provider licence; confiscation of assets under proceeds-of-crime legislation; public censure

Director personal liability: Directors and the Money Laundering Reporting Officer face personal criminal liability, personal fines, prohibition orders disqualifying them from managing licensed entities, and exposure to extradition proceedings in respect of predicate money-laundering offences
Transfer Pricing Misstatements

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustments by the Commissioner of Income Tax with interest; tax-geared penalties of up to 100% of the additional tax; secondary adjustments recharacterising excess payments as deemed dividends or loans

Director personal liability: Directors who approve related-party transactions without contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation may incur penalties for negligently incorrect returns, professional disciplinary action, and regulatory censure by the GFSC; exposed to personal liability in the event of insolvency or fraudulent trading
Unlawful Distributions and Debt Recharacterisation

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of debt as equity with denial of interest deductions; penalties for incorrect returns; insolvency claw-back actions; fines under the Companies Act 2014 for unlawful distributions

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable to repay unlawful distributions to the company and its creditors, face disqualification orders, and may be criminally prosecuted for fraudulent trading in egregious cases
