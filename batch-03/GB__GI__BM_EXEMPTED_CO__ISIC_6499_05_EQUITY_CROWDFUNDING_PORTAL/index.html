Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar - Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), Ministry of Finance; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation; corporate registry: Gibraltar Registrar of Companies, Companies House, https://www.csr.gov.gi

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company (BM_EXEMPTED_CO) must complete the following steps to procure a statutory Gibraltar tax identity: (1) File an external company registration with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 2014 and appoint a Gibraltar resident agent and registered office; (2) Obtain the requisite authorisation from the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) for the regulated equity crowdfunding portal activity; (3) Submit a taxpayer registration application to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office (Form IRO/TIN-REG) to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN), disclosing place of central management and control and the intended Gibraltar permanent establishment; (4) Register with HM Customs Gibraltar for a customs trader code (CTS ID) if any imports/exports of dutiable goods are contemplated; (5) Register for any applicable social/payroll withholding obligations (PAYE and social insurance contributions) if local staff are engaged.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar law does not permit a US-style 'check-the-box' fiscal transparency election. Available elections under the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended) and related statutes include: (i) seat-of-management election influencing tax residence evidenced by place of central management and control; (ii) group relief and consortium relief elections for qualifying tax groups; (iii) deemed-profit election for approved shipping and insurance activities; (iv) fixed-asset capital allowance elections; and (v) anti-hybrid rule elections aligning with OECD BEPS Action 2. The former Exempt Company and Qualifying Company regimes were abolished with grandfathering only for pre-existing certificates; no new exempt company status is available to a Bermuda Exempted Company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Consumption Tax/Customs and Excise Import Duty (no VAT or general sales tax)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable: Gibraltar levies no VAT/GST or general sales tax. Customs import duty/excise applies only to specified dutiable categories (spirits, wine, beer, tobacco products, hydrocarbon oils, and motor vehicles). A customs trader registration is mandatory for any importer of such goods irrespective of turnover; pure financial and crowdfunding intermediary services fall outside the scope of consumption tax, so no economic-turnover registration threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST, export 'zero-rating' in the EU/UK sense does not apply. Instead, goods physically exported from Gibraltar customs territory are relieved from import/customs consumption duties under the Customs Ordinance, conditional on proof that the goods left Gibraltar, while services rendered to non-resident customers (including crowdfunding technology services and platform access fees) are outside the scope of Gibraltar consumption tax under place-of-supply principles. Eligibility requires documentary evidence of the underlying commercial transaction, direct delivery to the foreign counterparty, and absence of subsequent redelivery into Gibraltar. The Bermuda Exempted Company must be the declared exporter on the customs entry; if it merely acts through a Gibraltar branch, branch records must demonstrate the goods/services left the territory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic entry lodged in the Gibraltar Electronic Customs system referencing the CTS trader ID)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (or equivalent multimodal transport document)
  • Commercial Invoice (itemised; matching declared value, tariff classification, and consignee details)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Credit Advice proving foreign-currency consideration and repatriation of export proceeds
  • Certified Packing List and Proof of Export (signed dock receipt or carrier certificate of departure)

Not applicable: Gibraltar does not operate a mandatory real-time e-invoicing clearance regime, fiscal digital stamping, or government API tax-clearance platform. Tax invoices must nonetheless satisfy the record-keeping requirements of the Income Tax Act 2010 and the GFSC conduct-of-business rules, which mandate issuance of proper invoices, retention for at least six years, and electronic submission of customs declarations through the Gibraltar Electronic Customs (GEC) system for any dutiable foreign trade transactions.

Fraudulent or incompletely documented export relief claims are audited by HM Customs Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office. Consequences include retrospective claw-back of all duties relieved with interest, confiscation/forfeiture of the goods, monetary penalties up to three times the value of the goods and/or imprisonment under the Customs Ordinance, and, in cases of deliberate tax-abuse connected to understating Gibraltar-source profit, penalties of up to 100% of the tax undercharged plus daily interest under the Income Tax Act 2010. The GFSC may additionally revoke the crowdfunding licence for AML/integrity failings where false export or FX repatriation documentation is used.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The UK and Gibraltar maintain full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the OECD Codes of Liberalisation; no exchange-control licence, pre-approval, or FX surrender applies to equity subscriptions, crowdfunding client collections, dividend repatriations, or intercompany loans. Profit and capital repatriation from Gibraltar to the Bermuda parent is free of exchange restrictions, subject only to CRS/FATCA reporting and bank-level Bank of England International Transaction Reporting System (ITRS) statistical filings. Crowdfunding investor funds are client moneys and must be safeguarded in a statutory trust or segregated escrow arrangement, not treated as corporate treasury assets.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. The Exchange Control (Repeal) Act 1979 (UK) abolished all surrender and conversion obligations for export and receipt proceeds; no mandatory conversion ratio or statutory timeline exists in the UK or Gibraltar. All crowdfunding ticket proceeds, platform fees, and disbursement returns may be held in any convertible currency and freely hedged; only passive statistical reporting applies via the banking system under the Bank of England ITRS, with direct reporting required if the entity holds foreign-currency balances above applicable de minimis reporting thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (UK central monetary authority; sets monetary policy for GBP and the Gibraltar pound at statutory par) and HM Treasury, which holds reserve exchange-control powers under the Exchange Control Act 1947 (repealed with effect from 1979; no controls currently in force). Gibraltar has no separate central bank; the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued at a 1:1 peg with GBP under the Currency Notes Act 1934 and is fully interchangeable with sterling. AML/CTF supervision for banking and payment services in Gibraltar is vested in the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility. FCA/GFSC-regulated e-money institutions (e.g., Wise, Currencycloud, Modulr) offer rapid multi-currency IBANs, SEPA/FPS connectivity, and automated FX conversion and are compatible with a Gibraltar-licensed crowdfunding portal for operational float only. Legal constraints: an EMI may not grant credit; client money must remain in segregated safeguarding accounts at an authorised credit institution; and most EMIs internally prohibit high-risk merchant sub-classes such as investment-based crowdfunding, requiring prior approved merchant due diligence. The primary client-money escrow must therefore sit with a Tier-1 bank or a dedicated trust/escrow agent, with EMIs used exclusively for fee and operational liquidity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from the Bermuda parent to the Gibraltar operating treasury are routed by SWIFT MT103; GBP settlements clear over CHAPS (same-day, above £1M) or Faster Payments (below £1M), with EUR finality over TARGET2/SEPA. Remittance information must state the purpose as a direct-investment equity contribution 'P.711 - Equity other than investment fund shares' (BPM6 financial-account code) and the sending bank must include the supporting board resolution, certificate of incumbency, and latest audited financial statements, which are provided to the receiving bank's relationship manager before crediting. For USD wires, intermediate correspondent routing (e.g., Barclays or HSBC New York CHIPS branch) is required; the receiving bank must be pre-notified to avoid AML pends.Disbursements to portfolio companies are executed via SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR, Faster Payments for GBP (subject to counterparty limits and risk thresholds), and SWIFT MT103 for USD and non-SEPA currencies; all settlements are correspondent-bank cleared and reportable under the Bank of England ITRS. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory de minimis threshold is published in Gibraltar; the Commissioner can require a Transfer Pricing Report on a risk basis for any controlled transaction. Practically, maintain Local File where annual controlled turnover exceeds GIP 1,000,000 or where related-party service fees exceed GIP 250,000. Master File and CbC Report apply to MNE groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 (approx. GIP 634,000,000) in the prior fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Yes. Gibraltar Economic Substance (Companies and Partnerships) Regulations 2021 apply. A crowdfunding portal falls within 'financing and leasing' / 'fund management' as a relevant activity. The entity must demonstrate in Gibraltar: (1) direction and management independence, (2) core income-generating activities (CIGA) physically performed in Gibraltar, (3) adequate premises, (4) qualified full-time employees, and (5) operating expenditure proportionate to income. Annual ESR report filing is mandatory; the low-tax exempt status of the Bermuda parent does not exempt the Gibraltar operation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion & Fraudulent False Returns

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2010: civil penalty up to 150% of the evaded tax; criminal conviction on indictment: unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 7 years; on summary conviction: fine up to GIP 50,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years. Interest at 7% p.a. runs from the date tax should have been paid.

Director personal liability: Any director, manager, or company secretary who 'knowingly causes, permits, or contributes' to the false return is jointly and severally liable for the company's tax penalty; subject to disqualification under the Company Directors Disqualification Act (Gibraltar) for up to 15 years; civil restitution orders and travel restraint orders may be issued to prevent departure from Gibraltar pending recovery.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Regulations 2021: financial penalty up to GIP 50,000 per failure plus daily default penalties up to GIP 1,000; the Commissioner may reclassify the entity as non-resident and assess 12.5% on all Gibraltar-source income without relief; automatic exchange of substance information with UK HMRC and EU member states under DAC6/CBCR frameworks.

Director personal liability: Directors' enabling of substance failure can result in personal penalties up to GIP 50,000 and disqualification; if the company becomes insolvent due to penalties, directors may face wrongful trading liability under Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 and contribution orders for the full penalty amount.
Unlicensed Crowdfunding Activity / AML-CTF Breach

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed activity: fine up to GIP 100,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years; each day of operation is a separate offence. AML failure under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2011 (as amended) and AML/CFT Regulations: substantial regulatory fines up to GIP 250,000 per breach; personal criminal liability for money laundering carries imprisonment up to 14 years and unlimited fines.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) are personally criminally liable for deliberate AML failures; travel bans and asset freezing orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act may be imposed; compensation orders in favour of defrauded investors are made directly against the directors' personal assets.
Repatriation & Transfer Pricing Distortion

Statutory sanction: Tax re-characterisation under Income Tax Act 2010 s.24A: the Commissioner substitutes the arm's length consideration, disallows the deduction, and assesses additional 12.5% CIT plus interest; penalty for negligent transfer pricing up to 100% of the additional tax; for deliberate transfer pricing concealment, up to 150%.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the CIT return knowing the transfer pricing adjustments were not arm's length are personally liable for the accuracy penalty; cross-border tax avoidance arrangements may trigger HMRC/Gibraltar anti-abuse rulings and director disqualification; failure to maintain TP documentation shifts burden of proof to the taxpayer and exposes directors to personal examination under oath.
