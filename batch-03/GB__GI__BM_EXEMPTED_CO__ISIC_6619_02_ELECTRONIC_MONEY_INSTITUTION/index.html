Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

The Companies Act imposes no minimum paid-up capital for an exempted company; however, the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission requires paid-up initial capital of not less than EUR 350,000 for an electronic money institution, which must be held in escrow and released only upon licence issuance and safeguarding of customer funds.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax); administrative portal: Gibraltar e-Gov Services at https://egov.gibraltar.gov.gi

E-government filing portal

For a Bermuda Exempted Company establishing a branch or place of business in Gibraltar: (1) register as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry (Form CR/Overseas-1) under the Companies Act 2014 and appoint a Gibraltar-resident local representative; (2) submit certified constitutional documents, certificate of incorporation, certificate of good standing from the Bermuda Registrar of Companies, and evidence of a registered Gibraltar office address; (3) apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar TIN (Form TIN-FE) using the Companies Registry number, local address, and parent jurisdiction identifier; (4) if the overseas company carries on a trade through a permanent establishment in Gibraltar, register for corporation tax within 30 days of commencement; if the entity remains purely Bermuda-exempt with no Gibraltar-source income and no PE, no corporation tax filing is required, though a TIN may still be issued for compliance and FATCA/CRS identifier purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — For a Bermuda Exempted Company establishing a branch or place of business in Gibraltar: (1) register as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry (Form CR/Overseas-1) under the Companies Act 2014 and appoint a Gibraltar-resident local representative; (2) submit certified constitutional documents, certificate of incorporation, certificate of good standing from the Bermuda Registrar of Companies, and evidence of a registered Gibraltar office address; (3) apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar TIN (Form TIN-FE) using the Companies Registry number, local address, and parent jurisdiction identifier; (4) if the overseas company carries on a trade through a permanent establishment in Gibraltar, register for corporation tax within 30 days of commencement; if the entity remains purely Bermuda-exempt with no Gibraltar-source income and no PE, no corporation tax filing is required, though a TIN may still be issued for compliance and FATCA/CRS identifier purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No Gibraltar check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available. Tax classification follows legal incorporation and central management and control; a Bermuda Exempted Company is classified as a non-resident foreign company and cannot elect Gibraltar pass-through status. A non-resident company may, under the Income Tax (Territorial Scope) Rules, restrict taxable income solely to profits attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment or to Gibraltar-source income; no election is required to preserve Bermuda-exempt status, but residency by central management and control in Gibraltar would override the foreign exempt status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

GST (Gibraltar Goods and Services Tax) – single-stage consumption tax, not a VAT; standard GST rate is 0%.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – Gibraltar has no turnover-based GST registration threshold because the standard GST rate is 0%; registration is necessary only for persons importing or exporting goods or making prescribed local supplies subject to GST administration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Gibraltar are outside the scope of, or effectively zero-rated for, Gibraltar GST because the standard rate is 0% and GST is a single-stage tax with no input-output credit mechanism. Offshore services supplied by an Electronic Money Institution, such as virtual IBAN issuance and digital wallet reserve management, are not subject to Gibraltar GST as they are outside the limited territorial reach of the tax. No input tax refund can be claimed because there is no input tax recovery system; conversely, no GST is chargeable on qualifying export transactions.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (electronic form via EDI)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Evidence of destination outside Gibraltar (customs statistics and export validation)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement evidence evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds through a Gibraltar-licensed bank (where applicable)
  • Certificate of Origin (only where preferential tariff treatment is claimed)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital invoice fiscalisation mandate exists for GST. Cross-border goods movements require electronic customs declarations through Gibraltar Customs' Electronic Data Interchange (EDI) system; there is no digital stamping requirement for commercial invoices or tax documents.

No GST export-refund or clawback mechanism exists because GST is not a creditable VAT. However, false or fraudulent customs export declarations are punishable under the Customs Ordinance with fines up to £10,000 per offence, forfeiture of goods and, on conviction, criminal sanctions. For income tax purposes, misattribution of Gibraltar-source profits from e-money issuance, wallet reserve management or payment processing to a foreign tax-exempt company may be recharacterised as a permanent establishment under the Income Tax (Territorial Jurisdiction and Permanent Establishments) Rules, triggering retrospective corporation tax assessments at the applicable 10% rate plus statutory interest and penalties for failure to notify registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains a fully liberalized, unrestricted capital and current account convertibility regime. There are no controls on the conversion of GIP into other currencies or on the repatriation of dividends, profits, loan interest, or capital. For an Electronic Money Institution, the regulatory capital and safeguarded client funds must be held in highly liquid instruments, but no prior approval is required for foreign exchange transactions. Profit remittance to a Bermuda-headquartered shareholder is unrestricted, subject only to standard sanctions screening and anti-money-laundering checks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation timelines are applicable under Gibraltar or UK exchange control laws. An EMI may freely retain, convert, or disburse foreign currency proceeds from any cross-border business without obligation to surrender funds to a central authority.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019; the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934 and is pegged at parity to the pound sterling (GBP). The Bank of England retains monetary policy authority over sterling; no specific exchange control authority exists as all exchange controls have been abolished.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High. A Gibraltar-licensed EMI may open settlement accounts with FinTech and Electronic Money Institutions in the UK and EEA, but the lapse of EU passporting post-Brexit necessitates bilateral correspondent agreements. Pure offshore digital EMIs (e.g., those in non-cooperative jurisdictions) are treated as higher-risk counterparties and require enhanced due diligence, increased collateral, and face-to-face verification. Practical clearing of GBP and EUR remains fully reachable through sponsored membership in CHAPS, Faster Payments, and SEPA via a local high-street bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections (share capital, share premium, subordinated loan) must be settled via SWIFT MT103/MT202 using purpose code C-001 (Direct Investment) for equity or S130 (Subordinated Debt) for loans; for EUR remittances, SEPA Credit Transfer with purpose code 'EQUI' or 'LOAN' and reference 'CAPTL'. The receiving bank must be provided with: (i) a notarised board resolution approving the capital increase; (ii) share issue register entries from the Bermuda Registrar; (iii) verified identification of the remitter and beneficial owner; (iv) a pre-notification email to the bank's trade operations desk; (v) a written confirmation from legal counsel that the transaction is exempt from Gibraltar's sanctions and AML regulations.Outbound trade settlements and operational disbursements should be executed over: (i) CHAPS or Faster Payments (FPS) for same-day GBP, with reason code 'TRADE' and a unique invoice reference; (ii) SEPA Credit Transfer via TARGET2 for EUR, using ISO 20022 pain.001 message with purpose 'TRCO' (Trade Payment) or 'PURC' (Purchase of Goods); (iii) SWIFT MT103/MT202 for cross-border payments in USD or other currencies, with MT202 used for correspondent fees and netting; (iv) for high-value or documentary trades, an irrevocable, confirmed commercial letter of credit, issued through a top-tier correspondence bank under SWIFT MT700, with proceeds released via MT202 to the beneficiary's account.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) on GIP/USD, GIP/EUR, and GIP/GBPDeliverable FX Forwards (FX Outright) under an ISDA 1998 FX Master AgreementFX Cross-Currency Swaps (coupon or principal exchange) with licensed counterpartiesEuropean-style currency options purchased from non-affiliated investment-grade banksFX plain vanilla swaps for temporary conversion of GBP to EUR and reverse transactions
