Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Gibraltar private limited company constituted on an exempted basis; subscription monies may lawfully be held in a client account pending allotment, and no escrow account is statutorily mandated. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar (HMGoG), 8 St. George's Lane, Gibraltar; e-Filing Portal: https://etax.gibraltar.gov.gi. Customs authority: HM Customs Gibraltar (Customs & Border Control Agency, Ministry of Finance), operating the ASYCUDA World electronic declarations portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/customs.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity, including a Bermuda Exempted Company, must first register with the Gibraltar Companies Registry as an 'external company' under the Companies Act 2014, appointing a licensed local registered agent and a registered office. The Companies Registry transmits the incorporation/external registration particulars to the Income Tax Office, which issues the company's Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN). In parallel, the bonded depot operator must register with HM Customs Gibraltar to obtain an EORI-GI number and a Bonded Warehouse Licence under the Customs Ordinance, supported by the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered office proof, site and security plans, financial standing evidence, AML/KYC identification of beneficial owners, and projected trading volumes. Income tax registration and customs licensing are independent obligations; both must be updated within 30 days of any material change of particulars.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (subject to territorial-source limitation). The Bermuda Exempted Company is exempt from Bermuda income tax; under Gibraltar's territorial income tax regime, only profits arising in or derived from Gibraltar are chargeable at the standard 12.5% corporate rate. Profits from goods passing through the bonded depot and exported outside Gibraltar may fall outside Gibraltar-source profit, but local storage, handling, logistics, and management activities physically performed in Gibraltar create allocable Gibraltar-source taxable profits. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (subject to territorial-source limitation). The Bermuda Exempted Company is exempt from Bermuda income tax; under Gibraltar's territorial income tax regime, only profits arising in or derived from Gibraltar are chargeable at the standard 12.5% corporate rate. Profits from goods passing through the bonded depot and exported outside Gibraltar may fall outside Gibraltar-source profit, but local storage, handling, logistics, and management activities physically performed in Gibraltar create allocable Gibraltar-source taxable profits. The free-zone/bonded-depot customs status does not by itself confer an income tax exemption on all operating income.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or elective pass-through regime exists in Gibraltar; entity tax classification is determined by statutory interpretation of the Income Tax Act and the Companies Act. The company may elect its residence treatment by reference to the location of central management and control (Gibraltar-resident vs non-resident company). Hybrid mismatch elections must follow the OECD BEPS Action 2 provisions implemented in Gibraltar; any transparent/opaque classification mismatch giving rise to double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes must be reported and is subject to disallowance. No qualifying-company preferential tax regime remains available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Customs Duty and Excise Duty Regime — No VAT/GST applies (Gibraltar is outside the UK and EU VAT territories).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT/GST exists, so no turnover threshold applies. A Bonded Warehouse Licence under the Customs Ordinance is mandatory regardless of turnover. Corporate income tax registration is required from the first day of business activity without any minimum turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating does not apply because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST. In customs terms, duty-relief is available for (1) goods admitted to a customs free zone or bonded warehouse under duty suspension, and (2) goods subsequently removed for export to a non-Gibraltar destination. Relief is granted when the operator holds a valid Bonded Warehouse Licence, stock movements are fully recorded and audited, a SAD export declaration is filed and cleared in ASYCUDA World before physical removal, goods leave the territory within the authorised period (ordinarily 3 months, extendable on application), and no prohibited or restricted commodity is involved. Offshore services supplied by the depot operator to non-resident customers are 'outside the scope' of Gibraltar income tax rather than zero-rated; no VAT refund mechanism exists in Gibraltar.

  • Single Administrative Document (SAD/C88) — ASYCUDA World export declaration with customs clearance stamp/reference
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of the goods from Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement evidence confirming receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer
  • Bonded warehouse removal order and inventory ledger evidencing duty-suspended stock movement
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin (where required by the destination customs authority)

Gibraltar operates no real-time electronic-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate. Customs export and import declarations must be lodged electronically via ASYCUDA World in the form of Single Administrative Documents (SAD); bonded warehouse stock records, transfers, and removals are subject to authorised electronic inventory controls. Electronically issued invoices are acceptable for tax audit purposes; complete records must be retained for at least 6 years.

HM Customs Gibraltar audits bonded warehouse operators under the Customs Ordinance; false export declarations, diversion into free circulation, or misuse of duty suspension trigger forfeiture of goods, immediate payment of the entire suspended duty, and financial penalties of up to three times the value of the goods, with imprisonment up to 7 years on conviction. On the income tax side, the Income Tax Office may raise retrospective assessments without time limit in cases of fraud or wilful default, impose an additional penalty charge of up to 100% of the undercharged tax plus statutory interest, and revoke the Bonded Warehouse Licence. Customs duty clawback assessments may cover a look-back period of up to 6 years across the audited supply chain.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The GIP operates on a currency-board-style peg to GBP with full legal convertibility at parity. There are zero exchange-control barriers to current-account or capital-account flows for both residents and non-residents; trade invoices, service payments, dividends, interest, royalties, share-capital injections, and loan repayments are freely convertible without ministerial or central-bank approval. A Bermuda Exempted Company operating through a Gibraltar external-company registration faces no FX consent requirement for profit remittance or for conversion of bonded-depot sales proceeds. Practical constraints arise only for non-convertible or heavily restricted currencies (e.g., CNY, INR, NGN), which require NDF or correspondent-bank conversion outside the GFSC-regulated banking perimeter. Additional care is required to align the Bermuda Economic Substance filing with the actual management-and-control locus in Gibraltar, so that the zero-tax classification is not eroded by UK anti-hybrid or CFC rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar and the United Kingdom impose no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement that bonded-warehouse or free-trade-zone export receipts be repatriated or converted within a defined period. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in original currency (GBP, EUR, USD) or converted at the group treasury's discretion. The only statutory obligations on the bonded-depot operation are customs-related: goods leaving the free zone must be declared via the Gibraltar Customs single administrative document (GDA/SAD) and any excise duty or import VAT must be settled on the declarant; neither obligation operates as an exchange-control surrender requirement.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no separate central bank. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the prudential regulator and licensing authority for banks and payment institutions. The Government of Gibraltar issues Gibraltar pounds (GIP) at a fixed 1:1 parity with UK sterling under the Currency Notes Act 1934, while the Bank of England serves as the de facto monetary anchor and ultimate sterling settlement reference. UK Treasury retains oversight of Gibraltar's external financial obligations, and Gibraltar payment institutions access UK clearing via CHAPS and related direct/indirect participation arrangements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for core treasury, customs-bond, and trade-finance functions; MEDIUM only as a supplementary collections gateway for small e-commerce receipts. Legal constraints: Gibraltar regulates electronic money under its Financial Services Act 2019 and e-money legislation, supervised by the GFSC. Following Brexit, UK and EEA EMI passports no longer extend automatically to Gibraltar, so an offshore digital EMI (e.g., a Lithuanian, UK, or Singapore-licensed institution) may on-board the Bermuda Exempted Company as a customer but cannot operate through a Gibraltar passport or branch. An EMI cannot hold interest-bearing deposits, cannot issue customs-bond guarantees or standby LCs covering bonded-warehouse duty liabilities, and cannot provide FX forward or swap hedging. Client funds must be segregated in qualifying accounts and are subject to nightly sweep obligations; therefore all residual balances must be reverse-settled to the primary GFSC-regulated bank within the same business day.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Standard inbound routing for the Bermuda Exempted Company's capital into its Gibraltar external-company account: (1) execute an authenticated board resolution authorising the share-premium or shareholder-loan injection and naming the designated receiving account; (2) the remitting bank or group treasury issues a SWIFT MT103 (single client credit transfer) and, for interbank cover through correspondents, an MT202 COV, using the narrative CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION GB__GI__BM_EXEMPTED_CO__ISIC_5210_03_FREE_TRADE_ZONE_BONDED_DEPOT with ISO 20022 purpose code B0101 where a structured code is required; (3) deliver to the receiving bank's compliance desk the executed board resolution, UBO declarations, source-of-wealth evidence, Gibraltar external-company registration certificate, and a remitting-bank reference confirming the origin of funds; (4) funds are parked temporarily in the Capital Escrow Account pending the bank's no-objection and any GFSC post-authorisation notification, then released to the Operational Account. Settlement finality is same-day via CHAPS for GBP, TARGET2 for EUR, and the Gibraltar clearPay retail fallback for GIP-denominated receipts; USD receipts are settled via SWIFT through the bank's nostro and are subject to OFSI/OFAC screening at receipt.Clearing-rail hierarchy for bonded-depot disbursements: (1) supplier settlements are executed as SWIFT MT103 with 35A narrative referencing the purchase invoice and Incoterm, using ISO purpose code C310 or D310; (2) documentary credits are issued as MT700, amended via MT707, and accepted through MT752 upon presentation of warehouse receipts, packing lists, delivery orders, and endorsed bills of lading; (3) customs duties and excise are settled through the Gibraltar Customs payment portal by debit authority on the Operational Account in GIP/GBP; (4) payroll, utilities, and domestic leases clear via local Faster Payments/BACS for GBP or SEPA/TARGET2 for EUR; (5) high-value disbursements above the bank's threshold (typically GBP 250,000) require a treasury pre-call and same-day settlement through CHAPS or SWIFT with prefunded nostro balances to avoid unarranged overdrafts. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW for core treasury, customs-bond, and trade-finance functions; MEDIUM only as a supplementary collections gateway for small e-commerce receipts. Legal constraints: Gibraltar regulates electronic money under its Financial Services Act 2019 and e-money legislation, supervised by the GFSC. Following Brexit, UK and EEA EMI passports no longer extend automatically to Gibraltar, so an offshore digital EMI (e.g., a Lithuanian, UK, or Singapore-licensed institution) may on-board the Bermuda Exempted Company as a customer but cannot operate through a Gibraltar passport or branch. An EMI cannot hold interest-bearing deposits, cannot issue customs-bond guarantees or standby LCs covering bonded-warehouse duty liabilities, and cannot provide FX forward or swap hedging. Client funds must be segregated in qualifying accounts and are subject to nightly sweep obligations; therefore all residual balances must be reverse-settled to the primary GFSC-regulated bank within the same business day.
