Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, HM Government of Gibraltar (Income Tax Office), 3-5 Secretary's Lane, Gibraltar; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation

E-government filing portal

For a Bermuda Exempted Company (BM_EXEMPTED_CO) that may be managed and controlled from Gibraltar, the foreign entity must (1) perform a central management and control (CMC) factual review: if CMC remains in Bermuda, the company is not liable to Gibraltar income tax and no tax registration is required unless a permanent establishment arises. (2) If a Gibraltar permanent establishment or CMC exists, apply online via the Gibraltar e-Tax portal by filing Form GTIN-1 (Application for Gibraltar Tax Identification Number) with the Income Tax Office, attaching the Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum/Articles, board registers, AML compliance pack, and a certified translation if not in English. (3) Upon approval, the Commissioner issues a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (GTIN); this functions as the company's statutory TIN for income tax and withholding tax filings. (4) If any taxable imports or exports of hardware or equipment occur, obtain an EORI number from the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Department via the electronic customs declarations platform. (5) For licensed algorithmic market-making, the GFSC licence approval with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission is a mandatory precondition and the GTIN must be presented in the licence application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda Exempted Company may elect to remain a tax-exempt undertaking in Bermuda under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966. In Gibraltar, there is no check-the-box regime; tax status follows the factual CMC test. A non-resident election may be made by filing a Statutory Declaration of Non-Residence with the Income Tax Office, supported by board minutes proving that management decisions are taken outside Gibraltar. If the entity is treated as tax-transparent under a hybrid mismatch arrangement, Gibraltar's OECD-aligned hybrid mismatch rules require the entity to file a supplementary return allocating any mismatch payment or set-off.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None (No VAT/GST/sales tax; Gibraltar levies only customs and excise duties on imported tangible goods)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT/GST because Gibraltar has no VAT or GST. No turnover threshold triggers an indirect tax registration. An EORI registration is compulsory from the first import/export transaction, irrespective of turnover value. (UK VAT threshold of GBP 85,000 does not apply in Gibraltar.)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable to proprietary HFT services: Gibraltar does not operate a VAT/GST export zero-rating mechanism. Exports of physical goods (e.g., trading servers or network equipment) may qualify for relief from Gibraltar customs and excise duties via a duty-suspension or export drawback procedure, provided the goods leave Gibraltar to a non-EU destination or are re-exported in identical state within 24 months. For intangible algorithmic trading services, the supply is outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation and no zero-rating claim is required.

  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Gibraltar imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping mandate. Import/export declarations must be submitted electronically through the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Electronic Data Interchange system; commercial invoices must be retained with the export package for at least 7 years (6 years for GFSC-regulated records). No government API integration is required for invoices beyond standard customs electronic filing.

Although Gibraltar does not apply VAT, false export declarations to obtain excise/customs duty relief are prosecuted under the Customs and Excise Duties Act. The Commissioner may claw back all relieved duty, impose a penalty of up to 100% of the duty value, charge interest at 8% per annum, seize the goods/equipment, and refer the matter for criminal prosecution. For income tax, mischaracterization of export turnover as non-Gibraltar source to avoid the 10% corporation tax may trigger transfer pricing adjustments, CFC reattribution, and penalties up to 60% of the tax under-reported, with a 6-year discovery window.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility. The GIP is pegged at parity with GBP and circulates freely alongside sterling. No currency surrender, conversion quota, prioritisation list, or pre-approval gate applies to capital inflows or outflows. Profit and dividend remittance is unrestricted provided the entity maintains current GFSC licensing, Economic Substance (Companies) Act 2020 filings, and acceptable corporate substance. For proprietary algorithmic market-making, cash balances in GBP, EUR, and USD may be swapped and remitted without exchange-control filings, but the treasury policy must document blocking of unlicensed crypto-fiat conversions and NDF usage limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Gibraltar imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio for physical exports or services. Gross trading and market-making receipts are classified as income and are free to be held in any currency. The entity is only subject to routine anti-money laundering (AML) transaction reporting, prudent foreign exchange exposure limits, and any specific supervisory notification obligations imposed by the GFSC as conditions of its investment firm licence.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Government of Gibraltar Currency Ordinance; monetary policy anchored to the Bank of England through the Gibraltar pound (GIP) to sterling (GBP) currency-board peg. There is no independent central bank in Gibraltar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Moderate. GFSC-regulated e-money institutions can provide ancillary payment and conversion services, but offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut) cannot serve as primary clearing venues because they lack direct exchange clearing membership, RTGS or Fedwire connectivity, and institutional MT103/MT202 settlement facilities. Post-Brexit, EEA/UK EMIs can no longer passport into Gibraltar; a separate GFSC e-money licence would be required. Consequently, the core trading treasury must sit on bank-grade direct clearing rails, while an EMI may be used only for minor operational expenses and discretionary travel disbursements.

0% – Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders; Bermuda imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by the Exempted Company; the UK does not impose dividend withholding tax on distributions from a Gibraltar-linked UK-resident parent.

Within 12 months after the end of the accounting period; for a 31 December year-end, the return and audited financial statements must be submitted by the following 30 November. Non-calendar accounting periods are due by the 30 November immediately following the end of the period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP (Gibraltar pound): Country-by-Country Report applies where the multinational group's consolidated turnover is at least EUR 750 million (approx. GIP 640M); Master File must be prepared when group turnover exceeds EUR 50M (approx. GIP 43M) and in-scope transactions exceed GIP 1 million; Local File is mandatory for Gibraltar-resident taxpayers (including Bermuda Exempted Companies managed in Gibraltar) with related-party transactions exceeding GIP 1 million per annum or intercompany financing above GIP 500,000. The Income Tax Office may request documentation for any controlled transaction above GIP 100,000 where the arm's length result is not prima facie compliant.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar substance rules (transposing EU ESR requirements) apply to companies undertaking 'relevant activities', including financing, leasing, fund management, and IP businesses; proprietary algorithmic trading as principal is ordinarily not an enumerated relevant activity, but a licensed investment firm or a fund-holding entity will be in scope. The managing-and-controlled test remains decisive for residence: if HFT strategy, risk limits, and clearing mandates are set in Gibraltar, the Bermuda Exempted Company is Gibraltar-tax-resident and must show premises, qualified employees, board meetings, and strategic oversight in Gibraltar. Concurrently, Bermuda's Economic Substance (International Tax Compliance) Act 2018 requires the exempt entity to file an annual substance declaration and demonstrate core income-generating activities and directed management in Bermuda for any 'relevant activity' (finance and leasing, IP business, etc.). Failure to satisfy both jurisdictions creates a dual-substance gap triggering exchange of information and penalty exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Fine of up to 100% of tax understated (deliberate concealed inaccuracy), daily surcharges of £10 per day, interest at the statutory rate, revocation of any prior tax ruling for the HFT book, public naming on the Gibraltar Income Tax Office defaulter list.

Director personal liability: Directors may face civil restitution assessments, freezing orders and travel bans while evidencing centrality of management, and criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act 2010 with an unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 2 years; under the Proceeds of Crime Act confiscation orders can attach personal assets.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to £10,000 for failure to file the Bermuda Economic Substance Declaration; default in Gibraltar substance reporting leads to automatic exchange with HMRC and Bermuda and a re-assessment treating the entity as Gibraltar-resident, with additional penalties up to 100% of underpaid tax plus naming on the International Tax Compliance public register.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit a false substance declaration are personally liable for penalties, disqualification under the Companies Act (up to 5 years), travel restrictions to protect the tax authority's enforcement, and personal liability for the company's tax debts where the corporate veil is pierced for sham or façade transactions.
Transfer Pricing and PE Attribution Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing Gibraltar assessable profits; penalty of 20% of the additional tax due (careless) rising to 50% (deliberate); late-payment interest; denial of deduction for the intercompany charge; potential secondary adjustment treated as a constructive dividend subject to the 0% dividend WHT but assessable on the recipient if a Gibraltar resident shareholder.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign incorrect group accounts can be pursued for contribution in insolvency; disqualification orders for 5 years if the TP adjustment materially contributed to balance-sheet insolvency; personal assessment for penalties up to £100,000 per Director under the duty of oversight provisions in the Income Tax (Administration) Act.
AML/CTF and Unlicensed Investment Business

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties of up to 10% of annual turnover for AML breaches and a fine of up to $2M (or GIP equivalent) plus 4 years imprisonment for carrying on regulated investment services without a licence under the Financial Services Act 2019; revocation of any GFSC authorisation; confiscation orders for funds linked to unlawful trading.

Director personal liability: Directors are individually liable for allowing unauthorised regulated activity, with personal fines up to GIP 1M and extradition exposure to the UK or US where predicate money-laundering charges exist; travel bans, surrender of passports, director disqualification for 10 years, and joint liability with the company for civil penalties imposed by the GFSC Enforcement Committee.
