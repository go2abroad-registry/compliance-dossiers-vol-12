Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office (ITO), HM Government of Gibraltar, 8 Governor's Lane, Gibraltar GX11 1AA, acting under the Commissioner of Income Tax (Income Tax Act 2010) and the Gibraltar Customs & Border Agency (indirect tax and customs). Official filing and payment portal: HM Government of Gibraltar eServices (eservices.gibraltar.gov.gi), covering annual corporate self-assessment returns, notices of liability, economic substance returns, and Purchase Tax/customs declaration processes.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Company law registration: a Bermuda exempted company establishing a place of business in Gibraltar must register as an overseas company with the Registrar of Companies within one month of establishing the Gibraltar branch, delivering a certified copy of the Bermuda certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, a Gibraltar registered-office address, and particulars of the local agent and directors. Step 2 - Tax registration: submit the Notice of Liability (Form C-G1) to the Income Tax Office disclosing the branch basis period, estimated Gibraltar-source profits, and the appointed Gibraltar-resident tax agent; the ITO then issues the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN), which must be quoted on all returns and payments. Step 3 - Ancillary registrations: register with the Customs & Border Agency for Purchase Tax when importing business equipment or supplies, and complete the annual Economic Substance Return (ESR) registration on the ITO portal once chargeable activity commences. Penalties apply for failure to notify chargeability within the period prescribed by the Commissioner.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Bermuda parent layer: Tax_Exempt_Zone under the Bermuda Exempted Companies Act 1981, with exemption certificate valid until 2035 or 2050; Gibraltar branch layer: territorial charge on Gibraltar-source profits at the 12.5% standard corporation tax rate; no pass-through or tax-transparent election is available to corporations in Gibraltar).

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box or classification election; companies are taxed at entity level. Available statutory elections: (a) group relief or loss-surrender election between Gibraltar-resident group companies (Income Tax Act 2010, sections 42-48); (b) election for the 2% preferential rate for qualifying regulated financial-services companies, restricted to licensed insurers, banks, and portfolio managers and therefore unavailable to an ISIC 5222 marine surveyor; (c) anti-hybrid elections under the Income Tax (Hybrid and Other Mismatch) Rules 2020 implementing ATAD; (d) optional alignment of the branch accounting period to the Gibraltar technical year of assessment running 1 July to 30 June; and (e) unilateral application to the Commissioner for revenue apportionment between survey work performed in Gibraltar territorial waters and work performed at foreign ports or on the high seas.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic-turnover threshold triggers Purchase Tax: the charge arises on the importation of goods at CIF value plus any customs duty, so all commercial importers must register with the Gibraltar Customs & Border Agency. For services, no indirect-tax registration arises because services are outside the Purchase Tax base. The draft future Gibraltar VAT contemplates a registration threshold in the region of EUR 10,000 for distance sales, but no such threshold is currently in force.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a VAT zero-rating: marine survey services are outside Gibraltar's Purchase Tax statute, so fees charged by the branch for vessel hull and cargo damage inspection, claims surveys, and reporting to overseas underwriters arise outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax and carry no output tax. Under the future Gibraltar VAT Act (not yet in force), supplies of marine surveying services connected to a vessel engaged in international voyages or to non-Gibraltar insured parties would qualify for zero-rating or export exemption, subject to documentary proof of the international transport nexus and primary evidence of the insured marine risk.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar SAD C88 or electronic e-Export) where any goods-related costs are separately claimed as export-supported
  • Bill of Lading or original Sea Waybill (or Air Waybill) evidencing international movement of the surveyed vessel or cargo
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing FX repatriation of the survey fee within 180 days
  • Signed Marine Survey Report (ISIC 5222 deliverable) identifying survey date, vessel IMO number, casualty particulars, and insured party
  • Underwriter settlement or policy document linking the survey mandate to the insured marine risk

None. Gibraltar does not mandate real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or PEPPOL BIS exchange. Purchase Tax is collected at customs clearance through the Gibraltar electronic declaration system (SAD/EAD), while corporate documentation such as invoices, survey certificates, and bank statements must be retained in original form for six years. No transition to EU continuous transaction controls is scheduled.

Misdeclarations, fraudulent export treatment, or abuse of Purchase Tax status attract: (i) under the Purchase Tax Act 2015, civil penalties up to 200% of the evaded tax, forfeiture of the goods, and up to seven years' imprisonment for fraudulent evasion; (ii) under the Income Tax Act 2010, assessment corrections on a six-year time limit, extended to twenty years for fraudulent or negligent returns, penalties up to 100% of the under-declared tax plus interest at the statutory rate of 8% per annum and potential criminal prosecution; and (iii) penalties under the Gibraltar Economic Substance rules for failure to substantiate the branch's activities. The Commissioner of Income Tax applies retrospective clawback of any zero-rating or refund benefit, together with surcharge, where the 180-day FX repatriation deadline is missed or the inward remittance certificate is false.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account. GIP is freely convertible into GBP at par, and GBP is concurrent legal tender in Gibraltar; USD and EUR conversion is freely available through the commercial banking system. The Bermuda Exempted Company enjoys statutory tax-exempt status and Gibraltar operates a territorial 10% corporation tax regime with exemption for qualifying non-resident activities, so profit remittance to ultimate shareholders attracts no withholding tax and no administrative dividend-blocking hurdles. Marine survey fees constitute current-account services revenue under ISIC 5222; remittance of such fees is unrestricted, absent sanctions exposure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Neither Gibraltar nor Bermuda imposes statutory timelines for export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation deadlines. Marine cargo damage survey income is treated as fee-for-service export revenue; it may be retained in any freely convertible currency, held offshore, or hedged in accordance with the treasury policy. There is no central-bank reporting obligation for individual commercial payments, only standard suspicious-transaction and sanctions reporting under GFSC rules.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the prudential and AML/CFT supervisor for banking business in Gibraltar; Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) as the home-state regulator of the Bermuda Exempted Company; Bank of England as the monetary anchor for the Gibraltar pound (GIP), which is pegged 1:1 to GBP. Exchange control administration is fully liberalised: the United Kingdom repealed exchange controls in 1979 and Gibraltar maintains no separate exchange-control ministry or prior-approval apparatus.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required where annual Gibraltar turnover exceeds GIP 10,000,000 or controlled transactions with the Bermuda parent exceed GIP 1,000,000. Master File required for groups with aggregate worldwide turnover exceeding GIP 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Yes. Marine insurance surveying falls within the relevant activity of 'Insurance' and 'Distribution and Service Centers'. The Bermuda Exempted Company must maintain at least two board meetings per year in Gibraltar, employ locally qualified surveyors and administrative staff, keep physical premises or recorded operational assets, and perform core income-generating activities in Gibraltar. Failure to satisfy economic substance creates a rebuttable presumption of tax evasion and a classification as a shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Additional assessment at 12.5% plus a penalty of 200% of the tax evaded, a criminal fine of up to GIP 500,000, and imprisonment for up to 7 years. Orders for asset forfeiture may be issued.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable under s.218 Income Tax Act for unpaid corporate tax, disqualified from board service for up to 15 years, and made subject to court-imposed travel bans preventing departure from Gibraltar.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Strike-off from the Gibraltar Register of Companies, cancellation of the Bermuda exemption certificate, and administrative penalty of GIP 50,000 per year of non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Director assets may be frozen by court order; directors receive a 10-year prohibition from acting as director of any company registered in a British Overseas Territory or Crown Dependency; civil liability for resulting tax deficiencies.
AML and Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Unlimited fines under the Proceeds of Crime Act (Gibraltar) and the AML/CFT Regulations, seizure of all transaction proceeds, and imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted for money laundering and receive a personal civil penalty of GIP 100,000; they may be barred from fiduciary positions and subject to Personal Liability Orders for unpaid confiscation amounts.
