Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax); online portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company does not require Gibraltar tax registration unless it derives Gibraltar-source income or maintains a permanent establishment. Application process: (1) complete the Income Tax Office Registration Form; (2) provide constitutional documents including Bermuda certificate of incorporation and memorandum of association; (3) provide director and shareholder identification; (4) submit a statement of expected Gibraltar-source income; (5) obtain the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number. No minimum capital or notarisation is required under the Income Tax Act 2010.

No check-the-box election is available in Gibraltar. A company may elect non-residence if central management and control is exercised outside Gibraltar, subject to Income Tax Act 2010 s.13. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or Sales and Use Tax (Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and imposes no indirect tax on goods or services)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – no VAT/GST registration threshold in Gibraltar

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – Gibraltar has no VAT/GST and therefore no export zero-rating regime. A Bermuda Exempted Company exporting goods or offshore services does not need to prove zero-rating eligibility to the Gibraltar tax authority; any indirect tax consequences arise solely in the destination jurisdiction.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Certificate of Origin (Made in Gibraltar / UK)

No mandatory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API reporting, or digital stamping is in force. Gibraltar does not operate a fiscal e-invoicing regime; invoices are governed by commercial contract law. If the entity sells into an EU jurisdiction by distance sales, the customer's VAT obligations may apply, but Gibraltar imposes no local electronic invoicing or reporting requirement.

No Gibraltar indirect tax clawback applies due to the absence of VAT/GST. However, false customs declarations or failure to retain export documentation may trigger penalties under Gibraltar's Customs Ordinance, including forfeiture of goods and criminal liability. If the exporter claims zero-rated supplies in a foreign VAT system, the relevant foreign tax authority may disallow the export VAT refund, assess penalties and interest, and impose retrospective clawbacks for fraudulent claims.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility with no exchange controls; GIP is freely convertible to GBP at par and the conversion into USD, EUR, or other major currencies is unrestricted, permitting unfettered profit repatriation to the Bermuda parent. Bermuda's Exchange Control Act 1972 and BMA Notices liberalize current-account and capital-account transactions for Exempted Companies that obtain 'non-resident' status; such entities may hold, convert, and remit foreign currency (USD, EUR, GBP) without prior approval, subject to filing annual declarations. Profit remittance from the POD custom-apparel flow is free of Gibraltar withholding tax, and the Bermuda exempted company pays zero taxation on distributions, leaving CRS/FATCA completeness and the Gibraltar economic-substance test (place of effective management) as the only practical convertibility and dividend-routing hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Neither Gibraltar nor Bermuda imposes any statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds. POD e-commerce sales receipts settled through Stripe and PayPal may be retained indefinitely in original currency (USD, EUR, GBP) or systematically swept into commodity treasury accounts. The only formal reporting obligations are: (i) the BMA non-resident/foreign-currency annual return (Form E or successor filing) for the Bermuda Exempted Company disclosing aggregated foreign-currency holdings, and (ii) contemporaneous documentation of intra-group and shareholder capital transfers exceeding the BMA de-minimis threshold to evidence the exempted-entity exchange-control designation.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar: Government of Gibraltar Treasury (Currency Commissioner) issues Gibraltar Pound (GIP) banknotes under the Currency Notes Act 1934 at full 1:1 parity with GBP; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the supervisory regulator for all banks, e-money institutions, and payment services operating in Gibraltar, with monetary policy effectively anchored by the Bank of England. Bermuda: Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) is the co-applicable home-jurisdiction central bank and exchange-control authority for the Bermuda Exempted Company (BM_EXEMPTED_CO), requiring non-resident designations and periodic filings in respect of capital movements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% (Gibraltar standard corporate income tax on assessable trading profits); 0% (Bermuda Exempted Company tier — Bermuda levies no income tax on exempted companies, and the new 15% Bermuda CIT applies only to MNE groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million, which is not triggered by this SME print-on-demand apparel structure). If the Exempted Co is managed and controlled from Gibraltar, Gibraltar taxes its worldwide profits at 12.5%; if board and control remain in Bermuda and only a Gibraltar branch exists, only Gibraltar-source attributable profits are taxed.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar-resident company or branch to any shareholder, including the Bermuda Exempted Co holding tier; Bermuda also imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions from the Exempted Co to ultimate beneficial owners. The composite repatriation chain is therefore WHT-free at every tier.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar: no statutory Local File/Master File monetary threshold for the SME tier; the arm's-length adjustment power under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 applies to all transactions between connected persons. In practice, prepare and retain a contemporaneous transfer pricing file when the annual value of related-party transactions (intercompany COGS, print-factory fees, IP licence fees, management fees, platform cost recharges) exceeds GBP 1,000,000 per annum. Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required only for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million or more (OECD BEPS Action 13 threshold), which this POD retail SME does not meet. The Bermuda CIT TP rules (mirroring the OECD standard) apply only above the same EUR 750 million group-revenue threshold and are therefore inactive for this structure; nonetheless, the Bermuda annual return requires confirmation that no relevant CIT registration threshold is exceeded.

Economic substance scope: Applicable in both territories. (1) Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations: 'distribution and service centre' is a relevant activity, which captures the print-on-demand retail function (order management, payment settlement, fulfilment coordination and customer logistics). The Gibraltar branch must show physical premises, full-time employees proportionate to the activity, adequate local operating expenditure, and core income-generating activities (contracting with print providers, pricing the POD catalogue, managing Stripe/PayPal settlement) performed in Gibraltar. Failure results in financial penalties, information exchange with HMRC and other jurisdictions, and possible re-characterisation of profits. (2) Bermuda Economic Substance Regime: the Bermuda Exempted Co, as a resident entity carrying on no Bermuda relevant activity (or acting as a pure equity holding company), must file the annual economic substance declaration on time; if it claims pure-holding status, it must evidence that its only assets are shares and that it has an authorised agent and registered office in Bermuda. To avoid a double-presence dispute, allocate operational staff and decision-making to Gibraltar, maintain the Bermuda entity as a passive holding vehicle, and document the split of core income-generating activities in the group TP file.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Suppression of E-commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010: civil penalty up to GBP 3,000 plus double the tax lost; interest at 8% per annum from the due date; criminal conviction for fraudulent evasion carries an unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 3 years. The Commissioner has statutory information powers to compel Stripe, PayPal, and the Bermuda and Gibraltar banks to produce full merchant settlement histories.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced for fraudulent trading: directors and shadow directors of the Bermuda Exempted Co are jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax and penalties under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014; director disqualification for up to 15 years; Gibraltar courts may impose travel bans and asset-freezing orders to secure collection, and the director could face extradition proceedings in cooperation with HMRC if the ultimate beneficial owner is UK-based.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance in Both Territories

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar: best-of-judgment assessment re-characterising the entire worldwide POD profit at 12.5%, plus late-payment interest and penalties for failure to notify chargeability; automatic exchange of information with HMRC under the OECD CRS/CBDR framework. Bermuda: financial penalty of up to BMD 250,000 (USD 250,000) for a resident entity failing the economic substance test, plus deregistration and restoration costs; repeated failure can lead to the entity being struck off, freezing its Stripe and PayPal payouts.

Director personal liability: Directors face strict personal fines (up to BMD 40,000 per director in Bermuda for substance-return failures) and joint and several liability for the taxes of the shell in Gibraltar; in combined cases the court may order disqualification, a travel ban, and personal contribution to the company's tax liabilities for wrongful trading.
