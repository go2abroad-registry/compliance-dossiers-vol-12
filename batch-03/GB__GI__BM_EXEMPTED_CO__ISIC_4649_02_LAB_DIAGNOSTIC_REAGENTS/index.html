Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No 'check-the-box' classification exists in Gibraltar. A Bermuda Exempted Company is not incorporated in Gibraltar and is taxable only if central management and control (CMC) is exercised in Gibraltar or if it carries on a trade in Gibraltar through a permanent establishment (PE). Election may be made to apply the Income Tax Act 2010 transitional rules for foreign-source dividends and disposal gains exemption; hybrid mismatch rules under OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (action 2) apply via domestic anti-avoidance provisions in respect of the UK/Gibraltar corporate intangibles and financing arrangements; no explicit dual consolidated loss election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST in Gibraltar; only selective import excise duties and a proposed Goods and Services Tax (not enacted).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - Gibraltar does not operate a VAT/GST registration threshold; no turnover-based indirect tax registration is required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar does not levy VAT/GST on exports; exported diagnostic reagents are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax regardless of destination, provided the goods leave Gibraltar for a foreign destination and the supplier retains transport evidence. If the Bermuda Exempted Company supplies goods via the UK mainland or from a UK VAT-registered branch, the UK VAT zero-rating at 0% applies to goods exported to non-UK customers (VAT Act 1994 s.30 and VAT Notice 703), including direct exports by seller or indirect exports via freight forwarder; export evidence must be obtained within 3 months of supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (via CHIEF/CDS in UK or locally; GSP/goods declaration in Gibraltar as a non-HMR Customs union member with free circulation arrangement)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing departure from Gibraltar/UK port or airport
  • Commercial Invoice (with Incoterms 2020 and commodity code) and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/TT bank credit evidence confirming settlement in foreign currency within 180 days
  • Certificate of Origin and Certificate of Free Sale for clinical IVD reagents (ISO 13485 / CE marking documentation)

No real-time fiscal clearance, API e-invoicing or digital stamping mandate applies in Gibraltar. Invoices for book-keeping purposes must comply with the Income Tax Act 2010 (record-keeping requirements); UK Making Tax Digital rules do not extend to Gibraltar, although the group may voluntarily adopt electronic invoicing for export efficiency. Swiss/German-style clearance is not applicable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange-control approvals or foreign-investment screening requirements for capital injections, trade settlements, profit distribution, or shareholder loan repayment. A Bermuda Exempted Company holding a Gibraltar bank account is not automatically Gibraltar tax-resident, but the practical remittance hurdle is enhanced AML/KYC verification, source-of-wealth evidence, and the requirement to demonstrate that the entity is not managed and controlled in Gibraltar without formal registration.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or statutory holding window exists in Gibraltar. IVD reagent export receipts may be retained indefinitely in GBP, EUR, or USD and converted at commercial rates when required. The receiving bank may require underlying invoices, packing lists, and bills of lading for reconciliation, but no percentage of proceeds must be converted into Gibraltar pounds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary anchor) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (prudential and foreign-exchange administration); the Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar pound at 1:1 parity with GBP

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low. A Gibraltar-licensed electronic-money institution or licensed payment firm is viable under Financial Services Commission supervision. An offshore UK/EEA digital EMI cannot passport into Gibraltar post-Brexit and is generally usable only for ancillary non-Gibraltar flows, not as a primary clearing bank. Legal constraints include the need for FSC authorisation for regulated payment business, limitations on Gibraltar IBAN issuance by foreign EMIs, and enhanced OFSI sanctions and source-of-funds scrutiny for a Bermuda Exempted Company with no local substance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the verified shareholder bank account to the Gibraltar operational account. The wire narrative must state 'share capital contribution' or 'shareholder loan', and the receiving bank should receive prior notice with the board resolution, allotment or share-register extract, certificate of good standing, and proof of the shareholder's identity. Funds must originate from the exact legal name of the shareholder; third-party wires, cash deposits, and cryptocurrency-linked transfers will be rejected. The bank will require its fragmented purpose-code mapping to be completed accurately on the wire advice and retained in the treasury file.Open-account supplier payments for IVD reagents should be settled via SWIFT MT103, or MT202 COV where a correspondent bank is interposed, using the clearing bank's telegraphic-transfer rail. High-value and conditional orders should use commercial documentary letters of credit under the MT700/MT707 framework, with standby letters of credit via MT760, or documentary collections where the counterparty accepts the documents against payment. SWIFT gpi should be enabled for same-day tracking. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GBP 1,000,000 per controlled transaction or GBP 10,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions requires preparation of a contemporaneous Local File; a qualifying multinational group with consolidated turnover above GBP 750 million requires a Master File and Country-by-Country report.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to distribution and service centre relevant activities, including wholesale distribution of diagnostic reagents. A Bermuda Exempted Company conducting a relevant activity must demonstrate direction and management in Gibraltar, adequate employees, premises, and expenditure in Gibraltar, and must file an annual economic substance report; failure attracts administrative penalties and compulsory strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / false returns

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 200% of understated tax plus interest; criminal fine up to GBP 25,000; publication as a named tax defaulter; immediate banking freeze order.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false returns are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax; they face personal civil recovery, travel bans, disqualification for up to 15 years, and up to 7 years imprisonment.
Transfer pricing mispricing / false documentation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority recomputation of taxable profit under the arm's length principle; penalty of 100% of additional tax, increased to 200% in deliberate cases; interest at 8% and cancellation of loss-offset.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable where the company is insolvent or struck off; personal asset tracing, prohibition from acting as a director in Gibraltar, and criminal prosecution in aggravated cases.
Shell company / economic substance failure

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty up to GBP 100,000 under the Economic Substance Regulations; enforced strike-off of the company; exchange of information with HMRC and Bermuda authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines, public disqualification, travel restrictions, and after strike-off joint liability for company debts and tax liabilities.
AML / wire payment abuse

Statutory sanction: Fines up to GBP 2,000,000 under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; freezing and forfeiture of bank accounts; cancellation of the Gibraltar banking relationship.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly concerned in laundering proceeds are criminally liable; confiscation of assets, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to 14 years.
