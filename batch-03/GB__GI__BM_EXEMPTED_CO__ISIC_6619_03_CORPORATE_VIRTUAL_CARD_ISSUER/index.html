Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar: HM Government of Gibraltar — Income Tax Office (Income Tax Act 2010) and Customs & Excise Division; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi with e-filing through the Income Tax Office electronic returns system. Bermuda (place of incorporation): Office of the Registrar of Companies, Government of Bermuda; official portal: https://www.gov.bm. The Bermuda Income Tax Office does not exist because Bermuda levies no income tax; the Registry and the Department of Social Insurance handle the relevant registrations.

E-government filing portal

1) Bermuda: Incorporate as an Exempted Company under the Companies Act 1981; obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and, if carrying on business in Bermuda, the Certificate of Exemption or a permit from the Registrar of Companies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda has no check-the-box election; an Exempted Company is zero-tax in Bermuda by default. The company may elect a de facto offshore tax-exempt position by keeping central management and control outside Gibraltar and outside Bermuda. If Gibraltar substance rules are satisfied, the company may elect to become Gibraltar tax resident (central management and control in Gibraltar), bringing it into the Gibraltar territorial corporation-tax regime at 12.5% headline rate and enabling treaty-network protection. A non-resident company with only a Gibraltar permanent establishment may elect to file Gibraltar returns limited to PE-attributable profits while retaining Bermuda tax-exempt status. Neither Bermuda nor Gibraltar offers formal hybrid-mismatch or reverse-hybrid elections; UK and EU anti-hybrid rules must be considered if the entity is dual-resident.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Purchase Tax and Customs Duties regime; no VAT, GST or Sales-and-Use Tax applies in Gibraltar or Bermuda. Cross-border financial services of a virtual card issuer are outside the scope of Gibraltar Purchase Tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT/GST currency threshold exists because Gibraltar has no VAT system. Registration with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office is mandatory from the first day of establishing a permanent establishment or Gibraltar-source income (GIP 0 economic threshold). Bermuda exempted companies have no turnover registration threshold; an annual government fee is payable regardless of turnover, and payroll-tax registration is required only when a Bermuda payroll is created. Gibraltar Purchase Tax registration arises when the company imports goods into Gibraltar, which is not expected for pure virtual-card issuing.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

There is no formal VAT zero-rating claim because Gibraltar levies no VAT; the functional equivalent is 'outside the scope of Gibraltar Purchase Tax'. The issuer may treat cross-border virtual-card fees, interchange, scheme fees, FX margins and spend-management fees as outside the scope of Gibraltar Purchase Tax and wholly exempt from Bermuda income tax. Eligibility criteria: (i) the corporate cardholder / client is resident outside Gibraltar and Bermuda; (ii) card settlements and funding flows are routed through non-Gibraltar acquiring banks; (iii) no physical goods or services are supplied to end-users within Gibraltar; (iv) the underlying Mastercard/Visa transactions occur on foreign payment networks; (v) the issuer holds a valid GFSC licence and maintains Bermuda exempted-company status; and (vi) documentary proof of export character is retained for audit. No Purchase-Tax input credit exists to reclaim because financial services are outside the charge.

  • Customs Export Declaration (C88/SAD) / Gibraltar Customs entry evidencing export status where any physical instruments or card stock are moved out of Gibraltar
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (for physical card stock or hardware exports)
  • Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation for Exempted Company
  • Bermuda Certificate of Exemption / Permit (if applicable)
  • Gibraltar Income Tax Office TIN confirmation and GFSC licence certificate
  • Mastercard/Visa network settlement statements and acquiring-merchant journal reports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103/MT202 proving foreign-currency repatriation
  • Executed offshore client agreement and tax invoice marked 'outside the scope of Gibraltar Purchase Tax'

Gibraltar and Bermuda impose no real-time e-invoicing, automated fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API reporting mandate. The issuer must nevertheless retain electronic invoices, card-network settlement files (Mastercard/Visa clearing and billing statements), and SWIFT MT103/MT202 payment records to prove the cross-border nature of supplies. GFSC AML/CFT Record-Keeping Regulations require a tamper-evident audit trail on every virtual-card transaction and periodic reporting to the GFSC; no tax-administration API clearance is required.

A wrongfully claimed zero-tax / outside-scope position exposes the issuer to: (i) Gibraltar Income Tax additional assessments on deemed Gibraltar-source profits, late-payment interest, penalties up to 100% of the tax due, and continuing daily fines for failure to file or cooperate; (ii) Customs & Excise Purchase Tax evasion penalties of up to 200% of evaded tax and criminal imprisonment on conviction; (iii) GFSC regulatory sanctions including fines, licence suspension or revocation, and public censure; and (iv) Bermuda action revoking the Exempted Company certificate, striking off, and forfeiture of limited-liability protection. Retrospective clawback may cover 3 tax years, extended to 6 years for fraudulent or negligent conduct, with re-assessment of all relevant profits and mandatory repayment of any incidental purchase-tax reliefs, exemptions, or credits together with statutory interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates as a special offshore territory within the UK monetary framework; the Gibraltar pound is pegged at parity to GBP sterling. There are no exchange controls imposed by the Bank of England or the GFSC on current account or capital account transactions. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and capital repatriation by non-resident shareholders are unrestricted, subject to (i) solvency and director-attestation requirements under Gibraltar's Companies Act, (ii) final tax clearance or self-assessment compliance for Gibraltar-resident entities, and (iii) standard AML/CFT-driven source-of-funds verification by the relevant clearing bank. The currency convertibility risk is therefore de minimis for United Kingdom and Gibraltar counterparties, but banks will still expect a documented economic-substance and tax-compliance narrative for any Bermuda exempted company structure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion timelines, or repatriation quotas for a Gibraltar-licensed entity. Trade receipts, card settlement proceeds, and commercial transaction revenues may be retained in any convertible currency (GBP, USD, EUR, or other freely tradable currencies). The only obligations are accounting, record-keeping, and regulatory reporting under Gibraltar's anti-money-laundering framework and the Bank of England's International Transactions Reporting System, where applicable; no percentage of export earnings must be converted into Gibraltar pounds or GBP.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for GBP and ultimate overseer of currency stability); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the designated prudential, licensing, and AML/CFT regulator for banks, payment institutions, and electronic money institutions operating in or from Gibraltar; HM Government of Gibraltar Treasury retains the local note-issuance function.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low without a GFSC-regulated local passport or full electronic-money licence. Offshore digital EMIs can support the virtual card programme through programme-management, card-processor, or white-label BIN sponsorship roles, but they cannot lawfully hold consumer or client funds, execute settlement, or clear transactions on behalf of a Gibraltar-licensed virtual card issuer unless themselves licensed or registered under the Gibraltar Financial Services Act 2019 or the UK temporary/permanent financial services permissions regime. A GFSC-regulated e-money institution or payment institution is treated as the safer and more viable structure; pure offshore EMIs are generally restricted to non-clearing ancillary services such as card scheme connectivity, KYC orchestration, and expense-management software.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections should be routed using SWIFT MT103 or MT202/202COV messages from the shareholder's bank to the Gibraltar clearing bank. No exchange-control approval is required in the United Kingdom or Gibraltar for a capital contribution by a non-resident shareholder. The wire instruction must include the corporate name of the Gibraltar entity, a clear payment narrative referencing 'Share Capital / Paid-In Capital Contribution under Gibraltar Companies Act', the economic-purpose code for inward direct investment or intercompany funding, and supporting evidence of the board-resolved capital structure. The Gibraltar bank will also require the standard downstream KYC pack, a certified copy of the capital subscription resolution, and, if the capital exceeds the bank's internal threshold, a source-of-funds questionnaire and solvency confirmation. For tax-complete operations, the inbound transfer should be tagged with the relevant Bank of England ITRS reporting category for equity capital, ensuring reconciliation with the company's statutory share premium and capital reserve accounts.Outbound trade and supplier disbursements are cleared via the ordinary SWIFT network, primarily MT103 for single isolated payments and MT200/202 or MT202COV for interbank and nostro-account settlement. For larger or documented trade transactions, a commercial letter of credit is established by the Gibraltar purchasing company through a UK or Gibraltar bank, issued as SWIFT MT700, with the card-issuing treasury team as the applicant and the overseas supplier as the beneficiary. Settlement currency may be GBP, USD, EUR, or the trade invoice currency; the FX cost is absorbed by the treasury desk. Each disbursement must be supported by the purchase order, commercial invoice, transport or service-provision evidence, and any applicable customs or regulatory declaration, and must pass sanctions screening across UN, HMRC, OFSI, OFAC, and EU/Swiss consolidated lists. For virtual card merchant payouts, settlement is pushed through the card network (Visa Direct, Mastercard Send) onto the merchant's bank account, with the underlying commercial obligation evidenced by a valid card-acceptance agreement and chargeback-capable dispute protocol.Deliverable Over-The-Counter FX forwards in GBP, USD, EUR, and Gibraltar-pound-equivalent denominations with a UK or Gibraltar authorised bank.European-style FX options transacted under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with a regulated counterparty for protection of budgeted FX exposure on card settlement floats.Cross-currency swaps for medium-term balance-sheet hedging of USD-denominated Mastercard/Visa settlement liabilities against GBP-denominated corporate capital.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies without a regulated onshore deliverable market, provided the trade is limited to the notional underlying commercial card settlement and executed for hedging only; no speculative NDF trading is permitted under Gibraltar prudential expectations.

For an accounting period ending on 31 December, the return and tax payment are due no later than 31 December of the following calendar year.
