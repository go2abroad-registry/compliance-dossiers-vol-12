E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda Exempted Company domestic status is tax exemption on foreign-source income; for Gibraltar this classification operates as territorial non-residence unless central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, in which case the Income Tax Act 2010 territorial charge applies at 12.5% on Gibraltar-source income only).

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box or general fiscal-transparency election. Available mechanisms: (a) assessment election for non-resident companies on Gibraltar-source profits; (b) group relief and loss-surrender election under Income Tax Act 2010, ss. 27–28; (c) capital-allowance election treating a Gibraltar permanent establishment as a separate enterprise; (d) transfer-pricing documentation election under the Gibraltar Transfer Pricing Rules 2019; and (e) customs valuation intercompany invoicing election for ship's stores. Hybrid-mismatch rules (ATAD-aligned) are mandatory for the Bermuda hybrid structure and no opt-out is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and the UK VAT system; no VAT, GST or sales-and-use tax is levied. Indirect taxation consists solely of customs import duty (Customs Ordinance 2010 / Import Duties Act 1965) and excise duty on hydrocarbon oils, tobacco, alcohol and vehicles. Marine fuel supplied as ship's stores for international voyages is relieved from import and excise duty.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Gibraltar imposes no VAT-registration threshold. Importers, customs agents and holders of excise-warehoused fuel must obtain an Economic Operator Registration number and the relevant Petroleum Products Storage Licence from HM Customs Gibraltar irrespective of turnover; no de minimis turnover threshold is available under the Import Duties Act 1965.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a VAT concept. The equivalent Gibraltar relief is the duty-free supply of ship's stores under s.21 of the Customs Ordinance 2010 and the Ship's Stores Regulations: marine fuel (crude oil or refined distillates under ISIC 4661) delivered to vessels engaged in international traffic is relieved from import and excise duty. Eligibility requires that (i) the vessel departs Gibraltar on an international voyage; (ii) fuel is physically supplied from a licensed bonded installation or bunker barge; (iii) quantities are consistent with engine capacity and voyage plan; (iv) the buyer is an international carrier or licensed trading house; and (v) the supplier holds a Petroleum Products/Ship's Stores licence. Pure brokerage or back-to-back trading without physical supply into a qualifying vessel is outside the relief, and the broker's commission is subject to Gibraltar income tax at 12.5% where a Gibraltar permanent establishment exists.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API or digital-stamping mandate applies in Gibraltar. Invoices are governed by the Companies Act 2014 (s.447) and electronic-signature regulations; bunker supply invoices must show the supplier's Gibraltar Company Number, Tax Identification Number, unique sequential invoice number, quantity in metric tonnes, and the legend 'Duty-free ship's stores — outside the scope of VAT'. Goods declarations are cleared electronically through the Gibraltar Customs electronic entry system and Authorised Economic Operator (AEO) portal; suppliers in bonded fuel installations must submit monthly stock-and-delivery reports to HM Customs Gibraltar and retain MARPOL-compliant Bunker Delivery Notes (BDN) for cross-match. No mandatory API interface or continuous transaction control is currently in force.

HM Customs Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office jointly audit duty-free bunker claims. Rejected or fraudulently substantiated claims trigger retrospective duty assessment plus a civil penalty of up to three times the duty evaded (Customs Ordinance 2010, ss.106–109); criminal conviction may add up to seven years' imprisonment, forfeiture of fuel and vessel, and public blacklisting of the supplier. The Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit may treat false BDN or customs documentation as money laundering under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, causing asset freezing and confiscation. Separately, non-compliance with the Economic Substance Regulations 2019 for the 'distribution and service centre' activity attracts significant escalating penalties, including a civil penalty equal to 10% of relevant Gibraltar-source income and daily default penalties; serious or persistent breach results in company strike-off and adverse notification to HM Revenue & Customs under the UK–Gibraltar information-sharing arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar is a British Overseas Territory using the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at par with Pound Sterling (GBP). The territory maintains full capital account convertibility for non-resident entities, with no exchange controls restricting current account transactions, dividend distributions, or inward direct investment. Profit remittance requires no prior approval; however, for physical wholesale brokerage of marine fuel oil and distillates, proceeds derived from sanctioned jurisdictions or high-risk counterparties will trigger enhanced due diligence by UK and EU correspondent banks. Current account transactions, including trade settlement, are fully free, while capital account movements involving loans or equity injections are reportable but not administratively pre-authorised. No mandatory repatriation of profits arising from non-resident-owned Bermuda Exempted Companies registered in Gibraltar obtains, provided the entity maintains valid economic substance in Gibraltar for tax residence purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in any currency, and no requirement applies to convert foreign currency into GIP/GBP within a prescribed period. The applicable European Union-derived sanctions regulations, as transposed into Gibraltar law, restrict dealings with listed entities and require freezing and reporting to the GFSC for any funds associated with sanctioned persons. Companies engaged in ISIC 4661 wholesale of crude oil and refined products must verify the underlying trade, retain bills of lading and inspection certificates, and comply with the Gibraltar Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2019 reporting duties for suspicious proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the designated competent authority for financial services; foreign exchange and payments oversight in Gibraltar is exercised under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Bank of England's prudential regulation framework for UK sterling clearing participants.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-high under a restricted corridor. Offshore digital EMI institutions licensed in the EEA (for example, Lithuania or Ireland) can provide GBP and EUR virtual IBANs to a Gibraltar-registered Bermuda Exempted Company only if the EMI maintains a proper agent or branch arrangement and performs full beneficiary owner verification. However, the exposure to marine fuel oil wholesale and high-risk ISIC 4661 energy commodities generally triggers rejection by mainstream EMI onboarding committees. The GFSC does not license pure EMI pass-through structures in Gibraltar itself; payment institutions require a full Financial Services Commission licence under the Financial Services (Payment Services) Act 2019. Legal constraint: the Bermuda Exempted Company carrying on the energy brokerage business must be registered as a Gibraltar 'external company' under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014, and any EMI must be satisfied that the ultimate beneficial owner has a demonstrable physical presence and substance in Gibraltar. For high-volume trade settlements, EMIs are viable only for pre-funding and working-capital float, not for irrevocable documentary credit settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed through the Gibraltar banking system using a standard SWIFT MT103 wire with the underlying purpose code 'GIBRALTAR external company equity contribution' and a clear narrative referencing the GFSC filing reference. The transfer must originate from the ultimate parent's corporate account, never from a personal account of a director, and must cite the corporate documents evidencing the Bermuda Exempted Company's right to receive equity. Any injection above GBP 50,000 will require the receiving Gibraltar bank to complete a source-of-funds form and, where applicable, a CRS declaration. The use of chain-bridging third-party corridors or crypto-fiat on-ramps is prohibited for the capital base. The funds must remain on the balance sheet for a minimum solvency period of six months to satisfy the economic substance rules.Outbound trade disbursements will be cleared through SWIFT MT103 and MT202 for direct payments to suppliers, and through Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary L/C) issued by the Gibraltar branch of a tier-1 European or UK bank for large cargoes. The Gibraltar bank's correspondent banking rail will clear GBP through the Bank of England CHAPS system and EUR through TARGET2. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where controlled transactions with Bermuda affiliates exceed GBP 1,000,000 per annum; Master File mandatory where consolidated group turnover exceeds GBP 10,000,000 or related-party transactions exceed GBP 5,000,000

Economic substance scope: Both Gibraltar Economic Substance Code (2018) and Bermuda Economic Substance Regulations (2019) apply to the exempted company engaged in distribution and service centre activity; it must prove physical office in Gibraltar, full-time qualified staff, binding board meetings in Gibraltar, and all core income-generating activities performed there; failure triggers information exchange with Bermuda and re-characterisation of profit to Bermuda

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 100% of tax evaded; daily default penalties; criminal conviction on indictment with unlimited fine and forfeiture of the commission income

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debt; travel restriction orders and passport surrender; imprisonment up to 7 years
Shell Company and Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Strike-off from the Gibraltar Companies Register after 30 days; loss of statutory license; annual penalty of GBP 1,000 plus daily fine; re-characterisation of residence to Bermuda or UK exposing profits to 12.5%-25% tax

Director personal liability: Disqualification as director for up to 15 years; civil restitution orders for diverted corporate profits; worldwide freezing injunctions over personal bank accounts
AML and Sanctions Violations in Marine Fuel Trading

Statutory sanction: Unlimited financial penalty under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2023; seizure of trading assets, revocation of the Oil Trading Licence, and cancellation of the money service licence

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability for money laundering; imprisonment up to 14 years; travel bans; removal from professional registers; personal confiscation orders for any benefit obtained from the trade
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to arm's length with tax at 12.5% on the upward adjustment plus interest at 8% per annum; penalties of 50%-100% of the additional tax for document failure

Director personal liability: Directors personally assessed where they knowingly signed inaccurate transfer pricing documentation; disqualification orders; personal liability arising where the business continued with a view to tax avoidance
