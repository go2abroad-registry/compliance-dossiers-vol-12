Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar — Commissioner of Income Tax (Income Tax Office, Ministry of Finance) and HM Customs Gibraltar (Customs House); parallel corporate registrar: Gibraltar Companies Registry. Portal: Income Tax Office e-Services accessible via www.gibraltar.gov.gi; customs electronic lodgement via the HM Customs Gibraltar electronic declaration portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — The Bermuda Exempted Company must deliver notarised/certified copies of its Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Exemption, memorandum and articles, and register of directors to the Gibraltar Companies Registry, and file the prescribed foreign-company registration application within one month of establishing a place of business or commencing commercial activity in Gibraltar; a Gibraltar Company Number is then issued. Step 2 — If the company becomes chargeable to Gibraltar income tax (Gibraltar-source trading income, or worldwide income if resident), it must file the Income Tax Office's prescribed registration form to obtain a Gibraltar Income Tax Reference Number; no VAT ID exists because Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST. Step 3 — Before any import/export declaration for prilled urea or potassium chloride is lodged, the company must obtain a Gibraltar customs operator identifier (GI-prefixed EORI-type code) from HM Customs Gibraltar. Step 4 — If central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, the company must notify the Income Tax Office of deemed residence and be assessed at the standard 10% corporate rate; otherwise it is treated as a non-resident foreign company taxed only on Gibraltar-source income arising through a permanent establishment or local trade.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box disregarded-entity election. Available statutory mechanisms include: (1) a residence election by central management and control, effected by board-meeting practice and a formal notification to the Commissioner of Income Tax for certification of residence; (2) a non-resident election limiting assessment to Gibraltar-source income when no permanent establishment is maintained in Gibraltar; (3) economic substance notification/election under the Economic Substance Ordinance 2019 for relevant activities (including distribution and service centre activities) performed by a Gibraltar-resident company; and (4) hybrid mismatch classification election — under the Income Tax (Hybrid Mismatch) Rules, the taxpayer may align the entity's classification with its Bermuda tax-exempt status where no avoidance of Gibraltar tax results. Bermuda-side protection may be secured by the exempted company's tax guarantee certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST — Gibraltar levies customs/import duties under the Customs Ordinance 1934 and the Gibraltar Customs Tariff; selective excise duty applies only to alcohol, tobacco and mineral oils. Prilled urea (HS 3102) and potassium chloride (HS 3104) attract a 0% headline import duty.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT or general sales tax registration threshold applies. Customs registration is mandatory from the first import/export transaction, giving an effective threshold of GIP 0; every trader lodging a customs declaration for fertilizer goods must hold a Gibraltar customs operator identifier (GI EORI-type code) and, for bulk ocean charter, must appoint a licensed customs broker where pre-arrival clearance is required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no export VAT zero-rating mechanism because no VAT exists. Under the customs duty regime, exported fertilizer is outside the scope of import/customs duty, and the relevant relief is duty drawback: a claimant may recover import duty paid on previously imported urea or potash where (i) the goods are re-exported within 24 months of import, (ii) the export is evidenced by a Gibraltar customs export declaration, (iii) the goods are not consumed or materially altered in Gibraltar, and (iv) the claimant is the importer of record. Goods moving through a customs warehouse for direct export enjoy equivalent suspension relief without payment of duty.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic, HM Customs Gibraltar)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial Invoice (specifying prilled urea and/or potassium chloride grade, quantity and unit price)
  • Packing List and Weight Certificate
  • Certificate of Origin (non-preferential; preferential EUR.1/GFX certificate where UK-Gibraltar preferential trade terms are claimed)
  • Bulk charter documentation (mate's receipt, trimming certificate or bill-to-ship report) for ocean cargo

Gibraltar does not impose real-time fiscal e-invoicing, continuous transaction controls, SAF-T reporting, digital stamping, or a mandatory national API clearance regime for domestic B2B invoices. Customs clearance is electronic: HM Customs Gibraltar requires pre-lodgement of the electronic declaration linked to the commercial invoice, packing list and weight certificate for each bulk shipment. Licensed declarants may use batch/API interfaces, but no proprietary e-invoice XML schema or national fiscal clearance database is mandated for sellers.

Fraudulent export zero-rating, drawback or customs-duty claims are prosecuted under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance; penalties include forfeiture of the exported goods, a monetary penalty of up to treble the value of the goods, imprisonment up to two years on indictment, and retrospective clawback of any duty or refund improperly obtained, plus statutory late-payment interest at the Commissioner of Income Tax's prevailing rate. Mis-declaration of HS codes (e.g., misdescribed urea or potash), false inward remittance certificates, or fabricated bill-of-lading documents are additionally referable to the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit as potential money-laundering or false-documentation offences. Voluntary disclosure before commencement of an investigation may mitigate penalties but does not extinguish the clawback obligation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at par to GBP sterling and circulates alongside GBP. There are no exchange controls, no approvals required for inward or outward remittances, and no restrictions on profit repatriation, dividend distribution, or capital returns for a Bermuda Exempted Company banking in Gibraltar. Profit remittance is subject only to anti-money-laundering documentation requirements and to Gibraltar withholding tax protocol; for the exempted company structure, no additional Gibraltar tax clearance is required where entity is not Gibraltar tax resident.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rule exists in Gibraltar. Export receipts from wholesale fertiliser sale of urea and potash may be retained in any freely convertible currency (USD, EUR, GBP, or GIP) without a mandatory conversion ratio or remittance timeline. The only statutory obligations are record-keeping under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and GFSC AML/CFT guidelines, including retention of underlying trade documentation for at least five years.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) (banking and FX regulatory supervisor); Commissioner of Currency, Government of Gibraltar (Gibraltar pound issuing authority); ultimate monetary sovereignty retained by the Bank of England / HM Treasury (UK).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with legal constraints. Offshore digital EMIs licensed in the UK/EEA may onboard the Bermuda Exempted Company via remote KYC and provide GBP/EUR/USD IBANs; however, EMIs cannot issue or confirm commercial letters of credit and are unsuitable for large physical commodity trade settlements. A GFSC-authorised EMI would be viable for low-value pre-export collections, vendor payments, and liquidity pooling, but due to GFSC anti-money-laundering rules the EMI relationship must be disclosed to the primary bank and may trigger enhanced due diligence. Non-EEA/offshore EMIs without a physical EU/UK/Gibraltar licence are materially blocked by GFSC and may not provide payment services into Gibraltar-regulated banking rails.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return and final tax payment are due no later than nine months after the end of the chargeable accounting period, or 30 days after issue of a notice to file if that is later. For a 31 December year-end, the ordinary deadline is 30 September of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Gibraltar Local File is mandatory where the aggregate value of controlled transactions of the Gibraltar tax resident exceeds GBP 1,000,000 in the accounting period. A Master File is required when the worldwide group consolidated turnover exceeds GBP 50,000,000 and the Gibraltar entity has controlled cross-border transactions. The Commissioner may also require contemporaneous documentation at any level where related-party sales have not been benchmarked.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance (International Tax Compliance) Regulations apply. A wholesale fertilizer distribution company engaged in physical chartering, storage, and resale of urea and potash is carrying on the relevant activity of 'distribution and service centre'. The entity must be directed and managed in Gibraltar, have suitable physical premises and employees, perform core income-generating activities in Gibraltar (sourcing, freight negotiation, stock risk, invoicing and payment), and file an annual ESR return. A so-called 'Bermuda Exempted Co' label does not override the Gibraltar regime; the effective place of management and CIGA evidence will control. Failure to satisfy ESR in any period creates a presumption of artificiality and exposes profits to reallocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 100% of the underpaid Gibraltar tax, plus interest and late-filing surcharges. Criminal liability can include fines up to GBP 50,000 and imprisonment up to seven years under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act and Criminal Justice legislation. The Commissioner may also overturn artificial structures and assess profits to the true trading entity.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who cause or perform false returns can be prosecuted. Courts may impose disqualification orders up to 15 years, seizure of travel documents, travel bans, freezing orders, and repatriation of diverted assets. In cross-border cases involving UK or persons resident in the UK, extradition or mutual legal assistance is available.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Failure to satisfy the Economic Substance Regulations attracts escalating penalties: an initial GBP 10,000 penalty, up to GBP 100,000 for continued non-compliance, possible enforced strike-off of the company by the Register of Companies, and reallocation of taxable profit to the jurisdiction of actual management. The company may also lose its tax residence certificate and be exposed to UK CFC attribution if shareholders are UK residents.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for trading after a strike-off order, for false ESR declarations, and for taxes due where the company is a facade. Disqualification cannot be avoided by delegating control to a nominee service company; the shadow director is equally exposed.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar tax authorities may make arm's-length price adjustments, add interest, and impose penalties of up to 100% of the extra tax. A 'significant' uncorrected misstatement can trigger increased penalties and criminal referral. The taxpayer is required within 30 days of notice to produce all TP documentation; failure is independently punishable.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute artificial pricing documentation or sign false CUP comparability statements may be jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax. Professional conduct referrals to chartered accounting bodies and disqualification are standard where a director was the controlling mind.
Anti-Money Laundering / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission may impose civil fines up to GBP 1,000,000 or 10% of turnover for AML failures, suspend or revoke the entity's bank/EMI relationships, and report to the NCA. Criminal money laundering convictions can lead to unlimited fines, confiscation orders, and imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners engaged in trade-based money laundering face individual prosecution, asset freezing, travel bans, passport surrender, and confiscation of the value of the commodity contracts. An order for costs may be entered against directors personally where the corporate is used as an instrumentality.
Failure to File Annual ESR Return and DTA/FATCA Forms

Statutory sanction: Non-filing of the ESR return attracts a penalty up to GBP 200,000, plus daily default fines and possible loss of the tax return submission right. The Commissioner can issue a default assessment, revoke authorisations, initiate information-gathering searches, and require production of shipment and banking records.

Director personal liability: The court may order personal liability for the company's unpaid fines if the default was caused by gross negligence on the part of the directors. Travel bans are routinely issued where a director leaves Gibraltar while taxes, ESR penalties, or confiscation liabilities remain outstanding.
