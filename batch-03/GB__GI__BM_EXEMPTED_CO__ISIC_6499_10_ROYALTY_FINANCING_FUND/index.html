Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar, Income Tax Office — official portal: MyTaxGIB (https://tax.gibraltar.gov.gi) and Gibraltar Companies Registry (https://www.gibraltarcompaniesregistry.gi)

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company seeking Gibraltar tax identity must first register as a foreign company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry (CR number acts as the corporate TIN). Thereafter, file Form PA1 (Application for Taxpayer Identification) with the Income Tax Office via MyTaxGIB. If the company is managed and controlled from Gibraltar, it will be treated as Gibraltar tax resident and issued a TIN; if not resident, a TIN is still issued for Gibraltar-source income reporting. No VAT ID is issued because Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT territory.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box election regime. Under the Income Tax Act 2010, a foreign company may elect to be assessed on a territorial basis if treated as non-resident; hybrid mismatch alignment elections are available under the Taxation (Hybrid Mismatch) Regulations 2017. For a Bermuda Exempted Company, the default classification remains tax-exempt in Bermuda, but Gibraltar tax residence follows central management and control; no special Gibraltar fund election abolishes the need for substance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Not_Applicable_No_VAT_GST_Sales_and_Use_Tax

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Nil in Gibraltar pounds (GIP). Gibraltar imposes no VAT or GST and therefore no economic turnover threshold for VAT registration. Customs import duty obligations arise on the physical importation of goods regardless of turnover; an EORI number is mandatory for customs declarations, but is not turnover-based.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

No VAT/GST zero-rating claim is possible under Gibraltar law because Gibraltar does not levy VAT or GST. Outbound royalty-financing, revenue-based financing, and associated fund services are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax. For physical goods moving through Gibraltar, relief may be available only for goods in transit/warehousing under the Customs Ordinance on a suspension basis, not as a refundable export zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD or ASYCUDA entry)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or FX confirmation for repatriated proceeds

Gibraltar has no real-time fiscal invoicing or digital stamping mandate. Tax returns and company filings are submitted digitally through MyTaxGIB and the Companies Registry e-portal. Customs entries are cleared through the ASYCUDA World electronic declaration system with real-time risk profiling; regulated financial transactions are separately reported under Gibraltar AML regulations through MLROS.

Although no VAT zero-rating exists, fraudulent claims to customs or tax exemption attract clawback of any excise/import duty relieved, fines up to £3,000 per offence, interest at 36% per annum applied by the Collector, and imprisonment of up to seven years under the Income Tax Act 2010 and Criminal Offences (Money Laundering) law. Where a fund misrepresents its Gibraltar tax-exempt status, the Income Tax Officer may retrospectively revoke the tax exemption and assess tax for up to six prior years together with penalty surcharges.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar has no exchange-control mechanism and no material convertibility restriction. The Gibraltar pound is fully convertible at a 1:1 peg to sterling, and both current-account flows (royalty income, financing fees, operating costs) and capital-account flows (equity subscription, shareholder loans, intercompany advances, profit distributions) may be converted and remitted freely. Profit remittance hurdles are non-FX in nature: the principal challenges are demonstrated economic substance, AML source-of-funds validation, sanctions screening, CRS/tax reporting, and arm's-length pricing of the royalty-financing arrangements. A Bermuda Exempted Company should maintain clear central-management-and-control evidence in Gibraltar to prevent adverse tax and substance challenges.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or fixed conversion deadlines in Gibraltar or the United Kingdom. Export and trade receipts may be retained in the original currency and repatriated at the treasury's discretion. Banks may be required to report balance-of-payments statistics for large cross-border receipts, and proceeds originating from high-risk or non-convertible jurisdictions require enhanced due diligence.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England as the sterling monetary authority; Government of Gibraltar as issuer of the Gibraltar pound tied at parity to GBP; Gibraltar Financial Services Commission as the financial conduct and AML/CFT supervisory authority; HM Treasury as the United Kingdom sanctions authority applicable to Gibraltar-linked sterling clearing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium, but only as an auxiliary collection and disbursement layer. A non-Gibraltar digital EMI or e-money institution, including a UK FCA-authorised EMI, may provide GBP and EUR virtual IBANs, open-banking collection rails, and direct-debit initiation; however, it is not a clearing participant in CHAPS, RTGS, or the Gibraltar sterling clearing network and cannot serve as the sole principal settlement bank for fund capital. Legal constraints include GFSC expectations that regulated treasury activity maintain a substantive banking relationship with a licensed Gibraltar bank, safeguarding rules for e-money funds, limits on onward payment risk, and sanctions-screening obligations. Use of an offshore EMI is therefore limited to ancillary receivables collection with daily sweeps to a Tier 1 Gibraltar bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File are required where the value of cross-border controlled transactions exceeds GBP 1,000,000 in a fiscal year; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's Economic Substance (Company and Branches) Regulations 2020 impose a real-presence test on 'relevant activities', including finance and leasing and fund management. The royalty financing fund must demonstrate: (a) adequate physical offices and employees in Gibraltar; (b) locally conducted core income-generating activities; (c) all board meetings and strategic decisions made in Gibraltar. Failure to meet these tests, or omission of the annual ES return, exposes the entity to sanctions up to GBP 10,000 plus daily default fines and possible strike-off; the Bermuda Exempted Company cannot rely on its Bermuda tax-exempt status to avoid Gibraltar ESR obligations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: A penalty of 100% of the under-documented tax plus interest at 8% per annum; criminal prosecution under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act and Proceeds of Crime Act; fine up to GBP 50,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years for responsible officers; forfeiture of the underlying assets.

Director personal liability: Directors may personally owe civil restitution for the avoided tax; are subject to travel bans preventing attendance at board meetings abroad; disqualification as director for up to 15 years in the UK/Gibraltar; extradition to UK courts where the conduct affects UK tax residence; custodial sentence for fraud and money laundering.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations and Companies Act 2014, regulatory fines up to GBP 25,000; daily default fine of GBP 1,000 for continued non-compliance; compulsory strike-off of the overseas branch and loss of Gibraltar trading rights; the company may be placed on the GFIU public warning list.

Director personal liability: Directors are liable for the unpaid penalties personally; a compliance failure may be treated as an unwarranted defense of the corporate veil, allowing Gibraltar courts to pierce the veil and order personal asset confiscation; travel bans and prohibition of re-entry are available where the director personally facilitated the shell structure.
AML and Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act (Gibraltar) and the Financial Services Ordinance, failure to file a suspicious activity report carries criminal liability with fines not limited in value and imprisonment up to 5 years; civil penalties of up to GBP 5 million from the GFIU; freezing orders and forfeiture of any monies linked to the violation.

Director personal liability: Officers who knowingly process illicit payments or fail to implement an AML program may be criminally convicted personally, disgraced, and barred from any Gibraltar financial administration; travel bans, extradition requests, and personal civil liability for investor losses can be enforced in UK courts; the corporate indemnity clause does not protect acting directors.
