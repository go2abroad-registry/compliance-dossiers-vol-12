Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office is permissible only for the statutory registered office address provided a fully licensed Gibraltar corporate service provider (registered agent) maintains a physical premises at which service of process can be effected and statutory registers are held. For a pure e-commerce operator under ISIC 4791, a virtual address is insufficient to establish Gibraltar tax residence or to satisfy economic substance expectations; a physical presence (leased or serviced office with locally engaged staff) is required for residence claims and for Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations reporting. The company may lawfully trade as a non-resident exempt company without active local operations, in which case the virtual registered office is acceptable but tax-exempt status may be conditioned on foreign control and foreign-source income.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar, 24–26 Main Street, Gibraltar; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on activities in Gibraltar or establishing a branch/place of business must apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN). Steps: (1) file Form A01 – Company Tax Registration, (2) provide certified charter/constitional documents, Gibraltar Companies House certificate of incorporation, and registered office details, (3) appoint a Gibraltar resident agent or tax representative when the entity is not managed and controlled in Gibraltar, (4) state the intended activity and confirm whether any Gibraltar-source income will arise. The office issues the TRN within approximately 10 working days. Because Gibraltar does not operate VAT, no VAT ID is issued or required; separate registration with HM Customs of Gibraltar is triggered only for import/export, warehouse, or excise activity.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (offshore holding / non-managed-and-controlled activity), with reversion to Entity_Level_Taxation at 12.5% for profits attributable to a Gibraltar trade or permanent establishment; Territorial_Scope applies to non-resident entities. — A foreign entity carrying on activities in Gibraltar or establishing a branch/place of business must apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (offshore holding / non-managed-and-controlled activity), with reversion to Entity_Level_Taxation at 12.5% for profits attributable to a Gibraltar trade or permanent establishment; Territorial_Scope applies to non-resident entities.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not recognise check-the-box elections. Statutory elections include: (1) group relief election under the Income Tax Act for qualifying corporations to surrender losses, (2) consortium relief election, (3) election for a dual-resident company to be treated as non-Gibraltar resident for double taxation agreement purposes, and (4) hybrid mismatch beneficial-rule election subject to OECD BEPS Action 2 rules as incorporated in Gibraltar law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST – Gibraltar imposes no value-added tax or goods and services tax; indirect taxation is limited to customs duties, excise duties, and the 'Rate' tax on property occupation.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A – no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. Any economic turnover arising in Gibraltar is outside indirect tax registration; however, import/export activity requires customs registration under the Gibraltar Customs Tariff Act regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable under Gibraltar law. Since Gibraltar operates no VAT/GST, supplies of goods or offshore services are not 'zero-rated'. Exported goods are outside the scope of Gibraltar taxation but do not carry any refundable VAT credit. When selling to EU/UK customers, the entity may be subject to the indirect tax of the destination jurisdiction under distance-selling rules; substantiation for such foreign VAT/GST purposes must follow the relevant foreign evidentiary standards.

  • Customs Export Declaration (C88/SAD or Gibraltar Customs Entry Form)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Consignment Note evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/FX credit evidence of export proceeds repatriation

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or SAF-T mandate. Invoices may be issued in paper or electronic form without government pre-clearance. Customs declarations are filed electronically through the Gibraltar Customs Declarations Service, with software-to-government API integration available for licensed agents; no unique invoice digital signature is required.

Fraudulent export declarations or falsified documentation are prosecuted under Gibraltar customs law, with penalties up to 200% of duty evaded, forfeiture of goods, and criminal fines. Misrepresentation in tax filings may additionally trigger penalties under the Income Tax Act (up to 150% of tax understated) and imprisonment for up to 7 years. Retrospective clawback assessments may be extended to 6 years in cases of fraud, negligence, or deliberate error.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar has full current-account and capital-account convertibility for non-resident entities, subject only to GFSC anti-money-laundering and financial-sanctions screening. The Gibraltar pound is freely convertible into GBP at par and into other major currencies without offshore exchange-control restriction. Profit remittance from a Gibraltar-licensed bank account to any jurisdiction is permitted without prior governmental authorization, but the transferring bank will require a valid tax residence certificate, a substance declaration for the Bermuda Exempted Company, and documentary proof of source of funds (bonds, paid-in capital legalization, or dividend resolutions). The absence of exchange controls is balanced by UK anti-avoidance rules (CFC) which do not convert into any currency convertibility barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar prescribes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts, including escrow proceeds generated by online maternity and infant-care e-commerce. Export proceeds may be retained in the original invoice currency. However, all inbound export settlements must be traceable through a licensed Gibraltar- or UK-regulated bank account; the GFSC expects settlement documentation to be retained for five years. For banking-AML purposes, the receiving bank will internally impute a 180-day repatriation or matching-payment cycle; no percentage-based conversion is applied.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC); monetary authority functions for the Gibraltar pound (pegged 1:1 to GBP) are exercised by the Government of Gibraltar's Financial Secretary, with sterling backing held through HM Treasury (UK). No distinct central bank is chartered in Gibraltar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High. Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut, Payoneer, and MultiPass are accepted as non-banking settlement payrails for the underlying Stripe/PayPal e-commerce flows, but not as primary treasury clearing counterparts. A Bermuda Exempted Company can open an EMI wallet where the EMI is licensed in the UK or Gibraltar and subject to FCA/GFSC AML supervision. Legal constraints: Gibraltar-licensed EMIs cannot provide proprietary cross-border FX or lending facilities without additional GFSC licensing. Direct acceptance of offshore EMIs from jurisdictions without an adequate anti-money-laundering framework will be blocked. Feasibility is conditional on proof of beneficial ownership, business-site information, and demonstrated transactional history from the regulated e-commerce platforms.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions and shareholder loans from UK or Gibraltar investors are routed via SWIFT MT103 with Field 50K properly identifying the remitter and Field 72 referencing 'INBOUND CAPITAL INJECTION – EXEMPTED CO'. Where the receiving bank operates a purpose-code framework, use ISO 20022 remittance code 'INVS' (Investment) or Gibraltar domestic banking code 'CAP' for capital transfer. Pre-transfer verification requires: (a) board resolution authorising the contribution; (b) deposit account agreement; (c) source-of-funds statement for the ultimate funder; (d) confirmation from a UK passport-based solicitor if the injection is from a UK corporate; (e) trade-receipt forecast showing capital usage. Any currency conversion from GBP/EUR to USD or vice versa is executed through the receiving bank's prevailing market FX rate, with no mandatory surrender.Outbound payments to verified baby-supply wholesalers and third-party logistics providers settle via SWIFT MT103 for cross-border supplier invoices and via Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) for high-value bulk importations from Asia. For UK-based suppliers, CHAPS (for same-day sterling clearing) and BACS (for low-value payments) are permitted; Gibraltar banks additionally use the Target2? (Gibraltar is not in SEPA, but UK-specific rails suffice). The standard payment protocol maps each supplier to a payment instruction with unique remittance references, freight document matching, and anti-fraud verification by the commercial bank.
