Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Bermuda (home): Government of Bermuda, Ministry of Finance — Office of the Registrar of Companies (no general income-tax registry because Bermuda levies no corporate income tax). Gibraltar (operating jurisdiction): HM Government of Gibraltar — Income Tax Office, Customs and Income Tax Department, Commissioner of Income Tax; official e-portal: Gibraltar Income Tax e-Services (TaxDirect) via gibraltar.gov.gi.

        
E-government filing portal
A Bermuda Exempted Company (Bermuda Exempted Co) carrying on vertical micro-drama streaming distribution through Gibraltar must: (1) be incorporated in Bermuda as an exempted company, obtaining a Certificate of Exemption and company registration number from the Bermuda Registrar of Companies, and file annual government fee and economic substance declarations (Bermuda does not issue a VAT/TIN because it has no income tax; the new Bermuda corporate income tax at 15% under OECD Pillar Two applies only to multinational groups with EU 750-million consolidated revenue, which the micro-drama vertical does not reach); (2) if commercially established in Gibraltar, register as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act (file certified Bermuda incorporation/charter documents, registered office details, director and shareholder registers, local address and representative, and pay registration fees); (3) apply to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office for a Tax Identification Number (TIN / GI number) using the prescribed non-resident company registration form, providing certification of the Bermuda certificate, memorandum and articles, proof of appointment of a local agent/tax representative, and declaration of intended Gibraltar-source activities; (4) register for Gibraltar PAYE and social insurance if staff are engaged and for import-duty accounts if physical goods are imported; no VAT registration is possible or required in Gibraltar because the jurisdiction is outside the UK/EU VAT territory.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Tax_Exempt_Zone

        
Available election forms
Available elections and safeguards include: (i) Bermuda statutory tax protection election evidenced by a Certificate of Exemption under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, guaranteeing exemption from Bermuda income/capital-gains tax on streaming revenue until 2035 (subject to substance and fee compliance); (ii) US federal check-the-box election on IRS Form 8832 to treat the Bermuda Exempted Co as either a corporation or a disregarded entity for US tax purposes (default class for a limited-liability foreign eligible entity is a corporation); (iii) Gibraltar territorial-source election — as a non-resident company, chargeability is confined to profits accruing in or derived from Gibraltar, so foreign streaming revenue may be excluded upon contemporaneous documentation; (iv) where treaty or UK/EU VAT obligations arise, no Gibraltar VAT election exists (no VAT system), but a non-established supplier may elect to register under UK VAT OSS or EU non-union OSS to account for VAT on B2C digital services; and (v) hybrid mismatch elections (fiscal-transparent or deemed-PE elections) may be filed under OECD BEPS Action 2 if the entity is treated as transparent in any counterparty state.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
No VAT or GST in Gibraltar; Gibraltar is outside the UK and EU VAT territories. The only indirect taxes are selective import customs duties and excise on physical goods. Vertically distributed digital micro-drama streaming subscriptions are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax.

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Not applicable — Gibraltar has no VAT/GST, so there is no Gibraltar registration threshold or local-currency turnover trigger. For connected inbound markets: UK VAT registration may be required for a non-established supplier selling digital services to UK consumers (no statutory GBP 90,000 threshold applies to non-established suppliers; the GBP 90,000 threshold refers to UK-established taxable persons); EU non-union OSS registration is required for B2C electronic services to EU consumers (the EUR 10,000 per-calendar-year threshold applies only to EU-established suppliers, not to this Bermuda/Gibraltar entity); no threshold applies in the US given the use of app-store distribution with sales/use tax administered by marketplace facilitators.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating claims are structurally not applicable in Gibraltar because Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST and Bermuda imposes no VAT or income tax on the exempted company. Streaming subscriptions to vertical micro-dramas are electronically supplied services; any UK VAT exposure (if the entity registers as a non-established supplier) arises at the customer's place of supply (UK standard rate 20%), and the services cannot be zero-rated merely because the distributor is offshore. For physical goods only, export zero-rating under VATA 1994 and Gibraltar customs practices is available if goods are removed from the UK/Gibraltar and evidence of export is retained within prescribed time limits.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
GIP is fully convertible into GBP at a fixed 1:1 parity and, through GBP, is freely convertible into USD, EUR, and CNH without exchange-control approval. The UK/Gibraltar combined regime applies no current-account or capital-account restrictions, permitting unfettered profit repatriation, dividend declarations, IP royalty payments, intercompany loan repayments, and shareholder distributions from the Gibraltar branch to the Bermuda parent and onward to non-resident shareholders. No Gibraltar withholding tax applies to outbound dividends, interest, royalties, or rental payments made by a Gibraltar-resident company to non-resident payees, and the Bermuda exempted company benefits from a zero corporate tax rate in Bermuda subject to Economic Substance Act 2018 filing obligations. The only frictions are documentary: banks will require CRS/FATCA self-certifications (Form W-8BEN-E and CRS tax-residency declarations) and a commercially rational explanation of the Bermuda entity's role as the IP/financing holding vehicle to prevent re-characterisation of the structure as tax evasion or aggressive avoidance.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
There are no statutory timelines and no mandatory conversion ratios for export proceeds in either Gibraltar or Bermuda. A Gibraltar-resident branch or merchant subsidiary may retain 100% of foreign-currency export earnings (USD, EUR, CNH) in non-GIP bank accounts, may convert any amount to GIP/GBP at the fixed 1:1 parity, and may deploy proceeds offshore without a domestic foreign-exchange market settlement requirement. Bermuda likewise imposes no surrender rules on USD receipts of an exempted company, provided the BMA receives statistical notification where Bermuda-dollar currency is involved. This enables the treasury to retain USD revenue for production and acquisition costs without forced conversion losses.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for the Gibraltar Pound under the Currency Notes Act 1934 as applied to Gibraltar, regulating issuance and redemption at fixed 1:1 parity with GBP); HM Government of Gibraltar (sole issuer of GIP banknotes and coins); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as prudential supervisor and competent authority for Gibraltar-licensed banks under the Financial Services Act 2019 (Gibraltar). For Bermuda-headquartered treasury activities, the Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) administers exchange-control permissions only for Bermuda-dollar transactions and imposes no restriction on free use of USD/GBP/EUR in external accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        The initial USD 50,000–100,000 shareholder injection (equity or subordinated shareholder loan) is routed from the UBO's Hong Kong or Singapore account to the Gibraltar bank's USD correspondent (typically Bank of New York Mellon, JPMorgan Chase, or Citibank New York) via SWIFT MT103. Field 70/Remittance Information must state 'CAPITAL INJECTION - BERMUDA EXEMPTED CO - GIBRALTAR BRANCH' and be accompanied by the board resolution for capital increase, the Bermuda Director's Certificate, the Bermuda share allotment form, and source-of-funds bank statements. No Gibraltar exchange-control approval is required, but the receiving bank must file a currency-transaction report to the GFSC for deposits above EUR 10,000, and the Gibraltar branch must file its Annual Return under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 if registered as a branch of an overseas company; the proceeds may be held in the USD Capital Escrow Account until the bank confirms the commercial purpose and CRS/FATCA classification.Production and distribution payments settle via (i) SWIFT MT103 for USD/GBP supplier payments above GBP 3,000; (ii) SEPA Instant or SEPA Credit Transfer for EU-based post-production houses using IBANs; (iii) UK Faster Payments or CHAPS for UK advertising agencies and staff payroll; (iv) Commercial Letters of Credit under ICC UCP600 for China-studio production commitments above USD 250,000, opened by the Gibraltar bank for the Bermuda parent with the studio's confirming bank (e.g. Bank of China, Hong Kong branch) receiving MT700; and (v) Hong Kong CHATS and the Offshore RMB clearing system (CNH) for settlements with contracted Hong Kong distribution partners. Because Apple IAP, Google Play Billing, and Stripe pay only to a bank account in the merchant country, a UK-registered overseas-company branch (Companies House Part 34) must maintain a GBP/USD settlement account at a UK clearing bank (e.g. Barclays UK, HSBC UK, or Mettle/Lighthouse EMI for volumes below USD 100,000) to receive the payout wires; the Gibraltar bank then consolidates balances nightly for FX conversion and investment. All outbound payments must carry a valid purpose code (e.g. ROY for royalties, LIC for licences, PUR for production) and pass the bank's sanctions screening against OFAC, UK OPSI, and consolidated EU lists.Outright deliverable FX forward contracts (USD/GBP, USD/EUR, USD/CNH) with tenors up to 12 months offered by Gibraltar-licensed credit institutionsOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on USD/CNY executed with an authorised NDF market-maker in Hong Kong or London to hedge legacy renminbi exposure on China studio disbursementsFX swaps (USD/GBP, EUR/GBP) for rolling operating liquidity without creating open currency positionsCross-currency swaps (USD/CNH, USD/GBP) for matched funding of production loans or commercial-letter-of-credit exposureVanilla FX call and put options (EUR/USD, USD/CNH) purchased from tier-1 bank counterparties to cap adverse currency movement without margin-call exposure
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: 2/5 for the Bermuda exempted company alone; 4/5 for a Gibraltar-registered branch or subsidiary. Legal constraints: (i) Apple In-App Purchase, Google Play Billing, and Stripe require payout to a real bank account in the merchant-company's country of registration or tax nexus; virtual IBANs issued by fintech EMIs are not accepted by Apple or Google treasury operations; (ii) UK-authorised EMIs (Wise, Airwallex, Currenxie, MultiPass) generally decline pure Bermuda exempted companies lacking a UK Companies House overseas-company branch registration, a UK VAT registration, or a CRS/FATCA category acceptable to their acquiring banks; (iii) EMIs remain feasible only for operational disbursements below USD 250,000 per month (cloud hosting, programmatic ad buying, distributed cast and creator payroll) and must be capped as concentration-risk counterparties in the treasury policy; (iv) Card-acquiring EMI rails cannot be used to settle Apple/Google trade funds due to platform-imposed settlement restrictions.
