Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Office of the Commissioner of Income Tax), 6-10 Bayside Road, Gibraltar; VAT and customs administration vested in HM Customs Gibraltar. Primary digital portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax; VAT e-filing and trade declarations are lodged through HM Customs Gibraltar e-services and the Gibraltar Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Register the Bermuda Exempted Company with the Companies Registry under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 (redomiciliation under s. 341 or registration as an overseas company under s. 330) to obtain the registered company number and certificate of incorporation. Step 2 - Submit the Income Tax Office company registration application (Company TRN form) with certified constitutional documents, registered office consent, and director/shareholder particulars to receive the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN). Step 4 - Obtain the Bunkering Operator's Licence (or port service licence) from the Gibraltar Port Authority under the Merchant Shipping (Port Services) Act before undertaking ship refuelling, and produce the licence to HM Customs Gibraltar for activation of the VAT account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - A Bermuda Exempted Company whose central management and control are exercised outside Gibraltar is not resident in Gibraltar and its foreign-source and offshore bunkering income falls outside the charge to Gibraltar income tax on the territorial basis. If management and control are exercised in Gibraltar, the entity becomes Gibraltar tax resident but remains taxable only on territorial profits and may elect into the fixed 10-year Qualifying Company regime at a 2% flat rate. The exempt status is conditioned on compliance with the Gibraltar Economic Substance (Companies and Foreign Tax Credit) Regulations 2020, under which vessel refuelling and shipping support are relevant activities requiring substantive presence (board meetings, premises, staff, and core income-generating activities) in Gibraltar or a proportionality analysis for foreign operations.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box classification; the relevant statutory elections are: (i) election for Qualifying Company treatment under the Income Tax (Qualifying Companies) Regulations 2011, providing a fixed 10-year flat 2% charge on qualifying territorial profits, with an option to revoke and to re-elect on expiry; (ii) election under the Income Tax (Exemptions) Regulations for a non-resident company to be treated as not carrying on a trade in Gibraltar; (iii) hybrid mismatch elections and disclosures under the Income Tax (Hybrid Mismatch) Regulations, including alignment of the Bermuda Exempted Company against UK CFC and US check-the-box counterparty classifications; and (iv) for a qualifying ship operator, election into the tonnage tax / shipping profits regime, which may extend to ancillary bunkering income where the vessel operations are managed and controlled in Gibraltar.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 100,000 annual taxable turnover for suppliers established in Gibraltar; a foreign entity not established in Gibraltar that makes taxable supplies in Gibraltar is registrable from the first supply regardless of turnover, subject to the place-of-supply rules and the B2B reverse charge where applicable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Supplies of marine fuel, lubricants, and ships' stores for the taking on board of a qualifying ship engaged in international voyages (a commercial voyage with a port of destination or operational leg outside Gibraltar territorial waters) are zero-rated under the Gibraltar VAT Act 2010, including warships and other vessels used for international freight or passenger transport. The zero rate extends to the supply of fuel by a bonded marine fuel supplier to the vessel owner or operator, provided the supplier retains the vessel itinerary, IMO registry extract, signed BDN, and payment evidence. The supply of bunkers to vessels engaged solely in domestic Gibraltar navigation or to pleasure craft not proceeding on an international voyage is standard-rated at 15%; supplies of fuel delivered outside Gibraltar territorial waters by a non-established supplier are outside the scope of Gibraltar VAT.

  • Customs or voyage export declaration filed through the Gibraltar Trade Single Window confirming quantity, grade, and destination of the bunker stem
  • Bunker Delivery Note (BDN) compliant with MARPOL Annex VI (vessel IMO number, fuel grades, quantity, supplier and buyer details)
  • Bill of Lading or, for operational bunkering, a Gibraltar Port Authority arrival/departure log extract confirming the international voyage
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103/MT202 evidencing receipt of sale proceeds in foreign currency

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, SAF-T reporting, state-monitored e-invoicing, or digital stamping. VAT returns are filed electronically through the HM Customs Gibraltar online VAT portal on a quarterly basis. Marine fuel imports and exports are declared electronically through the Gibraltar Trade Single Window (customs declaration platform within the UK-Gibraltar customs union), and vessel movements are lodged with the Gibraltar Port Authority. Bunker suppliers must issue a MARPOL Annex VI-compliant Bunker Delivery Note (BDN) for each stem and retain all invoices, voyage logs, tank soundings, and delivery records in digital form for at least six years.

HM Customs Gibraltar may deny zero-rating where the exporter cannot produce the complete documentary bundle, subject only to the 'reasonable excuse' mitigation. Wrongful zero-rating gives rise to a misdeclaration penalty of up to 15% of the net VAT turned over, increasing to 100% for deliberate inaccuracies or suppression of turnover; fraudulent evasion of VAT is a criminal offence punishable by fines of up to GIP 50,000 per invoice plus imprisonment, and interest accrues on all late or clawed-back VAT. HM Customs Gibraltar may issue retrospective assessments for up to 4 years, extended to 20 years in cases of deliberate fraud or concealment, enabling full recovery of wrongly claimed zero-rating credits and input VAT reversals. The Income Tax Office may also reopen the territorial-exempt status on misrepresentation of management-and-control facts, and the Economic Substance Regulations 2020 allow the imposition of daily penalties, loss of exempt status, and assessment at the prevailing corporate income tax rate on the gross profits of the relevant bunkering activity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates an open, fully convertible currency system integrated with the United Kingdom. No exchange control restrictions apply to current account transactions, including payments for marine fuel bunkering, port services, or lubricant replenishment. Capital account repatriation to Bermuda exempted company shareholders is freely permitted, subject to Gibraltar Companies Act solvency and distribution requirements, GFSC anti-money laundering source-of-funds verification, and standard bank onward-payment due diligence. No Gibraltar withholding taxes are imposed on dividends or interest paid to non-resident beneficial owners; however, the receiving Bermuda exempted company should maintain substance and banking records demonstrating that no Gibraltar-source trading income is generated other than through the locally licensed operation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or enforced repatriation timeline applies to export proceeds earned by a Gibraltar-incorporated operator from offshore ship bunkering and refueling activities. Receipts may be held in USD, EUR, or GBP according to commercial settlement preferences. A licensed bunkering entity should nevertheless maintain a documented treasury policy for liquidity cover and ensure foreign currency receivables are credited to a segregated operational account for audit transparency and sanctions compliance.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England as the ultimate monetary authority for the British Overseas Territory of Gibraltar; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the conduct and prudential regulator; HM Treasury retains residual exchange control competence. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued by the Government of Gibraltar through the Currency Notes Act 1934 and is fixed at 1:1 to pound sterling.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for GFSC-authorized e-money institutions licensed under the Financial Services Act 2019, and Low for offshore digital EMI providers not passported or separately licensed by the GFSC. A Bermuda exempted company acting as shareholder of a Gibraltar bunkering operator may use a GFSC-authorized EMI as a payment gateway for low-value disbursements, but direct cross-border onboarding of a digital EMI from outside Gibraltar is legally constrained by the requirement for an EU/EEA passport, which no longer applies to UK-licensed EMIs post-Brexit. The primary operating bank must remain a licensed Gibraltar bank because the bunkering license requires statutory settlement of port fees and customs obligations through a GFSC-supervised institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound funding from the Bermuda exempted company to the Gibraltar operating account should be executed by SWIFT MT103 wire with the transaction type 'TRF' and a narration of 'Intercompany Capital Injection / Shareholder Funding'. The purpose code should reference 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital' under the UK BOP/ION coding convention; for internal treasury transfers, the ISO 20022 structured remittance must contain 'INTCAP' or 'INTERCO-FUND'. The receiving Gibraltar bank will require the underlying board resolutions, the latest Bermuda balance sheet, and confirmation that the sending bank account is in the name of the Bermuda exempted company. Domestic operational payments in GIP/GBP can be cleared through the UK Faster Payments or CHAPS rails via the Gibraltar bank's UK sorting code connectivity. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for the Bermuda Exempted Company as registered in Bermuda; 12.5% Gibraltar Corporation Tax if central management and control / POEM is in Gibraltar or on Gibraltar-source profits attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividends; Bermuda also has no dividend WHT. Distributions by the Bermuda Exempted Company are payable gross without any Gibraltar or Bermuda deduction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar transfer-pricing rules apply to all cross-border controlled transactions; the Income Tax Office may require contemporaneous documentation where aggregate related-party turnover exceeds GBP 1,000,000 per accounting period or where a specific notice is issued. No formal local-file/master-file monetary safe harbour exists; MNE groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750m must file CbC notifications.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar ESR under the Income Tax Act 2010 applies to all tax-resident entities carrying relevant activities. Ship bunkering falls under 'Shipping' and ancillary 'Distribution and Service Centre'. The company must demonstrate core income-generating activities in Gibraltar: local dispatch decisions, fuel procurement, vessel scheduling, invoicing, adequate premises, full-time local staff and proportionate operating expenditure. An annual ESR self-declaration is required. The Bermuda Exempted Company must equally comply with Bermuda's Economic Substance Act 2018, which permits reliance on Gibraltar as a 'suitable foreign jurisdiction' only where real presence and staff exist there.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 100% of the tax understated plus statutory interest; criminal prosecution with unlimited fines and imprisonment up to 7 years under the Income Tax Act 2010 and the Criminal Code for fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors personally liable for fraudulent trading under s.232 Bermuda Companies Act 1981; in Gibraltar, directors signing false computations may face criminal conviction, civil restitution orders, and personal assessment in liquidation.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar ESR penalties up to GBP 100,000 plus daily default penalties up to GBP 5,000; strike-off from the Companies Registry; denial of tax residence certificate; retrospective assessment at 12.5% with penalties. Bermuda ESR penalties reach USD 250,000 plus director fines.

Director personal liability: Director disqualification up to 15 years; personal liability for false ESR declarations and for penalties where the director signed the offending returns; revocation of the bunkering licence by the Captain of the Port.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment to taxable profits, interest on unpaid tax, tax-related penalties up to 100% of the adjustment, and CbC notification penalties up to GBP 3,000 per failure.

Director personal liability: Directors approving knowingly distorted pricing face misfeasance proceedings and contribution orders in liquidation; personal penalties may be imposed for signed false tax computations.
AML / Sanctions / Trade Controls

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015: imprisonment up to 14 years, unlimited fines, asset freezing and confiscation; HM Treasury/OFSI sanctions penalties; licence suspension; Bermuda Proceeds of Crime Act 1997 equivalent sanctions.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil pierced for money-laundering offences; personal assets subject to forfeiture; travel bans and extradition under UK-Gibraltar-Bermuda mutual legal assistance; directors with knowledge of sanctions breaches face personal criminal liability.
Unlicensed Marine Bunkering Operations

Statutory sanction: Fines up to GBP 50,000 per incident, vessel detention, revocation of petroleum handling licence and criminal prosecution of the operator; environmental clean-up cost recovery.

Director personal liability: Masters, charterers and directors face personal criminal liability for pollution and unauthorised bunkering; environmental remediation costs recoverable personally from the directing mind in cases of gross negligence.
