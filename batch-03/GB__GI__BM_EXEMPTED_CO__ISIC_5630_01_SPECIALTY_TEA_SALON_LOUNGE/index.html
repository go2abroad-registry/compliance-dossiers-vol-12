E-government filing portal

For a Bermuda Exempted Company operating under Gibraltar jurisdiction: complete Form ITO-NR (Non-Resident Company Tax Registration) with the Income Tax Office within 30 days of establishing a permanent establishment; attach certified Bermuda certificate of incorporation, current certificate of good standing, Gibraltar registered office or local agent engagement letter, and beneficial ownership declaration; the ITO will issue a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (GTRN). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No Gibraltar check-the-box or entity classification election exists. In Bermuda, the Exempted Company may apply under the Exempted Companies Act 1981 for an Exemption Certificate confirming its non-resident tax-exempt status; that election is confined to Bermuda and has no effect on Gibraltar-source income. For hybrid mismatch purposes, Gibraltar applies OECD BEPS Action 2 rules; the treatment of the entity as exempt, mismatch-generating, or accommodating must be documented in statutory financial records if the entity is part of a multinational group.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST system; indirect taxation in Gibraltar consists solely of customs import duties, excise duties on specified products, and miscellaneous regulatory levies

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A — no economic turnover threshold exists for VAT/GST because no VAT/GST is imposed. Registration with HM Customs Gibraltar is triggered by the intention to import tea or ceramics, irrespective of turnover; no minimum local-currency sales threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — because Gibraltar imposes no VAT or GST, there is no export zero-rating claim to assert. Export sales are simply outside the scope of Gibraltar consumption taxation. Any import duty previously paid on tea or ceramic stock may be recovered only via duty drawback or inward-duty suspension procedures, subject to HM Customs approval and proof of export.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (electronic single administrative document equivalent)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation of export proceeds
  • Certificate of Origin (where preferential tariff treatment is claimed)

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, B2B e-invoicing, or digital stamping. Retail POS and commercial invoices must be issued and retained for at least seven years. Import and export declarations are filed electronically through Gibraltar Customs' electronic declaration system and receive a customs release reference; no algorithmic tax clearance applies to ordinary invoices.

False or fraudulent customs declarations attract penalties under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance (Cap. 91): fines up to £1,000 plus 100% of the duty evaded, forfeiture of goods, and imprisonment up to two years. Fraudulent duty drawback claims and fabricated export documentation trigger retrospective clawback of any duty refunded, interest at 8% per annum, and criminal prosecution. Income tax overstatement or mischaracterisation of export proceeds may result in civil penalties of 100% of the tax undercharged and imprisonment under the Income Tax Act 2010, section 53.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility prevails in Gibraltar. There are no exchange-control licences, prior-approval mechanisms, or conversion quotas affecting the Gibraltar pound (GIP), pound sterling (GBP), euro (EUR) or US dollar (USD). Arbitrage between GIP and GBP is economically neutral because GIP is pegged 1:1 and freely substitutable. A Gibraltar-resident operating entity may convert operating receipts, inject capital, pay foreign suppliers and remit dividends without central-bank consent. The only statutory constraints are corporate-law and substance-based: (i) profit distributions must satisfy the solvency and distributable-reserve tests under Part 19 of the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014; (ii) economic-substance filings must demonstrate the core income-generating activity in Gibraltar under the Gibraltar Economic Substance (Companies and Partnerships) Act 2018; and (iii) the Bermuda parent must retain its BMA 'Designated Non-Resident' status, which expressly permits unlimited external-currency accounts, free transfer of USD/GBP balances, and unimpeded dividend and capital repatriation outside Bermuda. No Gibraltar withholding tax, no UK dividend withholding tax and no Bermuda income tax attaches to the distribution chain, which makes the convertibility corridor tax-neutral at source.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Neither the United Kingdom nor Gibraltar imposes any statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation deadline for operating or trade receipts. The specialty tea salon lounge is a domestic hospitality business whose inflows are point-of-sale and credit-card settlement proceeds; these may be retained in the original settlement currency or converted at the corporate treasury's discretion. The only related obligations are prudential and documentary: (i) all receipts must be recorded in the statutory books maintained pursuant to section 147 of the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014; (ii) card-settlement and cash-deposit trails must be reconcilable to the underlying merchant-acquiring statement to satisfy GFSC AML/CFT expectations; and (iii) any future export-style activity (e.g., wholesale tea-lounge franchise supplies outside Gibraltar) would remain free of surrender requirements but must be evidenced by commercial invoices, bills of lading and customs declarations if dispatched to the UK via the Common Travel Area customs arrangements.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no domestically chartered central bank; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory supervisory authority for all Gibraltar-licensed banks, payment institutions, investment firms and foreign-exchange activity under the Financial Services Act 2019 (Gibraltar). The Government of Gibraltar issues Gibraltar pound banknotes and coins, which are pegged at par to Pound Sterling; the Bank of England (UK) remains the de facto monetary anchor because GBP is legal tender in Gibraltar and UK clearing systems (CHAPS, Faster Payments, SWIFT) settle the underlying liquidity. For the Bermuda-incorporated parent, the Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) exercises exchange-control and prudential oversight under Bermuda's Exchange Control Act 1973 and grants the Exempted Company 'Designated Non-Resident' status for free multicurrency treasury operations. The combined supervisory perimeter therefore comprises GFSC (prudential and conduct), BMA (Bermuda-side exchange control), and the Bank of England (monetary and settlement infrastructure only).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-LOW as a primary bank, MEDIUM as a secondary payments rail. UK-authorised FCA e-money institutions (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Tide, Currenxie) commonly reject classic offshore holding structures, and the Bermuda Exempted Company continues to attract enhanced due diligence because of the EU's historic inclusion of Bermuda on the non-cooperative jurisdiction list (removed October 2021) and the FATF/BMA transparency framework. Legal constraints: (i) post-Brexit, a UK EMI cannot passport into Gibraltar, so an EMI used by the Bermuda parent must either be FCA-authorized and serving the company on a cross-border remote basis only, or hold a Gibraltar payment-service licence under the Financial Services Act 2019; (ii) most EMI programmes exclude cash-intensive hospitality merchants and do not provide point-of-sale acquiring, which is the required target rail for the specialty tea salon; and (iii) EMI safeguarding accounts are generally not permitted to operate as capital escrow for direct-inward-investment equity, so the inbound Bermuda capital injection must land in a licensed Gibraltar bank account. Recommended architecture: operate the licensed bank account as the anchor for equity and trade settlement, and enable one UK EMI (e.g., Wise or Revolut Business) solely for low-value remote supplier payments in minor currencies, with all EMI balances maintained below GBP 20,000 to limit safeguarding-return risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for the Bermuda-to-Gibraltar capital injection: (1) the Bermuda Exempted Company passes a board resolution approving a share-capital or intercompany-loan contribution and certifies the BMA 'Designated Non-Resident' authority to remit GBP or USD; (2) the remitting Bermuda bank originates a SWIFT MT103 with strict structuring: beneficiary is the Gibraltar operating account, payment reference must read 'Capital contribution — Board Resolution No. [X] dated [Y] — BMA Designated Entity', and the ISO purpose code must be 'EQUI' (equity injection) for equity or 'INTC' (intercompany lending) if structured as a loan — vague narrative such as 'investment' or 'general business support' is prohibited; (3) the Gibraltar receiving bank performs incoming-wire verification against the source-of-funds dossier: Bermuda bank statement showing the debit, incumbency certificate, certificate of exemption, and the latest Bermuda annual return; (4) if the remittance is made in USD, conversion into GBP/GIP is executed at the bank's commercial rate with no FX-control approval; (5) the transaction is recorded in the company's balance-of-payments/statistical reporting and in the bank's transaction-monitoring system for a 24-month anti-flip review; and (6) for intercompany loans above GBP 50,000, a formal loan agreement with amortisation schedule and arm's-length interest rate must be lodged to preclude recharacterisation as equity thin-capitalisation. No Gibraltar stamp duty, capital duty or withholding tax applies to the inbound remittance.Clearing rails for operational outflows: (a) Routine supplier payments for tea, ceramics and salon consumables settle by SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer from the Gibraltar operating account, using purpose code 'GOOD' for goods imports and referencing the commercial invoice number; payments above USD 10,000 to origin-country suppliers require the supporting trade pack described in the AML/KYC defence file. (b) For high-value bulk purchases of loose-leaf tea (e.g., Ceylon tea auction lots in Sri Lanka and estate consignments from China/India/Kenya), a commercial letter of credit (UCP 600) opened by the Gibraltar bank is the recommended rail: the LC is irrevocable, confirmed (where the supplier requires a confirmation bank), and payable at 60 days after shipment against clean on-board bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin and phytosanitary/food-safety certificates. Documentary collections (DP/DA) are permissible for repeat suppliers but carry greater documentary-dishonour risk. (c) Customer receipts flow through Local_POS and Credit_Card rails: the licensed bank's merchant-acquiring agreement settles card transactions into the GBP/GIP operating account within T+1, with scheme fees netted; no surrender or conversion obligation attaches to card receipts. (d) Profit repatriation to the Bermuda parent is executed by MT103 with purpose code 'DIVI', supported by the directors' solvency statement, audited distributable-reserve certificate and the annual return filed in Bermuda; no Gibraltar withholding tax is deducted and no BMA clearance is required for a Designated entity. (e) Intercompany management fees, if used, must be paid via MT103 with code 'INTC' and an OECD arm's-length transfer-pricing memo to prevent deductible-payment challenges in Gibraltar tax filings.Deliverable FX forwards (GBP/USD and GBP/EUR) with a maturity of no more than 12 months, matched one-for-one to outstanding supplier invoices or merchant-settlement float, governed by an ISDA 2002 master agreement or bank master FX confirmation, and subject to a board-approved treasury policy limiting coverage to 125% of forecast net exposure; speculation is prohibited.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted or illiquid import currencies — CNY, INR, LKR and KES — executed through an authorised Gibraltar investment firm or the relationship bank's treasury desk, with settlement references to recognised benchmark fixings (CNH offshore rate, USD/INR RBI reference rate) and full trade-invoice substantiation retained for the bank's transaction-monitoring file.Cross-currency swaps (GBP/USD and GBP/EUR) for medium-term intercompany funding or commercial-LC refinancing, permitted only where the swap's notional matches the underlying treasury liability and where economic-substance documentation demonstrates that the Bermuda parent holds the counterparty risk in a Designated non-resident capacity.Vanilla FX options are legally permissible but excluded from the recommended blueprint for the specialty tea salon to maintain IFRS 9 / UK GAAP hedge-accounting simplicity; the treasury mandate is restricted to vanilla forwards and NDFs to avoid speculative classification and to preserve the low-risk AML banking profile.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-LOW as a primary bank, MEDIUM as a secondary payments rail. UK-authorised FCA e-money institutions (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Tide, Currenxie) commonly reject classic offshore holding structures, and the Bermuda Exempted Company continues to attract enhanced due diligence because of the EU's historic inclusion of Bermuda on the non-cooperative jurisdiction list (removed October 2021) and the FATF/BMA transparency framework. Legal constraints: (i) post-Brexit, a UK EMI cannot passport into Gibraltar, so an EMI used by the Bermuda parent must either be FCA-authorized and serving the company on a cross-border remote basis only, or hold a Gibraltar payment-service licence under the Financial Services Act 2019; (ii) most EMI programmes exclude cash-intensive hospitality merchants and do not provide point-of-sale acquiring, which is the required target rail for the specialty tea salon; and (iii) EMI safeguarding accounts are generally not permitted to operate as capital escrow for direct-inward-investment equity, so the inbound Bermuda capital injection must land in a licensed Gibraltar bank account. Recommended architecture: operate the licensed bank account as the anchor for equity and trade settlement, and enable one UK EMI (e.g., Wise or Revolut Business) solely for low-value remote supplier payments in minor currencies, with all EMI balances maintained below GBP 20,000 to limit safeguarding-return risk.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GBP 750,000

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies OECD compliant substance rules through the GITO practice notes. Although an Exempted Company with no Gibraltar trade may be outside Gibraltar's CIT charge, any transfer pricing adjustment requires the traders, contracts, invoicing, management, and risk control to be located in a jurisdiction with economic substance. A Bermuda Exempted Company carrying on a specialty tea salon lounge through a Gibraltar branch must actually staff, manage, and administer that branch in Gibraltar, or the territorial exemption will be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Superficial Territoriality

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of the full Gibraltar profit at 12.5%, surcharge of 100% of tax due, and financial penalties up to GBP 10,000; criminal prosecution may follow where fraud is established.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid Gibraltar tax and penalties; the court may issue asset freezing orders, travel bans, and disqualification from acting as directors for up to 15 years; custodial sentence of up to 5 years may be imposed for criminal tax evasion.
Artificial Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Automatic denial of territorial tax exemption, full imposition of 12.5% Gibraltar CIT, and potential 10% annual levy on gross receipts under abusive substance arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable under the Gibraltar Companies Act for carrying on business without genuine economic substance where the company is insolvent; the court may make a compensation order and impose a travel restriction pending an investigation.
Money Laundering / AML Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Assets capable of being frozen under the Proceeds of Crime Act; civil forfeiture of the diverted profits; possible court-ordered dissolution of the entity.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit such layering face criminal anti-money laundering charges, imprisonment up to 14 years, and personal civil liability for restitution to the defrauded tax authority.
False Filing / Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of GBP 2,500 plus GBP 25 per day for each day after expiry; escalation to an estimated assessment of 10% of local turnover; publication of the default may be made on the GITO registry.

Director personal liability: Persistent failure exposes directors to personal fines of GBP 5,000, a personal daily default fine, and potential committal proceedings for contempt of court in Gibraltar.
Franchise / IP Profit Repatriation Abuse

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act restoring arm's length profit to the Gibraltar trading entity; additional tax at 12.5% on adjusted profit, plus an isolation penalty of 50% of the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's length intra-group agreements become personally liable for the adjusted tax difference and can be subject to director compensation orders in insolvency proceedings.
