Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office (HM Government of Gibraltar; tax.gov.gi) is the competent authority for the Gibraltar jurisdiction. HMRC (gov.uk/hmrc) is the UK authority for any UK VAT or corporation tax nexus. The Bermuda Registrar of Companies (under the Bermuda Ministry of Finance) issues the Bermuda company registration number used as the entity TIN for CRS/FATCA and app-store tax forms.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Bermuda Exempted Company with the Bermuda Registrar of Companies and obtain the Bermuda registration number (TIN) together with the Tax Assurance Certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, securing exemption from Bermuda taxes until 31 December 2035. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone. The Bermuda Exempted Company is exempt from Bermuda income, profits, dividends, and capital gains taxation until 2035. In Gibraltar, it is classified as a non-resident external company taxed on a territorial basis, so offshore MMORPG app-store revenue is outside Gibraltar source and therefore not assessable. UK corporate tax residence is avoided by ensuring central management and control remains in Bermuda and by having no UK permanent establishment.

Available election forms

Bermuda: election to apply for a Tax Assurance Certificate guaranteeing exemption from future Bermuda taxes until 31 December 2035. Gibraltar: no check-the-box election exists; tax residence follows central management and control under the Income Tax Act 2010, and the entity may secure non-resident status through factual substance in Bermuda. UK: the Hybrid and Other Mismatch Rules (TIOPA 2010 Part 6A) operate without elective filing for an opaque Bermuda body corporate; the entity is treated as opaque for both UK and Gibraltar purposes, and no hybrid mismatch election is available or required for app-store revenue flows.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar/UK exchange-control framework imposes no general current-account or capital-account restrictions for corporate accounts, subject only to financial sanctions, AML/CFT verification, and reporting standards. The Gibraltar Pound is pegged to Pound Sterling at par and is freely issuable, but GIP is not a SWIFT-cleared international currency; the global treasury should execute settlement in GBP, EUR, USD and maintain only a working float in GIP. As a British Overseas Territory, Gibraltar does not hold separate IMF membership, but the United Kingdom's Article VIII obligations support full current-account convertibility for payments and transfers. Current-account flows, including Apple/Google in-app purchase proceeds, platform commissions, licensing fees, developer royalties, and profit remittances, are freely convertible and may be remitted without prior foreign-exchange approval, provided the underlying transaction is supported by a compliant evidentiary file. The capital account is also open: equity injections, shareholder loans, intercompany financing, dividends, and liquidation surplus movements may be freely credited to or debited from a Gibraltar account. For a Bermuda Exempted Company, there are no Gibraltar withholding-tax or exchange-control impediments to upstreaming profits because the entity is not Gibraltar-tax-resident if managed and controlled outside Gibraltar; however, a commercial bank may impose enhanced due diligence on distributions to establish ultimate beneficial ownership and to confirm that no UK/Gibraltar resident individual is using the offshore structure to evade tax.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender or conversion rules in Gibraltar or the United Kingdom. Export revenue, including digital in-app proceeds received from Apple App Store and Google Play payouts, may be retained in the original currency, converted wholly or partially to GBP/EUR/USD, or swept to a concentration account at treasury discretion. No minimum conversion ratio is mandated and no deadline exists for repatriation of foreign currency receipts into Gibraltar. The only mandatory operational controls are that proceeds clear through a licensed financial institution, the underlying sale/payout is accurately recorded in the accounting and transfer-pricing files, and the receiving bank is provided with an invoice or settlement report when the transaction is treated as an intercompany or treasury clearing flow.

Central bank supervision: No dedicated Gibraltar central bank exists. The United Kingdom's Bank of England anchors monetary policy and serves as the ultimate sterling liquidity backstop; HM Treasury (Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation) retains UK-wide sanctions and exchange-control authority; the Government of Gibraltar issues Gibraltar Pound (GIP) notes under the Currency Notes Act; and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the prudential and licensing authority for all Gibraltar-licensed credit and payment institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-to-High for FCA-licensed UK and GFSC-licensed Gibraltar digital e-money institutions; Low for non-EEA, offshore-only EMIs that do not hold a post-Brexit European passport. A UK-authorised e-money institution may onboard the Bermuda Exempted Company with remote/virtual KYC, provide local-currency collection IBANs, and execute FX conversion; however, an EMI is not a bank and cannot provide a formally isolated capital escrow account, documentary credits, or bilateral treasury hedging. An offshore EMI without FCA or GFSC authorisation is legally barred from holding client money in Gibraltar or executing regulated payment services; such a service may be used only as a secondary collection utility with all funds ultimately settled through a licensed credit institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound liquidity into the Gibraltar treasury must be transmitted from the parent company's regulated bank account to the pre-boarded capital escrow account held in the name of the Bermuda Exempted Company. Use SWIFT MT103 for client credit or MT202 COV for interbank cover; include a structured purpose narrative such as 'INTRAGROUP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - EQUITY/LOAN - GB__GI__BM_EXEMPTED_CO__ISIC_5820_02_HARDCORE_MMORPG_GLOBAL' to enable automatic reconciliation. The routing reference must include the unique capital-injection number, the issuing bank's SWIFT BIC, the source jurisdiction, and the final beneficiary legal name. The remitting bank should issue an authenticated SWIFT MT199 pre-advice to the receiving treasury contact before the first transfer. Prior to the first inbound flow, the Gibraltar bank must receive an executed capital resolution and source-of-wealth confirmation; the bank will hold the remittance in the escrow sub-account and, on evidence of commercial need, release funds to the operational concentration account. Inbound flows must not be split below reporting thresholds, must never be commingled with third-party or user funds, and must be recorded against the entity's capital ledger.Operational trade disbursements, including developer settlements, licensing fees, cloud infrastructure payments, Apple/Google revenue-share payments, payment-gateway costs, and merchant-of-record payouts, must clear over bank-grade rails. Material disbursements must be sent by SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary legal name, address, account/IBAN, BIC, and an invoice-mapped purpose code.
