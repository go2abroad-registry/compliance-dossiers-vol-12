Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office (HM Government of Gibraltar), 3-5 Secretary's Lane, Gibraltar; portal: www.gibraltartax.gov.gi. Registry counterpart: Gibraltar Companies House (www.companieshouse.gi). Customs: HM Customs Gibraltar (www.customs.gov.gi).

E-government filing portal

Stage 1: Register the Bermuda Exempted Company as an overseas company under Part 21 of the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 within one month of establishing a place of business in Gibraltar; obtain a branch registration number. Stage 2: File the foreign-company return and constitutional documents with the Registrar of Companies. Stage 3: Submit the tax registration application to the Commissioner of Income Tax, attaching the branch registration certificate, board resolutions, evidence of Gibraltar registered office, and particulars of resident directors and secretary; the Income Tax Office issues a local TIN linked to the branch number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (reflecting the Bermuda Exempted Company's home-jurisdiction exemption from Bermuda income tax until at least 2035). For Gibraltar direct-tax purposes, the entity operates under Territorial_Scope: Gibraltar charges corporation tax on profits arising in Gibraltar or derived from Gibraltar-source property, while foreign-source income not accrued in or remitted to Gibraltar may be excluded. Operational note: the Gibraltar Finance Act 2021 amended the Income Tax Act 2010 to apply a headline 20% corporate rate to profits from retail, wholesale and distribution of goods effective 1 July 2021; therefore a Gibraltar-attributed pharmaceutical API wholesale trade is typically taxed at 20% on Gibraltar-source profits. The Bermuda exemption does not shield Gibraltar-source trading profits.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Gibraltar. Available elections under the Income Tax Act 2010: (1) territorial-source claim for foreign-source income not remitted to or accrued in Gibraltar; (2) group-relief election enabling surrender of losses among qualifying 75% group companies; (3) capital allowances elections including pooling and annual investment allowance claims; (4) patent box election applying a 5% rate to qualifying IP income; (5) finance-company election applying a 7.5% rate to qualifying finance and licensing income; (6) no hybrid mismatch election is available, and OECD hybrid-mismatch rules apply to deny excessive deductions. All elections require written notice to the Commissioner of Income Tax within statutory deadlines.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - Gibraltar has no VAT, GST or general sales tax. Indirect taxation is limited to customs and excise duties on importation of prescribed goods (alcohol, tobacco, fuel, cosmetics and similar) under the Customs Duties Ordinance, and excise legislation. Cross-border sales of pharmaceutical APIs classified under HS Chapters 29 and 30 are outside the scope of any Gibraltar indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. Mandatory regardless of turnover: (i) EORI enrolment for all commercial customs operators; (ii) excise/customs duty registration and bonding where prescribed goods (alcohol, tobacco, fuel) are imported or warehoused. Local currency is the Gibraltar pound (GIP).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is inapplicable in the strict VAT sense because Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST. All exports of pharmaceutical APIs from Gibraltar are automatically outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation, producing a 0% effective charge with no refund mechanism required. To preserve this tax-neutral export treatment, the exporter must demonstrate: (1) physical export from Gibraltar supported by a customs SAD export declaration accepted by HM Customs Gibraltar; (2) sale to a non-Gibraltar purchaser evidenced by commercial invoice and contract; and (3) transport out of Gibraltar within a reasonable period, ordinarily 90 days. No Gibraltar VAT/refund application is necessary; the benefit is that no local indirect tax is levied on the export transaction.

  • Gibraltar customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document / electronic export entry) accepted by HM Customs Gibraltar
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing the Gibraltar entity as shipper and a non-Gibraltarian consignee
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list (API description, HS codes 2922/2933/3002 as applicable, quantities, Incoterms, and value)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103/MT202 evidence of receipt of export proceeds
  • Certificate of Origin (only where a preferential origin claim is made; otherwise optional)
  • Pharmaceutical wholesale/API distribution licence (General Wholesale Authorisation under the Gibraltar Medicines Ordinance) and destination-country health certificates where required

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based invoice stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing operates in Gibraltar. Commercial invoices may be issued on paper or electronically. All customs declarations must be lodged electronically through the Gibraltar Customs single window (SAD/EMS electronic interface aligned with UK CDS-equivalent processing). Taxpayers must retain invoices and underlying export documentation for six years for income tax and customs audit purposes.

HM Customs Gibraltar and the Commissioner of Income Tax jointly audit API export transactions. Penalties: (1) customs misdeclaration or evasion - up to three times the duty evaded, statutory fines, and forfeiture of goods; (2) false statements in tax returns - civil penalty of up to 100% of undercharged tax plus interest at 15% p.a.; (3) criminal penalties of up to GIP 10,000 and/or imprisonment for fraudulent evasion; (4) retrospective clawback of reliefs, exemptions, or territorial-source exclusions where export evidence is fraudulent, proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, or economic substance and transfer pricing requirements are not met; (5) revocation of the Medicines Ordinance wholesale licence and escalation to the high-risk AML/regulatory register; (6) minimum penalty of GIP 500 per unfiled customs export declaration.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound is fully convertible into GBP on a 1:1 basis, and Gibraltar operates under an open, liberalised exchange-control regime with no restrictions on capital account transactions or current account payments. The jurisdiction has no separate capital controls, and profit remittance to shareholders is unrestricted onshore, subject only to the FSC Gibraltar's anti-money laundering verification of the underlying source of funds and beneficial ownership. For a Bermuda Exempted Company managed from Gibraltar, the practical hurdles are limited to presenting clean structural documentation, a recognised Economic Substance filing where applicable, and demonstrating that the company is not treaty-resident in a blacklisted or non-cooperative jurisdiction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no deadline for the repatriation or conversion of export proceeds derived from the wholesale distribution of active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs). Export receipts can be retained in the currency of receipt, be it USD, EUR, GBP, or CHF, and can be applied directly toward trade settlement or FX hedging strategies without central bank approval.

Central bank supervision: Financial Services Commission of Gibraltar (FSC Gibraltar), operating in coordination with the Bank of England as the currency-issuing authority for the Gibraltar pound (GIP), which is pegged at par to the British pound sterling (GBP).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with strict legal constraints. Offshore digital EMI institutions (e-money institutions) licensed in jurisdictions such as the United Kingdom, Lithuania, and Malta can be used for low-value operational treasury movements and foreign-exchange conversions, but FSC-regulated banks and clearing members generally view such EMIs as unverified counterparties for pharma-API wholesale settlements above EUR 50,000. The use of an offshore EMI must be supported by a valid E-Money Institution authorisation under the respective national regime, a transparent custody arrangement for client funds, and a full audit trail of the underlying commercial invoices and batch records. Direct integration of an EMI with global clearing rails such as SWIFT and correspondent trade finance is often unavailable, making them unsuitable as the primary clearing venue for large API purchase orders.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections for the Bermuda Exempted Company are routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 COV from the parent's banking institution, with strict adherence to Gibraltar's Anti-Money Laundering Directive. The preferred protocol requires the parent to identify the purpose code as 'NEB' (new equity investment) or 'INT' (intercompany loan) in the SWIFT field 70, and the beneficiary account at Gibraltar International Bank or the chosen tier-1 counterparty must be a dedicated Capital Escrow Account. The following documents are required to be pre-validated with the receiving bank: board resolution authorising the capital increase, executed subscription agreement, notarised certificate of incorporation of the parent, UBO declaration for any equity injection above 10%, and a source-of-wealth statement for the subscribing shareholder. For an exempted company, the injection must be verified as not arising from a tax-exempt domestic activity requiring Economic Substance, and the funds must remain in the Gibraltar operating account until the relevant trade cycle is formally documented with the supporting purchase order and LC.Outbound trade disbursements for pharmaceutical API settlements utilise two primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 priority wire payment for non-documentary, low-risk invoices, USD-denominated disbursements, and emergency supplier payments up to USD 250,000, and (2) Irrevocable Documentary Letter of Credit (UCP600) for high-value API purchase orders where the supplier requires bank-backed payment security at the point of shipment. The commercial LC must be issued via a SWIFT MT700 message from the Gibraltar issuing bank to the beneficiary's confirming bank, with a required shipping-document package comprising Commercial Invoice, 3-Heading Bill of Lading, Certificate of Origin, Pharmaceutical Quality Certificate, and a cleanliness verification from the surveyor. Disbursements above internal escalation thresholds must pass through an Independent Cross-Border Payment Validation Gate, where the transaction is automatically screened against the SDN, OFAC, UK Sanctions, and EU Consolidated Lists. SWIFT GPI (Global Payments Innovation) tracking is mandated for all MT103 disbursements to ensure end-to-end traceability from the Gibraltar treasury node to the final beneficiary bank.Over-the-counter (OTC) deliverable FX forwards with a Gibraltar Financial Information System (GFSC) authorised counterparty, covering major currency pairs such as USD/EUR, USD/GBP, EUR/GBP, and GBP/USD, with a maximum tenor of 12 months and no embedded structuring that would create a speculative tax position.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for emerging-market currencies (e.g., INR, CNY, BDT, or MYR) used to settle API procurement from manufacturing hubs in India and China, struck only with a licensed global bank and fully backed by a documented firm purchase order or sales contract so as to remain within the scope of commercial hedging.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps or FX swaps between USD, EUR, GBP, and CHF used to align the funding currency of the operational treasury with the invoicing currency of the API trade contract, provided the notional value of each swap does not exceed 120% of the underlying commercial obligation, and the counterparty has a minimum S&P credit rating of A-.Prime-brokerage currency swap lines for short-term liquidity deployment, but only where the margin call structure and ISDA master agreement are pre-approved in writing by the Gibraltar treasury board and do not constitute speculative trading on the company's capital account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Gibraltar: 10% standard rate; 5% reduced rate for internationally-oriented companies (effective 1 January 2022). Bermuda domicile layer: 0% corporate income tax applicable to a Bermuda Exempted Company not managed and controlled in Gibraltar.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Gibraltar imposes no statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar-resident company; distributions by a Bermuda Exempted Company are likewise free of Bermuda and Gibraltar income tax withholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold is set in Gibraltar domestic law for Local File/Master File preparation; however, prudent compliance requires contemporaneous benchmarking documentation for every controlled cross-border transaction, with a recommended disclosure threshold of GIP 100,000 per related counterparty per year. Country-by-Country Reporting applies to MNE groups with consolidated group revenue equal to or exceeding EUR 750,000,000 (approximately GIP 750,000,000); the Gibraltar ultimate parent entity must file Form G-CBC through the e-Services portal.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Following Gibraltar's 2019 commitments to the EU Code of Conduct Group and implementation aligned with OECD substance requirements, a Gibraltar tax-resident internationally-oriented company - including a re-domiciled Bermuda Exempted Company engaged in API wholesale distribution - must demonstrate economic substance in Gibraltar: (i) direction and management exercised from Gibraltar; (ii) adequate qualifying premises in Gibraltar; (iii) adequate full-time employees in Gibraltar; (iv) adequate annual operational expenditure; and (v) performance of core income-generating activities (sourcing, pricing, contract negotiation, invoicing, logistics, and distribution decisions) within Gibraltar. The substance declaration is mandatory on each CT Return; failure to substantiate triggers reclassification from the 5% rate to the 10% rate, denial of UK-Gibraltar DTA benefits, reputational listing risk, and potential strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended), fraudulent returns attract a tax-geared penalty of up to 100% of the underpaid tax, statutory interest, unlimited fines, imprisonment of up to 7 years, confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, and seizure of Gibraltar bank assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who sign false returns or declarations are jointly and severally liable for the tax-geared penalties where the company is insolvent; they face director disqualification of up to 15 years, Magisterial travel bans, and custodial sentences for fraudulent trading.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Retroactive reassessment at the full 10% rate, economic substance compliance penalties, compulsory strike-off under the Companies Act 2014, denial of UK-Gibraltar DTA benefits, and adverse EU/OECD public listing causing revocation of the statutory pharmaceutical wholesale licence and correspondent banking lines.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise false substance declarations commit offences under the Companies Act 2014 and the Income Tax Act 2010; they face personal tax assessments, disqualification, travel restrictions, and prosecution for fraudulent trading under the Insolvency Act 2011.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment with statutory interest from the original due date, tax-geared penalties of up to 100% of the additional tax assessed, and recharacterisation of excessive payments as distributions; parallel UK CFC exposure may arise if a UK-resident parent controls the Gibraltar entity.

Director personal liability: Officers who sign TP schedules and financial statements known to be false are personally liable alongside the company; UK GAAR and civil penalty regimes can extend to directors; fraud-tainted adjustments may ground extradition-based prosecution.
AML / Sanctions Breach (High-Risk Pharma API)

Statutory sanction: Unlimited fines and imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (as amended); revocation of the statutory wholesale pharmaceutical licence; freezing of accounts under UN/EU/UK sanctions frameworks applicable to Gibraltar; regulatory enforcement by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission and adverse AML risk-registry listing.

Director personal liability: Directors qualify as statutory AML officers; failure to maintain adequate AML/CFT controls under the AML Regulations 2019 incurs personal criminal liability, asset confiscation, travel bans, and prohibition from managing Gibraltarian companies for up to 15 years.
Unlawful Profit Repatriation / Thin Capitalisation and Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Dividends declared in contravention of the Companies Act 2014 are void and recoverable; excessive interest deductions are disallowed and recharacterised as distributions; deemed distributions can trigger withholding assessments in the hands of non-resident shareholders where treaty abuse is established; the company may be struck off with asset recovery orders.

Director personal liability: Every director who signed the unlawful distribution resolution or solvency certificate is personally liable to repay the amount with interest; they additionally face travel bans, disqualification orders, and criminal liability for misfeasance of up to 5 years' imprisonment under the Companies Act 2014.
