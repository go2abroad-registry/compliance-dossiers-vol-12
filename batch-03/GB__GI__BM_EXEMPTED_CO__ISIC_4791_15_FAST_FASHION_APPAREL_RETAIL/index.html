Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (8 Secretary's Lane, Gibraltar GX11 1AA); foreign-company registration is administered by the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 2014; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi (Income Tax Office and e-filing sections). Gibraltar is a British Overseas Territory with fiscal autonomy and is not part of the UK for VAT or direct-tax purposes.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Establish nexus: The Bermuda Exempted Company (Bermuda Exempted Co) must register as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under Part 20 of the Companies Act 2014 within one month of setting up a place of business, branch, or tax-relevant physical presence in Gibraltar; alternatively a full continuation of domicile into Gibraltar may be effected under the Companies (Transfer of Domicile) Act 2004. Step 2 - Lodgment: File certified copies of the Bermuda certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, certificate of good standing from the Bermuda Registrar, register/registered office address in Gibraltar, and particulars of directors and company secretary. Step 3 - Tax registration: Submit the completed corporate taxpayer application to the Income Tax Office; the Commissioner of Income Tax assigns the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN/tax reference) to the company, normally within 5-10 business days of receipt of the complete file. Step 4 - Compliance cycle: The company becomes chargeable under the Income Tax Act 2010 on Gibraltar-source profits (and on worldwide profits if managed and controlled in Gibraltar, i.e. tax-resident); an annual corporation tax return is due within 12 months of the accounting-period end (31 December basis as standard). Step 5 - Nil-return duty: A dormant or loss-making entity must still file nil/declaration returns; the Bermuda Tax Exemption Certificate does NOT relieve Gibraltar-taxable profits of the Gibraltar permanent establishment, although no capital gains tax, withholding tax, or wealth tax applies in Gibraltar.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda domestic status: the Exempted Co holds an irrevocable Tax Exemption Certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, guaranteeing freedom from Bermuda income tax, capital gains tax, and withholding tax until at least 2035, with Government commitment to periodic extension). The Gibraltar permanent establishment or tax-resident branch is nevertheless subject to Entity_Level_Taxation under Gibraltar law at the flat corporation tax rate of 10% (a 12.5% rate applied only to profits above GBP 500,000 between 2015 and 2020; the top-up was phased out). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda domestic status: the Exempted Co holds an irrevocable Tax Exemption Certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, guaranteeing freedom from Bermuda income tax, capital gains tax, and withholding tax until at least 2035, with Government commitment to periodic extension). The Gibraltar permanent establishment or tax-resident branch is nevertheless subject to Entity_Level_Taxation under Gibraltar law at the flat corporation tax rate of 10% (a 12.5% rate applied only to profits above GBP 500,000 between 2015 and 2020; the top-up was phased out). Territorial_Scope: non-resident companies are taxed only on Gibraltar-source income; pass-through treatment is not available to corporate entities.

Available election forms

Bermuda: (i) application for Tax Exemption Certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966; (ii) designation as a 'long-term undertaking' extending statutory protection to 2050/2060; (iii) election as a permitted entity under the Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018 for the relevant activity. Gibraltar: no US-style check-the-box election exists; classification is fixed by the Companies Act 2014 legal form. Available Gibraltar elections: (i) group-loss relief surrender election for 75% related persons (Income Tax Act 2010, s.132); (ii) participation exemption/rollover treatment on disposal of qualifying shareholdings (s.126); (iii) capital-allowances pooling and apportionment elections. Hybrid mismatch rules: Gibraltar transposed OECD BEPS Action 2 via the Taxation (Hybrid and Other Mismatches) Regulations effective 1 January 2022; the rules impose mandatory deduction-denials for hybrid financial instruments, hybrid entities, and reverse hybrids (including UK/EU branch mismatches) and contain no elective mitigation, aligning with the UK's and EU ATAD's anti-hybrid frameworks.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar has NO value-added tax, NO GST and NO general sales tax; Gibraltar is expressly excluded from the EU VAT system and EU Customs Union (Treaty of Accession 1972, Art. 28 and Annex I, Part I) and remains outside the UK VAT territory. Fiscal excise/customs duties apply only to a closed list of imported goods (alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oils, and motor vehicles). Extraterritorial nexus: the fast-fashion e-commerce operator becomes subject to UK VAT (Value Added Tax Act 1994) on distance sales into the UK and to EU VAT (Directive 2006/112/EC) on consumer supplies into EU Member States, via the OSS and IOSS digital regimes.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: no indirect-tax registration threshold (no VAT). UK VAT: GBP 90,000 annual taxable turnover (from 1 April 2024) for distance sales of goods to UK consumers; online marketplaces that facilitate supplies of imported goods valued at up to GBP 135 are deemed suppliers and must register irrespective of threshold. EU VAT: EUR 10,000 annual cross-border distance-sale threshold for intra-EU B2C supplies (Art. 59c of VAT Directive); EUR 150 per-consignment threshold for Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) eligibility, above which import VAT applies at the destination Member State rate; no threshold applies to Union OSS/Non-Union OSS registration once any taxable distance or domestic EU supply is made.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a Gibraltar domestic concept because Gibraltar imposes no VAT; goods exported from Gibraltar are outside any Gibraltar indirect-tax charge and there is no Gibraltar zero-rating claim or VAT-refund mechanism. Where the Bermuda Exempted Co voluntarily registers for UK VAT as a non-established taxable person, export supplies of goods from Great Britain to non-UK destinations qualify for the 0% rate under VATA 1994, s.30 and SI 1995/2518, Reg. 129, provided the goods are exported within 3 months of the time of supply and the supplier holds valid commercial evidence of export (customs declaration plus transport documents). For EU consumer sales, goods imported into the EU with a consignment value of EUR 150 or less qualify for exemption from import VAT under the IOSS procedure, with output VAT declared at the destination Member State's standard rate at point of sale; intra-EU distance sales above the EUR 10,000 threshold are declared under OSS. Offshore services (e.g., digital marketing), if exported to non-resident business customers, may be zero-rated under UK VAT place-of-supply B2B rules (reverse charge applies to the recipient).

  • Customs Export Declaration: GB Customs Declaration Service (CDS) electronic export declaration (SAD format, C88) with confirmed exit status showing the exporter of record, commodity code (ISIC 4791 / HS Chapters 61-62 apparel), value, and final destination; or, if consigned from an EU Member State, the EU MRN from the National Export System with ECS exit confirmation.
  • Bill of Lading / Master or House Air Waybill / CMR international consignment note issued by the contracted carrier, evidencing physical removal of the goods from the UK/EU territory and signed receipt by the foreign consignee (this document is the primary evidence of export under UK Customs Notice 703 and EU Reg. 282/2011, Art. 14).
  • Inward Remittance Certificate: bank SWIFT MT103/credit advice (the internationally accepted equivalent of an inward remittance certificate) confirming receipt of the export sale proceeds by the Bermuda/Gibraltar bank account of the seller, matching the invoiced value, within 180 days of shipment.

Gibraltar: no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory e-invoicing, no API-based government clearance, and no digital stamping regime; accounting records and underlying e-commerce transaction logs must be retained for 7 years (Income Tax Act 2010, s.154; Companies Act 2014, Part 26). Where the Bermudan Exempted Co is registered for UK VAT, Making Tax Digital (MTD) requires digital-record keeping and quarterly filing through functional compatible software (VATA 1994 Sch. 11; SI 2018/261). Where registered for EU OSS/IOSS: OSS returns are filed quarterly and IOSS returns monthly via the registration Member State's web portal; certain destination Member States impose domestic B2B e-invoicing mandates (e.g., Italy SDI/FatturaPA, France e-invoicing from 2026 under ViDA transposition) only where the company holds local B2B VAT registrations. No e-invoice mandate applies to B2C fast-fashion garment sales settled through the target payment rails (Klarna BNPL, Shopify Payments).

UK VAT: if a zero-rated export is later disproved (goods never exported, diverted, or re-imported into the UK/EU), HMRC raises an assessment for the output tax plus default interest under VATA 1994 s.74 and behavior-based penalties of up to 100% of the tax (Schedule 24, Finance Act 2007); fraudulent claims or false documents trigger criminal prosecution under VATA 1994 s.72 (up to 7 years' imprisonment and unlimited fine) and forfeiture of goods under CEMA 1979 s.167. EU: Art. 14 of Implementing Regulation 282/2011 mandates retrospective correction of the VAT exemption where goods are altered or re-imported into the Union; Member States may assess unpaid VAT for 3-10 years (unlimited in the case of fraud for many States), with default interest and penalties (e.g., Germany AO s.370 up to 10 years' imprisonment; France CGI Art. 1741 up to EUR 500,000 and 5 years). Gibraltar: the Income Tax Office may raise assessments up to 6 years from the end of the accounting period (no time limit for fraud or wilful default), with late-filing daily penalties and civil penalties up to 200% of the tax understated for deliberate inaccuracies (Income Tax Act 2010, s.155); criminal prosecution may additionally apply for false statements. A successful clawback will also disqualify subsequent zero-rating claims and increase the entity's tax-risk rating for EMI and acquirer banking reviews.
