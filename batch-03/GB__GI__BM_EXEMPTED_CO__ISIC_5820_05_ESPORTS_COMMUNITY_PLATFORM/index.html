Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office of HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax); official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi; corporate registry: Companies House Gibraltar (Registrar of Companies).

E-government filing portal

Gibraltar imposes no VAT, so no VAT ID exists; the statutory TIN is the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN). A Bermuda Exempted Company establishing a Gibraltar fiscal presence must: (1) register as an overseas company under the Companies Act 2014 with the Registrar of Companies; (2) submit the corporate tax registration pack (Form G) to the Income Tax Office, comprising the Bermuda certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, Bermuda Corporate Registry extract, board resolutions, Gibraltar registered office address, a Gibraltar resident agent appointment where the company has a place of business, and evidence of economic substance; (3) obtain the Gibraltar TRN upon approval; (4) file annual tax returns (Form G filing) and pay quarterly instalment payments. A non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company deriving only Gibraltar-source income must register directly with the Income Tax Office and file on the territorial basis.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

In Bermuda, the Exempted Company may elect for an exemption undertaking from the Bermuda Minister of Finance, guaranteeing no Bermuda income/corporate tax until 31 December 2035. In Gibraltar, the former exempt-company regime was abolished in 2010; no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections are available. The flat 10% corporate tax applies to all companies on profits arising in or derived from Gibraltar (territorial scope). Available statutory elections include pooling of capital allowances, group/consortium loss relief elections, and the participation exemption for qualifying shareholdings. OECD BEPS-aligned hybrid mismatch rules neutralise deduction/non-inclusion outcomes arising from the Bermuda tax-exempt status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST in Gibraltar; the applicable indirect tax is customs import duty and excise duty under the Customs Ordinance (Cap. 54), with a generally duty-free tariff for most goods.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A - Gibraltar has no VAT/GST registration threshold; every import is subject to electronic customs declaration and duty assessment under the Gibraltar tariff (generally 0% for non-excise goods; excise duties apply to alcohol, tobacco, perfume, fuel, and motor vehicles).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Gibraltar: because Gibraltar does not impose VAT/GST, zero-rating does not exist for exported digital services, offshore services, or physical goods. The Bermuda Exempted Company's global esports platform revenues are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax. If the entity registers for UK VAT or EU OSS to collect VAT on B2C digital supplies, zero-rating/outside-scope treatment for exported services is granted only with contemporaneous evidence of customer location (IP address, billing address), payment rail records (Stripe/PayPal), and service delivery logs; no UK VAT registration threshold applies to non-established suppliers of digital services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic export entry filed with Gibraltar Customs or SAD/EX1 where applicable)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (foreign exchange receipt evidence)
  • Commercial Invoice (platform revenue substantiation)

No statutory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping mandate exists in Gibraltar; commercial invoicing follows ordinary company law. Customs import/export declarations are processed electronically through the Gibraltar Customs single-window e-services. For B2C digital esports services supplied to UK/EU consumers, the platform may incur UK VAT or EU OSS registration obligations, but Gibraltar will not impose any fiscal invoice clearance.

Gibraltar Customs may audit export entries for up to 7 years; misdeclarations or fraudulent export claims trigger seizure and forfeiture, monetary penalties up to GBP 250 per item, and imprisonment up to 5 years for customs fraud. For any UK/EU VAT registration, unsupported export zero-rating or deliberate misuse attracts 100% VAT clawback, statutory interest, penalty tiers up to 100% of evaded tax, and HMRC joint-and-several liability under the 'Placed and Arranged' rules for platform operators (VATA 1994 s. 77A, inserted by Finance Act 2019 s. 66).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP is pegged to GBP at a fixed 1:1 exchange rate under the Gibraltarian Currency Notes Act and both currencies are freely convertible for residents and non-residents. Gibraltar maintains no exchange-control approvals and draws no legal distinction between capital-account and current-account transactions: equity injections, inter-company dividends, royalties, trade settlements and profit remittances to Bermuda or the UK are unrestricted. Practical hurdles: GIP is not a deliverable settlement currency outside Gibraltar, so all cross-border SWIFT clears must be effectuated in GBP (with EUR and USD sub-accounts where required); single remittances above GBP 1,000,000 trigger enhanced due-diligence documentation (board resolution, source-of-wealth materials, tax-clearance certificate from the Gibraltar Commissioner of Income Tax) but never a convertibility denial. A Bermuda exempted company faces zero Bermuda exchange controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Neither Gibraltar nor Bermuda imposes statutory surrender timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation obligations on service-export proceeds. Esports platform revenue (matchmaking fees, tournament administration fees, sponsor/brand revenue) is classified as a cross-border service export; Stripe and PayPal settlement balances may be held in multi-currency merchant wallets and auto-swept to the Gibraltar operating account at the platform's discretion. GFSC best-practice guidance recommends clearing gateway receipts into the licensed bank within the same monthly business cycle so that the AML risk profile remains consistent with the declared Low_Risk_Standard tier.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary anchor for GBP); Government of Gibraltar - Financial Secretary and the Gibraltar Currency Commission for sole issuance of the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at fixed 1:1 parity with GBP; banking supervision and AML/CTF administration by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. GFSC-regulated payment-service firms, FCA-authorized UK electronic-money institutions (e.g., Transact365, Currenxie) and tier-one EEA e-money institutions will generally onboard a licensed Bermuda exempted company that is registered as a foreign company in Gibraltar. Legal constraints: an EMI may not take deposits or provide credit; it may only hold e-money client funds in segregated safeguarding accounts, so the Bermuda entity must keep its primary treasury balance at a full Gibraltar banking licence holder and use EMI rails solely for payout orchestration. The esports prize-disbursement velocity also triggers EMI program-monitoring departments: payout-volume forecasts, chargeback caps and merchant-verification reviews are mandatory before gateway limits are lifted.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Execute via SWIFT MT103 from the Bermuda corporate account (HSBC Bermuda or Butterfield) to the Gibraltar operating account denominated in GBP.
