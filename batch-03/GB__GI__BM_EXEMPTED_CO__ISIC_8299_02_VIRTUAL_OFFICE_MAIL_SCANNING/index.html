Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Commissioner of Income Tax, operating through the Income Tax Office (ITO) at 1–5 Treasury Place, Gibraltar; corporate returns and GST filings are submitted via the official GibTax online portal (http://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation).

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company conducting virtual office / mail-scanning activity from a fixed Gibraltar address must first register as an 'external company' with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under s.376 of the Companies Act 2014, filing a certified Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, authenticated memorandum and bye-laws, a list of directors, and the registered or representative office particulars in Gibraltar. Upon issue of the Gibraltar company number, the ITO automatically assigns a corporate Tax Identification Number (TIN). The foreign entity must then complete the corporate taxpayer registration (Form C) within 30 days, appointing a Gibraltar-resident person authorized to accept service and provide the completed Form 64 (declaration of particulars). Where no trading activity is anticipated, a non-resident company may instead apply for a tax clearance certificate confirming no liability to file periodic returns; however, a CMRA branch with an active street address and staff will ordinarily be treated as carrying on a trade in Gibraltar.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope (Gibraltar branch taxed on income accruing in or derived from Gibraltar); parent Bermuda Exempted Company retains Tax_Exempt_Zone status in Bermuda under the Bermuda Companies Act 1981 (Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope (Gibraltar branch taxed on income accruing in or derived from Gibraltar); parent Bermuda Exempted Company retains Tax_Exempt_Zone status in Bermuda under the Bermuda Companies Act 1981 (Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966). No pass-through eligibility exists.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election is available under Gibraltar law. The options comprise: (i) an irrevocable joint election for capital allowances on qualifying fixed assets under the Income Tax Act 2010; (ii) group relief election under s.42A permitting transfer of losses between 75% group companies; (iii) a s.6(1A) election enabling non-resident companies to restrict assessment to Gibraltar-source profits; and (iv) at the Bermuda parent level, a statutory election under s.132G of the Companies Act 1981 to be ring-fenced as a non-resident exempted company for exchange-of-information and eventual liquidating distribution purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

General Services Tax (GST) – Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area; no VAT is chargeable.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 90,000 (annual taxable supplies of goods or services made in Gibraltar; the threshold is set by the General Services Tax Act 2015 and adjusted by ministerial order). Once exceeded, the supplier must register for GST and file quarterly returns regardless of profit.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because the Gibraltar GST headline rate is 0%, 'zero-rating' is substantively equivalent to the 'outside the scope' treatment for B2B services supplied to a customer belonging in a foreign jurisdiction under the place-of-supply rule (customer's establishment). To claim the 0% rate, the CMRA operator must demonstrate that (a) the digital mail-scanning service is physically performed and delivered to a recipient resident outside Gibraltar, (b) the customer is not established in Gibraltar, and (c) the invoices and underlying contracts expressly designate the offshore place of supply. Physical exports of scanned hardware or mail matter additionally require proof of physical departure from Gibraltar customs territory.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD form via the one-stop shop, with HS Chapter 8471/8473 classification for scanner hardware if exported)
  • Commercial invoice and contract evidencing offshore customer establishment and place-of-supply terms
  • Transport document (Bill of Lading, Airway Bill, or courier waybill) with proof of departure from Gibraltar territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 from the paying offshore bank evidencing settlement of the export consideration

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate is in force in Gibraltar. GST returns are filed quarterly via GibTax; paper or PDF commercial invoices issued to offshore clients are accepted provided they include supplier TIN, customer name, date, description of mail-scanning services, and consideration. Records must be retained for six years.

The ITO may open an audit within six years of the relevant chargeable period (extended to 20 years on evidence of fraud or willful default). A false zero-rating or outside-scope claim triggers retrospective assessment, interest at the prevailing Bank of England base rate plus 3.5%, and a civil penalty up to 100% of the tax undercharged, mitigated only by unprompted voluntary disclosure. Deliberate falsification of customs documents or invoicing exposes the company and its officers to criminal prosecution under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance (cap. 26) and the Income Tax Act 2010 (s.135–s.140), punishable by fines unlimited in amount and imprisonment up to seven years. The clawback period runs from the date the refund or relief was granted, and liability attaches jointly and severally to the local officeholder in Gibraltar.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File documentation applies where annual Gibraltar-related controlled transactions exceed GIP 1,000,000. A Master File is required where the consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000. Below GIP 500,000 of controlled transactions, a simplified contemporaneous record is required. Country-by-Country reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's Economic Substance Regulations target geographically mobile activities such as banking, insurance, headquarters, holding, IP, distribution, and shipping. Commercial mail receiving and scanning is not an enumerated activity, but a company operating with a physical presence in Gibraltar may still fall within the 'distribution and service centre' or 'headquarters' category. A Bermuda Exempted Company must demonstrate adequate staff, premises, operational expenditure, and board activity in its claimed residence jurisdiction. Failure to maintain economic substance in Bermuda, particularly if management and control is in Gibraltar, may lead to tax residency re-characterisation and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Interest and penalties up to 200% of the tax evaded, daily default penalties, extended assessment period of up to six years (unlimited for deliberate concealment), and fines of up to GIP 1,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers and shadow directors face personal civil restitution for unpaid tax, disqualification from serving as a director for up to 15 years, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to 10 years under Gibraltar's Income Tax Act and Criminal Justice legislation.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Compulsory strike-off, dissolution, or revocation of tax status; disallowance of deductions; ESR penalties of up to GIP 50,000 and daily or lump-sum penalties of GIP 2,500 per day for non-compliance; deemed profit assessment based on secret profits.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for debts incurred while trading with no economic substance, forced to disgorge tax benefits, subjected to travel bans, and disqualified from managing companies for up to 15 years; criminal liability for wrongful trading also may arise.
AML and Unlicensed Virtual Office (CMRA)

Statutory sanction: Administrative fines up to GIP 500,000 per breach, suspension or revocation of the licence, freezing orders, and confiscation under the Proceeds of Crime Act; prohibition of continuing operations.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers and beneficial owners face criminal prosecution with up to 14 years' imprisonment for money laundering, personal asset confiscation, travel bans, and significant personal fines for AML compliance failures.
Transfer Pricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profits, additional tax of up to 100% of the underpaid tax, late payment interest of 5.5% per annum, and penalties of up to GIP 50,000 for failure to maintain proper TP records.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or execute sham cross-border arrangements can be held personally liable for the tax shortfall and penalties, subject to director disqualification, travel restrictions, and criminal sanctions for tax evasion if intent is established.
