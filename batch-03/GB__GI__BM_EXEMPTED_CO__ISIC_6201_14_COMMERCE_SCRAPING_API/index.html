Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar (HMGoG); Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, 6/8 Parliament Lane, Gibraltar; online portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation or https://www.gibraltartax.com

E-government filing portal

Gibraltar does not maintain a general VAT system because it is not part of the European Union VAT area, but companies with taxable activity must register for Gibraltar Income Tax (Gross Assessments under the Income Tax Act 2010 and Companies (Taxation and Consequential Provisions) Act 2020). A Bermuda Exempted Company that is tax-resident in Gibraltar due to central management and control must: (1) appoint a Gibraltar-resident company secretary and registered office; (2) incorporate or register as an external company under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014; (3) file Form CT1 (Company Tax Return) with the Income Tax Office; (4) obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (GTRN) from the Income Tax Office; (5) register for payroll tax (PAYE) and social insurance if it has Gibraltar employees; (6) register with the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) for AML/CFT supervision required for legal entities carrying on relevant activity. If the entity has no physical presence and is not centrally managed in Gibraltar, it is not tax-resident and has no Gibraltar income tax filing obligation, but it must still file annual returns with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies and maintain a registered office.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box classification equivalent to US federal income tax elections. However, the Income Tax Act 2010 permits election under the 'Gross Income Based Assessment' vs 'Income Tax Based Assessment' regimes; a qualifying company may elect to be treated as a 'Qualifying Company' under the Qualified Companies (Taxation and Consequential Provisions) Act 2020 subject to undertaking a specified activity and satisfying economic substance requirements. The Bermuda Exempted Company itself is generally exempt from Bermuda income tax under the Bermuda Companies Act 1981; in Gibraltar, the entity may elect to be treated as a non-resident company if central management and control is outside Gibraltar, subject to anti-avoidance rules and permanent establishment exposure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No Gibraltar VAT/GST; Gibraltar Customs duties and import excise taxes apply; UK VAT may apply to supplies of UK-established businesses under the UK VAT Act 1994 if the entity makes taxable supplies in the UK; Bermuda does not levy VAT/GST and instead applies customs duties on imports

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: no VAT threshold because no VAT is levied. For Gibraltar Income Tax, any taxable income arising in Gibraltar is subject to assessment; a company is registrable for taxation if it has a permanent establishment in Gibraltar or derives Gibraltar-source income. For UK VAT (if applicable due to UK establishment or distance selling), the compulsory registration threshold is £90,000 of taxable turnover over 12 months. Bermuda: no VAT threshold; Bermuda Customs duty applies at varying rates on imported goods and equipment, with no general sales tax registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Since Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and has no VAT, B2B cross-border supply of digital services (web scraping API and proxy gateway services) has no Gibraltar VAT charge. If the entity registers in the UK, exported digital services to businesses outside the UK can be zero-rated under section 7A VAT Act 1994 and Place of Supply of Services (Amendment) Regulations 2019, provided the customer belongs in a non-UK country, the supply is an electronically supplied service, and valid export evidence is retained. If the Bermuda Exempted Company maintains Bermuda operations, no export tax/zero-rating applies because Bermuda has no VAT. Supply of software/data-pipeline services to Gibraltar/UK B2C customers may be subject to UK VAT at 20% under the reverse charge if UK registered; otherwise no Gibraltar VAT applies.

Gibraltar does not mandate e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance for domestic B2B/B2C transactions. The Income Tax Office accepts digital tax returns via the iTax online system using Government Gateway credentials. The Customs Department requires electronic submission of import/export declarations through the Gibraltar Electronic Customs System (GECS) aligned with UK/NATO customs procedures. Bermuda: no e-invoicing mandate; customs declarations are filed electronically via BOAT (Bermuda Online Access to Trade) system. UK e-invoicing is not mandatory, but MTD (Making Tax Digital) requires digital record-keeping and API-based submission of VAT returns to HMRC where UK VAT registration applies.

Gibraltar Income Tax Office may re-open assessments within 6 years (or up to 20 years in cases of fraudulent or negligent conduct), impose penalties up to 100% of tax underpaid, and seek retrospective clawback of wrongly claimed export reliefs. For UK VAT, HMRC may assess for up to 4 years (extended to 20 years in deliberate wrongdoing), levy penalty percentage based on the 'behavioural' categories under Finance Act 2007 Schedule 24 (from 30% to 100%), and deny zero-rating where the supplier fails to obtain valid evidence of export within 90 days. Any misstatement of export zero-rating or use of unsubstantiated offshore structure could trigger accelerated payment notices under Finance Act 2014, and directors may be personally liable under Finance Act 2020 (Senior Accounting Officer and penalty rules). Bermuda: customs penalties under the Bermuda Customs Tariff Act 1971 and the Customs (Exportation) Regulations may require forfeiture of goods or monetary penalties equal to the value of the goods involved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates under a full-convertibility offshore monetary regime with zero capital account restrictions. The GIP is pegged at a fixed 1:1 parity to GBP, and both currencies circulate interchangeably. The Bermuda Exempted Company may freely remit profits, dividends, royalties, and intercompany settlements to any jurisdiction without exchange control approval or mandatory conversion. Web scraping API exports are classified as current account services (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 518210) under IMF BPM6, thus fully convertible without license or escrow. USDC and other virtual assets are not legal tender; however, GFSC-regulated entities may facilitate their conversion under the Financial Services (Distributed Ledger Technology) Act 2021. No capital admission, profit repatriation, or dividend remittance approval is required from the Bank of England or GFSC, subject only to FATCA/CRS reporting and Economic Substance (Companies) Act compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender obligation exists in either the United Kingdom or Gibraltar. The statutory conversion ratio is 0% and the repatriation timeline is indefinite; proceeds from cross-border data pipeline, API subscription, and commercial scraping services may be retained in GBP, GIP, USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency. The only administrative requirements are (i) accurate BoP statistical classification via the servicing bank, (ii) transfer pricing documentation evidencing arm's length retention, and (iii) Gibraltar Economic Substance Return showing income-generating activities consistent with retained balances.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (BoE) exercises sovereign monetary authority over the United Kingdom and its Overseas Territories; Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) supervises all licensed banks and virtual asset service providers in Gibraltar; Government of Gibraltar (HM Treasury, Currency Section) issues Gibraltar pound (GIP) notes and coin under the Currency Notes Act 1934, fully backed 1:1 by sterling reserves held in the Currency Note and Coin Reserve.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No Gibraltar statutory monetary threshold; OECD BEPS Action 13-aligned Local File should be prepared for controlled transactions exceeding GBP 1,000,000 per year; Master File is recommended when consolidated group turnover exceeds GBP 500,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to the business model of an exempted company providing data infrastructure and API services, classified as a 'distribution and service centre' or 'intellectual property activity'. The company must employ adequate full-time staff, incur operating expenditure, and make strategic decisions in Gibraltar in proportion to the value of its data pipeline and proxy gateway activities. A shell company without substance is treated as holding no Gibraltar source income, but faces a 0.1% ESR levy or re-characterisation of profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Statements

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to 100% of the tax understated plus statutory interest; criminal fine on conviction; strike-off of the Gibraltar registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow officers are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency; upon conviction, imprisonment up to 10 years and a travel ban from Gibraltar; the court may order civil restitution of the diverted profits.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Compulsory strike-off and deregistration; ESR assessment of GBP 100,000 annual concentration levy; denial of the participation exemption for the Bermuda parent.

Director personal liability: Section 234(7) disqualification orders of up to 15 years; personal liability for the issue of a certificate of compliance without substance; civil seizure order and travel restriction while assets are located.
AML/CFT Non-Compliance and Crypto Payments

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to GBP 250,000 per breach; criminal fine and forfeiture of cryptocurrency assets; suspension of any Financial Services Commission license held by an affiliated payment entity.

Director personal liability: Each director is personally liable for fraudulent compliance certificates; imprisonment of up to 5 years for causing/permitting prohibited conduct; the court may issue an international arrest warrant and freezing orders over foreign assets.
Transfer Pricing and Profit Repatriation Abuse

Statutory sanction: The ITO may adjust the transferred-profit to the arm's length standard, recompute taxable profits at 10%, assess underpaid tax and impose a 50%–100% accuracy penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign intercompany agreements at non-arm's-length rates face personal liability for tax deficiencies in insolvency; the UK-Gibraltar exchange of information allows the British court to make a property-freedom order, and intentional breach supports an extradition recommendation.
