Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

Bermuda Registrar of Companies, 55 Courts of Justice, 1 Court Street, Hamilton HM 12, Bermuda (formation of the exempted company); Gibraltar Registrar of Companies, Companies House, Gibraltar (registration of the foreign company with a place of business in Gibraltar).

Governing statute

Bermuda Companies Act 1981 (as amended) — primarily ss. 4 (exempted company status), 6 (memorandum of association), 7 (articles of association), 8 (registration), and 9 (certificate of incorporation), read with the statutory provisions on registered office, registers of directors and members, and the Bermuda Economic Substance Amendment Act 2019 — together with the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014, Part 9 (foreign companies with a place of business in Gibraltar).

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum of Association and Articles of Association filed with the Bermuda Registrar of Companies, culminating in the Certificate of Incorporation (Exempted Company); in Gibraltar, the Foreign Company Return (Form FC) filed under the Companies Act 2014, Part 9.

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by the Bermuda Companies Act 1981 for exempted companies; the memorandum must state an authorised share capital (customary standard US$12,000, e.g., 12,000 ordinary shares of US$1.00 each). Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar, Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax) — official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company desiring to operate in Gibraltar must first file a notice as an external (overseas) company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 2014. After the Registrar issues the Gibraltar registration number, the company submits the income-tax registration application to the Income Tax Office, together with the Bermuda certificate of incorporation, tax-exemption certificate, certificate of good standing, registered office address, and ultimate beneficial ownership particulars. The Income Tax Office then issues a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number. Where the company has no physical presence but contracts with Gibraltar customers, the Income Tax Office may register it as a non-resident company; no direct tax registration is required for purely non-situs trade.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — Provided the Bermuda Exempted Company is not managed and controlled in Gibraltar, it is treated as non-resident for Gibraltar income tax and all non-Gibraltar-source profits are exempt. If central management and control, or a permanent establishment, is established in Gibraltar, the company becomes taxable in Gibraltar on Gibraltar-source profits at the headline corporate rate of 12.5%.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box entity-classification regime. The Bermuda Exempted Company may rely on its Bermuda tax-exemption assurance under the Exempted Companies Act 2015 and may submit a formal non-resident company declaration to the Commissioner of Income Tax. No hybrid mismatch election exists under Gibraltar domestic income tax law; cross-border mismatch counteraction may be imposed through the UK hybrid mismatch rules where the entity is used within a UK-parented group.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — Gibraltar does not levy VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax. Importation is subject to Gibraltar customs duties and excise duties; export transactions are outside the indirect-tax net.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT/GST registration exists, therefore no economic turnover threshold applies. For goods movements the trader must obtain a Gibraltar customs registration (declarant identification number) from HM Customs; no compulsory indirect-tax registration arises for pure online resale of secondhand luxury goods except when goods are imported or exported physically through Gibraltar.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Gibraltar because no VAT is chargeable on domestic or export supplies. Consequently there is no zero-rating claim, no input VAT to reclaim, and no refund mechanism. If goods physically leave Gibraltar to the UK/EU, the destination-country import VAT regime governs at import; Gibraltar substantiation is limited to proof of physical export via a customs export declaration and commercial evidence of the cross-border supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration / combined declaration submitted via HM Customs Gibraltar
  • Commercial invoice (HS code, luxury-item description, condition/restoration certificate for vintage goods, sale value)
  • Bill of lading / air waybill / courier consignment note
  • Packing list with item-level serial numbers or unique identifiers
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 evidencing payment repatriation
  • Certificate of origin or proof of preferential origin (only if preferential customs treatment is claimed at destination)

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal-clearance, digital stamping, or API reporting mandate is imposed by Gibraltar. E-invoices are accepted, but the Income Tax Office requires maintenance of chronological invoice records and full underlying documentation for audit. Customs declarations are filed electronically through the Gibraltar customs declaration system.

Gibraltar HM Customs applies the Customs Ordinance 2016: false export declarations, forged invoices, or misdescribed luxury goods attract forfeiture, penalties up to three times the value of the goods, and on conviction imprisonment up to 5 years. The Income Tax Office can retrospectively revoke non-resident status if management-and-control facts are misrepresented; undeclared Gibraltar-source profits are clawed back for up to 6 years, with interest at 2% per month and penalties up to 100% of the omitted tax. Fraudulent claims to tax exemption in Bermuda cause voidance of the exemption certificate and liability for all Bermuda government fees.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound and sterling are fully convertible with no exchange controls, capital account restrictions, or profit remittance barriers for qualifying exempt companies; current account transactions are unrestricted, and dividends, liquidation proceeds, and routine trade payments may be repatriated without central bank approval. Non-resident shareholders face no currency conversion mandate, and the absence of a separate Gibraltar exchange control act obviates licence-based remittance hurdles. Beneficial ownership, economic substance, and AML obligations remain the only practical constraints on movement of funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion of export receipts applies in Gibraltar or the United Kingdom. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in the invoiced currency or any freely convertible currency. Banking institutions only require standard transaction documentation for anti-money laundering and sanctions screening purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for the Gibraltar pound, which is pegged at par to GBP) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the competent financial services and banking regulator

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rated Medium. GFSC-licensed electronic money institutions and UK FCA-authorised EMIs with appropriate passporting or recognition arrangements may open corporate accounts remotely for cross-border settlement. Pure offshore, unlicensed digital EMIs are not viable due to strict GFSC and UK AML screening, sanctions compliance, and the requirement that payment services be provided through a licensed intermediary in the EU/UK ecosystem.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing shall use SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for telegraphic wire transfers from the shareholder's bank to the Gibraltar operational account, with exact beneficiary name, IBAN or account number, and the SWIFT/BIC of the receiving Gibraltar bank. The remittance must reference a specific capital contribution agreement and corporate resolution. Payment purpose code should be completed as capital contribution (e.g. UK BoP 'C01' or bank-specific 'Capital Injection' code) and the bank should be supplied with a copy of the resolution, source-of-funds evidence, and onboarding documentation. For transfers in euro, SEPA CT can be used for smaller contributions, but for amounts above equivalent thresholds, a confirmatory SWIFT MT103 is preferred. All funds must pass through a dedicated GFSC-regulated bank account; no cash or third-party pass-through is acceptable.The preferred clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 wires for high-value international supplier settlements and commercial letters of credit (MT700) for batch procurements of vintage luxury inventory where the supplier requires shipment or title protection. For smaller euro disbursements, SEPA Credit Transfers can be used; for sterling, UK Faster Payments is acceptable for low-value operational costs. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Statutory filing deadline is nine (9) months after the end of the accounting period; for a 31 December accounting period, the return is due by 30 September of the following calendar year. Final assessed tax is payable within 30 days of the notice of assessment, and self-assessment instalments may be required for larger companies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds GIP 1,000,000 per accounting period; Master File is required where the multinational group consolidated turnover exceeds GIP 750,000,000, reflecting OECD BEPS Action 13 standards. The GRD may require the Local File below the threshold where there are related-party transactions with low-tax jurisdictions or high-risk payment flows.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance (Companies and Partnerships) for International Tax Purposes Act 2018 and the Bermuda Economic Substance (Exempted Entities) Act 2020 apply concurrently. An online consignment and secondhand luxury goods reseller is a 'distribution and service centre' where it purchases from related parties or performs service functions; therefore the entity must demonstrate core income-generating activities, senior management presence, adequate employees, physical premises, and local expenditure in the claiming jurisdiction. If management and control is in Gibraltar, the Bermuda exempt status cannot be used to shelter Gibraltar-source e-commerce profits. A pure shell company with no employees, no premises, and no board minutes in either Bermuda or Gibraltar fails both the economic substance test and the UK diverted-profits analysis; the GRD and the Bermuda Registrar will exchange information automatically with UK HMRC.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Mislabelling of Source

Statutory sanction: Reassessment on the full understated Gibraltar profits at 10%; a civil penalty up to 100% of the tax understated; statutory interest on unpaid tax; and criminal prosecution under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 with unlimited fines and imprisonment of up to seven years.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and penalties; the Gibraltar courts may impose travel bans and asset freezes under the Criminal Justice (Money Laundering) provisions; directors who knowingly sign false returns are subject to disqualification and custodial sentences.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The Bermuda Registrar may revoke the exempted-company status and strike the company off the register; the Gibraltar GRD may issue a domestic-source assessment and exchange information with HMRC; civil penalties of up to £100,000 apply under the Economic Substance Act; the UK HMRC may apply the diverted-profits tax rules and charge a 19% UK rate on profits attributable to a deemed UK PE.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a false economic substance declaration commit a criminal offence punishable by fines and imprisonment of up to five years; they face travel bans, disqualification from acting as directors of any Gibraltar-licensed entity, and civil restitution of the tax avoided.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Mandatory arm's-length adjustment of the chargeable profits; tax at 10% on the adjusted profit; penalty up to 100% of the additional tax; secondary adjustment treating the excess as a distribution; and daily surcharges for non-production of the Local File.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's-length pricing without a transfer pricing study may be held personally liable for negligence-based civil recovery; deliberate falsification of intercompany invoices triggers criminal liability, travel bans, and disqualification orders.
Anti-Money Laundering / Fraudulent Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the Gibraltar Money Laundering Regulations 2019, the company may face unlimited fines on indictment; e-money wallets and bank accounts are subject to freezing and forfeiture; enhanced Suspicious Activity Reports must be filed with the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit within 24 hours.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal liability for money laundering with imprisonment of up to fourteen years; travel bans, asset freezing orders, and seizure of passports are commonly imposed; directors may be permanently disqualified from managing a Gibraltar-licensed or EMI-regulated company.
Abusive Profit Repatriation / Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: UK HMRC may tax the distribution in the hands of a UK-resident individual at up to 39.35% (dividend upper rate); General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) counteraction; penalty up to 100% of the UK tax charge; interest from the due date; and denial of filing relief under the UK–Gibraltar arrangement.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise distributions while the company is insolvent are liable to insolvency officeholders for civil restitution; knowing participation in dividend stripping attracts criminal penalties, travel bans, and disqualification.
Failure to Maintain Books, Records, and Statutory Filings

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalties of £100 plus daily surcharges of £60 per day; company strike-off proceedings in both Bermuda and Gibraltar; loss of limited liability protection; disallowance of expenses for missing records; and automatic exchange-of-information requests from HMRC.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally accountable for the company's statutory obligations under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 and the Bermuda Companies Act 1981; persistent default leads to personal fines, disqualification, civil liability for debts incurred while defaults continued, and, in serious cases, imprisonment.
