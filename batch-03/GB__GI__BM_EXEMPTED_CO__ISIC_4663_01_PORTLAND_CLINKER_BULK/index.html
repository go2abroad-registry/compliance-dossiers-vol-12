Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax); HM Customs Gibraltar for customs and excise controls; UK HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) solely for UK VAT/NIC/CT where a UK taxable establishment arises.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company is not registered for Gibraltar corporation tax unless managed/controlled in Gibraltar or deriving Gibraltar-source income. If registration becomes necessary, file the prescribed business registration/income tax return support with the Income Tax Office; a Gibraltar tax file number is then issued. For customs, register with HM Customs Gibraltar for an importer/exporter code and comply with any transit/EORI requirement. No Gibraltar VAT ID exists because Gibraltar has no VAT. If a UK taxable establishment is created, UK VAT registration must be completed with HMRC, with the £90,000 taxable turnover threshold applying in addition to place-of-supply tests.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available under Gibraltar/UK law; the entity is classified by law as a non-transparent corporate body. The operative election is the Bermuda exempted company statutory tax exemption and the ability to maintain central management and control outside Gibraltar. Compliance with UK/Gibraltar anti-hybrid rules (UK TIOPA 2010 Part 6A; Gibraltar anti-hybrid provisions) is mandatory; no additional hybrid-mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax in Gibraltar; applicable indirect system is Gibraltar customs and excise duty (import duty and selected excise duties).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold applies (Gibraltar levies no VAT/GST/SUT); customs/excise registration and entry filing are required from the first import/export transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT, so no local export zero-rating or VAT refund claim is available. If a separate UK taxable establishment exists and goods are physically exported from the UK, UK zero-rating can be claimed under VATA 1994 Schedule 5 subject to HMRC evidence requirements; a pure Bermuda Exempted Company operating from Gibraltar and without UK establishment is generally outside the UK VAT net and cannot file a UK VAT refund.

  • C88/Single Administrative Document (SAD) or electronic customs export declaration accepted by the customs office of exit
  • Clean on-board ocean Bill of Lading or sea waybill evidencing loading at the port of export and delivery to the foreign buyer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin and ISIC 4663/HS cement clinker classification sheet
  • Inward remittance certificate, SWIFT MT103 advice or bank credit advice evidencing conversion and remittance of export proceeds
  • Bermuda Exempted Company status folder (incorporation certificate, tax exemption certificate), plus Gibraltar customs/exporter registration evidence

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time e-invoicing, continuous fiscal clearance, API data submission, or digital tax stamping for general wholesale trades. Customs entries are filed electronically to the Gibraltar/UK customs clearance systems; no fiscal e-invoice stamp is required for private commercial invoices.

As no Gibraltar VAT zero-rating exists, there is no local VAT clawback; but false export declarations or forged zero-rating evidence used to obtain UK VAT relief or customs duty suspension expose the exporter to retrospective HMRC and Gibraltar assessments (six years, unlimited where fraud), 100% tax/duty penalties under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007, forfeiture of goods and equipment, and criminal sanctions (including imprisonment) under the UK Customs and Excise Management Act 1979 and Gibraltar customs/anti-money laundering law.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both capital and current account. Gibraltar maintains no exchange controls, having mirrored the UK's liberalised regime since abolition of the Exchange Control Act (UK) in 1979. Inbound capital injections, profit remittances, dividend distributions, shareholder loan repayments, and repatriation of liquidation proceeds require no central bank, GFSC, or HM Treasury approval. GIP is a fixed-parity currency exchangeable 1:1 with GBP, and bank deposits are commonly held in GIP/GBP fungible cores with optional USD/EUR sub-accounts. The sole operational hurdles are ordinary AML/KYC verification, sanctions screening against the UK Consolidated List (extended to Gibraltar), and correspondent-bank source-of-funds diligence on the originating shareholder jurisdiction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender rules apply. Gibraltar imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation obligation for export proceeds. The Bermuda Exempted Company's Gibraltar branch may retain USD, EUR, and other foreign-currency clearing balances indefinitely in multicurrency accounts without conversion to GIP/GBP. Conversion is solely a treasury optimisation decision. Intra-group settlement from the GBP/GIP account to Bermuda is unrestricted and does not require exchange-control filing.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no domestic central bank. Monetary policy for the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) is effectively governed by the Bank of England (United Kingdom), as GIP is pegged at par to GBP. Currency issuance is administered by the Commissioner of Currency of the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act, while prudential regulation of banks, FX intermediaries, and clearing payment entities is delegated to the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Banking Act 2016.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. The GFSC licenses Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Electronic Money Regulations 2020; licensed EMIs may accept the Gibraltar-registered branch of a Bermuda Exempted Company on the basis of a valid Gibraltar branch registration certificate. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot issue documentary letters of credit (MT700), provide overdraft or working-capital facilities, or maintain protected deposits in the manner of a full bank; correspondent-bank friction also arises when settling USD-denominated ocean freight or non-SEPA trades. For bulk Portland clinker transactions, EMIs are viable only for auxiliary port-user charges, pre-export collection accounts, and small operational disbursements (up to 10-15% of monthly clearing volume), not as the primary liquidity rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed as an MT103 SWIFT wire from the shareholder's originating bank (typically an Asian or UK clearing bank) to the beneficiary account of the Bermuda Exempted Company's Gibraltar branch, with remittance narrative: 'Capital injection — Shareholder Loan Agreement dated [X] — Company ref [Y]'. For ISO 20022 CBPR+ corridors, the purpose code is B12 (equity) or S01 (related services), depending on the instrument. The branch must complete its Gibraltar BR registration and appoint a local resident representative prior to account activation. Amounts exceeding GBP 50,000 trigger a 24-48 hour compliance hold pending production of the board resolution, certificate of incorporation, and branch registration. GIP/GBP receipts are fungible at 1:1; USD receipts clear via the bank's US correspondent Nostro/Vostro without mandatory conversion.Outbound disbursements align with the target payment rails Swift_LC and TT_Wire. Supplier payments for Portland clinker are made by (i) SWIFT MT103 for freight deposits, port demurrage, tug and stevedore charges, inland haulage, and surveyor fees; and (ii) commercial letters of credit issued by MT700 (or MT760 standby LC for shipowner performance guarantees), commonly on at-sight or 90-day usance terms, with MT707 amendments and MT798 reimbursement instructions. Payment is conditional on presentation of the commercial invoice, Bill of Lading, SGS weight certificate, certificate of origin, and insurance certificate. Bulk ocean freight on CIF/CFR terms is settled by MT103 to the vessel owner's nominated bank in the charterparty currency (typically USD) via the Gibraltar bank's correspondent clearing network; GIP/GBP-denominated inland costs settle through UK Faster Payments/CHAPS where the beneficiary bank supports it.Deliverable FX Forward: USD-GBP, EUR-GBP and USD-EUR tenors up to 12 months, executable under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or standard bank Treasury Master Agreement.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF): permissible for emerging-market currency exposures (e.g., CNY, INR, IDR) where clinker is sourced from restricted-currency suppliers; booked offshore without any Gibraltar regulatory restriction.Vanilla FX Swap (rollover): temporary USD-GBP or EUR-GBP liquidity bridging between trade settlement dates.European-Style OTC Currency Options: permitted for hedging multi-month charterparty and commodity price exposures; requires basic appropriateness assessment if the Bermuda Exempted Company is not opted-up to professional-client status.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swap (CCIRS): permitted where a USD trade-finance or LC-discounting facility is converted to GIP/GBP liabilities; subject to collateral arrangements and disclosed in Bermuda GAAP financial statements.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

10%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar does not levy withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar company to non-resident shareholders, including a Bermuda Exempted Company.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a company with a 31 December year-end, the return is due by 30 November of the following year (e.g. FY2024 return due 30 November 2025); for non-calendar year accounts, due within 6 months after the accounting period end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Documentation (Local File and TP policy) is required when related-party transactions exceed GIP 500,000 in a fiscal year; a Master File is required for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue above EUR 750 million; all related-party transactions must be disclosed in the annual return.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance (Implementation) Regulations 2019 apply to all Gibraltar-resident companies and to non-resident companies claiming a tax advantage. For the core activity of wholesale distribution and ocean vessel freight of cement/clinker (ISIC 4663), the company must demonstrate: (i) physical office premises in Gibraltar; (ii) at least one full-time qualified employee in Gibraltar; (iii) board meetings and strategic decision-making conducted with a quorum in Gibraltar; and (iv) adequate operating expenditure and risk management undertaken in Gibraltar. Annual economic substance notification and reporting to the Gibraltar Commissioner of Income Tax are mandatory; failure subjects the entity to financial penalties and potential strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Non-Residence Declaration

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment of worldwide profits at 10% under the Income Tax Act; interest at 10% per annum; civil penalties of up to 100% of underpaid tax; criminal conviction for fraudulent evasion of income tax punishable by fines and up to 7 years' imprisonment for responsible officers.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the false declaration are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax and penalties; travel bans may be imposed during tax fraud investigations; disqualification as directors in Gibraltar for up to 15 years; confiscation of personal assets derived from the evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance penalties up to GIP 100,000 for the first year and up to GIP 100,000 for each subsequent year of non-compliance; re-categorisation of the entity as a shell with automatic exchange of information to Bermuda and UK tax authorities; strike-off and revocation of tax residency benefits.

Director personal liability: Company directors may be personally assessed for fines of up to GIP 100,000, subject to director disqualification orders, and the Registrar may pursue personal recovery of tax liabilities from directors of struck-off companies where the shell arrangement is proven.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Breaches

Statutory sanction: Civil and criminal penalties under Gibraltar's AML regime: fines up to GIP 5,000,000 for the company; confiscation of criminally derived funds; suspension of the entity's regulated licence; prosecution of money laundering offences carrying imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and the nominated Money Laundering Reporting Officer can be personally fined and imprisoned for failure to report suspicious transactions; travel bans and asset freezing are frequently imposed during AML investigations; personal liability for damages to affected third parties may also arise.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under the Income Tax Act: disallowance of inflated deductions, increasing taxable profit by the adjustment amount; interest on the resulting deficiency; penalties up to 100% of the tax avoided; referral of the case to the UK or other treaty partner for joint tax examination.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve false invoices or collude in the profit shift may be held jointly and severally liable for the additional tax and penalties; they may also face criminal charges for fraudulent accounting and personal disqualification; professional consequences include loss of certification as a Gibraltar fiduciary.
Profit Repatriation / Dividend Stripping Abuse

Statutory sanction: General anti-abuse rule (GAAR) reassessment re-characterising the dividend as a taxable distribution or denying deductions; penalties up to 100% of the tax avoided; cross-border exchange of information with Bermuda and UK where the arrangement is considered an impermissible tax advantage under Gibraltar law.

Director personal liability: Board members who approve abusive dividend-stripping schemes may be personally liable for the tax deficiency if the company is insolvent or struck off; travel restrictions can be issued during the GAAR investigation; directors may be prosecuted for fraudulent alienation of corporate assets.
