Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax), 42-44 Queen's Way, Gibraltar; taxpayer portal and e-services available through gov.gi/income-tax. Customs and excise administration: HM Customs Gibraltar, Customs House, Gibraltar, operating the electronic customs declaration interface with the UK Customs Declaration Service.

E-government filing portal

(1) Register the Bermuda Exempted Company as an external/overseas company under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014, appointing a Gibraltar registered office and local licensed company manager. (2) File the incorporation-and-registration pack with the Gibraltar Registry, including Memorandum and Articles, Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation and Certificate of Good Standing. (3) Submit application form to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar corporate tax reference number (Gibraltar Tax Identification Number), providing directors, shareholders, registered office and a declaration of central management and control. (4) Apply to HM Customs Gibraltar for a Customs Registration / Declarant Identification Code (Gibraltar EORI-style trader code) for all import and export declarations. (5) Obtain an Excise Warehousekeeper Licence and alcohol wholesale trade business licence covering bonded wine and spirits operations, together with an approved excise bond/security determined by HM Customs based on duty exposure. (6) Notify the Income Tax Office of the Permanent Establishment or branch activity in Gibraltar for assessment of Gibraltar-source trading profits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope; the Bermuda Exempted Company is taxed at the flat 10% Gibraltar corporate rate only on profits arising in or derived from Gibraltar (bonded wholesale wine and spirits trade), while foreign-source profits attributed to business conducted outside Gibraltar remain outside the territorial tax net. The historical Bermuda tax exemption certificate does not operate as an exemption in Gibraltar.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box regime exists. Gibraltar permits statutory elections: (i) tax-residency determination by central management and control under the Income Tax Act 2010; (ii) group relief or fiscal unity election for surrender of losses among Gibraltar-resident group companies; (iii) accounting-period / basis-period elections for first-year assessments; (iv) capital allowances elections (pooling and annual investment allowance); (v) anti-hybrid filing and adjustment elections aligned with BEPS Action 2 rules. No election can exclude Gibraltar-sourced bonded-trade profits from charge at 10%.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Customs and Excise Duty (Duty Suspension / Excise Bond regime). Gibraltar imposes no VAT and no GST; the proposed General Consumption Tax was never brought into effect. Import of wine and spirits is subject to customs/excise duty only upon release to home consumption in Gibraltar, while bonded movement and export operate under duty suspension.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT registration threshold exists in Gibraltar. Customs/excise registration is compulsory without any minimum turnover threshold; bonded wine/spirits warehousekeepers must hold an excise licence with a security bond fixed by HM Customs Gibraltar, and alcohol wholesale activity is separately caught by the Gibraltar Business Licensing Act irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT-style export zero-rating does not apply because Gibraltar has no VAT. Equivalent relief is duty suspension on export: wine/spirits removed from an approved excise bond for export outside the UK-Gibraltar customs territory are shipped free of import/excise duty; duty already paid on any such goods may be reclaimed under the duty drawback facility, subject to proof of actual export, conformity of goods, and expiry of no-residence-availability rules. Exports to the UK within the Single Customs Territory are not duty-exciting events and do not give rise to drawback.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD format lodged via HM Customs Gibraltar)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (original or authenticated electronic equivalent)
  • Duty-suspension removal document / warehouse movement record from the approved bond
  • Warehousekeeper's Bond Stock Ledger extract showing the specific removal
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with HS/CN code, weight, quantity and alcohol-by-volume details
  • Certificate of Origin and any required health/sanitary certificate for wine and spirits
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank inward-payment advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Excise movement document where transfer occurs within the UK-Gibraltar customs territory

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate (no SDI-type clearance). Gibraltar requires electronic customs declarations via HM Customs Gibraltar interfacing with the UK Customs Declaration Service; bonded excise stock movements must be recorded in the approved warehouse computerised stock system, with periodic electronic excise returns to HM Customs. E-invoicing is commercially acceptable but not tax-authority stamped.

HM Customs Gibraltar conducts risk-based audit of bond stock accounts, removal endorsements and export proof. False export claims or removal of duty-suspended goods without proper customs documentation triggers: (i) immediate clawback of all duty relieved or drawback paid; (ii) civil penalty up to 100% of the duty together with additional fines up to 3 times the value of the goods; (iii) criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 7 years and unlimited fines; (iv) forfeiture of the goods and any vehicle used; (v) revocation of the excise warehousekeeper licence and insolvency of the security bond. The absence of a statutory FX-patriation deadline does not remove the prudential 180-day benchmark imposed by the underlying bank and bonding arrangements as an evidence-integrity condition.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains an open, fully convertible exchange-control regime for current-account and capital-account transactions. There is no quota, no prior authorisation, and no differentiated 'blocked account' treatment for non-resident investors in a Bermuda Exempted Company. Profit remittance, liquidation proceeds, dividends and principal repayments may be freely converted and transferred in the original currency via a GFSC-regulated bank. Practical constraints are limited to AML/KYC source-of-funds verification and correspondent-bank sanctions filters; GIP is not accepted outside Gibraltar, so cross-border clearing is effected in GBP/EUR/USD at the official 1:1 GIP/GBP peg.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio and no prescribed conversion timeline for export receipts. 100% of receivables derived from bonded wine/spirits exports may be held in GBP, EUR, USD or any convertible currency, either onshore or offshore, provided the bank's transaction-monitoring system can reconcile each receipt to the underlying commercial invoice, Bill of Lading, warehouse warrant and excise duty-suspension record within 30 days.

Central bank supervision: HM Government of Gibraltar (Exchange Control) / Gibraltar Financial Services Commission; Bank of England acts as monetary anchor with Gibraltar pound pegged 1:1 to GBP

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium for a Bermuda Exempted Company in the bonded spirits trade. A UK FCA-authorised EMI cannot automatically passport into Gibraltar post-Brexit; a non-GFSC offshore digital EMI cannot lawfully hold segregated client money for excise duty-suspension or issue compliant bonded-transfer settlements, and most EMIs reject high-value wholesale wine/spirits and commodity flows. It may be used only as an ancillary collections account if licensed in the UK/EEA, but it must not hold capital escrow funds or be used for duty payments. Legal viability requires either a GFSC-authorised electronic-money institution or a full Gibraltar bank.
