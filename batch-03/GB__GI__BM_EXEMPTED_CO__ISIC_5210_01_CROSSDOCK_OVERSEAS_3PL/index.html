Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar: Income Tax Office, Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar (Ministry of Finance), 8 Secretary's Lane, Gibraltar; e-services portal at https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi and https://egov.gibraltar.gov.gi. Domiciliary (Bermuda): Bermuda Registrar of Companies, Ministry of Finance, and the Office of the Tax Commissioner, responsible for issuing the statutory Exemption Certificate; there is no Bermuda income tax authority because Bermuda levies no tax on profits. Customs: Gibraltar Customs Division of the Ministry of Finance for import-export licensing and duties.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Domicile: As a Bermuda Exempted Company, the entity is incorporated by the Bermuda Registrar of Companies and receives (a) a Certificate of Incorporation and (b) a Tax Exemption Certificate under the Exempted Companies Act 1981, guaranteeing no Bermuda income tax until at least 2035; no Bermuda TIN is issued because Bermuda does not impose profit tax. Step 2 - Gibraltar nexus: If the company carries on business in, or is managed and controlled from, Gibraltar, it must apply to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office on Form A (Application for Registration) to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN); there is short-form registration for non-resident companies claiming that no Gibraltar source income arises. Step 3 - No VAT ID: Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and imposes no VAT, GST or sales tax; therefore no VAT registration number is obtainable, and no VAT ID needs to be quoted on invoices. Step 4 - Customs: Any importation of goods into the Gibraltar cross-dock facility requires registration as an Importer with Gibraltar Customs under the Customs and Excise Duties Act, obtaining a Customs Economic Operator Code for electronic declarations. Step 5 - Documentation: Keep the Bermuda Certificate of Good Standing, Exemption Certificate, Gibraltar TRN acknowledgment, and customs registration in the corporate statutory records.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (default classification of the Bermuda Exempted Company under the Exempted Companies Act 1981, certified tax exemption until 2035), subject to the overriding condition that central management and control is not exercised in Gibraltar or through a Gibraltar permanent establishment. If management and control migrates to Gibraltar, the entity converts to a Gibraltar-resident company subject to Entity_Level_Taxation at the 12.5% Gibraltar corporate rate on worldwide profits; cross-dock fulfilment operations physically performed in Gibraltar may create a Gibraltar permanent establishment regardless of board location.

Available election forms

Bermuda: no check-the-box election exists; exempted-status and the tax exemption certificate are mandatory statutory incidents of the Exempted Company form, not elective. Gibraltar: the former Gibraltar Exempt Company and 0% Qualifying Company regimes were abolished by the Income Tax (Amendment) Act 2010; there is no elective check-the-box classification. Statutory elections available under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 include: (i) election of the standard 12.5% trading rate versus the 20% rate on non-trading investment income by activity classification; (ii) group loss relief elections and surrender of losses among Gibraltar-resident group companies; and (iii) for ATAD purposes, Gibraltar's Income Tax (Anti-Hybrid) Rules 2019 impose mandatory alignment of entity classification with the counterparty jurisdiction, permitting limited 'multilateral instrument' elections to neutralise hybrid mismatches. UK hybrid mismatch rules under TIOPA 2010 Part 6 TA do not extend to Gibraltar, so the entity should not rely on them.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar does not impose VAT, GST, or a general sales and use tax. The applicable indirect regime is the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Duties Act 2015 (as amended), which levies selective customs import duty and excise duties on goods imported into Gibraltar, principally alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oils, and motor vehicles. There is no tax on the supply of fulfillment, warehousing, cross-dock logistics, or e-commerce services. Exported goods are outside the charge to Gibraltar customs duty.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory economic turnover threshold triggers registration because no VAT exists in Gibraltar. Customs registration is instead obligatory from the first importation of goods into Gibraltar regardless of value or volume. A commercial presence, warehouse unit, or customs activity in Gibraltar for order fulfillment will require registration with Gibraltar Customs under the Customs and Excise Duties Act; there is no 'small trader' exemption for customs registrants.

Gibraltar has not implemented mandatory electronic invoicing (e-invoicing), real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) architecture. However, all customs declarations (import, export, transit, and re-export) must be lodged electronically through the HM Customs Gibraltar digital declarations gateway, which aligns with the UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS) and Union Customs Code datasets where applicable. Invoices for cross-dock and fulfillment services are not required to be fiscally validated, but must be retained in tamper-evident electronic format for at least six years to substantiate customs values and Income Tax Office audits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full capital- and current-account convertibility in both UK and Gibraltar. Exchange controls were abolished by repeal of the UK Exchange Control Act 1947 with effect from 24 October 1979; no equivalent restrictions are in force in Gibraltar, which follows the UK policy by Order. There are no prior approval, quota, licensing, interest-rate, or documentary requirements for inward/outbound dividend remittances, intercompany loan repayments, trade settlement, or repatriation of capital. Profit remittance from a Gibraltar-resident or non-resident exempted vehicle is subject to zero withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, or management fees at Gibraltar domestic level. The only operational hurdles are documentary, not statutory: banks require source-of-funds evidence, prescribed BoP reporting codes, and standard AML/KYC certification. Practical note: a Bermuda Exempted Company operating via Gibraltar for e-commerce 3PL should maintain a Gibraltar register of directors/UBOs to satisfy the GFSC's 'meaningful economic presence' assessment criteria, thereby mitigating de-risking.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation timeline, or currency-denomination restriction applies to export/fulfillment receipts in either the UK or Gibraltar. The historic UK surrender requirements under the Exchange Control Act 1947 were abolished in 1979; Gibraltar has never independently re-imposed them. Exporters and 3PL operators may retain foreign currency offshore, hold multi-currency accounts, settle in any freely convertible currency, and repatriate at their own discretion. Only residual statistical obligations exist: BoE/BOP VIT survey reporting (threshold-based, typically above GBP 20 million annual turnover per year) and HMRC Intrastat/extrastat declarations are no longer mandatory post-Brexit for non-EU trade but invoicing/financial statistics may be selectively requested by the Office for National Statistics. No BoE, HM Treasury, GFSC, or Gibraltar Statistics approval is required for fund conversion from USD/EUR into GBP/GIP.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (BoE) — UK monetary authority vested with financial stability oversight, GBP wholesale settlement system supervision (RTGS/CHAPS), and sanction/exchange-control policy competence. In Gibraltar, the Government of Gibraltar's Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the territory's banking and AML/CTF regulator; monetary and currency policy is pegged via the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) currency board arrangement at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP, with Bank of England notes and coin circulating interchangeably. The GFSC issues banking licenses under the Financial Services Act 2019, the Gibraltar Banking Act 1994, and the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, while BoE holds residual responsibility for UK financial stability, payment systems (FPS, CHAPS), and currency matters under the Bank of England Act 1998.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: High for Airwallex (the specified target payment rail), Revolut Business (UK EMR-licensed), Currencycloud (Visa-owned, e-money institute EMR 2011/2018), and Wise Business (full UK FCA EMI license); Medium-High for Tide/Modulr-based programs and multi-currency treasury abstraction layers. Legal constraints: (a) EMIs are not banks — they cannot lend, extend overdrafts, or accept deposits repayable on demand; funds held in safeguarded accounts (FCA safeguarding pursuant to UK EMR 2011, or GFSC for Gibraltar-licensed EMIs) do not benefit from FSCS protection but are ring-fenced on the EMI's balance sheet; (b) EMIs typically prohibit accounts for 'offshore exempted' structures unless the beneficial owner provides substantive economic-activity evidence (Gibraltar ES declaration, local contracts, rental invoices); (c) Airwallex and Currencycloud require multi-currency virtual IBANs — ensure the virtual IBAN is issued by an actual partner bank (e.g., Barclays, Standard Chartered, or a German/EU clearing bank) to avoid downstream correspondent bank de-risking; (d) flow must remain 'pass-through' — the treasury policy should designate the EMI as a sweepline collector for marketplace receipts and not as the primary fully-licensed settlement bank.

Compliant protocol: Equity injection from the Bermuda parent to the Gibraltar operational entity via SWIFT MT103 wire from the parent's corporate account (e.g., HSBC Hong Kong or Bermuda branch) to the Gibraltar bank's GBP/USD account. Transmission details: (a) Purpose code — for FDI-in-kind/equity: UK Balance of Payments code '1.A' (Direct Investment — Equity Capital, inward); for funding intercompany loans: BoP code '4.2' (Loans from Direct Investors) with reference 'IO/Intra-group funding'; (d) If the Gibraltar entity issues new shares, file the beneficial ownership update in Gibraltar and any Bermuda BMA capital-notification within 14 days.Clearing rails by priority: (i) SWIFT MT103 for ≥ USD/EUR/GBP 5,000 supplier payments, trade payments, and platform fee remittances; (iii) CHAPS for same-day high-value GBP disbursements to UK clearing counterparties (warehouse operators, buy-in settlement); (iv) SEPA Instant / SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated European warehouse and carrier settlements; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: High for Airwallex (the specified target payment rail), Revolut Business (UK EMR-licensed), Currencycloud (Visa-owned, e-money institute EMR 2011/2018), and Wise Business (full UK FCA EMI license); Medium-High for Tide/Modulr-based programs and multi-currency treasury abstraction layers. Legal constraints: (a) EMIs are not banks — they cannot lend, extend overdrafts, or accept deposits repayable on demand; funds held in safeguarded accounts (FCA safeguarding pursuant to UK EMR 2011, or GFSC for Gibraltar-licensed EMIs) do not benefit from FSCS protection but are ring-fenced on the EMI's balance sheet; (b) EMIs typically prohibit accounts for 'offshore exempted' structures unless the beneficial owner provides substantive economic-activity evidence (Gibraltar ES declaration, local contracts, rental invoices); (c) Airwallex and Currencycloud require multi-currency virtual IBANs — ensure the virtual IBAN is issued by an actual partner bank (e.g., Barclays, Standard Chartered, or a German/EU clearing bank) to avoid downstream correspondent bank de-risking; (d) flow must remain 'pass-through' — the treasury policy should designate the EMI as a sweepline collector for marketplace receipts and not as the primary fully-licensed settlement bank.
