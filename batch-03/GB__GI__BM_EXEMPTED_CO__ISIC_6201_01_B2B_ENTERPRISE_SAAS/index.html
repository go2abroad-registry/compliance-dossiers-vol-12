Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Gibraltar Companies Registry, a division of the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), eRegistry portal

Governing statute

Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 (ss. 5-27 formation and registration; s. 43 memorandum/articles; ss. 150-155 registered office; ss. 171-190 directors), read with the Income Tax Act 2010 (ss. 2, 8(1)(a): a company not managed and controlled in Gibraltar deriving no Gibraltar-source income remains outside the Gibraltar tax charge), the Economic Substance (Companies Provisions) Act 2019, and the Companies (Fees) Regulations. The Bermuda-model Exempted Company is constituted as a Gibraltar private company limited by shares carrying non-resident exempt status.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form GFM-1 (Application for Registration of a Private Company Limited by Shares) together with the notarized Memorandum and Articles of Association and Form GFM-4 (Statement of Compliance)

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up or authorized capital applies to a Gibraltar private company; a nominal issued capital of GBP 100 (100 ordinary shares of GBP 1 each, fully paid) is sufficient. No escrow or capital-gating account is mandated; subscription proceeds may be held in the company's client money account pending bank/EMI onboarding.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate and means-of-escape assessment for the registered office under Gibraltar fire safety legislation; occupational safety and health risk assessment for office premises; no civil-defense, structural-load or special workplace clearance applies to remote enterprise software operations.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up or authorized capital applies to a Gibraltar private company; a nominal issued capital of GBP 100 (100 ordinary shares of GBP 1 each, fully paid) is sufficient. No escrow or capital-gating account is mandated; subscription proceeds may be held in the company's client money account pending bank/EMI onboarding.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) for UK VAT and direct tax matters; Gibraltar Income Tax Office (ITO) and Gibraltar Customs & Excise Department for Gibraltar tax administration; Companies House Gibraltar for company registration

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company electing to register in Gibraltar must first obtain a Gibraltar Certificate of Incorporation or register as an oversea company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry. For Gibraltar tax purposes, apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) using Form GIL/1 (or equivalent corporate registration form). If the entity is managed and controlled in the UK, register with HMRC for UK Corporation Tax using Form CT41G and for VAT using Form VAT1 (or online via HMRC Government Gateway). As a non-resident company with no UK establishment, no UK TIN is required. The specific TIN format in Gibraltar is generally the company number issued by the Registrar of Companies.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda Exempted Company with Gibraltar branch or registered presence; tax classification depends on management and control / permanent establishment analysis). In Gibraltar, an exempted company is generally tax resident if managed and controlled there but is eligible for exemption on non-Gibraltar-source income. In the UK, if central management and control is in the UK, the entity is UK tax resident and subject to UK Corporation Tax on worldwide profits; otherwise it is non-resident and subject only to UK tax on UK-source income attributable to a UK permanent establishment. — A Bermuda Exempted Company electing to register in Gibraltar must first obtain a Gibraltar Certificate of Incorporation or register as an oversea company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda Exempted Company with Gibraltar branch or registered presence; tax classification depends on management and control / permanent establishment analysis). In Gibraltar, an exempted company is generally tax resident if managed and controlled there but is eligible for exemption on non-Gibraltar-source income. In the UK, if central management and control is in the UK, the entity is UK tax resident and subject to UK Corporation Tax on worldwide profits; otherwise it is non-resident and subject only to UK tax on UK-source income attributable to a UK permanent establishment.

Available election forms

Gibraltar: election for exempted company status under the Income Tax Act 2020 provides for exemption of non-Gibraltar-source income (subject to economic substance requirements). UK: no general check-the-box election exists; however, for US federal tax purposes, a Bermuda Exempted Company is a per se corporation and cannot elect pass-through treatment. A hybrid mismatch election may be relevant under UK CTA 2010 Part 6A if the entity is treated as fiscally transparent in another jurisdiction; a UK disregarded entity election is not available for a Bermuda exempted company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

UK: £90,000 (sterling) annual taxable turnover threshold for mandatory VAT registration over any rolling 12-month period. Gibraltar: no VAT registration threshold because VAT is not levied; customs registration applies for goods imported into Gibraltar.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For a Bermuda Exempted Company operating in Gibraltar, exports of enterprise SaaS services to B2B customers outside the UK/Gibraltar are generally outside the scope of UK VAT (place of supply: B2B services supplied to a customer belonging outside the UK are outside the UK VAT net). If the entity registers for UK VAT, zero-rating may apply to goods exported to non-UK destinations and to certain internationally related services, but digital B2B SaaS supplied to overseas business customers is not normally subject to UK VAT at all, so zero-rating is not usually claimed. For Gibraltar direct tax purposes, export of services does not attract Gibraltar VAT or customs duty; income from foreign-source B2B SaaS may be exempt if it is not derived from Gibraltar. Where UK VAT zero-rating is sought (e.g., exported digital goods), the supplier must hold proof of export and evidence that the recipient is a business customer belonging outside the UK, including the customer's VAT number or equivalent business registration evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (UK CDS or Gibraltar Customs SAD/documentation)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / Proof of Export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (for goods exports; for services, bank statements and payment records evidencing receipt of consideration from overseas B2B customers)

UK: Making Tax Digital (MTD) requires VAT-registered businesses to keep digital records and file VAT returns via HMRC-approved software; no mandatory real-time e-invoicing clearance or government fiscal stamping applies to normal B2B invoicing. A VAT invoice must be issued within 30 days for standard supplies, but no pre-clearance approval is required. Gibraltar: no e-invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate for corporate tax or customs purposes; import/export documentation is filed via the Gibraltar Customs electronic processing system.

HMRC may disallow zero-rating claims if documentation is incomplete or if the supply is treated as made in the UK. Penalties for deliberate or careless inaccuracy can be up to 100% of the VAT understated, plus interest. In fraudulent export cases, criminal penalties include unlimited fines and imprisonment. For Gibraltar tax, misdeclaration or failure to comply with economic substance requirements can result in penalties up to £10,000 per annum for low-risk relevant activities and higher for non-compliance, plus potential loss of exempt status and retrospective tax assessments. The Burlington Resources v HMRC case confirms that a taxpayer may elect to reduce a UK VAT reclaim by the amount of a foreign tax refund, but does not shift the UK zero-rating requirements.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no indigenous central bank. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued by the Commissioner of Currency (Government of Gibraltar) under the Gibraltar Currency Act 1934 and is pegged at par to the pound sterling, so UK monetary policy as administered by the Bank of England anchors the regime. Banking, e-money, and payments prudential supervision, licensing, and AML/CFT enforcement are conducted by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the GFSC AML/CFT Code of Practice; suspicious activity reporting is coordinated by the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit (GFIU). Sanctions screening obligations are owed to HM Treasury/OFSI consolidated list requirements as extended to Gibraltar, and to the GFSC-administered Gibraltar Sanctions Regulations 2020.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 5,000,000 for cross-border related-party transactions triggering Local File; group turnover exceeding GIP 50,000,000 triggers Master File; Bermuda ESR records are required regardless of threshold when any related-party transaction exists.

Economic substance scope: Bermuda ESR 2018 applies to the Exempted Company's relevant activities. Enterprise SaaS is treated as an IP business when income derives from proprietary software, requiring full economic substance in the registered territory: local employees, premises, expenditure and active management. If the entity is tax-resident in Gibraltar, the Gibraltar Economic Substance Code applies cumulatively.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Tax-geared penalty up to 100% of underdeclared tax, interest from the due date, fixed and daily late-return penalties, and criminal prosecution for false statements with fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise the false return are jointly liable for civil restitution, disqualified from acting as directors, and subject to travel bans and arrest warrants.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Bermuda ESR monetary penalties up to BMD 100,000, daily default fines, filing debarments and administrative strike-off; Gibraltar ESR and tax authorities may impose equivalent sanctions and deny treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable where the company trades with no substance; enforcement authorities may seek disqualification, asset confiscation and civil recovery orders against directors.
AML / Payment Rails Abuse

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission civil penalties, asset freezing orders, Stripe-Billing and Wise-B2B account closure, revocation of FinTech/EMI authorisation, and criminal prosecution under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2011 for money laundering.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners and directors may be extradited, banned from financial activities, subjected to unexplained wealth orders, and imprisoned for money laundering; the corporate veil is pierced for confiscation purposes.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Repatriation Mischaracterisation

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment, debt-to-equity recharacterisation, denial of deductions, corrective withholding tax, interest and penalties up to 200% of the misstated profits.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign intercompany agreements lacking economic substance face personal liability, civil recovery orders, travel bans and custodial sentences for fraudulent trading.
