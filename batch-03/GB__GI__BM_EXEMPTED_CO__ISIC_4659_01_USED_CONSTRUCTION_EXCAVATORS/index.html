Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

There is no minimum paid-up capital for a Bermuda exempted company; shareholders must subscribe the declared share capital in accordance with the Memorandum, and exempted companies are required to file annually a Compliance Certificate and, where relevant, an Economic Substance Declaration per the Economic Substance (Bermuda) Act 2018.

Funding & unlock path: Occupancy certificate from the Building Control Office and fire safety inspection by Gibraltar Fire and Rescue Service; annual electrical installation certification; workplace safety compliance under the Health and Safety at Work Act 1998 (as applied in Gibraltar) for staff operating weighbridges or moving heavy plant.

Paid-in capital rule: There is no minimum paid-up capital for a Bermuda exempted company; shareholders must subscribe the declared share capital in accordance with the Memorandum, and exempted companies are required to file annually a Compliance Certificate and, where relevant, an Economic Substance Declaration per the Economic Substance (Bermuda) Act 2018.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax), Eurotowers, Europort, Gibraltar — administering the Income Tax Act 2010 and the Corporate Tax Act 2024; corporate tax registration and returns are filed through the Gibraltar e-Taxation portal at https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation. Import/export indirect duties are administered separately by HM Customs of Gibraltar through the ASYCUDA World customs single-window.

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company must (1) appoint a Gibraltar-licensed corporate service provider to act as fiscal/registered agent if it requires a place of business or banking in Gibraltar; (2) file the Foreign Company Corporate Tax Registration Form (CTR-1 / TIN application) with the Income Tax Office, attaching notarised Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, register of directors and shareholders, beneficial ownership declaration (AML), and evidence of Bermuda exempt status; (3) within 5–10 working days the Income Tax Office issues the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN); (4) separately register as an economic operator with HM Customs of Gibraltar via ASYCUDA World for any import/export activity. If the company is non-resident and has no Gibraltar-source income, no tax return filing is required, but the TIN is mandatory for opening a Gibraltar bank account.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — a Bermuda Exempted Company is exempt from Bermuda taxation and, provided it is not managed-and-controlled in Gibraltar and does not carry on a trade through a permanent establishment in Gibraltar, it has no Gibraltar corporate tax exposure under the territorial source rules. The position switches to Entity_Level_Taxation (Gibraltar corporate tax at the standard 12.5% headline rate) only on profits attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment or Gibraltar-source trade. — A Bermuda Exempted Company must (1) appoint a Gibraltar-licensed corporate service provider to act as fiscal/registered agent if it requires a place of business or banking in Gibraltar; (2) file the Foreign Company Corporate Tax Registration Form (CTR-1 / TIN application) with the Income Tax Office, attaching notarised Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, register of directors and shareholders, beneficial ownership declaration (AML), and evidence of Bermuda exempt status; (3) within 5–10 working days the Income Tax Office issues the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN); (4) separately register as an economic operator with HM Customs of Gibraltar via ASYCUDA World for any import/export activity. If the company is non-resident and has no Gibraltar-source income, no tax return filing is required, but the TIN is mandatory for opening a Gibraltar bank account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — a Bermuda Exempted Company is exempt from Bermuda taxation and, provided it is not managed-and-controlled in Gibraltar and does not carry on a trade through a permanent establishment in Gibraltar, it has no Gibraltar corporate tax exposure under the territorial source rules. The position switches to Entity_Level_Taxation (Gibraltar corporate tax at the standard 12.5% headline rate) only on profits attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment or Gibraltar-source trade.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections for a foreign-incorporated entity are: (a) the transitional election under the Corporate Tax Act 2024 to step up the tax base cost of pre-2025 capital assets; (b) an election to deduct qualifying foreign taxes paid instead of claiming a foreign tax credit, or vice versa; (c) capital allowances pooling elections; and (d) ordering elections under the OECD BEPS hybrid-mismatch rules where the Bermuda exempt status creates deduction/non-inclusion or double-deduction mismatches. No pass-through election exists for a limited company; an Exempted Company is always taxed as a separate entity when Gibraltar-source profits arise.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT, GST or sales-and-use tax in Gibraltar; indirect taxation is confined to import customs duties (Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance), with exports of used machinery outside the scope of duty.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Gibraltar levies no VAT/GST, so no turnover threshold triggers indirect tax registration. However, any person importing goods into Gibraltar must obtain an HM Customs registration/economic operator code through ASYCUDA World, irrespective of turnover; duty arises on the first importation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Gibraltar: the jurisdiction has no VAT/GST system, therefore no 0% export VAT rating and no VAT refund mechanism exists. Used construction excavators and rollers (ISIC 4659) exported from Gibraltar are simply outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation. Where the goods were previously imported under duty suspension (e.g., customs warehousing or transit), eligibility for duty relief requires proof of physical export out of Gibraltar within the statutory time limits and lodgement of a valid export declaration in ASYCUDA World.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document lodged electronically via ASYCUDA World)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing physical exportation of the used excavators from Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or SWIFT MT103/telex confirmation) evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List describing the used hydraulic earthmovers/rollers (ISIC 4659 / NAICS 423810)
  • Certificate of Origin or preferential-origin document (EUR.1 / GSP Form A) where tariff preference is claimed
  • Heavy-machinery identification records (serial/chassis numbers and prior registration or ownership history)

No real-time fiscal e-invoicing, digital stamping or continuous transaction control mandates exist in Gibraltar. HM Customs of Gibraltar requires electronic lodgement of import/export declarations and supporting documents through the ASYCUDA World single-window; standard commercial invoices are accepted and no pre-clearance tax-authority invoice stamping applies.

HM Customs of Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office conduct post-clearance audits of export transactions. Although Gibraltar lacks VAT, fraudulent export substantiation triggers: (i) under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance, forfeiture of goods, monetary penalties of up to three times the duty value and criminal liability for false declarations; (ii) under the Income Tax Act 2010 and the Corporate Tax Act 2024, retrospective clawback of any exemption or relief obtained by misrepresentation, with interest and penalties of up to 100% of the tax understated; (iii) application of the General Anti-Abuse Rule, permitting recharacterisation of export structures lacking commercial substance, with a six-year assessment window (indefinite for deliberate fraud); and (iv) potential personal liability of directors/beneficial owners where the Bermuda Exempted Company is used to fabricate foreign sales or to disguise Gibraltar-source trading income.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates a fully convertible current and capital account regime. The Gibraltar pound is fixed at par to GBP sterling, with no exchange controls, no restrictions on capital movements, and no withholding taxes or profit remittance hurdles for non-resident shareholders. The Bermuda Exempted Company is tax-exempt in its home jurisdiction and, if managed and controlled outside Gibraltar, has no Gibraltar tax liability on its trading profits; nevertheless, the bank will require meaningful substance and a commercial rationale for a Gibraltar treasury booking centre.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Neither Gibraltar nor Bermuda imposes any statutory FX surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or earmarking requirement on export receipts. Trade proceeds may be held in USD, EUR, GBP, CNY via offshore nostro, or any freely convertible currency without compulsory conversion.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the integrated financial regulator; HM Government of Gibraltar via the Currency Notes Act as the issuer of Gibraltar pound (GIP); Bank of England as the monetary anchor and co-operator in UK financial sanctions administration.
