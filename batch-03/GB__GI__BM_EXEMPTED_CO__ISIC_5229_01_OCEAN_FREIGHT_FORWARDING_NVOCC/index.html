E-government filing portal

Step 1: incorporate the Bermuda Exempted Company and obtain the s.114 Companies Act 1981 Exemption Certificate from the Bermuda Registrar of Companies (no Bermuda TIN is issued; the company registration number serves as the identifier). Step 2: register the foreign company/branch with Companies House Gibraltar under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 to obtain a Gibraltar company registration number. Step 5: if the entity separately creates a UK taxable establishment, register with HMRC for UK VAT (parallel registration) and obtain a UK VAT number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda: the Exempted Company may elect under s.114 of the Companies Act 1981 to be exempt from Bermuda income, withholding and capital taxes (exemption guaranteed to 2035). Gibraltar: election of tax residence may be made by establishing management and control in Gibraltar, thereby limiting scope to Gibraltar-source profits under the Income Tax Act 2010 (standard 10% corporate rate). No check-the-box regime exists in either territory; UK anti-hybrid rules (CTA 2010 Part 6A) may disallow mismatches arising if the same payment is treated as deductible in Gibraltar and disregarded in the UK, and no elective safe harbour is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST in Gibraltar or Bermuda. Gibraltar levies customs and excise duties under the Customs Duties Act (aligned with the UK Global Tariff). The NVOCC ocean freight brokerage service is outside the scope of Gibraltar duties; UK VAT applies only to a completely separate UK taxable establishment.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory registration threshold in Gibraltar (GIP/GBP): no VAT system exists, so no VAT registration is required. Import/export customs registration with HM Customs of Gibraltar is required from the first transaction without a de minimis threshold. For a parallel UK establishment only, HMRC VAT registration becomes mandatory once taxable turnover exceeds GBP 90,000 in a 12-month period; below this, voluntary registration remains available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Locally not applicable: Gibraltar has no VAT, so the NVOCC's ocean freight forwarding from Gibraltar is outside the scope of indirect taxation and no 0% rate claim is made. Where a separate UK registration exists, 0% applies to freight transport and related freight-forwarding services if: (i) goods are physically exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK (ocean leg); (ii) the forwarder holds written evidence of export and shipping instructions; (iii) the supply is to a customer located outside the UK or directly to the exporter; (iv) the ocean shipment is not a domestic UK-to-UK movement; and (v) the books show the goods actually departed within statutory time limits. Bermuda imposes no transactional tax on export brokerage income of the Exempted Company.

  • Shipped-on-board Bill of Lading (ocean or house bill issued by the NVOCC) covering the port-to-port movement out of the UK

Goods connected with the UK are reported electronically through the UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS) for import/export pre-notification; invoices may be issued as paper or standard PDF without state fiscal codification. HMRC Making Tax Digital requires MTD-API returns only for UK VAT accounting, not for Gibraltar invoicing.

HMRC and HM Customs of Gibraltar may open a review at any time; assessments for under-declared or wrongly zero-rated outputs are normally issued within 4 years (extendable periods for fraud). Consequences of fraudulent export zero-rating: retrospective clawback of all incorrectly recovered input VAT with statutory interest; misdeclaration penalty up to 100% of the VAT amount (VATA 1994 s.67); penalties of up to 3 times the duty evaded and forfeiture of goods/vessels under CEMA 1979; criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 7 years; loss of 'reasonable excuse' protections; managerial penalties for careless errors; in Gibraltar, breach of the Customs Duties Act triggers duty repayment, monetary penalties, forfeiture, and revocation of the customs broker/forwarding licence; in Bermuda, misuse of the tax-exempt status can result in revocation of the Exemption Certificate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar imposes no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. The GIP is fully convertible to GBP, enabling unrestricted profit remittance and repatriation of capital for non-resident entities. Bermuda Exempted Companies registered as overseas companies in Gibraltar enjoy full access to foreign currency accounts and no approval is required for dividend or branch remittance. Tax-exempt status does not create any currency barrier, although a qualifying economic substance filing must evidence operational activity in Gibraltar to preserve any treaty benefits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Gibraltar. The entity is free to retain foreign currency (e.g., USD, EUR) in offshore or onshore accounts, invoice in its chosen trade currency, and convert as commercially needed. Beneficial owners must report balances under FATCA/CRS and local AML record-keeping requirements, but no forced repatriation or conversion is mandated.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the banking and AML supervisory authority; currency issuance is vested in the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act; monetary policy is pegged to the Bank of England's Sterling (GBP) via the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) at 1:1 parity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-to-Low for offshore-originated Bermuda Exempted Companies. Gibraltar is outside the EEA, so UK-licensed EMI (Electronic Money Institution) accounts may require a clear UK nexus or an EEA-registered address. A Gibraltar-registered overseas branch can open an account with certain UK EMIs (e.g., specialist freight-forwarding EMI platforms) if the ultimate beneficial owner profile and shipping documentation are sufficiently transparent. Legal constraints include lack of access to UK Faster Payments in GIP, and EMI providers often decline tax-exempt offshore entities due to perceived AML risk. A non-bank EMI can only serve as a secondary collection rail, never as the primary treasury settlement channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections are routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from a parent / shareholder bank to the entity's settlement account in Gibraltar. Recommended purpose codes: 'S0005' (Capital contributions) or 'G0200' (Trade-related credits) for operational funding. To avoid friction, the originator must include a reference to the Bermuda exempted company number and the Gibraltar CAS number. US dollar clearing is done via CHIPS or Fedwire through a correspondent of the Gibraltar bank; the bank will require a Funds Source Declaration and supporting share certificate. For NVOCC operations, use separate MT103s with proper FT (Freight) purpose codes, and avoid combining cargo payments with commission payments to reduce false-positive AML alerts. Mandatory Return Breakdown

The return is due on 30 June following the end of the chargeable period, i.e. within 6 months of the accounting year-end. A 31 December year-end therefore requires filing by 30 June of the following year. The Commissioner may grant an extension only on formal written notice.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: In GIP (Gibraltar pound), a Local File is required where the aggregate book value of controlled goods and services transactions exceeds GIP 1,000,000 per annum; a Master File is required where worldwide group revenue exceeds GIP 50,000,000 and the Gibraltar entity engages in controlled borrowing above GIP 2,000,000 or has gross intangible-related remittances above GIP 1,000,000. The documentation must be prepared before the filing deadline and produced on demand within 45 days.

Economic substance scope: Under the Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), a Bermuda exempted company carrying on ocean freight forwarding is treated as a 'relevant activity' where it provides services to affiliated undertakings. It must appoint at least two Gibraltar-resident directors, hold no fewer than four board meetings per year in Gibraltar, maintain a physical office, and keep adequate local staff and expenditure in line with the payroll returns. A company that is managed elsewhere but purports to be resident in Gibraltar is deemed a 'shell company' if it fails the adequacy of staff and expenditure test. Non-compliance attracts an initial fine of GIP 10,000 plus GIP 500 per day of default, escalating to a maximum GIP 500,000 for serious breaches; the Registrar may strike off the company after 30 days' notice.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraudulent Understatement)

Statutory sanction: Upon conviction in the Supreme Court of Gibraltar, the company is liable to a fine of up to GIP 500,000 and an order to pay restitution equal to 200% of the evaded tax. A non-conviction civil penalty of 100% of the understated tax is ordinarily imposed in the absence of voluntary disclosure.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false return face personal criminal liability for reckless fraud, punishable by imprisonment up to 7 years and a disqualification order of up to 10 years. The Commissioner may freeze personal bank accounts and issue a revenue judgment allowing execution against the directors' personal property after a corporate veil piercing analysis.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Income Tax Office may issue a deficiency notice requiring the company to demonstrate economic substance within 42 days. If the notice is unsatisfied, the company is deemed to have GIP 100,000 of taxable profits per year, taxed at 12.5%, and is liable to a persistent failure fine of GIP 50,000 and a 10% enhancement for each year of default.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the unpaid deemed tax and penalty, and the court may issue a travel restriction order preventing departure from Gibraltar without a security deposit equal to 150% of the total liability. A liquidator may also claw back salary or loans made to the directors as void payments.
AML and Transport Licensing Breach

Statutory sanction: The Financial Services Commission may impose a Level 4 penalty of GIP 250,000 for unlicensed remittance activity; the Supreme Court can order forfeiture of the full transacted value and impose imprisonment of up to 2 years for the responsible officer.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise the movement of funds may be prosecuted as beneficial owners, and the Companies Act permits the Commissioner to lift the veil and issue a charging order over the directors' personal property in Gibraltar to enforce unpaid fines.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting / Mispricing of Charter Hire

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner is empowered to make a transfer pricing adjustment, taxing the revised profit at 12.5%, imposing a penalty of 75% of the additional tax where the error is deliberate, and a separate GIP 20,000 penalty for failure to provide the Local File.

Director personal liability: Where the mispricing is intentional, each director is jointly and severally liable to the tax authority for the full adjustment up to GIP 500,000 and may be barred from holding any directorship of a Gibraltar entity for a period of 8 years.
