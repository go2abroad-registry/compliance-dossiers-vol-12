Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Not permissible for a company engaged in regulated Good Distribution Practice (GDP) pharma freight logistics. A physical registered office in Gibraltar is mandatory (Section 29, Companies Act 2014). The company must maintain a physical presence, including either (i) a lease of commercial premises in Gibraltar, (ii) a wholly-owned subsidiary with real office facilities, or (iii) a serviced office agreement with a licensed Gibraltar registered agent that provides dedicated desk space and staff. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility with zero exchange-control restrictions; there are no limits on capital injections, profit repatriation, dividend remittances, or liquidation proceeds for non-resident-owned Bermuda Exempted Companies. The GIP/GBP peg eliminates intra-Gibraltar conversion risk, and USD/EUR liquidity is freely accessible through correspondent banking. The Bermuda Exempted Company is designated non-resident for Bermuda exchange-control purposes, enabling unrestricted holding of foreign-currency accounts and global fund flows, subject only to GFSC anti-money-laundering record-keeping rather than prior approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation exists under Gibraltar or Bermuda law for a tax-exempt non-resident company; export receipts generated by the GDP freight forwarding scope may be held indefinitely in the original invoicing currency (USD, EUR, or GBP/GIP) without mandatory conversion or repatriation. The only compliance duty is the GFSC-required preservation of underlying trade documentation (invoices, airway bills, GDP shipment records) for five years to substantiate the source and end-use of funds during bank transaction monitoring.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) in coordination with the Bank of England; the Gibraltar pound (GIP) circulates at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP and the Government of Gibraltar issues local banknotes, while monetary stability and banking supervision are exercised through the GFSC under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Banking Ordinance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-to-Low viability; Gibraltar accepts only GFSC-licensed e-money institutions or passported UK FCA-authorised EMIs under the EEA temporary passporting regime, and no major offshore digital EMI (Wise, Revolut, or similar) holds a Gibraltar banking licence. Offshore EMIs without a physical Gibraltar representative and full banking relationship are unsuitable for the capital-escrow function because they cannot issue documentary credits, execute MT103 priority wires, or provide banker's acceptance for commercial LC flows. Use of unlicensed foreign EMIs for the GDP freight operational account carries a materially higher risk of frozen funds and regulatory rejection by counterparty commercial banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share-capital and working-capital injections route via compliant SWIFT MT103 bank-to-bank wires from the Bermudan parent or the UK holding entity directly into the Gibraltar operating account using purpose codes that clearly describe intra-group financing (e.g., 'Equity injection', 'Investment capital', or 'Shareholder loan') with corresponding commercial supporting memoranda. The remitting bank will require a signed board resolution authorising the transfer, evidence identifying the source of wealth at the corporate level, and a confirmation that the funds are not derived from a sanctioned jurisdiction; funds should be settled in GIP/GBP or USD only, and cryptocurrency or alternative payment systems must be avoided to maintain GFSC and correspond bank acceptability.Settlement of trade disbursements for the GDP logistics business is executed through the Swift gpi-enabled MT103 single customer credit transfer rail for low-to-medium value receivables and inventory payments, while high-value clinical logistics contracts settle via a commercial letter of credit opened by the Gibraltar bank acting as issuing bank under ICC Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600); the MT202 COV and MT202 generally must not be used for customer disbursements unless an investment or treasury payment is involved.
