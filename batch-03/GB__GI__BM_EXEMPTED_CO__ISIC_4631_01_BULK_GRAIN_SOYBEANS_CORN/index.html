Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office; official online portal: eTax (https://etax.gov.gi). Indirect taxes and customs are administered by HM Customs and Excise Gibraltar (https://customs.gov.gi).

E-government filing portal

The Bermuda Exempted Company, operating as a foreign corporate entity in Gibraltar, must first register external road presence under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 as an Overseas Company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry. Upon registration, the company receives a Gibraltar Company Number. For taxation purposes, the entity must then file Form GTS-01 'Application for Gibraltar Taxpayer Identification Number (GTIN)' with the Income Tax Office, attaching: (1) certified certificate of incorporation from Bermuda, (2) memorandum and articles of association, (3) certificate of good standing issued by the Registrar of Companies in Bermuda, (4) notice of appointment of a Gibraltar resident tax agent, and (5) declaration as to management and control location. The application is lodged through eTax and the GTIN is issued within 5-7 business days; for pure offshore non-resident activities, the GTIN will be annotated as 'NRDoF' (non-resident / offshore) with no assessable income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar is not a US-check-the-box jurisdiction. Available statutory elections include: (i) election to be treated as a Gibraltar tax-resident company under the territorial tax rules if central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar (Income Tax Act, s.22); (ii) election for the financial-year grouping of intercompany transactions for arm's length purposes; (iii) filing of a hybrid mismatch notice under the Income Tax (Hybrid and Branch Mismatches) Regulations, expressly electing out of deemed transparent treatment; and (iv) grandfathered 'Qualifying Company' election for legacy exempt entities (closed to new applicants). For the Bermuda Exempted Co., no election to be taxed at entity level in Bermuda is required as Bermuda imposes no income tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Customs and Excise Duty (no VAT, no GST, no sales and use tax – Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area but applies Customs and Excise Duties under Gibraltar law)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – Gibraltar does not levy VAT or GST. There is no economic turnover threshold for indirect taxation registration. A commercial importer or exporter of bulk commodities must hold a Customs Registration Certificate issued by HM Customs and Excise Gibraltar irrespective of turnover; exports of agricultural bulk grains are zero-rated. Excise licenses are required for alcohol, tobacco, and fuel, with no de minimis threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because Gibraltar has no VAT, zero-rating is achieved by the direct application of a 0% duty rate on goods physically exported to a destination outside Gibraltar. The Bermuda Exempted Company must satisfy HM Customs that: (i) the goods (soybeans, yellow corn, bulk grains) were supplied under a fully documented contract with a non-resident buyer; (ii) the goods were exported within 90 days of release; (iii) the freight and insurance documents conclusively demonstrate physical removal from Gibraltar; and (iv) payment from the foreign buyer is received in convertible currency with a corresponding inward remittance certificate. Cross-border brokerage services to offshore agricultural buyers are 'outside the scope' of Gibraltar income tax and customs duties, thereby functionally zero-rated without any refund application.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal e-invoicing in the EU VAT sense. Customs declarations must be filed electronically through the Gibraltar Electronic Customs System ('GECS'), which performs a real-time risk-assessment and issue of digital clearance messages before the goods leave the customs area. All export invoices for bulk grain, soybeans, and corn must be transmitted as structured electronic data (XCBL/UBL) for customs matching; no separate digital stamping is required for zero-rated commodity exports, but excise-controlled goods require digital duty stamps through the Excise Movement and Control System (EMCS).

HM Customs and Excise Gibraltar may audit any export zero-rating claim for up to 6 years. Where fraudulent documents have been filed or where exportation did not physically occur, the following penalties apply: forfeiture of the goods; a financial penalty greater than the duty at 300% of the evaded duty; retrospective clawback of any export-related relief, drawback, or exemption granted; interest at 8% per annum from the date of the false declaration; criminal prosecution of officers and/or directors with imprisonment of up to 7 years pursuant to the Customs Ordinance; disqualification of the Bermuda Exempted Company's local agent; and notification of the sanctions to Bermuda and the UK authorities through the competent authority exchange of information agreements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both Gibraltar pound (GIP) and Great British pound (GBP) are fully convertible with no separate exchange-control restrictions. Capital and current account transactions are freely permitted under the United Kingdom and Gibraltar legal framework. Profit remittance by a Bermuda Exempted Company is not subject to Gibraltar withholding tax, but dividend distributions and non-resident shareholding structures must satisfy economic-substance, beneficial-ownership, and source-of-funds verification requirements imposed by local authorised banks to avoid de-risking delays.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio applies to bulk grain and commodity export receipts held by Gibraltar-incorporated entities. Exporters are free to retain, transfer, or convert foreign currency earnings but must preserve underlying trade documentation for AML/CFT audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (United Kingdom monetary authority); H.M. Treasury retains competence for Gibraltar's foreign exchange policy; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the local regulatory supervisor for all banks operating in Gibraltar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High for auxiliary collection and low-value operational disbursements. Gibraltar and London-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise and Revolut may accept e-money balances for ancillary costs, but most EMIs classify agri-commodity wholesale brokerage as higher risk and restrict letter-of-credit or trade-settlement flows. Offshore-EMI reliance is not a compliant substitute for full-service clearing bank accounts tied to SWIFT MT103 and commercial LC operations.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% (standard Gibraltar corporation tax rate applicable to resident companies and to non-resident companies carrying on a trade through a permanent establishment)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Gibraltar does not impose dividend withholding tax. Dividends paid by a Bermuda Exempted Company are likewise outside Gibraltar's tax net because Bermuda levies no income tax, and Gibraltar legislation does not attribute dividends to source for foreign shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within twelve months after the end of the accounting period, unless a shorter period is stipulated by the Commissioner of Income Tax. For a 31 December year-end the deadline is 31 December of the following year; instalment payments of estimated tax are required on 30 April and 30 September.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Taxpayer Identification: registered legal name, Gibraltar registration number (if registered as an external company), registered office address, and tax residency classification. Enter the full Bermuda Exempted Company name as shown on the certificate of incorporation, the Bermuda exempted company number, and the address of the Gibraltar branch or agent. Mark the entity as a 'non-resident company' and, if relying on a tax-exempt status in Bermuda, attach the relevant extract from the Bermuda Registrar of Companies and the Certificate of Exemption from Bermuda taxation. Late filing attracts an initial fixed penalty of GBP 300 plus a daily default surcharge of GBP 10 for every day the return remains outstanding after a 30-day grace period, subject to a maximum of GBP 900. If the return is more than six months late, a further 10% of the tax liability is imposed. Inaccurate returns understatement penalties range from 50% to 100% of the additional tax where deliberate and concealed. Criminal prosecution can result for persistent failure to file.
Box 2 Gibraltar-source trading profits: all income and receipts attributable to a permanent establishment in Gibraltar, including brokerage commissions and margins from the sale or charter of bulk ocean cargoes of soybeans and yellow corn. Compute only the arm's length proportion of net profit attributable to the Gibraltar PE. In the absence of a PE in Gibraltar, state 'NIL' and attach a factual memorandum explaining that core decision-taking and commencement of contract negotiations occur outside Gibraltar. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Gibraltar Local File must be prepared and retained where aggregate controlled transactions (purchases, sales, fees, interest, and commodity brokerage commissions) exceed GBP 1,000,000 per accounting period. A Gibraltar Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds GBP 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations 2020 require any entity carrying on a relevant activity, including brokerage, in Gibraltar to meet 'Core Income Generating Activity' (CIGA) tests: physically establishing and managing the commodity brokerage, hiring qualified staff, incurring operating expenditure, and undertaking senior management functions in the jurisdiction. Non-resident Bermuda companies with a Gibraltar PE are subject to the same tests for their activity. Failure leads to reclassification as tax resident and penal taxes. The doctrine reinforces the need for a real office, accountable bank accounts, and locally negotiated letter of credit contracts for cargoes of soybeans and yellow corn.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Concealment of Income

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty equal to 100% of the underpaid tax; interest on the underpaid amount at 10% per annum; on conviction, a fine of up to GBP 500,000 and imprisonment of up to seven years. Additionally, the Commissioner may make an estimated assessment at 200% of the presumed chargeable profits.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax; the High Court may issue travel bans, asset seizure and a freezing order over the director's personal property under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015. Imprisonment terms of up to seven years are imposed on individuals.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Strike-off from the register of companies, cancellation of any Gibraltar electricity or trade licences, and an administrative penalty of up to GBP 100,000 for non-compliance with the Economic Substance Regulations. The Commissioner may also charge tax on worldwide profits at 12.5% on the basis of management and control deemed exercised in Gibraltar.

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified for up to 15 years and held personally liable for the corporate tax arrears; the corporate veil is pierced, exposing the directors' personal assets. Travel restrictions and 12-month bans on holding directorships may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Violation

Statutory sanction: Profits are adjusted to the arm's-length standard, with additional corporation tax at 12.5%; a negligence penalty of 50% of the substitution amount is applied, increasing to 100% for intentional distress. Interest is charged at 12% per annum on the adjustment. The failure to produce documentation attracts a fixed penalty of GBP 20,000 and daily penalties of GBP 500 until the documents are delivered.

Director personal liability: The finance director and all directors signing the tax return are jointly liable for any understated tax where the transfer-pricing records are falsified. A tribunal may issue personal contribution orders; serious deliberate misconduct results in criminal prosecution and imprisonment of up to three years.
AML, Beneficial Ownership and Fraudulent Conduit

Statutory sanction: Contravention of the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the Beneficial Ownership (Access) Regulations 2019 carries fines of up to GBP 10 million for corporate entities and up to GBP 500,000 for individuals. Real property and bank balances derived from the scheme may be confiscated under the Criminal Assets Confiscation Act. The company's banking relationship with a correspondent in London or elsewhere may be terminated and the LC dishonoured.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face criminal custodial sentences of up to 14 years; civil recovery orders may be made against any person who has directly or indirectly profited from the unlawful conduit; de facto directors are subject to asset freeze orders and travel bans pending trial; and individuals may be extradited under the UK-Gibraltar arrangements for investigation in the United Kingdom.
