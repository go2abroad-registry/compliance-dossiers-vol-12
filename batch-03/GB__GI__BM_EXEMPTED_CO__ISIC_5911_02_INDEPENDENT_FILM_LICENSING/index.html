Companies Act 1981 (Bermuda), ss. 4, 14, 129 (exempted company formation, bye-laws and exempted status); Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 (statutory tax exemption guarantee to 2035); Exchange Control Act 1972 and BMA consents; for the Gibraltar operating presence: Companies Act 2014 (Gibraltar), Part 22 (registration of external companies) and the Companies (External Companies) Regulations.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

H.M. Government of Gibraltar Income Tax Office, Grand Ocean Plaza, 61-63 Harbour View, Gibraltar; official portal at https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation-and-customs. Customs and excise matters are administered by the Gibraltar Customs Department under the Customs and Excise Duties Act.

E-government filing portal

A foreign Bermuda Exempted Company is not automatically obliged to register with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office. Registration is required only if the company is managed and controlled in Gibraltar, carries on a trade through a permanent establishment in Gibraltar, or derives Gibraltar-source income. Where registration is needed, the company must submit a certified copy of its Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, certificate of good standing/current compliance, memorandum and articles of association, a board resolution appointing a Gibraltar representative if required, and a completed company registration form to the Commissioner of Income Tax. The Commissioner will then issue a Gibraltar Tax Assessment Number (TAN). For non-resident companies, a voluntary TAN application is often made to satisfy bank due diligence, contract counterparties, or withholding-exemption requests.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - as a Bermuda Exempted Company, the entity is outside the scope of Gibraltar corporate tax while it remains non-resident and does not generate Gibraltar-source income. If management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, the entity is treated as Gibraltar-resident and becomes subject to entity-level Gibraltar corporate income tax; there is no pass-through or transparent treatment for limited companies in Gibraltar.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no US-style check-the-box or fiscal-transparency election. A Bermuda Exempted Company is treated as an opaque entity under Gibraltar law by default. An entity cannot elect to be fiscally transparent. It may, however, elect to be treated as non-resident if its central management and control is outside Gibraltar, and it may apply for a ruling confirming no taxable presence for film-licensing activities. Hybrid mismatch rules, aligned with the ATAD framework, will override any mismatching treatment if, for example, the Bermuda company is considered tax-transparent in another jurisdiction; Gibraltar will disallow or recharacterise such deductions and no taxpayer election can avoid that statutory recharacterisation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

VAT - not in operation in Gibraltar. Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and imposes no value added tax, GST, or general sales-and-use tax. Film-licensing royalties and cross-border digital distribution payments are not chargeable to Gibraltar VAT.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A - no VAT/GST registration threshold applies in Gibraltar; there is no Gibraltar VAT identification number. Registration with the Gibraltar Customs Department is required only for importers of dutiable goods such as alcoholic beverages, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil, and amusement/gaming equipment. No economic turnover threshold triggers indirect-tax registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

No Gibraltar VAT zero-rating or refund process exists because Gibraltar does not operate a VAT system. For pure film-licensing intangibles, no VAT is chargeable and therefore no zero-rating claim is required. If physical film deliverables are exported from Gibraltar, duty relief is available only upon satisfactory proof of physical export, but the licensing royalty stream itself is outside the ambit of Gibraltar indirect taxation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar SAD/Bill of Entry) where physical film media are exported
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical movement of goods from Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice confirming receipt of the licensing consideration
  • Original copyright licence agreement, royalty schedules, and documentary title-flow showing ownership of the film rights in the Bermuda Exempted Company

None - Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, API transmission, or digital stamping. Commercial invoices and licensing contracts govern the transaction. Physical goods exported or imported through Gibraltar must be declared using the Gibraltar Customs Single Administrative Document (SAD) / electronic customs entry platform, but this is a customs clearance mechanism, not a tax-invoice clearance system.

Gibraltar has no VAT zero-rating mechanism to claw back, but fraudulent customs declarations, forged export documents, or mischaracterisation of royalties as exported goods trigger severe consequences under the Customs and Excise Duties Act, including loss of duty relief, immediate assessment of duty, seizure and forfeiture of goods, civil penalties of up to 200% of the evaded duty, and criminal fines plus imprisonment. If the Bermuda Exempted Company is reassessed as Gibraltar-resident or as having a Gibraltar permanent establishment, undeclared licensing income becomes taxable at the Gibraltar corporate rate, with interest and default penalties of up to 100% of the tax due; the fraudulent non-disclosure limitation period does not apply.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP is fully convertible at 1:1 parity with GBP and no exchange-control restrictions are imposed by Gibraltar. The Bermuda exempted company, correctly designated as non-resident under Bermuda exchange control, holds external accounts and is free to convert, transfer, and remit both current-account royalties and capital-account equity without prior Bermuda Monetary Authority approval. Profit remittance to shareholders is unrestricted in form, but practical documentary hurdles remain: board minutes, dividend declaration, tax residence evidence, audited financial statements, and a demonstrable economic-substance file. There are no withholding or exchange-control gates on ordinary film-licensing royalty distributions through Gibraltar.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Neither Gibraltar nor Bermuda imposes mandatory export-proceeds surrender or conversion ratios on a non-resident Bermuda exempted company. Foreign-currency receivables from film licences may be retained in USD, EUR, or GBP accounts; conversion to GIP/GBP is executed at freely negotiated market rates. For audit and AML continuity, the receiving Gibraltar bank should issue an inbound remittance certificate and the company should preserve the underlying licensing invoice and collection statement for every receipt.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no separate central bank; the monetary anchor for the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is the Bank of England, banking conduct is supervised by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission, and exchange-control administration for the Bermuda exempted company is vested in the Bermuda Monetary Authority under the Bermuda Exchange Control Act 1953.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium but constrained. GFSC-regulated or UK FCA-authorised e-money institutions may provide quick SWIFT/IBAN account onboarding and low-value disbursement rails, but they are not full credit institutions. They generally cannot issue commercial letters of credit, provide treasury overdrafts, or execute deliverable FX forwards without a banking partner. For an offshore Bermuda exempted company, most EMIs will decline onboarding unless the company demonstrates genuine Gibraltar substance, audited accounts, and a natural-person director with identifiable tax residence. Use a tier-1 bank for capital flows and trade instruments; use an EMI only for minor administrative disbursements after full KYC is already held by the principal bank.

Late filing triggers an immediate GIP 250 fixed penalty, a GIP 30 per-day continuing surcharge, interest at 8% p.a. on unpaid tax, and additional tax-geared penalties of up to 200% of the assessed liability for deliberate non-compliance.
Box 2 — Accounting period Start and end dates of the fiscal year and any prior-period adjustments. Use the Bermuda financial year; where a Gibraltar branch exists, align the branch accounts to the same accounting period. Late filing triggers an immediate GIP 250 fixed penalty, a GIP 30 per-day continuing surcharge, interest at 8% p.a. on unpaid tax, and additional tax-geared penalties of up to 200% of the assessed liability for deliberate non-compliance.
Box 3 — Chargeable profits Gibraltar-source gross profits after territorial-scope adjustments and allowable deductions. Report only profits attributable to Gibraltar activities or to a Gibraltar permanent establishment; non-Gibraltar-source licensing income is excluded if no permanent establishment is present in Gibraltar. Late filing triggers an immediate GIP 250 fixed penalty, a GIP 30 per-day continuing surcharge, interest at 8% p.a. on unpaid tax, and additional tax-geared penalties of up to 200% of the assessed liability for deliberate non-compliance.
Box 4 — Deductions and loss relief Allowable deductions, depreciation, irrecoverable debts, and carried-forward losses. A non-resident company may claim only deductions wholly and exclusively attributable to Gibraltar-source income; foreign head-office costs are apportioned on an arm's-length basis. Late filing triggers an immediate GIP 250 fixed penalty, a GIP 30 per-day continuing surcharge, interest at 8% p.a. on unpaid tax, and additional tax-geared penalties of up to 200% of the assessed liability for deliberate non-compliance.
Box 5 — Tax computation and payment Gross tax at 12.5%, tax credits, prepayments, and balance due. If no Gibraltar-source profits or permanent establishment exists, file a nil return to avoid administrative default penalties. Late filing triggers an immediate GIP 250 fixed penalty, a GIP 30 per-day continuing surcharge, interest at 8% p.a. on unpaid tax, and additional tax-geared penalties of up to 200% of the assessed liability for deliberate non-compliance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and Local File are mandatory where Gibraltar gross revenue exceeds GIP 10,000,000 and related-party controlled transactions exceed GIP 1,000,000 in the accounting period; Country-by-Country Report notification applies where consolidated group revenue is at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Under the Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations 2020, film copyright licensing is classified as Intellectual Property Business, a relevant activity. The Company must establish management and control in Gibraltar, perform core income-generating activities through local qualified personnel, and maintain adequate physical offices and expenditure in Gibraltar. A passively held Bermuda Exempted Company without economic substance cannot claim Gibraltar tax residency and will fail an ESR assessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Civil restitution of all unpaid tax, interest at 8% p.a., a fine of up to 200% of the evaded liability, an additional criminal fine of up to GIP 500,000, and imprisonment for up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for restitution of evaded tax on veil-piercing grounds; the court may issue travel bans, impound passports, strike off the company, and disqualify directors for up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Annual ESR penalty of GIP 10,000, daily default penalty of GIP 1,000, disallowance of treaty benefits, reassessment of Gibraltar-source profits, and removal from the Companies Register.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false economic-substance declarations incur personal monetary penalties and disgorgement; the court may prohibit directorship for 5 years and issue a travel-ban notice.
AML / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to GIP 1,000,000 or 5% of global turnover, asset freezing, confiscation, and forfeiture under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, and imprisonment for up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate or ignore suspicious transactions face criminal liability as principals in money laundering, personal asset seizure, cross-border travel bans, and extradition surrender orders.
