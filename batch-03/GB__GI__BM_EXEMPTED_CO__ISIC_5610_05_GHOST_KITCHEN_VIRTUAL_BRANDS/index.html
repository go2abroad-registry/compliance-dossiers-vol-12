Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office (P.O. Box 452, 17 Lonsdale Street, Gibraltar); HM Government of Gibraltar e-filing portal: https://incometax.gov.gi

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity (e.g., a Bermuda Exempted Company) that acquires a Gibraltar permanent establishment or has assessable Gibraltar-source income must first apply for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN). The application is made to the Income Tax Office on Form C (Company Registration) and must include: a certified copy of the foreign certificate of incorporation and constitutional documents; a board resolution authorising the registration; a power of attorney in favour of the Gibraltar resident agent (if required); a statement of the principal place of effective management; and supporting evidence that the activity creates a chargeable presence. The ITO reviews the application and issues a TIN within ten business days. The company then becomes obliged to file annual tax returns (Form C) under the Income Tax Act 2010 and to appoint a tax representative if it is not tax-resident but carries on a trade in Gibraltar. If the entity has no chargeable nexus, it is not required to register, but may voluntarily apply for a TIN for administrative purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The Bermuda Exempted Company is statutorily exempt from Bermudian income tax until 2035 under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, and that exemption follows the entity in its home jurisdiction. For Gibraltar tax purposes, the entity may deliver a non-residence declaration (Form NR) to the Income Tax Office to establish that its central management and control is exercised outside Gibraltar, thereby restricting Gibraltar tax liability to income sourced from a taxable permanent establishment. Gibraltar does not operate an equivalent to the U.S. 'check-the-box' classification regime; however, if the entity is owned by U.S. persons, a U.S. federal check-the-box election (Form 8832) may be made to treat the entity as a transparent (disregarded or partnership) entity, and such election may indirectly affect U.S. worldwide taxation but does not alter Gibraltar entity classification. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Gibraltar Taxation (Hybrid and Mismatch) Act 2021 may require a protective election where the entity is treated as taxable in a foreign jurisdiction and such treatment results in a deduction/no-inclusion mismatch.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST in Gibraltar; limited customs and excise duties apply on importation

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A – Gibraltar has no domestic value-added tax, goods and services tax, or sales and use tax, so no economic turnover threshold triggers indirect tax registration in Gibraltar. Where the ghost kitchen platform services are provided directly to final consumers in the United Kingdom through electronic platforms (e.g., UberEats, DoorDash), the entity may be required to register for UK VAT if UK taxable turnover exceeds the mandatory threshold of GBP 90,000 in any 12-month period; for non-established taxable persons, no threshold applies and registration is immediate. The relevant threshold is therefore not in Gibraltar currency (GIP) but in British pounds sterling when UK supplies are made.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

As Gibraltar has no VAT or GST, all exports of tangible goods from Gibraltar to any destination outside Gibraltar are automatically outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation; they attract no Gibraltar exit duty and are therefore effectively zero-rated. To substantiate export treatment for customs and foreign tax purposes, the exporter must demonstrate that (a) the goods physically departed from Gibraltar and were delivered to a customer located outside Gibraltar; (b) legal title and risk of loss passed to that overseas customer; and (c) any invoiced amount was paid in a freely convertible currency or by an approved compensating payment. With respect to offshore services (including ghost kitchen franchise know-how, brand licensing, and central administrative services supplied from Gibraltar), the place of supply is determined under the Income Tax Act and not under a VAT framework; no Gibraltar export tax is levied, and the entity is not required to charge any export VAT. For goods that, via the online marketplace, enter the UK, zero-rating for UK VAT is available only if the goods meet the UK zero-rated export criteria, which include proof of removal from the UK within 30 days of supply and retention of a valid customs export declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document or electronic equivalent)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / other transport evidence showing departure from Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing receipt of payment in foreign currency within 180 days

Gibraltar does not mandate electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API interoperability for supplier invoices. A conventional paper or PDF invoice is sufficient for tax audit purposes. For imports into Gibraltar, a customs declaration must be submitted electronically through the approved Gibraltar Customs EDI system, but without transactional pre-approval. For UK VAT obligations, HMRC’s Making Tax Digital regime requires digital record-keeping and submission of VAT returns via HMRC-approved API software; invoices may be issued electronically but are not subject to live clearance or government stamping.

Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating claims are aggressively scrutinised. Under the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance, deliberate false documentation in support of an export claim is a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment of up to seven years and an unlimited fine. Civil consequences include recovery of any unpaid duty or tax (if a foreign VAT applies), interest at the statutory rate, and additional penalties of up to 200% of the total amount evaded. For UK VAT, where a Gibraltar-based entity improperly claims zero-rating on goods supplied into the UK, HMRC may issue a retrospective assessment extending back to the statutory limit of 20 years in cases of deliberate or concealed error; a penalty of up to 100% of the VAT evaded may be imposed, plus a detailed disclosure may be required under the UK Finance Act 2021. The Australian-style 'clawback' concept is transposed by the recovery provisions in the Gibraltar Taxation (International and Other Provisions) Act 2020, which disapplies treaty or exemption benefits where the principal purpose is to achieve a tax advantage, thereby enabling retrospective reclassification of exports and full recovery of underpaid indirect tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates a fully convertible current and capital account. GIP is freely exchangeable into GBP at par without any fiscal charge, and USD/EUR accounts are permitted without restriction for corporate treasury. Profit remittance is free of all Gibraltar withholding taxes—no dividend, interest, or royalty WHT—and the Bermuda Exempted Company status is classified as non-resident for Bermuda Exchange Control Act purposes, permitting unfettered capital movement between shareholders, the company, and its Gibraltar operating accounts without Bermuda Monetary Authority approval. No capital-account authorization or profit-repatriation permit is required in either jurisdiction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Neither Gibraltar nor the United Kingdom imposes any mandatory surrender percentage, conversion timeline, or holding-period requirement for export or platform proceeds. DoorDash/UberEats USD receivables may be held indefinitely in USD-denominated Gibraltarian accounts, freely converted at any market rate, or routed via UK/EEA clearing without central-bank pre-authorization. Bermuda's Exchange Control regulations likewise exempt a non-resident Exempted Company from any surrender obligations.

Central bank supervision: The Government of Gibraltar, acting under the Currency Notes Act 1934, issues the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) at a statutory 1:1 peg to GBP, with the Bank of England as the ultimate monetary backstop. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) licenses and supervises all credit institutions and payment services. No standalone central-bank exchange-control administration exists within Gibraltar; HM Treasury and the Bank of England apply the UK monetary framework, and the GFSC oversees the practical execution for all GIP-denominated clearing.

If a GFSC-regulated local EMI is preferred, a full Electronic Money licence will be required under the Financial Services Ordinance 2015, which is only practical for established settlement volumes. Recommended architecture: an EMI for global payment aggregation, layered over a Tier-1 Gibraltar bank for statutory clearing and supplier settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 750,000

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance (Companies) Act 2018 and the OECD Substance requirements apply. Any Bermuda Exempted Company operating a ghost kitchen through a Gibraltar PE must show core income-generating activity locally: the kitchen lease, the virtual brand management, delivery coordination, procurement decisions, and staff payroll must all be directed and staffed from Gibraltar. Failure to satisfy substance will trigger reclassification of the PE as a tax-resident shell and disqualify treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Sham Exemption

Statutory sanction: Revenue assessment on all Gibraltar-source profit; negligence/deliberate penalty up to 100% of the tax; interest at 8% per annum; court fine not exceeding £250,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly abused the exemption may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax; travel bans are imposed in serious cases; imprisonment up to two years for fraudulent evasion.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Action by the Gibraltar Commissioner: penalty of £1,500 under the Economic Substance Act; a further £5,000 for failure to file the substance return; and dissolvement or strike-off of the entity if correction notices are ignored.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be subject to disqualification orders and civil fines; the court may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for all unpaid tax and statutory payroll contributions.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Transgressions

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to GIP 100,000 per offence; confiscation orders; disqualification of professional advisers; bank account freezing.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal liability for money laundering if the shell is used to move proceeds from delivery operations; prison terms of up to five years and travel restrictions apply.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profit to an arm's length amount at 10%; interest at 8% per annum; transfer pricing penalty of GIP 2,000 or up to 25% of the shortfall disclosed in a scheme report.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable in tort for assisting in a tax rule misrepresentation; civil restitution orders are issued in the Gibraltar courts; extradition to the UK may be sought for deliberate underdeclaration.
