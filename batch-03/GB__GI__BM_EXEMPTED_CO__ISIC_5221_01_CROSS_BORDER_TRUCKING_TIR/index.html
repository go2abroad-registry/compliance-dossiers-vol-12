Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar - Commissioner of Income Tax; indirect taxation and customs administered by HM Customs Gibraltar. Registration and filing are conducted through the HM Government of Gibraltar Income Tax eServices portal (www.gibraltar.gov.gi) and the Gibraltar Customs electronic declaration system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Bermuda Exempted Company as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies (Companies Act 2014, s.110) by filing certified copies of the Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, constitutional documents, and a registered office address in Gibraltar, obtaining a Gibraltar company number. Step 2: Submit the Income Tax registration form (company particulars, directors, shareholders, place of business, and declaration of central management and control) to the Commissioner of Income Tax, who issues the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) - the statutory TIN. Step 3: For TIR cross-border trucking, obtain a Customs Operator Code from HM Customs Gibraltar in order to lodge electronic transit declarations and endorse TIR carnets. Step 4: File annual corporation tax returns; a non-resident company with no Gibraltar-source income must still submit a nil return. Failure to register attracts fixed and daily default penalties under the Income Tax Act 2023.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; corporate tax identity follows the place of central management and control (CMC). A Bermuda Exempted Company whose CMC and strategic decision-making sit outside Gibraltar is classified as a non-resident company and is subject to Gibraltar income tax only on profits derived from Gibraltar sources (territorial basis); a formal non-residence declaration may be filed to evidence this status. If CMC is exercised in Gibraltar, the company is taxed as a Gibraltar-resident company at entity level on worldwide profits at the 12.5% standard rate, with no elective exclusion. Large multinational groups may elect the OECD GloBE qualified domestic top-up tax under the Income Tax (Multinational and Domestic Top-up Tax) Act 2025; no hybrid-mismatch or transparent-entity (pass-through) election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST - Gibraltar has no value-added tax, general services tax, or sales and use tax; indirect taxation is limited to customs import duties and excise duties under the Customs Ordinance (Tariff) Act.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - there is no VAT or GST registration threshold in Gibraltar. Any entity undertaking TIR transit or import operations must register with HM Customs Gibraltar for a Customs Operator Code regardless of turnover; no turnover de minimis applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - the absence of VAT in Gibraltar means no 0% export VAT rate exists. The corresponding relief is the duty-suspension transit regime under the TIR Convention 1975: goods in bond moved through or from Gibraltar are relieved from import duty and excise duty, provided the haulier holds a valid TIR Carnet, is an approved TIR holder, the load is customs-sealed, and the transit operation is discharged at the office of exit/destination within the TIR time limit (normally 22 days). Cross-border freight services supplied by the Bermuda Exempted Company are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax.

  • TIR Carnet (IRU guarantee cover)
  • Customs Transit Declaration - SAD/Transit Accompanying Document lodged electronically with Gibraltar Customs
  • CMR International Consignment Note
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing freight payment repatriation

No electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance/stamping mandate is in force in Gibraltar. Electronic filing of customs declarations (Single Administrative Document and Transit Accompanying Document) is mandatory via the Gibraltar Customs electronic system; TIR carnets are processed under the eTIR framework where the guaranteeing chain supports digital endorsement. Commercial invoicing between freight principals and hauliers remains contractual without government API clearance or digital stamping.

Misuse of the TIR transit regime or fraudulent documentation exposes the operator to retrospective customs assessments (six-year time limit; unlimited in cases of fraud), forfeiture of goods and haulage vehicles, civil penalties of up to 100% of the duty evaded, and criminal prosecution under the Customs Ordinance (maximum fine of GIP 50,000 and imprisonment up to seven years). The IRU guaranteeing association pays outstanding duties and taxes and then claws back from the carnet holder, who may also be suspended or permanently excluded from the TIR system. Concurrently, the Income Tax Office may impose penalties of up to 100% of under-assessed tax plus interest where freight receipts are concealed, misstated, or not repatriated within the prescribed compliance period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains no exchange-control legislation and the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is legal tender at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP, freely convertible at par without central-bank approval. Both current-account and capital-account movements are unrestricted, including TIR freight receivables, shareholder equity injections, intercompany loans, and profit repatriation. Profit remittances from Gibraltar operating flows to the Bermuda Exempted Company and onward to non-resident shareholders carry no Gibraltar withholding tax and no Bermuda exchange-control restrictions. The only practical hurdles are bank-level documentary verification, sanctions screening, and the demonstration of sufficient economic substance in Gibraltar so that distributions are not recharacterized as offshore-sourced income without commercial purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. There is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export or transport service receipts under Gibraltar, United Kingdom, or Bermuda law. TIR freight proceeds may be lawfully retained in EUR, USD, GBP, or any other freely convertible currency. A sponsoring bank may impose internal open-position limits on non-GIP balances and request written treasury policy on currency retention, but no surrender to any government authority is ever required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (GB) provides the monetary anchor for the Gibraltar pound; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the competent licensing and supervisory authority for banks, electronic-money institutions, and AML/CFT compliance in Gibraltar; currency issuance itself is administered by the Government of Gibraltar (Financial Secretary) under the Currency Notes Act 1934. There is no separate Central Bank of Gibraltar.

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for GFSC-licensed EMIs; Low-Medium for UK or EEA EMIs servicing Gibraltar. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital arrives via SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the Bermuda parent or UBO account to the Gibraltar operational account, with the purpose narration strictly limited to 'SHARE PREMIUM' or 'SHAREHOLDER SUBORDINATED LOAN' and a reference to ISIC 5221 business scope. Settlement is cleared through the Gibraltar bank's London correspondent (typically Barclays Bank PLC) for GBP, or through a Frankfurt correspondent for EUR; the remitting bank must provide source-of-funds confirmation, and the pre-funded board resolution together with the updated share register or loan agreement must be delivered to compliance before booking. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% flat corporate income tax rate under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 (Cap 98); applicable to Gibraltar-source profits of a non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company operating through a fixed establishment in Gibraltar.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions made to any shareholder, resident or non-resident; the Bermuda Exempted Company may therefore pay dividends from its Gibraltar branch profits and from Bermuda reserves with no Gibraltar source deduction or DWT.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return must be delivered within 8 months after the accounting period-end (for a 31 December year-end, the due date is 31 August of the following year); a final balancing payment is due on the same date as the return; the Commissioner may issue a formal notice shortening the filing window to 31 days, in which case the notice deadline governs.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where aggregate controlled transactions with related parties exceed GIP 1,500,000 per accounting period (including fuel-card clearing, intercompany management fees, TIR carnet guarantee charges and intra-group loans); Master File is required where the worldwide MNE group consolidated revenue is equal to or greater than EUR 750,000,000 per the Gibraltar Income Tax (Pillar Two and Transfer Pricing) alignment rules.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar has enforced the Income Tax (Economic Substance) Rules since 30 June 2019; core income-generating activities relevant to the TIR freight business (fleet dispatch, route planning, driver contracting, fuel-card control, TIR carnet custody) must be exercised in Gibraltar, except where the company is tax-resident and substances in another jurisdiction such as the UK; non-resident Bermuda Exempted Companies claiming tax-exempt status abroad must file the ESR Additional Information Form and evidence managed-and-controlled activity; overland long-haul trucking under TIR carnets is not an expressly designated ESR-relevant activity category, but a Gibraltar resident company carrying on associated headquarters, distribution and service centre, or financing activities attracts the ESR Core Income-Generating Activities test and would be deemed to lack substance if it has no office, no employees, no board meetings in Gibraltar and no local risk control over its bank accounts and TIR fleet.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Offshore Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 150% of the tax underdeclared under the Income Tax Act 2010 (Cap 98, ss. 122-123), plus daily interest compounded at 5% above base; criminal fine at the discretion of the Supreme Court and imprisonment for up to 7 years for knowing or reckless false statements; detention and forfeiture of TIR tractor units, trailers and bank accounts holding undeclared freight proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or knowingly permit a false return may be jointly and severally assessed; the Commissioner may issue a liability certificate against the directors as officers; the Court may impose a travel ban, freeze share transfers and disqualify directors for up to 15 years under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 (Cap 100).
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR notification failure penalties of GIP 10,000 fixed plus GIP 1,000 per day; where an ESR relevant activity is found (e.g., distribution and service centre or headquarters), the Commissioner may compute chargeable profits on an arm's-length deemed amount with a 100% penalty and apply to strike off the Gibraltar branch register; persistent non-cooperation triggers cancellation of the Gibraltar banking relationship under the corporate service provider rules.

Director personal liability: Directors certifying the ESR Additional Information Form with false substance statements face personal fines up to GIP 100,000, restriction orders under Companies Act 2014, Court-ordered indemnification of the tax authority's costs, confiscation of assets under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, and a travel ban pending tribunal determination.
Transfer Pricing Distortion and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment of Gibraltar-source and UK-source profits with backdated interest at 5% above base; a 70% negligence penalty for careless inaccuracies and a 100% penalty for deliberate arrangements that lack reasonable excuse; cross-border documentation failures add a fixed GIP 20,000 penalty per controlled transaction; a material distortion triggers a UK-HMRC TIE/Group-lead review and possible joint audit under the UK-Gibraltar tax information exchange measures.

Director personal liability: Directors of both the Bermuda parent and the Gibraltar branch are personally liable where the adjustment is occasioned by their deliberate functional decision-making; the authorities may issue officer liability notices, restrain dividend distribution, order restitution, and apply travel bans and imprisonment (up to 7 years) where the TP scheme is accompanied by false invoicing or deliberate concealment.
AML, CRS and Payment-Rail Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: CRS audit-file omission is penalised up to GIP 10,000 per failed report and GIP 500 per day; offences under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 attract fines up to GIP 500,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years for arranging or facilitating criminal cash flows; the Financial Intelligence Unit may issue freezing orders on all branch assets; a high-risk finding compels bank account closure and referral to the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission.

Director personal liability: Directors and designated 'senior persons' face personal criminal liability for failure to make a suspicious activity report, up to 7 years' imprisonment and confiscation of personal assets; the Court may prohibit officer residence in the jurisdiction during investigation, impose travel bans, and order disqualification of the board for the entire group's banking access.
