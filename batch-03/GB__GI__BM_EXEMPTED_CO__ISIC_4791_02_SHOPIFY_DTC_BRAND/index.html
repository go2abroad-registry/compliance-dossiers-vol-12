Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The Bermuda Exempted Company must maintain a registered office in Bermuda at all times at a physical address in Bermuda, which may be the premises of the licensed registered agent; a PO Box or purely virtual address is not permissible for the Bermuda registered office. For the Gibraltar branch of the DTC e-commerce operations, a virtual office is permissible for trade-license purposes with a registered address in Gibraltar held via a licensed corporate services provider, provided that the company maintains demonstrable operational substance in Gibraltar sufficient to avoid being a 'pure non-resident shell' for Gibraltar ESR and taxation purposes. The Shopify Payments/Stripe/PayPal rails and the target EMI banking relationship generally require a physical or documented operational address; a virtual office is acceptable at pre-incorporation stage but must be upgraded to a serviced office or lease before the EMI's enhanced due diligence completes.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), HM Government of Gibraltar; Companies House Gibraltar (Registrar of Companies); Gibraltar Customs and Border Control for indirect/customs controls; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company obtains a Gibraltar TIN by applying to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office after registration as an overseas company with Companies House Gibraltar under the Companies Act 2014. The registration requires certified certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, incumbency certificate, register of directors and members, and a Gibraltar registered office or resident agent address. Following issue of the overseas company registration certificate, the entity files the prescribed income tax registration return, receives its statutory Gibraltar TIN, and preserves its Bermuda tax exemption by maintaining the Tax Exemption Certificate issued by the Bermuda Ministry of Finance. Payroll and social security registration is required only if employees are engaged in Gibraltar. No VAT identification number is obtainable or required because Gibraltar does not impose VAT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - Bermuda Exempted Company is exempt from Bermuda income tax; if central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, territorial-scope classification may apply such that only Gibraltar-source profits are taxed, with foreign-source profits outside the charge to Gibraltar tax.

Available election forms

1) Bermuda Exempted Company tax-exemption election under the Exempted Companies Act 1981 (Tax Exemption Certificate, renewable for 25 years). 2) Gibraltar Qualifying Company (Category 2) election under the Income Tax Act 2010, electing territorial taxation of Gibraltar-source income only. 3) OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch alignment election (dual-inclusion income and limb-rule statements) to prevent intra-group double non-taxation. 4) US check-the-box election via Form 8832 where the entity is required to have a US federal tax classification for CFC or reporting purposes. 5) Election to be treated as non-resident for Gibraltar tax by ensuring no management and control in Gibraltar and filing the relevant non-residence statement with the Income Tax Office.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar: No VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax; the territory is outside the EU VAT area and levies only limited consumption duties on alcohol, tobacco, fuel, and certain imported goods. UK VAT (20%) does not apply to the Bermuda Exempted Company unless it has a UK fixed establishment or makes UK-taxable supplies.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - Gibraltar has no VAT/GST law and prescribes no economic-turnover registration threshold; local currency is the Gibraltar Pound (GIP). For context, the UK VAT taxable-turnover threshold is GBP 90,000 and the overseas-seller distance-selling threshold is GBP 85,000, but neither will apply to the Gibraltar-entered exempted company absent a UK fixed establishment or UK-based inventory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar exports are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation, so no 0% VAT claim or local refund mechanism exists. Where goods are exported from the UK by a UK-registered seller to a non-UK customer, the supply may be zero-rated under VATA 1994 s.30 provided the exporter holds valid evidence of export and the goods are dispatched to a destination outside the UK within the statutory period. The Bermuda Exempted Company should ensure title and risk pass to the customer outside the UK, use delivery terms such as DAP or DDP at the customer border, and refrain from maintaining UK-warehoused inventory to sustain the export rationale.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document or electronic declaration) showing the exporter's EORI or Gibraltar customs reference
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of goods from the exporting territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and SWIFT/bank statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds from offshore counterparties
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and Shopify order ledger reconciling each export transaction
  • Bermuda Tax Exemption Certificate, certificate of incorporation, and any Gibraltar overseas-company registration certificate

None in Gibraltar - there is no real-time fiscal clearance, tax-platform API integration, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing standard. Records must be retained in a form directed by the Income Tax Office, and commercial invoices generated by Shopify are sufficient. UK Making Tax Digital and UK e-invoicing obligations apply only to UK VAT-registered persons, not to the pure offshore Gibraltar vehicle.

Fraudulent zero-rating or export claims are reassessed retrospectively; the zero rate is disallowed, the underdeclared tax is recovered with statutory interest, and penalties apply from 30% of the potential lost revenue for careless errors up to 100% for deliberate concealment in the UK. Gibraltar Customs and Border Control may impose forfeiture of goods, fines of up to three times the value of the goods, and custodial penalties for false export declarations. Failure to satisfy economic substance or offshore-status conditions can trigger retrospective clawback of Bermuda Exempted Company status or Gibraltar Qualifying Company concessions, together with late-payment interest, penalty assessments, and public disclosure under serious-default rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar imposes no exchange controls: the UK exchange-control regime was repealed in 1979 and no restrictions have been re-imposed. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible for residents and non-residents; GIP converts at par to GBP without restriction, and multi-currency accounts (USD, EUR, GBP) are standard across GFSC-regulated banks. For the Bermuda Exempted Company, there are no formal limitations on profit remittance, dividend distributions, or capital repatriation through Gibraltar banking channels; Gibraltar withholding tax on non-resident companies carrying on no Gibraltar trade is nil, subject to management-and-control substance analysis. Practical hurdles arise solely from AML/KYC documentation requirements, not from regulatory convertibility limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Gibraltar has no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement. The DTC e-commerce exporter may retain sales receipts in USD, EUR, GBP, or GIP in any GFSC-regulated bank or authorised EMI; there is no mandated repatriation timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. For tax and banking transparency, receipt records must nevertheless be reconciled with statutory books and the Bermuda/Gibraltar substance file, as onboarding banks will expect turnover consistency across Shopify, PayPal, and Stripe settlement statements.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no statutory central bank. Note issuance is conducted by HM Government of Gibraltar (Ministry of Finance) under the Currency Notes Act 1934; the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged one-for-one to the UK pound sterling, and sterling is legal tender alongside GIP. Monetary policy is de facto anchored by the Bank of England, and prudential supervision of banks and payment institutions is exercised by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Banking Act 2015.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity funding is remitted by shareholders via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from an onshore personal or holding corporate account to the company's Gibraltar operational account or authorised EMI multi-currency IBAN. Gibraltar imposes no exchange-control notification, pre-authorisation, or central-bank purpose-code filing; the only compliance gate is bank-level AML/CDD. The wire narrative should state 'Share Capital Contribution - Bermuda Exempted Company [name]' and reference the share subscription agreement; the remitter must match the company's registered member list. Amounts above the receiving bank's enhanced due-diligence threshold (typically USD 10,000 to USD 100,000) will require CRS/FATCA declarations and a source-of-funds breakdown. For injections routed from Bermuda, Bermuda Monetary Authority AML rules require the Bermuda remitting bank to hold clean originator KYC, with settlement in USD at the Bermuda Dollar/USD 1:1 parity and no regulatory approval required.Trade and logistics disbursements are executed through four primary rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border USD and GBP wires from the receiving account, using a 'Trade Settlement / Inventory Purchase' narrative and full supplier details for sanctions screening; (2) SEPA Credit Transfer, both standard and instant, for EUR payables to EU and EEA suppliers, accessible through UK-authorised EMIs whose SEPA participation extends to Gibraltar; (3) Commercial Documentary Letters of Credit under UCP 600 issued through a UK or Gibraltar confirming bank for high-value bulk stock replenishment from Asia, although standard telegraphic transfer payables are preferred for DTC e-commerce given low individual order values; (4) card-scheme clearing via Shopify Payments, PayPal, and Stripe, which settle received funds in the transaction currency directly into the corporate operating account or linked EMI wallet. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: High for execution, Medium for onboarding friction. GFSC-authorised e-money institutions are limited, so the pragmatic routing for a Bermuda Exempted Company is through UK-authorised EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Modulr, Payoneer, Revolut Business) that provide GBP, USD, and EUR local receiving accounts, virtual IBANs, and SWIFT/SEPA rails without requiring a Gibraltar branch. Legal constraints: EMIs may not accept deposits or grant credit; client balances must be safeguarded in ring-fenced omnibus accounts at a UK or EEA credit institution; enhanced due diligence applies to offshore exempted companies, often requiring the Bermuda registered agent's introduction, ultimate beneficial ownership disclosure, and complete Shopify payout evidence; certain UK EMIs auto-decline entities domiciled in 'offshore' jurisdictions even where operations are legitimate.
