Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office / Commissioner of Income Tax; Companies House Gibraltar (Registrar of Companies) for foreign-company registration; online tax portal: Gibraltar eServices (https://eservices.gibraltar.gov.gi).

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company seeking legal operation or tax presence in Gibraltar must first register as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies to obtain the Gibraltar company number. Within 30 days of commencing taxable activity or establishing central management and control in Gibraltar, the company must submit the income tax registration application to the Income Tax Office through eServices, attaching the Bermuda certificate of incorporation, certificate of good standing, memorandum and articles, register of shareholders/directors, Gibraltar registered office address, ISIC 6499 / SPAC business description, and expected financial year. The Income Tax Office will issue a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN). No VAT ID is issued because Gibraltar does not levy VAT or GST. A Bermuda Exempted Company that remains fully managed and controlled outside Gibraltar and has no Gibraltar-source income is not required to obtain a TRN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No Gibraltar check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists. Gibraltar applies residence-based taxation: a foreign-incorporated company is treated as Gibraltar-resident only if centrally managed and controlled in Gibraltar; if resident, it is taxed at 12.5% on worldwide profits. If non-resident, it is taxable only on Gibraltar-source income attributable to a permanent establishment. The Bermuda exempted status is a statutory tax exemption under Bermuda law, not a Gibraltar exemption, and cannot be elected in Gibraltar. A SPAC may structure its escrow and investment management functions outside Gibraltar to preserve non-residence and avoid a Gibraltar tax presence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST or general sales tax; Gibraltar operates a customs and excise duty system limited to imports and specified goods such as alcoholic beverages, tobacco and hydrocarbon oil.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; Gibraltar has no VAT/GST registration threshold in Gibraltar pounds. Businesses importing or exporting goods must obtain the relevant customs registration or authorization from HM Customs Gibraltar, but no turnover-based indirect-tax registration obligation arises in respect of SPAC share issuance, trust escrow receipts or merger consideration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Because Gibraltar imposes no VAT or GST, there is no export zero-rating claim or VAT refund regime. A Bermuda Exempted Company SPAC does not issue taxable supply invoices; its capital market activities, including IPO subscription proceeds, escrow investment returns and acquisition consideration, are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax. If the SPAC later acquires an operating group holding physical goods, only customs duty rules may apply to cross-border movement of those goods, and duty neutralisation on re-export would be documented through standard customs procedures, not VAT input recovery.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic declaration in the Gibraltar customs clearance system showing commodity code, export value and exporter reference)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or equivalent transport document evidencing physical export from Gibraltar
  • Foreign inward remittance certificate / verified bank SWIFT MT103 showing matching export proceeds

None. Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, B2B e-invoicing, continuous transaction controls, API-based tax reporting or digital stamping. Import and export transactions are cleared electronically with HM Customs of Gibraltar through the authorised customs declaration system. SPAC accounting records, escrow bank statements, investor placement documents and tax filings are retained under ordinary documentary and financial services compliance rules.

Although no export-VAT refund exists for a SPAC, any false declaration made to HM Customs of Gibraltar or the Income Tax Office will trigger rigorous audit scrutiny. Civil remedies include additional tax assessments, late-payment interest and penalties for negligent or deliberate understatement. Criminal sanctions under Gibraltar customs and excise legislation may include monetary fines, forfeiture of goods and imprisonment. Mischaracterising investment or escrow income as non-Gibraltar-source when central management and control is in Gibraltar will be reclassified retrospectively, with clawback of any duty relief or exemption together with mandatory interest and penalty surcharges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued by the Government of Gibraltar and pegged 1:1 to GBP; no sovereign exchange-control regime exists in the UK or Gibraltar, and the capital account and current account are fully and freely convertible. Profit remittance, dividend distributions, capital repatriation, and liquidation proceeds payable by the Bermudian Exempted Company suffer no withholding tax, conversion tax, or prior-authorisation hurdle; the only frictions are standard AML/KYC verification and CRS/FATCA reporting obligations. For the SPAC treasury, USD IPO proceeds held in trust escrow are freely convertible into GBP/EUR for operating costs, while the Bermudian tax-exempt status is preserved by the absence of profit extraction taxes in Bermuda, Gibraltar, or the UK (provided the SPAC is not UK tax-resident and does not carry on a trade in the UK through a permanent establishment). Systematic restrictions apply solely to sanctioned jurisdictions under the UK Global Human Rights and Sanctions Regulations and the Gibraltar Sanctions Regulations, requiring a compliance screen before each conversion or wire; no prudential ceiling, Tobin tax, or balance-of-payments disincentive applies to any such transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Exchange controls were abolished in the UK and Gibraltar in 1979; there is no statutory timeline for repatriation of export or subscription proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio into domestic currency. The Bermuda Exempted Company may retain 100% of all receipts in USD, GBP, or EUR for an unlimited period, subject only to the BMA's annual reporting thresholds and Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 suspicious-transaction reporting duties. The only quantified surrender-like requirement is the mandatory deposit of the SPAC's public offering proceeds into a segregated trust escrow account at a qualified bank, as a contractual condition of the listing venue's rules rather than an FX-control obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (sovereign monetary authority for GBP and Sterling policy; HM Treasury retains statutory responsibility for UK exchange-control competence). Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the licensing and prudential regulator for all Gibraltar-incorporated banks, EMIs, and investment firms under the Financial Services Act 2019. Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) is the home-state supervisor for the Bermuda Exempted Company and must issue the annual economic substance declaration and corporate consent that anchor the entity's banking approvals.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for trust and escrow functions; Medium only for incidental operational expense accounts. Gibraltar regulates EMIs under the Electronic Money (Gibraltar) Regulations 2012, but no licensed Gibraltar EMI offers the segregated tri-party custodian infrastructure, commercial LC issuance, or SWIFT MT103 clearing privileges required for a SPAC's IPO proceeds and business-combination payments. Cross-border digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA-authorized or EEA-licensed entities) are usable for petty operational payments in GBP/EUR below GBP 100,000 per transaction, provided the EMI accepts the Bermuda Exempted Company's UBO declaration and the Medium-Risk-Enhanced banking profile; however, any EMI holding public-offering funds would breach the listing venue's trust-escrow integrity requirement, so the escrow account must remain with a frontline Gibraltar bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Issuance proceeds and sponsor contributions are routed by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the exchange clearing agent, underwriter, or corporate broker directly to the Bermuda Exempted Company's Gibraltar operational account or, where the listing venue requires, straight to the segregated trust escrow account. Compliant ISO 20022 purpose codes include SECO (securities settlement) and TRADE (trade proceeds); the remittance reference must cite the prospectus date and placement tranche to satisfy the bank's source-of-funds reconciliation. Verification protocol: (i) pre-funding SWIFT test-key confirmation from the corporate broker; (ii) line-item reconciliation of each credit against the subscription register maintained by the transfer agent; (iii) funds-flow statement filed with the Gibraltar MLRO before the escrow deposit is effected; (iv) deposit into a segregated trust account held with a qualifying Gibraltar bank, with the trustee appointed as joint signatory alongside the SPAC CFO. Clearing mechanics: GBP credits settle via CHAPS; EUR via TARGET2; USD via Fedwire or CHIPS through the bank's correspondent chain; each inbound credit is reported under CRS and FATCA on the sponsor-level reporting matrix.Post-business-combination acquisition consideration, redemption payouts, and liquidation distributions are executed using a tiered clearing-rail architecture: (i) SWIFT MT103 for wire payments to selling shareholders and target-company creditors above GBP 100,000; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit issued via SWIFT MT700 for milestone-based escrowed payments that are contingent on operational covenants of the target; (iii) CHAPS for GBP payments between GBP 10,000 and GBP 500,000; (iv) SEPA Instant for EUR operational disbursements below GBP 100,000; and (v) internal book transfers with full audit trail for redemptions processed through the transfer agent. Each disbursement requires two-party release instruction (trustee plus SPAC CFO or board-designated signing officer), is evidenced by a board-approved disbursement matrix, and is recorded in the CRS/FATCA reporting file to preserve the Bermudian tax-exempt status and the Gibraltar branch's nil-additional-compliance exposure.Outright FX forward contracts (vanilla) on USD/GBP and EUR/GBP executed with a Gibraltar-licensed bank or GFSC-regulated investment firm, with tenor not exceeding 12 months and subject to a board-approved hedging policy.FX swaps (swap of USD principal against GBP or EUR at the same settlement date) permitted strictly for cash-flow matching of escrow release obligations and operating expense timing.Cross-currency swaps applied to hedge USD IPO proceeds versus GBP operating overheads across the full SPAC trust period, subject to ISDA 2002 master documentation and no speculative residual exposure.Vanilla FX options (calls, puts, and zero-cost collars) on USD/GBP, permitted but discouraged pending execution of the business combination because the acquisition consideration is typically USD-denominated.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are not applicable and are not permissible, because GBP and USD are freely deliverable currencies; GFSC policy does not endorse NDFs for hedging deliverable currency pairs.Contractual FX conversion clauses embedded within Commercial Letters of Credit, denominated in USD but payable in GBP at a pre-agreed conversion rate, are permitted as an ancillary treasury instrument.
