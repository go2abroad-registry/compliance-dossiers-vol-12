Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) for United Kingdom-wide VAT and Customs matters; for Gibraltar-specific direct and indirect tax administration, the Commissioner of Income Tax (Gibraltar Income Tax Office, part of HM Government of Gibraltar's Finance Centre). The relevant online portals are the UK Government Gateway for UK VAT registration and the Gibraltar Income Tax Office filing portal for local tax registration.

E-government filing portal

The Bermuda Exempted Company, as a non-resident entity, must first apply for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) via the Gibraltar Income Tax Office using an application for non-resident companies, supported by certified copies of the Bermuda certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, and a statement of no local trading activity if no Gibraltar-source income arises. For UK VAT purposes, a non-established taxable person making taxable supplies in the UK or receiving services subject to the reverse charge must register for UK VAT using form VAT1 (or online registration through the Government Gateway), providing evidence of business activity and, if required, appointing a UK tax representative. For customs and excise purposes, registration is via HMRC's Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) scheme, requiring a GB EORI number for any entity moving goods into or out of the UK customs territory.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope: Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda Exempted Company is tax-exempt in Bermuda under the Exempted Companies Act and may obtain a Certificate of Exemption from Bermuda's Ministry of Finance confirming no Bermuda taxes. In Gibraltar, a non-resident company with no Gibraltar-source income is generally outside the charge to Gibraltar income tax; however, if the entity becomes tax resident or derives Gibraltar-source income, it may be subject to Gibraltar's Income Tax Act at the standard corporate rate (currently 12.5% for qualifying trading income, with certain passive income taxed at a higher rate). No general 'check-the-box' entity classification election exists in Gibraltar; classification follows the Income Tax Act definition of 'company', with no partnership pass-through election for a Bermuda incorporated company. Hybrid mismatch rules under Gibraltar's Tax (Tax Treaty and Hybrids) Regulations and UK anti-hybrid rules should be reviewed where an entity is dual-resident or classified differently in the UK and Gibraltar.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

GST: Gibraltar Value Added Tax (VAT) is not applicable; the territory operates a separate Goods and Services Tax (GST) regime administered locally. For UK VAT, the entity is outside the UK VAT net unless it makes taxable supplies in the UK or imports goods into the UK customs territory.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar GST registration threshold: n/a (Gibraltar GST is levied only on a narrow, enumerated set of supplies, such as telecommunications and electricity, with a standard rate approximating the UK VAT rate; general goods and services are outside the scope). UK VAT registration threshold: GBP 90,000 of taxable turnover in a rolling 12-month period for UK-established businesses; non-established businesses making any taxable supplies in the UK are required to register from the first supply, without the benefit of an annual threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For supplies made in the UK: zero-rating applies to the export of goods from the UK to a destination outside the UK (including Gibraltar, although Gibraltar is outside the UK VAT area), provided the exporter retains evidence of export within the required time limits; supplies of services directly related to goods exported (e.g., freight, insurance) may also be zero-rated if the conditions under the VAT Act 1994, Schedule 8, Group 7 are met. For Gibraltar: the export of goods from Gibraltar is not subject to Gibraltar GST; no zero-rated supply mechanism applies because general supplies are outside the GST scope. For customs duty and excise, goods moved from Gibraltar to the UK are importations into the UK customs territory and do not qualify as exports.

  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical movement of the raw cotton bales to the non-UK destination

Gibraltar: no real-time e-invoicing clearance system exists; invoices must comply with the Gibraltar Value Added Tax Act record-keeping rules and, for cross-border movements, the EU/UK customs requirements do not apply to Gibraltar as it is outside the EU Customs Union and UK customs territory. UK: mandatory electronic invoicing is not yet universal, but HMRC is implementing the Making Tax Digital (MTD) regime requiring digital record-keeping and quarterly VAT returns via MTD-compatible software; customs declarations for imports and exports are filed electronically through the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) using API integration or approved software, without physical fiscal stamps. Data must be transmitted via the CDS API for all goods entering or leaving the UK, including any cotton bales transiting via the UK.

For UK VAT: incorrect zero-rating or fraudulent export claims attract penalties under the Finance Act 2007 and VAT Act 1994, including a penalty of up to 100% of the VAT evaded, interest on underdeclared tax, and the power of HMRC to issue a VAT assessment and to require a joint and several liability security deposit. For customs duty: errors may attract civil penalties under the Taxation (Cross-border Trade) Act 2018, including penalties of up to GBP 1,000 per declaration, with criminal prosecution for knowingly or recklessly providing false declarations, carrying an unlimited fine and up to 7 years' imprisonment. For Gibraltar: false declarations to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office or evasion of import duties may attract penalties under the Customs Duties Ordinance and the Criminal Offences (Customs) Act, including fines and forfeiture of goods; where the entity is a Bermuda exempted company, the Gibraltar authorities may apply the International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act, and failure to satisfy economic substance tests may result in financial penalties and potential strike-off or reclassification of tax status. Retrospective clawback of VAT zero-rating may occur within a 4-year assessment period, and there is no limitation period where fraudulent conduct is proven.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP is fully convertible at par to GBP, and no exchange-control legislation restricts either current-account trade settlements or capital-account transfers. Profit remittance from the Gibraltar banking entry of the Bermuda Exempted Company to Bermuda shareholders is unrestricted, although the paying bank may require a Gibraltar tax-residence certificate, the CRS beneficial-ownership declaration, and a simple board-dividend resolution as evidence of legitimate underlying profit.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil statutory surrender requirement. Gibraltar imposes no mandatory conversion ratio, no forced repatriation timeline, and no prescribed holding period for export proceeds. The cotton brokerage may retain export receivables in USD or convert freely, subject only to the licensed bank's internal suspicious-transaction-report thresholds and the counterparty terms of the applicable letter of credit or documentary collection.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no separate central bank. The integrated financial regulator is the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission; the Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) under the Currency Notes Act 1934; monetary conditions are anchored to the Bank of England via the legal parity of GIP 1:1 with GBP sterling.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium, with strict limits. FCA-regulated British EMIs and Gibraltar-authorised payment institutions may open a remote multi-currency operational wallet for the Bermuda Exempted Company and provide efficient GBP/EUR/USD pre-funding and expense payments; however, an EMI cannot issue a commercial letter of credit, handle documentary collections, purchase export bills, provide correspondent trade-clearing, or give the bank-guarantee features required in the cotton-bale sales chain. EMIs are thus a liquidity overlay only; the primary trade-finance rail must run through a GFSC-licensed commercial bank.
