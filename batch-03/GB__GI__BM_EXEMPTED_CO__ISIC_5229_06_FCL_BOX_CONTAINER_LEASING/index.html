Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar — Commissioner of Income Tax, Gibraltar Income Tax Office, 8 Secretary's Lane, Gibraltar (postal address: PO Box 64, Gibraltar); corporate registration and e-filing conducted through the Income Tax Office's Secure Online Portal (www.incometax.gov.gi); customs and excise matters are administered by the Gibraltar Customs and Border Agency (G-CBA), an operational arm of Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar; for the Bermuda parent entity, the competent authority is the Bermuda Registrar of Companies and the Office of the Tax Commissioner (Bermuda), which issues the annual Exempted Status Certificate under the Exempted Companies Act 1981

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company is not automatically registered in Gibraltar. If and only when the company acquires Gibraltar-source income or establishes a taxable presence (place of relevant activities under the Income Tax (Economic Substance) (Amendment) Rules 2019), it must: (1) file a Corporate Registration and Tax Return form (Form G-TOB/CORP) with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TRN); (2) register as an employer (payroll) only if it has staff in Gibraltar; (3) register with the G-CBA for excise and import procedures only if it physically imports controlled goods into Gibraltar; (4) file an annual Economic Substance Declaration (Form ES-1) if the relevant activity (headquarters, shipping management, or leasing) is performed in Gibraltar. Concurrently, the Bermuda Exempted Company must file its annual government fee return with the Bermuda Registrar of Companies and pay the fee under the Exempted Companies (Fees) Order to retain its statutory tax-exempt status in Bermuda; no Bermuda TIN is issued for exempted companies, only the annual Certificate of Exemption, which is subject to a permanent-term election and cancellation by the Registrar if fees remain unpaid for more than 90 days. Where no Gibraltar activities exist, the sole tax identity is derived from the Bermuda Exempted Certificate plus a supporting board resolution confirming offshore management and control.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda law provides no check-the-box election; the Exempted Company is automatically classified as non-resident and exempt from Bermuda income, capital, and withholding taxes, subject only to the annual government fee, by virtue of its incorporation status under the Exempted Companies Act 1981 (the 'exempted status' is itself the statutory election, selected at incorporation and evidenced by the Certificate of Exemption). In Gibraltar, no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election is available; however, a company may elect to apply the territorial-source rule by structuring central management and control (board meetings, lease negotiation, bank mandate, and container-fleet control) so that the profits arise outside Gibraltar and are not received or remitted into Gibraltar, thereby falling outside the charge to Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended by the Finance Act 2019). Gibraltar has adopted EU ATAD anti-hybrid rules (Income Tax (Anti-Hybrid) Rules 2019), so the Bermuda exempt company must not rely on double-non-taxation arising from its Bermuda exemption; rather, it should evidence that the lease income is sourced from international freight movement and not from Gibraltar territory.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No_General_VAT_No_GST — Gibraltar does not operate a VAT or GST system, is not part of the UK VAT or EU VAT territory, and levies only limited indirect taxes: Import Duty under the Customs and Duties Ordinance, Excise Duty on alcohol, tobacco, and hydrocarbon oils, and Purchase Tax under the Purchase Tax Ordinance 1956 (applied to prescribed luxury and motor goods). Intermodal ISO shipping container leasing and related cross-border freight services are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax and do not attract VAT, GST, sales tax, or services tax; customs duties may be triggered only on physical importation of chargeable goods into Gibraltar territory, which a re-exported container fleet avoids under temporary importation rules.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST, there is no general economic-turnover registration threshold. However, any person or entity importing or dealing in chargeable excise goods (hydrocarbon oils, alcohol, tobacco, or purchase-tax goods) into Gibraltar must apply for a Dealer's Licence from the G-CBA irrespective of turnover. For customs procedural purposes, the relevant de minimis is nil: every external movement of goods (including ISO containers) must be declared electronically through the G-CBA Customs Management System; containers entering under temporary importation are recorded by claim of 'Re-import in same state' without payment of duty, but with an administrative filing obligation and no value threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar does not operate a VAT zero-rating or VAT-refund regime, so there is no taxable-output claim to file. Instead, the export of container-leasing services and the re-export of the physical container fleet from Gibraltar achieve a de facto 0% tax result by two independent means: (a) under the territorial-source rule of the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, profits of a foreign-incorporated, Bermuda-tax-exempt company that are neither derived from Gibraltar activities nor remitted into Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar tax (0% effective rate); and (b) for customs purposes, ISO containers entering Gibraltar solely for loading, unloading, or transit quality under the Temporary Importation regime of the Customs and Duties Ordinance 1958 (s.64, Schedule III), provided the containers are owned or leased by a person established outside Gibraltar, are not sold, are re-exported within the 24-month period (extendable to 36 months by G-CBA discretion), and a bond or ATA Carnet is lodged where required. No VAT refund mechanism applies because no input VAT exists.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Export Entry or Re-export Declaration filed through the G-CBA Customs Management System) containing the container numbers, HS code (8609 00 90 for ISO containers), gross mass, and the T1/EXS transit reference where applicable
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill (negotiable or non-negotiable) evidencing transport of the containers aboard an international ocean-going vessel from Gibraltar port into the international freight network
  • Inward/Outward Remittance Certificate or bank MT103 SWIFT wire confirmation evidencing settlement of the container-lease invoice from the lessee, matched to the lease number and container serials
  • Equipment Interchange Receipt (EIR) — terminal-issued document evidencing receipt of each container by the ocean carrier or lessee on the export leg and redelivery on the import leg
  • Container Lease Agreement and fleet schedule identifying the Bermuda Exempted Company as legal owner/lessor with Gibraltar-Bermuda ownership chain disclosures
  • ATA Carnet or Temporary Importation Bond where containers cross into the EU customs territory or third-country frontiers under transport warranty

Gibraltar imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no B2B e-invoicing API, and no digital stamping mandate. Commercial invoices for container-leasing services must be issued in a standard paper or PDF format, include the lessor, lessee, container references, ISIN/IC codes, contractual basis, and be retained for a minimum of six years under the Income Tax Act 2010 (record-keeping obligation). For customs movements of containers, the G-CBA operates an electronic Customs Management System (CMS), which requires an electronic Export/Re-export Entry, ENS (Entry Summary Declaration) for inbound cargo from the EU, and an Exit Summary Declaration (EXS) for outbound movements; in-transit movements are cleared through the New Computerised Transit System — for external transit, the T1 regime applies where goods circulate between the EU customs territory and Gibraltar under a guaranteed transit declaration.

The Gibraltar Income Tax Office may audit the territorial-source assertion for up to six years (extended to twenty-one years where conduct is fraudulent or deliberate). If container-leasing income is recharacterised as Gibraltar-source or as received in Gibraltar, the company becomes liable to Income Tax at 10% plus interest, and penalties range from 50% of the tax underpaid for unprompted voluntary disclosure to 200% for deliberate concealment, with criminal prosecution available for false export declarations. Under the Customs and Duties Ordinance 1958, fraudulent use of the temporary-importation or re-export regime triggers forfeiture of the containers, seizure of the vessel's equipment, a customs penalty up to three times the duty evaded, and potential revocation of the dealer's licence; the G-CBA also runs post-clearance audits comparing EIRs, Bills of Lading, and bank remittances to detect phantom re-exports. On the Bermudian side, the Registrar of Companies may revoke the Exempted Certificate for non-payment of the annual fee, rendering the entity liable to Bermuda private-sector corporate tax (once the 15% economic substance top-up accounting adjustments are assessed for entities in scope of the OECD Pillar Two rules introduced April 2025), and in the event of revocation the shareholders' limited-liability protection fails, exposing the container fleet to general creditor attachment and personal recourse.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound and UK sterling are fully convertible for current and capital account purposes. Gibraltar applies no exchange controls or restrictions on inward/outward fund transfers, profit repatriation, or dividend remittances. Non-resident accounts can hold EUR, USD, or GBP freely. Profit and capital remittances from a licensed bank in Gibraltar require no prior regulatory approval, subject only to FATCA/CRS reporting and AML record retention.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar maintains no statutory export proceeds surrender rule. All commercial export receipts may be retained, defrayed, or remitted in any designated currency without conversion obligations. Trade proceeds must be documented under normal corporate recordkeeping but are not subject to mandated timelines or conversion ratios.

Central bank supervision: Government of Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as banking and regulatory supervisor; no separate Gibraltar central bank exists. The Bank of England retains sterling monetary authority, and Gibraltar issues its own pound (GIP) pegged at par with GBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium for liquidity management, Low for primary settlement. Lawful electronic money institutions (EMIs and payment institutions) authorised under Gibraltar's Financial Services Act may offer multi-currency IBANs, SWIFT, and SEPA-like rails, but they are not licensed credit institutions and cannot provide unsecured credit or take deposits. For container leasing cash flows, an EMI may be used as a sub-account for trade disbursements, but the primary treasury anchor should reside with a tier-1 bank to satisfy counterparty due diligence and trade finance requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 aggregate related-party transactions in an accounting period triggers mandatory Local File preparation; group consolidated revenue above GIP 750,000,000 triggers Master File and Country-by-Country Reporting obligations. All controlled transactions must in any event be supported by contemporaneous arm's length documentation.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance (Companies) Regulations 2019 apply. Container/finance leasing constitutes a relevant activity. A Bermuda Exempted Company managing a container-leasing fleet in or from Gibraltar must maintain and report core income-generating activities in Gibraltar: strategic leasing decisions, fleet risk management, funding and treasury function, contract negotiation, and board/management meetings. An annual ESR return is mandatory. If substance is not present, the company is treated as failing the economic substance test, subject to penalties of up to GIP 10,000 per month, potential recharacterisation of profits, and assessment of 10% Gibraltar corporate income tax on those profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax understated plus default interest at 8%; criminal conviction for evasion under Gibraltar income tax and criminal law exposes the company to an unlimited fine and imprisonment of responsible officers for up to 7 years. Underpaid tax is recoverable by distress and court order with restitution of the full tax and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally and jointly and severally liable for fraudulent trading under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014. The corporate veil is pierced where the company is used for tax evasion; civil restitution, director disqualification for up to 15 years, travel bans, asset freezing and seizure, and custodial sentences of up to 7 years may be imposed.
Shell Company / Failure to Meet Economic Substance Requirements

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar ESR framework, administrative penalties of GIP 10,000 per month may be imposed for continuing non-compliance. A false ESR declaration attracts a penalty of up to GIP 100,000. The tax authority may also recharacterise the profit, assess 10% corporate income tax on the avoided profits, and strike the company off the Gibraltar register.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false ESR declarations or permit the shell to trade without substance face personal penalty liability, statutory disqualification, and prohibition from acting as directors. Civil recovery, travel restrictions, and criminal sanctions extending to imprisonment are available where the sham structure is used to defeat Gibraltar tax or international tax transparency obligations.
Anti-Money Laundering / Terrorist Financing Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and AML/CFT regulations, offenders face an unlimited fine and imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering. Regulated entities and their officers may be fined up to GIP 1 million for systems and controls failures, together with confiscation and civil recovery orders over the proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, and compliance officers are personally liable for wilful or reckless AML failures. The corporate veil is pierced for civil restitution proceedings; travel bans, asset freezing, director disqualification, and custodial sentences of up to 14 years may be imposed, with joint and several liability for the value of laundered or concealed property.
