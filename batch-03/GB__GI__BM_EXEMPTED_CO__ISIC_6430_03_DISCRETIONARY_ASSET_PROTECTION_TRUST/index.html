Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
For Gibraltar: HM Government of Gibraltar Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax) under the Income Tax Act 2010; the Gibraltar Companies House for corporate registration; and the Gibraltar Customs & Excise Department for import/export duties. For the UK component: HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) for UK VAT and customs. For the Bermuda component: the Bermuda Registrar of Companies and the Office of the Accountant General.

        
E-government filing portal
Step 1: Incorporate the Bermuda Exempted Company under the Companies Act 1981 (Bermuda), obtaining the exemption certificate from the Registrar of Companies (no minimum capital). Step 2: Register as an oversea company at the Gibraltar Companies House under the Companies Act 2014, lodging a certified copy of the Bermuda certificate of incorporation, the exempted status declaration, Memorandum and Articles, a Gibraltar registered office (c/o a licensed fiduciary or law firm), and local director details. Step 3: Within 14 days after commencement, apply to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office for the tax reference number (GIB TIN) using Form CO-2010/1, disclosing the place of central management and control; attach the Bermuda Tax Exemption Certificate to support non-resident treatment if management and control is in Bermuda or a third country. Step 4: If administering trusts from Gibraltar premises, obtain a Class 3 fiduciary licence from the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Fiduciary (Trust Administration) Act 1991; the GFSC notifies the Commissioner of Income Tax automatically. Step 5: Register for Gross Income Tax; register as an employer for Payroll Tax and Gibraltar Social Security when hiring staff. Step 6: Receive the Income Tax Office Tax Status Letter confirming resident or non-resident classification; a non-resident company is chargeable only on income arising in Gibraltar.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Tax_Exempt_Zone: The Bermuda Exempted Company is exempt from all Bermuda income, withholding and capital gains taxes on overseas operations (Bermuda Exempted Companies Act 1981, sections 112-116), with continuation of exemption to 2035. In Gibraltar it defaults to territorial scope; if centrally managed and controlled outside Gibraltar, it is non-resident and taxed only on Gibraltar-source profit; if managed and controlled in Gibraltar, it is resident and chargeable on worldwide income remitted to Gibraltar. The irrevocable spendthrift trust assets are separate and remain outside the fiscal net of both Bermuda and Gibraltar.

        
Available election forms
Gibraltar has no US-style check-the-box election. Available elections: (1) Qualifying Company election (0% tax on foreign-source interest, dividends, royalties and capital gains; 2% on local rental income) subject to a capped annual charge and economic substance; (2) Non-Resident Company election under the Income Tax Act 2010 restricting tax to Gibraltar-source income; (3) Remittance basis election for foreign-source fee income; (4) Dual-residence tie-breaker application under the UK-Gibraltar Double Taxation Convention 2019; (5) Anti-hybrid notices under the Gibraltar Taxes (Anti-Hybrid) Rules 2019 to neutralise any mismatch arising from the Bermuda entity being treated as transparent in the UK; and (6) an irrevocable election under the Bermuda Exempted Companies (Amendment) Act 2011 fixing the tax-exempt status for 50 years.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
Gibraltar does not levy VAT, GST or sales tax; it applies customs duty (Customs Ordinance 2015) on imported goods at rates aligned to the UK Global Tariff. Where the entity makes taxable supplies in the United Kingdom, UK VAT (Value Added Tax Act 1994) applies at 20%.

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Gibraltar: nil; every importer of goods must file a customs entry (C88/SAD) irrespective of value. UK VAT: compulsory registration where taxable turnover exceeds GBP 90,000 in a rolling 12-month period; distance-selling threshold GBP 8,818 for UK-established businesses selling from abroad. The Gibraltar-resident branch of a Bermuda Exempted Company generally has no UK VAT registration requirement when it makes no taxable supplies in the UK.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
For Gibraltar: exported goods and services supplied to non-residents attract no Gibraltar customs duty or income tax; no zero-rating certificate is necessary. For UK VAT, trust administration services (ISIC 6430) supplied to customers outside the UK fall outside the scope of UK VAT under the Place of Supply Rules. Claims to zero-rating under VATA 1994 Schedule 8 require: the supply is of goods physically exported from the UK within 3 months, or of services directly connected with exported goods, and documentary evidence of export (export entry, bill of lading, and proof of receipt of consideration) is retained in the principal place of business. For the offshore discretionary asset-protection trust, the governing-law deed, non-UK addresses of settlors/beneficiaries, and the non-resident trust bank account in Gibraltar or UK are the core evidence.

Gibraltar has no electronic fiscal clearance, real-time reporting, or digital stamping mandate. Records must be kept electronically for six years under the Income Tax Act 2010 and the fiduciary licence rules of the GFSC. If UK VAT registered, HMRC Making Tax Digital requires quarterly electronic VAT returns; exports require electronic declarations through HMRC Customs Declaration Service (CDS), a Unique Consignment Reference and an Electronic Export Summary. The scheme is in the High_Risk_Special_License AML tier, so the local regulator and HMRC will treat any invalid zero-rating/outside-scope claim as deliberate concealment. Consequences: (1) HMRC assessments at 20% UK VAT, default surcharge (up to 15%) and Schedule 24 penalties (30%-100%, rising to 200% for deliberate and concealed); (2) Gibraltar Customs Ordinance 2015 forfeiture of goods and a fine up to treble the duty for false export entry; (3) if central management and control is found to be in Gibraltar, the non-resident certificate is voided and the company is retroactively assessed to Gibraltar Gross Income Tax at 12.5%-15% on worldwide income for six years (no time limit where fraud is proven); (4) Bermuda may revoke the exempted status on misrepresentation and claw back all avoided taxes together with interest at 10%; (5) transaction avoidance under section 423 Insolvency Act 1986 (as extended to Gibraltar) allows a court to set aside asset-protection transfers made within five years, causing recovery of trust property plus costs; (6) GFSC disciplinary financial penalties up to GBP 1 million or 10% of worldwide turnover, director disqualification, and revocation of the fiduciary licence.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Gibraltar operates under a full-convertibility currency board with no residual exchange-control restrictions on either current or capital account transactions. Trust contributions, capital injections, and profit or distribution remittances are fully convertible between GIP, GBP, EUR, and USD; however, cross-border movements must be reported under the GFSC AML/CFT Guidance Notes and the Fiduciary Services Act 2017, requiring authorized fiduciary licence status and CRS/FATCA-compliant beneficiary reporting before remittance is cleared.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Not applicable. Gibraltar imposes no export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline. For this exempted-company fiduciary treasury, the only regulatory constraints on retained foreign-currency proceeds are the GFSC counterparty-capital and liquidity-coverage rules, plus enhanced due diligence documentation for any inbound remittance exceeding the €10,000/EUR reporting threshold.

      

      
Central bank supervision: The Government of Gibraltar (Currency Notes Act 1934) is the sovereign note-issuing authority for the Gibraltar pound (GIP), which is pegged at par to GBP; banking and fiduciary supervision is delegated to the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC); the Bank of England acts as the anchor monetary authority for the GBP/GIP currency-board peg.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Opaque beneficial ownership arising from a discretionary offshore spendthrift trust
        
Root cause: Opaque beneficial ownership arising from a discretionary offshore spendthrift trust

        
Defense evidence: Certified copy of the irrevocable trust deed (including spendthrift and asset-protection clauses), the registered beneficial owner (RBO) filing lodged under the Gibraltar Beneficial Ownership (Access) Regulations 2017, the protector's formal consent, a current letter of wishes, and a structural diagram tracing the Bermuda Exempted Company as trustee to the trust estate.

      

      
      

        Unclear source of wealth and source of funds for the settlor's initial contribution
        
Root cause: Unclear source of wealth and source of funds for the settlor's initial contribution

        
Defense evidence: A JMLSG-compliant source-of-wealth report prepared by a Gibraltar-licensed accountant; audited financial statements or tax assessments for the prior three years; 12 months of original bank statements evidencing the accumulation of the funded assets; asset-disposal sale contracts; and full banking references from the remitting institution.

      

      
      

        Tax non-transparency and CRS/FATCA reporting misclassification
        
Root cause: Tax non-transparency and CRS/FATCA reporting misclassification

        
Defense evidence: Gibraltar income-tax registration and, where applicable, a certificate of residence; classification under the UK-Gibraltar FATCA IGA and the Gibraltar CRS (International Tax Compliance) Regulations; a complete controlling-person schedule listing the settlor, protector, and any enforcer as D.5 reporting persons; and proof of the Bermuda exempted-company's tax-exempt status with the Bermuda Registrar.

      

      
      

        Sanction-list ambiguity or unintended PEP involvement among discretionary beneficiaries
        
Root cause: Sanction-list ambiguity or unintended PEP involvement among discretionary beneficiaries

        
Defense evidence: A reverified final beneficiary class schedule or named-beneficiary listing, OFSI/UN/EU/OFCAC sanctions screening reports run within 30 days of onboarding, an independent PEP classification matrix, an attestation that no beneficiary is a designated person or a regime-affiliated entity, and a signed indemnity from the protector covering false-declaration exposure.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility for primary treasury use. Gibraltar has no standalone electronic-money-institution licensing regime separate from the GFSC Banking Act 2019, and major cross-border digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut, Airwallex) generally de-risk or decline entity types classified as High_Risk_Special_License with an Asset-Protection-Trust fiduciary mandate. At most, a GFSC-registered DLT/EMI-adjacent payment institution may be used as an auxiliary collections and disbursement rail, never as the primary trust-account custodian.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory when the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds GIP 1,500,000 in a fiscal year; Master File mandatory when the consolidated group revenue exceeds GIP 50,000,000 and the Gibraltar entity undertakes controlled transactions in excess of GIP 5,000,000.

      
Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Companies and Provision of Information) Act applies because the exempted company holds a fiduciary licence and undertakes trust administration. It must demonstrate: (i) physical office premises in Gibraltar; (ii) qualified full-time resident employees with authority to make decisions; (iii) board meetings held in Gibraltar with full minutes; and (iv) core income-generating activities performed in Gibraltar. A discretionary asset protection trust structure cannot be a pure shell; it must maintain real substance to uphold Gibraltar tax residence and treaty benefits.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion
        
Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under the Income Tax Act and Criminal Code: a fine of up to GIP 1,000,000, imprisonment for up to 7 years, and an additional civil penalty of up to 200% of the tax per evaded. The court will also order repayment of all unpaid tax plus interest.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns are personally and severally liable for the unpaid tax, face immediate travel bans, and may be disqualified from all trust and fiduciary appointments. Confiscation orders may be extended to their personal estates.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: Economic Substance penalties of up to GIP 150,000 on first strike, compulsory strike-off from the Gibraltar Companies Registry, and referral to the Financial Intelligence Unit for a money-laundering investigation. A tax residence certificate may be retroactively voided.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are personally fined up to GIP 50,000, prohibited from holding Gibraltar board seats for 10 years, and may be compelled to repatriate all trust assets to the Gibraltar court for costs.

      

      
      

        AML and Fiduciary Licensing Infraction
        
Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 5 years, fines up to GIP 1,000,000, forfeiture of all fees earned from the transaction, and revocation of the fiduciary licence. Regulator may also impose immediate suspension and public censure.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for civil restitution to the trust, may be added to the Gibraltar sanctions list, and face global asset freezing orders. In egregious cases, extradition requests can be honoured under UK-Gibraltar mutual legal assistance.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing / Thin Capitalisation
        
Statutory sanction: Mandatory tax adjustment under the Income Tax (Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalisation) Regulations; back-dated interest at 8% per annum; penalty up to 100% of the understated tax; and a public ruling naming the company if the deficiency exceeds GIP 100,000.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who approved a non-arm's length transaction are jointly and severally liable for the repayment and face personal fines of up to GIP 250,000. Persistent violations trigger disqualification from holding any Gibraltar fiduciary licence.
