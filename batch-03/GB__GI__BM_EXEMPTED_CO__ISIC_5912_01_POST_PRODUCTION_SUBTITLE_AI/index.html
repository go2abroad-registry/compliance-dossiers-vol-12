Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, HM Government of Gibraltar (Gibraltar Income Tax Office), official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity carrying on activities in Gibraltar must apply to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office for a Tax Reference Number (TRN). The application includes the incorporation certificate, constitutional documents, certified copies of director and beneficial owner passports, evidence of registered office or local branch, and a statement of management and control. Non-resident entities with no Gibraltarian source income may not be required to register, but must notify the Commissioner to obtain a determination.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box election. A Bermuda Exempted Company may, however, make an election under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 concerning territorial sourcing of profits, and may apply for a Gibraltar Certificate of Tax Residence to secure treaty protection. Hybrid mismatch elections are available only to the extent aligned with Gibraltar's adoption of OECD BEPS Action 2 recommendations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST; Gibraltar applies customs import duties and excise duties only

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – no VAT/GST registration regime in Gibraltar; customs registration is required only for importers/exporters of goods

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because Gibraltar levies no VAT/GST. There is no export zero-rating mechanism. For goods, customs import duty relief may apply on re-export under the outward processing or temporary admission procedures, provided the goods are exported outside Gibraltar. For services, no indirect tax is chargeable on export, but no refund mechanism exists.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (SAD / EX1 form)
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer evidence
  • Export packing verification report (where required)

Gibraltar does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance mandate, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping. Customs declarations are submitted electronically through the Gibraltar Customs electronic clearance system using the Single Administrative Document (SAD) or EX1 export declaration. Standard commercial invoicing is sufficient for indirect tax purposes.

Fraudulent export declarations or claims for duty relief trigger civil penalties up to 100% of the duty evaded, seizure and forfeiture of goods, and criminal prosecution carrying fines and imprisonment. Retrospective tax assessments for undeclared Gibraltarian source income may extend back six years, or twelve years in cases of deliberate concealment, with daily late-payment interest and surcharges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) and the British pound (GBP) are fully convertible at par, with no exchange control legislation in force. Current account and capital account transactions are completely liberalised; profit remittances, dividends, royalty repatriation, and service payments may be denominated in any freely usable currency without prior central-bank approval. The only material restrictions arise under the UK/Gibraltar sanctions regime (OFSI) and the AML framework, which require beneficiary checks and transaction purpose declarations but do not impose convertibility caps. A Bermuda exempted company operating through a Gibraltar branch can repatriate net profits to Bermuda without withholding tax or FX surrender obligation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or forced-repatriation rules apply to export proceeds. The Exchange Control Act 1947 (as extended to Gibraltar) was repealed effective 1979; accordingly, receipts for subtitle transcription, AI dubbing, and related post-production services can be retained in foreign currency, held offshore, or reinvested without any statutory timeline or conversion ratio. Record-keeping duties under the Beneficial Ownership (Access) Regulations 2017 and the GFSC AML/CFT Rulebook require that export invoices and settlement documentation be retained for at least five years, but this is a transparency rule and not a surrender requirement.

Central bank supervision: The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the integrated prudential and AML regulator for banking and electronic money activities; the Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at a fixed 1:1 parity with the Pound Sterling through the Currency Act 1934. The Bank of England remains the monetary and exchange-rate authority for the British pound, which circulates freely in Gibraltar. For practical foreign-exchange control purposes, the competent bodies are the GFSC, the Gibraltar Business (Corporate) Registry, and the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for everyday operating transactions (vendor payouts, freelance disbursements) but LOW/MEDIUM for initial tier-1 capital injection and escrow roles. Wise Business and Stripe can onboard the Gibraltar-registered overseas company as long as: (a) the ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) is identified on a valid passport; (b) the company maintains a Gibraltar registered office; (c) the EMI application is supported by a board resolution and a clear invoice-flow chart. Legal constraints: digital EMIs are licensed by the FCA (UK) or another EEA authority and operate in Gibraltar under an inward passporting arrangement; they are not banks and cannot create fiduciary deposits, take deposits for capital-accretion purposes, or offer lending. Synthetic media/AI voice cloning is not inherently prohibited, but the company must confirm the EMI's acceptable-use policy covers deepfake/audio-cloning services and, if requested, provide a compliance statement on biometric data usage under the Gibraltar Data Protection Act 2019.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For all paid-up capital subscriptions into the Gibraltar operating account, route via SWIFT MT103 from the Bermuda corporate bank account (or from a London or New York correspondent bank) using the following protocol: (a) narration: 'SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION - [BULK OF COMPANY] - BOARD RESOLUTION 2025-05'; (b) purpose code: '4001' (Bilateral Investments - Outside Banking Sector) or '6050' (Professional and Technical Services) depending on whether the wire is an equity injection or service prepayment; (c) attach a board resolution in PDF signed by two directors; (d) pre-alert the receiving bank with a copy of the corporate authorisation list; (e) if the amount exceeds GBP 50,000, file a report to the GFSC FIU through the bank's FIAU with the underlying initial-claim documentation. Verification at the receiving bank typically takes 2–4 business days; the bank will validate the UBO, confirm the existence of the Bermuda Certificate of Good Standing, and check OFSI/UN sanctions lists before passing funds to the operational account.Primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for both minority vendor payments and large master-license payouts. For transactions below GBP 10,000, the operation should use bank payment APIs or Wise Business direct country rails if both sides have FCA/EMI coverage; the trade settlement reference must include the client project code and service invoice number. For production houses requiring documentary security, a standby Letter of Credit (SBLC) or bank guarantee is acceptable because subtitling/dubbing is a service and not a goods shipment. Do not use a Commercial LC because title to goods does not exist; for service contracts requiring documentary checks, use a documentary collection under URC 522 with the equivalent of a sight draft and invoice, but this is rare in the CreativeTech industry. The bank will not apply exchange-surrender controls, yet will require each disbursement to be recorded with a purpose code (e.g., '1210' for films and audiovisual services) and an invoice copy retained in the compliance file for 5 years.Outright foreign-exchange forwards with Horizon of 1–12 months on GBP/USD, GBP/EUR, and EUR/USD, executed through a GFSC-regulated bank or UK FCA-authorised dealer, provided the underlying exposure is programme-level (client invoices and contractor payables) and documented in a board-approved FX policy.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on emerging-market currencies (INR, PHP, BRL) transacted via UK-licensed dealers with settlement in GBP or USD; these are permissible when the Gibraltar entity has no local bank account in the settlement currency.Cross-currency swaps (GBP/EUR or USD/EUR) for arbitrage-free matching of long-term production co-financing cash flows, executed under a bilateral ISDA Master Agreement with a GFSC-regulated counterparty, denominated as an end-user hedge with MiFID exemptions.Currency options (European-style) on GBP/USD for protecting revenue contracts that contain earn-out or royalty clauses; premium is paid upfront from operating cash flow and strikes are documented in the treasury policy.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For a Gibraltar-resident entity, a Local File is expected where connected-party revenue exceeds GIP 500,000 in the accounting period; a Master File is expected for groups with consolidated annual revenue above GIP 50 million. A non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company has no automatic Gibraltar TP filing threshold, but contemporaneous documentation must be maintained if it maintains a Gibraltar permanent establishment or derives Gibraltar-source income.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) to tax-resident entities undertaking relevant activities, including intellectual property holding, distribution, financing and digital services. An AI subtitle and dubbing business will ordinarily be characterised as an IP holding or mobile-business activity. The company must demonstrate real presence, qualified employees, physical premises, actual decision-making in the claimed jurisdiction, and core income-generating activities there. For a Bermuda Exempted Company, Bermuda's own Economic Substance Regulations are primary; failure to satisfy either Bermuda or Gibraltar substance requirements will allow the Commissioner to reclassify foreign income as Gibraltar-source and assess at 12.5%.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil reassessment on the full taxable profits at 12.5%; negligence penalty of 100% of the understated tax; deliberate evasion penalty of 200%; statutory interest at 9% per annum; criminal prosecution before the Magistrates Court or Supreme Court of Gibraltar.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for evaded tax under veil-piercing rules; courts may impose travel bans, worldwide asset freezes, civil restitution orders and imprisonment of up to 7 years.
Shell Company / False Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax exemption and back-assessment from inception; strike-off and dissolution under the Gibraltar Companies Act; director disqualification up to 15 years; fines of up to GIP 20,000 for each false economic substance declaration.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced to recover unpaid taxes and fees directly from directors' assets; directors may also be subjected to exit bans, disqualification, seizure of UK property and imprisonment for fraudulent trading.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Account freezing and confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act; AML penalties up to GIP 1 million or 10% of group turnover; criminal conviction for the Money Laundering Reporting Officer and for the company itself.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly consent to laundering AI licensing revenues are personally criminally liable; imprisonment up to 14 years, confiscation of personal and family assets, and civil restitution to defrauded third-party rightholders.
Transfer Pricing / Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Supply of transfer-pricing adjustments by the Commissioner or foreign tax authorities; disallowance of deductions; penalty equal to 50% of the adjustment; interest at 6% annual; extension of the statutory assessment period to 7 years for deliberate non-disclosure.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign tax returns containing false transfer-pricing declarations can be made personally liable for the adjustment; directors may be barred from acting as directors in the UK and Gibraltar and may face criminal penalties for evading the arm's-length requirement.
