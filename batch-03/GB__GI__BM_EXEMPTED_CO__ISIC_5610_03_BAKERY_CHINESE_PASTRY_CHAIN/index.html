Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Bermuda Exempted Company or to its Gibraltar external establishment. The company is formed with an authorised share capital of USD 100,000 divided into 100,000 ordinary shares of USD 1.00 each, of which 100 shares (USD 100) are issued and deemed paid-up under the first board resolution. No escrow account, bank confirmation, or capital-call procedure is statutorily required.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Income Tax Office, Government of Gibraltar (3-5 Secretary's Lane, Gibraltar; portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi) and HM Customs & Trade, Gibraltar (customs registration and clearance portal: Gibraltar One Government System).

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company establishing a place of business in Gibraltar must: (1) appoint a Gibraltar-resident company secretary or tax representative; (2) register as a foreign company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry within 30 days of commencement; (3) file Form IT-1 with the Income Tax Office attaching the Bermuda certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, and registered office statement to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN, 10-digit numeric format); (4) for import/export activity, obtain a Customs Trader Registration and an Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number through HM Customs & Trade; (5) for UK distance sales, separately register for UK VAT via HMRC and quote the GB VAT number on invoices.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A Bermuda Exempted Company establishing a place of business in Gibraltar must: (1) appoint a Gibraltar-resident company secretary or tax representative; (2) register as a foreign company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry within 30 days of commencement; (3) file Form IT-1 with the Income Tax Office attaching the Bermuda certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, and registered office statement to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN, 10-digit numeric format); (4) for import/export activity, obtain a Customs Trader Registration and an Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number through HM Customs & Trade; (5) for UK distance sales, separately register for UK VAT via HMRC and quote the GB VAT number on invoices.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election; the Bermuda Exempted Company is an entity-level taxpayer for any Gibraltar permanent establishment (PE). Available elections include: (i) Alternative Maximum Tax (profits cap) under Section 72A of the Income Tax Act 2010, applying 15% of emoluments plus 10% of occupation costs instead of the 12.5% standard rate; (ii) Qualifying Non-Resident Company election under Schedule IV for exemption on foreign-source passive income; (iii) UK hybrid mismatch rules (SI 2020/1558) require the Gibraltar PE to be treated as a separate person for specified cross-border arrangements; (iv) Bermuda's new corporate income tax (1 January 2025, 15% for MNE groups above EUR 750,000,000 consolidated revenue) provides a territorial credit mechanism. No 'tax transparency' election to flow profits to the Bermudian parent is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No Gibraltar VAT/GST; Gibraltar operates a customs import and excise duty regime entirely outside the UK and EU VAT territories.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for Gibraltar VAT/GST (none exists). UK VAT: for distance sales into the UK from a Gibraltar supplier, there is no monetary exemption threshold; registration is mandatory from the first GBP 1 of sales unless the goods are shipped via a marketplace operator that applies the UK marketplace VAT rules. For local UK physical supplies by a Gibraltar entity, the UK standard registration threshold of GBP 90,000 applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Since Gibraltar is outside the UK/EU VAT territory, exports of pastries and bakery goods from Gibraltar to the UK, EU, or other countries are outside the scope of any Gibraltar VAT/GST; the supplier neither charges VAT nor has an input VAT credit in Gibraltar (none exists). For UK VAT purposes, if the Bermuda Exempted Company is UK VAT-registered, goods physically exported from the UK to an overseas destination within 3 months of sale with commercial proof of export are zero-rated at 0%. Services (e.g., franchise royalties) supplied from Gibraltar to UK franchisees are outside the scope of UK VAT where the recipient belongs for VAT purposes in the UK; the UK franchisee must self-account under the reverse charge. Import of finished goods into the UK from Gibraltar triggers UK import VAT at 20%, recoverable via postponed VAT accounting (PVA) by the registered importer. Zero-rating is denied where the exporter cannot show physical export and consideration received within the statutory evidence period.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (SAD Form C88 / One System computerised entry) endorsed by HM Customs & Trade
  • UK/Gibraltar EORI number certificate and (if applicable) UK VAT registration certificate
  • Commercial Invoice in English with full HS tariff heading/country of origin statement
  • Packing List showing batch numbers, expiry dates, weights, and food hygiene export certificates for bakery products
  • Bill of Lading, CMR, or Air Waybill evidencing departure from Gibraltar customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 confirming full receipt and repatriation of export proceeds
  • Frame Agreement or Franchise Royalty Schedule identifying the export contract and payment terms (for cross-border services)

Gibraltar: no VAT e-invoicing mandate; customs declarations are filed electronically through the One System (asynchronous API clearance via GibCustoms). UK: HMRC Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT is mandatory for all VAT-registered exporters; functional compatible software and the MTD API must be used. UK import declarations on the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) require MARCUS/API gateway integration. Digital stamping and real-time fiscal clearance (e.g., Italy, Spain, Greece) are not applicable to Gibraltar operations.

Fraudulent export zero-rating or failure to substantiate export freight attracts retrospective assessments and severe penalties. Under Gibraltar Customs law, misdeclaration or failure to produce export documentation can result in forfeiture of goods, a civil penalty up to GBP 3,000, and 100% of evaded duty; criminal prosecution and imprisonment are possible in serious cases. For the UK, HMRC can issue a 20-year past-assessment under Section 77 VATA 1994, impose a minimum 100% error penalty, and issue personal joint-and-several liability notices to directors where there was knowledge of fraud (Section 61 VATA). MTIC carve-out rules reverse the burden of proof obliging the exporter to verify the physical supply chain and the absence of known linked fraud. Bingham-related transfer pricing adjustments apply to related-party royalty payments not repatriated within 180 days, with interest and penalties on underpaid tax. In Bermuda, a failure of the economic substance filing relating to core-income-generating activities for the franchise chain carries fines from USD 10,000 to USD 500,000 and possible imprisonment of directors for false declarations.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility in both the UK and Gibraltar; no exchange controls, no profit remittance authorization, and no ring-fencing of dividends, royalties, loan repayments, or repatriated capital. GIP/GBP parity is statutory and freely exchangeable. Capital account transactions (share subscriptions, shareholder loans, IP assignments) require no regulatory approval, but CRS/FATCA self-certification, substance documentation, and corporate solvency tests (Bermuda Exempted Companies Act) are mandatory to satisfy bank due diligence. The Offshore_Special_Territory status creates substance-attestation hurdles, not convertibility limitations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None in UK or Gibraltar. No statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation deadline applies to export receipts or franchise royalties; proceeds may be retained in GBP, USD, EUR, or CNH. Documentary discipline is required: receipts must trace to genuine pastry/franchise trade invoices to pass clearing-bank and correspondent-bank due diligence. Any inbound leg from mainland China must follow PRC SAFE verification (exports/imports and outbound FDI registration) before funds enter the Gibraltar clearing system.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary anchor for the Sterling Area and UK FX reserves policy); Government of Gibraltar Ministry of Finance (sole issuer of Gibraltar Pound notes/coin under the Currency Notes Act 1934, parity 1:1 with GBP); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) (prudential supervision of licensed banks and electronic money institutions).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium (3/5). Post-Brexit, UK FCA-authorized EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) may lawfully serve Gibraltar corporates without EEA passporting because Gibraltar is not an EU member; however, most EMIs apply house policies denying offshore tax-exempt domiciles such as Bermuda Exempted Companies unless the client provides Gibraltar substance proof and a regulated introducer. Gibraltar's own EMI regime under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2019 exists but has low issuance volume. EMIs cannot issue commercial letters of credit, provide committed credit, or act as prime clearing correspondents, so SWIFT MT103 rails via a licensed tier-1 bank remain the core treasury infrastructure; EMIs are used only for optimization of payroll and lower-value reimbursements.

EMIs cannot issue commercial letters of credit, provide committed credit, or act as prime clearing correspondents, so SWIFT MT103 rails via a licensed tier-1 bank remain the core treasury infrastructure; EMIs are used only for optimization of payroll and lower-value reimbursements.
