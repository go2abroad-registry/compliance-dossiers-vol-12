Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — Bermuda: the Exempted Company is exempt from Bermuda income tax, capital gains tax, and withholding tax; it pays an annual Government Fee and may not trade with Bermuda residents without a special permit. Gibraltar: non-resident unless managed and controlled in Gibraltar; a Gibraltar tax resident is taxable at 12.5% on territorial (Gibraltar-situs) profits only, and a branch/PE is assessed only on Gibraltar-source profits; Qualifying Company tax-neutral status is not available because QCs cannot carry on active business in Gibraltar. UK: the company is not UK tax resident; UK CFC and hybrid mismatch rules apply if UK management, assets, or risks are present.

Available election forms

Bermuda: no check-the-box or tax election regime; exempted status is statutory, and the Bermuda Ministry of Finance may issue a certificate of tax residency. Gibraltar: (i) Qualifying Company election under the Income Tax Act (fixed annual fee in lieu of tax) — incompatible with the Helpdesk trade; (ii) territorial/foreign-source income exclusion election under the Income Tax Act; (iii) hybrid mismatch neutralizing elections under Gibraltar hybrid-mismatch rules and UK TIOPA 2010/CTA 2010 Part 6A where the Bermuda entity is fiscally transparent in one state and opaque in another; (iv) Pillar Two GloBE/QDMTT election at 15% if group consolidated revenue reaches EUR 750M.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar imposes no VAT, GST, or sales and use tax (it is outside the EU VAT area; only customs/excise duties on imported goods and selective purchase taxes apply). For B2C digital helpdesk/SaaS supplies to UK consumers, UK VAT at 20% may apply because the place of supply follows the consumer; B2B supplies to UK business customers are reverse-charged.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in Gibraltar — no VAT/GST registration exists. UK: for a Bermuda Exempted Company established in Gibraltar, UK VAT registration is mandatory from the first GBP 1 of UK B2C digital services (no threshold for non-established suppliers); the GBP 90,000 UK threshold does not apply. Importers of dutiable goods into Gibraltar must register with the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Department irrespective of value.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

None at the Gibraltar level — Gibraltar has no VAT, so there is no export VAT zero-rating or input-VAT refund mechanism; Helpdesk SaaS exports from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax. For UK VAT (if registered): B2B cloud/helpdesk services to customers abroad are outside the scope of UK VAT; B2C digital services to non-UK customers are outside the scope (place of supply is the customer's country); physical exports of goods from the UK qualify for 0% zero-rating only where valid export evidence is held and goods leave the UK within 3 months. No input-VAT refund can be claimed in Gibraltar or Bermuda.

  • UK Customs Export Declaration (CDS) and declaration receipt — only where physical goods are exported from the UK
  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document) — only where physical goods transit or are exported through Gibraltar
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing goods movement to a foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103/MT950 credit advice, or Stripe_Billing/PayPal settlement report) evidencing FX repatriation of export proceeds
  • Signed SaaS helpdesk contracts, customer tax-residency certificates, IP-address/delivery logs, and ticket-resolution metadata substantiating the place of supply

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping mandate exists in Gibraltar or Bermuda. For UK VAT, Making Tax Digital requires VAT returns via HMRC's API; PEPPOL e-invoicing applies only to UK central-government suppliers. Recommended retention: structured e-invoices (EN 16931 XML or PDF) showing customer country, place of supply, outside-scope/reverse-charge indicator, and transaction traces from Stripe_Billing and PayPal.

Gibraltar: under the Income Tax Act 2010, the Commissioner may re-characterize management/control or PE situs and assess within 6 years (20 years for fraud/negligence), with penalties of up to 100% of tax plus interest; misdeclaration penalties apply to incorrect territorial sourcing. UK (if VAT-registered): HMRC may claw back zero-rating/outside-scope treatment where export evidence is missing or defective, assess under VATA 1994 s.73, and impose Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 penalties of up to 100% of the VAT involved for deliberate concealment; fraudulent evasion carries criminal sanctions and director disqualification. Bermuda: economic substance non-compliance triggers financial penalties and potential strike-off of the Exempted Company.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account. The GIP is pegged at a fixed 1:1 rate to GBP, is legally interchangeable with GBP at par for all settlement purposes, and is accepted by all domestic and correspondent clearing systems. There are no exchange licences, no prior-authorisation requirements for acquisition of foreign currency, no surrender certificates, and no restrictions on dividend distribution, profit repatriation, service fee outflows, or intra-group capital movements affecting a low-risk Helpdesk SaaS customer-support entity. Current-account transactions (subscription revenues, payouts to Stripe/PayPal, vendor disbursements) flow freely; capital-account transactions (share capital injection, shareholder loans, redemption of capital, dissolution remittances) are unrestricted. The GFSC applies no currency matching rules on operational bank accounts. The only practical constraint is the operational note-issuance boundary: GIP banknotes are legal tender only within Gibraltar and must be converted to GBP for external use, which the licensed banks perform at par without charge. Profit remittance is tax-free at source under the Tax Exempt Zone / Bermuda Exempted Company treatment, and no Gibraltar withholding tax applies to outgoing dividends, interest, or service royalties. A Bermuda Exempted Company that is tax-resident elsewhere may nevertheless maintain capital and operating accounts freely in GIP, GBP, EUR, or USD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Gibraltar imposes no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds or inbound service fees to the central monetary authority, no obligatory conversion timeline (e.g., no 30-day or 60-day repatriation deadline), and no minimum mandated conversion ratio. All proceeds from the customer-support Helpdesk SaaS business (Stripe Billing, PayPal) may be retained in the currency of receipt, held in a multi-currency account, or converted at arm's-length commercial rates. For the Bermuda Exempted Company, there is similarly no deemed-repatriation rule; funds may accumulate offshore indefinitely subject only to adequate substance and AML documentation under the Gibraltar Beneficial Ownership (Access) Regulations and the Companies Act. Best practice nevertheless requires that export-service proceeds are traceable to signed subscription agreements and invoicing ledgers, because banks will apply a 'source-of-funds' reasonableness test on cumulative balances.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for the United Kingdom, whose Sterling notes and coin are legal tender in Gibraltar alongside the Gibraltar Pound) and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), which acts as the integrated financial services regulator and issuer of Gibraltar banknotes under the Currency Notes Act 1934. Gibraltar has no independent central bank, no proprietary RTGS system, and no separate FX-control agency; monetary stability is achieved through the 1:1 currency-board-style peg between the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) and GBP, with GIP issuance fully backed by UK Government securities and Bank of England reserves held at the GFSC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for supplementary payment collection, MEDIUM for primary treasury. Gibraltar has a dedicated Electronic Money Institution framework under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2019, administered by the GFSC. Locally authorised EMIs (e.g., Zalgo Ltd, Moneycatcha Ltd, Alanda, and The Payment Group) provide multi-currency virtual IBANs, SEPA and Faster Payments rails, and cross-border USD/EUR settlement that align well with low-risk Helpdesk SaaS collections. Legal constraint: an unlicensed offshore EMI cannot maintain a Gibraltar operational account; a Bermuda Exempted Company must either open its own GFSC-licensed bank account (GIB, Jyske, EFG) or operate exclusively through a GFSC-authorised EMI payment-composition account. Because corporate banking due-diligence standards require a regulated bank relationship for capital injections and treasury concentration, the recommended structure is a dual-rail setup: (i) a licensed-bank operational GBP account at GIB or Jyske, and (ii) an EMI float account (USD/EUR/Stripe/PayPal settlement) with daily sweeping to the primary bank account to reduce counterparty concentration.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections are executed via SWIFT MT103 (Priority) or MT202 (Interbank) into the designated Capital Escrow Account at a GFSC-licensed bank. The payment reference must be structured as 'CAPITAL INJECTION — [Entity Name] — BO Resolution dd/mm/yyyy — Share Subscription No. [X]', and must carry ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' (Equity) or 'INVE' (Investment). The bank will require a subscribed capital worksheet, a board resolution certified by the secretary, and the Bermuda Exempted Company's share-certificate register updated to evidence issuance. Where the injector is a connected overseas entity, also provide an intra-group funding agreement and an investor declaration showing source of wealth, and confirm that the funds originate from a regulated bank account in an FATF-compliant jurisdiction. If the capital is used to acquire operating assets or to pre-fund the Helpdesk platform, the bank may open a blocked escrowed capital account: funds remain classified as 'restricted use' until the funding purpose is evidenced by vendor invoices or PSP settlement agreements. No Gibraltar FX levy or stamp duty applies to the inbound capital; however, it is prudent to sweep injected capital immediately to an Operational GBP Account with a clearly stated 'Share Premium Reserve' notation.Outbound settlement for trade disbursements and operating expenses uses the following rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border vendor payments, cloud-infra subscriptions (e.g., AWS, OpenAI API), and legal/professional fees, using purpose codes 'SUPP' (Supply of Services) or 'COMC' (Commercial Payment) with a matching invoice; (2) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant for EU suppliers via the bank's EUR correspondent (e.g., via EFG Global network); (3) Bank of England CHAPS for high-value GBP vendor settlements; (4) Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP600) or Standby L/C issued by the GFSC licensed bank for large platform-procurement agreements, with documents presented through a global correspondents network; and (5) Faster Payments Service for any payroll/national insurance obligations to Gibraltar residents. For recurring Helpdesk payouts to subcontracted support agents, the outbound instruction must embed a payroll/remittance reference and a monthly consolidated schedule to avoid fragmented-transaction flags. All outbound disbursements above GBP 10,000 require a standing instruction and debit-authorisation form pre-signed by the accounts team, with limits consistent with the approved treasury policy. Because GIP is non-clearing outside Gibraltar, any outbound trade settlement denominated in Gibraltar Pounds must be converted with the issuing bank at par to GBP prior to SWIFT/CHAPS release.Outright FX Forwards (deliverable, up to 24-month tenor) on GBP/EUR and GBP/USD, executed under an ISDA 2013 EMIR-cleared framework or a GFSC-bank-internal master agreement; must be aligned to forecast Stripe/PayPal settlements and vendor obligations to satisfy the hedging-relevance test.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) on restricted or non-convertible emerging-market currencies (e.g., US/GIP exposures against currencies subject to local FX controls), cash-settled in USD or EUR against a declared Reference Rate, used only when a corresponding deliverable forward is unavailable; GIP/GBP itself is pegged 1:1, so an NDF is unnecessary barring extreme parity-stress hedges.Cross-Currency Swaps (principal and interest-exchange) between GBP and USD or EUR for maturities 6–36 months, used to match the entity's financial-liability currency profile with its operating-account currency, including swap of Stripe USD settlements into GBP and the re-pricing of any intra-group funding notes.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH for supplementary payment collection, MEDIUM for primary treasury. Gibraltar has a dedicated Electronic Money Institution framework under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2019, administered by the GFSC. Locally authorised EMIs (e.g., Zalgo Ltd, Moneycatcha Ltd, Alanda, and The Payment Group) provide multi-currency virtual IBANs, SEPA and Faster Payments rails, and cross-border USD/EUR settlement that align well with low-risk Helpdesk SaaS collections. Legal constraint: an unlicensed offshore EMI cannot maintain a Gibraltar operational account; a Bermuda Exempted Company must either open its own GFSC-licensed bank account (GIB, Jyske, EFG) or operate exclusively through a GFSC-authorised EMI payment-composition account. Because corporate banking due-diligence standards require a regulated bank relationship for capital injections and treasury concentration, the recommended structure is a dual-rail setup: (i) a licensed-bank operational GBP account at GIB or Jyske, and (ii) an EMI float account (USD/EUR/Stripe/PayPal settlement) with daily sweeping to the primary bank account to reduce counterparty concentration.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Bermuda: under the Transfer Pricing Regulations 2023 and the accompanying Transfer Pricing Guidance, all exempted entities engaged in connected-party cross-border transactions must prepare contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation consistent with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines; no codified de minimis threshold applies, but in practice the Bermuda Registrar expects a full Local File where annual connected-party cross-border consideration exceeds BMD 1,000,000 and a Master File-equivalent group overview where worldwide group revenue exceeds BMD 100,000,000. Gibraltar: Income Tax Act 2010 s.88 (OECD-aligned) imposes the arm's-length standard without a statutory monetary filing threshold; GITO expects a Local File where Gibraltar-related-party cross-border turnover exceeds GBP 1,000,000 per annum and a Master File where the worldwide group turnover exceeds GBP 100,000,000; a simple memorandum benchmarking study is acceptable below those levels

Economic substance scope: Bermuda: the Economic Substance Amendment Act 2019 and ESR Regulations require every exempted company carrying on a 'relevant activity' — including 'distribution and service business' (cross-border provision of services to connected persons) and 'intellectual property business' — to maintain (i) direction and management in Bermuda (regular board meetings in Bermuda with actual quorum); (ii) adequate employees, premises, and expenditure in Bermuda proportionate to the activity; and (iii) performance of core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Bermuda. The customer-support/helpdesk SaaS operation will ordinarily be classified as 'distribution and service business' and must file an annual ESR declaration. Gibraltar: the Income Tax (Economic Substance) Rules replicate the same doctrine for all Gibraltar tax-resident companies: staff, premises, expenditure, and management must be adequate and proportionate; a Bermudan Exempted Company whose directors and operations are physically and managerially located in Gibraltar will satisfy neither the Bermudan nor the Gibraltarian substance test, exposing the structure to reclassification as Gibraltar-resident (12.5% tax) and to Bermuda ESR penalties

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: GITO will treat the company as Gibraltar tax-resident, assess 12.5% tax on worldwide profits, impose interest on unpaid tax (currently 1% per month or part-month), and levy tax-geared penalties up to 100% of the additional tax for deliberate inaccuracy (Income Tax Act 2010 s.233-236); a separate fixed penalty of GBP 100 plus GBP 60 per day of continued non-filing applies; criminal prosecution on conviction may produce an unlimited fine and imprisonment of up to 7 years

Director personal liability: Gibraltar law permits the court to make directors personally liable for the company's unpaid tax where the director knew of the false residency declaration or participated in the diversion of profits; criminal convictions can yield 7 years' imprisonment, confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, and travel bans; disqualification orders of up to 15 years may issue under the Company Directors Disqualification Act (Gibraltar)
Shell Company and Sham Residency

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Registrar: ESR penalties of BD$10,000 for false declarations plus daily default penalties, an Order compelling compliance, and strike-off from the Register; GITO may invoke the management-and-control test to pierce the Bermuda entity and charge Gibraltar tax from inception; the tax assessment and penalties are not creditable in Bermuda

Director personal liability: Bermuda directors who sign a false ESR declaration commit an offence punishable by a fine of BD$5,000 and 12 months' imprisonment; Gibraltar-resident shadow directors face joint and several civil liability for GITO assessments, disqualification, and freezing orders over personal assets where the company is insolvent and the shell arrangement operated as a facade
Economic Substance Failure (Dual-Residency Arbitrage)

Statutory sanction: Bermuda: escalating daily penalties capped at BD$10,000 and strike-off; Gibraltar: the GITO may deny PE/treaty attribution and reallocate the full pan-European revenue to the Gibraltar taxable base; an additional material penalty of up to 12.5% of the reallocated profit is levied where the substance deficiency is deemed deliberate; the company may also be reported to the UK HMRC under the UK-Gibraltar exchange of information arrangement, triggering an HMRC corporate- interest- restriction enquiry

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to ensure substance face personal civil claims by creditors for trading without economic substance (breach of duty); in Gibraltar, a director of an offending company can be made personally liable for the entire tax-assessed amount where the company is unable to pay; a criminal conviction for knowingly providing false substance information carries imprisonment of up to 2 years and an unlimited fine
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar: GITO will adjust the consideration to the arm's-length price under Income Tax Act 2010 s.88, increase chargeable profits, apply interest at 1% per month, and impose tax-geared penalties of up to 100% of the additional tax for a deliberate transfer-pricing error; Bermuda: the transfer-pricing provisions apply to the deemed profit allocation for ESR purposes, with penalties of up to BD$10,000 for non-documentation and a notification of non-compliance to the Bermuda Tax Authority

Director personal liability: Directors who approve accounts showing an understated inter-company charge are liable for knowing participation in tax evasion; civil recovery extends to repayment of dividends paid out of profits that the arm's-length adjustment eliminates; Gibraltar courts may order disqualification and director contribution orders equal to the tax shortfall; criminal sanctions include fines and imprisonment up to 7 years
