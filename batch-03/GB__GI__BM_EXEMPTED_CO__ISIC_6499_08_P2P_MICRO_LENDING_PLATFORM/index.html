Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax); official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/tax

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company conducting P2P lending business in Gibraltar must first register as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 2014, obtaining a Gibraltar company registration number. Thereafter, the company must apply to the Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) by filing the relevant taxpayer registration form and providing constitutional documents, beneficial ownership information, and evidence of central management and control. If the company carries on a trade through a branch or permanent establishment in Gibraltar, it becomes liable to Gibraltar income tax and must file annual returns on that TRN. The TRN is the functional TIN for all tax filings, customs transactions, and HM Government of Gibraltar correspondence. Non-resident companies without a Gibraltar permanent establishment are not required to register as taxpayers unless they receive Gibraltar-source income within the Income Tax Act.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

There is no United States-style check-the-box election in Gibraltar. The entity's Bermuda Exempted Company status confers tax exemption in Bermuda, but this is not automatic for Gibraltar tax purposes. Gibraltar legislation contains anti-hybrid rules designed to neutralize mismatches where an entity is treated differently for tax purposes in other jurisdictions. A Bermuda exempted company may elect to rely on the non-residence criteria under Gibraltar tax law, but once it has a permanent establishment in Gibraltar, it is taxed as an opaque entity under the Income Tax Act 2010. Gibraltar also provides for Qualifying Company status under economic substance regulations, which may apply to certain international business activities.

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing, API stamping, or continuous transaction controls are in operation in Gibraltar. Businesses must preserve commercial invoices, vouchers, and underlying transaction records for inspection by the Tax Office and Gibraltar Customs. Customs declarations are processed electronically via the Gibraltar Customs system.

No indirect-tax export-fraud regime exists due to the absence of VAT/GST in Gibraltar. However, any misuse of customs duty reliefs, preferential origin claims, or false export declarations may trigger penalties under Gibraltar Customs legislation, including duty clawback, monetary penalties of up to 500% of the duty value, forfeiture of goods, and criminal prosecution. HMRC or Gibraltar authorities may also apply general anti-abuse rules to cross-border lending arrangements with a tax avoidance purpose.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates a fully convertible, zero-exchange-control regime covering both capital and current account transactions. The Gibraltar pound is freely interchangeable with sterling at parity, cross-border remittances require no exchange-control approval, and profit repatriation from the Gibraltar operating hub to the Bermuda Exempted Company is unimpeded by Gibraltarian withholding taxes or FX restrictions. The Bermuda Exempted Company is treated as non-resident for Bermuda exchange-control purposes under the Exchange Control Act 1972 and may therefore hold and remit foreign currency freely, subject only to Bermuda Monetary Authority reporting where a designated deposit is maintained. Practical convertibility constraints arise exclusively from sanctions screening, source-of-funds verification, and segregated client-money safeguarding obligations; none of these restrict legal convertibility. The principal gating condition for lending-related proceeds is statutory licensure: a GFSC consumer-credit licence under the Consumer Credit Act 2013 for Gibraltarian borrowers, or FCA consumer-credit permission for UK borrowers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar law imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed holding timeline for export or loan-account receipts. For this P2P micro-lending platform, borrower principal and interest collections may be retained in the currency of collection or freely converted at commercial rates. Bermuda equally imposes no surrender obligation on the exempted company. The de facto compliance requirement is preservation of a complete audit trail of repayment provenance under the GFSC AML/CFT Guidance Notes, coupled with safeguarding of client funds in segregated accounts at an authorised credit institution.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of England acts as the central monetary authority for sterling, with the Government of Gibraltar exercising currency-issuing powers for the Gibraltar pound (GIP) under the Currency Notes Act 1934 at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP. Financial services and banking supervision in Gibraltar are vested in the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC). HM Treasury and the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) extend UK financial sanctions to Gibraltar. There is no separate Gibraltarian central bank; monetary policy is de facto determined by the Bank of England.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility. Gibraltar regulates e-money institutions under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Electronic Money Regulations, but no Gibraltar-licensed EMI currently offers SEPA-scale clearing comparable to UK EMIs. The platform should therefore adopt a dual-track approach: (i) obtain a GFSC e-money licence for local wallet operations, safeguarding and issuer functions, and (ii) contract with an FCA-authorised UK EMI (for example Modulr or Railsr) for GBP Faster Payments and SEPA Instant access via an agent or partner arrangement. Legal constraints include the post-Brexit absence of UK-to-Gibraltar passporting, requiring separate GFSC authorisation or a Gibraltar bank sponsorship, and strict client-money segregation rules under Gibraltar's Financial Services (Client Money) Rules where lending and e-money activities coexist.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity and subordinated loan capital should be injected from the Bermuda Exempted Company via SWIFT MT103, or via UK Faster Payments/CHAPS where the funding bank is UK-based, using ISO 20022 purpose code EQUI for equity injections or LOAN for intercompany shareholder loans. The remittance instruction must reference the board resolution, the capital contribution schedule, and Bermuda share-issue consideration. Post-arrival verification requires matching funds to executed subscription documents, updating the Bermuda register of members, recording the transaction in the GFSC capital-resources return where the platform is licensed, and preserving correspondent-bank transfer evidence for source-of-funds audits. Funds must arrive in a segregated Capital Escrow Account without commingling with client money, and remunerating flows must be routed separately to the Operational Account. All inward transactions are additionally subject to prevailing statistical-reporting returns (including Bank of England ITRS or GFSC data collections) as advised by the receiving bank.Loan disbursements will run over local wallet payouts and ACH-style clearing rails: UK Faster Payments Service (FPS) for GBP disbursements up to GBP 100,000, SEPA Instant or SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated micro-loans, and SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA borrowers or larger cross-border portfolio purchases. Commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700) are reserved for whole-loan acquisitions and syndicated warehouse facilities rather than consumer disbursements. All outbound instructions are executed exclusively from the Operational Account, pre-mapped to the scheduled principal and interest waterfall in each loan agreement, with beneficiary-name matching, velocity limits and mule-account detection applied before release. Client funds are never used for treasury or working-capital purposes.Deliverable over-the-counter FX forwards with tenors of up to 12 to 24 months executed with a Gibraltar-licensed or UK-authorised bank under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement, used to fix GBP/EUR/USD conversion costs on cross-border lending inflows and repayment streams.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for hedging illiquid or restricted emerging-market borrower currencies where deliverable convertibility is limited, with cash-settlement mechanics governed by the International Swaps and Derivatives Association definitions and the EMIR/UK EMIR margin framework.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) and single-currency interest-rate swaps for asset-liability matching between fixed-rate micro-loan assets and floating-rate warehouse funding, thereby reducing duration, interest-rate basis and FX basis risk across the treasury portfolio.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 aggregate value of controlled transactions for mandatory Local File; GIP 50,000,000 consolidated group turnover for Master File; Country-by-Country Report applies to groups with consolidated turnover above EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's Economic Substance Act 2018 applies. P2P micro-lending constitutes a 'financing and leasing' relevant activity, requiring full substance: employees with appropriate expertise, a physical office in Gibraltar, locally exercised management and board decision-making, and core income-generating activities (credit underwriting, loan contract administration, risk management, and funding decisions) performed in Gibraltar. A Bermuda Exempted Company claiming non-residence must still file a Substance Details Report; failure to meet substance tests triggers GIP 100,000 fixed penalties plus GIP 20,000 monthly ongoing penalties, potential forced striking-off, and automatic exchange of information with the UK and EU.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 200% of the undercharged tax plus interest at 1% per month; summary criminal conviction: fine up to GIP 10,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years; indictment: unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 7 years. The company also faces forfeiture orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved the false return are jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax. Courts may impose travel bans, asset restraint orders, director disqualification for up to 15 years, and personal criminal imprisonment up to 7 years.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Automatic failure of the economic substance test; fixed penalty GIP 100,000 and daily penalty GIP 20,000. Continuous non-compliance results in company strike-off and dissolution. The Gibraltar Commissioner may also assess a deemed taxable profit equal to 10% of gross lending margin.

Director personal liability: Directors of the shell and any Gibraltar shadow director are personally liable for the monetary penalties if they knowingly caused the substance deficiency. Travel bans, director disqualification up to 10 years, and personal liability for tax arrears upon liquidation are available to the Gibraltar authorities.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment recalculating taxable profit on an arm's-length basis; disallowance of excessive interest; 35% tax-geared penalty on understated profit; additional daily penalties for failure to provide documentation. Public disclosure of the adjustment under the EU Council Directive 2016/881.

Director personal liability: Directors of both the financing entity and Gibraltar borrower entity are jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax and penalties if they knowingly implemented non-arm's-length pricing. The courts may issue international asset tracing, travel bans, and personal contribution orders up to 10 years from the date of assessment.
Unlicensed Micro-Lending / AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed financial services activity: fine up to GIP 5,000,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years; the Financial Services Commission may require disgorgement of all profits derived from the illegal lending; regulatory revocation of the Gibraltar branch registration; public naming and sanction orders.

Director personal liability: All officers and de facto managers are subject to extradition requests, travel bans, and director disqualification for up to 10 years. Civil recovery orders may encumber personal assets. If the corporate veil is pierced for fraud, creditors and regulatory authorities may seek unlimited personal civil restitution for investor losses.
