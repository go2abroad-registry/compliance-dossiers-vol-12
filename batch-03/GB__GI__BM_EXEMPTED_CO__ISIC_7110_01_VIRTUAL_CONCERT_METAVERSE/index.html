Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Office of the Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar; portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company with a Gibraltar nexus must apply to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office for a Tax Reference Number (TRN) after registering its external company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies. As Gibraltar does not levy VAT, no separate VAT ID is issued. The application includes certified copies of the Bermuda certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, Gibraltar registered office address, beneficial ownership schedule, and a statement of expected economic activity. Non-resident foreign companies may be required to appoint a local tax representative. Issuance generally occurs within five working days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar law does not provide a check-the-box election for foreign entities. A foreign company may, however, become Gibraltar tax-resident by central management and control being exercised in Gibraltar; otherwise it remains non-resident and is taxed only on Gibraltar-source income at entity level. No separate hybrid mismatch election is available under Gibraltar domestic law, but the OECD hybrid mismatch rules apply to cross-border payments where relevant.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales-and-Use Tax (Gibraltar does not operate a general consumption tax; import/customs duties apply to physical goods)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT/GST; no registration threshold exists. For customs purposes, any importer with physical goods crossing Gibraltar's borders may require customs registration, and the import duty threshold is effectively zero (duties accrue from the first unit of applicable goods).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar does not levy VAT, therefore no 0% VAT export zero-rating regime applies. Exports of physical goods may be relieved from Gibraltar customs/import duties where the goods are permanently exported outside the jurisdiction. Offshore services, including virtual concert hosting, digital ticket tokenization, and metaverse performance revenues, are outside the scope of Gibraltar's customs duty system and are not subject to indirect tax. No indirect tax refund claim mechanism is available because no indirect tax is charged on such supplies.

  • Not applicable for digital virtual-concert services; no customs export declaration is required.
  • If physical goods are later exported: Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, commercial invoice, and bank/remittance evidence proving inward foreign exchange repatriation.

Not applicable. Gibraltar has no real-time fiscal clearance mandate, no e-invoicing clearance API, and no digital stamping or cryptographic invoicing requirement for services. Standard commercial invoices and underlying contracts are sufficient for indirect tax record-keeping purposes.

Although Gibraltar has no VAT zero-rating, fraudulent exemption claims, false export declarations to obtain customs duty relief, or misuse of a Bermuda Exempted Company's tax-exempt status may trigger reassessment, denial of exemption, recovery of unpaid duties and interest, fines of up to double the duty at risk, and criminal prosecution under Gibraltar's customs and income tax legislation. The Tax Commissioner may also cancel the Tax Reference Number or impose late-filing penalties for non-compliance with registration obligations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Currency convertibility is unrestricted; the UK Exchange Control Act 1947 has been fully repealed and Gibraltar maintains a liberalised current and capital account regime. Virtual-concert ticket revenue, metaverse licensing income, and USDC-denominated proceeds may be freely converted into GIP, GBP, USD, or EUR and remitted offshore without prior central-bank approval. Profit repatriation to the Bermuda parent by dividend or management fee is subject only to CRS/FATCA reporting and to the preservation of the exempted company's non-Gibraltar tax residence. No withholding tax applies on dividends, interest, royalties, or fees paid to non-residents, and no mandatory conversion or valuation requirement applies to foreign-currency balances held at Gibraltar banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules, conversion ratios, or repatriation timelines apply to export proceeds in Gibraltar or the UK. Digital-ticket receivables and platform settlements may be retained indefinitely in any currency, including USDC, and converted or reinvested at commercial discretion. The only binding obligations are record-keeping under the GFSC AML/CFT Regulations 2020 and accurate economic-substance reporting to the Bermuda Registrar to preserve the tax-exempt status of the parent entity.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no indigenous central bank. The Gibraltar Pound (GIP) is a local currency issuance of the Government of Gibraltar, pegged at par with GBP sterling and fully backed by UK reserves. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) acts as the prudential, conduct, and AML/CFT supervisor for all banks, e-money institutions, payment institutions, and DLT providers operating in Gibraltar. The Bank of England's monetary policy anchors the GIP peg, and UK HM Treasury remains the ultimate sovereign authority for sanctions and financial-crime frameworks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for a Bermuda exempted company with clean UBO records and 100% attributable ownership. GFSC-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) and UK FCA-authorised payment institutions represent viable offshore digital rails for Stripe payouts and USDC settlement. Legal constraints: (i) EEA passporting for UK/Gibraltar EMIs no longer exists post-Brexit, so a standalone Gibraltar e-money licence or a UK FCA authorisation with contractual Gibraltar servicing is required; (ii) USDC treasury flows must be intermediated through a GFSC-licensed DLT provider or a qualifying stablecoin exchange to avoid unlicensed custody activity; (iii) the EMI must maintain a safeguarding account at a Tier-1 Gibraltar bank or a UK clearing bank; (iv) the Bermuda entity must provide a certified register of directors and beneficial owners aligned with Bermuda economic-substance filings and must confirm that no Gibraltar management and control is exercised.
