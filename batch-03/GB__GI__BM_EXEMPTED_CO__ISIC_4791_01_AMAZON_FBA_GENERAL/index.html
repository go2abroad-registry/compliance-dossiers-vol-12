Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Bermuda exempted company. The company must have a minimum authorized share capital sufficient to issue at least one ordinary share with a par value (USD 0.01 or above); at least that share must be subscribed before delivery of the Certificate of Incorporation. No escrow account or capital deposit is required under Bermuda law; the board shall approve the initial allotment in the first board resolution.

Funding & unlock path: No fire-rescue or structural-load clearance required for a virtual online operation. If a Gibraltar office or small warehouse is opened, fire safety certification from the Gibraltar Fire and Rescue Service and an occupational workplace risk assessment under the Occupational Safety and Health Act (Gibraltar) will be obtained before staff or stock are introduced.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), administered under the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended); online portal: Gibraltar Tax E-Services at https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation; customs matters: HM Customs Gibraltar (Customs and Excise Department), ASYCUDA World portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Bermuda Exempted Company as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry (Companies Act 2014) by filing a certified copy of the Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, Register of Directors and Shareholders, and the appointment of a Gibraltar-resident registered office/agent. Step 2: Determine Gibraltar tax residence — a company is resident where its management and control is exercised (Section 8, Income Tax Act 2010); a Bermuda Exempted Company managed from Gibraltar is automatically resident. Step 3: Submit the tax registration application via the Income Tax Office e-services portal or on Form (Company Registration), accompanied by the Certificate of Good Standing from the Bermuda Registrar, the Gibraltar foreign-company registration certificate, statutory declarations on ultimate beneficial ownership (for AML/CDD), and a letter of appointment of a Gibraltar tax representative. Step 4: The Commissioner issues a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (GTIN) and annual assessment reference; the entity must thereafter file annual corporation tax returns and pay any tax assessed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Bermuda Exempted Company is statutorily exempt from Bermuda corporation tax, income tax, capital gains tax and withholding tax under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 (guaranteed until 2035). In Gibraltar, the entity is treated either as a non-resident company (taxed only on income arising in or derived from Gibraltar) or, if management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, as a Gibraltar tax-resident company charged on territorial profits at the standard corporate rate of 12.5% (20% for utilities, telecommunications and petroleum undertakings). Gibraltar imposes no capital gains tax, no VAT, no wealth tax, and no withholding tax on dividends or interest.

Available election forms

Bermuda: irrevocable election for a Tax Exemption Certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, guaranteeing freedom from all Bermuda taxes imposed after 2035 until 2050 (as extended). Gibraltar: no check-the-box regime exists; however, (i) an overseas company may elect to be assessed as a Gibraltar-resident company by filing a notice of election with the Commissioner of Income Tax, (ii) group relief elections under Sections 41-45 of the Income Tax Act 2010 permit loss surrender between 75%+ group companies, (iii) capital allowance elections may accelerate deductions on qualifying plant and machinery, and (iv) the UK-Gibraltar double taxation arrangement (in force from 1 January 2021) permits treaty tie-breaker residence elections and claims; no separate hybrid-mismatch election is available in Gibraltar for this structure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar operates no VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax. The only indirect tax in force is Import Duty under the Customs Ordinance 1835 (as amended), levied at the point of customs entry. For Amazon FBA operations physically stocked in the United Kingdom, the entity becomes subject to UK Value Added Tax (VATA 1994), current standard rate 20%, with compulsory registration before supply because the entity is not UK-established.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: Not applicable — no VAT/GST registration exists; importers are required to obtain a customs registration only, and no monetary threshold applies. UK VAT (applicable to UK-stocked FBA inventory): statutory registration threshold is GBP 90,000 per rolling 12-month period for UK-established businesses, but the threshold is reduced to zero for a non-UK-established Bermuda Exempted Company, which must register for UK VAT before making its first taxable supply of goods in the UK.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar: because Gibraltar has no VAT, no export zero-rating mechanism arises domestically; exported goods are outside the charge to Import Duty on proof of shipment out of Gibraltar. UK VAT (where the entity is registered for UK VAT through Amazon FBA UK): exports of goods sold by the entity to persons outside the United Kingdom — including EU consumers post-Brexit — are zero-rated under Section 30 VATA 1994 and the VAT (Export of Goods) Order 1995 (SI 1995/2518), provided (i) the goods are exported to a destination outside the UK within 3 months of the supply, (ii) the supplier obtains and retains the prescribed export evidence within the stated time limits, (iii) the goods are supplied to a recipient established outside the UK, and (iv) the goods leave the UK in the same state. Transfers of inventory to EU Amazon fulfillment centres qualify as zero-rated removals under the VAT (Removal of Goods) Rules where evidence of removal is retained. Input VAT recovery on export-related costs is available to the entity under normal refund rules provided the 180-day repatriation benchmark is satisfied.

None in Gibraltar — no real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no mandatory e-invoicing scheme applies. Customs import and export declarations are submitted electronically through the ASYCUDA World platform. For UK VAT, the entity, if registered, is mandated to maintain digital records and submit returns under HMRC Making Tax Digital (MTD) using compatible accounting software; no additional invoice clearance or digital stamping requirement applies in the UK.

Under UK law, where zero-rating is claimed without adequate export evidence, HMRC may raise a protective assessment under VATA 1994 Section 73 (standard 4-year assessment window extended to 20 years in cases of dishonesty), charge interest under FA 2009 Sections 101-105, and impose penalties under Schedule 24 FA 2007 of up to 30% for careless errors, 70% for deliberate but not concealed errors, and 100% for deliberate and concealed errors. A failure to meet the 180-day inward remittance benchmark will normally trigger a verification review and, in the absence of satisfactory explanation, a retrospective clawback of input tax credits and reversal of the zero-rating with assessment of 20% UK VAT on the full FBA sales value. Criminal liability may arise under VATA 1994 Section 72 (fraudulent evasion of VAT — up to 7 years' imprisonment) and under the Customs and Excise Management Act 1979 Sections 167-169 (false export declarations — civil penalties up to GBP 2,500 per offence, forfeiture of goods, and imprisonment for fraudulent evasion of customs duty). In Gibraltar, the Customs Ordinance imposes penalties for false declarations, including forfeiture of goods, fines not exceeding GBP 2,500, and, for fraudulent acts, criminal prosecution; no VAT clawback mechanism exists because no VAT regime applies in Gibraltar.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility applies in the UK and Gibraltar: there is no prior-approval requirement, exchange-control ceiling, or repatriation limitation on capital injection, operational distributions, dividend and royalty payments, intercompany loan repayments, or disposal proceeds. The Bermuda Exempted Company, qualified as exchange-control non-resident under the Bermuda Exchange Control Regulations 1973, is likewise unrestricted in foreign-currency holdings and remittances. Profit-remittance hurdles are purely documentary: banks require UBO declarations, economic-substance evidence for the Gibraltar admin office that directs the Amazon FBA activity, and OFSI/EU/UN sanctions clearance before any FX conversion or dividend outflow is executed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. The UK abolished exchange controls in 1979 and Gibraltar has never imposed such obligations. Amazon FBA sales proceeds received in GBP, USD, or EUR may be retained indefinitely in any currency without conversion into GIP. AML/CFT reconciliation requires traceability of proceeds to Amazon Seller Central settlement reports, supplier invoices, VAT declarations, and customs declarations where chargeable, but no statutory proportion is required to be surrendered or converted.

Central bank supervision: Chancellor of the Exchequer (HM Treasury) and Bank of England constitute the monetary-policy anchor for the UK and Gibraltar; Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the prudential and AML/CFT supervisor for banks and payment institutions in Gibraltar; Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) under the Currency Notes Act 1934 through the Currency Notes Fund at a statutory parity of 1:1 with GBP. There is no independent central bank in Gibraltar; FX oversight, financial sanctions, and restrictive-measures enforcement are administered by HM Treasury/OFSI and by the GFSC under the Financial Services (Gibraltar) legislation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility subject to safeguarding-law constraints: FCA-licensed UK electronic money institutions (Airwallex, Currenxie, Wise, and Stripe) can on-board the Bermuda Exempted Company remotely, issue UK sort codes and EU IBANs in the exact legal entity name, and provide both SWIFT and ACH/SEPA/FPS rails that match Amazon marketplace settlement mechanics. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Share-capital injection is cleared by SWIFT MT103 from the remitting shareholder's bank to the receiving Gibraltar bank or FCA-EMI account in the exact legal name of the Bermuda Exempted Company. Field 50K must match the remitting shareholder; Field 59 must match the beneficiary entity account name; Field 70 remittance information should read 'Share capital injection pursuant to Bermuda Exempted Company Constitution; no goods or services supplied.' UK/Gibraltar law does not mandate a numeric purpose code; banks apply narrative-based classification with OFSI sanctions screening, so the file must include a bank-stamped board resolution, share allotment ledger, and source-of-wealth declaration for each UBO before funds are released for trading. GBP transfers settle via CHAPS (same-day, unlimited) or FPS (up to GBP 1 million per payment); USD and EUR are routed through the bank's New York or Frankfurt correspondent network using standard MT103 banking-option protocols.Outbound Amazon-FBA inventory disbursements use SWIFT MT103/MT202 for direct supplier payments across mainland China, UK, and EU warehouse counterparties; high-value recurring purchase orders are supported by irrevocable documentary Letters of Credit (UCP600 sight, confirmed at the beneficiary bank) to mitigate supplier-country clearing and default risk. Amazon receivable collection follows marketplace clearing rails: US Amazon Payments ACH for USD, SEPA Credit Transfer/Instant for EUR, and BACS/FPS for Amazon UK in GBP. All proceeds are swept into one designated entity-verified settlement account to avoid name-mismatch rejections, reserve triggers, and multi-currency fragmentation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory in Gibraltar where annual turnover exceeds GIP 10,000,000 or where aggregate related-party cross-border transactions exceed GIP 1,000,000. Master File is required where the Gibraltar entity is part of an MNE group with annual consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750,000,000; the same group triggers Country-by-Country Reporting. Bermuda applies a parallel threshold of BMD 12,000,000 turnover for economic substance financial information filings.

Economic substance scope: The Bermuda Exempted Company is subject to the Bermuda Economic Substance (International Tax Compliance) Act 2018, and a Gibraltar-resident or Gibraltar-managed entity is concurrently subject to the Gibraltar Economic Substance (EU) Regulations 2019. Amazon FBA general retail constitutes a 'distribution and service center' relevant activity. The entity must demonstrate in the claimed jurisdiction: (i) physical premises; (ii) full-time employees proportionate to activity; (iii) annual operating expenditure; and (iv) performance of core income-generating activities, including procurement decisions, inventory risk management, pricing strategy, and logistics oversight. Annual economic substance returns and audited financial information must be filed in both Bermuda and Gibraltar where dual presence is asserted. A letterbox company with remote directors and no local decision-making fails both regimes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Assessments raised on the full worldwide profits at the 12.5% Gibraltar rate for all open years; tax-geared penalties up to 100% of the tax undercharged; fixed fines up to GIP 300 plus GIP 60 daily default penalties; criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion of income tax punishable by fines at the discretion of the court and imprisonment of up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participated in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax and penalties. Courts pierce the corporate veil where the exempted company is used as a mere facade; directors face confiscation orders, travel bans pending criminal proceedings, disqualification as directors, and custodial sentences under the Gibraltar Crimes Act and Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.
De Facto Gibraltar Tax Residence (Sham Offshore Structure)

Statutory sanction: Automatic recharacterization of the company as Gibraltar-resident with retroactive application under Income Tax Act 2010; full reassessment at 12.5% on worldwide e-commerce profits; late payment interest; penalties up to 100% for failure to notify chargeability; potential strike-off for continued non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors are personally assessed for penalties where the offshore structure is a sham. Gibraltar courts may issue freezing injunctions over personal assets, impose travel restrictions, and enter personal bankruptcy orders where the director knowingly allowed the company to trade while insolvent and tax-unpaid.
Permanent Establishment via Amazon Fulfillment Centers

Statutory sanction: Local tax authorities attribute profits to the PE and assess corporate income tax at the local statutory rates, with interest and penalties that commonly exceed 100% of the additional tax. VAT liabilities for unreported distance sales and OSS/IOSS registration failures may be assessed separately with surcharges up to 15% of the VAT due in some EU states.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Bermuda exempted company are treated as managing the PE and may be personally liable for withholding and VAT debts in civil-law jurisdictions where the company lacks assets; travel bans under European Enforcement Order procedures are possible, and directors may face criminal charges for deliberate non-registration.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Beneficial ownership filing failure attracts fines up to GIP 100,000 and daily continuing penalties. AML breaches under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 attract fines up to GIP 500,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering where tax evasion is the underlying predicate offense. Payment processors may terminate merchant accounts and report suspicious activity to the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, and the appointed Money Laundering Reporting Officer face criminal liability for recklessly permitting money flows from tax evasion. Personal assets are subject to civil recovery, travel bans may be imposed pending investigation, and directors face imprisonment where the exempted company structure is used as a laundering vehicle.
Economic Substance Failure and Penalty Regime

Statutory sanction: Bermuda imposes a civil penalty of BMD 250,000 for the first year of non-compliance and BMD 500,000 for subsequent years, plus daily fines of BMD 5,000 for continued failure after remedial notification; the Registrar of Companies may strike off the exempted company. The Gibraltar EU Economic Substance Regulations impose parallel financial penalties of up to GIP 500,000 and powers to exchange information with UK HMRC and EU tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for economic substance penalties where they signed false declarations; Bermuda law permits enforcement against directors for failure to file accurate returns, and Gibraltar courts may impose director disqualification orders, restrict re-entry to Gibraltar, and order personal restitution of revenues attributable to the deficient substance.
