VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

UK: GBP 90,000 annual taxable turnover threshold for mandatory VAT registration from 1 April 2022 (VATA 1994 Schedule 1). Gibraltar: nil threshold; no indirect tax registration regime in Gibraltar.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under VATA 1994 Schedule 8 Group 7 (export of goods) and Group 15 (transport of goods to or from a place outside the UK, including international air cargo and ancillary freight forwarding services). The supplier must obtain and retain valid commercial and customs evidence proving the goods or services were supplied to an overseas recipient and physically removed from the UK within statutory time limits. In respect of air cargo charter brokerage, B2B supply to an overseas principal is generally outside the scope of UK VAT where the place of supply is outside the UK, subject to holding the customer's VAT registration number and supporting contracts.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document C88) lodged via the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) or CHIEF
  • Air Waybill (AWB) evidencing international carriage
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT MT103 credit evidence confirming receipt of export consideration

UK: no real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no mandatory government-authorised e-invoicing API. Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT requires API-based software-to-HMRC digital submission of VAT returns and digital preservation of records (VAT Notice 700/63). Invoices must contain the statutory particulars of VATA 1994 Schedule 42 and SI 2011/2433. Gibraltar: no e-invoicing or electronic clearance mandate.

HMRC may issue a C18 post-clearance demand or VAT assessment to claw back zero-rated VAT together with interest (FA 2009 s.101) where export evidence is absent or defective. Inaccuracy penalties under FA 2007 Schedule 24 range from 30% to 100% of potential lost revenue, with up to 100% for deliberate concealed errors. Fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute criminal evasion under VATA 1994 s.72, punishable by imprisonment of up to 7 years and an unlimited fine. Gibraltar: the Gibraltar Income Tax Office may reopen assessments and apply anti-avoidance clawback under the Income Tax Act 2010 for transactions artificially deflecting income where foreign-source income is diverted to a tax-exempt entity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is fully backed by and pegged at par to Bank of England sterling; both Gibraltar and the Bermuda Exempted Company's designated non-resident status provide unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no FX licensing requirements for ordinary air-cargo charter invoicing, fee remittances or profit repatriation. Practical hurdles are not exchange-control but documentary: the receiving/remitting bank must evidence underlying commercial substance, source-of-funds/wealth, beneficial-ownership transparency, and the entity's valid tax-exempt status. Dividend/profit remittance from Gibraltar to the Bermuda parent may proceed without exchange-control approval but requires a distributable-reserves certificate, board minutes, and a provisional Gibraltar tax-compliance statement if the company is within the Gibraltar income-tax regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Neither Gibraltar nor the Bermuda Exempted Company's exchange-control designation imposes statutory surrender, mandatory conversion percentages, or holding-period requirements for export receipts. The air-cargo charter broker may therefore retain 100% of foreign-currency proceeds in offshore accounts in EUR/USD/GBP. However, all proceeds must be supported by executed charter agreements, airway bills, and supplier invoices; must be screened for sanctions and anti-money-laundering; and must be reflected in the company's books, economic-substance record, and annual tax returns under the applicable regime.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the financial-services and AML/CFT regulator; Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) as the exchange-control authority for the Bermuda Exempted Company; Bank of England for sterling finality through the UK RTGS.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium, secondary rail only. Legal constraints: GFSC-licensed electronic-money institutions (EMIs) can issue payment accounts and execute payments, but post-Brexit EU/UK passporting no longer applies. An offshore digital EMI with an EEA/UK licence cannot passport into Gibraltar and cannot hold itself out as Gibraltar-regulated; it may only service the company from abroad as a payment initiation or collection agent. For high-value aircraft charter settlements, EMIs are viable only as secondary rails for sub-US$250,000 operating expenses, staff payroll and initial deposits, because their correspondent coverage and segregated client-money frameworks are narrower than licensed bank wires; all final clearing must land in a GFSC-supervised bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All capital injections, whether equity or shareholder loan, must enter Gibraltar through a GFSC-licensed bank account via SWIFT MT103 using the bank's standard inbound payment instructions. The payment header must state the company legal name, unique client identifier and purpose reference 'Share Capital Contribution' or 'Shareholder Loan'. Purpose codes for the BOE/GFSC reporting matrix normally map to direct investment in Gibraltar, such as UK BOP code S010 or the receiving bank's equivalent. The remittance must be made directly by the account-holder shareholder; any third-party payment requires an intercompany settlement agreement and a full due-diligence file. Funds should be in the operating currency (GBP/EUR/USD) and cannot be split to avoid reporting. The bank will perform sanctions screening and source-of-funds verification, and may request an auditor or legal source-of-funds letter for amounts above €100,000.Use SWIFT MT103 for standard payments covering charter hire, fuel, handling fees and commissions. EUR payments settle via TARGET2, GBP via CHAPS/RTGS and USD via Fedwire/CHIPS. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% standard Gibraltar corporate income tax rate on chargeable profits; 0% Bermuda tax-exempt certificate rate for the Bermuda Exempted Company, provided the entity is not resident in Bermuda for corporate income tax purposes.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar does not impose withholding tax on dividend distributions, whether paid to Gibraltar residents or to non-resident shareholders. Bermuda similarly imposes no withholding tax on dividends. The Bermuda Exempted Company can repatriate accumulated profits by dividend without Gibraltar or Bermuda statutory dividend tax, but the beneficial owner should consider any residence-country tax on worldwide dividends.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Company Income Tax Return must be filed not later than 12 months after the end of the accounting period. The final payment of tax is due on the 1st day of the 10th month after the end of the accounting period. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 (Gibraltar pounds) of annual controlled transactions. A Bermuda Exempted Company carrying out air cargo charter brokerage with any related party, including the ultimate beneficial owner, a Bermuda holding company, a UK-linked charter broker, or a connected aircraft lessor, must prepare a Local File if the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds GIP 1,000,000 in an accounting period. Master File documentation is required where the group has worldwide turnover above GIP 100,000,000 and the Gibraltar entity engages in intercompany financing, intangibles, or agency arrangements.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies the Economic Substance Regulations implemented in accordance with the EU Code of Conduct (OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting, Action 5). A Bermuda Exempted Company must demonstrate an adequate degree of substance in Gibraltar when it is tax resident in Gibraltar or when it performs relevant activities, including air cargo charter brokering and any holding of aviation assets. For ISIC 5229 air cargo charter brokering, the core income-generating activity includes negotiating charter party agreements, accepting cargo reservations, coordinating flight landing permits, billing the customer, and managing crew scheduling. To satisfy economic substance, the company must maintain in Gibraltar: (a) a physical office or premises suitable for a charter brokerage; (b) a minimum number of full-time staff with aviation qualification or operations experience; (c) board meetings and registered office functions in Gibraltar; (d) operating expenditure in Gibraltar proportionate to the level of profits; and (e) all central decision-making for cargo contracts and risk management. A company that relies on the Bermuda tax exemption certificate without performing any substance in either Bermuda or Gibraltar will be considered a letterbox artificial arrangement and will be denied the benefit of any Gibraltar tax relief, excluded from the register of exempted companies, and reported to the international tax transparency authorities. The Gibraltar Commissioner may reattribute profits to a deemed Gibraltar permanent establishment of the Bermuda company and impose a 12.5% tax on the reattributed profits together with late payment interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, tax evasion penalties reach up to 200% of the tax evaded, plus interest at 8% per annum. Criminal conviction on indictment attracts an unlimited fine and imprisonment for up to seven years. Summary conviction carries a fine of GIP 10,000 and imprisonment for up to one year. The offender is also liable for the full unpaid tax plus costs.

Director personal liability: Directors may be subject to disqualification orders under the Companies Act 2014 if the evasion is attributable to their conduct. Civil proceedings can pierce the corporate veil where the Bermuda Exempted Company is a mere facade, ordering the directors to pay restitution of the evaded tax personally. Travel bans and asset-freezing orders can be issued under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, and imprisonment up to seven years may be imposed. Courts have jurisdiction to require a director to repatriate the profits from foreign bank accounts into Gibraltar HM Treasury.
Shell Company / Artificial Substance Avoidance

Statutory sanction: The Economic Substance Code (Gibraltar Taxation (Economic Substance) Regulations) imposes an administrative penalty of GIP 20,000 on a company failing the substance test, plus a further penalty not exceeding GIP 10,000 per day of continued failure. The Commissioner may also impose a penalty equal to 10% of the annual turnover attributable to the shell activity, capped at GIP 500,000. A 'letterbox' company is denied tax residency certificates, is struck off the Gibraltar register if it has a Gibraltar registration, and information will be exchanged with the UK and EU corporate tax authority.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who knowingly facilitate the artificial arrangement are jointly and severally liable for the administrative penalties. Personal liability extends to restitution of the economic benefit received by way of salary or dividend where the company is treated as acting as the director's professional agent. Travel restrictions can be imposed pending settlement of the penalty, and a failure to pay leads to compulsory examination of the director's personal affairs and potential imprisonment for contempt of the Gibraltar Supreme Court.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The Gibraltar Commissioner may make transfer pricing adjustments under the Taxation (Transfer Pricing) Rules, increasing taxable profit to the arm's length figure. A penalty of 40% of the additional tax is imposed on significant mispricing that is not notified to the Commissioner before submission of the return; 100% penalty applies where the mispricing is deliberately concealed. Interest on the adjusted tax runs from the due date of the return. Where the TP adjustment results in a UK tax mismatch, the UK-Gibraltar transparency exchange may trigger a secondary tax adjustment in the UK.

Director personal liability: The finance director and the director who signed the tax return owe a duty of care to the company to maintain proper TP records. Personal liability arises where the company becomes insolvent due to the TP penalty, as the Director Duties (Insolvency) (Gibraltar) Act gives creditors a direct claim against directors for reckless trading. Directors may also be disqualified for a period of up to 15 years. Travel bans may be issued where the TP penalty is unpaid and the director is about to leave Gibraltar, and imprisonment up to two years can follow for failing to comply with a notice to produce TP documentation.
AML / Sanctions / Counter-Financing of Terrorism

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2015 and the Sanctions Act 2019, penalties can reach GIP 5 million for corporate entities and GIP 2 million for individuals, plus up to ten years' imprisonment. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission will revoke the payment-provision licence or the air cargo broker operating licence under the Medium Risk Enhanced AML classification. All SWIFT/Fedwire transfers may be frozen under an asset-freezing order; the beneficial owner's assets in Gibraltar and overseas correspondent accounts are subject to confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and the MLRO personally bear criminal liability for failure to report suspicious transactions. Civil liability arises through breach of the Proceeds of Crime Act for recovery of sums representing the proceeds of unlawful conduct. Directors may be placed on the UN/EU sanctions travel-ban list, preventing them from transiting through international hubs. Extradition on money-laundering and sanctions-evasion charges can be sought by UK, EU, or US authorities. Imprisonment for up to ten years is a real and enforced outcome, and no corporate indemnity can shield directors from such personal liability.
