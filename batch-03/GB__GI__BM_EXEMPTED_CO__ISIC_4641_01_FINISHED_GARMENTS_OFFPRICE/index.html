Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Government of Gibraltar (Gibraltar Tax Directorate, Income Tax Office), web portal: https://www.gibraltartax.gov.gi

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company wishing to establish a fiscal presence in Gibraltar must apply for registration under the Income Tax Act 2010 and the General Consumption Tax Act 2019. Submit Form CPT1 (Corporation Tax Registration) and Form GCT1 (General Consumption Tax Registration) through the Gibraltar Tax E-Submission Portal or in person to the Income Tax Office, 32 Europa Point Retail Park, Europa Point, Gibraltar. The application must be accompanied by a certified copy of the Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, official Certificate of Exempted Status, constitutional documents (Memorandum and Articles of Association), evidence of registered office in Bermuda, confirmation of beneficial ownership filings, and the appointment of a Gibraltar-resident tax representative. Upon approval, the Registrar issues a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) in the format of a seven-digit numeric code with a 'G' suffix designating non-resident status. Registration is mandatory before any taxable supply or importation into Gibraltar is made.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar domestic law does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. However, hybrid mismatch adjustments are available under the Income Tax (Hybrid and Other Mismatches) Regulations 2021, enabling an entity to be treated differently for dual-resident or hybrid payer/payee classification to prevent double non-taxation. Additionally, a non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company may elect to become Gibraltar-tax-resident by exercising central management and control in Gibraltar, thereby entering the Gibraltar worldwide income tax net and gaining access to the limited UK-Gibraltar double-taxation arrangement.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£85,000 (annual taxable supplies of goods and services in Gibraltar, measured on a rolling 12-month basis; compulsory registration above this threshold)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for the physical export of finished garments from Gibraltar to any destination outside Gibraltar, provided the GCT-registered supplier obtains conclusive documentary proof of removal from the territory within three months of the time of supply. The goods must be shipped under customs control, with a valid Customs Export Declaration and a sea or air waybill evidencing delivery to a foreign destination. Services that are directly connected to export goods and performed for a non-resident customer outside Gibraltar, such as freight, packing, or inspection services, are similarly zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate is in force. A GCT-registered person must issue sequentially numbered GCT invoices containing supplier and customer name/address, date of supply, description, quantity, line values, and GCT amount. Invoices may be transmitted electronically; the Gibraltar Tax Directorate recommends the use of the official GCT100 return portal for digital submission of returns and periodic payment. There is no API-based clearance or mandatory third-party invoicing network in Gibraltar.

Fraudulent or improperly documented zero-export claims trigger prosecution under the General Consumption Tax Act 2019 and the Income Tax Act 2010. Civil penalties include a geup to 200% misdeclaration penalty, interest from the date of removal, and indefinite retrospective clawback of any zero-rated output tax where export documentation is false, proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, or goods are subsequently re-imported to Gibraltar without payment of GCT. Criminal conviction for tax evasion can result in fines of up to £200,000 and imprisonment of up to seven years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility in the UK and Gibraltar; no exchange-control legislation, no prior-approval requirement for cross-border capital inflows or outflows, and no restriction on profit remittance or dividend upstreaming. The Gibraltar pound is freely convertible at par into GBP; multi-currency accounts (GBP, USD, EUR) are standard. Practical remittance hurdles are limited to AML/KYC documentation, sanctions screening, corporate-law solvency tests, and Gibraltar Economic Substance reporting, rather than to FX rationing or conversion caps.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable: the UK and Gibraltar impose no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement. Export receipts need not be repatriated within any statutory timeline, and the mandatory conversion ratio into local currency is 0%. A trader may lawfully retain foreign-currency proceeds offshore indefinitely; only statistical UK Balance-of-Payments reporting obligations may apply to the banking relationship.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (UK monetary authority; sterling anchor and ultimate clearing settlement) jointly with HM Treasury (sanctions and financial-crime policy); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) (prudential supervision of Gibraltar-licensed banks); Government of Gibraltar (issuer of the Gibraltar pound, GIP, pegged at 1:1 to GBP).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for operational disbursements and collections; LOW for capital escrow and trade-finance instruments. Gibraltar does not operate a native EMI licence class; foreign EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Currenxie, MultiPass) generally onboard the UK-connected structure or a Gibraltar entity under the EMIs own risk tolerance, providing multi-currency IBANs, segregated safeguarding accounts, and access to SWIFT, SEPA, ACH, and Fedwire rails. Legal constraints: EMIs are not licensed banks, cannot issue commercial letters of credit or bank guarantees, and their safeguarding arrangements do not constitute deposit insurance from a deposit-taker; consequently, treasury policy should limit EMI balances to operational float below 10% of monthly settlement flow and never use a Fintech EMI as the sole liquidity-clearing counterparty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Recommended protocol: direct SWIFT MT103 / MT202 from the ultimate beneficial owner's or holding company's primary bank account into the Bermuda exempted company's segregated capital escrow account at the Gibraltar bank, referenced as Equity injection with a certified board resolution attached and, where UK reporting applies, coded as capital-equity inflow under the UK Balance-of-Payments classification. The receiving bank requires: (i) certified extract of the board resolution authorising the injection and designating the account; (ii) updated register of members showing the subscribing shareholder; (iii) source-of-funds declaration plus 6 to 12 months of remitter bank statements; (iv) signed shareholder loan facility letter if the injection is debt rather than equity, including interest and repayment mechanics; and (v) negative confirmation that funds are not derived from sanctions-restricted, cash-generative, or crypto-converted sources. GBP clearing may be routed via CHAPS or Faster Payments after conversion; USD clearing should run through the bank's New York correspondent (Fedwire) via its Nostro account. Under no circumstances route third-party unrelated company payments, split tranches, or cryptocurrency-converted funds, because such patterns immediately trigger enhanced due diligence.Trade settlements are executed over five rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 / MT202 for high-value supplier wires, with full invoice, transport, and customs documentation attached to the payment instruction; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit governed by UCP600, preferably irrevocable and confirmed through a UK or New York bank, for large or first-time off-price purchases; (iii) US ACH and UK Faster Payments for low-value, high-frequency logistics, inspection, and warehousing disbursements; (iv) SEPA Credit Transfers for EUR-denominated settlements with EU sellers and customs duty payments; and (v) Telegraphic Transfer (T/T) cash-in-advance stages of 30% deposit and 70% against documents for established suppliers. Each disbursement must be pre-screened against the bank's sanctions firewall and carry an economic-substance reference to the underlying apparel purchase, comprising the commercial contract, commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, customs entry, and inspection certificate. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Gibraltar headline corporation tax rate: 12.5% (Income Tax Act 2010) on worldwide profits of a company that is tax-resident in Gibraltar under the management-and-control test. A Bermuda Exempted Company with no management-and-control in Gibraltar is non-resident and is chargeable only on Gibraltar-source income (effectively nil for an off-shore wholesale trading office without a Gibraltar permanent establishment). Bermuda itself levies 0% corporate income tax, 0% capital gains tax and 0% advance corporation tax on the Exempted Company.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% in both jurisdictions. Gibraltar does not impose any withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar-resident company (Income Tax Act 2010), and Bermuda does not impose withholding tax, advance corporation tax, or any dividend tax on a Bermuda Exempted Company. A tax-exempt dividend stream may therefore flow from Gibraltar trading profits to the Bermuda Exempted Company with no statutory WHT and no gross-up.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The return must be filed within 12 months after the end of the chargeable period (for a 31 December 2025 year-end, the due date is 31 December 2026). The final tax payment is due on the same filing date, subject to quarterly instalments where the annual liability exceeds GBP 100,000; interest runs from the due payment date at 8% per annum compounding quarterly.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar: mandatory Local File and Master File must be prepared and retained where the Gibraltar entity is part of a multinational group that (i) employs 250 or more persons on average and (ii) has an annual turnover exceeding GBP 50,000,000 or a balance sheet total exceeding GBP 43,000,000. The CbC report threshold is consolidated group revenue exceeding EUR 750,000,000. Below the mandatory threshold, a slimmed benchmark is still required where controlled transactions exceed GBP 1,000,000 in annual value. Bermuda: no transfer-pricing documentation regime exists for income tax purposes, but the Bermuda Economic Substance Regulations require the Exempted Company to maintain contemporaneous records evidencing that core income-generating activities are performed in the jurisdiction declared.

Economic substance scope: Dual Economic Substance Regulations apply. Bermuda ESR 2018 categorise 'distribution and service centres' as a relevant activity, so the Bermuda Exempted Company must demonstrate (i) physical presence (office), (ii) full-time qualified employees, (iii) performance of core income-generating activities in Bermuda (risk assessment, stock-management decisions, takeover of liquidation lots), and (iv) operating expenditure ratable to the profits. Gibraltar ESR (Income Tax (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019) require a Gibraltar-resident company to be managed and directed in Gibraltar with adequate employees, premises and CIGA performance. For this structure, the two regimes must be coherent: the company cannot declare Bermuda substance for the Registrar while simultaneously claiming Gibraltar tax residence for the Income Tax Office. The practical compliant model is a Bermuda Exempted Company that is managed in Bermuda and operates through a Gibraltar branch or agent with genuinely performed commercial functions; an alternative is full Gibraltar residence with a Bermuda administrative agent, supported by a clear transfer-pricing allocation of profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Suppression of Receipts

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, fraudulent or negligent returns attract tax-geared penalties of up to 200% of the tax evaded plus daily interest at 8%; criminal prosecution on indictment may result in imprisonment for up to 5 years and an unlimited fine. The Commissioner may also raise assessments for up to 20 years on the discovered omissions.

Director personal liability: Civil restitution orders may be made against each director who signed the false declaration; the Gibraltar court may issue a departure ban (stop-list) preventing travel, impose director disqualification for up to 15 years, and set aside transactions at undervalue between the company and the directors. In egregious cases, extradition to the UK is available under the UK-Gibraltar surrender arrangements.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda ESR: administrative fine of BMD 10,000 plus BMD 10,000 per day of continued default; for a false declaration of economic substance, a fine of up to BMD 250,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 5 years. Gibraltar ESR: penalty up to GBP 100,000, daily default penalties, automatic exchange of information with HMRC, and reclassification of the company as resident in Gibraltar with backdated taxation at 12.5%.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines and imprisonment for signing a false ESR declaration; the corporate veil is pierced where the company is found to be a sham, leaving the directors and beneficial owners personally liable for the unpaid Gibraltar corporation tax plus interest; travel restrictions and UK arrest warrants may follow where the offending conduct touches UK counterparties.
AML / Structuring in the Off-Price Trade

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar's money-laundering legislation (Proceeds of Crime Act and Criminal Justice (Money Laundering) Offences Act) criminalises conversion, concealment and transfer of criminal property with a maximum penalty of 14 years' imprisonment and an unlimited fine; the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit may impose civil monetary penalties of up to EUR 1,000,000 on the reporting entity and confiscation orders against the proceeds held in the EMI/bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or negligently fail to prevent structured deposits are personally indictable; the court may order confiscation of personal assets, impose a travel ban and passport surrender, and, where the group operates from the UK, the director can be subject to a UK Serious Crime Prevention Order or extradition to the UK.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation of Liquidation Margins

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar transfer-pricing rules permit the Income Tax Commissioner to make a primary adjustment to the arm's-length price, impose secondary adjustments treating the excess as a deemed distribution, add interest at 8% compounded, and levy penalties of up to 100% of the understated tax; CbC and spontaneous exchange will notify HMRC and the Bermuda Registrar of the profit mismatch.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the transfer-pricing documentation certificate without an adequate functional-and-risk analysis expose themselves to personal penalties of up to GBP 50,000, disqualification as directors, and joint and several liability for the company's unpaid tax where the TP documentation is found to be deliberately false; the Bermuda directors face separate ESR penalties for the substance mismatch.
Wrongful Trading and Insufficient Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 permits the court to declare that directors personally contribute such sums as the court deems proper for wrongful trading; the Income Tax Commissioner may petition for a compulsory winding-up, and the unpaid tax accrues interest and penalties; fraudulent trading is a criminal offence carrying imprisonment and an unlimited fine.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable to creditors including the Revenue for the increase in the net deficiency caused by continued trading; the court may order civil restoration, personal bankruptcy, a departure prohibition order, and in fraudulent-trading cases imprisonment. Deficiencies may be pursued against the Bermuda parent where intercompany accounts show reckless dividend distribution to the exempt shareholder.
