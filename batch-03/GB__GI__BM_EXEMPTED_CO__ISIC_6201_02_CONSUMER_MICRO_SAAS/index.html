Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, H.M. Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax), e-filing through the Gibraltar Tax e-Services portal accessible via www.gibraltar.gov.gi; indirect tax on physical imports is administered by HM Customs Gibraltar (Asycuda World single window). HMRC of the United Kingdom is not the competent authority for Gibraltar, as Gibraltar is a self-governing British Overseas Territory with a distinct tax administration.

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company establishing a Gibraltar presence must: (1) apply for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) by filing the standard corporate registration questionnaire with the Income Tax Office, accompanied by certified copies of the Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, latest Certificate of Good Standing, and the Bermuda Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 Certificate of Exemption; (2) if a physical branch is opened, register as an 'overseas company' with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 2014; (3) submit a board resolution evidencing the intended place of management and control, as this determines Gibraltar tax residence; (4) where staff are engaged, obtain an employer/Gibraltar Reference Number (GRN) with the Income Tax Office for Payroll (PAYE) compliance; and (5) when opening a Gibraltar bank account, present the TRN, registered-agent details, and the entity's structural diagram. No VAT registration ID is available because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST. Processing is ordinarily 5–10 business days; a Gibraltar tax representative is recommended.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Bermuda Exempted Company benefits from a statutory exemption under Bermuda law (no Bermuda income or capital tax until at least 2035). However, in Gibraltar, the operative rule is residence-based: a company incorporated in Gibraltar is resident there, while a foreign-incorporated company (including a Bermudian exempted company) becomes Gibraltar tax resident only if its management and control is exercised in Gibraltar. Once resident, it is subject to Gibraltar corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide profits, with credit for foreign taxes; if not managed and controlled in Gibraltar, it is non-resident and taxed only on Gibraltar-source income. The default pipeline posture is Tax_Exempt_Zone via Bermuda, but this status is fully preserved only if board meetings, strategic decisions, and operational control are exercised outside Gibraltar; otherwise, entity-level taxation in Gibraltar at 12.5% applies. — A Bermuda Exempted Company establishing a Gibraltar presence must: (1) apply for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) by filing the standard corporate registration questionnaire with the Income Tax Office, accompanied by certified copies of the Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, latest Certificate of Good Standing, and the Bermuda Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 Certificate of Exemption; (2) if a physical branch is opened, register as an 'overseas company' with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 2014; (3) submit a board resolution evidencing the intended place of management and control, as this determines Gibraltar tax residence; (4) where staff are engaged, obtain an employer/Gibraltar Reference Number (GRN) with the Income Tax Office for Payroll (PAYE) compliance; and (5) when opening a Gibraltar bank account, present the TRN, registered-agent details, and the entity's structural diagram. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Bermuda Exempted Company benefits from a statutory exemption under Bermuda law (no Bermuda income or capital tax until at least 2035). However, in Gibraltar, the operative rule is residence-based: a company incorporated in Gibraltar is resident there, while a foreign-incorporated company (including a Bermudian exempted company) becomes Gibraltar tax resident only if its management and control is exercised in Gibraltar. Once resident, it is subject to Gibraltar corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide profits, with credit for foreign taxes; if not managed and controlled in Gibraltar, it is non-resident and taxed only on Gibraltar-source income. The default pipeline posture is Tax_Exempt_Zone via Bermuda, but this status is fully preserved only if board meetings, strategic decisions, and operational control are exercised outside Gibraltar; otherwise, entity-level taxation in Gibraltar at 12.5% applies.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no US-style 'check-the-box' election and no domestic hybrid-mismatch election. Available statutory elections under the Income Tax Act 2010 include: (a) group loss-relief and consortium surrender elections for Gibraltar-resident group members; (b) an election for qualifying trading companies to compute assessable profits on a deemed gross-margin basis where intra-group services are supplied at arm's length; (c) tonnage-tax election for qualifying shipping companies; and (d) gross-fee income assessment elections for regulated financial-services providers. Additionally, a non-resident company may evidence its status by formal board minute and organisational documentation confirming that management and control is exercised outside Gibraltar, which functions as the practical analogue to a 'check-the-box' foreign-entity election in tax-planning structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST in Gibraltar. The only Gibraltar indirect tax is Import Duty levied under the Customs Ordinance on physical goods imported into Gibraltar at rates ranging from 0% to 130% by tariff heading, with most non-EU-origin consumer goods attracting 20% and EU/UK-origin goods generally at nil or preferential rates. Digital services, SaaS subscriptions, and intangible supplies are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax. The UK's 20% VAT does not apply in Gibraltar and Gibraltar is not part of the UK VAT territory.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT/GST (no such regime exists). A zero-threshold customs trader registration applies under the Customs Ordinance for any party importing goods into Gibraltar. For B2C digital supplies of SaaS to consumers, there is no Gibraltar indirect-tax registration or threshold; the merchant-of-record payment rails (Paddle and Lemon Squeezy) are the legal suppliers to end consumers and are responsible for collecting and remitting VAT/GST/digital services tax in the consumer's jurisdiction, including UK VAT and EU VAT via their own registrations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — Gibraltar has no VAT and therefore no zero-rating or refund mechanism for exports. SaaS and digital services exported from Gibraltar are 'outside the scope' of Gibraltar indirect tax rather than zero-rated. For physical goods, an outward export declaration suspends import duty and removes goods from the Gibraltar charge. Analytically, the platform-supplied B2B supply chain (Gibraltar entity selling to Paddle/Lemon Squeezy as merchant of record) falls under destination-country B2B place-of-supply rules, generally outside the Gibraltar entity's VAT return; any UK/EU VAT on consumer purchases is collected by the merchant of record, and the Gibraltar entity has no VAT refund entitlement to determine.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Single Administrative Document (physical goods only)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or postal export evidence (physical goods only)
  • Commercial invoice and executed SaaS subscription agreement / terms of service
  • Merchant-of-record settlement statements from Paddle and Lemon Squeezy evidencing gross sales, platform fees, payment processing charges, and indirect taxes collected
  • Inward remittance records and bank statements demonstrating FX repatriation of net SaaS proceeds to the Gibraltar settlement account
  • Bermuda Certificate of Good Standing and Exemption Certificate, where applicable

Gibraltar imposes no real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or Continuous Transaction Control (CTC) mandate. There is no PEPPOL requirement and no government-authorised invoice repository for SaaS supplies. Commercial invoices issued via the merchant of record or by the Gibraltar entity are sufficient for record-keeping. The only electronic filing obligation with fiscal effect is the Asycuda World electronic customs declaration for physical import/export of goods.

Because Gibraltar has no VAT, no fraudulent export zero-rating claim can be made; the substantive audit exposure is corporate tax residence. If the Income Tax Office determines that the Bermuda Exempted Company is managed and controlled in Gibraltar, it will retrospectively assess worldwide profits at 12.5% plus interest at 10% per annum and civil penalties up to 100% of the tax undercharged (up to 50% for negligence), with additional penalties for failure to file, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion (unlimited fines and custodial sentences available on indictment in the Gibraltar Supreme Court). In parallel, the Bermuda Registrar of Companies may revoke exempted status for violation of the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 conditions, retroactively lifting the Bermuda tax shield and exposing the entity to Bermuda payroll and land tax. The payment facilitators' merchant-of-record reports and settlement records are the primary evidence trail scrutinised in any residence audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar has no standalone exchange control regime. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is freely convertible at par to GBP, and both current-account flows (subscription fees, software royalties, intercompany service charges) and capital-account flows (intercompany loans, equity injections, dividend remittances) are unrestricted in law. Profit repatriation to the Bermuda exempted company requires only standard Gibraltarian Companies Act solvency declarations, board resolutions, and tax clearance evidence where distribution is linked to retained profits; no central bank approval or currency conversion permit is required. Practical frictions are limited to commercial correspondent-bank sanctions, AML controls, and proof-of-economic-substance expectations, not regulatory convertibility limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion regime applies. Gibraltar imposes zero obligatory conversion ratios, no statutory repatriation timeline, and no surrender of export receipts. A consumer micro-SaaS business may retain proceeds in USD, EUR, GBP/GIP, or any freely tradable currency. The only relevant requirement is bank-level documentation that export receipts arise from genuine commercial activity, evidenced by SaaS subscription contracts, invoice schedules, and payment-aggregator settlement reports.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for the sterling peg) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the in-territory banking and conduct regulator; no separate Gibraltar central bank exists, and exchange control administration is subsumed within HM Treasury's pari passu application to Gibraltar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: High for collection aggregation and low-value treasury; Medium as a primary banking layer. The target payment rails (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy) are principal collection intermediaries, making a fintech EMI suitable for temporary holding and quick FX conversion. Legal constraints: offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business, and similar are not Gibraltar-licensed banks under the Banking Act; they typically operate under UK or EEA e-money licences with permissive passported access to Gibraltar. In practice, a Bermuda exempted company can onboard to these EMIs remotely with strong UBO documentation, but many EMIs will flag the Bermuda domicile as an offshore tax-haven risk and impose lower thresholds or closure risk. Therefore the blueprint directs fintech EMIs to serve as inbound collection and settlement accelerators, while the primary treasury, capital escrow, and outbound disbursement-grade liquidity must remain in GFSC-regulated banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from the Bermuda exempted company to the Gibraltar operating account should be routed by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 over correspondent banking. The recommended ISO 20022 category purpose code is 'CAPH' for capital injection or intercompany investment; when the injection is structured as a shareholder loan, the bank may require purpose code 'B230' or 'LOAN' with a signed loan agreement. The following compliance packet must accompany the transfer: board resolution from Bermuda shareholders authorising the funds flow; notarised certificate of incumbency; source-of-funds statement referencing the Bermuda audited balance sheet; intercompany loan agreement (if debt) or equity subscription instrument; signed beneficiary attestation. No mandatory Gibraltar exchange-control filing applies, but the receiving bank's currency conversion desk should be pre-notified to avoid automatic inspection on a potentially matching name-screening hit.For outbound vendor, infrastructure, and licensing settlements, the clearing rails are: (a) SWIFT MT103 for global non-SEPA USD/EUR/GBP payments, with settled delivery via Fedwire/CHIPS/CHAPS; (b) SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) for EU/UK-region EUR payments, executed through UK or Gibraltar correspondent banks participating in SEPA; (c) Commercial Irrevocable Documentary Credit (LC) only for high-value supply contracts requiring documentary compliance and trade finance; (d) virtual corporate cards linked to the primary account for recurring low-value SaaS infrastructure and platform fees. Documentary disbursement mandates should include an invoice, purchase order, acceptance of service, and, where relevant, beneficial-ownership declaration of the recipient. Turnover: Independent audit is mandatory if the Bermuda exempted company has a shareholder resident in Bermuda, carries on business through an actual place of business in Bermuda, holds a Bermuda Monetary Authority licence, or solicits public investment.
