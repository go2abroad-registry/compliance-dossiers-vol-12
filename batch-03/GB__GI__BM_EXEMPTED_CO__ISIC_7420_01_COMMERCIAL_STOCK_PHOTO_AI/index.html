Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office for direct taxation; HM Customs Gibraltar for indirect and customs matters; Gibraltar Companies Registry for corporate registration of overseas and external companies.

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company establishing a branch or place of business in Gibraltar must file an overseas company application with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies within one month. The company must then apply to the Income Tax Office on Form 1 (Application for a Tax Reference Number), submitting constitutional documents, proof of registered office or agent in Gibraltar, details of directors and shareholders, and evidence of central management and control location. The Income Tax Office issues a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) and determines resident or non-resident status. Non-resident companies deriving Gibraltar-source assessable income must register; entities with no PE and no Gibraltar-source income are not statutorily required to register, though a voluntary registration obtains a non-residence determination.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Tax_Exempt_Zone: The Bermuda Exempted Company is a separate corporate taxpayer in Bermuda, exempt from Bermuda income and capital gains tax by virtue of exempted status. In Gibraltar it remains an entity-level taxpayer under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010. If centrally managed and controlled from Gibraltar, it is resident and assessable at the 10% corporate rate on worldwide income; otherwise it is non-resident and assessable only on Gibraltar-source income. The entity is not pass-through eligible.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box election. The company may elect to evidence non-residence by keeping central management and control outside Gibraltar. For US federal tax purposes, the entity may file Form 8832 to check-the-box as a disregarded entity or corporation; hybrid mismatch rules under Gibraltar's Income Tax (Companies) Act and OECD BEPS reports limit mismatches. A Bermuda-issued tax assurance certificate under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 can guarantee no Bermuda tax on profit, income, or capital until 2035, but it does not affect Gibraltar taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST in Gibraltar; indirect system consists of Customs Import Duty, Excise Duty, and Consumption Tax on selected goods. Digital stock-photo and AI-generated visual asset supplies are not subject to Gibraltar VAT/GST.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A – Gibraltar does not operate a VAT-registration threshold. Gibraltar has no VAT registration for digital services. However, distance sales of digital services to EU Member State consumers may create OSS/VAT registration obligations outside Gibraltar once annual cross-border turnover exceeds EUR 10,000 in another Member State.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export treatment is not applicable because Gibraltar does not levy VAT. Digital deliveries of commercial stock photographs and AI-generated visual assets are outside the VAT zero-rating framework. For income tax substantiation, the company may claim that digital royalties and license fees are foreign-source if the purchaser is non-Gibraltarian, utilization occurs outside Gibraltar, and the contract is concluded and performed outside Gibraltar. Physical export of photographic media, if any, is not subject to export duty and requires standard customs documentation only where required by destination-country rules.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (SAD/C88) – required only for physical goods, not digital deliveries
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill – for physical photographic media shipments
  • Commercial invoice and digital license agreement or media subscription contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/PSP settlement statements evidencing FX repatriation
  • Digital delivery evidence such as server access logs, download URLs, license keys, and public-key verification records

Gibraltar imposes no e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate. Standard commercial invoices with full taxpayer identification suffices. If the entity triggers VAT registration in an EU destination jurisdiction, that jurisdiction's e-invoicing or clearance rules (e.g., Italian SDI, French Factur-X/PPF, German XRechnung) will apply to those supplies only.

Gibraltar has no VAT fraud or zero-rating clawback because there is no VAT regime. Customs export misdeclarations for physical consignments are penalized under the Customs Ordinance 1992: for each offence up to £5,000, plus three times the value of duty evaded and forfeiture of goods. False claims of non-residence or foreign-source income expose the company to retrospective reassessment for up to six years (twenty years where fraud is established), interest at 8% per annum, and a 35% surcharge on any understated income tax under the Income Tax (Self-Assessment) (Amendment) Regulations. Artificial arrangements designed to shift stock-photo licensing income to the Bermuda exempted company may be counteracted under Gibraltar's General Anti-Abuse Rule with restated taxable profits and corresponding sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar applies a full liberalisation regime for both current and capital account transactions. The Gibraltar pound is fully convertible at parity with GBP; profit remittance, dividend distributions, and capital returns are permitted without exchange-control authorisation or quantitative limits. The only applicable frictions are the standard AML/KYC documentation package, CRS/FATCA reporting, and the completion of a transparent ultimate beneficial ownership structure before a bank permits outbound remittances. For the targeted ISIC 7420 stock-photography and AI-generated visual-assets platform there are no sector-specific convertibility hurdles, no withholding surcharge on licensing revenue, and no restriction on retaining proceeds in GBP, EUR, USD, or other freely convertible currencies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation deadlines under Gibraltar law. All export and marketplace revenues may be retained or freely converted in GIP, GBP, USD, EUR, or any suitable settlement currency; no obligation exists to convert foreign receivables into GIP or to surrender receivables to a central authority.

Central bank supervision: HM Government of Gibraltar / Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) — Gibraltar does not maintain an indigenous central bank; monetary supervision is undertaken by the GFSC while the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is statutory pegged at a 1:1 parity with British pound sterling and therefore operates under the monetary anchor of the Bank of England.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High viability for marketplace collections and disbursement operations, but only as an auxiliary clearing layer. Licensed UK EMIs such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, Payer, and the Stripe Treasury / PayPal treasury rails are suitable for merchant settlement in GBP/EUR/USD and low-value contributor payouts. Legal constraints are: (i) an offshore EMI is not a full-service bank and will not act as a capital escrow provider or long-term treasury depository; (ii) a physical branch or substantive EMI presence in Gibraltar would require a GFSC Electronic Money Licence under the Financial Services Act 2019; and (iii) EMIs may block or curtail high-frequency IP-licence and AI-generated content settlement unless the platform supplies separate sales-provenance data, volume forecasting, and an AML-compliant merchant-of-record structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital and working capital injections into the Gibraltar corporate bank account must be sent via SWIFT MT103 using the remitter's own bank account, with the beneficiary Gibraltar account referenced in field 59, the correspondent bank in field 57C when applicable, and the remitter in field 50K. The stated purpose code should be 'Capital contribution / subscription for shares' and the ISO 20022 remittance information should include the reference GB__GI__BM_EXEMPTED_CO__ISIC_7420_01_COMMERCIAL_STOCK_PHOTO_AI plus the underlying board resolution number. No exchange-control approval is required, but the receiving bank will request: notarised board resolutions, shareholder subscription or loan agreements, audited financial statements where relevant, bank statements of the remitter, CRS/FATCA self-certification, and confirmation that the injection is not derived from third-party uncontrolled funds.
