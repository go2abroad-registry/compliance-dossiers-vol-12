Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax; corporate income-tax registration and TIN issuance) and Gibraltar Customs & Borders (Commissioner of Customs; General Indirect Tax (GIT) registration and administration). Consolidated official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation; GIT filings are submitted through the Gibraltar Customs single-window / iCustoms electronic portal.

E-government filing portal

A Bermuda Exempted Company (Bermuda ExCo) operating from Gibraltar must: (1) appoint a licensed Gibraltar registered agent and maintain a registered office; if the ExCo itself is managed and controlled in Gibraltar it is automatically Gibraltar tax-resident, whereas if a separate Gibraltar subsidiary is used the Bermuda entity must present its Certificate of Incorporation and Bermuda Tax Exemption Certificate to the Gibraltar agent; (2) file a company or foreign-company registration with the Gibraltar Companies House under the Companies Act 2014; (3) submit the corporate registration form to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office to obtain a Gibraltar TIN (company tax reference); (4) submit a GIT registration application to Gibraltar Customs & Borders before the first importation or supply of taxable goods in Gibraltar, with no de minimis waiver for alcohol importers; (5) file the annual Economic Substance return where the ExCo is Gibraltar tax-resident and maintain a local board minute book evidencing management and control; (6) obtain the alcoholic-beverage import/export trade licence from the Gibraltar Licensing Authority before beer trading commences. The GIT registration number and corporate TIN are issued within 5-10 business days of complete filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or elective entity classification exists in Gibraltar. The material elections are: (1) Bermuda Tax Exemption Certificate election under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, guaranteeing the ExCo is exempt from Bermuda and future Bermuda-imposed income taxes with protection running to 31 March 2035; (2) Gibraltar group-loss relief (surrender) elections between resident group companies under the Income Tax Act 2010; (3) capital-allowance pooling and discontinuance elections; (4) GIT election to treat qualifying international services, including cold-chain logistics ancillary to exported goods, as zero-rated where the recipient is outside Gibraltar; (5) hybrid mismatch measures are not elective: Gibraltar applies OECD BEPS Action 2 and ATAD-aligned anti-hybrid rules requiring alignment of Bermuda/Gibraltar tax treatment without elective opt-out.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 85,000 standard annual taxable-turnover threshold for suppliers of services and domestic goods; however, importers of goods, including craft beer, must register for GIT with nil threshold before the first importation into Gibraltar. The threshold is monitored on a rolling 12-month basis and by prospective 30-day testing.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated for GIT where: (i) the beer is physically removed from Gibraltar to a destination outside Gibraltar, including Great Britain and EU member states, within the statutory export shipment window; (ii) title and risk pass to a non-Gibraltar customer; (iii) the exporter retains verified transport documentation covering the cold-chain movement, such as carrier bills, CMR consignment notes, or seaway bills; and (iv) payment is received from the foreign counterparty. Ancillary international transport and cold-chain logistics services supplied to non-Gibraltar customers are also zero-rated. Zero-rating never exempts regulatory alcohol licensing or any Gibraltar excise duty that may apply at importation into Gibraltar.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / Gibraltar electronic export entry; UK CDS export declaration for movements via Great Britain)
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill; where road transport is used, a CMR consignment note with cold-chain temperature-recording annex
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT MT103 or equivalent proof of export proceeds received within 180 days
  • Commercial invoice annotated 'GIT zero-rated export' quoting the GIT registration number and Incoterms
  • Packing list and certificate of origin where preferential tariff treatment is claimed
  • Proof of arrival or delivery confirmation, including POD, warehouse receipt, or destination import customs entry

Gibraltar does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls (CTC), or digital stamping mandate. GIT-compliant invoices may be raised electronically subject to authenticity and integrity controls; all import and export movements of beer are declared electronically through the Gibraltar Customs single-window (SAD-based declaration), and movements to or from Great Britain additionally require entries through the UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS). Fiscal clearance is ex-post rather than real-time, and no separate e-invoicing API obligation is currently in force.

GIT zero-rating is audited by Gibraltar Customs & Borders on a post-clearance basis; where export evidence is deficient, the supply is re-characterised as domestic and GIT is clawed back at 12.5% with statutory interest. Fraudulent or reckless claims attract a civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax avoided, daily default surcharges, and criminal liability, including fines and imprisonment, for dishonest evasion under the General Indirect Tax Act 2018 as amended. Retrospective assessments may be raised for up to four years, or six years in cases of fraud; records evidencing each zero-rated export must be retained for at least six years. The Gibraltar Income Tax Office may additionally adjust deductions where over-claimed GIT affects income-tax computations.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound is fully convertible at parity with sterling and is 100% backed by a sterling reserve fund. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely permitted; there are no restrictions on profit remittances by a Bermuda Exempted Company conducting craft-beer distribution through Gibraltar. The Bermuda parent may repatriate dividends, repay shareholder loans, and settle intercompany fees without exchange-control permission, provided that the transactions are supported by board minutes, transfer-pricing documentation, and arm's-length commercial contracts. The bank will, however, require a source-of-funds explanation and may apply enhanced due diligence because the entity is resident in a tax-exempt offshore jurisdiction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

United Kingdom and Gibraltar law impose no mandatory surrender requirement, no statutory timeline for conversion, and no minimum conversion ratio for export proceeds. All receipts from craft-beer export sales may be retained in GBP, EUR, USD, or any freely convertible currency. As a sound treasury practice, the entity should adopt a 90-day trade-debtor conversion policy, but this is internal policy rather than regulatory mandate. Export proceeds must nevertheless be reported to the GFSC-regulated bank for balance-of-payments and International Transactions Reporting System ('ITRS') statistical purposes via the bank's commercial reporting forms.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no independent central bank. The de facto monetary authority is HM Treasury (United Kingdom) and the Bank of England operational settlement infrastructure, while the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission ('GFSC') is the statutory licensing and supervisory authority for banks, electronic money institutions, and foreign-exchange dealers in Gibraltar. The Gibraltar pound is issued by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934, at a fixed 1:1 peg with the pound sterling. Exchange-control administration is not vested in a separate Gibraltar body because the UK/Gibraltar monetary area operates under a fully liberalised regime consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed from the Bermuda parent or subscribing shareholders into the Gibraltar capital-escrow or operational account using SWIFT MT103 (single payments) or MT202 COV (when settling through a correspondent bank). The remittance instruction must contain, in field 70/77B, the purpose code 'CAPH' (capital contribution) or a clear textual notation 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — SHARE SUBSCRIPTION', together with the company legal name and the board-resolution reference. For balance-of-payments reporting, the receiving bank will classify the inflow under the Gibraltar / Bank of England ITRS equity-capital category; no foreign-exchange permit is required because the UK and Gibraltar maintain full capital-account convertibility. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Gibraltar corporation tax: 15% on chargeable profits up to EUR 500,000 and 18% on chargeable profits exceeding EUR 500,000 (Year of Assessment 2025 onward; prior flat rate 12.5%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Gibraltar does not impose dividend withholding tax on distributions made by Gibraltar-resident companies to shareholders, whether resident or non-resident. No Gibraltar tax is levied at the shareholder level on such distributions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return for a chargeable accounting period must be delivered within 12 months after the end of that period and no later than 30 November of the following Year of Assessment (YA, running 1 January to 31 December). Corporation tax is computed on the preceding-year basis; any balance of tax is due on or before the filing deadline, with instalment arrangements available at the Commissioner's discretion.

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Chargeable trading profits Adjusted trading profit from cold-chain keg wholesale and export of artisanal hazy IPAs, computed under Gibraltar tax principles from generally accepted accounting practice with statutory adjustments. A Gibraltar-resident Bermuda Exempted Company must include worldwide trading profits, subject to unilateral foreign tax credits and any foreign PE exclusion. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File documentation are mandatory where aggregate controlled transactions exceed GBP 1,000,000 in a chargeable period; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000 under the Gibraltar Income Tax (Country-by-Country Reporting) Regulations.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (Income Tax (Substance Requirements) Regulations 2019, as amended) apply to distribution and service centre activities, financing, holding, IP, headquarter, and other geographically mobile activities. The craft-beer distribution company must conduct its core income-generating activities in Gibraltar, specifically order processing, cold-chain stock and inventory management, invoicing and credit control, negotiation with microbrewery suppliers and wholesale customers, and transport or storage management. It must maintain adequate physical premises and staff in Gibraltar, incur adequate operating expenditure locally, and be managed and supervised from Gibraltar. An annual substance self-assessment report must be filed with the Income Tax Office; a pure equity holding company within the Bermuda Exempted structure files a reduced substance declaration. Bermuda exempt status does not displace Gibraltar ESR obligations where management and control and the relevant trading activity are located in Gibraltar.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Additional assessment equal to the tax evaded plus a penalty up to 100% of the understated tax; daily default penalties and interest on unpaid tax; criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act carrying fines up to GBP 100,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years; forfeiture of assets connected with the evasion scheme.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or knowingly participate in the fraud are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax and penalties. Gibraltar courts may order civil restitution, freeze personal assets, impose travel bans pending proceedings, and impose imprisonment; deliberate concealment defeats limited liability and pierces the corporate veil.
Shell Company and ESR Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: ESR administrative penalties: initial fixed penalty up to GBP 10,000 plus daily penalties up to GBP 1,000 for continued failure; escalated supplementary penalty up to GBP 100,000 for sustained non-compliance; exchange of information with UK HMRC; potential strike-off of the company from the Gibraltar Companies Register.

Director personal liability: Senior officers are personally liable for penalties where the ESR default is attributable to their consent, connivance, or neglect, with personal fines up to GBP 50,000; directors face disqualification for up to 15 years, and UK HMRC may recharacterise the arrangement by asserting a UK permanent establishment over the Gibraltar company's distribution profits.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustments by the Gibraltar Income Tax Office with compensating adjustments to chargeable profits; interest on underpaid tax; penalties up to 100% of the adjustment where documentation is absent or materially flawed; escalated penalties for repeat manipulation and deliberate concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal exposure where transfer pricing arrangements are artificial or lack commercial substance and are treated as a sham; the corporate veil may be pierced to recover adjusted tax from directors' personal assets, and disqualification proceedings may follow.
AML and Sanctions Breaches in the Distribution Chain

Statutory sanction: GFSC (Gibraltar Financial Services Commission) monetary penalties up to GBP 400,000 or more; criminal sanctions under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 for money laundering carrying imprisonment up to 14 years; confiscation of the proceeds of the wholesale trade; suspension of banking and EMI relationships and reporting to the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Money Laundering Reporting Officer are personally liable for failure to report suspicious activity, facing criminal imprisonment up to 14 years, civil penalties, confiscation orders against personal assets, travel restrictions, and disqualification; the corporate veil is pierced where the entity is used as an instrument of money laundering.
