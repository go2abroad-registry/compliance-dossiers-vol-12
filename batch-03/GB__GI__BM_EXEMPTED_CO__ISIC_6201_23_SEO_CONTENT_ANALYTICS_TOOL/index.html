Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar, Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), TARS e-filing portal at https://tars.gibraltar.gov.gi; supporting authorities: Gibraltar Registrar of Companies (Companies Act 2014) via GIBFLOW, and HM Customs Gibraltar at https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/customs.

E-government filing portal

(1) Incorporate or register the foreign entity with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies; (2) file Form TRN-1 (Application for Tax Reference Number) with the Income Tax Office and obtain the Gibraltar TIN, ordinarily within 5-7 business days; (3) where the Bermuda Exempted Company has no permanent establishment or Gibraltar-source income, TIN registration is elective rather than mandatory; (4) if physical goods are moved, obtain a Customs Business Code (CBC) from HM Customs Gibraltar; no minimum capital or local shareholding requirement applies.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — (1) Incorporate or register the foreign entity with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies; (2) file Form TRN-1 (Application for Tax Reference Number) with the Income Tax Office and obtain the Gibraltar TIN, ordinarily within 5-7 business days; (3) where the Bermuda Exempted Company has no permanent establishment or Gibraltar-source income, TIN registration is elective rather than mandatory; (4) if physical goods are moved, obtain a Customs Business Code (CBC) from HM Customs Gibraltar; no minimum capital or local shareholding requirement applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

(i) Qualifying Company election under the Income Tax Act 2010 (gross-receipts taxation at 2%-20% bands) instead of the standard 12.5% profit-based assessable-company regime; (ii) group relief and loss-surrender elections; (iii) International Headquarter Companies incentive (up to 50% reduction of assessable income) where substance criteria are met; (iv) documentation and elections under the Income Tax (Anti-Hybrid) Rules 2019; (v) Bermuda-side: exempted company statutory tax-exemption certificate (Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966) provides tax neutrality until 2035, subject to the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2025 which applies only to multinational groups with revenue over EUR 750 million (not met here).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar operates no VAT/GST; indirect taxation consists of import duties (ad valorem up to approximately 25%), excise duties and purchase taxes on specified goods; exports of digital SaaS/SEO analytics services are outside the indirect tax charge.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT/GST registration threshold exists in Gibraltar (GIP 0); importers of physical goods must obtain a Customs Business Code and register with HM Customs Gibraltar; UK/EU distance-selling and VAT OSS thresholds are not applicable because Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT territory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of SEO content-analytics SaaS (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 541511) from the Gibraltar vehicle attracts zero Gibraltar indirect tax because the jurisdiction imposes no VAT/GST; for direct-tax purposes, the income of the Bermuda Exempted Company falls outside the Gibraltar territorial charge unless attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment or to Gibraltar-source subscribers; the 0% position is evidenced by subscriber geographic data, foreign hosting and contract location, and the absence of local management activity.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/AES for physical flows; replaced by service delivery reports for pure SaaS)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / electronic delivery confirmation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / Stripe-Paddle settlement evidence
  • Commercial Invoice and Master SaaS Subscription Agreement
  • IP-licence and source-location records demonstrating non-Gibraltar source

No mandatory e-invoicing, clearance API, real-time fiscal stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) regime applies in Gibraltar; PEPPOL-compliant e-invoicing is voluntarily accepted for UK/Gibraltar cross-border trade; taxpayers must retain all invoices and underlying records for six years under the Income Tax Act 2010.

Gibraltar and UK law impose no statutory FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is a standard banking and AML monitoring convention. Penalties under the Income Tax Act 2010: negligent or fraudulent returns attract civil penalties of up to 150% of the under-assessed tax plus interest, and criminal liability (fines and up to 5 years' imprisonment) for tax-evasion offences; HM Customs Gibraltar may claw back duty drawbacks and levy penalty duties of 100%-200% of evaded duty on false export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies to both capital and current accounts. The UK abolition of exchange controls under the Exchange Control Act 1979 extends to Gibraltar, meaning a Bermuda Exempted Company faces no exchange-control authorization, no capital-repatriation permit, and no profit-remittance hurdle. Dividends, interest, and service fees may be paid freely to non-resident shareholders, subject only to AML wire-transfer reporting, CRS/FATCA disclosure, and the maintenance of a coherent economic-substance narrative for the flow of SaaS revenue from the SEO content analytics tool.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio apply. Export proceeds from subscription and SEO-analytics licensing income may be retained indefinitely in any convertible currency (GBP, USD, EUR, or other major currencies). GFSC-regulated banks nonetheless expect a documented trail via the entity's Gibraltar operational account and the payment-processor sub-ledgers (Stripe and Paddle) to substantiate the commercial source of cross-border receipts.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) acting as the integrated financial regulator and de facto currency authority under Gibraltar's Currency Notes Act; monetary stabilization is anchored to the Bank of England (BoE) via a fixed 1:1 parity between the Gibraltar pound (GIP) and pound sterling (GBP), with no autonomous central bank in the territory.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High viability as an operational treasury overlay but Medium viability as the sole regulated settlement location. Since Brexit, UK EMIs and EU electronic-money institutions do not automatically passport into Gibraltar; only GFSC-licensed payment-service providers or e-money institutions under the Financial Services Act 2019 may offer regulated payment services into the territory. A Bermuda Exempted Company may lawfully maintain funding and disbursement accounts with FCA-supervised or equivalent EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud) for global vendor payments, provided the principal settlement account remains with a GFSC-licensed bank and the EMI is subject to recognized home-state supervision with appropriate safeguarding of client funds.

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar imposes transfer pricing documentation obligations when the Gibraltar taxpayer's related-party revenue exceeds £5,000,000 or related-party cross-border payments exceed £1,000,000; Bermuda requires a Local File and a Master File once ultimate group consolidated turnover exceeds USD 850,000,000.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Tax Office can charge the undeclared profits at 12.5% plus a penalty of up to 100% of the tax and daily interest of 4.5%; criminal fines up to £10,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors risk joint and several liability for the tax unpaid, are subject to travel bans, disqualification orders for up to 15 years, and imprisonment for reckless evasion.
Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: In Bermuda, the Economic Substance Act imposes a rate of 0 if the company is compliant but non-compliance triggers a penalty of $5,000 per month, up to $50,000, and automatic exchange of information with the UK and Gibraltar. In Gibraltar, failure to pass substance rules revoked the tax status and imposes penalties up to £10,000.

Director personal liability: The director who signs a false economic substance return is criminally liable; personal fines and confiscation orders may be imposed even though the corporate form exists.
Transfer Pricing/TP Misstatements

Statutory sanction: A TP adjustment would increase Gibraltar taxable profits to 12.5%, impose a penalty of 35% of the underpaid tax, and require consequential adjustment; if the taxpayer fails to file a Local File, an additional penalty of £5,000 applies.

Director personal liability: Directors are liable for reckless/negligent pricing statements; in serious cases, personal liability for the total tax adjusted amount arises and director disqualification follows.
AML & Stripe/Paddle Compliance Breach
