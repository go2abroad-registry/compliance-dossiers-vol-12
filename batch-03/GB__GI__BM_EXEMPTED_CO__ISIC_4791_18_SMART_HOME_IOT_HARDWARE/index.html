Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda law provides no check-the-box election; the Bermuda Exempted Company is automatically an opaque entity-level taxpayer that is statutorily exempt from Bermuda income tax until 2035. In Gibraltar, no preferential entity election is available for post-2011 companies; the only administrative election is formal confirmation of non-residence status with the Commissioner of Income Tax where central management and control is located outside Gibraltar. In the United Kingdom, the non-established company may elect to be registered as a Non-Established Taxable Person (NETP) for VAT and may elect to apply the VAT place-of-supply rules for B2C digital services; no hybrid entity check-the-box election is available, and UK CTA 2010 Part 6A hybrid-mismatch rules apply automatically to neutralise any deduction/non-inclusion mismatch between Bermuda (tax-exempt) and Gibraltar (12.5%) outcomes rather than through a formal election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar: No VAT/GST (import and excise duties only); United Kingdom: Value Added Tax (VAT) under the Value Added Tax Act 1994, applicable where supplies of IoT hardware are made to UK-based consumers or where the goods are located in the UK at the time of supply.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: Not applicable — Gibraltar has no VAT/GST regime and therefore no registration threshold, although import duty applies on physical importation of goods. United Kingdom: GBP 90,000 taxable turnover threshold for UK-established businesses; non-established taxable persons supplying goods in the UK are generally required to register for UK VAT regardless of size if they sell goods subject to import VAT under the low-value consignment rules (consignments not exceeding GBP 135) into Great Britain, and voluntary registration is permitted to recover input VAT. European Union (for remote B2C sales into the EU): EUR 10,000 Union-wide distance-selling threshold may trigger OSS/IOSS registration, applicable where the Bermuda Exempted Company stores no goods in the EU and dispatches from outside the EU.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar: exports are not zero-rated for VAT because Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST; goods physically exported from Gibraltar are relieved of Gibraltar import duty and excise only when customs export formalities are completed under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance. United Kingdom: export zero-rating applies under VATA 1994 Schedule 8 to (i) goods exported by the supplier or on its behalf to a destination outside the UK, provided the goods depart the UK within 3 months of supply and the supplier holds valid evidence of removal, and (ii) qualifying international services, including IoT cloud-management subscriptions, firmware licences, and remote monitoring services, supplied to a relevant business customer belonging outside the UK. Sale of smart-home IoT hardware from a website (Stripe/Shopify Payments rails) to UK consumers is outside the scope of UK VAT when the goods are dispatched from outside the UK at the time of sale, but import VAT and, where applicable, the GBP 135 low-value consignment rules will apply at the UK border; no UK zero-rating is available for goods physically in the UK at the time of supply.

United Kingdom: Making Tax Digital (MTD) requires all VAT-registered businesses to maintain digital records and submit VAT returns through HMRC-recognised software using HMRC APIs for VAT periods beginning on or after 1 April 2022; HMRC does not mandate real-time e-invoicing or digital stamping for B2B retail, but PEPPOL e-invoices are required for UK public-sector suppliers. Customs declarations for goods moving between Gibraltar and the UK must be lodged through the UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS), with the UK-Gibraltar trade treated as import/export because Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT and customs territory.

Gibraltar: false or incomplete export declarations attract seizure of goods, forfeiture of conveyance, and customs penalties of up to three times the duty evaded under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance, with retrospective assessment of import duty where export evidence is defective. United Kingdom: HMRC may deny zero-rating where the 3-month export window or evidence bundle is defective, raise assessments for unpaid VAT up to 4 years (6 years for careless, 20 years for deliberate inaccuracy), and recover interest from the supply date. Civil penalties under FA 2007 Schedule 24 range from 30% to 100% of the VAT lost for deliberate or concealed errors; criminal prosecution under VATA 1994 section 72 can result in imprisonment up to 7 years and unlimited fines for fraudulent zero-rating or evasion. Additional CEMA 1979 penalties of up to GBP 2,500 per false customs entry may be imposed, and HMRC may hold online marketplaces jointly and severally liable for unpaid VAT of overseas sellers under the notified marketplace regime; the Bermuda tax-exemption guarantee does not shield against UK or Gibraltar indirect-tax assessments.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound is legally pegged at a one-to-one parity with the United Kingdom pound sterling and is fully convertible for current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no residual exchange-control restrictions on inward or outward transfers of funds for non-resident, tax-exempt corporate structures such as a Bermuda Exempted Company operating a Gibraltar branch or bank facility. Profit remittance to the ultimate security holders is lawful, provided the entity maintains adequate substance, board authority, and audited financial statements demonstrating distributable reserves. Sanctions screening, anti-money laundering verification and beneficial-ownership declarations still apply but are not convertibility hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The Exchange Control Act 1965 is obsolete in practical effect; no statutory surrender or forced conversion of export proceeds exists in Gibraltar. A Bermuda Exempted Company may lawfully retain export receipts in any convertible currency (USD, EUR, GBP, GIP) in its commercial bank accounts. The only mandatory duties are accurate bookkeeping, reporting of interest-bearing accounts where relevant, and compliance with GFSC and Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) transaction-reporting obligations. No minimum conversion ratio applies to IoT hardware retail revenues originating from Stripe, Shopify Payments, or traditional SWIFT receipts.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no indigenous central bank with independent monetary powers; the pragmatic monetary authority is HM Treasury and the Bank of England, while the Government of Gibraltar issues local pound notes at par with sterling under the Currency Notes Act 1934. The integrated banking, anti-money laundering and financial-services regulator is the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), exercising powers under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Banking Act 2015.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Genrally viable but conditionally restricted. Gibraltar-licensed electronic money institutions and banking-licensed fintechs are permitted to onboard the Bermuda Exempted Company for cross-border and multi-currency treasury flows. EMI accounts are appropriate for e-commerce payment pooling, API-driven settlement with Stripe/Shopify, and low-to-medium transaction volumes. Post-Brexit, European Economic Area EMIs no longer passport into Gibraltar automatically and require a Gibraltar licence or a local agent/trusted introducer. A fintech EMI route is therefore feasible if the provider holds a GFSC licence or an equivalent UK-asymmetric permission under the Temporary Permissions Regime; otherwise, viability is Low.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from the Bermuda parent entity to the Gibraltar operating bank account are routed via SWIFT MT103 or equivalent instant-CHAPS transfer, using the account owner's legal name (GB__GI__BM_EXEMPTED_CO__ISIC_4791_18_SMART_HOME_IOT_HARDWARE or the explicit company registration number) in field 50K and a clear payment purpose in field 70 identifying 'Equity Subscription', 'Shareholder Subordinated Loan', or 'Working Capital Injection'. Statement accounts must be supported by a board resolution of the Bermuda exempted company, a signed subscription agreement or loan facility letter, a source-of-funds memorandum, and a certificate of incorporation to prevent an incorrect classification of the credit.
