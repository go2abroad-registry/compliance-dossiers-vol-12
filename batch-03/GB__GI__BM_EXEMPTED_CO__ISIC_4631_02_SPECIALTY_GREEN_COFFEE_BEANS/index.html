Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Gibraltar Income Tax Office (GITO), Ministry of Finance, Government of Gibraltar; e-filing portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax; VAT and customs: HM Customs Gibraltar; central registration portal: Gibraltar Tax E-Portal.

E-government filing portal

For a Bermuda Exempted Company establishing a taxable presence in Gibraltar: (1) register as an overseas company with the Registrar of Companies (Companies House Gibraltar) under Part XXI of the Companies Act 2014 within one month of opening a branch, filing the Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation, memorandum/articles, directors list, and a Gibraltar registered office address; (2) obtain the Gibraltar Commercial Registration (CRC) number; (3) file the corporate tax registration form with GITO to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (GTRN); registration is mandatory within 30 days of commencing chargeable activity; (4) register for PAYE with GITO if any employee is engaged in Gibraltar; (5) register for VAT with HM Customs Gibraltar under the Value Added Tax Act 1996 (Form VAT-1) if taxable turnover exceeds GIP 100,000 annually, or electively where below threshold; (6) obtain a Gibraltar Economic Operator Code (customs EORI) before lodging import/export declarations. Alternative: the Bermuda Exempted Company may be continued into Gibraltar under the Companies (Continuance) Regulations, resulting in full Gibraltar incorporation and a local company number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box or entity classification election. Available elections include: (a) group relief election to surrender and claim losses among Gibraltar-resident group companies (Income Tax Act 2010); (b) election for first-year capital allowances on qualifying plant and machinery (20% allowance); (c) election under the territorial source rules for a non-resident company's Gibraltar permanent establishment to be assessed only on Gibraltar-source trading income; (d) voluntary VAT registration election under the Value Added Tax Act 1996; (e) election to apply the thin-capitalisation safe-harbour interest deduction rules; (f) transfer-pricing elections under the Gibraltar Transfer Pricing Regulations 2021 (OECD-aligned), including a small and medium enterprise compliance exemption election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Value Added Tax (VAT) under the Value Added Tax Act 1996; customs and excise duties under the Customs Ordinance 1963, administered by HM Customs Gibraltar; Gibraltar is outside the UK and EU VAT territory.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 100,000 annual taxable turnover (Gibraltar pound, pegged 1:1 with GBP), adjustable by ministerial order; mandatory VAT registration for all importers of goods irrespective of turnover; voluntary registration permitted below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of specialty green coffee beans is zero-rated where: (1) the goods are sold to a buyer outside Gibraltar and physically removed from Gibraltar by the supplier, the buyer, or an authorised carrier; (2) export is effected within 180 days of the supply date; (3) the supplier retains export evidence (customs export declaration, bill of lading, freight and insurance documents) for a minimum of six years; and (4) title and risk pass to the overseas buyer. Commissions, brokerage, logistics, and trade-finance services supplied to non-resident counterparties fall outside the scope of Gibraltar VAT under place-of-supply rules. High-seas and transit sales of green coffee that never land in Gibraltar are outside the scope of VAT and do not require refund claims. Given the 0% standard rate, virtually no recoverable input VAT arises; any residual input VAT or excise duty relief is claimed through the quarterly VAT return and audited by HM Customs Gibraltar.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (SAD/C88) evidencing physical export
  • Bill of Lading (or multimodal transport document) issued by the carrier
  • Commercial invoice signed by the selling entity
  • Packing list and certificate of origin (required for documentary letter of credit)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Bermuda Exempted Company corporate records and beneficial ownership register extract for customs and AML compliance checks

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or a proprietary e-invoicing API. VAT returns and tax filings are submitted electronically via the Gibraltar Tax E-Portal. Import and export declarations are cleared electronically in SAD/C88 format through the Gibraltar Customs computerised single window, with Import Control System declarations required for goods moving through the EU/UK transit corridor. No SAF-T format or continuous transaction control model is in force; authorised economic operator status is recognised.

The Commissioner of Income Tax and HM Customs Gibraltar may open an enquiry within four years, extended to six years for fraud or negligent conduct. For fraudulent export zero-rating, false customs declarations, or non-repatriation of export proceeds, penalties include: (a) civil penalty of up to 200% of VAT or duty evaded; (b) fixed penalty of up to GIP 5,000 per customs offence with seizure and forfeiture of the coffee consignment or its proceeds; (c) retrospective clawback of input VAT, excise duty relief, and related interest at 8% per annum; (d) assessment of unremitted export proceeds as Gibraltar-source trading profit on an accruals basis where funds are not received within 180 days; and (e) criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion with imprisonment of up to seven years and unlimited fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility under both current and capital account regimes in Gibraltar and Bermuda. GIP circulates at par with GBP and sterling is generally accepted as legal tender in Gibraltar, so no actual FX conversion is required for GBP-denominated flows; USD and EUR are freely convertible without approval. Profit and dividend remittances are unrestricted, and there are no Gibraltar withholding taxes, taxes on dividends, or capital controls on the repatriation of coffee-trading profits to Bermuda or elsewhere. The only material hurdle is a documentary one: a distributing bank will require evidence of economic substance in Gibraltar (further to Gibraltar's Economic Substance Act 2020) where the exempted company carries out relevant treasury activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement exists in Gibraltar or Bermuda. Export receipts from specialty green coffee container sales may be retained in the original settlement currency (USD/EUR/GBP), held in a Gibraltar bank account without mandatory conversion to GIP, and used directly to settle import payables to origin-country suppliers or to hedge currency exposure. No repatriation deadline or conversion ratio applies to the Bermuda Exempted Company's proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no independent central bank; bank licensing and prudential supervision are exercised by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), while the Government of Gibraltar acts as the note-issuing authority under the Currency Notes Act 1934, maintaining a 100% sterling-backed Gibraltar pound (GIP) at a statutory 1:1 peg to GBP. The Bank of England serves solely as the ultimate provider of sterling backing; it has no regulatory jurisdiction over Gibraltar banks. The Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) is also relevant because the account-holding entity is a Bermuda Exempted Company, although the BMA imposes no exchange or capital controls on non-resident exempted companies.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with constraints. UK-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, Currencycloud, and IFX can supply GBP/EUR/USD IBANs that support fast cross-border settlement into Gibraltar, but EU passporting ceased post-Brexit, and each EMI must either hold a Gibraltar e-money licence under the Financial Services (Electronic Money and Payment Regulations) framework or maintain an acceptable UK-Gibraltar market-access arrangement. EMIs are legally barred from issuing documentary credits, providing overdraft/credit, or holding client funds against risk; they should therefore be limited to sub-USD 50,000 operational disbursements (insurance premiums, origin sampling, freight top-ups), while the core trade settlement stream must run through a tier-1 licensed bank to preserve LC capacity and regulatory credibility.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity and shareholder-loan capital from the Bermuda parent or UBOs is routed by SWIFT MT103/MT202 wire to the Gibraltar operating account, with the remittance narrative citing 'Equity Subscription', 'Share Premium', or 'Shareholder Subordinated Loan' and a purpose code consistent with direct-investment data collection. The receiving bank requires board resolutions approving the issuance/allotment, the register of members update, source-of-wealth documents (audited parent financial statements and originating bank account statements), and a short-form explanation of the working-capital deployment into green coffee LC facilities. Because no Gibraltar exchange control prior approval exists, same-day value is achievable; GIP conversion is only triggered if physical GIP cash or local payroll in GIP is required, otherwise USD/EUR/GBP can be held as-is.Two-rail settlement strategy for specialty green coffee: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct wire transfers for disbursements below USD 50,000, sample parcels, balancing payments after container release, and open-account rolling settlements on 30-to-60-day terms; and (ii) irrevocable confirmed Commercial Letters of Credit issued under UCP 600 (SWIFT MT700 with amendments via MT707) for full FOB/CIF container contracts, advised through a correspondent bank in the exporter's country (e.g., banks in Colombia, Brazil, Vietnam, or Ethiopia). Correspondent clearing is executed over CHIPS/Fedwire for USD, TARGET2 for EUR, and CHAPS for GBP.
