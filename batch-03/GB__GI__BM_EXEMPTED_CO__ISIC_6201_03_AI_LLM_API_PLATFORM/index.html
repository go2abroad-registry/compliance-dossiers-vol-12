Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - The Bermuda Exempted Company is a limited liability entity relying on the statutory tax guarantee under Bermuda's Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 (tax protection to at least 2035, extended to 2050) and an excluded-entity/minimal-presence exclusion under the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (15% domestic top-up tax). Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification regime; tax status follows legal form and actual management and control. Available statutory elections and positions include: (i) joint group relief election under s.35 Income Tax Act 2010 for surrender of losses between 75% group companies; (ii) factual election for non-resident treatment where board meetings and central management and control are exercised outside Gibraltar, supported by substance documentation; (iii) Bermuda-side election to hold an Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection certificate (tax guarantee) and election/confirmation of excluded-entity status under the Bermuda Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 for entities not part of a large in-scope MNE group with Bermuda substance; (iv) OECD Pillar Two safe harbour and substance-based income exclusion elections where the MNE group exceeds EUR 750 million consolidated revenue; (v) treaty relief elections under the UK-Gibraltar double taxation arrangement (as amended) and any source-state treaty positions applicable to cross-border API licensing income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Customs and Excise Duties (no VAT / GST / general sales tax regime). Gibraltar is constitutionally outside the UK and EU VAT area (Protocol 3 of the 1972 Treaty of Accession, preserved post-Brexit) and therefore imposes no VAT, GST, or general consumption tax on services; indirect taxation is limited to customs/excise duties on specified imported goods (alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oils, vehicles), property transfer/stamp duties, and immigration-type fees. AI/LLM inference API token sales and subscription services are entirely outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - there is no VAT/GST registration threshold in Gibraltar because no VAT/GST regime exists for services. A customs/excise registration obligation with HM Customs Gibraltar arises only for importers of dutiable goods (alcohol, tobacco, fuel, vehicles and other tariff-listed items); no minimum economic turnover threshold triggers this registration, although the Traveller's Allowance Order and low-value consignment de minimis reliefs exempt small personal imports. For the AI/LLM API platform scope (ISIC 6201, software as a service), no indirect tax registration, fiscal representative, or turnover threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Classic VAT zero-rating is not applicable because Gibraltar has no VAT regime; the AI/LLM API platform services are inherently outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax and no refund mechanism or zero-rating election is required for them. For direct tax purposes, the export-services treatment is achieved by demonstrating that the income from non-Gibraltar customers does not accrue in or derive from Gibraltar under s.8 of the Income Tax Act 2010. The entity must evidence: (i) foreign place of contract formation and place of performance of the API services; (ii) absence of a Gibraltar permanent establishment, or arm's-length attribution of profits to any Gibraltar presence under s.53 and transfer pricing rules; (iii) central management and control exercised in Bermuda or another non-Gibraltar jurisdiction evidenced by board minutes and key-person functions; and (iv) no Gibraltar staff or fixed place of business used for core revenue-generating activities. Where these conditions are met, export revenue from AI/LLM API token sales is taxed at an effective rate of 0% in Gibraltar and 0% in Bermuda under the exempted company protections.

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate exists in Gibraltar. Gibraltar does not operate continuous transaction controls, mandatory government-cleared e-invoicing, API-based fiscal reporting, or digital stamping; businesses may issue electronic or paper invoices in accordance with commercial and accounting standards (Companies Act record-keeping and Income Tax Act substantiation requirements). Customs goods declarations are submitted electronically through the Gibraltar Trade Single Window / Customs Declaration System, and excise goods movements are tracked through the Gibraltar Excise Movement and Control System aligned with the EU Excise Movement and Control System requirements for intra-Community movements; none of these mechanisms apply to the cross-border supply of digital AI/LLM API services.

Gibraltar imposes no statutory exchange-control repatriation deadline, but the 180-day window is adopted in this dossier as the internal compliance benchmark for remittance of offshore AI/LLM API receipts into the Gibraltar or Bermuda settlement accounts; failure to maintain documented repatriation may trigger substance challenge. The Income Tax Office may open a substance-based audit where the Bermuda Exempted Company claims non-resident or source-free treatment: incorrect or careless returns attract interest plus civil penalties up to the additional tax due and a maximum GIP 100,000 penalty per inaccuracy; deliberate or fraudulent evasion is a criminal offence punishable by fines and imprisonment of up to ten years. Retrospective assessments may be raised within six years of the relevant year of assessment, extended to twenty years in cases of deliberate error or fraud. Bermuda-side clawback risk: breach of the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection certificate conditions or misapplication of the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 excluded-entity status triggers retrospective exposure to the 15% Bermuda corporate income tax with penalty interest from the effective date. False customs declarations to HM Customs (if any physical goods are exported) expose the entity to forfeiture of goods and penalties of up to three times the duty value. Although EU-style 'export zero-rating clawback' does not exist in Gibraltar, the analogous audit risk is recharacterisation of offshore income as Gibraltar-source with full 10% tax, penalties and interest, making contemporaneous substance and documentation mandatory.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar administers a full current and capital account convertibility regime without distinctions between residents and non-residents. The GIP is freely convertible at the fixed 1:1 peg into GBP, while USD and EUR are competitively traded through authorised banks. There are no restrictions on the conversion, retention, or repatriation of profits, dividends, loan redemptions, or capital disposal proceeds. A Bermuda Exempted Company operating from a Gibraltar treasury hub may receive equity subscriptions and remit dividends abroad without prior exchange control validation, provided the transaction is supported by audited financial statements, a valid tax-exemption certificate, and appropriate AML/CTF compliance. The legal absence of withholding taxes on dividends, interest, royalties, and capital gains further removes conversion-related frictions, leaving banks as gatekeepers solely for documentary compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion proportion, and no deadline for converting export receipts. Exporters of services, including generative AI and LLM API token licensing platforms, are permitted to invoice and retain currencies of their choice, including USD, EUR, GBP, and USDC when accepted by their payment processors. There is no requirement to surrender foreign exchange to the Government or to any designated monetary authority. Earnings can be held offshore or remitted to a Gibraltar account with full discretion; conversion of incidental USDC balances into GIP/GBP is executed at prevailing market rates through the counterparty bank.

Central bank supervision: The Financial Services Commission (FSC) of Gibraltar, acting under the Financial Services Act 2019, together with the Government of Gibraltar's Ministry of Finance (Exchange Control Unit) exercising residual powers under the Exchange Control Act (Gibraltar) 1972. Gibraltar does not possess an independent central bank; the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is a separate legal tender issued under the Currency Notes Act 1934, strictly pegged at a 1:1 parity with sterling. The Bank of England acts as the monetary anchor through the backing of every Gibraltar pound note with an equivalent pound sterling deposit held by the Government of Gibraltar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Moderate-to-Low. Post-Brexit, Gibraltar is no longer integrated with the EEA payment services passport regime, and the FSC has granted only a limited number of local Electronic Money Institution (EMI) and payment institution licences. Reputable cross-border digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, PAX) can sometimes open a merchant or treasury account for a Gibraltar-incorporated company, but they will typically decline a Bermuda Exempted Company structure due to enhanced due diligence sensitivities around zero-tax, non-resident investment vehicles. The main legal constraints include: (i) an absence of direct EMI passporting from the United Kingdom to Gibraltar; (ii) strict limitations in accepting USDC or other cryptocurrency receipts; (iii) a requirement for the EMI to be supervised by the GFSC if domiciled in Gibraltar; and (iv) an absolute prohibition on the use of personal or anonymous wallets in any treasury flow. The practical feasibility rating is 2 out of 5; a Gibraltar-licensed bank with an onboarded FX desk and a separate GFSC-licensed distributed ledger provider is the recommended alternative.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where annual aggregate controlled transactions exceed GIP 1,000,000; Master File mandatory where group consolidated turnover exceeds GIP 500,000,000 or aggregate controlled transactions exceed GIP 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Companies and Foreign Partnerships) Regulations apply in Gibraltar to any entity carrying on a relevant activity. An AI LLM API platform deriving revenue from alorithm licensing, model exploitation or API monetisation is classified as an Intellectual Property Business. Such an entity must maintain a Gibraltar resident board with quorum, physical premises, strategic staff, and core income-generating activities in Gibraltar. Failure to satisfy the substance test permits the Commissioner to exchange information with the UK and imposes full loss of territorial tax relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Fine up to GIP 100,000; assessed tax increased by up to 150%; forfeiture of proceeds under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; criminal prosecution before the Supreme Court of Gibraltar.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall; personal assets may be seized by court order; travel bans and detention under the Criminal Procedure Act; imprisonment up to seven years.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Compulsory tax status cancellation; strike-off from the Gibraltar Register of Companies; penalty up to GIP 100,000; exchange of information with overseas tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Veil piercing exposure; directors personally ordered to restore forgone tax; five-year company directors disqualification order; personal travel restrictions pending review of commercial substance.
AML/CFT and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fine up to GIP 500,000 or 10% of annual worldwide turnover, whichever is higher; separate criminal liability under the Proceeds of Crime Act; custodial sanctions up to ten years.

Director personal liability: The nominated Money Laundering Reporting Officer and all disqualified directors are personally liable; personal civil confiscation orders up to the value of laundered funds; international travel bans and prison terms.
Transfer Pricing and Intentional Profit Misattribution

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of chargeable profits to arm's length reality; penalty of 100% of the underpaid tax; interest at the statutory rate of 8% per annum; neglect penalties up to GIP 50,000 per document.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the mispricing face a personal penalty equal to the tax underpaid; a court may disallow corporate veil protection and attach bank accounts in Gibraltar, the UK and the EU.
