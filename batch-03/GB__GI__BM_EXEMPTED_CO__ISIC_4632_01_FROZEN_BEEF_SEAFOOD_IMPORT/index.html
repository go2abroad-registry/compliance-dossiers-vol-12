Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope: The Bermuda Exempted Company is classified as non-resident in Gibraltar unless management and control are exercised in Gibraltar. Non-resident companies are taxable only on Gibraltar-source income. If management and control are found to sit in Gibraltar, the company becomes a Gibraltar tax resident subject to corporate income tax at 12.5% on worldwide profits, with a 15% Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax applicable to in-scope multinational groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more under OECD Pillar Two. Bermuda exempt status is preserved in Bermuda but does not extend to Gibraltar-source income.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box or US-style entity classification regime. Available statutory elections include: (i) a territorial-source election for non-resident companies under the Income Tax Act; (ii) an election to apply the UK-Gibraltar Double Taxation Agreement, including permanent establishment determination and mutual agreement procedure relief; (iii) election under section 52B Income Tax Act for participation and dividend exemption relief; (iv) no hybrid mismatch election exists or is available, because Gibraltar has codified the OECD hybrid mismatch rules as mandatory anti-hybrid provisions that apply automatically to the Bermuda Exempted Company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar customs duties regime — no VAT, GST, or general sales tax applies in Gibraltar (Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area). Import Duty is chargeable on a defined list of goods; Excise Duty applies to alcohol, tobacco, and fuel. Frozen meat and seafood imports are generally not on the duty list and are therefore free of Import Duty unless locally consumed under specified categories.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold triggers indirect tax registration in Gibraltar. Every importer must obtain an Importer/Exporter Code from HM Customs Gibraltar before lodging the first customs declaration, regardless of value. A de minimis customs declaration exemption applies to commercial consignments of CIF value not exceeding GIP 135 and to postal consignments not exceeding GIP 22.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

As Gibraltar has no VAT, no zero-rating of export supplies exists. The applicable relief is Import Duty exemption on re-export: goods imported into Gibraltar, including deep-frozen meat and pelagic fish, and re-exported in an unaltered state within 24 months of import are exempt from Import Duty, provided the goods are not sold or consumed locally and the re-export is evidenced by a customs export declaration. Goods stored in a licensed bonded warehouse or free-zone facility remain under duty suspension. If a UK VAT-registered branch of the entity exports goods from the United Kingdom, UK VAT zero-rating under Value Added Tax Act 1994, section 30(6), would apply to those exports; however, this mechanism is not available for Gibraltar-domiciled supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD C88) filed electronically through the Synergi system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by a Gibraltar-licensed bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin where preferential duty rates are claimed
  • Re-Export or Removal Note from the bonded warehouse when applicable

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping for commercial invoices. All import and export declarations must be submitted electronically via the HM Customs Gibraltar Synergi single-window system using the Single Administrative Document (SAD, form C88). No digital fiscal stamp is required for frozen beef or seafood imports.

HM Customs Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office conduct post-clearance audits and data-matching checks against bill-of-lading records, warehouse records, and bank remittance evidence. False or fraudulent claims for Import Duty relief or re-export exemption are prosecuted under the Customs Ordinance as customs fraud: penalties of up to three times the value of the duty evaded or GIP 50,000, whichever is higher, plus forfeiture of goods and retrospective clawback of underpaid duty for up to six years. Fraudulent misrepresentation or concealment in tax declarations attracts interest at the statutory rate and penalties of up to 100% of the tax due under the Income Tax Act, with director disqualification, and criminal money-laundering liability where the proceeds of the fraud are concealed or transferred.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar imposes no exchange controls. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at 1:1 to GBP, and both capital account and current account transactions are fully convertible, including profits, dividends, and intra-group loans. The UK's Exchange Control Act was substantially repealed in 1979, and no post-Brexit capital controls have been introduced either to the UK or Gibraltar. Practical remittance hurdles are limited to standard AML/KYC scrutiny, documentary proof of beneficial ownership, tax residence clearance, and possibly a tax clearance certificate where the bank's compliance committee treats the Bermuda exempted company as an offshore concern. Profits remitted to shareholders will normally require audited financial statements and a certificate of good standing from the Bermuda Registrar.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable: neither the UK nor Gibraltar requires exporters to surrender foreign currency proceeds to a central bank, nor imposes mandatory conversion ratios or statutory repatriation deadlines. Banks may nevertheless impose contractual repatriation within 90–180 days in trade-finance facilities, and any financing against receivables will be monitored for offshore collection and potential diversion risk.

Central bank supervision: Not applicable: Gibraltar has no independent central bank. The Bank of England is the ultimate monetary authority under the Gibraltar pound currency-board arrangement, and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the licensing and prudential supervisor for banks and payment institutions within the jurisdiction.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for Gibraltar-licensed payment institutions and electronic money institutions under the GFSC's Payment Services framework; Low for unregulated offshore digital EMIs. Legal constraints include: an offshore EMI without a passport into Gibraltar will not hold a settlement account with a Gibraltarian bank; a Bermuda exempted company is not itself authorised to act as an e-money issuer; and the bank will require evidence that the EMI is licensed in the country where the corporate treasury account is domiciled. Practically, remote onboarding with a non-bank EMI may be accepted for low-velocity trade transactions, but the EMI's inability to provide commercial letters of credit or maintain a Gibraltar correspondent account makes it unsuitable for the primary clearing and LC disbursement functions of the frozen beef and seafood import operation.

Inbound shareholder or intra-group capital injections must be executed by SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank, with the beneficiary account denominated in GIP, GBP, USD, or EUR as agreed in the bank's account mandate. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium for Gibraltar-licensed payment institutions and electronic money institutions under the GFSC's Payment Services framework; Low for unregulated offshore digital EMIs. Legal constraints include: an offshore EMI without a passport into Gibraltar will not hold a settlement account with a Gibraltarian bank; a Bermuda exempted company is not itself authorised to act as an e-money issuer; and the bank will require evidence that the EMI is licensed in the country where the corporate treasury account is domiciled. Practically, remote onboarding with a non-bank EMI may be accepted for low-velocity trade transactions, but the EMI's inability to provide commercial letters of credit or maintain a Gibraltar correspondent account makes it unsuitable for the primary clearing and LC disbursement functions of the frozen beef and seafood import operation.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% on chargeable profits (Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010); the Bermuda Exempted Company is not subject to Bermuda income tax on either its Bermuda-sourced or foreign-sourced trading profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%: Gibraltar does not impose dividend withholding tax under the Income Tax Act 2010; the Bermuda Exempted Company itself is not subject to Bermuda withholding tax on dividend distributions, and no Gibraltar dividend withholding tax arises for a distributing Gibraltar-resident company.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 2,000,000 in aggregate annual connected-party transactions triggers an obligation to prepare contemporaneous arm's-length documentation, but the local file is only statutorily required where the Gibraltar entity is in a multinational group with consolidated revenue exceeding GIP 100,000,000 (approximately EUR 115,000,000). Master File is required when group consolidated revenue exceeds GIP 750,000,000 (matches the CbCR threshold). Even where documentation is not mandatory, the taxpayer must keep local transfer pricing supporting evidence for all connected-party meat and seafood procurement arrangements.

Economic substance scope: Applicable under two overlapping statutes. (1) Bermuda ESR 2018: if the Bermuda Exempted Company is tax resident in another jurisdiction (e.g., Gibraltar), it must report 'non-resident' and prove active participation; if treated as resident in Bermuda, it must satisfy core services, staff and premises requirements. The import and wholesale of frozen meat and fish is treated as a 'distribution and service centre' relevant activity. (2) Gibraltar ESR 2019: if the company is managed and controlled in Gibraltar or carries on a PE in Gibraltar, the same relevant activity triggers a requirement for local direction, employees, office, and core income-generating activities in Gibraltar. Both statutes impose annual economic substance returns and severe financial penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False Import Valuation & Understated Sales

Statutory sanction: Assessment to tax on the true chargeable profits; negligence or careless misstatement penalty of 30% of the tax understated; deliberate concealment penalty of 100%; in cases of serially fraudulent returns, criminal conviction on indictment carries imprisonment up to 10 years and an unlimited fine.

Director personal liability: The Supreme Court of Gibraltar may lift the corporate veil where the exempt company is used as a façade. Directors and shadow directors who signed the false return become personally liable for the underpaid tax as if it were their own liability; in the worst cases a sequestration order grants a travel ban prohibiting the director from leaving Gibraltar, the passport is surrendered, and imprisonment up to 10 years may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Abuse - Shell Procurement Intermediary

Statutory sanction: A primary transfer pricing adjustment increasing the Gibraltar PE's taxable profit by the difference from an arm's-length margin; restitution interest at the indexed Treasury rate; a transfer pricing penalty of 50% of the additional liability if documentation was not prepared, rising to 200% where false invoices were contrived; potential cancellation of the import licence for the corporate taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable if the arrangement is a sham or artificial transaction designed to defeat Gibraltar tax. UK HMRC and the GFSC can invoke mutual assistance; personal financial restitution can include clawing back management fees paid by the company to the directors; a Civil Recovery Unit may freeze personal assets and prohibit travel while the investigation remains active.
Breach of Economic Substance Regulations (Bermuda and Gibraltar)

Statutory sanction: Bermuda ESR: an initial financial penalty of up to BMD 50,000, escalating at BMD 10,000 per annum, and ultimately BMD 250,000; the Registrar may strike the company off and refer information to UK and Gibraltar authorities. Gibraltar ESR: penalty up to GIP 25,000 per relevant activity for the first year and GIP 50,000 per activity for repeat failure; the GFSC may require dissolution of the Gibraltar establishment; a 12-month strike-off exposes shareholders to personal liability for fees.

Director personal liability: Directors making a false economic substance declaration are criminally liable under the Economic Substance (Information Exchange) Regulations. Each director can be personally fined GIP 25,000 and disqualified from acting as a director for five years. A travel ban under the Proceeds of Crime Act can accompany a money-laundering investigation into the corporate monies.
AML / Counter-Financing of Terrorism - Revolving LC Trade Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) and the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, the entity is liable to an unlimited AML fine and the criminal court may order confiscation of all goods and proceeds derived from the offending trade. Responsible officers face up to 14 years' imprisonment for money laundering. The customs penalty for a false cargo declaration can reach three times the value of the goods, plus permanent revocation of the import licence.

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to 'controlling authority' liability and may be personally prosecuted where they consented to or connived in AML contraventions. Consequences include 14 years' imprisonment, unlimited personal fines, freezing orders, travel bans prohibiting exit from Gibraltar, passport surrender, and a court order barring the director from any future management role in a corporate entity in the UK or Gibraltar.
