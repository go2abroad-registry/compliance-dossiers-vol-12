Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax of Gibraltar (Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar; online portal: https://www.taxation.gov.gi) for corporation tax, income tax, PAYE and social insurance; and Gibraltar Customs (HM Customs Gibraltar), the customs and border authority of HM Government of Gibraltar (portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi), for import duties, excise and customs/export controls. Corporate registration: Gibraltar Companies Registry (Registrar of Companies).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Bermuda Exempted Company as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry under the Companies Act 2014 if it establishes a place of business in Gibraltar. Step 2: File a company tax-registration application with the Commissioner of Income Tax attaching: (i) Bermuda Certificate of Incorporation and exempted-company status evidence; (ii) memorandum and articles of association; (iii) register of directors and shareholders; (iv) Gibraltar place-of-business address; and (v) a signed Gibraltar tax agent/representative letter, if applicable. The Income Tax Office then issues the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN). Step 3: Apply to Gibraltar Customs for an economic-operator registration (EORI-equivalent) before the first import/export declaration; there is no turnover threshold. Step 4: If the branch employs staff, register as an employer for PAYE and Gibraltar social insurance contributions before payroll commencement. No minimum capital requirement applies for tax registration purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda domicile): the Bermuda Exempted Company is exempt from Bermuda income tax on profits, dividends, interest and capital gains, with statutory protection against future Bermudian taxation under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966. Territorial_Scope (Gibraltar PE): in Gibraltar, the entity is a non-resident foreign company chargeable to Gibraltar corporation tax only on Gibraltar-source profits attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment (the fulfillment, kitting and assembly facility) at the standard 12.5% rate. The entity is not within the UK corporation tax regime, as Gibraltar is a separate tax jurisdiction.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or entity-classification election exists in Gibraltar law. The 'exempted company' classification itself is a statutory election made before the Bermuda Registrar of Companies by application and payment of the annual government fee. In Gibraltar, the material elections are: (i) tax-residence determination - if central management and control are exercised in Gibraltar, the company becomes Gibraltar-resident and taxable on worldwide profits; otherwise it remains non-resident and taxable only on Gibraltar-source/PE profits; (ii) customs-status elections - Authorised Economic Operator (AEO) status and customs-warehousing (duty-suspension) authorisation may be elected for the fulfillment operation; and (iii) statutory reliefs under the Income Tax Act for qualifying foreign-source dividend income, subject to OECD anti-hybrid limitations. Hybrid-mismatch elections are not recognised; Gibraltar applies OECD-aligned transfer pricing and a general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Import Duty / Purchase Tax regime - no VAT or GST is levied in Gibraltar, and Gibraltar is outside both the UK VAT system and the EU VAT area. Separate excise duties apply to alcohol, tobacco and hydrocarbon oils.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A - no VAT/GST registration threshold exists because Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST. Mandatory registration: any entity importing or exporting goods through Gibraltar must be registered with Gibraltar Customs as an economic operator (EORI-equivalent) from the first transaction, with zero turnover threshold. Import duty is assessed per customs tariff line at ad valorem rates generally ranging from 0% to 18%, plus specific excise rates for alcohol, tobacco and fuels. Goods entered for customs warehousing, transit or onward export may suspend the duty point until final exit.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating does not exist in Gibraltar. Export relief from Gibraltar import duty/purchase tax applies instead where: (1) goods, including goods subjected to kitting, assembly and packaging in the Gibraltar fulfillment centre, physically depart Gibraltar customs territory to a foreign destination; (2) the export is declared by or on behalf of the Gibraltar customs-registered economic operator; (3) the goods are not released for free circulation or consumed in Gibraltar; (4) export movements are completed within the authorised time limits of the customs procedure, with an export-evidence benchmark of 180 days from duty-suspension entry or the declared export date; and (5) the operator holds full supporting transport, warehouse and payment documentation. Services supplied to non-Gibraltar customers are outside the scope of Gibraltar tax and require no zero-rating claim.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (electronic Single Administrative Document / full customs declaration referencing the economic-operator registration number)
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) or road consignment note (CMR) evidencing physical departure of the goods from Gibraltar customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT-credited payment evidence from the overseas buyer (due-diligence and audit benchmark)
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the Bermuda Exempted Company or its Gibraltar branch
  • Packing List and kitting bill of materials describing the value-added processing performed in the Gibraltar facility
  • Carrier and customs-broker appointment records, if a Gibraltar customs agent is engaged

No real-time fiscal e-invoicing, digital stamping, or government clearance API is mandated in Gibraltar. Tax invoices must satisfy the record-keeping and invoice-content requirements of the Income Tax Act and may be issued electronically (e.g., PDF or EDI). Customs declarations are lodged electronically with Gibraltar Customs through the approved customs declaration channel, and each consignment receives a customs release/clearance decision; duty payments are made through approved electronic banking channels. There is no continuous transaction-control or SAF-T reporting obligation.

Gibraltar Customs is empowered to conduct post-clearance audits, generally covering up to six years, of export declarations, warehouse records and duty-relief claims. False export declarations, fictitious export transactions, goods diverted back into the Gibraltar market, or forged customs documentation trigger: (i) immediate clawback of unpaid import duty and excise; (ii) default interest; (iii) civil penalties of up to 100% of the duty evaded, with higher amounts for aggravated or repeat conduct; (iv) forfeiture of goods; and (v) criminal liability for the company and responsible officers, including fines and imprisonment on indictment. The Commissioner of Income Tax may separately reassess Gibraltar corporation tax, deny reliefs, and apply the GAAR with penalties of up to 100% of tax understated. Although Gibraltar imposes no exchange-control deadline, failure to evidence foreign-currency repatriation within the 180-day benchmark is treated as a high-risk indicator in export-relief audits and can support duty clawback and penalty assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar has full de jure and de facto currency convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued at a fixed 1:1 peg to GBP and circulates interchangeably with Bank of England notes in Gibraltar. There are no foreign-exchange surrender obligations, no convertibility licensing, no repatriation restrictions, and no withholding tax on dividend, interest, or royalty outflows to non-resident shareholders. Profit remittance from a Gibraltar-operating branch or from a Gibraltar-resident trading vehicle is fully free; the Bermuda Exempted Company tax-exempt status further eliminates Gibraltar income-tax exposure on externally sourced trading income, preserving unobstructed dividend upstreaming.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender regime exists in Gibraltar or the United Kingdom. Export receipts may be held offshore in any currency without mandatory conversion to GBP/GIP. For ISIC 5210 third-party logistics and kitting services, invoicing may be denominated in GBP, EUR, or USD at commercial discretion. If the entity maintains a Gibraltar bank account and the underlying economic activity occurs in Gibraltar, no exchange-control filing is required; if UK establishment thresholds are triggered, HMRC currency-translation rules require annual accounts to be reported in GBP but impose no receipt conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for GBP anchor); Financial Services Commission (GFSC), Gibraltar, as statutory regulator of licensed banks under the Financial Services Act 2019; no standalone Gibraltar central bank or exchange-control authority exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High viability, subject to regulatory constraints. Gibraltar-licensed electronic money institutions are scarce; the practical route is a UK-domiciled FCA-authorized EMI operating under post-Brexit Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Amendment) (EU Exit) Regulations. Airwallex UK Limited is fully viable for GBP/EUR/USD virtual IBANs, mass disbursements to supplier beneficiaries, and automated reconciliation; Wise Platform offers similar B2B rails; Currenxie adds multi-currency custody. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder loans and equity injections route via SWIFT MT103 from the Bermuda bank or parent corporate account to the Gibraltar operating account. Recommended mandate: inject in GBP to eliminate currency mismatch. Use standard purpose code T1107 (Direct investment, equity capital) for equity or T1109 (Intercompany lending) for shareholder loans; businesses in Gibraltar are not subject to exchange-control documentation, but the receiving Gibraltar bank will require the underlying board resolution, a signed loan agreement (if debt), and a CRS/FATCA self-certification confirming that the transfer does not create undisclosed reportable income. For amounts above GBP 10,000, banks require the beneficial-owner CDD to match the remitter. Legal constraint: EMIs do not hold client money as banker and cannot offer overdrafts or commercial letters of credit; for LC-based trade settlement a fully licensed bank is required. The Bermuda Exempted Company must sign the EMI client agreement directly and complete Source of Funds (SoF) disclosure; fund flows must not create an unlicensed deposit-taking business in Gibraltar under the Financial Services Act 2019.
