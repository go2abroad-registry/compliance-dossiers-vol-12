Governing statute

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement. The Memorandum must declare authorized share capital (customarily GBP 1 to GBP 50,000) and at least one share (typically one Ordinary Share of GBP 1) must be allotted and fully paid on incorporation. To retain the Bermuda-model Exempted Company designation, an annual exempted-company certificate fee (currently GIP 250) must be settled with the Registry alongside the ex-territorial taxation concession claim.

Funding & unlock path: Not mandatory for a virtual registered office; if an operational back-office is established, the occupant must hold a fire-risk-assessment record accepted by the Gibraltar Fire and Rescue Service and a workplace clearance under the Health and Safety at Work Act, covering electrical safety, emergency egress, and structural load for any floor exceeding five employees.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), Ministry of Finance; official e-filing and registration portal: https://www.income-tax.gov.gi

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity (Bermuda Exempted Company) not tax-resident in Gibraltar must register for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number only if it derives Gibraltar-source income or establishes a permanent establishment. Submission to the Income Tax Office requires: (i) certified certificate of incorporation from the Bermuda Registrar of Companies; (ii) certificate of good standing; (iii) constitutional documents; (iv) details of the Gibraltar agent, if any; and (v) Form G1 individual/company particulars. Approval is typically issued within 14-21 business days. Non-resident status is evidenced by central management and control (CMC) remaining outside Gibraltar, supported by board minutes and emigration/corporate records.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box classification system. A Bermuda Exempted Company may elect, via submission of an annual corporation tax return, to be assessed as a non-resident company on a territorial source basis, thereby excluding foreign trading income. Alternatively, the entity may elect to be treated as Gibraltar tax-resident under the Companies (Taxation of Consequential Losses) Act if CMC is moved onshore; hybrid mismatch and transfer pricing adjustments under the Income Tax (Amendment) Act 2020 apply to cross-border structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar VAT-less system; no domestic VAT or GST exists. Indirect taxation is limited to HM Customs Gibraltar import and excise duties on physical goods entering Gibraltar. Cross-border B2B platform supplies of digital services are outside Gibraltar indirect-tax scope, though obligations may arise in the UK/EU customer jurisdiction.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - Gibraltar levies no VAT/GST and therefore has no turnover-based indirect tax registration threshold. A customs declarant/import registration with HM Customs Gibraltar is required only when physical goods are imported into Gibraltar.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable under Gibraltar law, as Gibraltar does not impose VAT, hence no 0% export rating exists. Exports of goods from Gibraltar are outside the charge to import duty and are declared under HM Customs Gibraltar outward-processing / full-export procedures. Services supplied by the Bermuda Exempted Company through its B2B marketplace are not subject to Gibraltar indirect tax, and corresponding income is taxable only if attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice

  • Bermuda Certificate of Good Standing (corporate existence evidence for counterparty due diligence)

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing validation, or digital stamping regime exists in Gibraltar. Invoices for B2B platform transactions may be issued freely; however, foreign-marketplace operators selling into UK/EU consumers or businesses must comply with the UK HMRC Making Tax Digital and EU/UK continuous transaction controls (CTC) or OSS regimes as applicable in the destination jurisdiction.

Gibraltar has no VAT clawback mechanism. However, false or fraudulent customs export declarations attract penalties of up to GBP 3,000 per declaration plus forfeiture of the goods under the Customs Ordinance. For income tax purposes, undocumented or artificial cross-border funding routed through the B2B platform may be recharacterised as Gibraltar-source profits and assessed at 12.5%, with interest and additional penalties of up to 100% of the tax due, pursuant to section 65 of the Income Tax Act 2010 and the Income Tax (Anti-Avoidance) Rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The GIP is fully convertible and freely transferable at a fixed 1:1 parity to GBP Sterling. No exchange control legislation is in force in Gibraltar; both current account and capital account transactions enjoy unrestricted convertibility. A Bermuda Exempted Company managed from or banking in Gibraltar may repatriate profits, declare dividends, repay shareholder loans, and effect intra-group capital movements without prior central bank or Treasury approval, and there are no currency surrender, deposit, or conversion mandates. The only practical hurdles are documentary: banks will require certified corporate resolutions, economic substance evidence, and clean UBO/Source of Funds profiles before processing high-value current account or capital account remittances, given Gibraltar's commitment to OECD transparency standards.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory export proceeds surrender rules do not apply in Gibraltar. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no timeline for repatriation of export receivables, and no obligation to sell foreign currency to a local authorised dealer. For a B2B cross-border marketplace operator, proceeds arising from platform sales in USD, EUR, GBP or other currencies may remain in offshore multi-currency settlement balances or be converted at commercial banking rates at the operator's discretion. Prudent treasury policy nonetheless recommends daily pooling of USD and EUR settlement receipts through the primary operational account to reduce FX drift and satisfy the bank's transaction rationale thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar does not maintain an independent central bank. The monetary anchor is the Bank of England through the Gibraltar pound (GIP) currency peg at par with GBP; the Government of Gibraltar issues banknotes under the Currency Notes and Coins Act 2012, and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory prudential regulator for all licensed banks and payment institutions operating in Gibraltar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Post-Brexit Gibraltar is outside the EEA payments passport; an EU/EEA EMI cannot passport automatically into Gibraltar. Non-Gibraltar digital EMIs (Airwallex, Wise Business, Currencycloud, Revolut Business) may still onboard a Gibraltar-incorporated or Gibraltar-managed B2B operator on a commercial non-banking basis, but only as settlement facilitation layers, not as primary custodian accounts. Legally, any EMI holding customer funds for the operation must hold a UK EMI authorisation or a Gibraltar Payment Services licence under the Financial Services Act 2019. For treasury integrity, the blueprint restricts EMIs to collection-side aggregation (e.g., Alibaba Pay merchant settlements) with daily sweeps to a tier-1 licensed bank, and prohibits the EMI from being used for capital escrow or hedge collateral custody.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: inbound equity and shareholder loan capital is transmitted by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 COV where interbank coverage is required) from the non-resident parent or UBO's bank to the Gibraltar bank's SWIFT BIC, utilising the Capital Escrow Account for the first injection and thereafter the Operational Account. Correspondent banking lines are HSBC, Deutsche Bank and Barclays; payment narrative must cite the company's Board Resolution authorising the capital increase and, where applicable, the Shareholder Loan Agreement reference. Purpose code in the SWIFT field 70 (Remittance Information) should state 'Equity capital contribution under internal group resolution' or 'Direct investment capital' in line with BPM6 classification. No capital control approval is required in Gibraltar, but the receiving bank will verify the UETR, the source-of-funds declaration for the wiring account, and a certified copy of the executed corporate resolutions before funds are released from escrow to the trading account. For Alibaba_Pay-integrated escrow settlements, inbound merchant funds are swept as pooled MT103 transfers in EUR or GBP from Alipay's European partner EMI, accompanied by a transaction-level settlement report and the platform's merchant ID; the Gibraltar bank treats these sweeps as third-party commercial settlements and requires standing merchant-authorisation documentation to be filed at account opening.Clearing rails for trade settlements are layered by value and risk: (i) SWIFT MT103 direct transfers for supplier invoices up to USD 50,000 or equivalent, executed via the bank's host-to-host channel with upload of a CSV payment file carrying supplier account details and ISIC 4791 B2B reference codes; (ii) irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit confirmed by the beneficiary's bank under UCP600 rules for high-value purchase orders exceeding USD 50,000, with documents drawn at sight and negotiated against the marketplace order number; (iii) documentary collection (D/P and D/A) as a secondary rail for mid-value trade with quota-controlled jurisdictions; and (iv) Alibaba_Pay collection rails used solely on the demand aggregation side, never for supplier disbursement, to preserve a clean correspondent-banking audit trail. The treasury policy fixes FX conversion at the London 16:00 GMT fixing for all same-day invoices and operates a notional netting ledger internally, while executing each legal payout on a gross basis through the licensed bank account to guarantee traceability and regulatory defensibility.Deliverable outright FX forwards in USD/EUR against GBP/GIP with tenors up to 12 months, executed with Banco Sabadell (Gibraltar Branch) or NatWest International under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement, permissible without GFSC product intervention because the GIP is fully deliverable at par with GBP.Vanilla FX options (both calls and puts) on EUR/USD and GBP/USD pairs for hedging procurement cost-overrun exposure, requiring the bank's FX Derivatives Execution Agreement and an appropriately documented board derivative authorisation resolution.Cross-currency swaps (mark-to-market) covering USD floating-rate working capital borrowings versus EUR receivable streams, with legal netting under bilateral ISDA/CSA collateral arrangements with an authorised Gibraltar or London counterparty.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in secondary exotic currencies (such as INR, IDR, and BRL) sourced through a London-based non-bank liquidity provider where the underlying B2B buyer settlement jurisdictions lack deep local FX forward markets; these NDFs are permissible as hedge-only instruments and are prohibited from speculative use under the treasury policy.
