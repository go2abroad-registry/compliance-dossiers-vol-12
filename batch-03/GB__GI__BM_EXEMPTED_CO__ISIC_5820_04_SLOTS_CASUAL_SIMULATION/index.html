Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The Bermuda Exempted Company may make the statutory election to be treated as an exempted undertaking under the Bermuda Companies Act and may apply for a Tax Exemption Certificate guaranteeing the absence of Bermuda income tax for up to fifty years. Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime, but the company may elect to be treated as a Gibraltar Qualifying Company if it satisfies statutory substance conditions, or as a non-resident company if central management and control is exercised outside Gibraltar. Hybrid mismatch elections are constrained under the OECD BEPS Action 2 rules as reflected in Gibraltar's Income Tax Act, which disallows any unintended double non-taxation outcome.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar does not impose VAT or GST; indirect taxation is limited to customs and excise duties on physical imports. Digital supplies of simulated social casino and virtual slots games via Apple IAP and Google Play Billing are outside any Gibraltar turnover tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in Gibraltar because no VAT or GST is in force. If UK VAT applies because the entity is the supplier of record for digital services to UK consumers, HMRC requires compulsory registration regardless of turnover for non-established suppliers; the domestic GBP 85,000 threshold is not a relevant exemption for a non-UK taxable person.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Digital services cannot be zero-rated merely because the recipient or download occurs abroad. Under UK VAT rules, B2C digital supplies to UK consumers are standard-rated at 20%; B2B digital supplies to a customer outside the UK are outside the scope or zero-rated where the supplier retains conclusive evidence of the customer's business status and location. Physical goods exported from the UK or Gibraltar to a third-country destination may be zero-rated only when the exporter holds the customs export declaration, transport documentation, and proof of removal from the fiscal territory within the statutory period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document or electronic export declaration) endorsed by HM Customs Gibraltar or HMRC
  • Commercial invoice and packing list stating the exact value and nature of the goods or digital service in the settlement currency
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR haulier document, or equivalent third-party transport proof evidencing export to a destination outside the fiscal territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit advice showing transfer of export proceeds to a Gibraltar-licensed EMI bank account within 180 days
  • For digital services: IP and geolocation evidence, customer VAT registration where applicable, and Apple/Google app-store transaction settlement ledger proving the place of supply

Gibraltar has no real-time electronic fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate for digital services. Invoices may be issued in ordinary commercial form. If the company becomes UK VAT-registered, HMRC Making Tax Digital requires returns to be filed via API-compatible software, but no invoice-level clearance is imposed. Apple and Google transaction receipts, IAP validation records, and payment provider settlement files constitute acceptable electronic fiscal evidence.

Zero-rating or outside-the-scope treatment is denied ab initio where the mandatory export evidence bundle is incomplete. The tax authority may assess underdeclared VAT retrospectively for four years, extended to twenty years in the case of deliberate error, and impose a civil penalty of up to 100% of the VAT evaded. Where fraudulent documentation is submitted, criminal penalties including imprisonment and unlimited fines may apply under UK VATA 1994 and the Gibraltar Criminal Justice Ordinance; personal liability may extend to directors where the Bermuda Exempted Company is dissolved.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full unrestricted convertibility without exchange controls. The United Kingdom and Gibraltar impose no current-account or capital-account restrictions; GIP and GBP circulate interchangeably within Gibraltar and USD, EUR, and other major currencies are freely bankable and transferable. Profit remittance (dividends, interest, royalties, management fees) from the Bermuda Exempted Company attracts zero Gibraltar withholding tax and requires no convertibility or remittance permit; the only practical frictions are GFSC-mandated AML/CFT verifications, source-of-funds documentation, and proof that beneficial ownership is transparent across the Bermuda, Gibraltar, and UK sovereignty chain.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or mandatory repatriation obligations exist. The UK Exchange Control Act 1947 was repealed for both the UK and Gibraltar, and Gibraltar has enacted no successor surrender regime. Export receipts (Apple and Google app-store net settlements, licensing revenues, and content royalties) may be retained in GIP, GBP, USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency, held onshore or offshore, and applied to intercompany loans or expenses without conversion ratios, registration, or statutory time limits, subject only to general GFSC AML/CFT record-keeping duties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England as monetary-policy anchor for the Gibraltar pound (GIP) under a fixed 1:1 GBP parity; Government of Gibraltar Currency Commission as sole GIP note and coin issuer; Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as statutory banking and AML/CFT supervisor under the Financial Services Act 2019 and Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) as home-state regulator of the Bermuda Exempted Company for Certificate of Exempted Status, Beneficial Ownership, and Economic Substance filings.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium with legal constraints. A GFSC-licensed Payment Institution or Electronic Money Institution under the Financial Services (Payment Services) Act 2019 is a legal and viable supplementary treasury rail in Gibraltar. However, a foreign (UK FCA-authorised, EU PSD2, or offshore) EMI cannot passport into Gibraltar post-Brexit and must itself obtain GFSC authorisation; in practice most digital-first EMIs (UK or Malta neobanking platforms) reject Bermuda-Exempted-Company clients lacking local Gibraltarian management, a physical office, and a full UBO interview. Recommended architecture: the tier-1 Gibraltar bank remains the principal clearing and liquidity nexus, with a GFSC-licensed EMI added only for segregated low-value inbound app-store settlements and domestic working-capital disbursements; unlicensed or non-passported offshore EMIs should be avoided entirely at this medium-risk-enhanced tier.

Recommended architecture: the tier-1 Gibraltar bank remains the principal clearing and liquidity nexus, with a GFSC-licensed EMI added only for segregated low-value inbound app-store settlements and domestic working-capital disbursements; unlicensed or non-passported offshore EMIs should be avoided entirely at this medium-risk-enhanced tier.
