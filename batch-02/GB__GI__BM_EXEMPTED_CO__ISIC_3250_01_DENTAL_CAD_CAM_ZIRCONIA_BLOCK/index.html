Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a private exempt company. Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For the Bermuda Exempted Company, the statutory route is: (1) incorporate and obtain the Bermuda Exempted Company number and Exemption Certificate from the Bermuda Registrar of Companies; Bermuda does not require income-tax registration because it levies no corporate income tax and issues no TIN. (2) If the company undertakes business through a place of business in Gibraltar, file as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry, obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number from the Income Tax Office, and obtain a Gibraltar EORI number from the Customs & Tax Department for customs declarations. (3) If a UK permanent establishment arises, register with HMRC for corporation tax (CT41G) and obtain a UK UTR; no UK registration is needed for the Bermuda-exempt offshore principal if no UK nexus exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The Bermuda tax exemption is statutory and automatic under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966, not an annual election. Gibraltar has no check-the-box or transparent pass-through election; a Gibraltar-tax-resident company is an entity-level taxpayer. Group loss relief/surrender elections may be available under Gibraltar tax law. UK/Gibraltar hybrid mismatch rules can disallow deductions or recharacterise payments involving a hybrid/transparent foreign entity, but no taxpayer election overrides those rules. Continuance/redomiciliation into Gibraltar is possible under the Companies Act but extinguishes the original Bermuda exempted status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST; Gibraltar imposes non-VAT customs and excise duties under the Customs Duty Ordinance and related excise legislation.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - Gibraltar has no VAT/GST system and therefore no turnover threshold for VAT registration. Importers/exporters must obtain an EORI number and customs registration irrespective of turnover. The local accounting currency is the Gibraltar pound (GIP), pegged at par with GBP.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT export zero-rating is not applicable because Gibraltar does not levy VAT. For Gibraltar customs purposes, export duty relief or a customs duty drawback may be claimed when the dental zirconia blocks are definitively exported from Gibraltar to a third-country customs territory, in the same condition or after authorised processing, provided the exporter is established/registered in Gibraltar, holds a valid EORI, and evidence shows physical departure, foreign consignee receipt, and receipt of export consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar customs export declaration or equivalent electronic declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin for the zirconia blocks
  • Purchase order/contract and exporter's EORI endorsement

Gibraltar does not impose mandatory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital invoice stamping. Customs entries are submitted electronically to the Customs & Tax Department using approved customs declaration software; fiscal invoices must be retained for audit and produced on statutory demand. Commercial e-invoicing is permitted but has no statutory pre-clearance effect.

The customs and tax authorities may reopen export declarations, test the underlying physical exports, verify the destination consignee, and confirm that FX proceeds were received within the 180-day deadline. A false, inflated, circular, or reversed remittance, or missing/inconsistent shipping documentation, invalidates the duty relief/drawback and up to 100% of the relief is clawed back with statutory interest. Civil penalties, forfeiture of goods, criminal prosecution for tax evasion/fraud, disqualification of directors, and money-laundering sanctions may also be imposed on responsible persons.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The GIP is fully and freely convertible into GBP at par with no restrictions on current account or capital account transactions. Direct profit remittance from a Gibraltar-registered entity to foreign shareholders is unrestricted subject to completion of standard corporate records, solvency verification, and, where relevant, compliance with the Gibraltar Companies Act and anti-money laundering obligations. There are no separate exchange-control approvals or central bank permits required for dividend distributions or capital repatriation. Transfer pricing and economic substance reporting obligations remain the only practical compliance thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in any currency, held in offshore accounts, or converted freely. Financial institutions, however, may require full underlying trade documentation and invoice records to meet UK/Gibraltar anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing regulations for clearing and settlement of export receipts.

Central bank supervision: Financial Services Commission of Gibraltar (FSC) acting as the integrated financial regulator and monetary authority for note issue; the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is managed under a currency board system pegged at 1:1 to the British pound sterling (GBP) with ultimate monetary policy oversight in coordination with the Bank of England for sterling-denominated clearing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI services are viable only to a Limited extent where the provider holds a full licence from Gibraltar FSC under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act or an equivalent EEA/UK FCA-authorised EMI passporting into Gibraltar per existing transitional arrangements. Purely offshore, unregulated EMIs are functionally prohibited and cannot access the Gibraltar clearing system or hold client funds. Feasibility rating: Low for non-regulated offshore EMIs; Moderate for FSC or FCA licenced EMIs with a Gibraltar payment institution licence.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar has no statutory threshold expressed in local currency for Local File / Master File preparation. However, the tax authority expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for related-party transactions exceeding approximately GBP 10,000,000 in aggregate annual turnover. All related party transactions must comply with the arm’s length principle under the Income Tax Act and OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines.

Economic substance scope: The Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019 require Gibraltar tax-resident companies carrying on relevant activities (including distribution, service, financing, holding, and IP activities) to maintain economic substance in Gibraltar: board meetings, CIGA performed locally, proportionate premises and employees. Annual ESR reporting (Form ES-1) is mandatory. A Bermuda exempted company with management and control in Gibraltar is within scope. Pure equity holding entities benefit from reduced substance requirements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Failure to File

Statutory sanction: Summary conviction: fine up to GBP 5,000 and imprisonment up to 6 months. Indictment: fine up to GBP 20,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years. Additional late-filing penalties apply; assessments may be extended up to 20 years for fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable where the corporate veil is pierced for fraud; civil claims for restitution, disqualification up to 15 years, travel bans, passport surrender, and imprisonment apply.
Economic Substance Non-Compliance (ESR)

Statutory sanction: First failure: GBP 10,000 penalty. Second failure within 12 months: GBP 40,000 or 10% of relevant income (whichever is greater); further non-compliance may lead to striking off and assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable where non-compliance is attributable to their neglect; disqualification and personal penalty orders may follow.
Transfer Pricing Understatement and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Commissioner may adjust profits to arm's length, impose interest and penalties up to 100% of undercharged tax; GAAR may counteract artificial arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve fraudulent transfer pricing may face civil restitution, confiscation orders, and criminal liability for cheating the public revenue.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: AML failures: imprisonment up to 5 years and unlimited fines under Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; false register statements: fines up to GBP 10,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years; restrictions on banks and company winding up.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may suffer asset freezing, confiscation, travel restrictions, disqualification, and personal civil liability.
Tax Residence Fraud (Bermuda / Gibraltar Split)

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar may reassess all open years (up to 20 years in fraud cases) plus penalties; Bermuda may strike off the exempted company for unpaid fees, resulting in asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid Bermuda fees and for debts incurred after striking off, and may be subject to Gibraltar court judgments.
Sanctions and DTA Abusive Claim

Statutory sanction: Rejection of treaty claim, recovery of underpaid WHT plus interest, penalties up to 100% of unreported tax, and application of Gibraltar GAAR.

Director personal liability: Directors face civil misrepresentation claims, disqualification, and possible extradition for deliberate fraudulent treaty claims.
