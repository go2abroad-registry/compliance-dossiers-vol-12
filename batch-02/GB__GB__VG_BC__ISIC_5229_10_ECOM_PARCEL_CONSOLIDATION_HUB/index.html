Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registry of Corporate Affairs, Financial Services Commission, Government of the British Virgin Islands, Road Town, Tortola, BVI; and Companies House, Crown Way, Cardiff, CF14 3UZ, United Kingdom for the UK establishment registration.

Governing statute

BVI Business Companies Act, 2004 (as amended), ss. 2, 5, 7, 8, 9, 25, 43, 45; BVI Companies Regulations, 2005 (as amended); and for the UK branch: Part 34 Companies Act 2006 and the Overseas Companies Regulations 2009 (SI 2009/1801), regs. 3–7, 52–63, filing Form OS IN01.

Formation / constitutional filings

For the BVI BC: Memorandum and Articles of Association, Form 1 (Application for Incorporation of a BVI Business Company), Consent of Registered Agent, Declaration of Compliance. For the UK: Form OS IN01 (Registration of Particulars of a UK Establishment) and supporting certified copies.

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under the BVI Business Companies Act, 2004; no escrow account obligation. Standard procedure: issue and allot one ordinary share of no par value (deemed value US$1) to the subscriber; UK establishment registration imposes no separate paid-up capital requirement.

Funding & unlock path: Before physical operation, the hub must satisfy the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 (full fire risk assessment and, if required, a fire safety certificate), Building Regulations 2010 Approved Document B (structural fire safety), and the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 with a documented workplace risk assessment. A local building control completion certificate is required for newly constructed sorting areas or racking installations, and occupational clearance for driving/dispatch areas under the Workplace (Health, Safety and Welfare) Regulations 1992.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC); online portal: GOV.UK (https://www.gov.uk/topic/business-tax); VAT services via HMRC online services.

E-government filing portal

The foreign BVI Business Company (BC) must first determine whether it carries on any taxable business activities in the United Kingdom. If the BC has a UK permanent establishment, it must register for Corporation Tax with HMRC using forms CT41G/CT600 procedures and obtain a Company Tax Reference and Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR). For VAT, the BC must register online through the GOV.UK VAT registration portal using form VAT1 and a Government Gateway account; HMRC will issue a UK VAT Registration Number. If importing or exporting goods, the BC must also obtain a UK EORI number from HMRC. If the BC employs staff in the UK, it must register as an employer for PAYE, National Insurance, and, where applicable, the Apprenticeship Levy. The BC should not rely on its BVI Tax Exempt status for UK purposes; UK tax obligations arise from UK-source activities and permanent establishment exposure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone under BVI BC law; however, for UK direct tax purposes, a non-resident company is not automatically exempt. If the BC carries on a trade in the UK through a permanent establishment, the attributable profits are subject to Entity_Level_Taxation under the Corporation Tax Act 2010. The BC is not treated as a pass-through entity for UK tax purposes.

Available election forms

The United Kingdom does not operate a check-the-box election system. Available elections and mechanisms include: optional VAT registration for businesses below the threshold; VAT group registration under section 43 of the Value Added Tax Act 1994; option to tax land and buildings for VAT; election to disregard a UK permanent establishment in certain double taxation treaty contexts; and claims under double taxation agreements for relief from UK withholding tax. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules apply, and no elective repatriation regime exists for foreign companies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 of taxable turnover in a rolling 12-month period; non-established businesses must register from their first taxable supply in the UK and may be required to appoint a tax representative. Businesses making only zero-rated supplies may apply for registration or, in limited cases, be exempt from registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, zero-rating is available where the goods are exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK, either through direct export or via a UK freight-forwarder, and the exporter retains sufficient commercial and transport evidence within the prescribed VAT Notice 703 time limits. For services, zero-rating is available where the place of supply is outside the UK under the general business-to-business rule, or where the services are listed in the Place of Supply of Services Order 1992 and supplied to an overseas non-business customer. A BVI offshore customer will qualify only if the place of supply rules and genuine overseas receipt requirements are satisfied.

  • EORI number and Customs Export Declaration (electronic declaration submitted through the UK National Export System)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/remittance confirmation showing receipt of export consideration
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Freight or forwarding documentation proving physical departure from the UK
  • Copy of contract, purchase order, and proof of the overseas customer's identity and location

The UK does not operate real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory digital stamping, or continuous transaction control clearance. VAT-registered businesses are required to maintain digital records and file VAT returns using HMRC Making Tax Digital (MTD)-compatible software. Electronic invoices are accepted if authentic, accurate, and readable, but there is no statutory obligation to use a specific e-invoicing format or to obtain pre-clearance from HMRC.

HMRC may reject zero-rating where export evidence is incomplete, contradictory, or untimely, and may assess the unpaid VAT with statutory interest. Civil penalties can reach up to 100% of the potential lost revenue for deliberate and concealed inaccuracies, with lower penalties for careless errors. Failure-to-notify penalties apply when the registration obligation is missed. In cases of fraudulent export VAT evasion, criminal prosecution under the Value Added Tax Act 1994 and director-level liability may follow. HMRC assessment time limits can extend to 20 years for deliberately concealed evasion, and retrospective clawback generally includes interest from the date the VAT should have been accounted for.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The UK maintains full currency convertibility for both current-account and capital-account transactions under the Exchange Control Act 1947, which was effectively repealed in 1979. No prior approval is required for capital injections, dividend remittances, intercompany loan repayments, or trade-related transfers. Profit remittance hurdles arise only from general AML/KYC, financial sanctions screening, and tax compliance documentation. For a BVI Business Company, there are no UK-specific FX restrictions; however, the receiving UK bank will require source-of-funds evidence and tax classification forms (W-8BEN-E, CRS self-certification as applicable).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The UK imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion to GBP, and no statutory timeline or minimum conversion ratio. A UK-connected exporter or clearing hub may retain export receipts in foreign currency in any bank account or EMI multi-currency account and may repatriate or disburse them freely, subject only to financial sanctions compliance and standard transaction monitoring by the account provider.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary and financial stability); HM Treasury via the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) for restrictive measures; Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) for AML/CTF supervision of banks and e-money institutions under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017. There is no dedicated foreign-exchange control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High viability for Airwallex, Wise Business, Currencycloud (Visa), and Revolut Business, each authorized or registered as an e-money institution (EMI) by the FCA under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011. These platforms allow a BVI BC to open multi-currency accounts entirely remotely, with virtual IBANs, local clearing in major currency corridors, and SWIFT MT103 correspondence. Legal constraints: an EMI is not a UK bank and funds deposited are not FSCS-protected; the EMI must segregate client funds in safeguarding accounts; BVI BC accounts are accepted only after full UBO verification, sanctions screening, and commercial rationale review. There is no UK physical-presence requirement for EMI onboarding.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% main rate; 19% small profits rate (profits ≤ £50,000); marginal relief between £50,000 and £250,000

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% under UK domestic law — no UK withholding tax applies to dividends paid by a UK tax-resident company; non-resident shareholders are outside UK income tax on distributions. No special UK dividend WHT liability arises for BVI shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: UK does not require automatic filing of Local File/Master File below €750m consolidated group turnover (≈ £645m). For groups above that threshold, OECD-aligned Master File/Local File and Country-by-Country report must be prepared/fileable to HMRC within 12 months of year-end; HMRC may request TP documentation within 30 days in all cases, and contemporaneous arm's-length evidence is required to protect against penalties.

Economic substance scope: A UK tax-resident VG_BC (managed and controlled in the UK) is fully subject to UK corporation tax on worldwide profits and is outside BVI ESR only if it provides the BVI Registry with evidence of foreign tax residence and files the annual economic substance return online. If the BVI BC is not tax-resident elsewhere and carries on a relevant activity (potentially a distribution/service centre for a parcel hub), BVI Economic Substance Regulations require economic presence, employees, expenditure and management in the BVI; no comprehensive UK-BVI DTA exists to prevent dual filing exposure. The UK anti-avoidance substance test under GAAR may also disregard any artificial arrangement where management is formally in BVI but commercially controlled from the UK.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Cheating the Public Revenue

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 200% of the tax concerned under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007; interest on unpaid tax; HMRC criminal prosecution for promoting cheating of the public revenue (max 7 years' imprisonment and unlimited fine on indictment).

Director personal liability: Directors who personally direct or sign the false return may be pursued for civil restitution; the corporate veil may be pierced where the company is a facade. Directors face Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986 orders (up to 15 years disqualification), freezing orders, travel bans and up to 7 years' imprisonment.
Failure to Notify UK Tax Residence / PE

Statutory sanction: Failure-to-notify penalty under TMA 1970 s.7: up to 100% of the tax that would have been due on offshore assets/UK profits or the maximum inaccuracy penalty; back-duty assessments up to 20 years where careless/deliberate; daily penalties after HMRC direction.

Director personal liability: Non-corporate veil-piercing: a director knowingly involved in the failure can be jointly liable under TMA s.28A? (procedure) and may be named in tribunal proceedings; civil recovery and restraint orders under POCA can include freezing of personal assets; serious fraud prosecutions carry custodial sentences.
Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalisation Manipulation

Statutory sanction: HMRC can adjust profits under TIOPA 2010 Part 4 to the arm's length result; penalties under Schedule 24 FA 2007 from 30% to 200% of the additional tax; interest; possible corporate interest restriction disallowance and CFC action if BVI intermediate retains passive income.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve fraudulent pricing may be hit with civil contribution claims from shareholders/creditors; in liquidation, transactions at an undervalue or preference claims can require directors to restore amounts under Insolvency Act 1986 (ss.238/239/214). HMRC may petition to wind up the company; travel bans can be ordered to enforce examination.
