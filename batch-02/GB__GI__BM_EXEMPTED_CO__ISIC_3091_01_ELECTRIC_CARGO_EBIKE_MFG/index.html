Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Bermuda exempted company. One ordinary share of USD 1.00 (or BMD 1.00) must be issued and fully or partly paid to the founding subscriber; no escrow account or capital-blocking requirement is imposed. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, HM Government of Gibraltar (Income Tax Office); online portal: Gibraltar Taxation e-Services (www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation)

E-government filing portal

For the Bermuda Exempted Company (foreign entity) establishing a presence in Gibraltar: (1) register as an oversea company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies (Companies House) within one month of establishing a place of business; (2) within 30 days of commencing trade, submit Form TR-1 (Taxpayer Registration) to the Income Tax Office, enclosing certified certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, register of directors and shareholders, registered office confirmation and a Gibraltar tax agent authorisation; (3) obtain the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN), which is the statutory TIN for all corporate tax filings, customs declarations and EU/OECD exchange-of-information purposes; (4) for offshore tax status, file the Qualifying Company application under s.24A of the Income Tax Act 2010, supported by a substance statement; approval is conditional on non-resident beneficial ownership, absence of trading with Gibraltar-resident persons, and continued compliance with the Economic Substance requirements.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — non-resident offshore exempted company; chargeable to Gibraltar income tax on a territorial basis only (income arising in or derived from Gibraltar); foreign-source trading profits are exempt upon Qualifying Company (s.24A) approval; no capital gains tax, no inheritance tax, no wealth tax and no withholding tax on dividends, interest or royalties paid to non-residents. — For the Bermuda Exempted Company (foreign entity) establishing a presence in Gibraltar: (1) register as an oversea company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies (Companies House) within one month of establishing a place of business; (2) within 30 days of commencing trade, submit Form TR-1 (Taxpayer Registration) to the Income Tax Office, enclosing certified certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, register of directors and shareholders, registered office confirmation and a Gibraltar tax agent authorisation; (3) obtain the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN), which is the statutory TIN for all corporate tax filings, customs declarations and EU/OECD exchange-of-information purposes; (4) for offshore tax status, file the Qualifying Company application under s.24A of the Income Tax Act 2010, supported by a substance statement; approval is conditional on non-resident beneficial ownership, absence of trading with Gibraltar-resident persons, and continued compliance with the Economic Substance requirements.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — non-resident offshore exempted company; chargeable to Gibraltar income tax on a territorial basis only (income arising in or derived from Gibraltar); foreign-source trading profits are exempt upon Qualifying Company (s.24A) approval; no capital gains tax, no inheritance tax, no wealth tax and no withholding tax on dividends, interest or royalties paid to non-residents.

Available election forms

Qualifying Company election (s.24A, Income Tax Act 2010) — irrevocable election for full exemption of foreign-source income; requires non-resident beneficial ownership, no trade with Gibraltar-resident persons, and satisfactory economic substance in Gibraltar.; Participation exemption election — election to exempt qualifying dividends and capital gains arising from a 10% or greater shareholding in a non-resident subsidiary that is subject to a tax of at least 12.5% in its state of residence (anti-hybrid aligned).; Hybrid mismatch alignment election under the Income Tax (Hybrid Mismatch Rules) — optional election to align the Gibraltar tax classification of the entity with that of the counterparty jurisdiction in order to neutralise deduction-without-inclusion outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Purchase Tax (import consumption duty) — Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and applies no VAT, GST or general sales tax. Purchase Tax under the Purchase Tax Act is levied at importation on a closed list of designated goods (alcohol, tobacco, petroleum, motor vehicles and specified luxuries). Electric cargo bicycles, e-bike components and manufacturing inputs are not listed and are outside the scope of the tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no turnover-based registration threshold. The importer must be registered with HM Customs Gibraltar and must file an import entry/declaration for each consignment; Purchase Tax is a transaction-based levy arising on each importation regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exemption equivalent to zero-rating applies because Gibraltar levies no VAT. (a) Goods manufactured in Gibraltar (the cargo e-bikes) and exported outside Gibraltar are outside the scope of Purchase Tax, provided physical export is evidenced and the goods are not diverted to local consumption; (b) imported goods re-exported within three months qualify for Purchase Tax relief under customs warehousing/transit procedures. Sales to the UK and EU are third-country exports: the foreign buyer accounts for import VAT or reverse charge in the destination state, and the Gibraltar seller retains transport, customs and payment documentation as the condition of exemption.

  • Customs Export Declaration — Gibraltar Customs export entry (Integrated Tariff / C88 equivalent) containing the unique consignment reference and the exporter TRN.
  • Bill of Lading (original set, consigned to the foreign buyer) or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Gibraltar and delivery at destination.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice or foreign-exchange repatriation certificate retained in the economic-substance and transfer-pricing file (not a statutory customs condition in Gibraltar, but required as evidence of commercial reality for the s.24A exemption).

None — Gibraltar imposes no electronic invoicing mandate, no real-time fiscal clearance API and no digital stamping. Invoices must comply with Income Tax Act record-keeping rules (six-year retention). Customs declarations are processed through the Gibraltar Integrated Tariff system on a pre-clearance/post-clearance model; no machine-readable invoice clearance is required.

HM Customs Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office jointly audit export substantiation and Qualifying Company files. Deficient or fraudulent export evidence triggers assessment of Purchase Tax as if the goods had been consumed locally, with interest from the date of importation; penalties of treble the duty or GBP 500 (whichever is greater), forfeiture of goods and criminal liability (imprisonment up to two years) apply to deliberate misdeclaration. Qualifying Company abuse (trading with Gibraltar residents without Commissioner approval, or failure of economic substance) causes retrospective revocation of the s.24A exemption, assessment on worldwide profits at 12.5% for up to six years, late-payment interest and penalties up to 100% of the tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account. GIP is freely convertible into GBP at 1:1 (no margin, no quota) and into EUR/USD through the bank's own FX desk; there is no statutory limit on foreign-currency account balances, no import/export licensing for funds, and no withholding tax on dividend, interest, royalty or management-fee payments remitted by a Gibraltar company to its Bermuda parent. Profit-remittance hurdles are limited to accounting and tax formalities: (i) the company must file audited annual financial statements and a tax return, or an Economic Substance return under the Economic Substance (Companies and Partnerships) Act 2020 where the company is tax-resident; (ii) dividends declared must comply with the Companies Act 2014 solvency test; and (iii) the remitting bank will ask for a board resolution declaring the dividend, a FATCA/CRS self-certification, and confirmation that no Gibraltar tax deduction is required because Gibraltar levies no withholding tax on outbound dividends. Capital-account injections from Bermuda are free of prior approval but are subject to post-fact compliance screening by the receiving bank for source-of-funds evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Gibraltar maintains no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory time window for the repatriation of export receipts. Proceeds from EU, UK and third-country sales of electric cargo bicycles (HS 8711.60) may be retained indefinitely in any currency in a GFSC-licensed bank account. The only reporting duties of a Gibraltar exporter are: (i) annual economic substance and tax reporting to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office; (ii) Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit (GFIU) suspicious-transaction reports under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 for high-value or unusual trade-payment patterns; and (iii) UK/Gibraltar Common Foreign Security Policy (CFSP) sanctions screening of all counterparties and their banks. Documentary evidence of each export sale (commercial invoice, packing list, proof of dispatch, ocean/air waybill) must be retained for six years to satisfy GFSC conduct rules and any trade-finance audit.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory regulator and licensing authority in Gibraltar for credit institutions, investment firms, insurers and payment-service providers. Gibraltar has no independent central bank; the currency, the Gibraltar pound (GIP), is issued by the Commissioner of Currency (Government of Gibraltar) under the Currency Notes Act 1934 as sterling-linked notes (GIP 1 = GBP 1, fully backed by Bank of England sterling reserve holdings). Monetary stability derives from the Bank of England because GIP is pegged at par to GBP and sterling is legal tender in Gibraltar; the GFSC carries out prudential supervision of Gibraltar-licensed banks pursuant to the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Banking Act 2017, with no exchange-control restrictions. Because the UK Exchange Control Act 1947 is permanently suspended, the GFSC exercises no currency-rationing, no capital-repatriation approval and no FX-transaction registration function.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: RESTRICTED-MEDIUM. UK FCA-authorised e-money institutions (Wise Plc, Revolut Ltd) can on-board a Gibraltar-registered corporate for payroll, operating expenses and corporate cards, but cannot lawfully hold the capital escrow or serve as the primary settlement rail because (i) post-Brexit, the UK-EMI cross-border licence does not passport into Gibraltar; the EMI must rely on reverse solicitation or obtain its own GFSC E-Money licence under the Financial Services Act 2019; (ii) Gibraltar banks' own AML rules treat a CDD package built only on an EMI-issued IBAN as materially weaker; and (iii) trade-centric counterparties (LC advising banks in China and India, EU distributors) reject payment orders that do not arrive as SWIFT MT103 from a BIC-identified credit institution. Recommended use: expense cards and payroll under GBP 10,000 per transaction. Prohibited use: LC cover, supplier down payments above GBP 25,000, tax payments, and the Capital Escrow Account.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% — flat corporate income tax rate on chargeable profits under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended), applied identically to resident and non-resident companies that carry on a trade or derive income sourced in Gibraltar.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar does not impose withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar company to non-resident shareholders, whether final, interim or in-kind distributions. No deduction at source, advance corporation tax or qualifying holding period applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 (approximately GBP 1,000,000) aggregate value of controlled transactions per chargeable period triggers mandatory Local File preparation. A combined Master File and Local File is required where Gibraltar-resident turnover exceeds GIP 20,000,000. Country-by-Country reporting applies to multinational groups whose consolidated group revenue is at least EUR 750,000,000, with the Gibraltar constituent entity designated as the filing entity or surrogate filer.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies a mandatory economic substance regime aligned with OECD and EU standards, enacted through the Income Tax Act 2010 and the Gibraltar Economic Substance (Income Tax) Regulations 2019 (GRES). A company conducting a relevant activity — banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, distribution, service centre, or pure equity holding — must demonstrate: (i) direction and management in Gibraltar evidenced by board meetings held in Gibraltar, quorum, minutes and substantive decision-making; (ii) physical premises proportionate to activity; (iii) full-time employees commensurate with core income-generating activities; and (iv) performance of core income-generating activities in Gibraltar. A pure equity holding company benefits from a reduced substance test: registered office, one director, one shareholder, and timely filing of accounts and returns. An exempted-status manufacturing company claiming non-residence must evidence at least one substantive CIGA in Gibraltar (for example, procurement, assembly supervision, quality control or contract manufacturing management); failure to do so creates UK/Gibraltar tax-residency risk under the management-and-control test and exposes the entity to reassessment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing (Income Tax Act 2010; Crimes Act 2011, Part 16)

Statutory sanction: Civil: tax-geared penalty of up to 100% of the understated tax, statutory interest, and best-of-judgment assessments. Criminal: fraudulent evasion of income tax carries up to 7 years' imprisonment; general fraud under the Crimes Act 2011 carries up to 14 years' imprisonment and/or an unlimited fine; forfeiture of assets derived from evasion is ordered under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil where the exempted company is a mere façade or instrumentality of fraud; directors are jointly and severally liable for restitution of evaded tax and civil penalties, subject to disqualification orders of up to 15 years under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014, travel restraint orders pending criminal trial, confiscation and Serious Crime Prevention Orders, and imprisonment on conviction.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Non-Compliance (GRES Regulations 2019; Income Tax Act 2010)

Statutory sanction: Reassessment at 12.5% with attribution of deemed Gibraltar-source profit; administrative economic substance penalties of up to GIP 100,000 per period; forced registration as a Gibraltar tax-resident company; strike-off and dissolution under the Companies Act 2014; enhanced information exchange under Gibraltar's Tax Information Exchange Agreements; and referral of the file to the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit for money-laundering review.

Director personal liability: Directors may be ordered personally to discharge the company's reassessed tax and penalties where they knowingly permitted the substance failure. Directors face disqualification for up to 15 years, personal liability for fraudulent trading under the Companies Act 2014 (civil liability for the full amount of debts incurred during the substance-deficient period), and, in egregious cases, travel bans pending criminal investigation for tax evasion.
Anti-Money Laundering and Financial Crime (Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; Criminal Justice (Money Laundering) Regulations 2016)

Statutory sanction: On indictment: imprisonment for up to 14 years and/or an unlimited fine; confiscation of the proceeds under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; civil forfeiture; administrative monetary penalties of up to GIP 2,500,000 imposed by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission; and revocation of corporate service provider, EMI or banking arrangements, resulting in loss of the payment rails.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are personally liable where the corporate veil is pierced for money-laundering activity; courts may issue travel bans, impose Serious Crime Prevention Orders, order disqualification from directorship for up to 15 years, require disgorgement of laundered profits, and authorise extradition to the United Kingdom or EU member states under Gibraltar–UK and Gibraltar–EU cooperation instruments.
