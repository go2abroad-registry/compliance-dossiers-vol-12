Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar Income Tax Office, accessible via the Gibraltar Portal (www.gibraltar.gov.gi); customs matters fall under the Gibraltar Customs Department within the Ministry of Finance

E-government filing portal

The foreign Bermuda Exempted Company must first register with the Gibraltar Companies Registry under the Companies Act 2014 to obtain a Gibraltar company/registry number. For income tax purposes, the company must file Form GIB-TIN with the Income Tax Office if it has a Gibraltar source of profits, a Gibraltar place of business, or a tax resident director. If the company is not resident and has no Gibraltar-source income, no Gibraltar tax identification number is issued; the registry number serves as the identifier. A foreign company establishing a branch or registered place of business in Gibraltar must also file the Form FC/BR with the Companies Registry and a corresponding tax notification with the Income Tax Office. All registrations may be effected electronically via the Gibraltar e-Services portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. A Bermuda Exempted Company may elect to be treated as a Qualifying Company under the Qualifying Companies Regulations 2019, provided it meets the substance conditions and obtains prior certification from the Commissioner of Income Tax. It may also avoid residence and thus Gibraltar taxation by ensuring that central management and control is exercised outside Gibraltar, supported by formal board minutes and strategic decision documentation. Hybrid mismatch election mechanisms under the OECD/G20 rules are available only to the extent implemented in the Income Tax Act 2010 as amended.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST; Gibraltar levies selective import and excise duties (effectively a sales/use system is absent)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; Gibraltar does not operate a VAT, GST, or general sales tax registration threshold. Import duty registration may be required for businesses effecting imports into Gibraltar; the threshold for such registration is nil once import activity commences.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

As Gibraltar levies no VAT, exports of die-cast scale models from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar purchase or consumption taxes, so no formal zero-rating claim is required. Where the Bermuda Exempted Company is separately registered for UK VAT (e.g., through a UK-established warehouse or UK fixed establishment), exports of goods from the UK to customers outside the UK may be zero-rated under Value Added Tax Act 1994 section 30 and Schedule 8, Item 2, provided the goods are exported within three months of supply. Services directly related to the goods, including shipping and insurance, may also be zero-rated in certain circumstances. The exporter must hold and preserve valid commercial evidence of physical export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Export Declaration system or UK EXR/SAD equivalent if UK VAT is engaged)
  • Bill of Lading or Waybill (original or certified copy evidencing physical export)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of export consideration
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of delivery or receipt signed by the overseas buyer
  • Insurance certificate or transit documentation where applicable

Gibraltar has no mandatory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based clearance requirement. Invoices for export transactions may be issued in paper or electronic form, but must satisfy the record-keeping requirements of the Income Tax Act and the Gibraltar Customs Department, including identification of the parties, description of goods, and export declarations.

For UK VAT zero-rating: where export evidence is inadequate or false, HMRC may claw back the zero-rating and assess VAT at 20% together with interest, apply misdeclaration penalties up to 100% of the VAT evaded, and pursue criminal prosecution under the Customs and Excise Management Act 1979. In Gibraltar, fraudulent export declarations or evasion of import/excise duty attract forfeiture of goods, civil penalties of up to three times the duty evaded, and imprisonment for dishonest conduct. Retrospective clawbacks may cover a four-year assessment period in Gibraltar and a six-year period where UK VAT is at issue.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar is a Special Member of the UK financial system and uses the Gibraltar pound (GIP) pegged at parity to GBP sterling, which is fully convertible. There are no exchange controls, no capital account authorization requirements, and no restrictions on current account payments. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and liquidation surplus repatriations may be executed freely in any major currency, subject only to standard bank anti-money laundering verification and tax residency documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement is imposed by Gibraltar law or UK regulations. Export receipts may be retained in any designated currency, whether GBP, USD, EUR, or CNY, without mandatory conversion or prescribed remittance timeline. Financial institutions may, however, require a supporting sales contract and trade invoice to classify incoming trade collections for regulatory reporting.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) in coordination with the Bank of England on monetary stability matters; no separate Gibraltar central bank or foreign exchange administration exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Medium with material legal constraints. A Gibraltar-licensed electronic money institution regulated by the GFSC may be used for low-value PayPal settlement and payment aggregation, but it is not a substitute for a full commercial bank for capital escrow or trade account structures. Offshore digital EMIs without a physical or licensed presence in Gibraltar or the UK generally have low onboarding viability due to the inability to access Gibraltar Faster Payments and intra-UK clearing without a correspondent banking sponsor; they are acceptable only as settlement technology rails, never as primary treasury counterparties.
