Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC). Principal portals: GOV.UK (https://www.gov.uk), HMRC Online Services/Government Gateway (https://www.gov.uk/log-in-register-hmrc-online-services), and the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) for UK customs declarations. VAT registration for a non-established taxable person (including a BVI Business Company) is processed via GOV.UK VAT registration (form VAT1) or by the HMRC Non-Established Taxable Persons (NETP) unit.

E-government filing portal

A BVI-incorporated Business Company (BC) holds no automatic UK TIN. A UK Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR), VAT registration number (9-digit 'GB' prefix), and PAYE reference arise only upon registrability. VAT: a non-established taxable person making any taxable supplies in the UK is mandatorily registrable with no turnover threshold; apply online (VAT1) or by post, supplying certified BVI certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, registered office/agent details, identity/proof of officers, UK representative (where required) and, for grant-making activities, evidence that payments are non-supply grants. Corporation Tax/PAYE: register via HMRC within 3 months of commencing a UK trade or employing UK staff if a UK permanent establishment or UK-source profits exist. In the default scenario (non-resident, no UK PE, no UK taxable supplies), no UK TIN is required; the BC evidences status through its BVI Registry number (BC prefix), BVI tax exemption certificate, and BVI Economic Substance confirmation. The BC cannot register with the Charity Commission for England and Wales as it is not constituted under UK or English law.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (BVI domestic status: exempt from BVI income/profit tax). For UK direct tax, the BC is a non-UK resident body corporate and is outside the charge to UK Corporation Tax absent a UK permanent establishment or statutory UK-source income (e.g., UK property rental, Non-Resident Capital Gains Tax chargeable gains from 6 April 2019 on UK land/property-rich entities). No pass-through treatment is available; where the BC enters the UK charge, entity-level Corporation Tax applies at the small profits rate of 19% (profits up to GBP 50,000), main rate of 25% (profits over GBP 250,000), with marginal relief between GBP 50,000 and GBP 250,000. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (BVI domestic status: exempt from BVI income/profit tax). For UK direct tax, the BC is a non-UK resident body corporate and is outside the charge to UK Corporation Tax absent a UK permanent establishment or statutory UK-source income (e.g., UK property rental, Non-Resident Capital Gains Tax chargeable gains from 6 April 2019 on UK land/property-rich entities). No pass-through treatment is available; where the BC enters the UK charge, entity-level Corporation Tax applies at the small profits rate of 19% (profits up to GBP 50,000), main rate of 25% (profits over GBP 250,000), with marginal relief between GBP 50,000 and GBP 250,000. UK charity reliefs are unavailable unless HMRC recognizes the BC as a charity for UK tax purposes, which for a BVI entity requires the usual charitable purpose test and any applicable residence/recognition conditions for charities.

Available election forms

The UK has no check-the-box or analogous entity-classification election; classification follows statutory incorporation and central management and control. Relevant elections/mechanisms available to the BVI BC: (i) voluntary VAT registration (VATA 1994); (ii) Option to Tax (election to waive the VAT exemption) on UK land and buildings (VATA 1994 Sch 10, para 2); (iii) VAT group registration (VATA 1994 s.43) where the BC's UK establishment is grouped with an eligible UK representative member; (iv) dual resident investing company election under CTA 2009 s.18A if applicable; (v) for charitable recognition, application to HMRC Charities for a Charities Reference Number and applicable tax reliefs; (vi) hybrid mismatch rules under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A are mandatory and provide no elective relief; no US-style 'hybrid' election exists in the UK.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 90,000 annual taxable turnover for UK-established businesses (effective 1 April 2024); deregistration threshold GBP 88,000. Non-established taxable persons (e.g., the BVI BC with no UK establishment) are registrable mandatorily without any turnover threshold when making taxable supplies in the UK; distance-selling of goods to UK consumers triggers registration from the first sale where the supplier is not a deemed-supplied online marketplace. Voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold, subject to HMRC conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies to supplies of goods exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK under VATA 1994 s.30 and Sch 8 Group 7, provided the supplier and buyer are identified in the evidence, the goods are physically removed from the UK within 3 months of the time of supply, and the prescribed commercial evidence of export (VAT Notice 703) is held within 3 months of the time of supply. Services: B2B services supplied to a customer belonging outside the UK are outside the scope of UK VAT under VATA 1994 s.7A and Sch 4A place-of-supply rules (customer local reverse charge applies), and are not 'zero-rated' as such. Charitable grants and endowment distributions are not supplies for VAT purposes because no consideration exists; they are outside the scope of VAT regardless of the beneficiary's country, and no VAT liability or reclaim arises on the grant payment itself.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged via CDS (Movement Reference Number/MRN or legacy CHIEF export entry) identifying the BVI BC as exporter
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) showing UK departure and non-UK destination
  • Commercial invoice and contract of sale naming the BVI BC as seller and the foreign counterparty as buyer
  • Packing list, delivery note, and transport records evidencing removal from UK territory
  • VAT Notice 703 export evidence pack (e.g., CDS departure messages, C88, goods departure messages, or authenticated shipping/airline records)
  • Bank inward remittance certificate or SWIFT credit advice showing export proceeds credited to the BVI BC's account
  • For grant distributions: board resolution, grant agreement, and bank wire instruction evidencing a non-supply charitable payment

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government e-invoicing clearance platform operates in the UK. Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT is mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses: VAT records must be kept digitally and VAT returns submitted by software using the HMRC MTD application programming interface (API). Invoices must meet the content and format rules in VAT Notice 700 (full VAT invoice for B2B supplies; simplified invoices for retail); electronic invoices (including EDI/PEPPOL) are valid if compliant. UK customs exports must be declared electronically through CDS (Movement Reference Number issued); no stamped paper fiscal receipts are required.

HMRC audits zero-rating claims against the export evidence and can disallow the 0% rate and assess VAT at 20% where goods were not in fact exported, evidence is deficient, the 3-month removal/evidence deadlines are missed, or goods are diverted/re-imported (including MTIC/carousel-type abuse). Key consequences: VAT assessments under VATA 1994 s.73; statutory assessment time limits of 4 years, extended to 20 years for dishonest conduct (VATA 1994 s.77); default interest under VATA 1994 s.74; inaccuracy penalties under FA 2007 Sch 24 up to 100% of potential lost revenue (higher with deliberate concealment) and failure-to-notify penalties under Sch 25; criminal liability for fraudulent evasion of VAT under VATA 1994 s.72 (imprisonment up to 7 years and/or unlimited fine); joint and several liability of connected parties in MTIC cases under VATA 1994 s.77A. The pipeline's 180-day FX repatriation baseline is conservative: UK VAT operates on a 3-month removal/evidence window, and records proving export status must be retained for at least 6 years. Mischaracterizing grant distributions as zero-rated export transactions is treated as a deliberate inaccuracy and attracts the same clawback, interest, and penalty regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full capital and current-account convertibility with no exchange-control legislation in force. The Exchange Control Act 1947 was repealed in 1979. A BVI Business Company may freely hold, receive, convert, and remit GBP or any freely convertible foreign currency, including donations, endowment injections, investment flows, and grant disbursements. No statutory profit-remittance or dividend-repatriation approvals are required for a non-resident BVI entity. Practical hurdles arise exclusively from bank-level enhanced due diligence, source-of-wealth review, OFSI sanctions screening, and internal de-risking policies, which may delay or reject remittances to high-risk jurisdictions or counterparties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The UK imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline. Exporters and recipients of foreign-currency funds are not obliged to convert to GBP or to sell receipts within any period. For a private charitable foundation undertaking no export trade, the rule is not applicable. Where the bank receives foreign-currency credits, it may require the foundation to categorise the funds as charitable contributions, endowment capital, or export proceeds for regulatory reporting; no conversion obligation attaches.

Central bank supervision: The Governor and Company of the Bank of England (Bank of England), acting jointly with HM Treasury and the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) for exchange-control, sanctions, and financial-restrictions purposes; the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) supervises regulated payment and foreign-exchange services.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

FCA-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Revolut Business, Wise, Currencycloud, and Airwallex are highly feasible as secondary clearing venues for a BVI Business Company. They offer fully remote onboarding, multi-currency IBANs, and rapid SWIFT/SEPA access without a UK branch visit. Legal constraints under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011 include safeguarding of customer funds, minimum capital requirements, and a prohibition on accepting deposits or offering credit. EMIs cannot provide escrow-grade banking, documentary letters of credit, or same-day CHAPS clearing on their own balance sheets. Therefore, overall viability is rated Medium-High where paired with a tier-1 bank for capital escrow; as standalone treasury venues they are not recommended for a charitable endowment vehicle.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be initiated as SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers to the UK receiving bank’s BIC and IBAN, with the payment narrative clearly showing 'CAPITAL INJECTION - [Foundation Name] - ENDOWMENT/DONATION'. The purpose code field should use the receiving bank’s acceptable code, commonly 'GRIN' (gift or remittance) or 'CHAR' (charitable payment); for intra-group funding, an accompanying board resolution and BVI capital introduction certificate are required. The UK bank will screen the remitter, verify the transaction against the established KYC profile, and may place a 24-hour hold pending source-of-funds confirmation. For USD wire transmissions routed via an intermediary bank, the correspondent BIC and account must be pre-agreed to avoid delays. All supported inbound instruments confirm the principle that charitable endowment receipts are freely creditable without UK exchange-control authorisation.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements and grant distributions must be executed through FCA-compliant clearing rails: CHAPS for same-day high-value GBP transfers, Faster Payments for UK GBP payments under the payment limit, SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant for EUR payments within the EEA, and SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA cross-border payments. Where the foundation procures goods or services under an international sale contract, the UK bank may issue a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) under UCP 600, requiring presentation of the commercial invoice, transport document, packing list, certificate of origin, and insurance certificate. Charitable grant distributions are generally settled by SWIFT MT103 or SEPA CT with the narrative 'GRANT DISBURSEMENT', supported by the board grant-approval minute, beneficiary bank details, a sanctions check, and the purpose statement.
