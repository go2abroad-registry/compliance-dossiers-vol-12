Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC), 100 Parliament Street, London SW1A 2BQ. Digital portal: GOV.UK (www.gov.uk) – Business Tax Account, Government Gateway, Corporate Tax digital service, Making Tax Digital (MTD) software APIs, and Customs Declaration Service (CDS) for import/export declarations.

E-government filing portal

For a BVI Business Company (non-UK incorporated, BVI tax-exempt) conducting UK-facing M&A advisory: (1) Corporation Tax UTR: If the BC has a UK permanent establishment (PE) or chargeable UK-source income, register as a non-UK resident company via HMRC form SA401 (Charities, Savings and International unit) or the online Company Tax Service; provide BVI Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, BVI registered office, UK agent/branch details, and statement of UK activities; HMRC issues a 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR). (2) VAT Registration: Non-established taxable person applies via form VAT1 online; no turnover threshold applies to non-established businesses making any UK taxable supplies; exemptions available only where all supplies are zero-rated; HMRC issues a 9-digit VAT number; a UK VAT representative may be required under VATA 1994 s.48 unless the UK has mutual assistance arrangements with the BVI. (3) GB EORI: Obtain via GOV.UK for customs declarations on goods movements. (4) PAYE Employer Reference: Register for UK payroll if UK employees or directors are paid through a UK branch. (5) If the BC has no UK PE, no UK-source income, and no UK taxable supplies, no UK registration is required; the only compliance is BVI-side, i.e. BVI International Tax Authority (ITA) registration for CRS/FATCA (BVI FARS system) and BVI Economic Substance filing with reporting of the BC's BVI registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (BVI level) with Territorial_Scope for UK tax purposes: As a BVI Business Company, the entity is exempt from BVI income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, and stamp duty (except on BVI land/transfers of BVI shares). Under UK domestic law the BC is non-domiciled and non-resident unless central management and control (CMC) is exercised in the UK; if CMC is in the UK, it becomes UK tax resident and is subject to UK corporation tax at 19% (small profits rate) / 25% (main rate) on worldwide profits from April 2024. Absent UK CMC, only UK-source profits of a UK PE are chargeable to corporation tax under CTA 2009 s.19, and UK rental income/UK land gains are taxed under CTA 2009 ss.18-18A as amended by FA 2019. No pass-through election is available; the entity is an Entity_Level_Taxation vehicle where a UK PE or tax residence exists.

Available election forms

The UK has no legislative check-the-box regime; entity classification is statutory and judicially determined (incorporation test under CTA 2009 s.14; central management and control test under Bullock v Unit Construction (1960)). Available mechanisms include: (a) computation of UK PE profits on an OECD separate-enterprise basis with no formal filing election, supported by HMRC-BEPS PE attribution guidance (INTM); (b) for non-resident companies, the statutory attribution of capital to a UK bank/PE and elections under CTA 2009 s.19 for the 'best practice' approach; (c) VAT partial exemption: election for a special method under SI 1995/2518 reg. 102 and annual adjustment election under reg. 99; (d) VAT registration exemption application for non-established businesses making only zero-rated supplies; (e) CFC: the BVI is within the UK's CFC (Excluded Territories) Regulations 2013 (SI 2013/2997), so the BC's non-UK passive income may be automatically excluded from the UK CFC charge where the conditions (no UK PE, no significant IP income, no UK-linked profits) are satisfied; (f) no UK-BVI double taxation treaty exists; only a Tax Information Exchange Agreement (TIEA), so no treaty election is available; unilateral foreign tax credit relief may be claimed under TIOPA 2010 Part 2, though BVI applies a 0% rate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 annual taxable turnover (effective 1 April 2024) for UK-established businesses; non-established (BVI) businesses must register if they make or intend to make any taxable supplies in the UK, irrespective of turnover, unless all supplies are zero-rated, in which case an exemption from registration may be granted; registration is also triggered on the import of goods into Northern Ireland above £90,000. Voluntary registration is permitted for taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating under VATA 1994 s.30(6) and SI 1995/2518 reg. 129-130 applies where goods are exported from the UK within 3 months of supply and HMRC holds both (i) evidence of removal (e.g. authenticated commercial transport document) and (ii) commercial evidence (invoice, contract, payment). Services: for an M&A advisory boutique, cross-border B2B services supplied to an overseas customer are 'outside the scope' of UK VAT (not formally zero-rated) under the place-of-supply general rule (customer's country), requiring the supplier to obtain the customer's business status and VAT/registration/EORI number; B2C services to a non-UK consumer remain UK standard-rated (supplier's place of supply applies to advisory services, with no 'use and enjoyment' override for M&A services). Supplies of intermediary/brokerage services strictly connected with transactions in shares/securities may be exempt under VATA 1994 Sch. 9 Group 5 Item 6; full-scope transaction advisory (financial due diligence, valuation, structuring) is outside that exemption. Claim substantiation requires documentation proving the overseas customer's identity and non-UK establishment, the nature of the supply, and payment receipt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CDS declaration with MRN) evidencing physical removal of goods from the UK
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR consignment note, or equivalent authenticated transport document as evidence of dispatch
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order/contract referencing M&A advisory services, fee structure, and payment terms
  • Overseas customer's business evidence: VAT registration number, EORI number, or equivalent foreign tax registration certificate confirming non-UK business status
  • Engagement letter / KYC file documenting the client's identity, place of establishment, and place of supply analysis
  • Financial remittance evidence: SWIFT MT103, inward remittance certificate, or bank statement evidencing settlement of the fee within the agreed term

The UK does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing for VAT. Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT is compulsory for all VAT-registered businesses (since 1 November 2022), requiring digital record-keeping and quarterly VAT Return filing via HMRC-recognised software APIs; no HMRC pre-clearance of individual invoices is required. Standard electronic invoicing (including Peppol UBL format) is accepted but voluntary. For customs, the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) is the sole digital channel for import/export declarations and mandatory clearance; a UK EORI number is required, and output of the MRN (Movement Reference Number) constitutes the export declaration evidence. A real-time 'fiscal clearance' regime akin to Latin American models does not exist in the UK.

Where zero-rating/outside-scope treatment is claimed without sufficient evidence, HMRC may issue an assessment under VATA 1994 s.73 within 4 years of the end of the accounting period (extended to 20 years for deliberate or concealed error), clawing back output VAT at 20% plus late-payment interest under VATA 1994 s.74. Inaccurate returns and deliberate under-declarations attract penalties under FA 2007 Sch. 24: up to 30% (unprompted disclosure) to 100% (deliberate and concealed plus disclosure and adjustments) of the potential lost revenue, with rights of appeal. Criminal liability for dishonest evasion arises under VATA 1994 s.72 (imprisonment up to 7 years and/or unlimited fines), and fraudulent evasion of customs/excise duty under CEMA 1979 s.170 carries penalties up to 7 years' imprisonment; civil penalties for inaccurate customs declarations apply under FA 2003 ss.24-32 (up to £2,500 per contravention). HMRC requires export evidence to be retained for at least 6 years; failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day commercial benchmark (no UK statutory FX repatriation deadline applies) exposes the entity to enhanced substantive review, potential denial of the zero rate, and suspected transaction recharacterisation as a domestic supply on which UK VAT at 20% is chargeable.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility of GBP, USD and EUR under the UK regime; no exchange controls, no CFMS measures and no central-bank approval requirements apply to inbound capital, profit remittance, dividends or success-fee repatriation from the BVI BC to non-resident shareholders. The only fetters are regulatory: customer due diligence, OFSI/OFAC sanctions screening, and FATCA/CRS reporting. As the BVI BC is tax-exempt in its home jurisdiction and BVI imposes no withholding tax on distributions, no remittance-tax hurdle exists; however, if central management and control is exercised from the UK, the entity risks UK tax residency, which the treasury KYC file must address through an independently documented board-process protocol.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable — neither the United Kingdom nor the British Virgin Islands imposes any statutory timeline for surrender of export receipts or any mandatory conversion ratio. Advisory success fees and cross-border deal receivables may be retained in the invoiced currency, held freely in GBP/USD/EUR clearing accounts and converted at market rates. The only operative restriction is the UK financial-sanctions freezing duty: funds connected with a designated person or UN/EU/UK sanctions nexus must be frozen immediately and reported to OFSI within 14 days of suspicion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (with Prudential Regulation Authority) as monetary and banking supervisor; HM Treasury and the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) jointly administer the liberalised UK FX regime; the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) supervises payments, e-money and conduct aspects of banking onboarding.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Low as a sole treasury rail; feasible only as a hub-and-spoke operational overlay. UK-regulated e-money institutions (Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex, Modulr) operate under the E-Money Regulations 2011 and Payment Services Regulations 2017 and can remotely open multi-currency IBANs for a BVI BC. Legal constraints: (i) EMI safeguarding is statutory but uninsured, making EMIs unsuitable for capital escrow or large success-fee settlements; (ii) aggregator-model EMIs frequently decline BVI corporate structures as Medium_Risk_Enhanced absent full BO-register disclosure; (iii) no non-EEA/third-country corporate passported access exists under the FCA Temporary Permissions Regime; (iv) EMI daily-volume caps impede CHAPS-scale liquidity. Recommended architecture: Tier-1 bank account for clearing, escrow and FX; EMI account for low-value disbursement and virtual-card operating expenses.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections route via SWIFT MT103 (or UK CHAPS same-day settlement for GBP amounts at or above £1,000,000) from the shareholder's own regulated bank account. The instruction must bear the BVI BC legal name, BVI company number, a pre-agreed reference such as 'CAP/2024-06', and an ISO 20022 purpose code of 'CAPC' (capital contribution) or 'FUND'. The receiving Tier-1 bank cross-references the wire to its approved KYC file and applies a customary 24–48-hour funds-availability hold pending OFSI and UK Sanctions List screening. For USD inbound, credit is effected through the UK bank's US correspondent (typically Bank of New York Mellon) via an MT103 COVER; both the cover ordering bank and the ultimate beneficiary identifiers must appear to avoid OFAC false positives.Outbound settlement proceeds over multiple clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border success-fee wires (typically above USD 100,000), with OFAC/OFSI screening at each correspondent hop; (ii) commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT710) where a deal milestone is conditioned on documents such as a signed SPA or completion certificate; (iii) UK CHAPS same-day guaranteed settlement for time-critical UK outflows above £250,000; (iv) Faster Payments Service (FPS) for sub-£1,000,000 UK-denominated operational disbursements; (v) BACS for batch low-value reimbursements. Each outbound leg must cite the engagement or escrow reference and trace back to a segregated treasury ledger evidencing source-of-funds provenance.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX forwards (tenors up to 12 months) executed through the Tier-1 bank dealing desk under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or bank-form FX termsNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for illiquid or restricted deal-target currencies such as TRY, NGN and ARS, cash-settled in GBP or USDFX swaps / cross-currency rollovers for staged deal-milestone funding, with maximum roll period of 90 days to preserve hedge-accounting electivesPurchased vanilla FX options (long-only, no short option writing) for asymmetric protection on contingent success-fee receipts, with premium capped at the expected fee cashflow
