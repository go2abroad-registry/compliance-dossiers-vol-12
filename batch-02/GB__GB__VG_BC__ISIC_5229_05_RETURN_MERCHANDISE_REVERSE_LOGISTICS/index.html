Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Companies House - Registrar of Companies for England and Wales (executive agency of the Department for Business and Trade)

Governing statute

Companies Act 2006 (c. 46), Part 34, ss. 1044-1059, as supplemented by the Overseas Companies Regulations 2009 (SI 2009/1801); the underlying entity is governed by the BVI Business Companies Act, 2004 (as amended).

Formation / constitutional filings

Form OS IN01 - Application for registration of an overseas company and its UK establishment

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 22 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital applies. A BVI Business Company has no authorised or par-value share capital; UK registration of an overseas company only requires disclosure of issued share capital on Form OS IN01, with no escrow account or capital deposit obligation in the UK.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Risk Assessment and premises safety clearance under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, issued by the local Fire and Rescue Authority; structural load inspection certificate from a chartered structural engineer confirming floor loading capacity for pallet racking; workplace clearance under the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999; DSEAR risk assessment where returned aerosols or flammable goods are stored.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital applies. A BVI Business Company has no authorised or par-value share capital; UK registration of an overseas company only requires disclosure of issued share capital on Form OS IN01, with no escrow account or capital deposit obligation in the UK.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC); official portal: www.gov.uk; VAT and Corporation Tax filing via Government Gateway and HMRC Online Services (www.tax.service.gov.uk); Making Tax Digital (MTD) VAT API via HMRC Developer Hub.

E-government filing portal

The BVI Business Company is a non-UK incorporated non-resident company and holds no automatic UK tax identifier. Procedure: (1) Assess whether the entity has a UK permanent establishment or makes UK taxable supplies (UK warehousing, employees, or agent activity for reverse logistics will create a UK VAT fixed establishment and potentially a UK PE for corporation tax). (2) For corporation tax, notify HMRC through the Non-Resident Company registration channel; HMRC issues a 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR). (3) For VAT, register as a non-established taxable person (NETP) via the online VAT registration service at www.tax.service.gov.uk/vat/register or submit form VAT1 with the HMRC non-established taxable persons unit; provide constitutional documents, BVI registered office evidence, UK premises or agent details, turnover forecasts, and bank account information. (4) HMRC allocates a GB-prefixed nine-digit VAT registration number and the entity may also obtain a GB EORI number from HMRC for customs purposes. There is no registration fee.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - the BVI Business Company is exempt from BVI income tax, corporation tax and capital gains tax under the BVI Business Companies Act; however, for UK purposes it is subject to corporation tax only on profits attributable to a UK permanent establishment and to UK VAT as a taxable person where it makes taxable supplies in the UK. It is not eligible for pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

The UK has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections include: (1) VAT grouping election under VATA 1994 s.43, permissible only where the BVI BC establishes a UK establishment and joins a UK VAT group; (2) option to tax UK land under VATA 1994 Sch 10 where UK logistics premises are held; (3) election under CTA 2009 s.18A for non-resident companies to bring UK property business income into the UK corporation tax charge; (4) hybrid mismatch adjustment under Finance Act 2016 s. 24 cannot be elected by a BVI BC because it is an opaque tax-exempt corporate; (5) application of the UK gateway for foreign permanent establishments under CTA 2009 s.5 may be mitigated through treaty or domestic PE limitation where a double taxation treaty exists, but no comprehensive UK-BVI DTT applies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 90,000 annual taxable turnover (mandatory registration threshold for UK-established persons from 1 April 2024; deregistration threshold GBP 88,000). A non-established BVI BC is not entitled to the threshold and must register from its first UK taxable supply, unless it makes only zero-rated supplies and applies for voluntary registration to recover input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated under VATA 1994 s.30 when physically exported from the UK by the supplier within the applicable time limit (standard 90 days; up to 3 months where lawfully allowed and evidenced), supplied to a non-UK customer, and the supplier retains satisfactory evidence of export. Services: the B2B place-of-supply rule (VATA 1994 s.7A, SI 1992/3123) places the supply where the business customer belongs; for a BVI BC providing reverse-logistics, returned-goods quality grading, or refurbishment services to a foreign business customer, the supply lies outside the UK and no UK VAT is chargeable. Where the customer is UK-established, zero-rating of services may apply only if the services are directly related to and performed on goods that are actually exported, as required by VATA 1994 s.30(6) and HMRC Notice 703.

  • Customs Export Declaration (HMRC Customs Declaration Service reference or SAD entry evidencing physical departure from the UK)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill, CMR or postal certificate of posting) evidencing transportation out of the UK
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank transfer or FX settlement receipt evidencing payment repatriated within the 180-day period)

The UK does not require real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-approval of e-invoices. Mandatory Making Tax Digital (MTD) requires all VAT-registered businesses (including non-established entities) to maintain digital records and submit VAT returns electronically via MTD-compatible software through the HMRC VAT Return API. Supplier-to-customer e-invoicing is voluntary (Peppol-BIS 3.0 standard is available); HMRC conducts post-clearance, risk-based real-time analytics. Late or non-digital submission triggers interest and points-based late-submission penalties (with occasional penalty-free COVID-era easements).

HMRC applies strict export-evidence standards and retrospective assessments for unjustified zero-rating or export refund claims. Where proof of export is absent or insufficient, HMRC may issue assessments under VATA 1994 s.73 within four years of the accounting period, extended to twenty years for deliberate or concealed errors. Civil penalties under Finance Act 2007 Sch 24 apply: up to 30% for careless inaccuracies, 70% for deliberate inaccuracies, and 100% for deliberate and concealed inaccuracies, plus interest and Schedule 55 late-payment penalties. Criminal dishonesty penalties under VATA 1994 s.72 may reach seven years imprisonment and unlimited fines; participation in MTIC/carousel or other revenue fraud leads to disallowance of input VAT recovery, withholding of export refunds, and joint and several liability under Finance Act 2016 anti-fraud measures. Good faith mitigation requires robust chain-of-custody documentation and prompt voluntary correction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII and OECD Capital Movements Code obligations. Sterling, US dollar, and euro balances may be held, transferred, and remitted freely by a non-resident BVI Business Company without central bank clearance. Profit and cash-flow repatriation to BVI or third-country accounts is unrestricted, subject only to OFSI sanctions screening, AML/CTF reporting, and HMRC tax filings for any UK permanent establishment or source income. No split exchange rates or non-resident transfer taxes apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline exists in the United Kingdom. A reverse-logistics operator may retain export and service proceeds in any currency in any UK, BVI, or third-country account indefinitely. However, all inbound/outbound payment narratives must support HMRC balance-of-payments reporting and maintain documentary evidence of the commercial substance of each flow for five years, and all counterparties must pass OFSI consolidated sanctions screening, including USD-dollar clearing intermediaries.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (BoE) via the Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) for banking supervision; HM Treasury and the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) administer sanctions-related payment controls; the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) licenses E-Money Institutions. The UK imposes no general foreign-exchange control commissioner or ex-ante currency approval regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility through FCA-authorized E-Money Institutions including Airwallex, Currencycloud, Revolut Business, and Wise Business. These EMIs routinely onboard BVI companies remotely, provide multi-currency IBANs and global accounts, and offer GBP Faster Payments, SEPA EUR, and USD SWIFT clearing. Legal constraints: EMI balances are not deposits and are not FSCS-protected; the EMI must conduct enhanced due diligence on BVI beneficial ownership and may require a UK principal place of business; some EMIs reject pure holding-company structures without operating contracts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route inbound funds from the BVI Business Company treasury to the UK collection account via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 cover) with the remittance narrative 'Capital Injection – BVI BC – Reverse Logistics – ISIC 5229'; no central bank purpose code is mandatory in the UK, but the bank must record a clear narrative for OFSI screening and BoP statistics. For USD-denominated transfers, use a London-based USD correspondent to keep clearing inside the UK; retain the signed board resolution, source-of-funds pack, and loan or contribution agreement in the KYC file.Settle outbound payments to EU/US reverse-logistics carriers, grading labs, and e-commerce platforms through: (1) SWIFT MT103 serial payments for cross-border invoices; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit via SWIFT MT700 and MT707 amendments where suppliers require documentary payment; (3) SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA CT) for euro payables via an FCA-authorized EMI branch; (4) UK Faster Payments for GBP-cleared balances below £1 million and CHAPS same-day settlement for high-value disbursements; (5) Airwallex Local ACH routing for US-dollar domestic clearning where the counterparty holds a US bank account.
