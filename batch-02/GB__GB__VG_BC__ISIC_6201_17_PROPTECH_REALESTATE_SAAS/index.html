Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) – GOV.UK portal (www.gov.uk/vat-registration, www.gov.uk/register-for-corporation-tax)

E-government filing portal

A non-resident BVI Business Company carrying on a trade in the UK through a permanent establishment must register for Corporation Tax within 3 months of commencement by submitting form CT41G online or by post; HMRC issues a 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR). For VAT, the company must register with HMRC under the Non-Union VAT OSS scheme (VAT 1 form / VAT 64) from the first supply of digital services to UK consumers; UK-established suppliers apply online at www.gov.uk/vat-registration when taxable turnover exceeds £90,000. If employing UK staff, register as an employer for PAYE and obtain a PAYE reference. Foreign companies may appoint a UK tax representative if required by HMRC for VAT compliance, but direct registration is the default.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope (non-resident corporate entity; taxed only on UK-source income and any UK permanent establishment profits; not eligible for pass-through treatment) — A non-resident BVI Business Company carrying on a trade in the UK through a permanent establishment must register for Corporation Tax within 3 months of commencement by submitting form CT41G online or by post; HMRC issues a 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope (non-resident corporate entity; taxed only on UK-source income and any UK permanent establishment profits; not eligible for pass-through treatment)

Available election forms

No check-the-box election in the UK. A BVI BC is always treated as an opaque body corporate. Hybrid mismatch rules in TIOPA 2010 Part 6A apply to restrict deduction/non-inclusion outcomes, and the UK's Taxation of Transparent Entities heads (e.g., Scottish LP) do not cover a BVI company. Where a UK PE is present, the company may elect for the PE's standalone treatment, but no statutory entity-classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 (annual taxable turnover) for UK-established businesses; nil threshold for non-UK established suppliers of digital services to UK consumers (registration required from first supply)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to physical exports of goods from the UK to an address outside the UK, and to certain services under VATA 1994 (e.g., freight, supplies to outside the EU). Digital PropTech/SaaS services (e.g., real estate screening subscriptions) supplied to B2B customers outside the UK are outside the scope of UK VAT (place of supply = customer location). For B2C digital services to non-UK consumers, the place of supply is the customer's residence (destination principle), also outside UK VAT. No customs approval is required for services; evidence of customer location and business status must be retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/C88)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or other proof of payment)

Making Tax Digital (MTD) mandate: all VAT-registered businesses must keep digital records and submit VAT Returns via MTD-compatible software or APIs; no mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping; electronic invoicing is voluntary (Peppol/EDI acceptable); export declarations use the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) for goods.

HMRC may deny zero-rating or 'outside scope' treatment where export evidence is inadequate, issuing a VAT assessment for the under-declared output tax plus interest. Inaccurate VAT returns attract penalties under FA 2007 Sch 24 (up to 100% of the lost revenue for deliberate concealment, mitigated by disclosure). Fraudulent evasion of VAT is a criminal offence under VATA 1994 s.72, with penalties up to 7 years' imprisonment and unlimited fines. Retrospective clawback applies where goods are not exported to the destination within the prescribed time, requiring the supplier to account for VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full convertibility of GBP for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange control approvals, surrender requirements, or advance licensing requirements for profit distributions, intercompany loans, or capital repatriation from a BVI-registered entity's UK bank account. Dividends and capital proceeds are freely remittable, subject exclusively to compliance with anti-money laundering ('AML'), know-your-client ('KYC'), and financial sanctions screening obligations. The absence of any foreign investment capital registration requirement for a BVI Business Company facilitates frictionless treasury flows, yet banks will independently assess ultimate beneficial ownership ('UBO') and source-of-funds documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. UK law imposes no mandatory timeline for conversion or repatriation of export proceeds; export receipts may be retained in foreign currency indefinitely. Bank account opening processes, however, require a declared intended purpose and periodic transaction review. Companies under the UK Economic Crime Act are expected to maintain accurate transaction records and to provide prompt substantiation for any unusually high-value or high-frequency trade payments to overseas counterparties, including BVI ultimate beneficial owners.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England / HM Treasury (Exchange Control jurisdiction is fully liberalized; the Bank of England retains systemic monetary authority, while HM Treasury administers financial sanctions and counter-terrorist financing controls).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: High for operational disbursements and low-value SaaS payment collection; Medium for primary treasury settlement and long-term liquidity concentration. Authorized Electronic Money Institutions ('EMIs') under the UK Financial Conduct Authority, such as Revolut Business, Wise Platform, and Payoneer, may onboard a BVI Business Company without a physical UK nexus. However, these institutions generally restrict high single-value cross-border club deals, hold transactional balance caps, and cannot provide full short-term liquidity or commercial letters of credit. The BVI entity should therefore maintain at least one Tier-1 bank account as the clearing anchor and use fintech EMIs only for initiating partial outbound payments and isolating low-risk subscription revenue.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The BVI Business Company may receive a shareholder capital contribution or intercompany loan via a SWIFT MT103 credit transfer referencing a UK corporate banking purpose code 'T0100' or 'S0300' for intercompany investment/loan, with the underlying board resolution and source-of-funds evidence attached. UK receiving banks treat inward remittance from a BVI parent as a normal capital transaction; no Bank of England approval is required. For operational receivables from international customers, the issuer shall use ACH Direct or Stripe Billing to collect card and bank-account payments; funds are aggregated through Stripe's settlement to a UK-based commercial bank or an FCA-authorized EMI, with the payment description explicitly referencing the relevant SaaS license and property management service.Primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer), SWIFT MT202 (bank-to-bank settlement, for interbank liquidity movements only), and commercial letters of credit issued in the seller's favor for high-value software escrow and property closing disbursements. The UK's faster payments and CHAPS systems are not advised for large cross-border vendor payments because CHAPS is predominantly domestic; SWIFT and correspondent banking networks must be used for cross-border liquidation of USD, EUR, and GBP positions.
