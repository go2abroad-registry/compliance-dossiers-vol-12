Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda: statutory election under section 129 of the Companies Act 1981 to be an exempted company (exemption from Bermuda income/capital taxes), plus discretionary election to obtain a government-issued Tax Residency Certificate. Gibraltar: no 'check-the-box' election exists; the Income Tax Act 2010 applies a territorial basis, charging non-resident companies only on Gibraltar-source income; a company resident in Gibraltar cannot elect to be non-resident but may rely on the Gibraltar/UK Double Taxation Arrangement and place-of-effective-management rules. Hybrid mismatch: Gibraltar enforces OECD BEPS Action 2 and anti-hybrid rules requiring alignment of entity/instrument characterisation between jurisdictions; no elective hybrid-favourable classification is available. UK VAT: non-established sellers may elect to register for VAT from the first taxable supply (GBP 0 threshold), may not join the Flat Rate Scheme, and may optionally use the OSS/IOSS scheme for EU distance sales.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar: no VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax (outside the UK VAT area); indirect taxes are limited to customs import duties, excise duties on alcohol/tobacco/fuel, and registry/transaction levies. UK mainland: Value Added Tax (VAT) at the standard rate of 20% applies to supplies of goods made in Great Britain and to imported consignments; online marketplaces (e.g., Amazon) are deemed suppliers for imported goods not exceeding GBP 135 sold from outside the UK. Bermuda: no indirect tax system exists.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: no VAT registration threshold because no VAT regime is in force. UK: for a Bermuda-incorporated non-established seller, the registration threshold is GBP 0 for supplies of goods to UK consumers (mandatory from the first taxable supply if the seller is the supplier); for UK-established businesses the threshold is GBP 85,000 (VATA 1994, Schedule 1). EU: EUR 10,000 intra-EU distance-selling threshold; EUR 0 for a non-EU supplier using IOSS for consignments not exceeding EUR 150. Bermuda: no registration requirement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar imposes no VAT, so zero-rating is not applicable to Gibraltar-only sales; goods exported from Gibraltar are outside the scope of UK VAT and are free of Gibraltar customs duty when export is evidenced. If the Bermuda Exempted Company is required (or elects) to register for UK VAT because goods are in Great Britain at the time of sale (e.g., Amazon FBA), zero-rating applies under VATA 1994 section 30 and the VAT Export (Goods) Order 2000: the goods must be supplied to a non-UK customer, physically exported to a destination outside the UK within 3 months of the supply, and the exporter must hold valid evidence of export (customs declarations, transport documents, payment records). Offshore services incidental to the sale of the beechwood wooden blocks (design, licensing, digital content) are zero-rated where the place of supply is outside the UK under section 7A VATA. Input VAT on eligible costs (raw beechwood, freight, packaging) may be recovered by a UK-registered person, while Gibraltar customs duty drawback applies under the Customs Ordinance (Cap. 22) on re-export.

Gibraltar: no e-invoicing mandate, no real-time fiscal clearance, and no digital stamping; invoices must satisfy minimum content rules but may be issued in paper or electronic form. UK: no real-time clearance or digital stamping; however, HMRC Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT mandates digital record-keeping and functional-compatible software linked to HMRC APIs for all VAT-registered businesses from April 2022 irrespective of turnover; VAT returns must be submitted electronically via software-to-HMRC API and cannot be filed through the legacy portal.

UK HMRC scrutiny of export zero-rating is intensive: undue zero-rating or evasion attracts a penalty of up to 100% of the VAT evaded under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007, plus interest from the date of supply; dishonest evasion is a criminal offence under VATA 1994 section 72 punishable by fine and imprisonment. Deliberate inaccuracies extend the assessment window to 20 years (4 years for careless errors); marketplaces and facilitators may face joint and several liability. Retrospective clawback applies if export evidence is missing, forged, or fabricated; no retrospective or backdated export certificates are accepted, and the exporter loses the corresponding input VAT deduction. Gibraltar HM Customs: false declarations render goods liable to forfeiture and expose the declarant to penalties of up to three times the value of the evaded duty under the Customs Ordinance, plus import-compliance bans. Additional regulatory exposure for the Gibraltar corporate service provider/director includes fines above GBP 5,000 per CRS/VAT breach and disqualification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar Pound (GIP) is issued at a fixed 1:1 parity with the Pound Sterling and is fully freely convertible for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange-control approvals required for equity injections, intra-group loans, trade settlements, or divident/profit remittances by a non-resident-owned exempted company. The only practical remittance hurdles are material-substance documentation under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 and the requirement to maintain an adequate beneficial-ownership register under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014; a tax-exempt Bermuda Exempted Company must be prepared to evidence its economic substance profile to avoid reclassification as a Gibraltar tax resident by default.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters and trading intermediaries may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts in GIP, GBP, USD, EUR, or any fully convertible currency of their choice. No GFSC or Bank of England notification, conversion, or repatriation timeline applies to export proceeds, provided the underlying invoices, shipping documents, and customs declarations substantiate genuine commercial transactions in the toy manufacturing and distribution supply chain.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for Gibraltar as a British Overseas Territory) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) (banking, payments, and AML/CFT regulatory supervision under the Financial Services Ordinance 2019)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating for offshore digital EMIs: Low-to-Medium without a local license. Post-Brexit, an EEA- or UK-licensed electronic money institution cannot passport its license into Gibraltar; any EMI serving a Gibraltar treasury must obtain separate authorization from the GFSC under Gibraltar's electronic-money and payment-services framework, or operate strictly through a GFSC-licensed Gibraltar bank as settlement agent. Global collection aggregators such as Stripe and Amazon Pay are acceptable inbound collection rails for B2C card sales, but they cannot act as the primary commercial bank for escrow, documentary letters of credit, or FX hedging, and all aggregate settlements must cascade into a Tier-1 Gibraltar bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit
