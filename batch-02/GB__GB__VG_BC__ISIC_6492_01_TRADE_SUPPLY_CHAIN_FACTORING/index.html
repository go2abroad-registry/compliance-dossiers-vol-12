Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC). Digital delivery: HMRC Online Services via the Government Gateway; Making Tax Digital (MTD) application programming interfaces (APIs) for VAT record-keeping and filing; Customs Declaration Service (CDS) for export and import customs entries; official guidance at gov.uk.

E-government filing portal

BVI layer: The BVI Business Company (BC) is incorporated under the BVI Business Companies Act, 2004; the BVI imposes no income tax or VAT on the BC; the BVI does not issue a TIN for offshore operations, and the registered agent maintains statutory registers and economic substance filings. UK Corporation Tax: If the BC is centrally managed and controlled in the UK (UK tax residence) or carries on a trade in the UK through a permanent establishment (PE) (for example UK staff, UK decision-making, or UK client procurement), the BC must register for Corporation Tax with HMRC within 3 months of commencing UK trading, using the online service 'Register an overseas company for Corporation Tax'; HMRC allocates a 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR). UK VAT: The BC must register for VAT on form VAT1 or via the non-established taxable person (NETP) online route as soon as it makes taxable UK supplies; an NETP has no GBP 90,000 turnover threshold and must register before the first taxable UK supply; HMRC may require a financial security deposit from a BVI BC. HMRC issues a GB-prefixed 9-digit VAT registration number. Employer duties: PAYE and Real Time Information (RTI) registration for any UK employees; Companies House overseas-company branch registration applies if a physical UK establishment is created. The BVI tax-exempt status does not displace UK tax nexus.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The UK has no 'check-the-box' or general pass-through election for a BVI BC; a body corporate is always opaque and subject to UK Corporation Tax at entity level when UK resident or trading through a UK PE. Available elections and mandatory rules: (i) VAT grouping election under VATA 1994 s.43A, which may include a non-UK company with a UK establishment; (ii) option to tax (waiver of exemption) on UK land and buildings under VATA 1994 Sch 10; (iii) attribution of profits to a UK PE on a separate-enterprise basis under OECD and double-taxation treaty practice, which is in the nature of an allocative rule rather than an election; (iv) no elective opt-out from the UK hybrid mismatch rules (TIOPA 2010 Part 6A), which apply mandatorily where factoring receivables are treated as financially transparent in one jurisdiction and opaque in another; (v) for factoring, income recognition follows GAAP/IFRS classification as loan-relationship or trading income with no special fiscally transparent election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 90,000 of taxable supplies made in the UK in any rolling 12-month period, or expected to exceed GBP 90,000 in the next 30 days alone, effective 1 April 2024 and maintained by the Autumn Budget 2024; zero threshold for non-established taxable persons. Supplies of credit and the advance of funds in factoring and reverse factoring are VAT-exempt under VATA 1994 Sch 9 Group 5 and excluded from the threshold test; separately charged debt-collection and administration fees are standard-rated and count toward the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: Zero-rating applies under VATA 1994 s.30 and VAT Notice 703 where goods are exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK and the supplier holds valid export evidence within 3 months of the time of supply (direct exports), or where the overseas customer exports the goods within 3 months of the supply (indirect exports); the export must be supported by a customs declaration (MRN), transport documents, and commercial documentation. Services: Factoring and reverse-factoring services supplied to a business customer belonging in a non-UK country are outside the scope of UK VAT under the place-of-supply rule (VATA 1994 s.7A; VAT Notice 741A), with full input tax deduction for the fee element; the same supply to a UK business is exempt for the credit advance and standard-rated for separately charged collection and administration services. Supplies to a non-UK private consumer remain exempt from UK VAT but carry no input tax recovery. The offshore-services route is an 'outside the scope' treatment rather than statutory zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/CDS declaration with Movement Reference Number (MRN) and export summary declaration), retained for the statutory record-keeping period
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing the goods departed from the UK to the overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bilateral bank payment confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and Incoterms evidence for the goods transaction
  • For factoring services: executed factoring or reverse-factoring agreement, invoice-factoring schedules, proof of the overseas customer's business status (overseas VAT registration or equivalent), and service invoices demonstrating an overseas place of supply

The UK does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, pre-clearance, or digital stamping regime for invoices. Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT is mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses (extended to all registrants from April 2022): VAT records must be kept digitally in functional compatible software and VAT returns must be submitted through HMRC APIs; paper filing is unavailable to VAT-registered entities. B2B e-invoicing, including the PEPPOL network, is permitted voluntarily but is not reported transaction-by-transaction to HMRC. Export controls are fully electronic: customs declarations must be lodged on the Customs Declaration Service (CDS), which has replaced the CHIEF export platform, and certain controlled goods require pre-lodgement and an 'arrived' declaration; there is no additional governmental digital stamping requirement.

HMRC actively audits export zero-rating and offshore-services claims. For goods, absence of valid customs and shipping evidence within 3 months renders the supply standard-rated and permits an assessment under VATA 1994 s.73(1) with statutory interest; assessment time limits extend to 4 years for careless inaccuracies and 20 years for deliberate or concealed arrangements. Civil penalties under FA 2007 Sch 24 range from 15% to 100% of the VAT understated depending on carelessness or deliberateness and the quality of disclosure; HMRC may issue information notices requiring production of remittance, banking, and customs records and may deny input tax recovery until substantiation is provided. Fraudulent evasion of VAT on export claims is a criminal offence under VATA 1994 s.72, carrying on conviction on indictment imprisonment of up to 7 years and an unlimited fine, together with confiscation proceedings under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002. For factoring services, a false 'outside the scope' claim exposes the supplier to the full 20% output VAT on the fee and the same penalty and retrospective-clawback framework, including reassessments of up to 20 years in cases of deliberate wrongdoing.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains complete current-account and capital-account convertibility with no exchange-control approvals, no surrender quotas, and no advance-import deposit schemes. GBP operates as a freely floating hard currency. Cross-border profit remittance and capital repatriation are unrestricted subject only to company-law solvency tests, filing of accounts, and transfer-pricing documentation; dividend withholding tax is nil, and interest/royalty withholding is generally 0% under UK domestic law or applicable treaties. For the BVI Business Company, identical convertibility applies in both the UK and the BVI, so GBP, USD, and EUR capital injections, distributions, intercompany advances, and trade collections can flow without central-bank licensing. Residual compliance frictions are confined to AML/CTF obligations under MLR 2017, OFSI/UN/EU sanctions screening, CRS/FATCA reporting, Companies House filing duties, and HMRC balance-of-payments statistical returns; none of these constitute currency controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The UK imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no deadline for repatriation or conversion into GBP. Exporters, trading companies, and factoring entities may lawfully hold export collections indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts at any UK-authorised bank or FCA-registered EMI. Obligations are limited to: (i) truthful invoicing and record-keeping for the assigned trade receivables; (ii) UK Internal Market and UK-Global Tariff statistical declarations (UKGTS/Intrastat equivalents) where value thresholds are crossed; (iii) HMRC ITRS reporting for direct investment, trade credit, and financial flows meeting threshold criteria; and (iv) screening of debtor/supplier parties against OFSI, OFAC SDN, UN, and EU lists. Non-reserve-currency proceeds from emerging-market portfolios may be converted or hedged at the entity's discretion using deliverable or non-deliverable instruments.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (primary monetary authority, financial stability regulator, and issuer of reserve settlement balances); HM Treasury (holder of retained exchange-control policy powers and financial sanctions under OFSI); HMRC (administers the International Transactions Reporting System / ITRS statistical regime and cross-border trade data collection). The UK repealed the Exchange Control Act 1947 with full liberalisation effective October 1979.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for operational disbursement and receivable-collection accounts; LOW for capital escrow, client-money pooling, and regulated trade-finance settlement where a full credit institution is legally required. FCA-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (Revolut Business, Wise Business, Currencycloud, Modulr, ClearBank, and Airwallex) operate under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011 and PSD2. They offer rapid onboarding (2 to 7 days), multi-currency IBANs, FX conversion APIs, and SWIFT/SEPA connectivity; however, an EMI cannot accept deposits and must safeguard customer funds in segregated bank accounts. Therefore the factoring treasury should use EMIs solely for low-value operational flows and treasury equalisation, while the capital escrow account, invoice-purchase funding account, and any third-party funds held in connection with reverse factoring must sit at a tier-1 UK bank. This dual-rail structure requires the EMI to be linked to a master account at the principal bank to preserve audit clarity.

Compliant protocol: (1) Instruct the BVI BC's bank to transmit a SWIFT MT103 credit transfer to the UK operating account using ISO purpose code 'INVEST' with narrative 'Equity Subscription - VG_BC into UK Factoring OpCo'; (2) for same-day GBP settlement when both banks are members of CHAPS, instruct a CHAPS payment carrying identical purpose coding; (3) deliver pre-funding documentation comprising the share subscription agreement, UK Companies House SH01 statement of capital, KYC pack, source-of-funds evidence, board resolutions, and CRS/FATCA self-certifications; (4) after credit, verify via MT103 copy and bank credit advice; (5) record the inflow in HMRC ITRS returns (direct-investment classification) and file the Companies House Confirmation Statement. No Bank of England approval or FX conversion licence is required. If the injection is structured as shareholder debt rather than equity, execute a written intercompany loan agreement with arm's-length interest and complete Companies House mortgage registration where security is granted.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border supplier payments, trade advances, and assigned-invoice settlement outside the UK/EEA; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit issued via MT700/MT798 for import-export transactions where the factoring entity funds against documentary collateral; (iii) CHAPS for high-value same-day GBP disbursements exceeding £1 million; (iv) Faster Payments Service (FPS) for immediate intra-UK payments up to £1 million per transaction; (v) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant for euro-denominated collections and disbursements to EU debtors; (vi) TARGET2 for high-value euro clearing; and (vii) Treasury equalisation sweeps across the GBP/EUR/USD accounts using internal book transfers and execution of spot FX at the principal bank. All rails must carry full purpose narratives and trade references consistent with PSR 2017 requirements, with overt full-sweep monitoring to prevent OFSI/OFAC adverse screening blocks.Deliverable FX Forwards (GBP/EUR/USD) executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or a UK non-bank counterparty framework agreementFX Swaps and swap-points rollover transactions for working-capital maturity managementCross-Currency Interest Rate Swaps for matched long-term funding of international factoring portfoliosEuropean-style OTC FX Options (call/put and zero-premium collars) to hedge collection-timing uncertaintyNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) against restricted-convertibility emerging-market currencies linked to underlying factored trade receivables, written by UK-regulated clearing banks under ISDA definitions, subject to FCA product-governance rulesExchange-traded FX futures and options on CME or Euronext for transparent fully margined hedging, subject to EMIR reporting and margin segregation

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH for operational disbursement and receivable-collection accounts; LOW for capital escrow, client-money pooling, and regulated trade-finance settlement where a full credit institution is legally required. FCA-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (Revolut Business, Wise Business, Currencycloud, Modulr, ClearBank, and Airwallex) operate under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011 and PSD2. They offer rapid onboarding (2 to 7 days), multi-currency IBANs, FX conversion APIs, and SWIFT/SEPA connectivity; however, an EMI cannot accept deposits and must safeguard customer funds in segregated bank accounts. Therefore the factoring treasury should use EMIs solely for low-value operational flows and treasury equalisation, while the capital escrow account, invoice-purchase funding account, and any third-party funds held in connection with reverse factoring must sit at a tier-1 UK bank. This dual-rail structure requires the EMI to be linked to a master account at the principal bank to preserve audit clarity.
