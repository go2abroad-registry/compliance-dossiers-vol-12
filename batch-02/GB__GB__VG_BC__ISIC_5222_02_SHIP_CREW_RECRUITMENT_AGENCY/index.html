VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 rolling 12-month taxable turnover (from 1 April 2024); compulsory registration for non-established taxable persons making any taxable UK supply unless exempt, reverse-charged, or outside UK VAT scope.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from the UK to a non-UK recipient qualify for 0% VAT under VATA 1994 Sch 8 Gp 6/Gp 7 with proof of export retained within 3 months of supply. B2B crew management and seafarer recruitment services supplied to a customer belonging outside the UK are outside UK VAT scope (place of supply of B2B services is the customer's country under VATA 1994 s.7A). B2C crew services and services supplied to UK shipowners remain taxable at 20%, subject to special marine navigation or territorial water provisions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CDS/SAD accepted declaration and movement reference number)
  • Bill of Lading or alternative shipping evidence (Sea Waybill, CMR, Air Waybill)
  • Crew Service Agreement / Manning Agency Contract evidencing overseas customer and service scope
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation of payment from the foreign shipowner
  • HMRC export evidence retention record (commercial invoice, packing list, insurance certificate)

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate. HMRC requires Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT, mandating digital record-keeping and VAT return submission via HMRC-recognized API-integrated software; electronic invoicing is voluntary except for self-billed reverse-charge invoices on offshore B2B services; no continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance architecture is in operation.

HMRC may assess a further VAT liability plus default interest where export zero-rating is unsubstantiated, goods are diverted, or funds are not received from the foreign customer within 180 days. Civil penalties up to 100% of evaded VAT for deliberate or concealed error; reduced penalties for prompted or unprompted disclosure. Fraudulent evasion of VAT carries criminal liability (VATA 1994 s.72) with unlimited fines and imprisonment; retrospective assessments may extend up to 20 years for deliberate inaccuracies under FA 2008 Sch 36 and FA 2007 Sch 24.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full de jure and de facto convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls, no pre-notification requirements, and no approval gates for dividend distributions, trade-related remittances, capital injections, or the repatriation of liquidation proceeds. The United Kingdom's OECD and IMF Article VIII status obligates unrestricted current account convertibility; profit remittances by a foreign-owned BVI Business Company are entirely free of currency control or central bank permission, subject only to standard corporate governance documentation and applicable tax reporting under UK domestic corporate tax rules (if tax resident) or the dual-residence tie-break under the UK-BVI double taxation agreement, as applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender obligations, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no requirement to hold export earnings in any designated bank account in the United Kingdom. A BVI Business Company operating under a UK commercial presence may retain 100% of its export revenue in any freely convertible currency. For accounting and UK tax purposes, the company must maintain accurate transactional records and, where a UK permanent establishment or UK PAYE/NI obligations arise, must report in GBP under GAAP/HMRC rules. No central bank surrender is applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (HM Treasury retains policy authority over the United Kingdom's exchange control and foreign exchange framework)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for an EMI-licensed multi-currency borderless operational account with a UK or EEA-located fintech provider, provided the provider accepts BVI-incorporated non-resident businesses. Practical constraints: digital EMI providers such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, and Currencycloud/Starling Bank (via BaaS) require the BVI company's beneficial owners and directors to pass sanctioned-party, adverse-media, and source-of-funds checks. Some EMIs restrict high-risk maritime payroll and seafarer payments due to correspondent banking downstream limits. A hybrid approach is recommended: a tier-1 UK commercial bank as the primary settlement account and an EMI for multi-currency payroll treasury acceleration. Legal constraint: UK-registered EMIs under Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) e-money regulations may not offer true lending or overdraft facilities, and funds held in e-money accounts are not covered by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme (unlike bank deposits), therefore liquidity must be sweept nightly to the tier-1 deposit account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) Inbound shareholder capital injection from the BVI parent or third-party investors should be remitted to the UK Operational Account designated as the principal treasury hub, using SWIFT MT103 with an accurate purpose code. (2) For equity injection, use UK purpose code 'REF = Share capital subscription' (ECB purpose code 'DAM' for inward direct investment) or BEA F17E code; for intercompany loan, use 'Intercompany loan' (ECB purpose code 'DAL'). (3) The bank requires the underlying board resolution and executed investment agreement; the funds must appear on the company's UK books as equity or subordinated debt. (4) Before booking, verify that the BVI side has obtained a valid BVI AML/CFT certificate and that the sending bank is a legitimate financial institution; the UK receiving bank will apply one-day standard deposit hold, then immediate availability. (5) To avoid automatic Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) screening, any inbound aggregate flow above GBP 20,000 should be pre-notified to the relationship manager with full source-of-funds documentation (settlement statement, audited financials, contract). (6) Commingling of payroll and capital is permitted only if clearly coded; otherwise the bank may tag it as mixing of treasury functions.Preferred clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 (MT103 single credit transfer) for all seafarer payroll, agent commissions, contract settlements, vessel supply payments, and beneficiary remittances; the company's payroll processor must transmit through an ISO 20022 channel that maps to the UK bank's Faster Payments service for domestic GBP payments (FPS is the default rail for same-day settlement below GBP 1,000,000), while international crew payments in non-GBP use SWIFT MT103 via the SEPA/CHAPS/EBA clearing gateways. (2) Commercial letters of credit (documentary LCs) governed by UCP 600 should be used for bulk procurement of vessel stores, fuel, and supplies, particularly for supplies exceeding USD 100,000; LCs are issued through the UK bank's trade finance desk, confirming that the BVI company is the applicant. (3) Direct debits and open account trade are not recommended for crew payroll unless supported by a separate receivables workout. (4) The disbursement instruction template must include the vessel IMO number or charterer reference to reduce bank review friction. (5) For multi-currency payroll, the treasury desk should aggregate currencies and run a weekly FX forward to fix conversion cost and ensure rail finality.Short-dated forward contracts (spot to 12 months) in USD/GBP, EUR/GBP, and USD/EUR desked through the UK relationship bank under ISDA Master Agreement or a short-form master forward confirmation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted currencies are pipeline non-permissible for crew disbursements in ordinary course; however, for exceptional crew costs in illiquid currencies (e.g., AED, ZAR), an NDF may be permitted only as a hedging overlay if the underlying expense is predictable and a full verification of the offshore NDF counterparty is carried out.Currency swaps for cross-currency rebalancing of USD working capital against GBP payroll, limited to a maximum 6-month tenor and only for matching operational cash flows.European-style currency options (for risk management, not speculation), permissible where treasury policy has been adopted by the board and the option is struck against actual crew payroll obligations, and where the nominal amount does not exceed 110% of the near-expected payroll cash flow.
