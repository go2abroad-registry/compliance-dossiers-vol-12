Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to the BVI Business Company, and no capital escrow or deposit account is required. The company may issue shares with or without par value. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC); HMRC Online Services via the UK Government Gateway (https://www.gov.uk/register-for-vat; https://www.gov.uk/log-in-register-hmrc-online-services); ancillary registration with Companies House (overseas company UK establishment registration, Companies Act 2006, Part 34, s.1046).

E-government filing portal

A BVI Business Company (BC) is a non-UK resident company whose UK registration duty is triggered by physical or economic presence. Procedure: (1) Determine whether a UK permanent establishment (PE) exists, typically through a fixed UK cold-storage warehouse; if so, register for Corporation Tax with HMRC using form CT41G online within 3 months of commencing the UK business and obtain a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR). (2) Register for UK VAT as a non-UK established business using form VAT1 (or the online 'Register for VAT' service) from the date of the first taxable supply in the UK; the nil-threshold rule applies to non-residents, and the application must attach the BVI certificate of incorporation, BVI Registry of Corporate Affairs excerpt, opening balance sheet, and the identity of the UK tax representative/agent to obtain a GB VAT registration number. (3) Obtain a UK EORI number (GB followed by 12 digits) for customs export declarations. (4) File Form OS-IN01 with Companies House within one month of opening the UK establishment. (5) Register for PAYE where UK employees are engaged.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The UK has no US-style check-the-box election; a BVI BC is an opaque, non-transparent body corporate charged to UK Corporation Tax at entity level on profits attributable to a UK PE (small profits rate 19% below £50,000; main rate 25% above £250,000; marginal relief between those amounts from 1 April 2023). No election can disapply this classification. Relevant elective mechanisms: (a) Option to Tax land (VATA 1994, Sch 10) to waive the exempt supply of an interest in or licence to occupy UK cold-storage premises, enabling full input VAT recovery; (b) UK VAT group registration (VATA 1994, s.43); (c) Corporation Tax group relief surrender elections and intangible fixed asset group elections (CTA 2009, s.858); (d) CFC qualifying short-term loan relationship elections. The UK hybrid mismatch rules (TIOPA 2010, Part 6A) apply mandatorily with no elective override; the BVI tax-exempt status does not neutralise a UK hybrid mismatch outcome.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 annual taxable turnover for UK-established businesses (effective 1 April 2024); nil threshold for non-UK established businesses, so a BVI BC supplying cold-storage services in the UK must register from the first taxable supply; no post-Brexit distance-selling threshold for overseas suppliers of goods delivered to UK consumers; optional Import One Stop Shop (IOSS) applies to consignments of goods not exceeding £135 in value.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods are zero-rated (VATA 1994, Sch 8, Grp 15, Item 1) where the goods physically leave the UK within 3 months of the time of supply and the exporter obtains and retains valid evidence of export within 3 months of departure. Cold-storage and warehousing services relating to goods that are to be exported are zero-rated (Sch 8, Grp 15, Item 4) where the recipient belongs outside the UK or is the exporter, the services are performed in connection with the export, and the goods are genuinely exported; storage without an active export nexus remains standard-rated. Where the BVI BC supplies warehousing services to a customer belonging outside the UK, the B2B place-of-supply rule places the supply outside the UK VAT scope, subject to the UK establishment of the customer and effective-use-and-enjoyment carve-outs. A non-UK established business that is not VAT-registered in the UK may recover UK input VAT under the VATA 1994 s.39 refund mechanism; a UK VAT-registered BVI BC recovers input VAT through its VAT return subject to partial exemption rules. Under this compliance framework, export consideration is benchmarked for repatriation within 180 days of export; HMRC does not impose a fixed statutory FX repatriation deadline for VAT zero-rating, but abnormal payment delay is probative evidence in assessing whether a genuine export occurred.

  • Customs export declaration accepted on the Customs Declaration Service (Movement Reference Number)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure of goods
  • Commercial invoice and warehousing contract stating Incoterms and export terms
  • Export packing list and weight/measurement certificates
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit evidence of payment
  • BVI BC certificate of incorporation and UK VAT/EORI registration references
  • Cold-chain temperature monitoring records for pharmaceutical logistics compliance

The UK does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing clearance regime. Mandatory obligation: Making Tax Digital for VAT (MTD) requires all VAT-registered businesses (all turnovers from 1 April 2022) to maintain digital records and file VAT returns using MTD-compatible software connected to HMRC application programming interfaces (APIs); paper filings are prohibited except for exemptions. Exports require electronic pre-departure declarations submitted through HMRC's Customs Declaration Service (CDS), generating a Movement Reference Number (MRN); safety and security declarations are required for goods leaving the UK. No tax-authority pre-approval of individual invoices is required.

HMRC may disallow zero-rating, claw back input VAT, and issue assessments under VATA 1994 s.73 where export evidence is deficient or the export did not in fact occur. Civil penalties under FA 2007, Sch 24 are calculated on the potential lost revenue: up to 30% for careless errors, 70% for deliberate errors, and 100% for deliberate and concealed conduct; late payment interest runs from the due date. Assessment time limits: 4 years for careless conduct, 20 years for deliberate or fraudulent behaviour, and no time limit where a return is omitted. Criminal liability for fraudulent evasion of VAT (VATA 1994, s.72) carries up to 7 years' imprisonment and unlimited fines, with HMRC 'naming and publishing' sanctions for dishonest taxpayers. Incorrect customs declarations attract penalties of up to £2,500 per declaration and potential revocation of EORI status; connected parties may be pursued under the joint and several liability regime, and retrospective clawback extends to all related input VAT claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full convertibility for both current account and capital account transactions. No statutory exchange control approval is required for trade settlements, dividend distributions, intercompany loans, or equity repatriation. Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject only to corporate tax compliance, transfer pricing documentation, and applicable financial sanctions screening. Free use of GBP, USD, and EUR accounts is permitted for resident and non-resident entities, including BVI-incorporated companies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender of export proceeds exists. Exporters and logistics operators in the UK may retain all foreign currency receipts in any currency and use them for operational or treasury purposes without conversion requirements. There is no statutory timeline for converting export receipts into GBP, and no ratio of proceeds must be sold to the central bank. Reporting obligations arise only under sanctions, anti-money laundering, and customs/Intrastat thresholds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England and HM Treasury (Exchange Control abolished in 1979; no separate FX administration; sanctions and capital controls enforced via HM Treasury/OFSI and Bank of England prudential rules)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High. FCA-authorized electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, and ClearBank can provide remote onboarding and flexible multi-currency IBANs suitable for an established BVI company. However, cold-chain warehousing operations with high-value trade disbursements may trigger enhanced due diligence given the BVI ownership layer and the need for physical UK or EU distribution counterparties. Pure offshore digital EMIs without a UK licence are not viable because they cannot legally hold client funds or settle in UK payment systems without FCA or equivalent EEA authorisation.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% domestic dividend withholding tax in the United Kingdom. Dividends paid by a UK-resident company to a BVI BC shareholder are not subject to UK withholding tax, regardless of whether a double tax treaty applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For a UK PE of a BVI BC, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is mandatory for large enterprises: more than 250 employees and either turnover above GBP 50 million or balance sheet assets above GBP 43 million. Connected-party transactions with a UK tax impact should be documented whenever the aggregate cross-border controlled amount exceeds GBP 5 million. Country-by-Country Reporting is triggered for groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million, applying HMRC's annually published GBP sterling equivalent.

Economic substance scope: The BVI Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Act 2018 applies to the BVI BC for any relevant activity undertaken in BVI. A UK cold-storage warehouse physically operated from the UK is not a BVI relevant activity, but it is a UK permanent establishment to which HMRC attributes profits under the OECD authorised approach based on functions performed, assets used, and risks assumed. In the UK, economic substance must be evidenced through premises, employees, decision-making, and capital allocation; artificial arrangements lacking substance may be countered under the General Anti-Abuse Rule, the Diverted Profits Tax, and the control of foreign company rules.
