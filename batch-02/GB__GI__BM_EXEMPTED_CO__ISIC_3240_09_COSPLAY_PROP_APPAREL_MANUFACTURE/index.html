Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office (GITO), HM Government of Gibraltar; official portal: www.gibraltar.gov.gi. Company registration: Registrar of Companies, Gibraltar Companies House. Customs: HM Customs of Gibraltar (EORI and SAD clearance). HMRC (United Kingdom) maintains competence solely for any separate voluntary UK VAT registration; it has no direct tax jurisdiction over Gibraltar-source activities.

E-government filing portal

Stage A - Corporate Existence: incorporate a Gibraltar private company or register the Bermuda Exempted Company as an External Company under Part 12 of the Companies Act 2014 with Gibraltar Companies House. Stage B - Income Tax Registration: within 30 days of commencement, file the Corporate Taxpayer Registration application with GITO to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN); attach the Bermuda certificate of incorporation, certificate of compliance, constitutional documents, and beneficial ownership register. Stage C - Indirect Tax Registration: apply to HM Customs for a Gibraltar EORI number before importing raw materials (synthetic fibres) or exporting finished cosplay costume robes, synthetic wigs, and prop apparel. Stage D - Employer Registration: if the Gibraltar operation hires tailors or retail staff, register for PAYE and Gibraltar Social Security with GITO and the Gibraltar Social Security Authority. Stage E - Fiscal Representation: if no physical Gibraltar office exists, appoint a Gibraltar-resident fiscal representative (consideration person) for receipt of tax notices and service of process.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box or fiscal-transparency election. Available statutory mechanisms: (i) Non-Resident Company treatment election under the management-and-control test of the Income Tax Act 2010, limiting Gibraltar taxation to Gibraltar-source profits; (ii) Qualifying Company election under the Qualifying Company regime of the Income Tax Act 2010 - available only following conversion to a Gibraltar-incorporated company wholly owned by non-residents, with no Gibraltar employees or premises and all business conducted outside Gibraltar, and replacing the 12.5% corporate tax rate with a fixed annual fee; (iii) Group relief election for surrender of losses between Gibraltar-resident group companies; (iv) Outward Processing Relief election via HM Customs for goods imported into Gibraltar, tailored or finished, and re-exported; (v) voluntary UK VAT registration election (HMRC form VAT1) where goods are fulfilled from a UK warehouse; this election does not create any Gibraltar VAT liability.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Purchase Tax and Customs Import Duty (VAT not in force in Gibraltar; UK VAT not applicable within the territory)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 0 (nil threshold). Every importer of goods into Gibraltar, irrespective of turnover or consignment value, must hold a Gibraltar EORI number and register with HM Customs under the Purchase Tax Ordinance; no economic-turnover exemption equivalent to a VAT threshold exists. For e-commerce sales through Stripe and Shopify Payments, the Bermuda Exempted Company or its Gibraltar branch, as importer of record, must complete registration before goods are shipped into Gibraltar; a separate registration is required even if the entity is already UK VAT registered because Gibraltar is outside the UK VAT area.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Gibraltar to destinations outside Gibraltar, including the United Kingdom under the UK-Gibraltar customs union, attract a de facto 0% rate of Gibraltar Purchase Tax and Customs Duty. Eligibility conditions: (i) the exporter holds a valid Gibraltar EORI number at the export date; (ii) the goods (ISIC 3240 - tailored theatrical costume robes, synthetic wigs, and cosplay prop apparel under HS headings 6114, 6504, and 9503) physically leave Gibraltar within 3 months of import or manufacture; (iii) an electronic SAD export declaration is lodged and customs authorization for exit is obtained before departure; (iv) a genuine onward sale to a non-Gibraltar buyer is evidenced by a contract, commercial invoice, and payment settlement; (v) where duty-paid goods are re-exported after finishing, the exporter must obtain Outward Processing Relief and claim a refund of import duty within 12 months; (vi) remote tailoring and design services supplied to overseas customers are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax and are therefore effectively zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD EXP, lodged electronically with Gibraltar Customs Service)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Gibraltar
  • Commercial Invoice with full HS classification (6114.30, 6504.00, 9503.00) and ISIC 3240 product description
  • Packing List with weights and carton counts
  • Certificate of Origin (Gibraltar or Bermuda preferential origin statement, where claimed)
  • Proof of prior importation (Import Entry Summary or Import SAD) where the goods were initially declared duty-paid
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or merchant settlement statement (Stripe or Shopify payout record) evidencing receipt from the overseas buyer
  • Carrier export validation receipt (UPS, DHL, or FedEx) for courier dispatches
  • Customs Outward Processing Relief authorization, where repayment of import duty is sought

No real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory e-invoicing, and no API digital-stamping requirement applies in Gibraltar. Customs clearance is electronic through the Gibraltar Customs Service portal using the Single Administrative Document (SAD) and the NCTS/ICS systems operated jointly with UK HMRC. A standard commercial invoice (PDF or EDI) and packing list satisfy documentation requirements; for Shopify and Stripe settlements, the platform payout statement is accepted as evidence of consideration. No government fiscal stamp or invoice pre-clearance is required.

HM Customs and GITO may open retrospective audits for up to six years, with no time limit in cases of fraud. A false export zero-rating claim exposes the exporter to clawback of the full unpaid Purchase Tax or Import Duty, an additional default penalty equal to 100% of the duty, and interest at 4% per annum from the departure date. Fraudulent export documentation, including fabricated bills of lading, circular remittances, or fictitious buyers, may be prosecuted under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance with fines of up to GIP 500,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 7 years. For income tax purposes, a disallowed export claim triggers the 50% mitigation penalty in the Income Tax Act 2010 and the loss of any applicable export incentive. At the Bermuda level, knowingly false declarations may vitiate the Exempted Undertaking tax-protection undertaking and may result in director disqualification by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP is fully convertible into GBP at par, and the jurisdiction imposes no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. Gibraltar retained no residual controls following the UK's repeal of exchange control legislation (1979); profit repatriation, dividend distributions, returns of capital, and intercompany loan repayments are free from FX licensing, prior approval, or earmarking requirements. Multi-currency accounts (GBP, EUR, USD) may be held freely, and e-commerce proceeds settled by Stripe or Shopify Payments may be retained or converted without GFSC direction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender rules exist. There is no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or requirement to repatriate export proceeds into GIP. Receipts from the export of cosplay apparel, licensed theatrical costume robes, synthetic wigs, and props may be maintained indefinitely in foreign currency or to fund overseas supplier disbursements without GFSC or fiscal approval.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no standalone central bank. Issuance of the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) is administered by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934 with a statutory 1:1 parity to GBP; monetary policy is effectively delegated by alignment to the Bank of England (BoE) for GBP money market conditions. Financial services regulation, banking supervision, AML/CFT supervision, and licensing of payment and electronic money institutions are exercised exclusively by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for GFSC-licensed EMIs; High for UK FCA-authorised EMIs used in a secondary clearing capacity. Legal constraints: Gibraltar is outside the EEA passporting regime, so UK/EU EMI authorisations do not extend automatically to Gibraltar-provided payment services; a Gibraltar-incorporated corporate client may nonetheless use a UK EMI (e.g., Wise Platform, Currencycloud, OpenPayd) as a foreign customer, provided the EMI's terms permit the intended merchant and supplier payout flows. The primary treasury ledger, capital escrow custody, and GIP clearing must sit with a GFSC-licensed bank; downstream EMI rails are acceptable for supplier payments, marketplace settlements, and working-capital sweeps, subject to the EMI's own onboarding requiring evidence of the GFSC bank relationship and the underlying trade contracts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (1) remit via SWIFT MT103 from the Bermuda parent's licensed bank (e.g., HSBC Bermuda or Butterfield) to the GFSC-licensed receiving bank; (2) use ISO 20022 purpose code 'INTC' (intra-company payment) for shareholder loan funding or 'CAPC' (capital payment) for paid-in equity; (3) populate the remittance information field with the capital injection reference (e.g., 'CAP-INJ-2025-001') and cross-reference the board resolution; (4) provide the receiving bank a pre-notification compliance pack: board resolution, updated share register, UBO certification, and source-of-wealth documentation; (5) travel-rule data on the payer must be complete for all wire amounts, with enhanced verification applicable to transfers at or above EUR 1,000 equivalent; (6) instruct charge code SHA (shared) to preserve net settlement value; (7) register the injection in the company's statutory records and reconcile against the banks' beneficiary advice for audit trail completeness.Clearing rails: (a) SWIFT MT103 supplier payments for fabric, dyes, synthetic wig fibres, fasteners, and trim under USD 100,000 per transaction, using ISO purpose code 'SUPP' (supplier payment) or 'TRAD' (trade payment) and referencing the purchase order; (b) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) for bulk container shipments of polyurethane props, moulds, and costume accessories, issued at 90-day usance with the beneficiary's confirming bank; (c) Documentary collections under URC 522 for medium-value shipments where the supplier accepts draft against documents; (d) direct debit and card rails through the downstream EMI (Wise/Currencycloud) for low-value raw-material top-ups and overseas warehouse fees; (e) domestic GIP operating expenses (payroll, rent, utilities) cleared through the GFSC-regulated interbank settlement system in Gibraltar; Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

10% flat corporate income tax on the assessable profits of a Gibraltar-resident company (Income Tax Act 2010, s.34); a non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company is charged at 10% only on Gibraltar-source profit attributable to its Gibraltar permanent establishment. No Gibraltar capital gains tax, no VAT, no wealth tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The GITO issues a Notice to File under s.143 ITA 2010 after the accounting period end. The return must be delivered within 30 days of the notice; for a 31 December accounting date the administrative filing date is 30 September of the following year, and assessed tax is payable within 30 days of the assessment notice. Interest runs at 8% p.a. on late-paid tax.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Returns due within 15 days after the end of each calendar quarter; payments due on the same date.
