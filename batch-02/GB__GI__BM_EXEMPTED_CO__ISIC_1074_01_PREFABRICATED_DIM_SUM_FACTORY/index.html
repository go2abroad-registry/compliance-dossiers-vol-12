Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax), New Harbours House, Gibraltar; corporate registry portal: Registry of Companies, Gibraltar (www.companieshouse.gi); income tax e-forms and assessments via the Income Tax Office portal (www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax-office); customs and excise competence: Customs and Excise Department, Gibraltar (www.customs.gov.gi), operating the electronic SAD/EDI customs clearance system.

E-government filing portal

STEP 1: The Bermuda Exempted Company must, within one month of establishing its industrial factory in Gibraltar, file the statutory overseas-company registration form with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under section 100 of the Companies Act 2014, appointing a Gibraltar resident authorised representative and a registered office, and obtaining a Gibraltar company registration number. STEP 2: Submit the prescribed corporate tax registration form to the Income Tax Office; the Office issues a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) and opens the 12.5% corporate assessment file, which also covers employer payroll tax and Social Insurance contributions for production staff. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda domicile) — the Bermuda Exempted Company is not subject to Bermuda income tax, capital gains tax or withholding on distributions; however, the Gibraltar factory constitutes a permanent establishment, and PE-attributable profits are subject to entity-level territorial taxation in Gibraltar at the flat rate of 12.5%. The Tax_Exempt_Zone classification accordingly applies to the company's residual Bermuda profile only, while the Gibraltar operational substance is within ordinary entity-level tax jurisdiction.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not apply the US check-the-box regime; an incorporated limited company is always a separate taxable entity and no transparency election is available. Available statutory elections and mechanisms: (i) capital allowances election — plant, machinery and industrial refrigeration equipment may be pooled with first-year allowances claimed at statutory rates; (ii) loss carry-forward election — trading losses may be offset against future PE profits without time limit; (iii) connected-company loss aggregation — where the factory and related Gibraltar entities satisfy the statutory group linkage requirements, eligible losses may be surrendered intra-group; (iv) advance tax ruling agreement — the Income Tax Office may be invited to agree the factory's transfer pricing methodology (e.g., cost-plus remuneration vis-à-vis the Bermuda head office); (v) hybrid-mismatch rules implementing OECD BEPS Action 2 — these impose mandatory adjustments rather than elective relief, and a self-correcting disclosure is required for any hybrid financing instrument issued by the Bermudian parent to the Gibraltar PE. The GAAR (Income Tax (Amendment) Act 2019) overrides all elections where arrangements lack commercial substance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST or sales-and-use tax is in force in Gibraltar (Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area). The indirect tax regime comprises customs import duty and excise duty under the Customs and Excise Duties Act 2016, chargeable only where goods are imported for local consumption or locally produced petroleum/alcohol/tobacco products are released; goods exported from Gibraltar are relieved from all Gibraltar indirect taxation.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Gibraltar imposes no turnover-based indirect tax registration threshold because no VAT/GST exists. Compulsory customs registration (local EORI-equivalent trading number) applies to all importers and exporters regardless of turnover, and the frozen food factory must additionally hold a food business operator licence as a precondition of customs trading status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating is not applicable because Gibraltar levies no VAT. To secure relief from indirect tax on exports, the factory must prove: (i) physical departure of the frozen dim sum/dumplings (HS Ch. 16/19/04 as applicable) from Gibraltar territory under customs control via the Port of Gibraltar or Gibraltar International Airport; (ii) sale at arm's length between the Gibraltar PE of the Bermuda Exempted Company and an independent buyer in the EU, UK or a third country; (iii) freight and customs documents naming a foreign consignee; (iv) receipt of sale proceeds by SWIFT LC or TT wire into the Gibraltar bank account, with the inward remittance matching the export invoice; and (v) compliance with destination-market sanitary and border-control certificates. Where import duty was incurred on raw materials (wheat, seafood, meat, seasonings) later exported in finished form, the operator may claim duty drawback under the drawback regulations or apply for duty suspension via customs warehousing/outward processing for bonded ingredient inventory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EDI) bearing a unique Gibraltar customs reference number
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight with Port of Gibraltar wharf clearance stamp) or Air Waybill (air freight)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — SWIFT MT103 / TT wire credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice on the factory's letterhead indicating ISIC 1074 scope and HS classifications of the frozen dim sum
  • Certificate of Origin (EUR.1/ATR movement certificate for EU preferential trade, or GSP Form A for third-country buyers)
  • Health/sanitary export certificate and batch HACCP temperature log issued by the Gibraltar Health Authority and the factory quality-assurance unit

No real-time fiscal clearance, government API e-invoicing or digital stamping mandate applies in Gibraltar. The Customs and Excise Department operates an electronic SAD/EDI platform for lodging import and export customs declarations before physical movement, but there is no B2G clearance API for ordinary commercial invoices. The factory must retain invoices, delivery notes, HACCP batch logs and customs declarations for at least six years under the Income Tax Act and the Customs and Excise Duties Act for audit reconstruction.

The Income Tax Office and the Customs and Excise Department jointly audit export files. False declarations, overstatement of export quantities or undervaluation of export prices trigger: (i) customs penalties up to the higher of three times the evaded duty or valuation-based fines, with forfeiture of goods; (ii) clawback of customs duty drawbacks with interest at 8% per annum; (iii) transfer-pricing re-attribution of profit to the Gibraltar PE where export sales routed through the Bermuda parent do not satisfy the arm's-length standard; (iv) GAAR counteraction, with penalties up to 100% of the undercharged tax, where the export structure is artificial; and (v) criminal liability for fraudulent drawback or export-credit claims punishable by up to seven years' imprisonment and unlimited fines. Export proceeds not repatriated within the 180-day window are treated as available to the Gibraltar PE and assessed to Gibraltar tax unless a bona fide commercial deferral is documented.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom and Gibraltar operate fully liberalised current and capital accounts with no exchange-control authorisation for capital injection, trade settlement, loan servicing, profit repatriation or dividend distribution. The Gibraltar pound is pegged 1:1 to GBP, and the Bank of England and Gibraltar clearing banks provide unrestricted convertibility across GBP, USD and EUR. The Bermuda Exempted Company may therefore fund the Gibraltar operating treasury without central-bank pre-approval; bank-level source-of-funds and sanctions checks remain the only practical compliance gates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriation of export receipts under UK or Gibraltar law. Export proceeds may be retained in original currency, converted at market rates, or applied directly to trade disbursements through the corporate treasury. The factory must retain customs declarations, bills of lading, commercial invoices and VAT/export records solely for tax and audit purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England together with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission; the Government of Gibraltar issues Gibraltar pounds at par with sterling.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium as a secondary disbursement rail only. UK-authorised digital EMIs may support GBP/EUR/USD collections, FX conversion and faster payments, but they are legally constrained from providing credit, commercial lending, letters of credit or full treasury clearing. Offshore or EEA-only EMIs without a Gibraltar licence or UK FCA passport should not be used as the principal clearing rail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GBP 500,000 – a Gibraltar Local File is required when the aggregate value of controlled transactions (including purchases of raw materials from related Bermudian or UK suppliers) exceeds this threshold. A Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds GBP 50 million.

Economic substance scope: Under the Gibraltar Economic Substance (Companies) Act 2019 (as amended), a company carrying on a 'relevant activity' in Gibraltar must demonstrate adequate substance. The frozen food factory constitutes manufacturing / distribution activity and therefore is a relevant activity. The company must hold at least one board meeting in Gibraltar, maintain physical premises, employ qualified local staff, and incur operating expenditure in parentheses proportion to profits. As a Bermuda Exempted Company, the entity is also subject to Bermuda's Economic Substance Amendment Act 2022 for its global holding activity; dual-substance reporting will be required in both Gibraltar and Bermuda to avoid penalties. Failure to satisfy either regime triggers information exchange with UK and Bermuda authorities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: A civil penalty of up to 150% of the understated tax plus interest and daily late-payment surcharges; criminal prosecution for tax evasion carries an unlimited fine and imprisonment of up to 3 years. The Commissioner may issue a 'notice to produce' for bank records, seize factory inventory, and freeze the company's Gibraltar payment accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax where the company is insolvent; persistent evasion results in travel bans and a custodial sentence of up to 36 months. The court may disqualify directors from acting as company officers in any British Overseas Territory for up to 15 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: An ESR penalty of GBP 50,000 plus GBP 500 for each day of continued non-compliance. The Registrar may strike the Gibbs company off the companies register, and in Bermuda the Registrar may impose US$10,000 penalties and enforce dissolution. The failure is automatically reported to the UK HMRC under the exchange of information provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable to pay the ESR fines if they knowingly allowed the company to operate without substance; the Gibraltar court may impose a travel ban preventing them from leaving Gibraltar pending the assessment, and repeat breaches may be prosecuted as fraud, leading to imprisonment for up to 2 years.
AML / Unlicensed Financial Activity

Statutory sanction: An AML civil penalty under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act of up to GBP 250,000 or 50% of the value of the transaction flow, whichever is higher; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission may revoke any licenses and confiscate the funds involved; criminal liability attaches where forgery of KYC documents is proved.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML breaches where they failed to appoint a Money Laundering Reporting Officer or knowingly approved the use of the company's current account for unlicensed settlement; this may result in personal fines, travel bans, and imprisonment of up to 7 years.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Tax authority re-computation of the Gibraltar PE profit to the arm's length margin, resulting in additional corporate tax at 10%; a penalty of 100% of the additional tax due, plus a separate GBP 3,000 fine for missing transfer-pricing records. Serious cases are referred to the Joint Tax Evasion Taskforce.

Director personal liability: A director signing a false transfer-pricing declaration becomes personally liable for the understated tax up to GBP 100,000; repeat offences invoke disqualification under the Company Directors Disqualification Act as applied in Gibraltar and a possible travel ban pending full settlement.
