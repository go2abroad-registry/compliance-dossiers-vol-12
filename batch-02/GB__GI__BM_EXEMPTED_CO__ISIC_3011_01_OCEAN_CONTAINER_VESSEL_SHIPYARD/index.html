Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar Commissioner of Income Tax (Income Tax Office, Gibraltar) together with Her Majesty's Customs Gibraltar (Customs & Excise Department, Gibraltar). Corporate registry portal: https://www.gibraltarlawcourts.gov.gi and tax forms portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company that commences a shipyard activity in Gibraltar (including a permanent establishment, branch, or direct shipbuilding operation) must first obtain a Gibraltar Company Certificate of Registration under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014, which generates a unique company number. It must then file a corporate tax registration application (Form TP-30) with the Commissioner of Income Tax, providing certified constitutional documents, the registered office lease in Gibraltar, director and shareholder particulars, and evidence of commercial substance (shipyard premises, bank account, and operational staff). Upon approval, the Income Tax Office issues a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN). Employer registration for PAYE and social insurance must be completed within 14 days of the first local hire. If the entity imports or exports vessel parts and inputs, an Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number must be activated through the Gibraltar Customs' Trader Portal prior to the first customs declaration. There is no separate VAT registration because Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area. A non-resident company that has no Gibraltar-source income and no PE is not required to obtain any Gibraltar tax identifier, but it must file a nil return annually if it is registered at the Companies House.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box classification system. A foreign-incorporated entity (e.g., the Bermuda Exempted Company) is treated as a non-resident company and is taxed only on profits arising in or derived from Gibraltar (Income Tax Act 2010, Section 8A). The company may nevertheless elect to be treated as an Ordinary Residence company by demonstrating that its central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar. For hybrid entities, the Gibraltar Tax (Hybrid and Arbitrage Arrangements) Rules 2020 require the filing of Form HYB-45 to disclose any mismatch outcome in accordance with ATAD/EU anti-hybrid directives. A Bermuda Exempted Company engaged in shipbuilding may also make a protective election under Section 44B of the Income Tax Act 2010 for the application of transfer pricing / arm's-length rules to its Gibraltar branch transactions. No statutory category of 'tax-exempt company' exists under Gibraltar law for a locally registered branch; exemption is limited to the Bermuda head-office level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Customs & Excise Duty System (no VAT, no GST, no general sales tax; selective excise duties, customs import duty, and insurance premium tax at 5% apply; shipbuilding inputs are eligible for duty relief under the Gibraltar Customs (Capital Goods) Arrangements)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold exists because there is no VAT regime. Any commercial importer or exporter operating through a Gibraltar shipyard must hold a Gibraltar Customs EORI number irrespective of transaction value. Importation of raw materials and shipbuilding equipment into Gibraltar may attract customs duty at rates between 0% and 12% ad valorem; however, machinery, naval equipment, and inputs intended for the construction, repair, or fitting-out of ocean-going vessels are generally entered duty-free under the Capital Goods and Industrial Development concessions. The de minimis threshold for simplified customs declarations is GIP 1,000 per consignment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating / 0% indirect tax is not applicable because Gibraltar levies no VAT on domestic or exported supplies. Instead, an export rebate / duty remission regime applies under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance: a shipyard that has imported inputs (steel plates, engines, electrical systems, fittings) on which customs duty was paid may claim a full rebate when those goods are incorporated into an ocean container vessel that is exported to a non-Gibraltar destination. Eligibility criteria: (1) the exporter holds a valid Customs Registered Shipyard licence; (2) the vessel is physically delivered to a buyer outside Gibraltar; (3) the exported goods are exported within 24 months of the original importation; (4) the shipyard maintains a consumption ledger linking each duty-paid input to the exported vessel; and (5) the vessel is declared under customs procedure EX1 with proof of arrival at the destination port. Services rendered for the construction, repair, or conversion of a qualifying vessel (offshore services) are outside the scope of Gibraltar duty and do not require a rebate application.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (SAD Form C88) with procedure code EX1
  • Bill of Lading (ocean transport) issued to the non-resident buyer evidencing port-to-port shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or SWIFT MT103 / bank statement) confirming receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Inward Processing Relief Authorization (C85) for duty-free importation of shipbuilding inputs, where claimed
  • Shipyard production and input-output consumption ledger (duty-paid and duty-suspended goods)
  • Build certificate issued by the classification society (e.g., DNV, ABS, Lloyd's Register, ClassNK) confirming the vessel specification and contract value
  • Commercial pro-forma invoice and sales contract providing full description of the ocean container vessel and the buyer's details

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal e-invoicing or continuous transaction controls. All customs operations are processed through the Gibraltar Customs Computerised Declaration (GCCD) Gateway, which accepts the Single Administrative Document (SAD) via EDI/API protocols at https://customs.gibraltar.gov.gi. Commercial exporters of newly built vessels must submit an electronic export declaration with procedure code EX1 (definitive export) and receive a customs export acceptance number before the vessel leaves the port. Digital stamping (excise markers) is mandatory only for alcohol, tobacco, and hydrocarbon oil; plain electronic manifest reporting applies to shipyard goods. Vessel intra-community or third-country supply documentation must be cleared through the GCCD within 48 hours of vessel departure.

Customs duty rebate and IPR claims are subject to post-clearance audit by the Comptroller of Customs under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance (Section 50). The audit window extends for eight years from the date of export clearance. Where an exporter is found to have made a false statement, misdescribed goods, or failed to physically export the vessel within the prescribed period, the law authorizes: (a) immediate withdrawal of the rebate/concession with retrospective clawback of all duty remitted; (b) a civil penalty of up to GIP 10,000 or three times the evaded duty, whichever is greater; (c) forfeiture of the goods or, if exported, a monetary penalty equal to the full export value; and (d) prosecution as a criminal offence with fines up to GIP 100,000 and terms of imprisonment up to seven years for directors and officers. Additionally, the Commissioner of Income Tax may re-characterize a fraudulent export transaction under the anti-avoidance provisions of Income Tax Act 2010, Section 37, imposing a 100% restitution charge on the benefit obtained and disallowing any related deductions. In cases involving financing arrangements, failure to repatriate and remit the export proceeds within 180 days triggers foreign-exchange and tax reporting sanctions under the Exchange Control (Gibraltar) Notice, unless an extension is obtained prior to expiry.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound enjoys full, unrestricted convertibility into GBP at par, and no exchange-control licences are required for current or capital account transactions. Non-resident Bermuda Exempted Companies may freely hold and remit USD, EUR, GBP or GIP; profit repatriation and capital repatriation are subject only to standard AML/CFT documentation, beneficial-ownership verification and economic-substance records. There are no surrender, approval or priority requirements on inward or outward remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar and the UK impose no mandatory export-proceeds surrender or statutory conversion ratio. Commercial shipbuilders and related trading entities may retain 100% of foreign-currency export receipts. Banks apply only suspicion-based reporting under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar) and record-keeping under the AML/CFT regime; there is no central-bank exchange-control form for export cash-flow.

Central bank supervision: Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar, acting through the Financial Services Commission (prudential and AML/CFT supervision) and the Gibraltar Currency Notes Act (currency issuance); the Bank of England operates as the de facto monetary anchor because the Gibraltar pound is pegged at par with sterling.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating is Medium (supplementary only). Gibraltar-law e-money institutions regulated by the Financial Services Commission can provide low-value payment accounts, virtual IBANs and cross-border collection services, but an offshore digital EMI without physical substance in Gibraltar cannot serve as the sole clearing bank for a Medium-Risk-Enhanced shipyard treasury. Legal constraints include EMIR/PSD responsibilities, inability to access CHAPS directly, and bank-compliance reluctance to rely on an unlicensed EMI as a defence against AML risk.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% – Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar company or on profits remitted by a Gibraltar branch to its foreign head office. The Bermuda Exempted Company receiving dividends from its Gibraltar PE or branch is paid gross, with no Gibraltar withholding obligation.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return for a Year of Assessment is due by 30 November of the immediately following calendar year (e.g., YA 2025 return due 30 November 2026). Where the PE prepares accounts to a date other than 31 December, the profits of the accounting period ending within the YA are assessed, and the 30 November deadline remains fixed. There are no quarterly CIT instalments in Gibraltar; the full 12.5% tax liability is due by the same 30 November deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar's Income Tax (Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalisation) Rules, aligned with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, require a mandatory contemporaneous Local File where the aggregate annual value of controlled cross-border transactions exceeds GIP 1.0 million (approximately GBP 1.0 million), and a Master File where worldwide group turnover exceeds GIP 50.0 million and the Gibraltar PE's controlled transactions exceed GIP 5.0 million. The container-vessel shipyard (ISIC 3011) is expected to exceed these thresholds through steel procurement, equipment imports, group management fees and construction financing, thereby mandating a full OECD tiered documentation file: Local File, Master File, PE allocation schedules, and a benchmarking study prepared before the filing deadline.
