E-government filing portal

For a non-UK incorporated entity (BVI Business Company) with a UK establishment or UK taxable activities: (1) register the UK establishment at Companies House (Form FC01 / overseas company registration) if creating a branch or place of business; (2) apply to HMRC for a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) for UK corporation tax using form CT400 or via HMRC's online non-resident company registration service; (3) register for UK VAT through HMRC's VAT online service using the Non-Established Taxable Person (NETP) route if the entity has no UK establishment, or as a UK-established taxable person if it has a permanent establishment here; (4) obtain a Government Gateway user ID and activate the relevant tax services; (5) provide original incorporation documents, registered office address, UK place of business address, and details of directors/beneficial owners for anti-money-laundering verification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The UK has no check-the-box election. A BVI Business Company is generally treated as an opaque, non-UK-resident company for UK tax purposes unless central management and control is exercised in the UK. The UK's hybrid mismatch rules (CTA 2010 ss. 259A–259EB) contain election and counteraction mechanisms for entities treated as transparent in one jurisdiction and opaque in another; no permanent formal election is available for a BVI company as such, but taxpayers may be required to submit disclosure notifications to HMRC under the hybrid mismatch regime for certain arrangements. The BVI tax-exempt status does not confer UK tax-exempt status; UK corporation tax applies on any UK-source trading income attributable to a UK permanent establishment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 taxable turnover in a rolling 12-month period for UK-established businesses; non-established taxable persons (NETPs) have no registration threshold and must register before making taxable supplies in the UK if an assessment of taxable business activity is established

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under UK VAT is available for goods exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK, provided the supplier retains evidence of exportation within statutory time limits (usually removal within 3 months, with alternative evidence, and records kept within 6 months of removal). For virtual CFO and outsourced accounting services supplied by a UK-established taxable person to an overseas business customer, the services are generally outside the scope of UK VAT under the B2B place-of-supply rules where the customer belongs outside the UK, with the customer being liable for VAT in its own jurisdiction. To claim outside-the-scope treatment, the supplier must hold proof of the customer's business status (e.g., VAT registration number or equivalent tax reference), the nature of the services, and verification that the customer belongs outside the UK. For B2C recipients outside the UK, the place of supply is the UK and VAT must be charged unless covered by a specific zero-rating provision.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic export declaration submitted to the UK Customs Declaration Service)
  • Commercial invoice and contract/purchase order
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill or other transport evidence
  • Packing list
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank payment confirmation if applicable as supplementary evidence
  • Proof of business status of overseas recipient (e.g., VAT registration number or equivalent tax identifier)

The UK does not operate a mandatory central e-invoicing clearance regime, real-time fiscal stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) authorisation. Electronic invoices are permitted and must be retained in their original digital form. All VAT-registered businesses are required to keep digital records and submit VAT returns via functional compatible software under Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT using HMRC-recognised APIs. There is no requirement for pre-clearance or digital stamping of invoices by HMRC before issue.

HMRC may conduct substantive post-export verification and audit of zero-rated removals and outside-the-scope service supplies. Failure to retain sufficient evidence may result in assessment of unrecovered VAT plus interest, with retrospective clawback of input VAT previously reclaimed and of the zero-rating benefit. Penalties include misdeclaration penalties of up to 100% of the tax involved for deliberate inaccuracies, default penalties based on VAT owed, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion of VAT (including deliberate false documentation or fraudulent export claim). Deliberate errors may extend HMRC's assessment time limit to 20 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account with zero exchange-control restrictions and zero prior-approval requirements for non-resident entities. The BVI Business Company (BVI BC) may freely hold, convert, and remit sterling and any foreign currency. Profit repatriation and dividend distributions in favour of a BVI ultimate parent are unrestricted, and there is no UK withholding tax on dividends or interest paid by a UK company to a BVI company. Practical hurdles are purely documentary: UK banks require proof of UBOs above 10%, a Companies House overseas-company registration (if a UK establishment is maintained), HMRC AML registration for accountancy service providers under MLR 2017, and the BVI entity's Economic Substance Notification/Filing evidencing compliance with BVI Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Act 2018. Caution: if central management and control is exercised from the UK, the BVI BC becomes UK tax resident and is chargeable on worldwide profits (main rate 25% above £250,000; small-profits rate 19% below £50,000, with marginal relief in between). Board meetings, key decision-making, and client advisory activity must therefore be substantively conducted from the BVI or another non-UK jurisdiction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The UK imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export or service-export proceeds. All receipts from outsourced virtual-CFO and multicurrency bookkeeping services (ISIC 6920) may be retained 100% in foreign currency or converted at the entity's discretion. The only applicable obligations are: (i) the retained EU Cash Controls Regulation 2018/1672 requiring a written declaration for cash or bearer negotiable instruments of €10,000 or more entering or leaving the UK; (ii) Office for National Statistics balance-of-payments survey reporting where the entity falls within the direct-investment statistical net; and (iii) OFSI sanctions-screening and transaction-blocking obligations under the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018. No foreign-exchange custody timeline, surrender ratio, or central-bank conversion rule applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (BoE), the United Kingdom's central bank; exchange-control and currency sovereignty reside with HM Treasury under the Currency and Bank Notes Act 1954. The United Kingdom abolished all exchange controls upon repeal of the Exchange Control Act 1947, and no separate foreign-exchange administration exists. Prudential regulation of authorised banks is exercised by the Prudential Regulation Authority (a BoE division); conduct and markets conduct is regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Anti-money-laundering supervision of accountancy and bookkeeping service providers is performed by HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) under the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017 (MLR 2017).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for operational collections and vendor disbursements; Medium for primary treasury custody of large balances. Wise Payments Limited (FCA-authorised E-Money Institution, FRN 900507) and Stripe Payments UK Limited (FCA-authorised E-Money Institution, FRN 900467) can onboard a BVI Business Company, providing a UK sort code and account number. Wise clears via Faster Payments/CHAPS, while Stripe Billing operates card-scheme collection. Legal constraints: (i) EMI balances are safeguarded in segregated accounts at tier-1 UK banks and are not covered by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme, but are statutorily safeguarded under the E-Money Regulations 2011; (ii) onboarding requires the BVI BC to be in good standing with the BVI Registry of Corporate Affairs, a notarised register of directors and UBOs above 10%, and a confirmation-of-account letter from an existing regulated bank — a circular remittance-block that is best resolved by first opening a tier-1 UK account; (iii) EMIs may impose limits on payouts to offshore beneficiaries and on high-volume professional-fee flows, though moderate advisory revenue is accepted; (iv) Wise and Stripe are not designed for high-value HMRC tax payments — CHAPS from a tier-1 bank is mandatory for large tax disbursements.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For equity injection from the BVI parent or ultimate shareholder, the compliant route is a SWIFT MT103 from the remitting bank to the BVI BC's UK-based account, referenced as 'Contribution to share capital — no consideration' (not a UK taxable receipt). The MT103 must carry the beneficiary BIC/IBAN and the beneficiary account name must exactly match the registered BVI BC name; the sending bank applies UK sanctions and sub-sanction screening. No UK exchange-control approval is required. For operational revenue, the rails are: (i) Stripe Billing for card/ACH collections from UK and EU clients, settled via SEPA to the company's Wise or tier-1 account; (ii) Wise Business for cross-border MT103/MT102 batch conversions; and (iii) direct client payments via CHAPS for amounts above £1,000,000. Inbound payments should quote ISO 20022 purpose code SUPP (supplier payment) or INTC (intercompany). For equity injections, the bank files ONS direct-investment survey data on the entity's behalf; no separate declaration is required for service receipts.Primary clearing rails for outbound disbursements: (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border vendor payments, shareholder dividends, and redemptions, executed through the tier-1 relationship bank; (ii) CHAPS for same-day sterling payments to HMRC (quoting the relevant HMRC tax reference) and for disbursements above £1,000,000; (iii) Faster Payments Service for UK sub-£1,000,000 vendor invoices; (iv) SEPA Credit Transfer for euro-denominated vendor payments; and (v) Commercial Letters of Credit — technically permissible but inapplicable to ISIC 6920 advisory services; where a client mandates an LC, a 'clean reimbursement' LC via SWIFT MT700/MT740 with the BVI BC as beneficiary is achievable only if the BC maintains a trade-services mandate at HSBC or Standard Chartered. The BC must not co-mingle client money with its own treasury balances; any funds held for onward payment on behalf of clients (e.g., HMRC tax deposits) must be booked in a separately labelled 'Client Account', otherwise they are exposed to the BC's creditors and constitute a regulatory breach.Outright deliverable FX forwards under a standard ISDA/FXA master agreement with a UK bank market-maker for GBP/USD and GBP/EUR; permitted without UK FX-control approval and available where the BVI BC qualifies as a professional client or eligible counterparty.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies with non-convertible or restricted spot markets (e.g., USD/CNH, USD/INR, USD/NGN) where the BC must hedge multi-currency client receipts; permitted under the FCA regulatory perimeter, subject to an ISDA 1998 NDF Annex and a bilateral credit-limit facility.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) for long-dated conversion of GBP/USD liability or revenue streams; permitted under UK law; mandatory clearing at a UK-recognised CCP (e.g., LCH SwapClear) applies only if the BC is a financial counterparty or exceeds the UK EMIR clearing threshold; otherwise the swap is non-cleared but must be reported to a UK trade repository.Vanilla European FX options for event-driven hedging of large dividend payments or milestone billings; permitted with upfront premium and an ISDA Credit Support Annex (CSA) requiring cash collateral.Passive multi-currency overlay via Wise Multi-Currency Account manual conversions and Stripe Currency Conversion (which applies a markup); these are operational settlement tools, not true hedge instruments, and shall not be used for balance-sheet risk management.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High for operational collections and vendor disbursements; Medium for primary treasury custody of large balances. Wise Payments Limited (FCA-authorised E-Money Institution, FRN 900507) and Stripe Payments UK Limited (FCA-authorised E-Money Institution, FRN 900467) can onboard a BVI Business Company, providing a UK sort code and account number. Wise clears via Faster Payments/CHAPS, while Stripe Billing operates card-scheme collection. Legal constraints: (i) EMI balances are safeguarded in segregated accounts at tier-1 UK banks and are not covered by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme, but are statutorily safeguarded under the E-Money Regulations 2011; (ii) onboarding requires the BVI BC to be in good standing with the BVI Registry of Corporate Affairs, a notarised register of directors and UBOs above 10%, and a confirmation-of-account letter from an existing regulated bank — a circular remittance-block that is best resolved by first opening a tier-1 UK account; (iii) EMIs may impose limits on payouts to offshore beneficiaries and on high-volume professional-fee flows, though moderate advisory revenue is accepted; (iv) Wise and Stripe are not designed for high-value HMRC tax payments — CHAPS from a tier-1 bank is mandatory for large tax disbursements.
