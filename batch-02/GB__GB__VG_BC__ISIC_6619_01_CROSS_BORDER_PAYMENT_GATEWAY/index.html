Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) — unitary national tax authority for the United Kingdom. Primary digital portal: GOV.UK HMRC services via the HMRC Government Gateway (gov.uk/register-for-vat; gov.uk/paye-online; gov.uk/corporation-tax-register/overseas-company).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident BVI Business Company is not automatically registered for UK taxation. Procedure: (1) If taxable supplies of goods or services are made in the UK, the foreign entity must register for VAT before the date of first supply — non-established taxable persons use Form VAT1 accompanied by Form VAT1E, submitted to HMRC's Non-Established Taxable Persons Unit; HMRC issues a UK VAT registration number, effective from the date the obligation arises. (2) For Corporation Tax, a non-resident company with a UK permanent establishment (UK PE) must apply to HMRC using Form CAG1 (Registration for Corporation Tax of a non-resident company); HMRC issues a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) and 10-digit Corporation Tax reference. (3) If income arises from UK property or other UK-source income not attributable to a PE, the BVI company remains within the charge to income tax and must issue Form SA547/SAO registration as appropriate. (4) If staff are physically employed in the UK, registration for Pay As You Earn (PAYE), Class 1A NICs, and the Construction Industry Scheme (if applicable) may be required. (5) All HMRC dealings require prior activation of a Government Gateway ID, appointment of an authorised agent via Form 64-8, and adherence to Making Tax Digital (MTD) obligations for VAT. No statutory certificate of residence is issued by HMRC; reliance on the BVI tax exemption is based on the BVI International Business Companies Act and the UK-BVI Tax Information Exchange Agreement, as no full double taxation treaty exists between the UK and BVI.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The UK does not operate a check-the-box election equivalent to US Treas. Reg. §301.7701-3; classification of foreign entities follows domestic law and OECD permanent establishment principles. Relevant statutory elections include: (1) an election under CTA 2010, s.18(2) for a non-resident company to be charged to Corporation Tax only on profits attributable to a UK PE; (2) hybrid and branch mismatch adjustment elections under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A (including coordinated elections under the OECD BEPS Action 2 recommendations) to align deduction/corporate tax outcomes; (3) notification obligations under Diverted Profits Tax (FA 2015 ss.80-93) where arrangements lack economic substance; (4) CFC (Controlled Foreign Company) election/justification under CTA 2010 Part 9A where BVI profits are attributable to a UK resident controller; (5) option to tax (waiver of exemption) on UK land under Schedule 10 VATA 1994 if property interests are held; (6) VAT grouping election under VATA 1994 s.43A for closely-linked UK-established entities to simplify VAT administration; (7) partial exemption annual adjustment election under VAT Regulations SI 1995/2518 Reg. 102-104. All elections are made via HMRC returns or formal notices; none confer pass-through status for a BVI BC in the UK.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

UK-established taxable persons: £90,000 (12-month rolling turnover). Non-established (non-resident) persons: nil threshold — any taxable supply made in the UK triggers compulsory VAT registration regardless of turnover. Separate effective threshold of £90,000 for distance sales into the UK (post-Brexit); Northern Ireland remains aligned to EU VAT distance selling rules under the Windsor Framework. No registration threshold for receiving services from abroad under the reverse charge mechanism.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% applies strictly to exports of goods removed from the UK to a destination outside the UK in the course of a supply by a taxable person (VATA 1994 Sch. 8 Group 6; VAT Notice 703), including: (a) direct exports by the supplier; (b) goods sold to overseas customers and shipped by the customer's own agent; (c) goods removed to a customs warehouse in the UK prior to export; and (d) certain international transport, freight forwarding, and ancillary services (VAT Notice 744A). For a cross-border payment gateway / merchant acquiring service (ISIC 6619), the principal supplies are payment processing, clearance, and settlement services; where provided to a business customer outside the UK, the B2B place-of-supply rule (VATA 1994 s.7A; SI 1992/3121 Reg. 9) places the supply outside the UK — such supplies are 'outside the scope' of UK VAT, not technically zero-rated. Supplies made within the UK to any customer (including UK merchants) of payment transaction services fall under Schedule 9 Group 5 (exemption for transactions concerning payments or transfers), which is exempt rather than zero-rated, leading to partial exemption restrictions on input VAT recovery. Non-resident PSPs may recover residual UK input VAT only if they have a UK VAT registration and satisfy HMRC repayment requirements.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill as carrier-issued evidence of export movement

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or pre-clearance authorisation of invoices exists in the UK regime. Electronic invoicing is permitted and increasingly standard, but HMRC mandates Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT: all VAT-registered businesses (from 1 April 2022, with mandatory digital links from 1 April 2023) must maintain digital VAT records in MTD-compatible software and submit each VAT return using HMRC's MTD VAT Application Programming Interface (API) over OAuth 2.0 with JSON payloads. E-invoicing must otherwise satisfy the content and authenticity requirements of VATA 1994 Sch. 11 and SI 1995/2518 Regs. 13-17 (full invoice or simplified invoice). For public sector transactions, PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 e-invoicing is compulsory for suppliers to UK public bodies under the UK government's e-invoicing mandate (from 4 April 2022). No obligation exists to obtain HMRC approval prior to issuing invoices, but accurate VAT control accounts and audit trails are required.

HMRC may subject all zero-rating and repayment claims to post-clearance audit under Regulation 199 of SI 1995/2518 and VAT Notice 703 procedures. If documentary evidence is absent, false, or untimely, HMRC issues an assessment for the unpaid output tax plus statutory interest under VATA 1994 s.74. Penalty exposure: (a) inaccuracies in VAT returns — up to 30% for careless errors, up to 70% for deliberate errors not concealed, and up to 100% for deliberate and concealed errors under FA 2007 Sch. 24; (b) dishonest evasion — up to 100% penalty under VATA 1994 s.60-61; (c) Failure-to-notify penalties under FA 2008 Sch. 41 up to 30% (or 100% if deliberate and concealed). Retrospective clawback operates through HMRC assessments with time limits of 2 years (ordinary), 4 years (careless), and 20 years (deliberate) under FA 2008 Sch. 36. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export zero-rating or missing trader intra-community fraud under VATA 1994 s.72 exposes individuals to imprisonment up to 7 years and unlimited fines, with Director Disqualification Orders for company officers. Payments made to foreign PSPs in connection with exports may also be scrutinised under anti-money laundering and sanctions controls imposed by the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation and FCA payments regulations.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GBP is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions under IMF Article VIII obligations; there is no statutory distinction between current and capital account treatment and no prior regulatory approval is required for capital injection, dividend distribution, or profit repatriation from UK-based accounts of a BVI corporate entity; the only operative restraints are AML/CTF verification under MLR 2017, sanctions screening against the UK Sanctions List and OFAC, source-of-wealth enquiry by receiving banks, and HMRC reporting obligations; dividend/profit remittance by a UK branch of a BVI company may attract interest-withholding duties under domestic anti-avoidance rules but no exchange-control consent

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil — the UK imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio; a BVI-incorporated exporter or payment gateway may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts in unrestricted offshore or domestic accounts; the only statutory obligations are balance-of-payments data returns to the Office for National Statistics (ONS/BoP survey) and suspicious transaction reporting under POCA 2002, TA 2000, and the Money Laundering Regulations 2017

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (HM Treasury exercises policy ownership; the Financial Conduct Authority administers the Payment Services Regulations 2017 and Money Laundering Regulations 2017; the UK abolished statutory exchange controls in 1979 and maintains no separate Foreign Exchange Administration authority)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium — FCA-authorised e-money institutions operating in the UK (e.g., Revolut Business, Tide, Wise Platform, Modulr, and Payment Services Provider-class EMIs) will consider onboarding a BVI business company only where the entity demonstrates UK substance and an authorised sponsor; legal constraint: a BVI-incorporated merchant acquirer under ISIC 6619 cannot itself hold an FCA EMI licence, so the PSP must either obtain FCA authorisation via a UK entity or execute a formal sponsorship/agency arrangement with an FCA-authorised payment institution; most EMIs contractually prohibit unlicensed merchant acquiring, impose strict velocity and monthly-volume caps, and require enhanced due diligence on BVI beneficial owners; standalone, unlicensed EMI onboarding probability is Low-Medium; viability materially improves with (i) an FCA-authorised UK principal, (ii) a registered UK service address, (iii) audited financial statements, and (iv) a licensed card-scheme sponsor agreement

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (main rate for profits exceeding £250,000); 19% (small profits rate for profits not exceeding £50,000); marginal relief between £50,000 and £250,000. Non-UK resident companies are taxed at UK corporation tax rates on profits attributable to a UK permanent establishment (PE).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the UK does not impose any withholding tax on dividends since 2016. BVI levies no withholding tax on distributions made by a BVI Business Company.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The return must be delivered no later than 12 months after the end of the accounting period. Corporation tax payment is due 9 months and 1 day after the accounting period end for non-large companies; large companies pay by quarterly installments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The UK does not prescribe a fixed statutory threshold for preparing a Local File or Master File. HMRC expects robust contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation if (i) the company is not a 'small or medium enterprise' (SME), i.e., more than 250 employees, turnover > £50 million, or balance sheet > £43 million; (ii) related-party cross-border transactions exceed £5 million per category (services, finance, goods); or (iii) the multinational group is required to file Country-by-Country (CbC) report (consolidated group revenue ≥ €750 million). In practice, a fully documented Local File and Master File should be prepared for UK-related party transactions above £5 million.

Economic substance scope: The UK does not have Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the BVI company is subject to BVI ESR. A BVI Business Company carrying on 'relevant activity' (e.g., financing, headquarters, distribution) must file an Economic Substance Return in BVI and demonstrate substantial activities there. For a cross-border payment gateway, the core income-generating activity is typically considered to be the provision of payment processing services. If the company is tax resident in the UK by virtue of central management and control, it may be exempt from BVI ESR, but must declare UK residence to the BVI International Tax Authority. Operationally, the UK PE must maintain real presence, qualified staff, decision-making records, and substantive payment gateway contracts in the UK; otherwise HMRC can apply the Diverted Profits Tax (25%) or general anti-abuse rule.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: HMRC may impose penalty up to 100% of the additional tax (higher for concealed and deliberate conduct), interest, and criminal prosecution for cheating the public revenue. Imprisonment up to 14 years and unlimited fines are possible.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may be prosecuted under the Criminal Finances Act 2017 for facilitating tax evasion (corporate offence) with unlimited fines; disqualification as a company director under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986 for up to 15 years; court orders freezing assets and imposing travel bans.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance / Diverted Profits Tax (DPT)

Statutory sanction: Diverted Profits Tax at 25% of the 'avoided profits', plus additional HMRC penalties; accelerated payment notices (90 days) and interest; counteraction under CTA 2010 or GAAR. HMRC can also issue a VAT penalty where appropriate.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for FCA and HMRC penalties if the payment gateway continues to operate without a valid licence; they can be prohibited under the Senior Managers & Certification Regime (SMCR); disqualification and personal civil liability for fraudulent trading under Companies Act 2006 s.993.
AML / PSP Regulatory Breach

Statutory sanction: Criminal offence punishable by unlimited fine and/or up to 2 years' imprisonment for individuals; corporate fines up to 20% of annual turnover (FCA regime); licence revocation, public censure, and asset recovery orders under POCA 2002.

Director personal liability: Directors who consent or connive in the offence are personally criminally liable; FCA can impose fines on individuals and bar them from senior positions; travel bans, restrictions on managing regulated entities, and potential extradition for serious economic crime.
