Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) — UK tax authority; online portal: GOV.UK / HMRC online services (https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/hm-revenue-customs)

E-government filing portal

A foreign (BVI) entity with no UK nexus has no UK tax registration obligation. Obligations arise where the entity is UK tax resident (central management and control in the UK) or has a UK permanent establishment. In such cases, the BC must register for Corporation Tax with HMRC within 3 months of the trigger using HMRC's Corporation Tax online service; HMRC issues a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) and a Corporation Tax reference. For VAT, any foreign entity making UK taxable supplies or exporting goods from the UK must register for VAT (voluntary or mandatory once taxable turnover exceeds £90,000) via form VAT1 online and receives a VAT registration number (VRN); an EORI number is separately required for customs. UK PAYE registration is needed if the BC employs staff.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

UK law does not have a check-the-box election. For direct tax, a BVI BC is always an entity-level taxpayer (body corporate) and cannot be treated as a pass-through or disregarded entity. The UK does not permit an election to change entity classification; however, certain VAT elections may be made, such as VAT grouping under VATA 1994 Schedule 9A. Anti-hybrid mismatch rules under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A apply automatically and cannot be overridden or eliminated by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 (taxable turnover over the previous 12 months); deregistration threshold £88,000

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods exported from the UK, 0% UK VAT applies if (a) the goods are physically removed from the UK to a destination outside the UK, (b) the supplier has obtained and retained proper evidence of export within 3 months of the time of supply, and (c) the supply is made to a person outside the UK. For services, including the anti-cheat gaming middleware, a supply of services to a non-UK business is generally outside the scope of UK VAT because the place of supply is the customer's country; the supplier should hold the customer's VAT ID where applicable, the contract, and technical documentation evidencing the non-UK business customer and location. For B2C electronically supplied services to customers outside the UK, the supply is outside UK VAT if the customer belongs outside the UK, and the supplier may need to register under the relevant non-UK OSS scheme if it makes such supplies.

  • Valid Customs Export Declaration (submitted and accepted through the UK Customs Declaration Service / CDS)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from the UK
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or corresponding bank/SWIFT payment proof (where foreign exchange repatriation is required)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and any additional customs-required documents (e.g., export licence if applicable)

The UK does not operate a real-time invoice clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing regime. However, the Making Tax Digital (MTD) regime obliges VAT-registered businesses to maintain digital VAT records and submit VAT returns via HMRC-recognised software using HMRC's Application Programming Interface (API). Invoices themselves do not require HMRC pre-authorisation. Customs export declarations are filed through the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) with a valid EORI number.

HMRC may challenge a zero-rating claim by examining the export evidence and underlying contracts. If the claim is unsupported, HMRC can issue an assessment for undeclared output VAT, claw back input VAT previously reclaimed, and assess output VAT on the full supply, for up to 4 years (6 years for deliberate inaccuracy or failure to notify, and up to 20 years for offshore non-compliance in serious cases), plus statutory interest. Penalties for inaccuracy range from 30% to 100% of the VAT understated depending on behaviour. Where the false claim is made dishonestly, VAT evasion is a criminal offence under VATA 1994 s. 72, carrying liability to imprisonment and/or an unlimited fine.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GBP is freely convertible under IMF Article VIII. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are generally unrestricted, subject only to UK financial sanctions, anti-money-laundering (AML) checks, and FATCA/CRS reporting. Profit remittance from a UK operating bank account to the BVI Business Company or its shareholders is lawful without central-bank consent, provided the BVI BC has valid board resolutions, satisfies any applicable solvency test for distributions, and can evidence beneficial ownership to the paying bank. No conversion or surrender obligation is imposed on service-fee, royalty, or dividend flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. UK and BVI exchange-control regimes do not require exporters (including a BVI BC with UK banking counterparties) to surrender foreign currency receipts or convert export proceeds into GBP. Export proceeds may be retained in original currency, swept through multi-currency accounts, or hedged with authorised institutions. If the BVI BC's UK bank is a correspondent, the bank may apply enhanced monitoring for high-volume inbound export receipts but cannot require exchange control forms or post-transaction central bank approval.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (central monetary and banking authority); HM Treasury (overseer of financial sanctions and residual exchange-control powers under the Exchange Control Act 1947, which has been largely repealed). The United Kingdom does not maintain a dedicated foreign-exchange-administration or surrender authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium — workable as an ancillary settlement rail, not as the primary treasury host. An FCA-authorised UK EMI (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, or similar) can be onboarded with a BVI BC if a certificate of incumbency, registers of directors/UBOs, and a clear commercial explanation are provided. However, an EMI cannot extend credit, issue a commercial LC, operate a true escrow facility, or offer margin FX lines; and UK EMIs often reject companies whose product is an anti-cheat/security engine unless the bank's policy classifies it as standard, non-high-risk software. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a BVI BC using a UK bank, inbound capital injections should be executed by SWIFT MT103 with the originator shown as the funding shareholder and the beneficiary as the BVI BC's designated account at the UK bank. Field 70 should carry a purpose description matching the corporate act, e.g. 'SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION' or 'CONTRIBUTION TO CAPITAL — NO CARRIAGE OF GOODS'. Where the funding is by way of debt, a signed intercompany loan agreement and interest-pricing worksheet must be held on file. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed HMRC legal threshold is published for Local File; however, HMRC expects OECD-compliant Local File and Master File documentation where cross-border connected transactions exceed £10,000,000 and the company exceeds SME limits (more than 250 employees, turnover above £25.8m, or balance sheet above £22.8m). Mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting arises for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least £200,000,000 (€750m equivalent). A zero-tax BVI BC earning high-margin anti-cheat licensing income should maintain contemporaneous TP documentation even below those thresholds.

Economic substance scope: The UK has no general Economic Substance Regulation, but Diverted Profits Tax, CFC rules and GAAR address artificial profit shifting. Because the underlying entity is a BVI Business Company, the BVI Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Act applies: the development, licensing and middleware IP activity is an intellectual property business, and the BC must demonstrate adequate BVI substance (managed and directed in BVI, local employees, physical premises, core income-generating activities and expenditure in BVI) and file an annual economic substance declaration with the BVI International Tax Authority.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Cheating the Public Revenue

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties under FA 2009 Sch 24 up to 100% of additional tax; criminal prosecution under common law cheating the public revenue and Fraud Act 2006; Criminal Finances Act 2017 corporate failure-to-prevent tax evasion penalties with unlimited fines; HMRC may freeze assets and issue restoration orders.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted for fraudulent evasion and conspiracy; senior accounting officers can be personally named and fined under FA 2009; disqualification under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986 for up to 15 years; POCA restraint orders, court-ordered travel bans and cross-border arrest warrants may be imposed.
Shell Company / BVI Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: BVI economic substance penalties up to US$25,000 plus daily default penalties and potential striking-off; UK HMRC can apply Diverted Profits Tax at 25% of diverted profits, CFC charges at the mainstream Corporation Tax rate, GAAR counteraction with a 60% penalty, and FA 2009 accuracy penalties on top of tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and registered agents who approve false declarations may be jointly and severally liable for BVI penalties; UK directors can face personal liability for fraudulent trading (Insolvency Act 1986 s.213), wrongful trading liability (s.214), disqualification, and HMRC personal liability notices where the corporate veil is pierced.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: BVI AML/FT framework imposes civil penalties and criminal fines; UK Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 money-laundering offences carry up to 14 years' imprisonment; UK Money Laundering Regulations 2017 impose unlimited fines and up to 2 years' imprisonment; HMRC can also invoke sanctions under the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018.

Director personal liability: MLROs and directors who consent to or are negligently involved in AML failures are personally liable; POCA restraint orders and travel bans may be applied; directors can be disqualified; beneficial owners participating in money laundering face criminal prosecution and confiscation of proceeds.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Adjustments under TIOPA 2010 Part 4, secondary adjustments and interest; FA 2009 Sch 24 penalties up to 100% of the additional tax; Diverted Profits Tax at 25% of diverted profits; CFC attribution charge if profits are artificially diverted to the BVI BC; GAAR counteraction with a 60% penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false TP documentation may face criminal penalties for fraudulent evasion; senior accounting officers can be fined £5,000 for failing to certify accounting systems; in insolvency, HMRC can claim against directors for wrongful trading, and travel restrictions may follow under insolvency or POCA proceedings.
