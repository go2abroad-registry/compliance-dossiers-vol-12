Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC); primary digital portal is the HMRC Government Gateway (via gov.uk), incorporating the Corporation Tax online service, VAT online service, and Making Tax Digital (MTD) API endpoints.

E-government filing portal

A BVI Business Company acting through a UK permanent establishment or otherwise making UK-taxable supplies must register for UK Corporation Tax within 3 months of commencing UK business activity using HMRC's 'Register a trust, company or partnership for Corporation Tax' online service (CT41G process), obtaining a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR). For VAT, registration is filed online via the HMRC VAT Registration Service (Form VAT1; non-established taxable persons use the VAT1 form and may be required to appoint a UK-established tax representative under VATA 1994 s.8). The effective date of VAT registration is either the date of liability or the date of application, whichever applies. Feasibility of remote registration by foreign directors is available via the Government Gateway using agent-authorisation procedures and non-UK ID verification.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation - the BVI Business Company is a body corporate and is not fiscally transparent. As a non-UK resident company, it is within the charge to UK Corporation Tax only on income attributable to a UK permanent establishment and on UK-source income (including UK land gains), at the main rate of 25% (19% small profits rate and marginal relief for profits between £50,000 and £250,000, subject to associated-company aggregation). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the BVI Business Company is a body corporate and is not fiscally transparent. As a non-UK resident company, it is within the charge to UK Corporation Tax only on income attributable to a UK permanent establishment and on UK-source income (including UK land gains), at the main rate of 25% (19% small profits rate and marginal relief for profits between £50,000 and £250,000, subject to associated-company aggregation). The entity retains its BVI tax-exempt status domestically, but no UK exemption is automatic.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or full partnership-filtration regime exists in UK law. Available elections: (1) VAT group registration under VATA 1994 s.43 for closely-linked UK entities; (2) option to tax (election to waive exemption) on UK real property interests; (3) transfer pricing elections for dormant/comparables simplification (CTA 2010 s.485); (4) UK loan relationship and derivative contract accounting elections; (5) post-Brexit EU-UK cross-border loss relief elections where treaty provisions apply; (6) OECD Pillar Two global minimum top-up tax safe harbour elections (qualified domestic top-up tax under Finance Act 2023) where the group is within scope.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 (annual taxable turnover threshold for established UK businesses; a non-established taxable person making taxable supplies in the UK must register if total taxable supplies exceed £90,000 in a rolling 12-month period, or if there are reasonable grounds to believe that this will be exceeded. The £90,000 threshold applies from 1 April 2024; distance-selling and imported-goods consignment thresholds also align at £90,000.)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating applies under VATA 1994 Sch.8, Group 14 where physical goods are exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK and the supplier retains valid commercial evidence of export within 3 months of supply. Services: for game 3D art outsourcing and character-rigging services supplied B2B to a non-UK customer, the place of supply is where the customer belongs (VATA 1994 s.7A and SI 1992/3121), rendering the supply outside the scope of UK VAT and subject to the customer's local reverse-charge or VAT rules; the supplier must hold evidence of the customer's business status (foreign VAT registration number, jurisdiction, and nature of business) to substantiate the non-UK belonging. If the services are deemed performed in the UK (e.g., in-person digital asset delivery to a UK-established intermediary), the B2B reverse charge will apply rather than zero-rating. Claiming a refund of UK input VAT is available via the 13th Directive (VAT 403/13) scheme for non-established businesses.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government pre-authorisation for standard B2B invoices in the UK. Mandatory Making Tax Digital (MTD) requires all VAT-registered businesses (from 1 April 2022, extended to all from 1 November 2022) to keep digital records and submit VAT returns via HMRC-recognised MTD-compatible software using HMRC's Application Programming Interface (API). E-invoicing is permitted on a bilateral commercial basis (PEPPOL BIS 3.0 supported) and HMRC has indicated future consultation on a phased statutory e-invoicing framework aligned with EU ViDA principles, but none is currently in force.

HMRC may issue assessments under VATA 1994 s.73 to claw back zero-rated VAT, plus default interest, where export evidence is absent, inadequate, or not retained for the statutory 6-year record-keeping period. Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 penalties apply for careless (up to 30%) and deliberate (up to 70%, or 100% where concealed) inaccuracies in VAT returns. Fraudulent zero-rating or deliberate mis-declaration constitutes a criminal offence under VATA 1994 s.72, exposing directors to fines and imprisonment of up to 7 years for conspiracy to defraud, and up to 2 years for evasion of VAT. There is no general UK statutory exchange-control rule mandating inward remittance within 180 days; however, HMRC applies the 180-day benchmark to VAT refunds via the bad debt relief waiting period (VATA 1994 s.36) and expects export proceeds to be remitted within commercial terms; prolonged non-repatriation, without economic substance, may trigger transfer-pricing and diverted-profits-tax enquiries at 20% to 30% of adjusted profits plus interest.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Sterling (GBP) is fully convertible on both current and capital account; the UK has no exchange-control approval, no surrender requirement, and no repatriation restriction on dividends, royalties, or loan repayments owed to a non-resident BVI shareholder. A BVI Business Company remitting profits from UK trade faces only: (a) UK corporation tax if a UK permanent establishment arises; (b) withholding tax on UK-source interest and royalties under domestic law and any applicable treaty; (c) OFSI financial sanctions screening; and (d) Bank of England balance-of-payments statistical reporting. Dividend payments by a UK payer to the BVI BC are not subject to UK withholding tax. The main practical hurdle is banking compliance evidence of economic substance in BVI or business substance in the UK, not currency conversion per se.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement for UK export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained at the exporter's discretion in GBP, USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency in onshore or offshore accounts. The only statutory reporting obligation is the Bank of England International Debit/Credit (IDC) survey compiled by the receiving UK bank under the Balance of Payments (BPM6) framework, which applies to cross-border flows above the reporting threshold, together with anti-money-laundering transaction reporting under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (the UK central bank) exercises prudential supervision through the Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA); HM Treasury holds residual currency and exchange-control powers; the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) licenses banks, e-money institutions, and payment institutions. The United Kingdom maintains no separately legislated Foreign Exchange Administration; the architecture is a single national treasury regime under the Bank of England Act 1998 and the Payment Services Regulations 2017.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. FCA-authorised UK EMIs and digital banks (Airwallex, Currencycloud, Wise Business, Revolut Business, and Tide) offer materially faster onboarding for a BVI BC than Tier-1 banks, with fully remote video KYC, multi-currency IBANs and wallets, and embedded FX. Legal constraints: (i) an EMI is not a bank and cannot accept deposits or extend credit; client funds must be safeguarded in segregated accounts under the Payment Services Regulations 2017; (ii) only FCA-authorised EMIs (or EEA-authorised firms with a UK branch or FCA permission post-Brexit) may provide UK account identifiers; offshore and unlicensed digital EMIs are prohibited from holding or clearing GBP; (iii) risk appetite for BVI tax-exempt entities is mixed, and several EMIs will decline purely on jurisdiction policy; those that accept will still require UBO attestations and source-of-funds evidence.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: UK transfer-pricing rules apply without a general de-minimis monetary threshold; the SME exemption applies where the enterprise has fewer than 250 employees, turnover does not exceed £50 million, and balance-sheet assets do not exceed £43 million, unless the transaction is a tax-motivated avoidance arrangement. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) applies only to MNE groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least £750 million; a UK Local File and Master File consistent with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines must be prepared contemporaneously and produced to HMRC within 30 days of a statutory notice. s.109 TMA 1970 penalty protection applies only where OECD-compliant transfer-pricing documentation is maintained.

Economic substance scope: UK: a BVI BC managed and controlled from London is UK tax resident on CMC principles and taxable on worldwide profits; anti-avoidance overlay includes Diverted Profits Tax at 25%, CFC rules under TIOPA 2010 Part 9A, anti-hybrid rules, and a permanent-establishment substance test. BVI: the BC is subject to the BVI Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) administered by the International Tax Authority (ITA); a 3D game-art outsourcing BC carrying on an intellectual-property business or distribution and service-centre business must demonstrate in the BVI (i) board management and direction, (ii) qualified employees, (iii) physical premises, and (iv) adequate operating expenditure. An annual economic substance return must be filed with the BVI ITA. Failure penalties: USD 5,000 standard filing penalty; USD 20,000 for first non-compliance; USD 40,000 per year for continuing non-compliance; up to USD 100,000 for IP-holding businesses; plus potential strike-off and compulsory dissolution. HMRC may obtain substance records through the UK-BVI exchange-of-information arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Concealment in Corporation Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 penalty for deliberate and concealed understatement: up to 200% of the Potential Lost Revenue. Fraudulent evasion of income tax/corporation tax under s.106A TMA 1970 and fraud by false representation under the Fraud Act 2006 carry up to 10 years' imprisonment and unlimited fines; HMRC may also invoke asset seizure, receivership, and public-procurement debarment.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal prosecution for fraudulent trading (Companies Act 2006 s.993, criminal; Insolvency Act 1986 s.213, civil), wrongful trading (s.214 IA 1986), and misfeasance (s.212 IA 1986). HMRC may seek confiscation orders, Serious Crime Prevention Orders with travel bans of up to 5 years, director disqualification of up to 15 years, and joint-and-several civil restitution against UK shadow directors of the BVI BC.
Shell Company / BVI Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: BVI ESR: USD 5,000 standard penalty, USD 20,000 for first non-compliance, USD 40,000 per year for continuing non-compliance, and up to USD 100,000 for IP-holding businesses; the BVI ITA may strike the company off and seek dissolution. In the UK: Diverted Profits Tax at 25% plus interest, CFC apportionment under TIOPA 2010 Part 9A taxing undistributed profits, and disallowance of deductions under targeted anti-avoidance rules.

Director personal liability: BVI directors face fines, removal, and disqualification by the International Tax Authority; UK resident directors and shareholders who exercise direction over the BC are treated as shadow directors and may face secondary tax assessments, contribution orders, travel bans, and disqualification. Proof of genuine BVI substance is required to defeat a CFC or DPT charge.
AML & Sanctions Compliance Breach

Statutory sanction: Money Laundering Regulations 2017: HMRC may impose unlimited civil penalties for serious regulatory breaches; on summary conviction a fine up to the statutory maximum applies, and on indictment an unlimited fine and imprisonment of up to 2 years. Sanctions breaches under the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018 attract monetary penalties up to £1 million or 50% of the value of the funds, whichever is greater.

Director personal liability: Senior officers and MLROs may be prosecuted for consenting or conniving in the offence; directors of the BVI BC and of the UK payment-rail company are exposed to imprisonment of up to 2 years, unlimited fines, confiscation proceedings, and disqualification orders where the corporate AML failure is attributable to their consent, neglect, or wilful blindness.
Failure to Prevent Tax Evasion (Criminal Finances Act 2017)

Statutory sanction: Criminal Finances Act 2017 ss.45/46: strict-liability corporate offences; unlimited fine, loss of government contracts, and reputational debarment. The offence reaches facilitation of UK tax evasion by associated persons whether the associated person acts in the UK or the BVI.

Director personal liability: Directors, officers, and shareholders who knowingly participate in or consent to the facilitation face personal prosecution for being knowingly concerned in fraudulent evasion of tax, carrying up to 7 years' imprisonment, unlimited fines, confiscation orders under POCA 2002 Part 2, and travel bans; no safe harbour exists once the principal offence is attributable to their consent.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Incorrect CbCR and Master File Declaration

Statutory sanction: s.109 TMA 1970: £3,000 penalty per statutory notice plus daily default penalties of up to £600/day; CbCR and Master File late or incorrect filings attract an initial £300 penalty and £60 per day continuing. HMRC may additionally impose a transfer-pricing adjustment, a 25% Diverted Profits Tax charge, interest from the date the tax should have been paid, and Schedule 24 accuracy penalties of up to 200% of the understated tax where conduct is deliberate.

Director personal liability: Directors are not automatically personally liable for transfer-pricing penalties absent participation; however, any deliberate or dishonest facilitation of an incorrect pricing position exposes them to s.106A TMA 1970 prosecution for fraudulent evasion, fraudulent-trading liability, and joint-and-several civil liability for the understated profits without right of indemnity from the BVI BC.
