E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated entity (e.g., Bermuda exempted company) must register as an 'overseas company' with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies (companieshouse@gibraltar.gov.gi), then apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Reference (GTR). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope (entity-level tax on Gibraltar-source income; foreign-source income generally not taxable; election capable)

Available election forms

Qualifying Company election under Gibraltar's Qualifying Company (QC) regime (submission of Form A/B and annual fee) conferring a fixed 0% income tax rate on eligible activity; the Non-Resident Company route may also be used; no check-the-box hybrid election is available as Gibraltar has not implemented US-style classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No national VAT/GST; Gibraltar applies import Customs Duty, Excise Duty, and a limited General Consumption Tax on select services (historically). For standard goods turnover no VAT registration exists.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no turnover-based VAT/GST registration. Customs registration is required only for importers/exporters of dutiable goods; thresholds vary by excise category (e.g., alcohol/ tobacco).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT zero-rating means. For customs purposes, exports from Gibraltar to non-Gibraltar destinations are outside the scope of Gibraltar import duty. Claiming relief requires evidence that goods have left Gibraltar and been delivered to a foreign destination; services supplied to non-residents with consumption outside Gibraltar fall outside the territorial scope of Gibraltar income tax.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EX) lodged in GCMS
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign exchange repatriation report
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export licences for controlled medical devices (if applicable, e.g., UK Medical Devices Regulations)

No mandatory e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance for domestic transactions. Customs declarations must be submitted electronically via the Gibraltar Customs Management System (GCMS) / European Community Single Administrative Document (SAD) for goods entering or leaving Gibraltar; digital stamping only applies to excise stamps on alcoholic beverages and tobacco.

Penalties for fraudulent export claims under Gibraltar's Customs Ordinance include up to €100,000 fine and/or imprisonment, forfeiture of goods, and retrospective clawback of any duty relief plus interest. Tax fraud under the Income Tax Act attracts penalties of up to 200% of tax evaded and criminal liability for officers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both current-account and capital-account convertibility are unrestricted for non-resident and offshore entities. The GIP is freely convertible into GBP at par on demand; no exchange-control permissions, prior-approval queues, or profit-remittance restrictions apply to a Bermuda Exempted Company banking in Gibraltar. Remittance hurdles are practical rather than legal: CRS/FATCA self-certifications, UBO registry verification, GFSC payment-transparency evidence, and source-of-wealth documentation for the capital injection. USD and EUR current accounts are widely available, so a GBP/USD/EUR tri-currency cash pool removes convertibility friction; tax leakage is limited to Gibraltar-sourced income or a 2% Class 8 qualifying-company election if the entity is treated as Gibraltar resident.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender regime, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation timeline. Exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in any convertible currency (GIP, GBP, USD, EUR) in onshore accounts. The only obligations are indirect: GFSC-licensed banks must apply sanctions screening, file suspicious activity reports under the AML/CFT Code, and include commercial invoice references and HS codes in SWIFT UETR messaging for statistical and transparency purposes.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no independent central bank. Banking regulation is exercised by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services (Banking) Act 2019; currency issuance is vested in the Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Currency) under a currency-board arrangement that pegs the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at 1:1 to GBP; monetary policy authority is therefore effectively delegated to the Bank of England, with the GFSC serving as the de facto monetary supervisory authority for licensed banks and the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit (GFIU) as the AML authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM for transactional operating flows, LOW as a primary treasury rail. Gibraltar has its own e-money authorization framework (Electronic Money Act 2015, regulated by the GFSC), but UK/EEA EMIs such as Revolut, Wise Business, and Airwallex no longer possess passporting rights into Gibraltar post-Brexit and generally reject a Bermuda Exempted Company unless it is first registered with the Gibraltar Commissioner for Companies as an overseas company with a place of business. Even then, EMIs do not provide documentary letters of credit, escrow custody for capital injections, or trade finance, and they typically cap balances below GBP 100,000. Legal constraints: the EMI must apply full CDD to the Bermuda legal entity, nominees and bearer-share structures are rejected outright, and UBO certifications must be apostilled and accompanied by Gibraltar economic-substance evidence. Viable use case: R&D outlays, travel, supplier prepayments under EUR 25,000, and low-value FX conversion only.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% flat rate on chargeable profits (Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, as amended; utilities remain at 20%; regulated large retailers remain at a concessional composite rate between 12.5% and 20%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Gibraltar statute (Income Tax Act 2010, as amended) imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar company to a Bermuda Exempted Company shareholder.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Nine months after the end of the chargeable accounting period, i.e., 30 September for a 31 December year-end. Where the Commissioner issues a formal notice to file, the return must be delivered within 30 days of service. Any assessed tax is payable within 30 days of assessment; large taxpayers make interim payments on account on 30 June and 31 December.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where aggregate cross-border related-party transactions (purchases, sales, financing, royalties) equal or exceed GIP 1,000,000 per annum, or where related-party financing exceeds GIP 500,000. Master File: mandatory where the ultimate parent (the Bermuda Exempted Company) has consolidated turnover exceeding EUR 750 million, or where the Commissioner designates the company. Documentation must follow OECD TP Guidelines Chapter V and be delivered within 12 months of the statutory filing date.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Alignment with the EU Code of Conduct) Regulations 2022 apply. Pure manufacturing of orthopedic implants is not a relevant activity; however, the following limbs are triggered: (i) IP business, because a Bermuda Exempted Company owning patents, trade marks and electron-beam process know-how receives royalty income; and (ii) distribution and service centre, where the Gibraltar entity performs procurement, logistics or customer-facing sales support for the Bermudian principal. The entity must demonstrate core income-generating activities in Gibraltar: local board meetings, strategic decisions taken by Gibraltar-resident directors, adequate premises, full-time employees proportionate to income, and an annual ESR filing. A passive Bermuda Exempted Company or a Gibraltar mailbox company without employees and without responsibilities fails ESR and the OECD principal-purpose test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion by misreporting tax residence

Statutory sanction: Assessment on the entire Gibraltar-source profit at 12.5%; penalty up to 200% of the understated tax for deliberate and concealed behaviour; interest at 5% per annum; criminal prosecution with a fine up to GIP 250,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years on conviction on indictment.

Director personal liability: Directors and the beneficial owner may be made personally liable for unpaid tax where they knowingly caused the false return; the court may issue a travel ban and a restraint order over personal assets until all tax, penalties and interest are settled; disqualification as a director for up to 15 years under the Gibraltar Companies (Disqualification) Act.
Shell company and empty economic substance

Statutory sanction: ESR penalty of GIP 10,000 plus GIP 1,000 per calendar day continuing for up to 12 months; substitute assessment of 12.5% on deemed income; strike-off by the Gibraltar Registrar; exchange of a 'no substance' declaration with HMRC under the OECD transparency framework.

Director personal liability: Each director who authorised the shell arrangement is jointly and severally liable for the ESR penalty and the substitute assessment; the beneficial owner risks a confiscation order under the Proceeds of Crime Act, a 5-year travel ban, and imprisonment of up to 2 years for failure to file the ESR declaration.
Transfer pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to arm's length; penalty of 100% of the additional tax arising, reduced only where qualifying contemporaneous documentation exists; fixed penalty GIP 5,000 per missing document; interest applies.

Director personal liability: The finance director and tax manager are personally liable for penalties if they signed financial statements that grossly misrepresent intercompany transactions; the court may order personal restitution of underpaid corporate tax from directors who participated in the pricing decision.
AML and Proceeds of Crime abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015: on conviction on indictment, imprisonment up to 12 years and an unlimited fine; civil forfeiture of the funds; account freeze and reporting to the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit; a suspicious activity report must be filed within 24 hours of suspicion.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally subject to criminal liability where they knew or suspected money laundering and failed to report; personal travel restraint, surrender of passport, and disqualification from acting as a director of any Gibraltar company apply; the corporate veil is pierced for civil restitution to creditors and tax authorities.
Treaty shopping and UK GAAR abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits; UK GAAR counteraction with a penalty of 60% of the tax counteracted; double taxation without credit in Bermuda; an HMRC exchange-of-information request to the Gibraltar Commissioner requiring production of the implant company's bank records.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed a false beneficial-ownership certification are personally liable to the Gibraltar Commissioner for the full amount of unpaid tax where the company is struck off; high-risk UBOs face a worldwide travel ban and extradition under the UK-Gibraltar Extradition Treaty.
