Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Corporate Affairs, Registry of Corporate Affairs, BVI Financial Services Commission (FSC), Road Town, Tortola, British Virgin Islands.

Governing statute

BVI Business Companies Act, 2004 (as amended) - the Companies Code of the British Virgin Islands, a British Overseas Territory of the United Kingdom - Sections 5 (incorporation), 7-10 (memorandum and articles), 44 (registered agent), 47-50 (registered office), and 109-116 (directors and register of directors), as read with the BVI Business Companies Regulations, 2009, the BVI Beneficial Ownership Secure Search System Act, 2017, and the Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Act, 2018.

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum and Articles of Association (MAA) together with the Application for Incorporation and the Certificate of Registered Agent, submitted electronically via the Integrated Regulatory Filing System (IRFS) by an FSC-licensed registered agent.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 25 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or deposit requirement; standard authorized share capital is USD 50,000 (fee tier USD 350); shares may be issued with par value, at premium, or no par value, and consideration may be in cash or other value.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate supported by a fire risk assessment under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, with means-of-escape certification and annual servicing of extinguishers/alarms; structural load inspection of mezzanine and racking under the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Workplace (Health, Safety and Welfare) Regulations 1992; HSE accident-reporting and first-aid provisions; DSEAR risk assessment if aerosol containers, batteries, or other dangerous substances are stored; occupational clearance of the fulfilment facility by the local authority building control (structural and electrical) prior to dispatch operations.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or deposit requirement; standard authorized share capital is USD 50,000 (fee tier USD 350); shares may be issued with par value, at premium, or no par value, and consideration may be in cash or other value.

RESOLVED, that the subscribers' share subscription be ratified and the allotment of one ordinary share of no par value to the initial subscriber be approved, consideration being fully paid; RESOLVED, that the registered office in the British Virgin Islands and the appointment of the licensed registered agent be adopted; RESOLVED, that [Director Name] be appointed sole Director, and [Officer Name] Company Secretary, serving until removed by written resolution; RESOLVED, that corporate bank account opening and engagement of professional advisors be authorized; RESOLVED FURTHER, that the Director take all steps for statutory compliance, filings, and commencement of subscription operations.
Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

No sectoral industrial operating license is mandated for ISIC 4791 subscription-box retail; product-safety surveillance is exercised by the Office for Product Safety and Standards (OPSS) and Local Authority Trading Standards; extended producer responsibility is enforced by the Environment Agency / SEPA / Natural Resources Wales through the packaging EPR registration regime.

Category: No mandatory operating permit; voluntary UKCA conformity marking for regulated products embedded in boxes (e.g., electronics, cosmetics, toys) and mandatory registration under the Packaging Waste (Data Reporting) (England) Regulations 2023 (EPR) for the relevant packaging tonnage.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC); online portal: Government Gateway (gov.uk)

E-government filing portal

A non-UK (BVI) company with a UK taxable presence must first determine registration obligations. If it is UK resident or has a UK permanent establishment, apply for a Corporation Tax UTR via HMRC. For VAT, register as a non-established taxable person using Form VAT1 (or online) if making taxable UK supplies. HMRC requires identification documents, proof of establishment, and details of a UK agent. Approval typically takes 2-4 weeks.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A non-UK (BVI) company with a UK taxable presence must first determine registration obligations. If it is UK resident or has a UK permanent establishment, apply for a Corporation Tax UTR via HMRC. For VAT, register as a non-established taxable person using Form VAT1 (or online) if making taxable UK supplies. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime. A non-UK resident company may elect to calculate Corporation Tax under CTA 2009 (UK permanent establishment separate treatment). Hybrid mismatch rules require disclosure of election statuses under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A. A BVI Business Company is not entitled to pass-through or transparency elections for UK tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 annual taxable turnover for UK-established businesses; no threshold for non-established businesses (a BVI company must register if making any taxable supply in the UK)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK are zero-rated provided the supplier exports them within one month of supply and retains proof of export within three months. Services relating to subscription boxes (e.g., fulfillment/logistics) may be zero-rated if supplied to a business customer outside the UK. Physical subscription boxes sold to non-UK customers qualify where the goods are dispatched from the UK with adequate export evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CDS Export Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Commercial Invoice and Inward Remittance/Payment Proof

No real-time fiscal e-invoicing clearance. Mandatory Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT: businesses must use HMRC-approved API-compatible software to submit VAT returns; no digital stamping or pre-clearance requirement.

HMRC may issue assessments and penalties up to 100% of VAT involved (VATA 1994, CEMA 1979). Input VAT reclaimed is clawed back where export evidence is insufficient or later denied, with interest. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion may apply.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full liberalisation of both current and capital account transactions for residents and non-residents. Sterling is freely convertible; no prior exchange-control approval is required for trade receipts, dividend remittances, loan repayments, or capital injections. Profit remittance obstacles are absent at exchange-control level; however, distributions from a UK branch of a BVI Business Company are subject to UK tax-compliance procedures if a taxable permanent establishment exists, and the BVI entity must comply with its own constitutional and solvency distribution tests.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in the United Kingdom. Export proceeds may be freely retained in any currency, held in UK-authorised bank accounts, or kept offshore. For statistical purposes, UK resident businesses must report aggregate international receipts under the Bank of England's International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS), but there is no obligation to convert foreign currency into Sterling or to repatriate proceeds within a prescribed timeline.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority) in coordination with HM Treasury under the largely abolished Exchange Control Act 1947; residual exchange-control powers are administered by HM Treasury's financial sanctions and anti-money laundering supervisory regime

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility for transaction-level receivables, but only as a complementary clearing rail rather than a full banking substitute. An FCA-authorised Electronic Money Institution (EMI) may onboard a BVI Business Company remotely using digital KYC and provide multi-currency payment accounts for subscription-box merchants. Legal constraints include the absence of a UK banking licence, prohibition on lending and credit facility issuance, restrictions on overdrawing, and the requirement for the EMI to comply with the FCA's E-Money Regulations 2011 and the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2019. Passporting restrictions apply to EEA-authorised EMIs after Brexit.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Bearer share structure, nominee shareholders, or an opaque corporate ownership chain

Root cause: Bearer share structure, nominee shareholders, or an opaque corporate ownership chain

Defense evidence: Certified copy of the BVI Register of Members; complete beneficial-ownership cascade signed by the registered agent; attestation that no bearer instruments exist; a current BVI Certificate of Good Standing; and a professional introduction letter from the BVI licensed registered agent.
Unclear source of wealth or unexplained accumulation of subscription-box receivables

Root cause: Unclear source of wealth or unexplained accumulation of subscription-box receivables

Defense evidence: Latest six months of bank statements from the BVI licensed bank; Stripe Billing and Authorize.Net merchant settlement reports; payment processor underwriting letters; a business-plan narrative clearly showing recurring subscription income; and audited or management accounts for the BVI Business Company.
High-risk counterparty exposure or payment flows involving sanctions-sensitive jurisdictions

Root cause: High-risk counterparty exposure or payment flows involving sanctions-sensitive jurisdictions

Defense evidence: Commercial contracts and purchase orders evidencing the trade chain; customs import/export documentation; sanctions-screening results demonstrating a clear negative match; and sworn statements confirming that the transaction chain does not involve prohibited persons or territories.
Economic substance mismatch or perceived interposition of the BVI entity without commercial rationale

Root cause: Economic substance mismatch or perceived interposition of the BVI entity without commercial rationale

Defense evidence: BVI Economic Substance Filing confirmation; detailed explanation of the UK treasury function; UK physical office lease; payroll records for UK-based directors or employees; board meeting minutes authorising the UK banking relationship; and the group structure chart showing the underlying commercial substance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from the BVI operating bank to UK settlement accounts must be executed by SWIFT MT103 wire transfer using the BVI bank's correspondent banking network. The remittance field must specify a clear purpose descriptor such as 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION', 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN', or 'TREASURY INJECTION', together with the BVI Business Company's registration number. UK receiving banks perform sanctions screening and request a source-of-funds verification package comprising the executed loan agreement, board resolution, BVI certificate of good standing, FATCA/CRS classification report, and confirmation of HMRC registration (where applicable). The applicable UK MIR/BOP statistical purpose codes should follow the receiving bank's internal guidance; no single legally mandated purpose code is prescribed for sterling or foreign-currency wires into the UK.Outbound supplier settlements must be executed over SWIFT MT103 for international telegraphic transfers, using UCP600-compliant commercial letters of credit for high-value or documentary trade, and CHAPS for same-day sterling-denominated disbursements to UK creditors. For letter-of-credit operations, the BVI Business Company's UK treasury account must settle under the reimbursement clause via MT202 COVER. Disbursement instructions must always be supported by the underlying supplier invoice, purchase order, customs declarations (if goods physically cross customs frontiers), and counterparty sanctions-screening confirmation.
