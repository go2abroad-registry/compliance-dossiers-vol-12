Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office (ITO), HM Government of Gibraltar; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax-office; e-filing is conducted via licensed Gibraltar tax agents. Interfacing authorities: HM Customs Gibraltar (import/export declarations and duties), Gibraltar Companies Registry (external company registration), and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) for EMI and payment-account onboarding.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Bermuda exempted company as an external company under Part 18 of the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014, filing the certified Bermuda certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, register of directors, a Gibraltar registered office address, and a Gibraltar-resident representative. Step 2: Obtain the Gibraltar corporate number from the Companies Registry. Step 3: Where any Gibraltar-source trade or profit arises, including through a permanent establishment (PE), file the initial corporate notification/registration with the Income Tax Office within 30 days of commencing Gibraltar trading; the ITO will issue a distinct tax reference number (TRN) and the corporation tax instalment profile. Step 4: Register for PAYE and GRO social insurance with the ITO if the entity engages Gibraltar-resident employees or directors. Step 5: If central management and control remain in Bermuda on a Tax Exempt Zone basis and only physical outbound logistics pass through Gibraltar, register solely with HM Customs as an exporter; a TRN is not required in the absence of Gibraltar-source profits. Step 6: File the annual economic substance return and, where relevant, CRS/FATCA self-certification as an Active Non-Financial Entity (ANFE) to support the Stripe and Authorize_Net settlement rails.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope (Gibraltar nexus) / Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda home). The Bermuda exempted company holds an Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection certificate valid until 31 March 2035, shielding Bermuda tax on profits. In Gibraltar: (a) no PE and all trading executed from Bermuda results in no Gibraltar tax charge; (b) a Gibraltar PE attracts tax only on Gibraltar-source profits attributable to that PE at the 12.5% corporation tax rate, applying the OECD attributable-profit approach; (c) if central management and control is in fact exercised in Gibraltar, the entity becomes Gibraltar tax resident and profits are taxable under the 12.5% rate; (d) goods exported from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar import duty and no VAT/GST applies.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no US-style check-the-box regime; the Bermuda exempted company remains a body corporate taxable on its own profits. Available statutory elections and protective filings: (1) group relief election permitting immediate surrender of losses between 100% Gibraltar-resident group members under the Income Tax Act 2010; (2) capital allowances pooling elections for plant, machinery, and industrial buildings at statutory rates from 3% to 25%; (3) PE profit-attribution election to obtain advance ITO clearance under the Authorised OECD Approach fixing capital and interest allocation between the Bermuda head office and the Gibraltar PE; (4) anti-hybrid disclosure election aligning with OECD BEPS Action 2, requiring confirmation that no reverse-hybrid or imported-mismatch deduction is claimed; (5) the former Gibraltar Qualifying Company status is closed to new applicants and provides no election path.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Import Duty and Excise regime; no VAT, no GST, no sales-and-use tax. UK VAT does not extend to Gibraltar. Exports from Gibraltar are free of Gibraltar indirect tax; UK import VAT at 20% and UK customs duties arise only upon arrival of the goods into the United Kingdom market.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NIL threshold; no VAT registration exists in Gibraltar. Import duty is assessed per consignment at the point of entry and is not turnover-based; goods of UK preferential origin, including printed collectible trading cards, generally enter at 0%. For income tax, a Bermuda exempted company must register with the ITO from the first day of Gibraltar-source trading with no de minimis threshold; a single Gibraltar invoice triggers the filing obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax because no VAT system exists to zero-rate. Duty-free export treatment requires: (1) the holographic cold-foil collectible trading cards are in free circulation in Gibraltar; (2) they physically depart Gibraltar customs territory by sea or air to a foreign destination under a valid export declaration; (3) the sale is at arm's length and documented by a direct commercial contract with the overseas buyer; (4) the goods are not re-imported into Gibraltar. For income tax substantiation, the Bermuda exempted company must demonstrate that title and risk pass abroad on FOB or EXW terms and that no Gibraltar PE profit is generated, keeping the export margin outside Gibraltar's 12.5% corporate tax and protected by Bermuda's exemption until 31 March 2035.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (SAD-equivalent electronic declaration including Exit Summary and Safety and Security data) lodged through the HM Customs Gibraltar e-portal, quoting commodity code 4911.99.00.00 for printed trading cards or 9504.50.00 for playing cards, with declared net mass and export value.
  • Commercial invoice in English specifying the holographic cold-foil security printing and finishing specifications, INCOTERMS (FOB or EXW), unit price, and total consideration.
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Air Waybill for air freight issued by the carrier for international movement from the Port of Gibraltar or Gibraltar International Airport to the buyer's destination, evidencing physical export.
  • Packing list and edition/serial-number authentication manifest for the collectible trading cards, enabling customs inspection and anti-counterfeit traceability.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate, SWIFT MT103, or bank statement extract evidencing receipt of foreign currency settlement from the overseas buyer within 180 days of shipment.
  • Certificate of Origin (UK-Gibraltar preferential form or EUR.1 / GSP certificate) where the consignee claims preferential customs treatment in the destination jurisdiction.

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate applies in Gibraltar; there are no continuous transaction controls (CTC), no B2G PEPPOL e-invoicing, no digital tax stamp, and no SAF-T filing. Export clearance is electronic and mandatory: exporters submit a SAD-equivalent electronic customs declaration, including safety and security data, through HM Customs Gibraltar; the declaration is risk-assessed and an exit approval is returned before goods leave the Port of Gibraltar or Gibraltar International Airport. Business invoices must be issued in English and retained for at least 6 years, and the ITO may demand electronic accounting data during audit.

Gibraltar income tax and customs audits target fictitious or inflated export transactions. Under the Income Tax Act 2010, an incorrect or fraudulent return exposing undeclared export profit triggers reassessment at 12.5% plus statutory interest, a civil penalty of up to double the tax undercharged for neglect, and a penalty of up to 200% for deliberate concealment, with criminal prosecution for fraud. Under the Customs Management Ordinance, a false export declaration or misuse of duty-free export status makes the goods liable to seizure and forfeiture and the exporter liable to a penalty of up to three times the duty evaded, with imprisonment available on indictment. If the ITO determines that the Bermuda exempted company's claimed export arrangements are a sham, it may reclassify the entity as Gibraltar tax resident and retrospectively claw back tax on worldwide profits. Voluntary disclosure made before the commencement of an enquiry substantially reduces penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full de facto and de jure currency convertibility: GIP is freely interchangeable with GBP at parity, and there are no current-account or capital-account controls, so profits, dividends, intercompany payments, and capital redemptions may be remitted without exchange-control approval. The Bermuda Exempted Company, when validly designated non-resident under the Bermuda Exchange Control Act 1972, is outside Bermuda's exchange-control net, and the BMD is pegged 1:1 to the USD, eliminating translation risk for the USD-denominated trading-card business. The only practical remittance hurdles are bank due-diligence requirements: documented source of funds, economic-substance evidence linking the structure to Gibraltar, and confirmation that the structure is not a tax-window shell (Gibraltar applies income-based corporation tax to profits attributable to Gibraltar activity).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Neither Gibraltar nor Bermuda (for a non-resident exempted company) imposes statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratios, or a repatriation timeline. Receipts may be held in USD, GBP, or EUR at treasury discretion. As a bank-policy matter, the receiving bank will expect commercial invoices and shipping or fulfilment records for card sales to evidence the underlying transaction, and will apply standard suspicious-transaction reporting if proceeds are immediately routed onward without a business rationale.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the licensing and prudential regulator for banks, e-money institutions, and payment service providers in Gibraltar; the Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at a fixed 1:1 peg to sterling under the Currency Notes Act 1934; the Bank of England supplies the ultimate sterling monetary anchor, while HM Treasury and the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) administer UK/EU/UN financial sanctions applicable to Gibraltar. For the Bermuda Exempted Company leg, the Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) administers exchange-control permissions and licensing duties under Bermuda's Exchange Control Act 1972.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (Bermuda Exempted Company not in an in-scope MNE group and benefitting from the offshore exempt-status order in Gibraltar); 15% from 2025 under Bermuda's Corporate Income Tax only for multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding €750m

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% under Gibraltar and Bermuda domestic law; neither jurisdiction levies withholding tax on dividends paid by a resident company to non-resident shareholders. A Bermuda Exempted Company distributing dividends to UK or EU shareholders will not trigger Gibraltar or Bermuda tax; UK dividend taxation, if any, applies at the shareholder's personal/corporate level and may be relieved under the UK-Gibraltar DTA.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Bermuda: no mandatory TP documentation threshold applies to exempted companies outside an MNE group, but a contemporaneous TP policy is advisable for all connected-party transactions exceeding BMD 750,000 per year. Gibraltar: mandatory Local File and Master File are required when aggregate connected-party revenue exceeds GIP 8,000,000 (or £1,000,000 of specific controlled transactions), and any taxpayer failing to provide the documentation upon request becomes subject to penalties of up to GIP 1,000,000 per standard OECD penalty rule.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Bermuda Economic Substance Act 2018 requires every exempted company that carries on a 'relevant activity' to maintain adequate physical presence, local management, and core income-generating activities in Bermuda. Manufacturing, distribution of collectible trading cards, and pure holding of shares are treated distinctly: a passive holding company may rely on the 'calculation test' requiring no more than four non-resident directors and no local substance, provided it does not actually hold a trading card IP through which it controls a foreign manufactory. Gibraltar's Economic Substance Regulations apply to resident companies claiming non-tax-resident status or exempt-status trades; failure to satisfy the economic substance test causes revocation of the exempt status, immediate taxation of all worldwide profits at 12.5%, and a penalty of GIP 200,000 plus daily interest at 8% above bank rate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalty of up to 200% of the understated tax, plus interest from the statutory filing date; a Gibraltar superior court may impose a civil fine of up to £100,000; in Bermuda, an officer who knowingly furnishes a false return may be fined BMD 25,000 per offence and the corporate entity may be struck off the register.

Director personal liability:
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Bermuda Economic Substance Act: failure to file a declaration attracts BMD 250,000 - BMD 500,000 fine, and failure to satisfy the economic substance test carries penalties of BMD 300,000 plus compulsory registration on the public non-compliant register. Gibraltar ESR: revocation of exempt-status order and retroactive application of 12.5% tax on all profits, plus a penalty of up to GIP 1,000,000 for continuous non-compliance.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly permits the company to lack substance may be personally liable for restitution of the company's tax shortfall, disqualified from acting as a director for up to 10 years, subjected to a travel ban pending cooperation, and, in cases of fraudulent transfer pricing documentation, imprisoned for up to 7 years.
AML / Beneficial-Owner Concealment

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act penalties include an unlimited fine and up to 14 years' imprisonment for money laundering; Bermuda Anti-Money Laundering Act imposes fines of up to BMD 1,000,000 on corporate entities and BMD 250,000 per officer; regulatory authorities may suspend the payment processor authorisation and impose daily penalties of BMD 5,000 while non-compliance continues.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and beneficial owners are each independently liable; the court may impose a confiscation order equal to the extracted value, a travel ban until all AML fines are paid, and a custodial sentence of up to 14 years in the most severe case.
DTA Abuse and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of all treaty benefits, reclassification of royalty as intra-group financing exposed to a UK 20% deduction at source, recapture of underpaid tax plus interest at 10% per annum, and a penalty equal to 100% of the avoided tax under the UK Disclosure of Tax Avoidance Schemes rules.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the company to enter artificial tax-avoidance arrangements may be assessed personally under UK CTA 2010 s.212 Franks liability provisions; courts may make a conduct order, require civil restitution of the entire tax benefit, and impose imprisonment of up to 3 years for deliberately misleading the revenue authorities.
