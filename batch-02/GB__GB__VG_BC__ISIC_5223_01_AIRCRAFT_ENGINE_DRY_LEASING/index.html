Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital; a BVI Business Company may issue authorized but unpaid shares, and no escrow account is required for a dry aircraft engine leasing special purpose vehicle.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable – the SPV has no occupied premises. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Revenue and Customs (HMRC); digital services accessible via gov.uk (Corporation Tax, VAT, Customs Declaration Service); non-resident company registrations are processed by HMRC's Non-Resident Company Unit and the VAT Registration Service at National Insurance Contributions and HMRC (NRM).

E-government filing portal

A non-UK established BVI Business Company (BC) is outside the automatic UK Corporation Tax net until it commences a UK trade through a permanent establishment (PE). Step 1: Determine UK residence by central management and control; a UK-resident BC must register for Corporation Tax within 3 months of commencing activity and receives a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) via the gov.uk 'Register a company for Corporation Tax' service or form CT41G. Step 2: A non-resident BC with a UK PE (e.g., engine maintenance oversight or leasing negotiations concluded through a UK fixed place of business) must register for Corporation Tax within 3 months of starting the UK leasing trade. Step 3: For VAT, a non-established taxable person making taxable supplies of aircraft-engine dry-leasing services in the UK, or importing engines, must register for VAT directly with HMRC (form VAT1A or VAT1 through the Government Gateway) without reference to the GBP 90,000 threshold; HMRC issues a GB-prefixed VAT registration number. Step 4: The BC must maintain its BVI Economic Substance filing and IE profile to evidence substance for HMRC information-exchange and CFC challenges. No UK TIN is issued to a purely offshore entity lacking UK nexus.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope: a non-UK resident BVI BC is chargeable to UK Corporation Tax only on profits attributable to a UK permanent establishment and on limited UK-source income (Corporation Tax Act 2009 s.5); the BVI-level classification remains Tax_Exempt_Zone (0% BVI tax on foreign-source income), subject to the UK CFC regime where a UK parent or UK controlling interest exists.

Available election forms

The UK does not operate a check-the-box or entity-classification election. Available mechanisms: (1) CFC elections and excluded-territory clearance under TIOPA 2010 Part 9A to prevent attribution of BVI leasing profits to a UK parent; (2) the EEA-resident company election under CTA 2010 s.18A is unavailable to a BVI BC because the UK has no double-taxation treaty with the BVI; (3) VAT zero-rating for qualifying-airline aircraft-engine leases is secured through lessee certification and an intended-use declaration rather than a formal statutory election; (4) HMRC non-resident clearance, advance thin-capitalisation, and BEES (Business, Enterprise, and Employment) clearances may be obtained to confirm that dry-leasing profits are not attributable to a UK PE.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 90,000 annual taxable turnover for UK-established businesses (VATA 1994 Sch. 1); a nil threshold applies to non-established (offshore) suppliers - any taxable supply made in the UK triggers compulsory VAT registration unless the supply is zero-rated or the place of supply is outside the UK under the reverse-charge or B2B place-of-supply rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under VATA 1994 Sch. 8 Group 2 applies where a turbofan engine is supplied (including a lease that transfers possession of the engine) to a 'qualifying airline' for use on international routes, or is supplied for incorporation into an aircraft so used; the lessee must provide a signed qualifying-airline certificate and a declaration of intended use, and any non-qualifying use (generally limited to 10% or less of operating hours) must be apportioned and accounted for. Direct export of an engine out of the UK qualifies for zero-rating under the VAT (Export and Removal of Goods) Regulations 2021, provided the goods leave the UK within 3 months of supply and the exporter holds the required evidence. Cross-border B2B dry-leasing services supplied to a non-UK lessee fall outside the scope of UK VAT by place-of-supply rules (VATA 1994 s.7A) rather than by export zero-rating.

  • CDS customs export declaration (Customs Declaration Service electronic declaration with ERN/DUCR reference)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of the engine from the UK
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or UK bank statement evidencing receipt of lease rentals in foreign currency from the non-UK lessee
  • Signed qualifying-airline certificate and engine utilisation/route log (HMRC VAT Notice 731 - Aircraft)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list aligned to the CDS declaration and VAT invoice

No real-time e-invoicing or digital fiscal-stamping clearance is mandated. Mandatory Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT applies: all VAT-registered businesses must keep digital records and file VAT returns via HMRC-approved software APIs; manual paper VAT returns are abolished. Exports and imports must be declared electronically through the Customs Declaration Service (CDS), with goods movement references (DUCR/ MUCR) and safety-and-security declarations as applicable. VAT invoices must satisfy VAT Regulations 1995 regs. 13-16 (sequential numbering, GB VAT registration number, supplier/customer identification, tax point).

HMRC may retrospectively re-characterise a zero-rated supply as standard-rated where export evidence is not held, the engine is not physically exported within 3 months, or the engine is diverted to non-qualifying use; assessments for under-declared VAT are issued with interest under VATA 1994 s.73 and Finance Act 2009 s.101. Civil penalties under FA 2007 Sch. 24 range from 30% of the potential lost VAT for careless inaccuracies, 70% for deliberate inaccuracies, and 100% for deliberate and concealed inaccuracies. Fraudulent export zero-rating claims are subject to the VAT criminal penalty regime: a penalty of up to 100% of the VAT evaded and imprisonment of up to 7 years under VATA 1994 s.72. Repayment claims and refunds already released are clawed back with interest where the zero-rating conditions are retrospectively defeated.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility in accordance with IMF Article VIII status. There are no statutory exchange controls, no prior approval requirements for profit, dividend, interest, royalty, or intercompany remittances, and no restrictions on holding balances in foreign currency. Practical impediments arise only from UK, EU, UN, and OFAC sanctions regimes, anti-money laundering requirements, and bank-level know-your-customer policies. A BVI Business Company may freely operate and convert GBP, USD, EUR, and other major currencies within the United Kingdom.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation exists. The Exchange Control Act 1947 was repealed and modern UK law imposes no mandatory timeline and no conversion ratio for export receipts. Exporters of aircraft engine leasing services or related proceeds may retain foreign currency offshore or in resident accounts, and may repatriate funds to the United Kingdom without regulatory notification. Banks may require underlying invoices, lease agreements, and customs/export documentation solely for financial crime compliance and statistical reporting purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (central bank); HM Treasury (exchange control policy and financial sanctions administration)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for UK FCA-authorized electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, and similar Payment Services Regulations 2017 firms); Low for offshore or EEA-headquartered EMIs without a UK branch, particularly after Brexit passporting removal. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot accept deposits, provide credit, issue letters of credit, or structure full escrow accounts; they must safeguard client funds in segregated accounts and do not fall within the Financial Services Compensation Scheme. Many UK EMIs impose enhanced due diligence on BVI legal persons, often declining accounts lacking tangible UK economic nexus, existing UK regulated entity support, or clear aircraft leasing documentation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan injections to the UK-banked BVI account are cleared via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the originating bank, with full remittance information including the BVI Business Company's registered legal name and LEI (549300VG_BC where applicable), lease agreement reference, and a standard business purpose narrative such as 'equity injection' or 'intercompany loan'. Because the UK abolished exchange controls, no central-bank conversion approval is required; however, the receiving bank will require the board resolution and corporate authority package, and will screen the payment against sanctions. Where the BVI entity receives capital for engine acquisition or security deposits, the payment instruction should align with the underlying lease contract, use the appropriate trade payment narrative, and be accompanied by a certified source-of-funds letter from the sending institution.Outbound distributions for trade settlements use SWIFT MT103/MT202 messages through Tier-1 clearing banks; for GBP payments, CHAPS is available; for USD payments, the bank will route via Federal Reserve (Fedwire) or correspondent Nostro processing; for EUR, TARGET2 is used.
