Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom imposes no general exchange control restrictions on current account or capital account transactions. Sterling is freely convertible. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and loan repayments may be made without prior exchange control approval. However, transactions involving sanctioned jurisdictions, persons, or dual-use goods are subject to OFSI and HMT sanctions; revenues routed via the BVI entity must demonstrate economic substance and beneficial ownership to avoid anti-avoidance challenges. The BVI Business Company is not itself subject to UK exchange control, but any UK bank account and clearing activity will be governed by UK AML and financial sanctions law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement in the United Kingdom for exporters or digital goods suppliers to surrender export proceeds to the central bank, nor any mandatory conversion ratio into sterling. Companies may hold and deploy foreign currency proceeds freely. Nonetheless, the BVI company must ensure that all receipts from App Store and Google Play payouts are traceable through the commercial settlement chain and comply with UK VAT (digital services) and OFSI reporting requirements where applicable.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority) and HM Treasury (UK Foreign Exchange Control administration is exercised through the Bank of England and HM Revenue & Customs for regulatory reporting)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for the BVI entity using regulated UK Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) or authorised payment institutions, provided the EMI accepts non-resident corporate clients. Legal constraints include: UK EMI licensing under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011; the EMI is not a bank and does not offer deposit protection (FSCS); the BVI entity must be able to satisfy internal EMI KYC and source-of-funds diligence for digital gaming revenue. Recommended EMIs include Wise Business, Currencycloud, ClearBank (for sponsored banking), and Modulr, with multi-currency IBANs and SWIFT/local clearing access.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection into the BVI entity from the UK parent or shareholders must be made via SWIFT MT103 wire transfer with accurate OFAC/UK sanctions compliance. The recommended purpose code under UK banking settlement conventions is G200 (direct investment into a company), or equivalent BOP code for convertible sterling/financing. Supporting documents include board resolution authorising share capital subscription, shareholder loan agreement, share issuance certificate, BVI registrar confirmation, and source-of-wealth evidence. For USD flows, use a clearing bank that offers FEDWIRE or CHIPS access; for GBP, use Faster Payments or CHAPS where the bank supports corporate SMS. The BVI company's bank account should clearly be designated as a treasury/holding account to enable downstream disbursement.The BVI entity should make disbursements through UK clearing rails with the following standards: SWIFT MT103 for international vendor payments and platform fees; commercial letters of credit (UCP 600) for contractual trade-related payments where required; SEPA Credit Transfer for EU based gaming service providers; Faster Payments (FPS) for UK operational expenses; CHAPS for high-value same-day settlements. The treasury function must attach the underlying invoice, licensing agreement, or service agreement to every disbursement to satisfy anti-money laundering and sanctions controls. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: UK transfer pricing rules under TIOPA 2010 apply where a UK tax resident entity transacts with a related party in a non-UK jurisdiction; small and medium-sized enterprises are generally exempt unless the counterparty is located in a territory on HMRC's tax haven list, which includes the BVI; OECD-aligned Master File and Local File documentation is expected for large businesses with consolidated group turnover above GBP 200 million; Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory where group consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750 million (approximately GBP 650 million)

Economic substance scope: A BVI Business Company monetising a hardcore MMORPG global IP is engaged in 'intellectual property business' and 'distribution and service centre' activity for the purposes of the BVI Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Act 2018; it must satisfy the economic substance test by demonstrating management and direction in the BVI, conducting core income-generating activities in the BVI, and maintaining adequate employees, premises, and expenditure in the BVI, or by showing that it is UK tax resident and subject to UK corporation tax on the relevant income; failure to do so attracts BVI ESR penalties and reinforced UK GAAR and Diverted Profits Tax scrutiny

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Cheating the Public Revenue

Statutory sanction: Tax-geared penalties of up to 100% of understated tax under FA 2007 Schedule 24 for deliberate and concealed inaccuracies; criminal prosecution for cheating the public revenue at common law carrying up to 14 years' imprisonment and an unlimited fine; confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in or fail to prevent evasion face joint and several exposure to penalties, criminal imprisonment, director disqualification for up to 15 years under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986, travel bans, asset freezes, and personal confiscation orders
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance (GAAR and Diverted Profits Tax)

Statutory sanction: UK General Anti-Abuse Rule (FA 2013 Part 5) with a 60% GAAR penalty on counteracted tax advantages; Diverted Profits Tax (FA 2015 Part 4) at 25% plus interest on diverted profits; BVI ESR financial penalties of up to USD 200,000 and daily default penalties, followed by strike-off from the BVI register for persistent non-compliance

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced for fraudulent or wrongful trading under the Insolvency Act 1986 sections 213 and 214; directors face personal civil restitution for extracted assets, disqualification, and joint liability where the company is left without assets to satisfy HMRC assessments
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Thin Capitalisation
