Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: the Bermuda Exempted Company is permanently exempt from Bermuda income, capital, withholding and dividend taxes. In Gibraltar it is prima facie a non-resident foreign company; however, if central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar it becomes Gibraltar-resident and taxable at the 10% corporate income tax rate on income arising in or derived from Gibraltar, which a UHT plant, warehouse and packaging line in Gibraltar will normally trigger.

Available election forms

No check-the-box regime exists. Available elections: (1) preserve non-resident status by holding board meetings and exercising strategic control outside Gibraltar; (2) elect Gibraltar tax residence to access the UK-Gibraltar double taxation arrangement and its associated benefits; (3) legacy Qualifying Company flat-rate election is not open to new applicants; (4) file a hybrid-mismatch entity classification election under Gibraltar's anti-hybrid (OECD BEPS Action 2) rules where the Bermuda-exempted entity is used for IP licensing or intra-group financing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax. Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area; indirect taxation is limited to import duties and excise duties on selected goods entering Gibraltar.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; no VAT registration threshold exists. For customs purposes, no economic threshold applies - every importer of oats, aseptic packaging materials and plant machinery must hold an HM Customs trader registration and file entry declarations for duty assessment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

No 0% export VAT exists because the system is not VAT-based. Instead: (a) finished aseptic UHT oat milk cartons exported from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar import duty and bear no Gibraltar indirect tax; (b) duty-paid raw materials and packaging physically incorporated into exported goods qualify for a duty drawback (refund) claim; (c) duty suspension and transit relief are available for inputs held under customs control and for goods passing through Gibraltar.

  • Customs export declaration (Gibraltar SAD/EX electronic declaration)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of lading or airway bill evidencing physical departure from Gibraltar
  • Certificate of origin where preferential tariff treatment is claimed
  • Inward remittance certificate or SWIFT evidence of export proceeds settlement
  • Duty-paid import records and production reconciliation for drawback claims
  • Certificate of analysis and health certificate where required by the destination country

None. Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, SAF-T reporting, digital stamping or government-API invoicing. Ordinary commercial invoices and electronic customs declarations suffice; accounting and customs records must be retained for the statutory limitation period.

HM Customs Gibraltar may conduct post-clearance inspections comparing export declarations, physical stock, bill of lading dates and FX settlements. Fraudulent or negligent claims trigger repayment of the full duty relieved plus statutory interest, civil penalties up to GIP 25,000 or three times the duty, forfeiture of goods and conveyances, debarment from duty-suspension schemes, and criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 7 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates with the Gibraltar pound at a fixed 1:1 peg to GBP, freely interchangeable for clearing in the UK. Both capital and current account transactions are fully convertible with no surrender, approval, licensing, or remittance restriction. A Bermuda Exempted Company using a Gibraltar trading vehicle may freely receive equity, extend intercompany loans, service trade payables, and repatriate profits or dissolve proceeds. There is no Gibraltar withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, branch remittances, or capital distributions, and no exchange controls limiting inbound or outbound settlement currencies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rule or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Gibraltar or in the UK for export receipts of a Gibraltar-domiciled entity. Export proceeds may be retained in the original currency, converted at customer-determined market rates, or used offshore without time-bound repatriation obligations. For financial reporting, export receivables and proceeds must be translated into the functional currency (ordinarily GBP) at the prevailing spot rate, but no central-bank conversion certificate is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (ultimate monetary authority for GBP); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as financial regulatory and AML/CFT supervisor; HM Treasury as the sovereign body having historically retained reserve powers under the Exchange Control Act 1947, all operative exchange controls having been abolished.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for UK Financial Conduct Authority-authorised e-money institutions (EMIs) operating through banking-as-a-service rails when the underlying settlement account is held at a GFSC-licensed bank in Gibraltar; Low-Medium for pure offshore digital EMIs not authorised in the UK or Gibraltar, because Gibraltar requires payment and e-money business to be licensed under the Financial Services (Investments and Fiduciary Services) Act 2019, and unlicensed third-country wallets cannot be segregated as client money. Post-Brexit, EEA EMI passports do not automatically extend into Gibraltar; therefore an offshore EMI must rely on UK third-country processes or a local Gibraltar EMI licence. Legal constraints also include mandatory safeguarding of client funds, sanctions screening on payment counterparties, and the prohibition on using digital wallets for high-value capital injections or trade disbursements where a properly formed banking relationship is expected.

The bank will require a board resolution authorising the capital call, subscriber ledger, and a source-of-funds declaration from the remitting entity. The receiving Gibraltar bank will verify the BIC, account number, and a unique reference string exactly matching the task composite key for audit trail.
