Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A BVI Business Company is a non-UK resident company. Step 1 — Corporation Tax: register with HMRC via form CT41G or the online 'Corporation Tax: registration' service within 12 months of the end of the first accounting period in which a UK permanent establishment, UK property business, or other UK chargeable income arises; HMRC then issues a company Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the BVI Business Company is exempt from BVI income tax, corporation tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, and stamp duty under the BVI Business Companies Act, 2004 (as amended). The entity is not fiscally transparent for UK purposes: once a UK permanent establishment, UK property business, or UK-source chargeable activity exists, UK Corporation Tax applies at entity level under CTA 2009/CTA 2010 — small profits rate 19% (profits up to £50,000), main rate 25% (profits above £250,000), with marginal relief between the bands. A UK PE is attributed profits under the OECD authorised approach and is chargeable to UK corporation tax on those attributable profits.

Available election forms

The UK does not operate a check-the-box or pass-through election regime. Available statutory elections for a foreign BVI BC include: (1) CTA 2009 s.18A election — a non-UK resident company may elect to be charged to corporation tax solely on its UK property business profits and UK land gains without otherwise coming within the charge; (2) VATA 1994 Schedule 1 paragraph 4 voluntary-registration election — an intending trader may elect to register for VAT from the date of first intended taxable supply; (3) VATA 1994 Schedule 10 Option to Tax — election to waive exemption on an interest in land to recover input VAT on the fit-out of a UK ghost-kitchen unit; (4) TIOPA 2010 Part 6A hybrid/other-mismatch rules apply automatically without unilateral election, counteracting deduction/non-inclusion and double-deduction mismatches in BVI BC-pass-through arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 rolling 12-month taxable turnover (VATA 1994, Schedule 1, effective 1 April 2024; prior threshold £85,000 until 31 March 2024). Non-established taxable persons have no threshold and must register before making any taxable supply in the UK. In the ghost-kitchen context, hot takeaway food is standard-rated and counts towards the threshold; cold takeaway food is generally zero-rated and treated as disregarded non-taxable supply for threshold computation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under VATA 1994 s.30(6) and Schedule 8 Group 7 requires cumulative satisfaction of: (1) an export supply — goods physically removed from the UK by the supplier or on their behalf (or supplied to a non-UK resident who exports them), evidenced by a CDS export declaration; (2) timely export — goods must leave the UK within 3 months of the time of supply (VAT Regulations 1995, Regulation 162 evidence-of-export rules); and (3) documentary proof — the commercial and customs evidence bundle must be retained. Supplies of services to overseas business customers (e.g., cross-border brand-licence fees or platform-assignment fees settled outside the UK) are outside the scope of UK VAT under the place-of-supply rules (customer's country) without a zero-rating certificate; B2C electronically supplied services to non-UK private consumers are likewise located overseas, subject to effective use-and-enjoyment provisions. All zero-rating and outside-scope claims presuppose the 180-day foreign-exchange settlement condition: export proceeds must be received and settled into the approved bank account within the 180-day safe-harbour deadline to avoid recharacterisation as a doubtful-receipt or non-repatriated transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration — accepted by the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) with the unique Movement Reference Number (MRN) assigned
  • Bill of Lading — or Air Waybill/CMR consignment note evidencing physical departure of the goods from the UK within 3 months of the time of supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate — bank or foreign-exchange confirmation evidencing settlement of the export proceeds within the 180-day FX repatriation deadline

No real-time government clearance, digital fiscal stamping, or continuous transaction control (CTC) mandate applies in the UK. Making Tax Digital for VAT is mandatory for all VAT-registered businesses: digital records must be kept and VAT returns submitted via HMRC-recognised software using the HMRC VAT API (application-to-application); direct portal keying is not permitted. The Customs Declaration Service (CDS) requires electronic export declarations and issues a unique Movement Reference Number (MRN). E-invoices need no fiscal clearance or stamping; UK public-sector procurement voluntarily uses the PEPPOL BIS Billing 3.0 standard, and cross-border B2B e-invoices must show the recipient's VAT identification number and be retained under the VAT Regulations 1995 (SI 1995/2518), Regulation 31/13A.

HMRC audits export zero-rating through the export-evidence compliance programme (VAT Notice 703 protocol) and may retrospectively assess previously zero-rated output VAT, with default interest under VATA 1994 s.74 and penalties under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007: up to 30% for careless inaccuracy, 70% for deliberate inaccuracy, and 100% for deliberate inaccuracy with concealment of the VAT lost. Assessment time limits are 4 years for careless errors and up to 20 years for deliberate or concealed behaviour (FA 2008, Schedule 39). Fraudulent evasion of VAT, including the use of sham export evidence, is a criminal offence under VATA 1994 s.72(1), punishable on indictment by imprisonment and/or an unlimited fine, with the Fraud Act 2006 dishonesty offences (up to 10 years' imprisonment) available in aggravated cases. In cross-border goods-fraud chains HMRC may disallow input tax and invoke joint-and-several liability under VATA 1994 s.73A against exporters who knew or should have known that the supply chain was connected with fraudulent evasion, with retrospective clawback of both input and output VAT from the date the fraud became apparent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. There is no general exchange control legislation restricting payments to non-residents, and profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriation may be effected freely in any permitted currency. However, transactions involving sanctioned jurisdictions, persons, or entities are prohibited under the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018, and banks will independently impose enhanced due diligence and may reject certain high-risk payment corridors notwithstanding formal FX liberalisation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation in the United Kingdom requiring the surrender or mandatory conversion of export proceeds into GBP. Export receipts may be retained in any currency. Reporting obligations are limited to those imposed by the Bank of England's data collection framework for balance-of-payments statistics (via the International Transactions Reporting System where applicable) and general anti-money laundering record-keeping obligations under the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority) and HM Treasury (for financial sanctions and exchange control matters)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High, with legal constraints. Authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) or equivalent EEA passported institutions may provide GBP IBANs and cross-border virtual accounts without requiring a physical branch relationship. Key constraints: (a) the BVI company must have a registered office and directors' identification meeting the EMI's KYC policy; (b) EMIs cannot offer lending or hold client funds as deposits, so funds held are e-money safeguarded accounts, not bank deposits; (c) several UK EMIs restrict account opening for BVI-registered entities or impose 100% shareholder/director disclosure and source-of-funds evidence; (d) due diligence under Money Laundering Regulations 2017 requires confirmation of the ultimate beneficial owner's identity, address, and source of wealth. Recommended FMIs include Wise (TransferWise Ltd), Revolut Ltd (FCA-regulated EMI), Airwallex (UK) Ltd, and Currencycloud (Visa-owned), with viability depending on exact onboarding criteria and transaction profile.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for shareholder loans or equity injection from BVI parent to the UK operational entity/account should use SWIFT MT103 over the CHAPS/Faster Payments clearing system for same-day GBP, or TARGET2/SEPA for EUR, and Fedwire for USD. Recommended purpose/message codes: for equity investment, use 'Equity injection / capital contribution' with reason code consistent with BOP (e.g., UK BOP 'A' series or bank-specific 'CF - Capital Funding' descriptor); for shareholder loans, create a formal intercompany loan agreement with a fixed maturity and arm's-length interest rate, and use purpose code 'Intercompany loan' / 'Trade finance' to avoid misclassification. The BVI company must provide evidence of source of funds (e.g., audited financials, dividend warrant, or sale agreement) and the UK bank will require a completed source-of-funds questionnaire. Where the UK entity has a separate account, a board resolution in both BVI and UK entities authorising the injection is mandatory. For amounts above the applicable threshold, additional enquiry may arise under the Reporting of Payment Statistics or FATCA/CRS, but no prior regulatory approval is required for capital account inflows.Trade settlements for food supplies, packaging, logistics, and platform fees should be executed through SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) or MT101 (pre-instruction for multiple payments) via the UK bank's commercial banking channel. For larger/recurring cross-border purchases, a Commercial Letter of Credit (sight or usance) can be used, although acceptance may be low for typical ghost-kitchen operating expenses. Acceptable rails include: (a) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments in GBP, USD, and EUR; (b) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for eurozone suppliers; (c) CHAPS for GBP urgent payments; (d) Faster Payments for low-value domestic GBP disbursements; (e) FPS/Open Banking payment initiation to release settlement funds to delivery platforms for merchant payouts. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High, with legal constraints. Authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) and payment institutions regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) or equivalent EEA passported institutions may provide GBP IBANs and cross-border virtual accounts without requiring a physical branch relationship. Key constraints: (a) the BVI company must have a registered office and directors' identification meeting the EMI's KYC policy; (b) EMIs cannot offer lending or hold client funds as deposits, so funds held are e-money safeguarded accounts, not bank deposits; (c) several UK EMIs restrict account opening for BVI-registered entities or impose 100% shareholder/director disclosure and source-of-funds evidence; (d) due diligence under Money Laundering Regulations 2017 requires confirmation of the ultimate beneficial owner's identity, address, and source of wealth. Recommended FMIs include Wise (TransferWise Ltd), Revolut Ltd (FCA-regulated EMI), Airwallex (UK) Ltd, and Currencycloud (Visa-owned), with viability depending on exact onboarding criteria and transaction profile.
