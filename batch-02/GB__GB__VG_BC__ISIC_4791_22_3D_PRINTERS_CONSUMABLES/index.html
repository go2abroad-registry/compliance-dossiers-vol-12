VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 UK taxable turnover for UK-established businesses; no registration threshold for non-established overseas entities that make any taxable supply in the UK. Distance selling and online marketplace rules may treat the marketplace as liable for overseas sellers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK are zero-rated if exported by the supplier or a person acting on their behalf within statutory time limits (generally 3 months from the time of supply) and evidence of export is retained. B2B services supplied to a customer belonging outside the UK are generally outside the scope of UK VAT under place-of-supply rules; services directly connected to exported goods may be zero-rated under VATA 1994 s.30 and SI 1995/2518.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD/C21 via Customs Declaration Service)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Waybill showing departure from the UK
  • Commercial invoice confirming 0% VAT treatment and exporter details
  • Packing list and weight certificate
  • Bank or payment service settlement evidence (e.g., SWIFT or Stripe payout records) proving export proceeds were received

No real-time fiscal clearance or government-mandated e-invoicing. However, Making Tax Digital (MTD) requires VAT-registered businesses above the threshold to keep digital records and file VAT returns using MTD-compatible software with HMRC APIs. Imported goods require customs declarations via the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) and postponed VAT accounting is available.

Fraudulent or careless export zero-rating claims attract HMRC assessments for up to 4 years (20 years in cases of deliberate error), penalties under Finance Act 2007 Sch 24 up to 100% of VAT lost, default surcharge under VATA 1994, and interest. Criminal prosecution under CEMA 1979 s.170 and VATA 1994 s.72 may result in imprisonment, full VAT clawback, and forfeiture of goods.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom operates a fully liberalised capital and current account convertibility regime. There are no general exchange controls, no restriction on foreign-currency accounts, and no statutory authorisation requirement for the repatriation of dividends, profits, or share capital. Convertibility may be impeded only by targeted financial sanctions administered by HM Treasury's Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI), antimony-laundering countermeasures, or where a counterparty bank has independent internal policy restrictions on jurisdictions perceived as tax havens. Profit remittance from a UK bank account of a BVI Business Company is lawful and unregulated provided that the underlying distribution satisfies the BVI Companies Act solvency test and the receiving bank's beneficial ownership and source-of-funds checks are cleared.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds under UK law. Export receipts may be retained, settled, or converted into any currency at the exporter's discretion without obligation to surrender to the Bank of England or HM Revenue & Customs. The only applicable requirements are conventional banking record-keeping, anti-money-laundering reporting for suspicious transactions, sanctions screening, and accurate bookkeeping for corporation tax and VAT purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (with HM Treasury as the competent authority for financial sanctions and residual exchange control matters)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High feasibility, subject to FCA authorisation or registration as an Authorised Electronic Money Institution (AEMI) or Authorised Payment Institution (API). Offshore digital EMIs without a UK establishment can provide merchant accounts and virtual IBANs to BVI companies, but must apply equal KYC/CDD standards and typically require a genuine commercial nexus, summary financial statements, and proof of physical trade. Legal constraints include the loss of EEA passporting into the UK post-Brexit, tougher source-of-funds verification for tax-exempt offshore entities, and OFSI sanctions screening. EMI accounts are operationally viable for low-risk Ecommerce receipts from Stripe and Shopify Payments, but are not yet a mature substitute for a tier-1 clearing bank for high-value international wire settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection should be executed via SWIFT MT103 from the beneficial owner's personal or corporate bank account to the BVI company's UK mirror or operational account. The payment instruction must include the full legal name of the beneficiary, the BVI company's registered address, the UK bank account IBAN, and a purpose narrative such as 'Capital contribution to BVI Business Company for investment in UK/EU e-commerce operations'. Although the UK does not maintain a codified positive list of purpose codes, the bank may require the remitter to articulate whether the funds are equity capital, shareholder loan, or working capital. A board resolution approving the capital contribution, executed share allotment documents, and a source-of-funds declaration should accompany the first transfer to satisfy AML checks. Payments above EUR 10,000 are automatically screened under the Funds Transfer Regulation and no structuring or split-transaction techniques may be used.Supplier and trade disbursements for the procurement of desktop 3D printers, resin filaments, and consumables should be settled through SWIFT MT103 direct wires for low-value recurring purchases, and through commercial letters of credit (MT700) where a supplier requires documentary payment security. The standard rail is an irrevocable documentary letter of credit opened by a UK or international tier-1 bank in favour of the overseas manufacturer, with invoices, packing lists, bills of lading or airway bills, certificates of origin, and inspection certificates as the required presentation documents. For high-frequency e-commerce inventory replenishment, a bank-open account with supplier factoring or trade credit may be accepted where a proven payment history exists. All disbursements must be processed in a manner consistent with OFSI sanctions screening and must preserve a complete audit trail of purchase orders, supplier due diligence, currency spot-rate confirmations, and actual Swift MT103 or MT700 advise.Deliverable FX Forwards (Vanilla FX forwards) with a UK-authorised bank or FCA-regulated payment institution for deterministic inventory-replenishment cyclesNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for emerging-market supplier currencies where convertibility is restricted, such as certain Asian or Middle Eastern currency pairs, provided the BVI company's bank accepts the credit exposureCross-Currency Swaps for medium-term shareholder loans or trade finance facilities to manage principal and interest exposure between USD, GBP, EUR, and CNYFX Spot transactions executed at transparent market rates with spread limits agreed by the treasury policyOptions-based structures such as FX collars and vanilla FX options are permissible, but are not considered necessary for a low-risk hardware e-commerce treasury profile
