Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, HM Government of Gibraltar Income Tax Office; corporate registry: Gibraltar Companies House (Registrar of Companies); portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi (Income Tax Office and Companies House sections); customs and excise: Gibraltar Customs Department (HM Government of Gibraltar).

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Bermuda Exempted Company status; non-resident owned and controlled; Gibraltar operates territorial taxation and no VAT; profits attributable to foreign source trading not managed and controlled in Gibraltar are outside Gibraltar corporation tax; entity-level taxation only on Gibraltar-source profits at 12.5% standard rate).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box for a Bermuda Exempted Company. Available statutory elections in the Gibraltar/Bermuda framework: (i) Bermuda Exempted Company annual certificate election under Bermuda Companies Act 1981 s.129A enabling a class-A/class-B exempt undertaking election; (ii) Gibraltar company tax residence election based on place of central management and control; (iii) group relief / loss surrender election under Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 for qualifying group members; (iv) capital allowance pooling elections for plant and machinery; (v) hybrid mismatch neutralizing election aligning with OECD BEPS Action 2 as implemented through UK/Gibraltar cooperation, relevant for defense procurement payment structures involving government wires and diplomatic letters of credit.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar does not operate VAT, GST, or sales and use tax. Indirect taxation comprises Import Duty (Customs Ordinance, Cap. 107), Excise Duty on alcohol, tobacco, and hydrocarbons, Stamp Duty on transfers of property and securities, and a small number of environmental/health levies.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 0 - no VAT threshold exists because Gibraltar has no VAT. Any person importing goods into Gibraltar must obtain a Customs Importer Code regardless of value; excise registrations are mandatory for alcohol, tobacco, and hydrocarbon products. For defense goods subject to strategic export control, registration occurs at the license application stage via the Export Control Joint Unit (ECJU), not via a turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as Gibraltar has no VAT; therefore no 0% VAT export zero-rating can be claimed. Instead, direct exports from Gibraltar of armored military tactical vehicles and their parts are outside the scope of Gibraltar import duty and excise duty, provided the goods leave the territory permanently under an approved customs export procedure (free circulation → direct export). Raw materials imported for manufacturing within a Gibraltar authorized economic operator facility may be imported duty-free under inward-processing/suspension arrangements, with duty discharged and a drawback possible upon export of finished defense goods.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (electronic entry with unique consignment reference)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing carriage to the final government/diplomatic end user
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 confirmation of export payment
  • End-User Certificate and End-User Form (for military/dual-use goods)
  • ECJU Individual Export Licence or Open General Export Licence authorization number
  • Certificate of Origin (Gibraltar Chamber of Commerce) supporting preferential duty claims, if any

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime is in force in Gibraltar; there is no B2B e-invoicing mandate. Import declarations must be submitted electronically through the Gibraltar Customs Department's electronic entry processing system, using harmonized EU/UK customs data standards, with pre-arrival declaration for controlled defense goods. Goods moving into or out of Gibraltar customs territory must reference the approved customs declaration number; for defense equipment, clearance is further conditioned on prior ECJU license approval and compliance with the UK Strategic Export Control regime. The 'one-time' physical inspection by the Gibraltar Customs Department is performed for cargo flagged as High-Risk under dual-use and military end-use screening.

Gibraltar Customs and the ECJU apply enhanced scrutiny to defense-sector export transactions. Misdeclaration, diversion, or failure to substantiate the export of controlled armored vehicles triggers: (i) seizure and forfeiture of goods and any related equipment; (ii) customs penalty of up to three times the value of the duty evaded or GIP 10,000 per entry under the Customs Ordinance; (iii) criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion with imprisonment up to seven years; (iv) immediate revocation of the ECJU license and prohibition on future exports; (v) retrospective clawback of all duty relief/drawback granted, including interest; (vi) civil monetary penalties under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act for money laundering risks associated with defense payments; (vii) cross-border enforcement via HM Revenue & Customs and international sanctions/military end-use enforcement groups.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is fully convertible into pound sterling at parity. No exchange-control approval is required for current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittance to Bermuda or any other jurisdiction is unrestricted, but a licensed Gibraltar bank may require audited financial statements, board resolutions, source-of-funds documentation, and an enhanced AML/CFT review before executing a large dividend or capital-transfer payment. There are no Gibraltar withholding taxes on dividends, interest, or royalties, which reinforces free convertibility for treasury purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar has no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds to the central monetary authority, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no exchange-control repatriation timeline. Export receipts for ISIC 3040 armored tactical vehicles may be retained in GIP, GBP, USD, or EUR, and may be used directly for cross-border trade settlements without prior exchange administration approval.

Central bank supervision: Government of Gibraltar (Currency Notes Act 1934) as currency-issuing authority; Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as banking and FX-licensed conduct supervisor; Bank of England as monetary anchor through the Gibraltar pound's 1:1 peg to sterling.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. Offshore digital EMIs not licensed by the GFSC cannot open a compliant operational account for a high-risk defense-tactical hardware manufacturer. Gibraltar-based electronic money institutions are permitted only if they hold a valid EU/UK-equivalent authorization or a Gibraltar Financial Services Act 2019 licence; even then, most EMI/PSP platforms explicitly exclude arms, munitions, and military equipment transactions due to sanctions, export controls, and reputational risk. An unregulated Bermuda or UK fast-tech EMI is legally incapable of supporting Government_Direct_Wire or Diplomatic_LC settlement flows for this business scope.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from the Bermuda Exempted Company parent to the Gibraltar treasury entity will be routed over SWIFT MT103 with clear remittance head-office reference: 'equity capital contribution' or 'subordinated working capital loan'. The bank mandate will require the following control bundle: board resolution approving the capital call, executed equity subscription or loan agreement, source-of-funds and source-of-wealth report, consolidated group financials, beneficial ownership declaration, and GFSC-compliant purpose-code annotation under the BOP/statistical reporting classification for cross-border treasury movements.
