Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The UK does not operate a check-the-box classification system; entity classification follows domestic legal form. However, non-UK entities can be treated as transparent under UK hybrid mismatch rules if they resemble UK partnerships, and certain companies may make elections under CTA 2010 Part 6A (hybrid mismatch measures) or elect to align accounting periods and tax returns. No general deemed-registration election applies for BVI Business Companies in respect of UK indirect tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 90,000 for UK-established taxable persons; non-UK established businesses have no registration threshold and must register from the first taxable supply (or as required under the reverse charge for electronically supplied services).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

UK VAT zero-rating applies to goods physically exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK, provided the supplier obtains and retains valid commercial evidence of export within the statutory time limit (normally three months for goods exported by the seller). Services supplied to a business customer established outside the UK are generally outside the scope of UK VAT, while services to non-business customers outside the UK may be zero-rated if the place of supply rule places them outside the UK. For goods consigned to an overseas customer, evidence must include customs documentation, commercial transport, and payment records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/electronic Export Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document
  • Proof of legal export (port/airport departure evidence)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation (FX repatriation evidence)
  • Commercial export invoice matching the customs declaration

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime. Electronic VAT invoicing is permitted, and businesses subject to Making Tax Digital for VAT must keep digital records and submit VAT returns through HMRC-recognised software using its API, but there is no prior electronic invoice clearance obligation.

HMRC may issue VAT assessments to claw back zero-rating benefits on exports if evidence is deficient, apply default surcharge and interest, impose penalties of up to 100% of the VAT involved for dishonest conduct, and commence criminal proceedings for fraudulent evasion of VAT. Full retention of export evidence for at least six years is required.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure convertibility exists on both current and capital accounts: the Exchange Control Act 1947 was repealed in 1979, and no UK statute restrains conversion, denomination, holding, or repatriation of e-money float, dividends, licence fees, trade settlements, or liquidation proceeds. Dividends from a UK tax-resident EMI to the BVI parent are not subject to UK withholding tax and require no Bank of England approval. Operational friction is limited to FCA safeguarding rules requiring client e-money funds to remain segregated and unencumbered, AML/KYC source-of-funds verification at onboarding, and EMIR/MiFID transaction-reporting obligations attached to any treasury FX hedge.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. UK law imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no exchange-control approval for holding foreign-currency receipts. For an electronic money institution, the only enforceable receipt-side duties are: (1) record-keeping and travel-rule data capture for each cross-border payment under the Payment Services Regulations 2017; (2) UK Intrastat and EC Sales List filings when intra-EU trade activity breaches HMRC statistical thresholds; and (3) FCA client-asset reconciliation rules requiring any foreign-currency float to be valued at closing Bank of England reference rates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (BoE) is the central bank and operates the United Kingdom's high-value settlement system via CHAPS and Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS). HM Treasury holds policy power over monetary and payments legislation, but there is no standalone foreign-exchange control bureau. For licenced electronic money institutions, day-to-day FX-related supervision is exercised by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011 and the Payment Services Regulations 2017.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability: High, but only through FCA authorisation. The BVI Business Company cannot itself operate as the UK EMI; the compliant structure is a UK-incorporated, FCA-authorised EMI subsidiary with initial capital of EUR 350,000, or a UK branch of an EEA-licensed EMI under the now-expired Temporary Permissions Regime (terminated 31 December 2023). Offshore remote e-money licences from BVI or Pacific jurisdictions are not recognised as passported providers and cannot issue e-money into the UK market; they may act solely as upstream shareholders or distribution partners. Practical operating sponsors include ClearBank, Griffin, Modulr, and comparable banking-as-a-service platforms connecting the FCA-licensed EMI to CHAPS, Faster Payments, and SEPA via an EEA partner bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% main rate on profits above GBP 250,000; 19% small profits rate on profits up to GBP 50,000; 26.5% effective marginal rate on profits between GBP 50,001 and GBP 250,000 (UK financial year 2025 rates).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%: the UK imposes no withholding tax on dividend distributions paid by a UK-resident company; dividends are paid gross under CTA 2010 Part 23.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

File no later than 12 months after the end of the accounting period; pay corporation tax by 9 months and 1 day after the period end where annual taxable profits are below GBP 1.5 million; quarterly instalment payments apply where annual taxable profits exceed GBP 1.5 million.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: UK TIOPA 2010 Part 4 imposes no monetary threshold, but the SME exemption applies where the group has fewer than 250 employees and either turnover not exceeding GBP 43 million or balance sheet total not exceeding GBP 37 million; larger groups must prepare OECD-compliant Local File and Master File; CbC reporting applies where consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 750 million (approximately GBP 645 million). UK thin capitalisation rules apply to intra-group debt from the BVI parent, requiring arm's-length interest rates and debt capacity.

Economic substance scope: The BVI Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Act 2018 applies to the BVI BC's finance and leasing and headquarters activities: an annual Economic Substance Return must be filed in BVI, claiming exemption only by demonstrating UK tax residence through an HMRC tax residence certificate; without that certificate, the BVI entity must satisfy the BVI economic substance test. On the UK side, the EMI must evidence genuine management and control, UK employees, premises and decision-making at its UK head office, and the FCA e-money licence itself mandates a UK head office and UK-based senior management, aligning with the economic substance expectations of HMRC.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Deliberate and concealed inaccuracy penalties up to 100% of the tax under FA 2007 Schedule 24; offshore asset penalties up to 200% under Schedule 45; prosecution under TMA 1970 with an unlimited fine and imprisonment of up to 7 years; interest at Bank of England base rate plus 2.5%.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted for cheating the public revenue; POCA 2002 civil recovery, freezing orders and confiscation proceedings may be imposed; travel bans and departure restrictions are available; disqualification as a director for up to 15 years under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986.
Failure to Prevent Facilitation of Tax Evasion (Corporate Criminal Offence)

Statutory sanction: Unlimited fine under the Criminal Finances Act 2017 sections 45 and 46; debarment from public procurement; FCA enforcement action; asset freezing and remediation costs.

Director personal liability: The offence is corporate, but the directing mind and senior officers may face secondary liability for aiding and abetting evasion; FCA Senior Managers and Certification Regime enforcement may remove approvals; director disqualification may follow.
AML / Counter-Terrorist Financing Breach by an Electronic Money Institution

Statutory sanction: Unlimited FCA financial penalty; revocation of the EMI licence under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011; penalties under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017; money-laundering offences under POCA 2002 sections 327-329 carrying up to 14 years' imprisonment; asset forfeiture and freezing.

Director personal liability: The MLRO and senior managers are personally liable; FCA prohibition orders may bar individuals from regulated functions; criminal prosecution for consenting to or facilitating laundering; failure to disclose under POCA 2002 sections 331-332 carries up to 5 years' imprisonment; travel restrictions and confiscation orders are available.
Shell Company / BVI Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: BVI International Tax Authority penalties escalate from USD 10,000 to USD 200,000; continued non-compliance may reach USD 400,000 with strike-off and dissolution of the BVI Business Company; in the UK, submission of false information to HMRC triggers Schedule 24 penalties and potential fraud prosecution.

Director personal liability: BVI directors making knowingly false declarations face imprisonment of up to 5 years under the BVI ESR Act; UK directors face joint and several liability for fraudulent evasion and civil restitution; automatic disqualification and travel restrictions may follow BVI strike-off and UK insolvency proceedings.
