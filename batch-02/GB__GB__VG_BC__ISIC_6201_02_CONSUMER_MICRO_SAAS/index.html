Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital applies. A BVI Business Company may be incorporated with nil or a single no-par-value share; capital may be designated in any currency and no escrow or capital deposit requirement exists at incorporation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC); official online portal: https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/hm-revenue-customs

E-government filing portal

The target entity is a BVI Business Company (BC) which, under BVI law, is tax-exempt. For UK tax purposes the BC is treated as a foreign company, not as a pass-through entity. The BC must register for UK VAT as a non-established taxable person if it makes any taxable supplies in the UK from which UK VAT is chargeable. For SaaS/digital services supplied to UK private consumers, the place of supply is the UK and the non-established supplier has a mandatory VAT registration obligation from the first supply, with no UK turnover threshold. Registration procedure: complete HMRC form VAT1 (for non-EU/non-UK established businesses) or use the HMRC online VAT registration service for non-established taxable persons; provide the BVI Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, evidence of business activity (website/subscription platform, merchant agreements with Paddle or Lemon Squeezy, sample invoices), bank account reference from an eligible EMI or bank, and, if appointed, details of a UK tax representative. HMRC will issue a 9-digit GB VAT registration number. For UK Corporation Tax, a non-UK resident company is registrable only if it carries on a trade in the UK through a permanent establishment. If a UK PE exists, the BC must register for Corporation Tax within 3 months of commencing UK activity using form CT41G and HMRC will issue a 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference. If no UK PE exists, no UK Corporation Tax registration is required. Where Paddle or Lemon Squeezy acts as Merchant of Record, the platform may be the supplier of the underlying services to the end consumer and may account for UK VAT; in that case, separate UK VAT registration by the BC may not be required, but contractual allocation of VAT liability must be documented and confirmed by legal advice.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The UK does not operate a check-the-box regime for foreign entities. The BVI BC is an opaque, separately taxable entity for UK tax; it cannot elect transparent/pass-through treatment. There is no hybrid mismatch election available; the UK hybrid mismatch rules in CTA 2010 Part 6A operate automatically to recharacterise payments where there is a mismatch in tax treatment. For VAT, a non-established taxable person may elect to register voluntarily before making supplies and may, if it has a UK fixed establishment, apply to join a UK VAT group. If the BC has a UK permanent establishment, it may make a UK PE notification and is required to apply arm's length attribution to the PE under the UK transfer pricing rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for non-established taxable persons; the annual registration threshold of GBP 85,000 applies only to UK-established businesses. A BVI BC supplying digital services to UK consumers must register from the first supply because the place of supply is the UK.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For the target Micro-SaaS business, the principal supplies are digital services, not physical goods. Zero-rating for goods applies only where physical goods are exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK within the time limits in the Export (Goods) Order 2000: the goods must be removed to a non-UK destination within 3 months of supply; the supplier must hold evidence of export in the form of official customs documents and transport documents; the recipient must be outside the UK; and the export must satisfy HMRC's direct or indirect export conditions. Digital services supplied B2C to UK consumers are subject to UK VAT at 20% and are not zero-rated; digital services supplied to non-UK business customers or non-UK consumers fall outside the scope of UK VAT because the place of supply is outside the UK, rather than being zero-rated exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/C88 submitted via the UK Customs Declaration Service or National Export System)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / CMR consignment note or recognised courier shipment evidence
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract
  • Bank transfer / SWIFT / remittance certificate evidencing receipt of foreign currency consideration
  • Evidence of non-UK customer status (overseas VAT registration number or tax identification number)
  • Export packing list and freight invoices substantiating physical removal from the UK

The UK does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control mandate. E-invoices are permitted and must satisfy VAT invoicing content requirements. VAT returns must be filed electronically via HMRC's Making Tax Digital (MTD) system for VAT using MTD-compatible software; businesses must keep digital records and submit returns using functional compatible software. No prior HMRC fiscal authorisation is required for the issuance of individual invoices.

HMRC may deny or claw back zero-rating where export evidence is incomplete, inconsistent, or obtained after the supply. Where zero-rating is denied, HMRC may issue an assessment for the unpaid UK VAT, plus statutory interest from the date the VAT should have been accounted. Civil penalties for inaccuracy or tax evasion may be imposed under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007: careless inaccuracy attracts up to 30% of the potential lost revenue; deliberate but unconcealed acts up to 70%; deliberate and concealed acts up to 100%, reduced for prompt or unprompted disclosure. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion of VAT may be pursued under Section 72 Value Added Tax Act 1994, carrying unlimited fines and imprisonment. Assessment and clawback time limits are generally 4 years, extended to 20 years where fraud or deliberate non-disclosure is involved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom operates a fully liberalised foreign-exchange regime. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible; no prior authorisation is required for purchases or sales of foreign currency. Profit remittances, dividends, interest, and liquidation proceeds may be remitted abroad without restriction, subject only to standard AML/CFT verification and, where relevant, taxation under UK source rules or double-taxation relief. The BVI Business Company is a non-resident entity for UK tax purposes unless UK management and control are exercised; a zero-rate BVI tax profile does not create a UK remittance restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no UK statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirements for export receipts. The Exchange Control Act 1947 was repealed in 1979; exporters and service providers may retain, convert, or repatriate foreign currency freely. For statistical and financial-prudential reporting, UK banks apply Balance of Payments reporting codes for cross-border credits and may require supporting trade documents, but no percentage of export proceeds must be surrendered to the Bank of England or converted into sterling.

Central bank supervision: The Governor and Company of the Bank of England (the Bank of England) acting jointly with HM Treasury for exchange-control policy; the Prudential Regulation Authority (UK) as the micro-prudential banking supervisor; His Majesty's Revenue and Customs as the anti-money-laundering and transaction-reporting authority under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility for an FCA-authorised or EEA-passported Electronic Money Institution such as Revolut Business, Tide, or Wise Business. Such EMIs routinely accept BVI corporate clients when final beneficial owners are disclosed and supporting substance documents are provided. Legal constraints are material: the EMI must hold safeguarding accounts for client funds, use EEA or UK payment clearing rails, and must not be used as a substitute for a full-service corporate bank. Unregulated offshore digital EMIs are not viable and should be rejected for treasury purposes.
