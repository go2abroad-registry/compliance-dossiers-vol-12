Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC); online services through HMRC Government Gateway, Making Tax Digital (MTD) platform, and the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) for export filings.

E-government filing portal

A BVI Business Company not resident in the UK has no automatic obligation to obtain a Corporation Tax Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) or register a UK VAT ID unless it acquires a UK taxable presence (e.g., permanent establishment) or makes taxable supplies in the UK. To secure a UK VAT ID, the foreign entity must file form VAT1 (or register through the Government Gateway) and may be required to provide a UK tax representative or security if not established in the UK. For UK Corporation Tax, if the company is treated as carrying on a trade through a UK PE, it must apply for a UTR via form CT41G and register for self-assessment with HMRC's Corporation Tax online service. For VAT on import/export of goods, the entity must obtain an Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number or a UK customs EORI if transacting at the border.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

The UK has no US-style 'check-the-box' classification. A foreign entity's tax status is determined by UK domestic law principles of residence (central management and control) and permanent establishment. Available elections include: (i) an election under Taxation of Chargeable Gains Act 1992 s.13D to treat a non-resident company's UK property business as a separate notional business enabling certain capital losses; (ii) elections to allocate income or give notice under the UK hybrid and other mismatch regimes (Corporation Tax Act 2010 Part 6A) to counteract deduction/non-inclusion mismatches; (iii) option to tax (election for VAT purposes) on land and buildings under the Value Added Tax Act 1994 Sch 10; (iv) controversy remains regarding 'tax transparency elections' for foreign PPA/LPA structures, but such treatment is not statutorily available for BVI BCs. All available elections are procedural and cannot alter the statutory tax classification of a non-resident corporate entity at will.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 taxable turnover in any rolling 12-month period (with effect from 1 April 2024). A non-established person supplying goods or services in the UK is subject to compulsory VAT registration from the first supply irrespective of the £90,000 threshold if it is not established in the UK and distance selling or online marketplace rules apply. There is also a separate £270,000 registration threshold for persons making only distance sales into the UK from abroad, unless the goods are in UK excise duty suspension. Additional notification obligations apply for the One Stop Shop (OSS) and Import One Stop Shop (IOSS) schemes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods applies under Value Added Tax Act 1994 s.30 and Schedule 8 Group 6. Eligibility requires: (i) a supply of goods which are directly exported by the supplier (or an agent acting on the supplier's behalf) from the UK to a destination outside the UK; (ii) the goods are exported within three months from the time of supply (unless HMRC has extended this period); (iii) evidence of export must be obtained and preserved as required by HMRC VAT Notice 703, comprising transport documents, customs declarations, and commercial evidence; and (iv) for services, zero-rating may apply to international services (VATA 1994 Sch 8 Group 7) where the recipient belongs outside the UK, subject to exclusions. For offshore B2B services, the place of supply rule places the supply where the customer belongs; if outside the UK the supply is outside the scope of UK VAT, and input VAT recovery is generally available to the supplier subject to output supply, with substantiation required under Notice 741A.

The UK does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping regime for B2B e-invoicing. VAT-registered businesses must comply with Making Tax Digital (MTD) requiring digital record-keeping and submission of VAT returns via HMRC-recognised software using Application Programming Interfaces (APIs). No statutory pre-clearance of individual invoices is required; however, export declarations to the UK CDS must be electronically submitted before export to obtain the declaration's Movement Reference Number (MRN). Peppol-based e-invoicing is permitted but not mandated. Import VAT is accounted for through postponed accounting on periodic returns, with no automatic digital import VAT certificate.

HMRC has broad audit powers to scrutinise export evidence and may issue VAT assessments on missing or insufficient proof of export. For dishonest conduct or deliberate inaccuracies causing non-recovery or wrongful zero-rating, the penalties and consequences include: (i) tax-geared penalties of up to 100% of the VAT evaded under the Finance Act 2007 Schedule 24; (ii) increasing penalties in cases of deliberate concealment, up to 200%; (iii) possible criminal prosecution under VATA 1994 s.72, the Fraud Act 2006 (fraud by false representation, failure to disclose) or the common law conspiracy to defraud, carrying custodial sentences and unlimited fines; (iv) retrospective clawback of zero-rated VAT credits and denial of input tax deduction including via the doctrine of 'missing trader intra-community' (MTIC) fraud countermeasures; (v) an assessment period extended to up to 20 years in cases of de emeliberate concealment; and (vi) director disqualification and joint and several personal liability for company officers under the Companies Act 2006 and relevant HMRC protective provisions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Sterling is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts. There are no exchange controls on the repatriation of profits, dividends, capital, or trade proceeds. BVI corporate shareholders may receive payments from UK bank accounts to any jurisdiction, subject only to standard AML and sanctions screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline applies. Export receipts may be retained in foreign currency, converted freely, or held in any authorised UK bank account.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority) and HM Treasury; the UK repealed the Exchange Control Act 1947, leaving no separate foreign-exchange-control authority.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% UK main corporation tax rate for taxable profits over £250,000; 19% small profits rate for profits below £50,000; BVI BC local tax rate is 0% on offshore re-invoicing income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% statutory dividend withholding tax: UK law does not levy withholding tax on dividends paid by UK companies to non-residents, and BVI law imposes no withholding or dividend tax on distributions made by a BVI BC.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: UK does not impose a fixed monetary filing threshold for a mandatory Local File/Master File. The operative threshold is 'large enterprise' status: an entity is outside the SME definition if it has at least 250 employees or an annual turnover above €50 million or a balance-sheet total above €43 million. CbC Report filing trigger for a UK-parented MNE group is consolidated group revenue of at least €750 million (approximately £645 million). The BVI BC should maintain contemporaneous OECD-compliant TP documentation for any controlled re-invoicing with connected UK or offshore parties; HMRC may compel production by information notice.

Economic substance scope: BVI Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to the BVI BC if it carries on a relevant activity. General re-invoicing between connected parties normally constitutes 'distribution and service centre business', requiring BVI core income-generating activities, management and control, an adequate BVI physical office, proportionate full-time employees, and annual local expenditure. The UK applies the diverted profits tax (25%) and OECD Action 3 CFC legislation where offshore profit is diverted through a BVI entity lacking substance; HMRC can treat the BVI BC as tax resident in the UK if central management and control is exercised from Great Britain.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: HMRC deliberate inaccuracy penalties up to 100% of the additional tax due; confiscation and asset-forfeiture proceedings; criminal prosecution for cheating the public revenue and fraudulent tax evasion with unlimited fines and imprisonment. The Criminal Finances Act 2017 also imposes strict criminal liability on companies for failing to prevent the facilitation of tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors face personal prosecution, disqualification under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986 for up to 15 years, travel restraints, confiscation orders, and imprisonment where the BC is used as a façade; courts will pierce the corporate veil to allow civil restitution from personal assets.
BVI Economic Substance Failure / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: BVI International Tax Authority penalties on ESR failure of up to USD 200,000 for sustained non-compliance, possible deregistration and strike-off; UK HMRC may additionally assess the company as UK-resident and charge UK corporation tax on worldwide profits plus penalties and interest.

Director personal liability: BVI officers who knowingly file false economic substance returns may be fined and imprisoned for up to 5 years; in the UK, directors may be personally liable for penalties attributable to their actions; travel bans and worldwide asset-freezing orders can be imposed in confiscation proceedings.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Criminal money laundering under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 carries up to 14 years' imprisonment and unlimited fines; UK anti-money-laundering regulators may strike off the firm, and banking partners may freeze accounts and file Suspicious Activity Reports; HMRC may issue civil penalties for deficient AML controls.

Director personal liability: Directors are 'relevant persons' under the UK Money Laundering Regulations; knowing or reckless involvement in laundering exposes them to personal criminal liability, disqualification, travel bans, confiscation, and loss of the corporate shield if the BC is run as a personal vehicle.
Transfer Pricing / CFC / Diverted Profits Tax

Statutory sanction: HMRC will apply transfer pricing adjustments under TIOPA 2010 and may impose Diverted Profits Tax at 25% on the diverted profits plus interest and penalties. The UK CFC rules can attribute certain offshore profits back to UK parent companies; failure to comply with information notices carries fixed and daily penalties.

Director personal liability: Group directors who are knowingly involved in arranging artificial pricing can be personally assessed under the Criminal Finances Act 2017 on the theory of failure to prevent facilitation; UK courts may pierce the veil where the BVI BC has no independent economic existence, allowing restitution orders against directors' assets and imposing custodial sentences for fraudulent tax evasion.
