Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK may be zero-rated provided the goods are physically removed from the UK by or on behalf of the supplier within the statutory time limit and proper commercial or official evidence is retained. Offshore towage and salvage services supplied to overseas customers are zero-rated where the place of supply is outside the UK or the services directly relate to the international transport of goods or passengers, subject to the normal evidence requirements.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital invoicing clearance is currently imposed. Making Tax Digital (MTD) requires VAT-registered businesses to maintain digital records and submit VAT returns via functional compatible software; HMRC may issue e-invoicing mandates only for public-sector supply via PEPPOL standards in future but no live national mandate exists.

HMRC can conduct enhanced scrutiny of zero-rated claims; for fraudulent or erroneous export zero-rating, the VAT is clawed back with statutory interest, and penalties under FA 2007 Schedule 24 may reach up to 100% of the potential lost revenue (higher for deliberate and concealed errors). In serious cases, criminal prosecution for cheating the public revenue or VAT fraud may be pursued.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for GBP and all major trade currencies. There are no statutory exchange controls on capital injection, profit remittance, dividend distribution, intercompany loans, liquidation proceeds, or salvage award transfers in favour of a BVI Business Company holding a UK bank account. Profit remittance to the BVI parent or non-resident shareholder is freely permitted, subject to (i) HMRC withholding tax documentary checks on beneficial ownership, (ii) OFSI and OFAC sanctions screening of all parties to the wire, and (iii) CRS/FATCA self-certification. The practical hurdles are documentary, not conversion-based: a UK tier-1 bank will require the BVI BC's Register of Directors, Register of Members, registered-agent KYC pack, certificate of good standing, source-of-funds evidence for injected capital, and, in the maritime towage/salvage sector, evidence of vessel ownership or charter and P&I insurance. Since ISIC_5222 operations may involve a tug physically entering UK territorial waters, the bank may also request a UK corporation-tax analysis or HMRC clearance to ensure no permanent establishment exposure, but FX conversion itself is unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The UK imposes no statutory timeline for export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to deposit salvage or towage receipts with the Bank of England. Cross-border earnings may be retained in USD, EUR, or any convertible currency in UK foreign-currency accounts or offshore accounts indefinitely. The governing obligations are the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017 (MLR 2017), which require full originator and beneficiary information on all cross-border wires, and the payment-fraud prevention rules applicable to faster payments. For documentary letters of credit, proceeds must comply with UCP600 and are credited directly to the BVI BC's nominated account; no surrender to the central bank is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England, acting with HM Treasury and the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) for financial sanctions and exchange-control-adjacent policy; the United Kingdom has no standalone Foreign Exchange Administration, with the Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) supervising clearing banks and the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) regulating EMIs and payment institutions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: UK requires TP documentation for transactions exceeding £1 million in aggregate for goods, £1 million for services, and £1 million for intangibles. Large enterprises (turnover > £200 million) must prepare a Master File and Local File.

Economic substance scope: UK applies economic substance principles for tax residency and anti-avoidance. The Diverted Profits Tax (DPT) targets arrangements lacking economic substance. For BVI BCs, the BVI ESR applies, but for UK tax, the company must demonstrate real management and control in the UK to be tax resident.

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 100% of tax liability, criminal prosecution, imprisonment up to 7 years.
