Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC); official portal: GOV.UK – https://www.gov.uk (HMRC Online Services for VAT, Corporation Tax, and Customs).

E-government filing portal

A non-UK BVI Business Company must: (1) assess corporation tax registration duty under CTA 2010 s.5 and s.19 – required only if it is UK resident by central management and control or carries on a trade through a UK permanent establishment; if required, obtain a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) via form CT600 and register through HMRC's Corporation Tax Online Service; (2) register for UK VAT using VAT1 or the online VAT Registration Service if it makes taxable supplies in the UK – non-established businesses have nil registration threshold; (3) for digital services supplied through Apple App Store or Google Play to UK consumers, VAT may be accounted for by the store operator as deemed supplier, thereby eliminating the BVI supplier's VAT registration obligation; (4) maintain an HMRC agent link if represented by an accountancy firm.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under UK tax law. A UK-resident company may elect for exemption for foreign permanent establishment profits (CTA 2010 s.18A-FD). Hybrid mismatch adjustment elections may be made under TIOPA 2010 Part 6TA. A non-resident BVI BC cannot use the UK PE exemption; it may enter the VAT group registration election (VATA 1994 Sch 9A) if it has an establishment in the UK, and may exercise the option to tax land/buildings under Sch 10 if relevant.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 annual taxable turnover for businesses established in the UK; nil threshold for non-established businesses; digital services supplied by a non-UK supplier to UK consumers are subject to UK VAT with no registration threshold unless an electronic interface is the deemed supplier under the marketplace rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

UK VAT zero-rating for exports is available only for physical goods despatched to a destination outside the UK/Isle of Man within statutory time limits, with evidence retained. For services, including mobile utility software, zero-rating is not the correct mechanism; B2B supplies to non-UK business customers are outside the scope of UK VAT under the place-of-supply rules, while B2C supplies to UK consumers attract 20% UK VAT. If the BVI supplier sells through Apple/Google to UK consumers, the platform is deemed supplier and the supplier is not entitled to zero-rate such supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate

No mandatory continuous transaction control or real-time fiscal clearance. HMRC mandates Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT-recorded businesses above the £90,000 threshold, requiring digital record-keeping and VAT return submission via HMRC-recognised software using MTD APIs. E-invoicing in CEN EN 16931 is required only for B2B sales to UK public-sector bodies through PEPPOL; no digital stamping or pre-clearance obligation.

False or unsupported export zero-rating is a civil fraud subject to penalties up to 100% of the VAT evaded under FA 2007 Sch 24, plus interest; criminal prosecution may also be pursued under VATA 1994 s.72. HMRC may claw back repaid input VAT retrospectively if evidence is deficient and may extend assessment periods to six years for deliberate non-disclosure. Good-quality transport, customs, and FX remittance evidence must be kept for at least six years to survive audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The GBP is fully convertible under both capital and current account. No exchange controls exist in the UK. Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject to standard corporate formalities and anti-money laundering compliance. No surrender or conversion requirements apply to export proceeds. Non-convertible currencies or restrictions are absent, except sanctions and counter-terrorist financing obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in the United Kingdom. Export proceeds are freely retained and payable in any currency; only record-keeping under HMRC customs and trade regulations is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (in coordination with HM Treasury and HM Revenue & Customs for regulatory reporting)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility is High for reputable overseas EMIs (e.g., Revolut Ltd, Wise Europe) authorised by the FCA in the UK or licensed in their home jurisdiction and passporting into the UK under the Payment Services Regulations. Constraints: EMI accounts are not equivalent to full commercial bank accounts; treasury limits and certain clearing services (e.g., traditional letters of credit) may be unavailable. Regulatory constraints also include allocation of client funds safeguarding and potential restrictions on non-UK corporate clients in higher-risk sectors or jurisdictions.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

BVI incorporation with single shareholder and nominee officers

Root cause: BVI incorporation with single shareholder and nominee officers

Defense evidence: Certificate of incorporation, register of directors, register of members, beneficial ownership filing (BO form submitted to BVI Registry), certified passports of all individuals, source of funds declaration, detailed business plan evidencing UK app-store operations and treasury flows, and an AML risk self-assessment aligned to UK JMLSG guidance.
No physical substance in the UK / registered office at an agent address

Root cause: No physical substance in the UK / registered office at an agent address

Defense evidence: UK office lease or controlled virtual office service agreement, physical contact or employee located in the UK, utility bill or business rates statement, company website and app-store listing evidencing active commercial activity, UK VAT and tax references (if any), and audited financial statements or an independent accountant's reference confirming that economic activity occurs.
Unusual transaction profile: early-stage equity injection from offshore account

Root cause: Unusual transaction profile: early-stage equity injection from offshore account

Defense evidence: Shareholder resolution approving capital injection, executed subscription agreement or loan agreement (if loans are used), a source-of-wealth questionnaire for each beneficial owner, supporting bank statements from the contributing account, a tax opinion or legal memo confirming there is no artificial bypass of UK tax obligations, and a completed remittance purpose form designating 'Capital Subscription' or 'Shareholder Subordinated Loan'.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be routed via SWIFT MT103/202 using purpose codes 'Capital Subscription' (BOP code 1A) or 'Intercompany Loan' (BOP code 5A), accompanied by the executed subscription agreement, board resolution, and a certificate of good standing from the BVI Registry. The receiving UK bank applies standard sanctions screening and verifies the beneficial ownership alignment with records filed at the UK Companies House (if any). Any remittance exceeding GBP 10,000 triggers a Reportable Payment to HMRC under the Money Laundering Regulations; additional source-of-funds documentation will be required. Funds are typically cleared via the Faster Payments Service (domestic) or CHAPS for same-day value.Trade settlements for operational expenses will primarily utilise SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) for international payments. For domestic GBP payments, Faster Payments (limit ≤ £1 million) or CHAPS (same-day, no limit) are used. For hardware or services procurement where the supplier requires a bank guarantee, a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) is issued via a UK banking partner (e.g., HSBC Bank plc). All disbursements must be supported by supplier invoices, trade contracts, VAT/goods description, and ESPQ or EORI registration where applicable.
