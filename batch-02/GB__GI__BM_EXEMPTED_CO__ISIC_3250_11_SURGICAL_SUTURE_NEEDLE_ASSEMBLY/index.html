Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (with Territorial_Scope overlay in Gibraltar). The Bermuda Exempted Company is not subject to Bermuda corporation income tax, capital gains tax, or dividend/interest withholding tax, and enjoys statutory tax exemption under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966. In Gibraltar, the manufacturing permanent establishment (surgical suture needle assembly) derives only Gibraltar-source trading profits chargeable to Gibraltar corporation tax at 12.5% under the Income Tax Act 2010; no UK corporation tax applies because the entity is not UK tax resident and has no UK permanent establishment. The Tax_Exempt_Zone classification holds only while the entity remains outside the scope of Bermuda's Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (i.e., group revenue below EUR 750m) and subject to compliance with economic substance and anti-abuse rules.

Available election forms

Neither Bermuda nor Gibraltar operates a US-style check-the-box election; both jurisdictions treat the Exempted Company as an opaque, non-transparent legal person automatically. Available elections and declarations: (a) Gibraltar - formal application/declaration for tax residence where central management and control is in Gibraltar, which triggers territorial charge on all Gibraltar-source profits; (b) Gibraltar - group loss relief and consortium relief elections among Gibraltar-resident group companies; (c) Bermuda - election for the exempted company to file under the Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 and apply the Transitional QDMTT Safe Harbour where within Pillar Two scope; (d) Bermuda - annual BES economic substance classification election (relevant activity vs. non-resident foreign tax resident status). Hybrid mismatch: since the entity is opaque in Bermuda, Gibraltar, and the UK, the UK hybrid mismatch rules (TIOPA 2010 Part 6A) and the Gibraltar hybrid rules are not triggered; no special hybrid election is required, though documentation should evidence non-transparent status in all jurisdictions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales-and-Use-Tax in Gibraltar or Bermuda. Gibraltar is outside the UK VAT area and the EU VAT regime; indirect taxation is limited to the Gibraltar Import Duty and Purchase Tax (applicable to selected goods and services such as alcohol, tobacco, fuel, hotel accommodation, and luxury items). Bermuda imposes customs (import) duty and a payroll tax but no general sales tax or VAT. The UK VAT system (20% standard rate) does not extend to Gibraltar.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None - because no VAT/GST system exists, no turnover threshold triggers indirect tax registration. All entities carrying on business in Gibraltar are required to register with the Income Tax Office regardless of turnover; any importer/exporter must register with the Gibraltar Customs & Excise Division without a de minimis threshold. For territorial context only, the UK VAT registration threshold of GBP 90,000 (taxable turnover) would apply only if the entity made taxable supplies in the UK, which is not the case for the Gibraltar-based assembly operation; inbound raw material movements into the UK from Gibraltar are treated as imports, not supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because Gibraltar does not levy VAT or GST on any supply, the statutory concept of export zero-rating is structurally inapplicable: exports of sterile absorbable polyglycolic acid braided sutures (HS 3006.10) and suture needles (HS 9018.32) from Gibraltar are outside any indirect tax charge, and no VAT refund mechanism exists. Import duty relief is available on raw materials imported into Gibraltar for inward processing and subsequent re-export, subject to a prior end-use authorisation from the Gibraltar Customs & Excise Division. On the Bermuda side, no export duty, excise, or sales tax is imposed on the assembly output; the exempted status under the Exempted Undertakings Tax Protection Act 1966 prohibits any future Bermuda tax on the export profits. To substantiate tax neutrality, the taxpayer must evidence cross-border movement of goods out of Gibraltar, the non-resident status of the foreign buyer, and the absence of domestic consumption of the exported surgical consumables.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar CED - electronic single administrative declaration filed with HM Customs & Excise Division and signed by the exporter or licensed customs broker)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (negotiable transport document evidencing physical shipment, destination, and consignee)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (including HS 3006.10 / 9018.32 classification, Incoterms, and unit values)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or TT Wire credit advisement evidencing the receipt of export sales proceeds from the foreign buyer (required under the statutory 180-day repatriation benchmark)
  • Certificate of Origin (Gibraltar Chamber of Commerce or approved issuer) where preferential tariff treatment is claimed in the destination jurisdiction
  • End-use / Inward Processing Authorisation (Gibraltar Customs) evidencing that duty-free imported sterile inputs were incorporated into the exported finished sutures

Gibraltar imposes no mandatory electronic invoicing, no real-time fiscal clearance, no API-based clearance, and no digital stamping for B2B or cross-border transactions. Customs procedures are handled electronically through the Gibraltar Customs Declaration System (single administrative declaration); all import and export declarations must be lodged in the national electronic system by the licensed customs broker or the registered operator. Bermuda similarly does not mandate CTC e-invoicing; import declarations are filed via the Bermuda CATS portal. The UK Making Tax Digital regime for VAT does not apply because the entity has no UK VAT registration.

Gibraltar - Income Tax Act 2010: any claim to territorial exclusion or relief for export profits is subject to rebuttal by the Commissioner of Income Tax; negligent or fraudulent misstatements attract penalties up to double the tax undercharged plus statutory interest (8% per annum), and criminal liability on conviction with fines and/or imprisonment for up to two years. Gibraltar Customs & Excise offences: misdeclaration of export value, destination, or diversion of duty-free imported inputs into domestic consumption triggers seizure and forfeiture of goods, a per-declaration statutory fine (up to GBP 10,000 or three times the evaded duty, whichever is greater), and retrospective clawback of all import duty relieved; repeat breaches result in revocation of the customs broker or authorised economic operator licence and disqualification of directors. Bermuda - Corporate Income Tax Act 2023 (if in scope): false or misleading export records attract administrative penalties of up to BMD 50,000, plus a deficiency assessment of up to 100% of the tax underpaid and interest; fraudulent conduct is criminalised. UK sovereign overlay: HMRC may apply the Diverted Profits Tax (25%) or transfer pricing recharacterisation where profits are artificially shifted from the UK market to the Gibraltar/Bermuda structure, and civil penalties may be charged under Schedule 24 to the Finance Act 2007 based on the amount of any resulting UK tax understatement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar Pound is fully and freely convertible at parity within Gibraltar; both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted, and there are no exchange-control laws, no central-bank approval gates for inward or outward remittances, and no profit-remittance restrictions. A Bermuda Exempted Company holding a Gibraltar treasury account may therefore remit dividends, management fees, intercompany loan repayments, and third-party trade settlements without currency surrender or licensing. Practical convertibility frictions are limited to (i) the Gibraltar Pound being legal tender only within Gibraltar, so the treasury executes transfers in GBP, EUR, or USD; (ii) bank-level CRS/FATCA self-certification requirements; and (iii) the obligation to substantiate economic substance and beneficial ownership on demand.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline applies in Gibraltar. All export receipts from the assembly and distribution of surgical suture needles and absorbable polyglycolic-acid braided sutures may be fully retained in the original invoicing currency (GIP, GBP, EUR, USD) in any licensed commercial bank account. To credit such proceeds, the receiving bank requires standard supporting trade documents: commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, export customs declaration, and, where an LC was used, the SWIFT MT700/MT752 record. Funds are cleared as available value within T+0/T+1 after receipt, subject only to sanctions screening and AML verification.

Central bank supervision: The Government of Gibraltar acts as the Gibraltar Pound issuing authority under the Currency Notes Act, operating a currency-board-style peg at strict parity with sterling (1 GIP : 1 GBP) and supported by HM Treasury and the Bank of England relationship framework. Gibraltar has no independent central bank; prudential supervision of banks and financial services is exercised by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC). The sterling convertibility anchor is maintained for monetary stability, while payment and settlement infrastructure effectively clears in GBP, EUR, and USD according to commercial banking practice.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore and Bermuda/UK-licensed digital payment institutions cannot passport into Gibraltar following the end of EU passporting; a non-GFSC-licensed EMI may neither hold client funds nor provide payment services to a Gibraltar-resident treasury operation. Direct licensing under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Regulations 2019 requires minimum initial capital of approximately GBP 350,000, a local AML/CFT compliance function, and a GFSC-approved board, rendering it commercially uneconomic for a single production entity. Feasibility is therefore LOW-MEDIUM: the recommended model is to maintain primary clearing at a licensed commercial bank and, only if small customer refund or distributor-commission flows arise, to appoint a fully GFSC-licensed Gibraltar EMI as a distribution agent with daily balance sweeping back to the bank account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar's transfer pricing rules (Income Tax Act as amended by the OECD BEPS alignment) require contemporaneous documentation and a Master File/Local File where the group's consolidated turnover exceeds GBP 600 million (approx. GIP 600 million) and intra-group transactions exceed GBP 5 million per annum, or where the Gibraltar entity has related-party transactions above GBP 1 million with a low-tax jurisdiction; locally, the threshold triggering mandatory detailed transfer pricing documentation is typically set at GIP 600 million for the local file, but even below this, a lesser TP form/declaration is required for all cross-border related-party transactions. For a mid-market surgical suture assembly operation, prepare a full Local File if intercompany sales exceed GBP 5 million and the parent jurisdiction (Bermuda) applies no corporate income tax.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's Economic Substance Regulations require any entity tax-resident in Gibraltar that carries on relevant activity—including manufacturing, distribution, financing, or holding company activities—to maintain substance proportionate to its core income-generating activities: physical presence in Gibraltar (premises), qualified full-time employees, local board meetings, and adequate expenditure. For the ISIC 3250 surgical suture needle assembly activity, if the entity is not tax-resident in Gibraltar and the manufacturing is physically performed outside Gibraltar (e.g., Bermuda or another jurisdiction), it should rely on the non-resident exemption and prove tax residence in Bermuda. A Bermuda exempted company must separately comply with Bermuda's Economic Substance Act 2018, which requires for relevant activity, if it is not tax-resident in another jurisdiction, adequate substance in Bermuda. If the entity can demonstrate tax residence in a qualifying jurisdiction outside Bermuda, it may avoid Bermuda substance requirements; otherwise, avoid any Gibraltar substance that could inadvertently create a Gibraltar permanent establishment or shift tax residence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Exempt-Company Misuse

Statutory sanction: Assessment to tax on the entire trading profit at 12.5%; interest at statutory rates; penalties up to 100% of the tax due (civil penalty regime under Gibraltar Income Tax Act s. 92); criminal fine on conviction; forfeiture of the exempt status; strike-off and disqualification orders.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors and shadow directors may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes; liability for fraudulent trading under Gibraltar Companies Act; travel bans, director disqualification for up to 15 years, and imprisonment for up to 7 years for tax fraud or false statements.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Primary TP adjustment increasing Gibraltar taxable profits at 12.5%; secondary adjustments (recharacterisation of the adjustment as a dividend or deemed loan); penalty of up to 100% of the tax understated; late payment interest; possible daily penalties for failure to produce documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved false TP documentation face personal liability for consequent penalties; UK HMRC can pursue parent company directors under the UK Criminal Finances Act 2017 for failure to prevent facilitation of tax evasion; civil restitution orders and freezing injunctions in the Gibraltar Supreme Court; imprisonment for up to 7 years on conviction for cheating the public revenue.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / Regulatory Strike-off

Statutory sanction: In Bermuda: BD$250,000 initial fine, BD$500,000 for repeat offences (by the Bermuda Registrar of Companies). In Gibraltar: failure to file the economic substance return results in penalties up to GBP 5,000 and daily default fines; failure of the substance test can lead to an information notice, and ultimately the company's name may be struck off the register of companies; directors banned.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be named and publicly identified; directors of a struck-off company may be personally liable for debts if the company traded while insolvent; non-resident principals may be investigated for the effective commission of conspiracy to avoid substance obligations; travel sanctions and possible extradition in egregious money-laundering-linked cases.
AML / Financing of Sanctions Evasion / False Banking Representation

Statutory sanction: Fines under Gibraltar AML regime (Proceeds of Crime Act 2015) and the Financial Intelligence Unit: up to GIP 50,000 for controllers and enhanced fines for regulated institutions; asset forfeiture under civil recovery; criminal confiscation; withdrawal of bank facilities; inclusion on national and EU/UK financial sanctions list if connected with sanctions evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face criminal prosecution for money laundering (maximum 14 years imprisonment) and failure to submit suspicious activity reports (SARs) (up to 5 years imprisonment); travel bans; freezing orders; corporate veil piercing if the company is a vehicle for fraud; personal confiscation of benefits obtained from the offence; extradition to the UK or EU under mutual legal assistance treaties.
Failure to Maintain Statutory Records and Audit Obligations

Statutory sanction: In Bermuda: fine of BD$10,000 plus daily fines for failure to file financial statements; for the Gibraltar branch or if registered in Gibraltar, fines under Gibraltar Companies Act for failure to keep accounting records; a court may order return of documents or criminal sanctions for destruction of documents; auditor reports subject to qualified opinion leading to regulatory scrutiny.

Director personal liability: Officers responsible are liable to fines and imprisonment up to 2 years for failure to preserve records; where the lack of records prevents the quantification of tax, assessments will be made against the company, and directors can be held personally liable for the unassessed tax if they caused the default; disqualification from acting as a director in the UK and Gibraltar.
