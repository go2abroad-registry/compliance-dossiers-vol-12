Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC), 100 Parliament Street, London SW1A 2BQ; primary customer portal is the Government Gateway (www.gov.uk/government/organisations/hm-revenue-customs) complemented by the HMRC Developer Hub API platform (software-backed submissions for VAT via Making Tax Digital, Corporation Tax via CT Online Filing, and the UK One Stop Shop (OSS) scheme for digital services).

E-government filing portal

The BVI Business Company (VG_BC) is a non-UK resident body corporate; UK tax registration arises only when a UK filing obligation or statutory supply nexus exists. Step 1: Assess UK permanent establishment (PE) exposure under CTA 2009 s.19 / OECD PE article in the absence of a UK-BVI double tax treaty (no DTT; only a Tax Information Exchange Agreement and the Multilateral Convention for tax administrative assistance apply). Step 2: If trading through a UK PE, register for Corporation Tax within 3 months of commencing UK trade using the CT41G non-resident company registration process; HMRC issues a 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) and an Agent code. Step 3: If the BC makes taxable UK supplies (most relevant: B2C digital subscriptions sold directly to UK consumers), register for VAT in the 'VATEC' online flow; a non-established taxable person has NO turnover registration threshold. For digital e-services, registration is made under the UK OSS non-Union scheme (registered via HMRC's OSS portal, using the entity's BVI Company Number for identity). Step 4: If the BC also imports physical goods into the UK, apply for a UK EORI number through the Customs Declaration Service (CDS). Step 5: Where Apple/Google act as merchant of record for App Store / Play Billing sales, the BC generally holds no UK VAT registration, as the platform collects and remits UK VAT; the BC's treasury proceeds settled to BVI do not trigger UK registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

The UK does not operate a general check-the-box or pass-through election; an incorporated BVI BC is per se an opaque 'body corporate' under CTA 2010 s.1121, taxable on UK-source profits and PE-attributable income. Available mechanisms: (i) voluntary Corporation Tax registration where no PE exists (rare; creates unwelcome UK nexus and is discouraged); (ii) UK OSS non-Union scheme election for B2C digital/e-services — elective once UK VAT registration is triggered and permits quarterly consolidated digital-services returns via HMRC’s OSS portal; (iii) option to tax (election to waive VAT exemption) on any UK land interest, which is inapplicable to the webnovel platform scope; (iv) hybrid mismatch compliance under TIOPA 2010 Pt. 6A s.259A–259N — the BVI BC is treated as an opaque corporate, so no hybrid mismatch arises, and no elective counteraction is needed (UK-BVI mismatch neutralisation provisions nonetheless remain applicable if an intermediary LLC were introduced); (v) appointment of a UK fiscal/agent representative under VATA 1994 s.48 may be required by HMRC as a condition of non-established VAT/OSS registration. No UK-BVI DTT relief election is possible; reliance is exclusively on domestic UK law and the Multilateral Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 (VAT Notice 700/1, in force from 1 April 2024) for UK-established businesses on historic rolling-12-month taxable turnover; £0 / nil threshold for non-established taxable persons — registration is compulsory from the first taxable supply of digital services to a UK consumer. Supplies of B2B digital services and physical e-commerce distance sales imported into the UK similarly trigger registration without a de minimis threshold for non-UK suppliers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating applies only to physical goods exported from the UK outside the EC within statutory time limits, supported by prescribed evidence per VAT Notice 703 (shipment, transport, and payment records; missing evidence within 90 days recharacterises the supply as standard-rated). Services: UK VAT law does not zero-rate international services; instead the place-of-supply rules (VAT Notice 741A) take B2B supplies and B2C digital services supplied to non-UK customers 'outside the scope' of UK VAT. For the WebNovel Serial Platform: (a) subscriptions sold to UK consumers via Apple IAP / Google Play Billing — the app store is typically merchant of record and levies 20% UK VAT; the BC's B2B supply of content sub-licence to the app store is outside the scope of UK VAT, provided the BC holds the platform's valid VAT/tax identifier; (b) direct B2C subscriptions supplied by the BC to UK consumers without app-store intermediation fall within UK 20% VAT, with no zero-rating; (c) subscriptions supplied by the BC to non-UK consumers are outside the scope of UK VAT and no refund/zero-rating claim is required. The BC cannot claim zero-rating for digital export 'credits'; substantiation rests on platform merchant reports, IP licence agreements, and settlement statements.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or combined transport document evidencing physical departure from the UK) — required only for physical goods exports
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (describing goods, EORI, HS classification, value)

  • For offshore digital services: executed content licence and subscription contract, the WebNovel platform end-user terms, Apple/Google merchant-of-record statements and settlement remittance advices, plus subscriber geolocation reports (no customs declaration is generated for intangible digital supplies)

No real-time pre-clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), digital stamping, or mandatory fiscal e-invoicing regime operates in the UK. The binding mandate is Making Tax Digital for VAT (MTD): every VAT-registered business, including non-established digital suppliers, must maintain digital records and submit VAT returns by API-based software (HMRC transforming e-invoicing/flat files are not accepted; software communicates through HMRC's MTD VAT API endpoints using MTD-compatible schemas). E-invoicing for B2G contracts follows PEPPOL voluntarily and is not part of the digital webnovel B2C flow. There is no pre-clearance stamping of individual invoices; HMRC performs system-side assurance risk scoring after submission.

HMRC scrutiny of export and OSS claims is risk-based. False or unsupported export zero-rating/outside-scope treatment results in retrospective clawback assessments: standard 4-year lookback (20 years where deliberate error or fraudulent conduct). Penalties under FA 2007 Sch.24 range from 15% (careless) to 30-100% (deliberate and concealed); VAT evasion penalties under VATA 1994 s.60-70 apply with up to 100% of VAT plus interest. Criminal prosecution under VATA 1994 s.72 for fraudulent export/digital-supply misdeclaration attracts imprisonment up to 7 years and unlimited fines. OSS-specific errors are pulled back by HMRC with re-attribution to member states and penalty interest accrual; failing to retain export evidence within 90 days (goods) or platform merchant records (services) triggers automatic downside reassessment. The 180-day inward remittance window is not a UK statute but is the evidentiary benchmark adopted by HMRC’s international services audit teams; failure to evidence settlement within this window raises diverted-profits-tax (DPT) and GAAR suspicion, though no capital controls or statutory repatriation deadline exist in the UK.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom operates a permanently liberalised capital and current account regime. Sterling is fully convertible and no prior approval is required for dividend distributions, loan repayments, royalty payments, or reinvestment of proceeds. However, a BVI Business Company that carries on a UK trade through a permanent establishment is within the UK corporation tax net, and profit extraction via interest, royalties, or certain annual payments may attract withholding tax in the absence of treaty or domestic exemptions. A UK treasury bank will require source-of-funds evidence and tax/transfer pricing documentation before executing any FX conversion or cross-border distribution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements in the UK, nor mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts. The BVI BC may retain and deploy its receipts in any freely tradable currency. It remains obliged to maintain invoicing, settlement, and treasury records for at least six years for HMRC, and to respond to Bank of England/HMRC statistical surveys if the activity is captured; no surrender or repatriation timeline is imposed.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (acting through the Prudential Regulation Authority) and HM Treasury; the United Kingdom does not operate a centralised exchange-control authority, and the historical Exchange Control Act restrictions were substantially repealed by 1979.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High as a supplementary settlement and multi-currency rail, but Low as the sole treasury banking relationship. FCA-authorised e-money institutions and payment institutions (e.g., Wise Platform, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Revolut Business) are legally available to BVI companies under the E-Money Regulations 2011 and Payment Services Regulations 2017. They can process app-store payouts, issue virtual IBANs, and perform FX at transparent spreads. However, they are not credit institutions and cannot offer deposit protection, overdraft, or escrow services. Some EMI risk teams reject BVI-registered companies or require a UK-resident director or UBO; therefore the BVI BC should maintain at least one tier-1 bank account as liquidity anchor and use EMIs only for segregated settlement batches.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound investment from shareholders or lenders must be routed through SWIFT MT103 (or CHAPS for sterling) naming the BVI BC exactly as account holder, with a single purpose code and no splitting to avoid structuring suspicion. The bank will expect the remittance to carry the ISO 20022 business-purpose code relevant to the flow: 'EQUI' for equity subscription and an internal shareholder-loan code for loan disbursement; the narrative should cite the corporate registration number, board resolution date, and counterparty reference. Supporting documents include the executed subscription agreement or term sheet for the shareholder loan, written board resolution approving the capital injection, updated register of members, and source-of-wealth statement from the originating bank. The receiving bank will complete UK balance-of-payments reporting as required and may place funds in a capital escrow account until the full KYC pack is approved.For trade payments, the BVI BC should use SWIFT MT103 for all international vendor settlements and content-licensing fees; CHAPS is the appropriate rail for high-value sterling payments inside the UK; Faster Payments for sub-threshold domestic disbursements; BACS for fixed-amount payroll and recurring royalties; SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant for EU beneficiary accounts; and a commercial LC only where the platform acquires physical printed derivatives or merchandise, to be issued by the tier-1 bank and advised through the beneficiary's bank. Apple and Google payout files arrive as direct credits from the app store or its payment intermediary; these should be reconciled monthly against subscription dashboards and stored with the merchant-of-record agreement to satisfy both treasury and UK tax and AML evidence requirements.Outright deliverable FX forwards between GBP and USD or EUR to hedge app-store revenue against operating expenses.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies not legally deliverable in the UK time zone, using an FCA-authorised ISDA counterparty; this is permissible only where the BVI BC has an underlying commercial exposure, not on a speculative basis.Cross-currency swaps to align a USD shareholder loan into GBP floating liabilities and hedge long-term contract economics.FX vanilla options and zero-cost collars to protect committed content acquisition and marketing budgets while preserving upside.FX swaps to manage short-term treasury gaps between USD-denominated app collections and GBP or EUR supplier disbursements; all derivative transactions are subject to EMIR clearing, margining, and transaction reporting where they exceed clearing thresholds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: UK requires transfer pricing documentation for transactions exceeding £1 million (goods) or £500,000 (services) with related parties. Master File required for groups with annual turnover > £200 million.

Economic substance scope: BVI Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to BVI companies; must maintain economic presence in BVI for relevant activities. For UK tax purposes, a UK PE may trigger corporation tax obligations and require substance in the UK.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalties up to 100% of the tax underpaid, criminal prosecution, and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for unpaid tax if they knowingly participate in evasion; civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: HMRC can recharacterise transactions, impose penalties up to 100% of tax, and potentially treat the company as UK tax resident.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for tax debts if the corporate veil is pierced; asset seizure and director disqualification.
AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines up to £500,000, revocation of operating licenses, and criminal prosecution for money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned up to 14 years for AML breaches; travel bans and confiscation orders.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of taxable profits, penalties up to 100% of additional tax, and interest on underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for penalties if they knowingly approve non-compliant TP policies; civil penalties and disqualification.
