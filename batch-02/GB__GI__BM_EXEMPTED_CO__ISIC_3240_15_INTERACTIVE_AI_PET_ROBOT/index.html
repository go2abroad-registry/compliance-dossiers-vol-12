Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar: HM Government of Gibraltar - Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax) and Gibraltar Customs & Excise Department, portal www.taxation.gi; UK (for cross-border distance sales to UK consumers): HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC), online portal gov.uk/vat and gov.uk register-for-vat.

E-government filing portal

The Bermuda Exempted Company (non-domiciled in Gibraltar) first obtains its Bermuda Certificate of Exemption under the Companies Act 1981. To establish a branch or taxable presence in Gibraltar, it files a written application with the Income Tax Office together with certified constitutional documents, certificate of good standing, economics-substance evidence, and a Gibraltar-registered-address undertaking; the Commissioner then issues a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN). No Bermuda TIN is issued because exempted companies are outside Bermuda income tax unless they employ local staff (payroll tax registration would then apply). For sales of goods situated in or dispatched to the UK, the company must separately register for UK VAT through HMRC's online service; a non-established seller receives a GB VAT number and must file UK VAT returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Bermuda: statutory election for an exempted undertaking certificate under s.114 of the Companies Act 1981 (exemption from all Bermuda taxes until 2035), with optional payroll-tax registration if local employees are engaged. Gibraltar: no check-the-box election exists; a foreign branch is taxed on its Gibraltar-sourced trading profits under the Income Tax Act 2010 at the standard company rate, and a qualifying-company election may apply in limited cases. UK: no check-the-box mechanism; a non-established seller may elect to use the Import One-Stop-Shop (IOSS) for low-value imported consignments not exceeding GBP 135 and may voluntarily register for UK VAT under the VAT Regulations 1995.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar: no general VAT or GST; import and excise duties under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance apply to prescribed goods only. UK: 20% VAT (VATA 1994) applies to B2C online distance sales of the interactive AI pet robot to UK consumers, whereas sales to Gibraltar consumers and to consumers outside the UK VAT area are outside UK VAT scope.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: no VAT registration threshold exists (only excise/import duty permits). UK: a non-established overseas seller must register for UK VAT from the first supply of goods situated in the UK at the time of sale (no threshold); the GBP 85,000 annual threshold applies only to UK-established businesses; for goods imported from outside Great Britain into the UK, the low-value consignment threshold for IOSS purposes is GBP 135.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

UK VAT at 0% is available under VATA 1994 Schedule 8 Group 7 only if the goods are physically exported from the UK (including removal to Gibraltar, which lies outside the UK VAT area) to a destination outside the UK within 3 months of supply, and the supplier holds the prescribed evidential documents. Offshore services ancillary to the product (AI cloud updates, speech-synthesizer firmware subscriptions) supplied to non-UK business customers are outside the scope of UK VAT via B2B reverse charge, and supplied to non-UK consumers are outside UK VAT where effective use and enjoyment occurs outside the UK. Gibraltar imposes no local VAT on these exports.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Great Britain
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank/Stripe settlement statements proving receipt of foreign-currency proceeds)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List for customs valuation and tariff classification

Gibraltar: no mandatory e-invoicing and no real-time fiscal clearance. UK: HMRC's Making Tax Digital (MTD) requires VAT-registered businesses to keep digital VAT records and file VAT returns via HMRC-recognised API software; HMRC does not operate real-time invoice clearance or digital stamping, and no continuous-transaction-control e-invoicing mandate applies to this low-risk consumer merchandise line. The company must nevertheless preserve full transactional evidence from Stripe and credit-card processors to support UK VAT accounting and export-zero-rating claims.

UK HMRC may assess all under-declared VAT, charge interest, and impose penalties of up to 100% of the VAT for dishonest or reckless conduct (VATA 1994 ss.60-72), with criminal prosecution for fraudulent zero-rating documentation and retrospective clawback of invalid export VAT claims; online marketplaces may also face joint and several liability for overseas sellers. Gibraltar Customs and Excise may recover up to three times the duty evaded, seize exported goods, and revoke duty permits for false export declarations. Bermuda imposes no income-tax clawback given the tax-exempt status, but the Registrar may impose civil penalties for false declarations in filings affecting payroll or economic-substance obligations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Sterling (GBP) is fully convertible on both current and capital account. Gibraltar and the United Kingdom impose no exchange controls on capital or current transactions. Profit remittance from Gibraltar is permitted without tax withholding for a Bermuda Exempted Company, provided the entity does not acquire Gibraltar tax residence under the Income Tax Act 2010. The only practical hurdles are documentary: banks require source of funds evidence, transfer purpose declarations, up-to-date KYC, and commercial reasonableness checks. There are no convertibility restrictions on invoicing in GBP, USD, EUR, CNH or other convertible currencies, and no prior exchange licence is required for capital injection, shareholder loans, or trade settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement exists in Gibraltar or the United Kingdom. There is no mandatory conversion ratio and no prescribed timeline for converting export receipts into local currency. Exporters may retain receipts in any permitted currency, sell them through licensed banks at commercial rates, or hold them in foreign currency accounts. For banking classification purposes, export receipts from the sale of interactive AI pet robots should be documented with the underlying commercial invoice and logistics evidence to be categorised as trade in goods proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (UK central bank) and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the joint prudential and supervisory authorities for Sterling liquidity and banking conduct in Gibraltar

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for settlement float and payout receipt; Low as the sole treasury rail for high-volume trade disbursements. A Gibraltar-registered or UK-FCA-authorised Electronic Money Institution may be used, but it does not replace the protections of a tier-1 licensed commercial bank. After Brexit, EEA e-money passporting does not automatically cover Gibraltar; therefore the EMI must hold a GFSC e-money licence or a UK FCA EMI authorisation with a lawful servicing route to Gibraltar. Offshore digital EMIs based in non-UK/EEA jurisdictions are not viable because they cannot access the UK Faster Payments/CHAPS clearing networks and will be rejected by GFSC-regulated introducing agents. Safeguarding rules require the EMI to ring-fence client funds. Recommended treasury architecture: Stripe payouts routed into the EMI or bank account, with any surplus swept daily to the tier-1 operational account at Trusted Novus Bank.
