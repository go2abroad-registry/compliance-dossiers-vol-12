Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement; authorised capital may be denominated in any currency; shares may be issued with or without par value; no contribution needs to be made before incorporation. If shares are issued for non-cash consideration, the directors must file a declaration under section 43 of the Act before allotment.

Funding & unlock path: The company must obtain a Fire Risk Assessment and, where more than five employees are present, a fire safety certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Authority (or BVI Fire Service). A structural engineer's certificate confirming that the server room and safe-load floor capacity are adequate must be secured if the office houses heavy electrical cabinets. An occupational assessment under the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 (or BVI Labour Code, 2010) must confirm the workplace layout, evacuation routes and first aid provision.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement; authorised capital may be denominated in any currency; shares may be issued with or without par value; no contribution needs to be made before incorporation. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC), United Kingdom; online portal: GOV.UK HMRC Online Services via https://www.gov.uk (Corporation Tax Online, VAT Registration Online, Making Tax Digital for VAT, Customs Declaration Service/CDS).

E-government filing portal

A BVI Business Company is a non-UK incorporated opaque entity. UK direct tax exposure is limited to UK-source income and profits of a UK permanent establishment (UK PE) under Corporation Tax Act (CTA) 2010 Part 2. The BVI BC must first determine whether it has a UK PE (fixed place of business or dependent agent in the UK) or chargeable UK-source income. If a UK PE exists, the non-UK company must register for Corporation Tax with HMRC through the Corporation Tax Online Registration Service, providing the BVI company name, BVI incorporation/registration number, registered office or UK agent, UK PE address, expected accounting period, source and amount of UK chargeable profits, and the name of the officer responsible for the company. HMRC will issue a Corporation Tax UTR (Unique Taxpayer Reference). Separately, if the BVI BC makes any taxable supply in the UK, it must register for VAT before making the first supply because a non-established taxable person is subject to a nil registration threshold; use the HMRC VAT Registration Online Service or Form VAT1 to receive a UK VAT registration number. If exporting goods or making customs declarations, the BVI BC must obtain UK-issued EORI (Economic Operator Registration and Identification) number beginning with GB. Where the BVI BC has a registrable UK establishment/branch under Companies Act 2006 Part 34, it must also register with Companies House. If there is no UK PE, no UK taxable supply, and no UK customs activity, there is no automatic UK TIN/UTR obligation; however, evidence of non-residence and no UK nexus must be preserved for treaty and territorial-scope purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (opaque foreign BVI company; UK Corporation Tax only on UK PE/UK-source trading profits; not pass-through and not a UK tax-exempt zone)

Available election forms

The UK does not provide a check-the-box or entity classification election comparable to US tax rules. Available statutory mechanisms include: UK VAT group registration election under VATA 1994 s43; option to tax land (waiver of exemption) under Schedule 10 VATA 1994; VAT cash accounting, annual accounting, and partial exemption special method elections; Corporation Tax group relief claims and loss relief elections; overseas foreign tax credit election for the credit method; and automatic application of hybrid mismatch rules under CTA 2009 Part 6A / CTA 2010 Part 6A. No elective deconsolidation of a BVI BC for UK tax purposes is permitted. Double tax treaty residence/PE protection may be evidenced through non-residence facts rather than by making a domestic election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 taxable turnover in a rolling 12-month period for UK-established taxable persons (actual and expected taxable supplies, excluding exempt supplies and capital assets). Non-established foreign businesses have a nil threshold and must register with HMRC before making any taxable supply in the UK. Equity crowdfunding intermediary fees, to the extent treated as arranging the issue of securities, are exempt rather than taxable supplies, and therefore excluded from the threshold subject to a detailed supply characterisation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the direct export of goods from the UK where: (1) the goods are removed from the UK in the course of the supply; (2) the supplier holds the required evidence of export under HMRC VAT Notice 725; and (3) the goods are exported to a destination outside the UK within statutory time limits. For services supplied to overseas customers, the 0% or outside-the-scope treatment is available where the service falls within VATA 1994 sections 7 or 7A and the recipient belongs outside the UK under the B2B place-of-supply rules. Equity crowdfunding services comprise the arrangement or placing of securities and are generally exempt from VAT under Schedule 9 VATA 1994 rather than zero-rated; where the recipient belongs outside the UK, such exempt services may fall outside the scope of UK VAT, but the portal must not automatically treat all crowdfunding fees as zero-rated without a precise supply analysis.

  • Commercial invoice and export packing list
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, CMR, or other transport document evidencing physical export from the UK
  • Evidence of removal from the UK (customs records, port/airport clearance, container tracking, or similar)

  • Sales contract or purchase order identifying the overseas customer and consignee

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate applies to UK B2B VAT invoices. HMRC has, however, mandated Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT: every VAT-registered business must maintain VAT records digitally and submit VAT returns through MTD-compatible software using HMRC's MTD-VAT API and Government Gateway authentication. Invoices themselves do not require HMRC pre-approval or an electronic stamp. For goods movement, customs declarations pass through the Customs Declaration Service (CDS), which provides real-time clearance functions, but this is not an invoice clearance obligation. E-invoicing may be agreed commercially or used for public procurement (e.g., PEPPOL), but HMRC has not made it compulsory for ordinary B2B e-invoicing.

HMRC may audit export evidence, transport records, FX remittance flows, customer solvency, and the commercial substance of export transactions. Failure to provide adequate proof of export or claiming 0% VAT where not substantiated triggers: assessment of unpaid VAT under VATA 1994 s73 with statutory interest from the due date; civil penalties up to 100% of the VAT for careless or deliberate inaccuracies under Finance Act 2007 Schedule 24, and for evasion of VAT under VATA 1994 s60/s63; criminal prosecution under VATA 1994 s72 (and customs prosecutions under CEMA 1979) involving imprisonment and/or unlimited fines; retrospective clawback or denial of VAT refunds for periods up to 4 years (careless), 6 years (deliberate), or 20 years (deliberate concealment); and, in MTIC/export-fraud scenarios, denial of input VAT recovery if the portal knew or should have known that the transaction chain was connected to fraud. HMRC also reviews whether securities-arranging fees are VAT-exempt/outside scope rather than zero-rated and may recharacterise fee structures where artificial arrangements attempt to obtain refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange controls restricting the repatriation of dividends, interest, fees, or liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance from a UK branch or subsidiary of a BVI Business Company is unrestricted, subject to corporate tax compliance, transfer pricing documentation, and the filing of annual returns with Companies House and HMRC. A BVI company that is managed and controlled from the UK may become UK tax resident and must file UK corporation tax returns; profit extraction through dividends or distributions is generally subject to UK dividend withholding tax rules unless treaty relief applies (UK-BVI no double taxation treaty, so domestic rules apply).

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory timelines or mandatory conversion ratios for export receipts in the United Kingdom. Exporters are free to retain foreign currency in any bank account inside or outside the UK, and there is no obligation to repatriate or convert export proceeds. For a BVI Business Company trading through a UK establishment, export receipts must be properly recognised in UK financial statements and transactions must be reported for VAT and corporation tax purposes; however, no surrender to the Bank of England or HM Treasury is required.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority) and HM Treasury (overall economic policy and financial regulation); foreign exchange administration is liberalised under the UK Exchange Control Act 1979 (repealed), with no separate foreign exchange authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

UK-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Revolut Ltd, Wise Europe (or UK branch), and Currencycloud (owned by Visa) are viable for low-risk treasury operations including inbound fee collection and outbound supplier payments. However, for an equity crowdfunding portal with a high AML/CFT risk tier, most mainstream digital EMIs will reject onboarding or impose severe transaction limits. Dedicated merchant-acquiring and escrow-focused EMI platforms that specialise in regulated investment intermediaries, such as Mangopay or Stripe Connect (with FCA-registered payment institution status), have higher feasibility, but full banking-grade settlement rails typically require a Tier 1 bank partner.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a BVI Business Company raising capital through UK-based equity crowdfunding, all inbound capital contributions must flow through a properly designated client-money escrow account held at an FCA-regulated bank or an authorised EMI. The recommended routing is: (i) investor wire transfer from a personal or corporate account using SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'N401' (investment in equity) or 'N410' (crowdfunding contribution) under UK balance of payments reporting; (ii) funds are credited to the UK operational escrow account under the name of the crowdfunding portal as nominee; (iii) upon successful campaign close, funds are transferred to the BVI company's operational account after deducting platform fees; (iv) the BVI company must retain all contractual documentation, investor agreements, and KYC records for at least five years to evidence the source of funds and to satisfy UK bank counterparty due diligence.Outbound trade disbursements from the crowdfunding portal to investee companies or suppliers are recommended through the following clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for direct cross-border wire transfers where speed and traceability are required; (ii) UK Faster Payments Service (FPS) for domestic GBP disbursements under £1 million; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700 series) for large-scale trade settlements involving goods or services that require documentary compliance; (iv) CHAPS for high-value same-day GBP settlements above £1 million, with mandatory reference fields including invoice number and purpose code 'T101' for general trade. All outbound transactions must pass through the bank's sanctions screening (OFAC, UK sanctions, EU sanctions) and must be supported by underlying trade contracts, invoices, and customs documentation if applicable.FX spot contracts for immediate conversion of investor capital between GBP, USD, and EUR, provided the underlying transaction is a genuine commercial investment movement.Booking of outright FX forwards (up to 12 months) with a UK-authorized bank to hedge projected repatriation of dividends or operational cash flows, requiring documentary proof of the underlying exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not generally permitted for GBP (GBP is freely deliverable), but NDFs may be considered for exotic currency exposures arising from target companies; banks will only execute NDFs with a BVI entity if there is a genuine underlying commercial transaction and full board approval.Cross-currency swaps (GBP/USD/EUR) may be permissible for large-ticket crowdfunding infrastructure financing, but they require the BVI company to demonstrate that the swap relates to an actual foreign currency asset or liability and that the entity has the risk management capacity to assess mark-to-market exposure.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

19% / 25% with marginal relief (19% small profits rate for profits up to £50,000; 25% main rate for profits above £250,000; marginal relief between £50,000 and £250,000)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the United Kingdom does not levy any dividend withholding tax on corporate distributions made by UK-resident companies or on the distribution of a UK permanent establishment's profits.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory in documentation-sensitive circumstances when the UK entity or foreign parent in the BVI has annual turnover exceeding £200 million and group assets exceeding £2 billion, or when the aggregate of controlled transactions between the UK and BVI BC exceeds £5 million. Master File and Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) are required if the multinational group has consolidated gross revenue of at least £750 million (€750 million equivalent).

Economic substance scope: The BVI Business Company must demonstrate economic substance in the jurisdiction where its actual decision-making occurs. Under BVI ESR, a pure equity holding or finance-related activity must have adequate BVI presence, but a digital crowdfunding portal that performs all management in London creates UK tax residence and UK PE exposure. HMRC applies anti-abuse associated enterprise rules to disregard the separate existence of a BVI shell and reallocate profits to the UK substance-bearing headquarters, where statutory CT of 19%-25% applies on the reattributed income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion / Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: Under Schedule 24 FA2007, penalties of up to 200% of the understated tax; prosecution under FA2020 for failure to prevent facilitation of tax evasion carrying unlimited fines; HMRC may demand serious-default penalties, interest and assets freezing orders. Criminal conviction for fraudulent evasion under TMA1970 s106 provides up to 14 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally assessed for reckless or deliberate losses to HMRC; disqualification orders under CDDA1986 for up to 15 years; civil restraint orders, travel bans and seizure of personal assets if deliberate concealment is proved.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: HMRC will make transfer pricing adjustments, impose UK secondary adjustments, charge interest at 7.75%, and levy penalties up to 100% of under-assessed CT under TMA1970 s94; intentional transfer pricing abuse attracts a higher tax-geared penalty of 200%.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false or misleading TP documentation face director disqualification, personal liability for the tax repayment under CDDA, and in flagrant cases, a travel ban under HMRC's civil investigation powers.
Crowdfunding AML / KYC Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds Regulations 2017, an unlimited fine for each contravention; FCA enforcement can withdraw operating licence and impose restitution orders; under POCA2002, proceeds of the portal can be confiscated for tainted funds.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face up to 14 years' imprisonment under POCA if money laundering is connived in; the FCA may impose direct, unlimited financial penalties on the director; asset freezing and personal travel restrictions are imposed pending trial.
BVI Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The BVI International Tax Authority may charge ESR penalties of US$50,000 for first non-compliance and US$10,000 per month thereafter; full or partial deregistration of the BC in BVI may follow; HMRC will apply associated enterprise reallocation under CTA2010 s454 and additionally a 100% CT miss-catching penalty if no UK return was filed.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the BVI ESR affidavits or tax returns without adequate substance expose themselves to joint personal liability for BVI penalties and UK HMRC recovery; courts may allow piercing of the corporate veil where the BC is a mere sham, leading to attachment of personal bank accounts and a maximum term of imprisonment of 7 years for false declaration.
