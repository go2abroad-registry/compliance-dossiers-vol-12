E-government filing portal

Available election forms

United Kingdom has no US-style check-the-box regime; a BVI BC is a separate legal person, non-UK resident for corporation tax if central management and control is in the BVI and no UK permanent establishment exists; UK-source trading profits of a UK PE are taxable at 25% main rate. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 (taxable turnover in any trailing 12-month period; frozen for 2024-2026); deregistration threshold £120,000. A non-UK established business must register irrespective of the threshold if it makes any taxable supplies of goods in the UK and is not otherwise required to register (VATA 1994 Sch 1 para 1(4)-(7), para 10). The zero-rated and reduced-rated supplies count toward the threshold; exempt supplies generally do not, except in specified circumstances.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies under VATA 1994 s.30 and Schedule 8 Group 7 for supplies of goods exported to a destination outside the UK, or shipped as stores on an aircraft 'flying foreign' on an international route (VAT Notice 703, Notice 744A). For airline inflight catering, qualifying supplies include meals, beverages, and duty-free goods delivered to aircraft operating international flights (departure from the UK to a destination outside the UK, Isle of Man, or EEA countries being treated as a foreign voyage only where the aircraft leaves UK airspace); supplies consumed on purely domestic UK or Isle of Man legs remain standard-rated (20%). Additional eligibility for B2B services (e.g., menu logistics management to an overseas airline) as 'outside the scope' under the place-of-supply rule at s.7 and Sch 4A VATA 1994 when the recipient is a taxable person in another country; supply must be directly connected and the recipient must not be UK-established. Claim zero rating requires the exporter to obtain and retain prima facie evidence of export within 3 months of the time of supply (VAT Notice 703 para 4.2), possess the goods when they are removed from the UK, and issue a valid VAT invoice bearing a zero-rate annotation; services must be supported by a separate B2B agreement with the recipient's VAT number in another Member State (or equivalent).

No real-time fiscal clearance, stamping, or digital-fiscal-marker regime (no SAF-T, no CFDIs). Mandatory electronic reporting framework: Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT requires all VAT-registered businesses (including NETPs) to keep digital records and submit VAT returns to HMRC via HMRC-recognized MTD Application Programming Interfaces (APIs) through functional compatible software (VAT Notice 700/23). E-invoicing per se is voluntary and contractual; although directive PEPPOL-aligned standards are recommended for B2B aerospace/catering contracts, HMRC accepts PDF/EDI invoices if authentic. Customs: the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) is the sole electronic interface for export declarations; all export declarations and goods movement references (GMR) must be transmitted through approved software using CDS APIs; the National Export System (NES) generates the export declaration unique consignment reference (DUCR). For aircraft inflight catering, Article-type 'aircraft stores' export declarations (declaration code 21) and the use of bonded warehouse inventory links are mandatory where goods are transferred from the bonded kitchen to a customs-suspended aircraft; no digital stamp is applied but a CHIEF/CDS 'Goods Released' output and flight departure report must be retained.

HMRC's Export Verification Taskforce and National Assurance teams conduct post-export audits specifically targeting zero-rating claims in inflight catering; penalties and clawback risks include: (1) under FA 2007 Sch 24, inaccuracies in returns attract penalties of 30% (careless), 70% (deliberate), and 100% (deliberate with concealment) of the potential lost VAT (PLR); (2) under FA 2008 Sch 39, HMRC can raise retrospective VAT assessments for up to 4 years (non-deliberate), 6 years (careless), and 20 years (deliberate), with no time limit in cases of fraud; (3) fraudulent zero-rating claims on exports are criminal offences under CEMA 1979 s.167 (improper exports) and s.168 (false statements), carrying up to 7 years' imprisonment and/or an unlimited fine; civil evasion penalties of up to 100% of the VAT evaded apply under CEMA 1979 s.167(4A); (4) VATA 1994 s.36A (recovery of VAT credit to the Crown) operates as a quasi-clawback for credits improperly made on the basis of the export claim; (5) goods or ships/aircraft may be seized and forfeited under CEMA 1979 s.139-141 if export evidence is false; (6) a retrospective clawback extends to related input tax recovery (VAT Notice 706 partial-exemption de-grouping), interest under VATA s.74 (default interest from the date the return was due until payment), and potential disqualification of company officers; higher penalties apply in MTD non-compliance (FA 2022, Sch 1).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full and unrestricted convertibility of the pound sterling (GBP) for both current account and capital account transactions. The Exchange Control Act 1947 was repealed in 1979, and no general foreign exchange control regime, central bank approval, or quantitative restriction applies to inbound or outbound capital flows. There is no legal distinction between current and capital account convertibility, and no administrative convertibility licensing requirement for BVI corporate entities opening or operating GBP accounts. Profit remittance from a UK establishment of a BVI Business Company (VG_BC) is freely permitted; however, the practical hurdles are tax compliance rather than FX control: the entity must file UK corporation tax returns if the UK branch or permanent establishment generates trading profits, must comply with the UK Corporate Interest Restriction regime on net interest expense, must observe transfer pricing documentation standards, and must beware of the diverted profits tax (DPT) where arrangements lack economic substance. Dividend distributions from a UK subsidiary to the BVI parent are generally paid without UK withholding tax on dividends, whereas interest payments by UK payers to the BVI parent may trigger UK withholding tax at 20% unless reduced under the UK-BVI tax information exchange arrangement, which does not provide full treaty benefits; therefore, debt pushdowns should be structured carefully via arm's length loans and, where appropriate, UK domestic exemptions under the qualifying private placement or quoted Eurobond exemptions. In practical banking terms, the BVI BC may convert GBP to USD, EUR, or any freely traded currency for working capital purposes, settle trade invoices, and repatriate surplus funds without exchange control approval, subject only to anti-money laundering (AML) source-of-funds verification and Sanctions Office scrutiny of counterparties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules in the United Kingdom. Exporters and trading entities, including a BVI BC operating as an airline inflight catering kitchen in the UK, are not required to convert foreign currency receipts into pounds sterling, are not required to surrender export proceeds to the Bank of England, and face no mandatory timeline for repatriation of trade receivables. Foreign currency proceeds from airlinemeal production and ramp-side delivery contracts may be retained in multi-currency accounts, hedged freely, or converted into GBP at the commercial rate chosen by the entity. The only compliance constraints are indirect: (i) all payments must be routed through authorised payment service providers and clearing systems such as CHAPS, Faster Payments, SWIFT, or SEPA; (ii) the entity must maintain adequate accounting records under the Companies Act 2006 and the UK CTA 2010 for tax purposes; and (iii) transactions involving sanctioned jurisdictions, restricted goods, or designated persons must be screened against HM Treasury's Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) consolidated list. Because OFAC or EU sanctions lists may also be enforced by UK clearing banks, the treasury function should apply dual-list screening to all counterparties in the airline catering procurement and settlement chain.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority and financial stability regulator); HM Treasury (policy oversight for sanctions, financial restrictions, and exchange control matters); Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) and Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) as subsidiary banking and payments regulators

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for outward and inward payment processing, trade settlements, and multi-currency pooling; MEDIUM for primary banking relationship if the kitchen needs overdraft facilities, commercial cards, or physical branches. The UK's Electronic Money Regulations 2011 (SI 2011/99) permit FCA-regulated e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise (formerly TransferWise) Platform, Revolut Ltd, Airwallex (UK) Ltd, Currencycloud, and modulr to open virtual IBANs and local account numbers for BVI corporate clients. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs do not offer the full regulatory protections of a UK deposit-taking bank because e-money funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts, not protected by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS); (ii) the FCA requires EMIs to perform full due diligence on a BVI Business Company, including production of certified register of members, directors' ID, and UBO declarations; (iii) many digital EMIs refuse to onboard BVI companies unless the BVI entity can demonstrate substantive trading activity, a UK or EU operational office, and an existing contractual relationship with airlines; and (iv) the FCA Perimeter Guidance on payment services requires that safeguarding accounts be held at an authorised UK or EEA credit institution or the Bank of England, so an EMI treasury structure remains dependent on a tier-1 bank sub-account. Recommended architecture: maintain one tier-1 UK bank account (HSBC or Standard Chartered) as the master liquidity hub, and use one or two FCA-regulated EMIs as secondary disbursement rails for supplier payments and low-value cross-border transactions, while keeping capital injection and escrow balances solely in the tier-1 bank to avoid counterparty concentration risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The BVI Business Company (VG_BC) will inject initial equity, shareholder loans, or intercompany loans into its UK operational bank account. Compliant routing requires the following protocol. (1) The BVI parent must issue a board resolution authorising the transfer, specifying the exact amount, currency (GBP or USD), and the receiving UK account identified by its six-digit sort code and eight-digit account number, converted to an IBAN for SWIFT purposes. (2) The remitting bank must send a SWIFT MT103 message with the field 50K naming the BVI company, field 57D or 57A containing the UK beneficiary bank's BIC (e.g., HSBCUKGH or SCBLGB2L), and field 72 or 77B including a structured beneficiary reference such as 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION VG_BC', which must match the purpose description on the UK bank's KYC file. (3) For GBP clearing, the wire may alternatively be sent via CHAPS, the UK's same-day high-value settlement system, using the beneficiary sort code and account number; CHAPS is mandatory for sums above the Faster Payments limit (historically £1,000,000 per payment, now capped in practice at £1,000,000 per instruction) and is the preferred rail for capital injections because it provides irrevocable settlement. (4) For USD inbound wires from the BVI or upstream shareholders, the payment must be routed through the bank's New York correspondent with the appropriate SWIFT cover payment (MT202 COV) if an intermediary bank is used, and the UK account must be set up as a multi-currency or USD-denominated account to avoid unnecessary conversion. (5) The purpose code field in the SWIFT message should state 'STAT' (statistical) or 'INVC' (invoice) as requested by the bank; however, the UK does not prescribe specific central-bank purpose codes, so the critical compliance point is the narrative line, which must read 'BVI PARENT CAPITAL INJECTION FOR UK INFLIGHT CATERING KITCHEN' or a similar exact phrase. (6) All inbound injections require contemporaneous documentation — board minutes, subscription agreement for shares, or executed loan agreement — which must be retained on file for CRS/FATCA reporting, UK tax filing, and any future audit by the bank's financial crime team. The capital injection should not be commingled with trade receivables; therefore, the blueprint maintains a dedicated 'Capital Contribution Receiving Account' inside the same bank, segregated in the internal ledger, until the funds are transferred to the operational account.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH for outward and inward payment processing, trade settlements, and multi-currency pooling; MEDIUM for primary banking relationship if the kitchen needs overdraft facilities, commercial cards, or physical branches. The UK's Electronic Money Regulations 2011 (SI 2011/99) permit FCA-regulated e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise (formerly TransferWise) Platform, Revolut Ltd, Airwallex (UK) Ltd, Currencycloud, and modulr to open virtual IBANs and local account numbers for BVI corporate clients. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs do not offer the full regulatory protections of a UK deposit-taking bank because e-money funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts, not protected by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS); (ii) the FCA requires EMIs to perform full due diligence on a BVI Business Company, including production of certified register of members, directors' ID, and UBO declarations; (iii) many digital EMIs refuse to onboard BVI companies unless the BVI entity can demonstrate substantive trading activity, a UK or EU operational office, and an existing contractual relationship with airlines; and (iv) the FCA Perimeter Guidance on payment services requires that safeguarding accounts be held at an authorised UK or EEA credit institution or the Bank of England, so an EMI treasury structure remains dependent on a tier-1 bank sub-account. Recommended architecture: maintain one tier-1 UK bank account (HSBC or Standard Chartered) as the master liquidity hub, and use one or two FCA-regulated EMIs as secondary disbursement rails for supplier payments and low-value cross-border transactions, while keeping capital injection and escrow balances solely in the tier-1 bank to avoid counterparty concentration risk.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% main rate on profits above GBP 250,000; 19% small profits rate on profits below GBP 50,000; marginal relief applies between GBP 50,000 and GBP 250,000 producing an effective rate range of 19%-25%; rate set by Finance Act 2021, effective for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 April 2023; limits are divided by the number of associated companies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - the UK does not impose withholding tax on dividend distributions made by UK-resident companies or by the UK permanent establishment (PE) of a non-resident company. The deemed dividend under CTA 2010 s.1109 (treating the UK PE as a UK company distributing profits to the foreign head office) is not subject to UK income tax. Therefore, a BVI Business Company can repatriate after-tax UK catering profits to BVI as dividends with no additional UK dividend withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory GBP threshold requires automatic filing of a Local File/Master File with HMRC; instead, the UK applies the OECD-aligned 'reasonable care' documentation standard. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be retained and produced within 30 days of an HMRC information notice. The mandatory TP regime applies to (i) large enterprises (satisfying at least two of: turnover > GBP 36,000,000; balance sheet total > GBP 18,000,000; > 250 employees) and (ii) small or medium-sized enterprises only where the controlled transaction exceeds GBP 1,000,000 per transaction category; medium-sized enterprises can claim a reduced documentation regime with a 'general summary' plus benchmarking evidence. A BVI BC with a UK PE undertaking cross-border catering supply, management fees, brand royalties, and intercompany loan arrangements should prepare a full OECD Master File unless the enterprise qualifies for the SME exemption. The UK transfer pricing rules under TIOPA 2010 Part 4 must be applied regardless of whether the counterparty is in a no-treaty jurisdiction such as the BVI.

Economic substance scope: The BVI Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Act 2018 (the 'ESR Act') applies to every BVI Business Company that carries on a 'relevant activity'. 'Airline in-flight catering' falls within 'distribution and service centre business' as defined in the BVI ESR Code, being the provision of services (including catering and logistics) to a connected or unrelated party. A BVI BC that is resident for BVI tax purposes must demonstrate economic substance in the BVI: physical presence, qualified and full-time employees, actual management in BVI, adequate expenditure, and a core income-generating activity conducted in the BVI. Alternatively, a BVI BC that is non-resident in BVI (i.e., tax resident in another jurisdiction, notably the UK by central management and control) may apply for an exemption from the BVI substance test by filing audited/non-audited financials and evidence of UK tax residence, but the UK HMRC must confirm or the BVI International Tax Authority will question the claim. The BVI annual economic substance filing must be submitted within the prescribed window after the company's financial year end; failure to file triggers automatic penalties and potential strike-off. The UK has its own equivalent doctrine under the 'diverted profits tax' (DPT) at 25% where arrangements lack economic substance and result in a tax advantage, plus the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) in FA 2013.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Corporation Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Finance Act 2007 Sch. 24: deliberate inaccuracy with concealment penalty up to 200% of the understated tax; Fraud Act 2006 s.2 (fraud by false representation) and s.3 (fraud by failing to disclose): unlimited fine, up to 10 years' imprisonment on indictment; TMA 1970 s.106A: evasion of income tax up to 7 years' imprisonment; HMRC 'naming and shaming' of deliberate defaulters; confiscation orders under POCA 2002 may reach the total benefit gained.

Director personal liability: Company officers are jointly and severally liable for penalties attributable to their own conduct (FA 2007 Sch.24 para.19); fraudulent trading under Companies Act 2006 s.993 gives courts power to make a personal contribution order; wrongful trading under Insolvency Act 1986 s.214 requires directors to contribute to company assets where the BVI BC trades while insolvent; director disqualification orders up to 15 years; HMRC may apply for bankruptcy restriction orders and travel bans; where VAT fraud is proven, director personal liability under VATA 1994 s.72 for officers' consent or connivance; imprisonment of up to 7 years may attach personally.
Offshore Evasion, Failure to Correct and Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: BVI ESR Act: initial penalty of USD 20,000 for failure to satisfy economic substance, escalating to USD 100,000-USD 400,000 for continued default, plus the BVI Registrar may strike off and dissolve the company; FA 2016 Sch. 26 'Failure to Correct' penalties up to 200% of the tax where offshore assets were not disclosed before 30 September 2018; UK GAAR counteraction with a 60% penalty on the counteracted advantage; Diverted Profits Tax at 25% on diverted profits; HMRC may issue 'naming and shaming' notices, asset freezing orders, and payment notices.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in a false BVI ESR declaration risk personal fines under BVI law and disqualification; UK Senior Accounting Officer regime (FA 2009 s.93) imposes personal GBP 5,000 penalties for failure to certify tax accounting arrangements; directors may be pursued as shadow directors for fraudulent trading; travel bans are available under POCA investigatory powers; custodial sentences of up to 7 years may be imposed for conspiracy to defraud HMRC.
