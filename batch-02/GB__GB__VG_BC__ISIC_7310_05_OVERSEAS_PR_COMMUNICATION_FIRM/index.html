Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) — online portal via Government Gateway at gov.uk (Corporation Tax, VAT, PAYE, and Making Tax Digital services); Companies House for overseas entity registration.

E-government filing portal

The BVI Business Company (BC) is a non-UK resident company for corporation tax purposes unless its central management and control is exercised in the UK. If it commences a taxable trade in the UK through a permanent establishment (PE), it must register for Corporation Tax via HMRC within 3 months of starting the UK business, obtaining a Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR). For VAT, a non-UK established supplier making any UK taxable supplies must register for VAT via HMRC online (Form VAT1) and obtain a VAT Registration Number (VRN); there is no turnover threshold for non-UK established persons. The BC must also register at Companies House under the Register of Overseas Entities (ROE) and obtain a UK establishment registration number if it opens a UK branch/establishment. Registration requires the BVI certificate of good standing, constitutional documents, and identification of beneficial owners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The UK applies case-law classification (central management and control) rather than a check-the-box election; a non-UK company can elect to be charged to corporation tax on UK property income under CTA 2009 s. 19A and make a s. 42A election for a deemed separate PE for property. Hybrid mismatch rules under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A may deny double deductions where the BC is treated as transparent in BVI but opaque in the UK. No full UK-BVI double taxation treaty exists, so the BC cannot rely on treaty PE protection; unilateral UK credit relief under TIOPA 2010 s. 18 applies only to foreign tax on overseas income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 for UK-established businesses (taxable turnover in trailing 12 months or forecast next 30 days); nil threshold for non-UK established suppliers making any UK taxable supplies, except distance-sale transactions below £10,000; separate £8,000 registration threshold for intra-Community acquisitions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under VATA 1994 s. 30 and SI 1995/2518 applies to exports of goods physically removed from the UK to a non-UK destination within statutory time limits (usually within 3 months of supply). PR and communication services supplied to a business customer established outside the UK fall outside the scope of UK VAT (place of supply: recipient's country) under VATA 1994 s. 7A, making them effectively 0% without input tax recovery issues; services supplied to non-business overseas recipients may also be outside scope where the recipient belongs outside the UK. The supplier must hold valid evidence of export or of the customer's business status and establishment within prescribed record-keeping rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD form / electronic declaration via CHIEF or CDS, with MRN reference)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank transfer confirmation from the overseas client
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Evidence of export (e.g., carrier receipts, consignment notes) or evidence of recipient's business status for services (contract, VAT/sales tax registration certificate, client confirmation)

Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT mandates digital record-keeping and API-based software filing of VAT returns to HMRC; no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping is imposed. E-invoicing is not universally mandated for B2B/B2C supply, but all VAT invoices must contain the statutory minimum data fields (VATA 1994 Sch. 6); HMRC reserves the right to require digital submission of supplementary export evidence.

HMRC may deny zero-rating retrospectively where export evidence is incomplete or time limits are missed, assessing VAT at 20% plus statutory interest. Deliberate or careless inaccuracies attract penalties up to 100% of the VAT lost under Finance Act 2007 Sch. 24; fraudulent evasion of VAT is a criminal offence under VATA 1994 s. 72, carrying unlimited fines and imprisonment up to 7 years. Missing Trader Intra-Community (MTIC) and export-fraud rules empower HMRC to raise joint-and-several liability assessments and impose denial of input tax recovery; transfer pricing and 'final results' principles under TIOPA 2010 also allow HMRC to reallocate profits if the 180-day repatriation benchmark is not met, with interest and penalties up to 100% of any under-declared corporation tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full convertibility on both current and capital accounts. No general exchange-control legislation is in force, and no Bank of England consent is required for the BVI Business Company to fund or operate a UK bank account. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, capital repatriation, and liquidation proceeds may be freely transferred in any currency. The only frictions are standard AML/KYC verification, corporate-substance expectations, and UK tax-filing obligations if the BVI company is treated as having a UK permanent establishment or branch.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The UK imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export earnings may be retained offshore or in any currency indefinitely. Reporting obligations arise only through statistical surveys administered by the Bank of England/HMRC for selected cross-border service providers, or through tax-return disclosures where a UK taxable presence exists. The BVI BC should maintain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation and invoice trails for international media and PR services to avoid recharacterisation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (the central bank of the United Kingdom), with HM Treasury and HM Revenue & Customs respectively exercising prudential, financial-crime supervisory, and exchange-reporting functions under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 and the Money Laundering Regulations 2017.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating for FCA-authorised electronic-money institutions (EMIs) is Medium-to-High for a BVI Business Company, provided the jurisdiction risk layer is mitigated with substance evidence. Legal constraints: the EMI must conduct full customer due diligence under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017; the BVI BC is not required to register with Companies House to hold a payment account, but many EMIs require a UK or regional operating address. EMIs may issue electronic money, process cross-border payments, and offer FX conversion, but cannot grant credit or deal in derivatives. Wise Business, Revolut Business, and Currencycloud are operationally viable if ultimate beneficial owners, source of funds, and transaction flows pass sanctions and AML screening.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant rail for injecting capital from the BVI parent into a UK operational account is a SWIFT MT103 international wire or an equivalent SOC-verified transfer. The remitter must use the beneficiary UK bank’s BIC and IBAN and clearly state the payment purpose as 'Capital injection – intercompany funding' or 'Direct investment by BVI parent'. The remitting bank will apply its own ISO 20022 purpose category; the UK receiving bank performs sanctions, AML, and source-of-funds verification. No foreign-exchange permission is required. The supporting file must include the BVI board resolution approving the injection, a source-of-funds statement (share issue proceeds or shareholder loan), and the latest BVI economic-substance confirmation.Clearing rails for outbound settlements from the UK account include: SWIFT MT103 for international USD/EUR/GBP wires; CHAPS for urgent same-day high-value GBP payments across UK accounts; Faster Payments for low-value domestic GBP disbursements; SEPA Credit Transfers for EUR payments to beneficiaries in the EEA; and SWIFT MT700 or MT710 documentary credits (Commercial Letter of Credit) only when a supplier in an overseas PR, media-buying, or content-distribution chain contractually requires bank-backed settlement.
