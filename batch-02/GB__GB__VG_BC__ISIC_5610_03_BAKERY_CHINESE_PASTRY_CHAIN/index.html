Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC), operating through GOV.UK and HMRC Online Services (portal: gov.uk/log-in-register-hmrc-online-services); customs filings are made through the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) and the UK Single Trade Window.

E-government filing portal

The VG_BC is a BVI Business Company and, absent central management and control (CMC) exercised in the UK, is non-UK tax resident. Step 1: Determine whether a UK permanent establishment (PE) exists under CTA 2010 s.1119 and CTA 2009 s.5; a fixed place, branch, office, workshop or dependent agent in the UK will trigger UK Corporation Tax on profits attributable to the PE only. Step 2: If a UK PE is established, register the foreign company for Corporation Tax via the HMRC 'register a new tax' service on Government Gateway; HMRC issues the 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) and the company must file Form CT41G(X) and an annual CT600 return. Step 3: The BVI BC is a non-established taxable person (NETP) for VAT purposes; under VATA 1994 Sch.1 NETP registration rules, registration is mandatory before the first UK taxable supply, with no economic threshold; file Form VAT1 and VAT1C with certified BVI certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, directors' register, overseas tax reference, UK correspondence or establishment address, and the entity's EORI number; HMRC turnaround for NETP registration is typically 4 to 6 weeks pending compliance review. Step 4: If the franchise operation employs UK staff, register as an employer for PAYE and National Insurance through the HMRC Employer Registration Service, obtaining an Employer PAYE reference and Accounts Office reference. Step 5: Apply separately to HMRC for a UK EORI number (GB prefix) for all UK customs declarations and export clearances. In the absence of a UK PE, the only compulsory registrations are VAT (on first UK taxable supply), customs EORI, and any food-business licensing; no UK corporation tax registration is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The UK has no check-the-box entity classification; status follows residence and PE attribution. Available elections and mechanisms for the VG_BC: (i) VAT option to tax on UK commercial property (VATA 1994 Sch.10, Form VAT1614A); (ii) VAT flat-rate scheme election for small businesses meeting turnover and UK-establishment conditions; (iii) VAT annual accounting and cash accounting elections available only where the entity has a UK establishment (VAT Notice 731); (iv) group relief election under CTA 2010 s.130 where the BVI BC joins a 75% UK group, allowing loss surrender; (v) intangible fixed assets regime election under CTA 2009 s.730 for UK goodwill and IP; and (vi) hybrid mismatch rules under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A provide no protective election but impose mandatory self-disclosure and deduction-denial where hybrid instruments or entities produce mismatch outcomes; the UK-BVI relationship has no double taxation treaty, so the UK's unilateral territorial relieving provisions govern.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 of taxable turnover in any rolling 12-month period (effective 1 April 2024; deregistration threshold £88,000). The threshold applies only to suppliers established in the UK. A non-established taxable person (NETP), including the VG_BC, must register before making any UK taxable supply regardless of value. Distance-selling and online-marketplace rules apply separately to overseas sellers; for the bakery franchise, staple bread and plain cakes are generally zero-rated under VATA 1994 Sch.8 Group 1, while chocolate-covered biscuits, some confectionery, and supplies with on-premises consumption are standard-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exported goods under VATA 1994 s.30(6) and VAT Regulations 1995 regs.133-134. The supplier must prove that qualifying goods were removed from the UK to a destination outside the UK. The goods must depart within 3 months of the time of supply, the supplier must hold and preserve removal evidence, and the export must be made as a normal commercial export through the CDS with a valid GB EORI number. Offshore services, including franchise royalties and bakery chain management fees, may be zero-rated where the recipient belongs outside the UK under the general B2B place-of-supply rule in s.7A VATA 1994, provided the services are supplied to a business customer in another jurisdiction. Payment via inward remittance is recommended as corroborative evidence but is not of itself proof of export; physical departure evidence is determinative.

  • UK Customs Export Declaration (CDS Entry/E2 with Movement Reference Number)
  • Original commercial invoice stating seller, buyer, Incoterms, goods description, quantity and value
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing UK loading and overseas discharge
  • Packing list detailing goods description, weights, marks and container number
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export proceeds

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping platform, or central e-invoicing clearance regime exists in the UK. Mandatory fiscal controls are instead implemented through: (i) Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT, requiring all VAT-registered businesses to maintain digital records and submit returns via HMRC-approved functional compatible software using the HMRC MTD for VAT APIs; (ii) standard VAT invoice requirements containing 11 mandatory fields (VAT Regulations 1995 reg.14), simplified invoices for supplies at or below £250, and modified invoices where the customer is in another territory; (iii) the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) as the sole electronic frontier declaration platform, with export declarations (E2) submitted through CDS-recognised software or API integration before goods depart; and (iv) participation in the UK Single Trade Window (UK STW). Failure to comply with MTD or CDS requirements attracts penalties and may result in mandatory security being required from HMRC.

HMRC may deny zero-rating and assess output VAT under VATA 1994 s.73 where export evidence is insufficient, goods remain in the UK beyond 3 months, or documentation is retrospectively altered; default interest is charged under s.74. Civil penalties for careless or deliberate inaccuracies in VAT returns reach up to 100% of evaded VAT under FA 2007 Sch.24. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion of VAT under VATA 1994 s.72 carries a penalty of up to 7 years' imprisonment and an unlimited fine. HMRC's MTIC and cross-border fraud investigation teams may invoke the extended 20-year assessment window for deliberate error, impose retrospective clawbacks on contrived circular export chains, require VAT security, suspend VAT registration, cancel EORI authorisation, and pursue directors of the VG_BC personally where fraud or dishonest conduct is proven. The 180-day FX repatriation deadline operates as the pipeline's presumptive commercial-remittance window; absence of inward remittance within that period triggers heightened HMRC scrutiny of whether an export transaction has economic substance and whether transfer-pricing adjustments are required under TIOPA 2010 Part 4.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full current and capital account convertibility. There are no exchange control laws restricting foreign direct investment, profit remittance, dividend distribution, liquidation proceeds, or repatriation of capital. Sterling and foreign currency accounts may be opened and funded freely by non-resident companies. The only material constraints arise from UK financial sanctions regimes administered by HM Treasury's Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation, which may require a specific licence for dealings with sanctioned persons or jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds or convert them into sterling. Exporters may retain, hold, and reinvest foreign currency receipts at their discretion. The only reporting obligations are statistical in nature (HMRC Intrastat/Extrastat where volume thresholds apply) and tax-related (corporate tax filings and transfer pricing documentation). No Bank of England approval is required for holding or repatriating trade proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority) and HM Treasury; the Financial Conduct Authority supervises payments, e-money, and FX firms under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 and the Payment Services Regulations 2017.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for UK FCA-authorised electronic money institutions and payment institutions offering multi-currency IBANs, SWIFT, and Faster Payments rails. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot hold client funds as deposit accounts without a deposit-taking licence, are subject to safeguarding rules, and may not offer credit or interest-bearing products. UK FCA passports for EEA EMIs largely ceased after Brexit; a non-UK EMI must obtain UK authorisation or use an FCA-registered agent. BVI Business Companies are treated as moderate risk, so an EMI will typically require a UK nexus or a clear commercial rationale for UK bank accounts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Large enterprise threshold: turnover > £200 million, assets > £2 billion, or 250+ employees. SMEs (turnover ≤ £200 million, assets ≤ £2 billion, employees ≤ 250) are exempt from full documentation but must maintain contemporaneous records for transactions exceeding £1 million.

Economic substance scope: UK tax law requires that a non-resident company with a UK permanent establishment has sufficient economic substance in the UK to support its activities. The BVI company must also comply with BVI Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for its BVI entity, but for UK tax purposes, the PE must demonstrate real management, staff, and assets in the UK.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 200% of the tax understated, plus interest. Criminal prosecution may lead to unlimited fines and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for penalties if they knowingly participate in evasion. Travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed under Proceeds of Crime Act 2002.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: HMRC may recharacterize the structure, impose transfer pricing adjustments, and levy penalties up to 100% of the additional tax. The company may be struck off and directors disqualified.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for unpaid tax if they knowingly allow the company to operate without substance. Disqualification orders up to 15 years.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to £5,000 per breach, or criminal penalties including imprisonment up to 2 years. The company may be subject to asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined and imprisoned if they consent or connive in money laundering. Travel bans and extradition may apply.
VAT Fraud

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 100% of the VAT evaded, plus interest. Criminal prosecution for VAT fraud can lead to imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for VAT debts if they act in bad faith. Joint and several liability notices can be issued.
