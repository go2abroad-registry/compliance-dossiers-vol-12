Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates a fully convertible currency regime with no exchange controls, no prior approval mechanisms, and no quantitative restrictions on either current account or capital account flows. GIP is pegged at par to GBP, and GBP, EUR, and USD are routinely used in trade settlement. Profit remittance and capital repatriation by a non-resident-owned, tax-exempt Bermuda Exempted Company are legally unrestricted, subject only to standard GFSC-regulated anti-money-laundering verification and correspondent-bank risk screening. The residual practical impediments are de-risking policies by global correspondent banks rather than statutory FX or conversion hurdles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements exist in either the United Kingdom or Gibraltar. Exporters, including a Bermuda Exempted Company conducting e-commerce distribution through Gibraltar, may retain sale proceeds in GBP, EUR, USD, or CNY in freely convertible multi-currency accounts with GFSC-licensed banks. The only compliance burdens are record-keeping under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and, where applicable, standard corporate tax and economic substance reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (ultimate monetary authority for the United Kingdom and its overseas territory of Gibraltar); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the prudential banking and financial services supervisor of Gibraltar; Government of Gibraltar as the issuing authority of the Gibraltar pound (GIP), which circulates at a fixed 1:1 parity with, and interchangeably against, GBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-LOW for a tax-exempt Bermuda Exempted Company. Following Brexit, UK-authorised EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Currencycloud) no longer passport into Gibraltar, and Gibraltar does not maintain a dedicated domestic EMI licensing track; the sole GFSC licensing route is the Distributed Ledger Technology Providers regime. Mainstream fintech EMIs generally onboard Gibraltar-incorporated companies but classify this structure as an enhanced-risk offshore legal form and frequently reject it at the platform screening stage. A viable alternative is onboarding via a GFSC-licensed professional introducer who attests beneficial ownership, source of funds, and board authority, allowing account opening through the EMI's trusted-introducer channel. Legal constraints: EMIs may not provide credit, escrow, or investment services, and cannot replace a licensed bank as the ultimate settlement institution for Amazon Pay or Stripe payouts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is an inbound SWIFT MT103 credit from the shareholder's regulated bank to the Gibraltar beneficiary account, with the remittance narrative in field 70 strictly stated as 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION - SHARE PREMIUM' together with the internal entity reference ELF_CUSTOM_BM_EX; any ambiguous wording such as 'fees', 'services', or 'loan' must be avoided. The remitting institution should apply an ISO 20022 or EPC purpose code denoting investment or capital transfer; the recommended narrative tag is '/INVE/ CAPITAL INJECTION' or equivalent investment-purpose code. The Gibraltar receiving bank will require a certified board resolution, updated certificate of incumbency, share subscription agreements, and confirmation that funds originate from a regulated bank account. The funds must be held in a Capital Escrow Account until full KYC and source-of-wealth verification is complete, after which the balance is released to the operational account against an approved board spending plan. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar does not impose withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar-resident company or a foreign-incorporated company registered in Gibraltar; the Bermuda Exempted Company is similarly not subject to Bermuda dividend withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Gibraltar Local File and Master File are required where the Gibraltar entity's annual turnover exceeds GBP 10,000,000 or aggregate connected-party transactions exceed GBP 1,000,000; country-by-country reporting notification applies where the worldwide group has consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000. A Bermuda Exempted Company with no Gibraltar taxable presence is not automatically exempt from documentation if connected-party transactions are booked through Gibraltar.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Act 2019 applies to entities that are resident in Gibraltar, including foreign-incorporated companies with central management and control in Gibraltar, and entities claiming Qualifying Offshore Company status. The Act covers relevant activities such as distribution and service centres, headquarters, financing, leasing, and intellectual-property holding. Precision laser cutting and assembly of wooden puzzles is a physical manufacturing activity and is not per se a listed relevant activity; however, if the entity invoices connected-party sales, holds inventory in Gibraltar, or provides group administrative services, it may be treated as a 'distribution and service centre' or 'headquarters' and must satisfy the full economic substance test, including premises, employees, and expenditure proportionate to activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Criminal Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2010 penalties on the company: up to 150% of the tax understated, fixed penalties of up to GBP 100,000, and criminal fines; the court may also order confiscation of the gross proceeds of the undeclared sales. Interest accrues at 8% per annum on the unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly signs a false return is personally liable to pay restitution equal to the tax avoided, may be disqualified as a director for up to 15 years, and may be subject to a travel ban. In aggravated cases, directors face imprisonment under Gibraltar's income tax and proceeds-of-crime legislation.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltarian Economic Substance Act 2019 and related Companies Act provisions: financial penalty of up to GBP 100,000, mandatory strike-off from the Gibraltar Register of Companies, six-month default penalty for continued non-compliance, and automatic exchange of the substance failure with the competent authorities of the United Kingdom and Bermuda.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally examined at a public inquiry, ordered to contribute to company liabilities, and made subject to confiscation orders; failure to comply with the substance determination can lead to arrest warrants and travel bans. The corporate veil is pierced where the company is merely a facade concealing director-controlled trading.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Impermissible Avoidance

Statutory sanction: The Gibraltar Commissioner may adjust the taxable profit to the arm's length position, impose a tax-geared penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax, and apply late payment interest. Failure to produce documentation also attracts penalties of up to GBP 20,000 per chargeable period.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve an artificial pricing structure are jointly and severally liable for the additional tax where the arrangement is found to be a sham; they may also face disqualification proceedings and personal penalty notices for causing the company to make false returns.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) / Payment-Rails Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under Gibraltar's Proceeds of Crime Act and AML/CFT regime: unlimited fines for the company, civil forfeiture of the tainted funds, revocation of any financial services or EMI banking relationship, and inclusion on the Gibraltar sanctions list; the offence of possessing criminal property carries a maximum term of 14 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are personally liable for money-laundering offences and cannot hide behind the company. The court may order personal restitution of all misappropriated or laundered amounts, impose a travel ban pending trial, and sentence individuals to imprisonment; bankruptcy or disqualification of the director is a mandatory consequence upon conviction.
