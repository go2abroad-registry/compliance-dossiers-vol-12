Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) – main tax administration; online filing and registration portal: Government Gateway (gov.uk).

E-government filing portal

A BVI Business Company (non-UK resident) must first determine whether it carries on a trade in the UK through a permanent establishment (PE) or has UK-source income. If a PE exists, the company must register for Corporation Tax by submitting form CT41G (Non-resident company) to HMRC; a UK Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) is issued. For VAT, the company must register if it makes taxable supplies in the UK; a non-established taxable person (NETP) uses form VAT1C or VAT1 online through the Government Gateway. Required evidence includes: certificate of incorporation, proof of registered office in BVI, certified director/passport identification, UK agent details if applicable, and bank or settlement records evidencing UK transactions. If no UK supply activity is present, the BVI BC may still voluntarily register for VAT (form VAT1, option 2) for input tax recovery or compliance reasons.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

United Kingdom law has no 'check-the-box' election analogous to the US. A BVI Business Company is taxed at entity level under the Corporation Tax Acts if it is resident or carries on a trade through a UK permanent establishment; its BVI tax-exempt status does not automatically exempt UK profits. The company may, however, elect under CTA 2009 s18A for deemed UK residence only if dual residence applies, and may make a 'limited liability partnership election' only if the entity is a partnership under foreign law (not applicable to a BC). Hybrid mismatch rules under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A require disclosure and adjustment, but allow no elective exclusion; affected entities must notify HMRC via form CT600 and the hybrid mismatch return within the filing deadline.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 taxable turnover in any rolling 12-month period for UK-established businesses; zero threshold for non-established taxable persons making taxable supplies in the UK, who must register from the first supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A BVI BC may zero-rate exported goods under VATA 1994 s30 and Schedule 8 Group 12 when goods are removed from the UK to a destination outside the UK and the supplier retains valid evidence of export within statutory time limits. Services supporting exported goods (handling, storage, freight) may be zero-rated under Group 7. For the hotpot restaurant business, overseas retail supply of condiment products or packaged food qualifies as zero-rated export, while dining services supplied in the UK remain standard-rated. Offshore services (e.g., brand licensing or management fees to a BVI affiliate) are outside the scope of UK VAT where the place of supply is outside the UK and the recipient is not a UK taxable person; zero-rating for services is available under Schedule 4A subject to Part 3 of the Value Added Tax (Place of Supply of Services) Order 1992.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CDS – C88/Commercial Declaration with MRN)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR consignment note
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT payment confirmation evidencing non-UK customer settlement
  • Proof of arrival at destination outside the UK (delivery confirmation / POD)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list

No real-time fiscal clearance or government digital stamping applies to standard UK VAT invoices. VAT-registered businesses must comply with Making Tax Digital for VAT, using HMRC-recognised functional compatible software (API-based) to keep digital records and submit VAT returns. E-invoicing is voluntary outside public procurement, but exports require customs declarations through the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) using API/XML; an Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number is mandatory for customs transactions. No pre-clearance invoice authorisation exists.

HMRC may raise assessments for incorrectly zero-rated exports within 4 years (20 years for deliberate error), claw back input tax, and impose penalties under FA 2007 Schedule 24 of up to 100% of potential lost revenue. Criminal prosecution under VATA 1994 s72 carries up to 7 years’ imprisonment and unlimited fines. Fraudulent export claims trigger retrospective VAT, interest under FA 2009 s197, and reputational debarment; disclosure via the Contractual Disclosure Facility mitigates penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains unrestricted current- and capital-account convertibility and is in full compliance with IMF Article VIII; no prior approval is required for repatriation of profits, branch remittances, dividends, or trade proceeds payable to a BVI Business Company. The BVI likewise operates no domestic exchange-control regime (its official currency is the US dollar), so capital flows from the BVI BC into the UK restaurant operation are unencumbered. The sole practical hurdles are UK/EU sanctions screening administered by OFSI, CRS/FATCA reporting, and substance documentation for the BVI vehicle; none of these constitutes a convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. The United Kingdom abolished exchange controls in 1979, and neither the Bank of England nor HM Treasury imposes any statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. The BVI imposes no surrender or conversion obligations. Restaurant trade proceeds (card settlement and POS receipts) may be retained freely in GBP or converted into any currency. The only binding obligations are statistical reporting (Bank of England/OFSI surveys and foreign direct investment declarations for flows exceeding reporting thresholds) and compliance with OFSI licensing requirements where a sanctioned person is involved.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority) in coordination with HM Treasury's Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI); there is no separate standalone foreign exchange administration bureau in the United Kingdom.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High operational viability as a secondary banking layer, but not a substitute for a Tier 1 relationship bank. FCA-regulated electronic money institutions (Revolut Business, Wise, Currenxie, Airwallex) will on-board a BVI Business Company under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011 and Payment Services Regulations 2017, subject to enhanced due diligence covering 100% UBO identification, a registered-agent letter, and certified constitutional documents. These EMIs offer GBP/EUR/USD virtual accounts, mid-market FX conversion, and SWIFT disbursements. Key constraints: EMIs cannot extend commercial credit, do not provide documentary letters of credit, and post-Brexit passporting into the EEA is restricted; retail face-to-face merchant acquiring for the restaurant should be contracted with a licensed acquirer rather than an EMI.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (main rate for profits exceeding £250,000); 19% small profits rate for profits up to £50,000 with marginal relief between £50,001 and £250,000

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — the UK abolished dividend withholding tax for distributions made by UK resident companies on or after 6 April 2009; payments of dividends by the UK PE or a UK permanent establishment branch do not generate any statutory withholding; however, the BVI parent will generally receive branch profits via capital distributions or loan account credits without UK tax, profits having already borne corporation tax at the PE level.
