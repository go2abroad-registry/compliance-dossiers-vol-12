Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC); online service via GOV.UK 'HMRC Online Services' (Government Gateway authentication) and the Making Tax Digital (MTD) VAT API endpoint. Companies House is not involved unless the BVI BC registers a UK establishment of an overseas entity.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Assess UK nexus: non-UK resident BVI BC is chargeable to Corporation Tax (CT) only on profits of a UK permanent establishment (PE) under CTA 2009 s.5; pure cross-border B2B software licensing creates no UK CT nexus. Step 2 - If a UK PE exists: notify HMRC within 3 months of commencing UK trading via CT registration (form CT41G or online), obtaining a 10-digit Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR). Step 3 - VAT: register as a Non-Established Taxable Person (NETP) using form VAT1 (online via HMRC). B2C supplies of remote screen-share/IoT telemetry software (electronically supplied services) to UK consumers trigger VAT registration from the first pound of turnover (no threshold); B2B supplies to UK VAT-registered customers are reverse-charged by the customer. Step 4 - Alternative for B2C e-services: register for the UK Mini One Stop Shop (Non-Union VAT MOSS scheme) and file quarterly UK VAT returns via the HMRC portal. Step 5 - Payment-rail nuance: where Paddle acts as merchant of record, Paddle (not the BC) is the VAT-visible supplier; retain Paddle's VAT registration evidence and contractual deed of assumption. Where Stripe is used as a pure payment gateway without merchant-of-record service, the BC remains the VAT supplier. Step 6 - KYC/verification: certified passport copies, UBO declarations (BVI register), bank reference, and HMRC agent authorisation form 64-8 where a UK tax agent is engaged.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (opaque, non-UK resident BVI BC - UK CT applies only to profits attributable to a UK PE; no pass-through). Territorial_Scope (UK-source trading income chargeable; overseas active business income falls outside UK CT unless routed through the UK PE). Tax_Exempt_Zone (BVI home regime - the BC is exempt from BVI income tax and capital gains tax under the BVI Business Companies Act, Cap 218; this does not confer UK exemption). Pass_Through_Eligible: No.

Available election forms

The UK has no check-the-box election. Applicable elections and designations available to the BC: (a) voluntary VAT registration (VATA 1994 Sch 1) and the VAT Cash Accounting / Annual Accounting schemes (turnover not exceeding GBP 1.35m); (b) capital allowances elections - full expensing at 100% for main-rate plant and machinery and 50% for special-rate assets (from 1 April 2023), plus Annual Investment Allowance up to GBP 1m; (c) hybrid mismatch designation elections under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A (s.259) addressing the interaction between BVI tax-exempt status and UK deduction/payee treatment; (d) Corporate Interest Restriction (CIR) group and public-infrastructure elections under TIOPA 2010 Part 10; (e) transfer-pricing Advance Pricing Agreement via HMRC mutual agreement procedure; (f) accounting-framework election (IFRS vs FRS 101) as the statutory baseline for CT computation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 90,000 annual taxable turnover (from 1 April 2024) for UK-established businesses. Nil threshold for the non-established BVI BC where it makes B2C supplies of electronically supplied services (remote screen-share and IoT telemetry software) to UK consumers. B2B-only suppliers are generally outside the UK registration requirement because the reverse charge transfers output VAT accounting to the UK customer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods export: 0% VAT applies where goods are physically removed from the UK to a non-UK destination, with proof of removal obtained within 3 months of supply (VATA 1994 s.30; SI 1995/2518 reg 129; VAT Notice 703). Software services: cross-border B2B supplies are outside the scope of UK VAT under place-of-supply rules (customer's country of establishment), so no UK VAT is charged and the customer self-accounts via reverse charge. B2C supplies to non-UK consumers are also outside UK VAT scope; only UK-resident B2C consumers attract 20% VAT (enforced via IP-geolocation evidence). The BC may reclaim UK input VAT as a NETP using form VAT65A (per VAT Notice 184) where UK VAT is incurred on local costs, provided the BC holds an active UK VAT registration or is covered by a reciprocal refund arrangement. For pure digital services no customs declaration applies; export substantiation rests on the licensing agreement, customer VAT/VIES evidence, IP address data, and payment remittance records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/C21) lodged via the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) - applicable only where IoT hardware accompanies the software; not required for pure remote software delivery
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / certified proof of physical removal from the UK (mandatory within 3 months where goods exports are claimed at 0%)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds (commercial benchmark: 180 days from invoice date)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (for any bundled IoT hardware component)
  • Signed service contract / end-user licence agreement evidencing the remote screen-share and IoT telemetry scope, place of supply, and recipient's country of establishment
  • Customer VAT registration number and VIES certificate (B2B reverse-charge substantiation)

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping regime exists in the UK. Mandatory: Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT - all VAT records must be kept digitally and returns filed through HMRC-recognised MTD-compatible software via the HMRC MTD VAT API (JSON payloads, OAuth 2.0 authentication). E-invoice format is not prescribed; PDF and structured e-invoices are acceptable. Invoice content must satisfy VATA 1994 Sch 6: full VAT invoice showing supplier name/address/VAT registration number, invoice date, tax point, description of services, quantity, net amount, VAT rate, and VAT payable; simplified invoices are permitted for amounts up to GBP 250. Invoices must be issued within 14 days after the tax point. No HMRC pre-approval or fiscal stamping of invoices is required.

HMRC may claw back improperly claimed zero-rating by assessing 20% VAT under VATA 1994 s.73 plus statutory interest (Bank of England base rate + 2.5%), with a 4-year assessment window extended to 20 years for fraud or concealment. Civil penalties: failure-to-notify penalties (fixed and daily, VATA 1994 s.71); inaccurate VAT return penalties under FA 2007 Sch 24 - up to 30% for careless errors, 70% for deliberate errors, and 100% for deliberate and concealed errors. Criminal exposure: evasion offences under VATA 1994 s.72 - up to 7 years' imprisonment and an unlimited fine; fraudulent export/MTIC arrangements additionally trigger asset seizure and account freezes under POCA 2002. For digital services, HMRC systematically uses IP-geolocation and payment-processor data from Stripe/Paddle to recharacterise B2C supplies, back-register the BC for up to 4 years, and assess 20% VAT plus interest; deliberate non-disclosure attracts the 100% Sch 24 penalty and inclusion in HMRC's public 'deliberate defaulters' list. Note: the 180-day repatriation benchmark is a financing/compliance convention, not a UK statutory deadline; prolonged offshore retention of export proceeds may nonetheless be scrutinised in transfer-pricing and diverted-profits-tax reviews.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The UK maintains a fully liberalised capital and current account regime. GBP, USD, and all major currencies are freely convertible for trade, investment, dividend repatriation, and intercompany financing, with no prior authorisation, licensing, quantity restriction, or profit-remittance hurdle. A UK operating entity owned by a BVI Business Company may remit dividends, loan repayments, and royalties without Bank of England consent. Residual constraints arise only through targeted sanctions administered by the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation and discretionary call-in powers under the National Security and Investment Act 2021, neither of which impedes routine software-export revenue conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. UK law imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation on export receipts. SaaS subscriptions and remote-access licence revenues settled in USD through Stripe or Paddle may be retained indefinitely in multi-currency accounts or converted to GBP or EUR at the entity's discretion. The only obligations are commercial record-keeping under the Companies Act 2006, HMRC corporation-tax reporting, and, where the entity provides payment services, safeguarding duties under the Payment Services Regulations 2017. No Bank of England or HM Revenue and Customs foreign-exchange declaration applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority) as the central banking and bank-supervision authority, with HM Treasury retaining ultimate responsibility for exchange-control and financial-sanctions policy and the Financial Conduct Authority regulating banking, payment, and e-money conduct. The United Kingdom abolished general exchange controls with effect from 24 October 1979 and operates no dedicated foreign-exchange administration comparable to a SAFE-style regulator.

Stripe Fit: High

High viability, subject to FCA licensing and safeguarding constraints. Stripe (Stripe Payments UK Ltd, FCA-authorised under the Payment Services Regulations 2017) and Paddle (UK merchant-of-record authorised as a payments institution) are fully viable as acquiring and settlement rails for a remote-desktop software vendor with a BVI ultimate parent. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from the BVI parent to the UK operating entity is routed as equity subscription or shareholder loan.
