Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the Bermuda Exempted Company holds a statutory exemption from Bermuda income tax, capital gains tax, and withholding tax until 31 December 2035. In Gibraltar the company is classified as a non-resident entity unless central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, in which case Gibraltar-source trading profits become assessable under the Income Tax Act 2010 at the standard 10% company rate. Profits of a UK permanent establishment are outside the Bermuda exemption and may be subject to UK corporation tax (19-25%) or UK diverted profits tax (25%) where applicable. Gibraltar itself grants no exempt-zone status to foreign manufacturing branches.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Bermuda, Gibraltar, or the UK. Available statutory elections and positions: (a) Bermuda exempted-undertaking election via the Certificate of Exemption application (guarantee of no tax until 2035); (b) Gibraltar non-resident company position, established by maintaining central management and control outside Gibraltar (board meetings and key decisions in Bermuda); (c) the Gibraltar Qualifying Company election is closed to new applicants (regime abolished for new entities after 31 December 2016), so no 10% fixed-rate election is available; (d) UK group VAT registration election under VATA 1994 Schedule 1 where the Gibraltar branch and UK-registered entities form a VAT group; and (e) hybrid mismatch offsetting adjustments under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A, which apply mandatorily rather than by election, with an OECD-compliant offset election available to neutralise any deduction/non-inclusion mismatch caused by the Bermuda tax-exempt status.

Gibraltar: no turnover threshold exists; every commercial importer/exporter, including the non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company, must be registered with H.M. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar (effective 0% export outcome): import duty on raw materials is relieved or repaid by drawback under the Customs Duties Act 2018 where the finished canned boba tapioca pearls (HS 1903, tapioca preparations) are exported from Gibraltar to a destination outside Gibraltar, provided the duty-paid raw materials are traced in stock records and the finished goods leave Gibraltar within the prescribed statutory period. United Kingdom (where export zero-rating is claimed): VATA 1994 s.30 and SI 1995/2518 require the supplier to export the goods to a place outside the UK VAT territory, transfer ownership to an overseas customer before departure, and retain robust proof of export and payment for six years, with physical export normally required within three months of the time of supply. Bermuda: no VAT or GST exists, so there is no zero-rating claim; the outward shipment records and the Certificate of Exemption evidence the complete absence of Bermuda indirect tax.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates a fully convertible currency regime on both current and capital accounts. No exchange controls, no prior-approval requirements, and no documentary barriers apply to capital repatriation, profit remittance, dividend distribution, or liquidation proceeds of a non-resident Bermuda Exempted Company. The GIP is pegged at par to GBP, and banks freely denominate accounts and settle in GIP, GBP, USD, EUR, and CNY/CNH at spot and forward rates. The only administrative burden is statistical: transfers touching a UK clearing system above GBP 50,000 must be coded into the Bank of England Balance of Payments (Green Book) return; no approval is triggered. Withholding tax on outbound dividends is zero under Gibraltar law for non-residents, and the Bermuda Exempted Company enjoys an exempt-status certificate that is recognized as tax-transparent at the treasury level.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline applies in Gibraltar or the wider UK. Export receipts for canned boba tapioca pearls may be retained in any convertible currency, in any jurisdiction, indefinitely. For SWIFT MT103 clearing, the remitting bank will require proof-of-shipment evidence (Bill of Lading, CMR, or air waybill) and the commercial invoice to record the transaction under the UK/Gibraltar Balance of Payments statistical regime; no central-bank conversion form is required. A Bermudian Exempted Company may therefore maintain its USD/EUR receivable pools offshore without conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the authorized prudential and conduct regulator for all banks, e-money institutions and payment services. Gibraltar pound (GIP) note issuance is the statutory function of the Government of Gibraltar Ministry of Finance under the Currency Notes Act 1934. Monetary stability is anchored to the Bank of England (United Kingdom) through the fixed 1:1 GIP/GBP peg and the UK sovereignty umbrella; there is no separate Gibraltar central bank or exchange-control authority.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity injections route as SWIFT MT103/TT wire from the shareholder's home bank into the Gibraltar International Bank or Banco Santander operational account, using ISO 20022 purpose code ECNR for equity capital or QUES for quasi-equity shareholder loans; the MT103 field 50K must present the corporate name and account number exactly as held in KYC records. Verification bundle consists of: (i) notarized board resolution authorizing the contribution, certified by the Bermuda registered agent; (ii) source-of-funds pack for the natural-person UBO; (iii) Certificate of Good Standing from the Bermuda Registrar of Companies issued within 30 days; and (iv) the bank's compliance risk assessment scoring. Contributions exceeding GBP 50,000 are logged on the Bank of England Green Book BOP return with transaction code D.4 (portfolio/equity), and a capital-account memorandum is created on the bank's records. No waiting period applies after the compliance sign-off, but the statutory license requirement for canned boba manufacturing must be evidenced before escrow release.Trade settlements execute through three sanctioned clearing rails: (1) Irrevocable Confirmed Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) opened by the Gibraltar issuing bank in favor of tapioca starch and aseptic-equipment suppliers, governed by UCP600, against presentation of the full clean-on-board bill of lading, phytosanitary certificate, ISO 22000 certificate, certificate of analysis, and the aseptic-tubing validation record; confirmation by a US or EU correspondent bank protects against supplier-country risk. (2) SWIFT MT103 open-account payments for repeat suppliers below the bank's credit threshold, tracked end-to-end via SWIFT GPI; the beneficiary must be pre-vetted and the payment narrative must include the trade invoice number and the Incoterms rule. (3) Standby Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT760) and documentary collections (MT707) for the purchase of high-value aseptic filling machinery, with an advance-payment guarantee in favor of the machinery supplier. All disbursements must be denominated in GIP/GBP or matched to the invoicing currency; USD and EUR disbursements trigger an automatic spot conversion and dual-record BOP reporting.Deliverable outright FX forward on GIP/GBP versus USD, EUR, and CNH, executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with Credit Support Annex (CSA) or a plain-documentation forward FX agreement with the GFSC-licensed Tier-1 bank; tenors up to 12 months.FX swap (combined spot and forward) for short-term liquidity matching between USD raw-material payables and EUR/USD or GBP/USD receivable conversion cycles.Cross-currency interest rate swap (CCIRS) for the USD-denominated aseptic-filling equipment lease, swapping USD floating-rate liability into GIP/GBP fixed-rate exposure.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) - permitted only for offshore currencies where no onshore deliverable market is accessible (e.g., CNH if the counterparty cannot settle into mainland CNY); governed by a separate ISDA NDF master confirmation.European-style vanilla currency options (put and call) covering forecast USD and EUR trade receivables, premium paid from the operational account; speculative short option positions are prohibited under MiFID II and the GFSC conduct-of-business rules.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Moderate-Low feasibility for the core treasury mandate. Gibraltar-licensed EMIs operate under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the E-Money Regulations 2023, but post-Brexit they have lost EEA passporting into the UK and are not SWIFT-member banks; they cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, cannot access CBPR+/MT700 rails, and cannot offer GIP-denominated clearing. For the canned boba manufacturing scope, EMIs such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, or Airwallex are viable only for operational sub-threshold payments (payroll, software subscriptions, supplier advances below GBP 10,000). All trade settlement, LC operations, and FX hedging must be executed through GFSC-licensed, SWIFT-member banks. Overall rating: Medium for operational narrow-use, Low for cross-border trade-clearing rails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 250,000 per controlled cross-border transaction or GIP 1,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions per accounting period; exceeding these thresholds requires mandatory Local File and, where group turnover exceeds GIP 50 million, Master File preparation.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance (Requirements) Act 2020 applies to tax-resident companies and to non-resident companies maintaining a Gibraltar permanent establishment. The manufacture of canned boba tapioca pearls is a relevant activity requiring adequate staff, premises, and core income-generating activities in Gibraltar. Failure to satisfy the economic substance test exposes the entity to penalties, compulsory information exchange, and denial of tax benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Concealment of Gibraltar Tax Liability

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2010, s.120, a penalty of up to GIP 100,000 or 100% of the tax evaded, compounded with interest at 8% per annum and criminal prosecution in the Supreme Court.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for civil restitution, subjected to travel bans restricting exit from Gibraltar, disqualified from directorship for up to 10 years, and sentenced to a maximum of 5 years imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: GIP 50,000 penalty plus GIP 2,500 per day for continued non-compliance; deregistration from the Gibraltar Companies Register; mandatory exchange of beneficial ownership information with foreign tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors face individual fines of GIP 10,000 and disqualification from managing any Gibraltar company for 3 years.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Under the Companies Act 2014 (as amended) and AML/CFT legislation, fines up to GIP 500,000 and custodial penalties up to 5 years for deliberate concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors are subject to personal fines, disqualification orders, and confiscation orders over assets obtained through or associated with money laundering.
