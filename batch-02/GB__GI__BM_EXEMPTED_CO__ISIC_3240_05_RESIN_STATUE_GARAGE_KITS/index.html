Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar - Income Tax Office (ITO) and the Customs and Excise Department; online portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation and https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/customs

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a Gibraltar presence (including a branch or subsidiary of a Bermuda Exempted Company) must apply to the Gibraltar Companies Registry for incorporation or registration, obtain a Gibraltar company number, and then register with the Income Tax Office for tax purposes. The ITO issues a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) upon submission of the application for tax residency/registration (Form IT1 or equivalent) together with the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, and proof of registered office. Entities that are not tax residents but carry on a taxable activity in Gibraltar are also required to register for tax and obtain a TIN. For VAT purposes, registration is made with the Customs and Excise Department using the GRA/VAT registration application, and the VAT registration number is issued electronically.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar maintains a territorial and exempt regime for Qualifying (Category 2) Companies and certain offshore entities; however, a Bermuda Exempted Company conducting commercial activity through a Gibraltar branch may irrevocably elect to be treated as a Gibraltar tax resident under the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended), subject to prior approval by the Income Tax Office. Alternatively, the entity may elect to remain non-resident and claim exemption from Gibraltar tax on foreign-source income and passive income under the territorial basis, while ensuring compliance with the EU/Gibraltar anti-hybrid rules and the UK-Gibraltar double taxation arrangement. No check-the-box election equivalent to US Subchapter S exists; entity classification is determined by the Companies Act and Income Tax Act, and hybrid mismatch elections are limited to aligning the entity's treatment with the transparent/opaque classification of the parent jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

GST (Gibraltar Value Added Tax under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance; aligned with EU VAT principles for goods and services)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A - Gibraltar imposes no general VAT/GST on local supplies; registration is not triggered by turnover. However, if the entity voluntarily registers for similar fiscal purposes or engages in UK VAT-distance-selling, a UK VAT registration threshold applies (currently £90,000). For goods exported from Gibraltar to the UK/EU, the UK/EU VAT registration thresholds and distance selling thresholds may apply to the Gibraltar entity's cross-border sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar does not levy VAT on exports; thus exports of polyresin statue garage kits by a Gibraltar-registered entity are treated as outside the scope of Gibraltar taxation. To benefit from zero-rating in the destination jurisdiction (e.g., UK/EU) where applicable, the entity must demonstrate that the goods are exported outside the jurisdiction of the importing VAT territory and that the buyer is a taxable person or that the distance-selling conditions are met. For UK VAT purposes, the Gibraltar entity claiming zero-rating on export supplies must hold valid evidence of export and satisfy the conditions in section 30 of the VAT Act 1994 and the VAT (Penalty) Regulations. Off-shore services supplied to non-resident businesses (e.g., design prototyping services related ISIC 3240) are zero-rated under the place-of-supply rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar Customs single administrative document or electronic customs declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing physical shipment of resin statue goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank confirmation of receipt of export proceeds in the entity's Gibraltar or Bermuda bank account)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and purchase order referencing the ISIC 3240 scope and the specific resin statue garage kit series
  • Proof of origin (certificate of origin if claiming preferential tariff treatment)

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance for local supplies. Invoices must comply with the Invoice and Receipt requirements set out in the Customs and Excise Duties (Records and Returns) regulations and must be retained in Gibraltar or in an accessible electronic format for at least six years. For UK/EU cross-border sales of polyresin statue kits, the UK MTD (Making Tax Digital) framework requires digital record keeping and electronic submission of VAT returns if the entity registers for UK VAT; EU actor requirements (SAP/e-invoicing) apply only if the entity operates from an EU member state, which is not the case for a Gibraltar-based Bermuda Exempted Company.

The Gibraltar Customs and Excise Department and the Income Tax Office may conduct audits of export transactions. If an exporter claims zero-rating or export exemption without genuine physical export or with false customs declarations, the entity is liable to: (i) repayment/assessment of the uncollected tax (where UK VAT is in issue), (ii) fines up to 100% of the tax amount or £3,000 per failure for customs record-keeping failures under Gibraltar regulations, (iii) criminal sanctions for fraudulent evasion of customs duty or tax (up to 7 years imprisonment and unlimited fines under the Customs and Excise Offences Act), and (iv) retrospective clawback of any tax relief or refund for up to six years from the date of the irregularity. The Gibraltar tax authority may also impose a civil penalty of up to £1,000 for each failure to preserve export evidence, and the Bermuda parent company may face anti-money-laundering scrutiny if the FX repatriation deadline is not met, potentially triggering a suspicious activity report and regulatory review by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The combined Bermuda/Gibraltar structure faces no material convertibility barrier. Gibraltar operates a fully open capital and current account regime: GIP is pegged 1:1 to GBP and freely convertible; multi-currency accounts (GBP, USD, EUR) are standard; dividends, interest, and royalties may be remitted without Gibraltarian withholding tax or FX permission, subject to GFSC customer due diligence and sanctions screening under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar) and Sanctions Act 2019 (Gibraltar). The Bermuda entity, when designated non-resident for exchange-control purposes, may freely hold and deploy USD and any other foreign currency with no surrender obligation. Residual friction is documentary rather than regulatory: banks will require proof of source of funds, source of wealth, and economic substance for a tax-exempt Bermuda vehicle; UK CFC rules may also be engaged if UK-resident individuals hold the company. Additionally, if the Bermuda exempted company's central management and control is exercised from Gibraltar, it risks Gibraltar tax residence at 10%; governance planning must keep statutory board meetings and strategic decision-making outside the UK and Gibraltar.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion rules apply to either leg of the structure. Bermuda: exempted companies designated as non-resident are expressly relieved of the obligation to surrender or convert export/foreign-currency proceeds into Bermuda dollars. Gibraltar: there is no requirement to convert export proceeds, pre-order deposits, or retail settlements into GIP/GBP; all receipts may remain in original currency. The only mandatory obligations are transactional record keeping for five years and threshold-based suspicious transaction reporting under the GFSC AML/CFT framework.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the integrated financial regulator and monetary supervisory authority for Gibraltar, overseeing banking, e-money, payments and anti-money-laundering compliance; the Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at parity with GBP under the Currency Notes Act 1934, forming an informal currency board arrangement with the Bank of England. The Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) remains the exchange-control authority for the Bermuda Exempted Company in its home jurisdiction.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a secondary collection and settlement rail, but LOW as the bank of record. FCA/GFSC-regulated EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut Business) will generally onboard a Bermuda exempted company where clean corporate documentation is supplied, and can receive Stripe and PayPal payout flows. Legal and operational constraints: (i) EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot provide overdrafts, letter-of-credit facilities, or FX limit/hedging lines; (ii) FCA safeguarding rules require client funds to be ring-fenced in UK credit institutions, and there is no FSCS depositor protection; (iii) EMIs often cap monthly volumes and enforce enhanced due diligence on tax-exempt offshore vehicles, which may create settlement latency; (iv) use of UK EMIs may require a UK business address or a Gibraltar/UK introducer. Recommended structure: Stripe and PayPal settle daily into a Wise Business or GFSC-regulated e-money account, or directly into the Gibraltar bank, with a daily zero-balance treasury sweep to Gibraltar International Bank or Xapo Bank; never warehouse commercial float in the EMI.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a secondary collection and settlement rail, but LOW as the bank of record. FCA/GFSC-regulated EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut Business) will generally onboard a Bermuda exempted company where clean corporate documentation is supplied, and can receive Stripe and PayPal payout flows. Legal and operational constraints: (i) EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot provide overdrafts, letter-of-credit facilities, or FX limit/hedging lines; (ii) FCA safeguarding rules require client funds to be ring-fenced in UK credit institutions, and there is no FSCS depositor protection; (iii) EMIs often cap monthly volumes and enforce enhanced due diligence on tax-exempt offshore vehicles, which may create settlement latency; (iv) use of UK EMIs may require a UK business address or a Gibraltar/UK introducer. Recommended structure: Stripe and PayPal settle daily into a Wise Business or GFSC-regulated e-money account, or directly into the Gibraltar bank, with a daily zero-balance treasury sweep to Gibraltar International Bank or Xapo Bank; never warehouse commercial float in the EMI.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Gibraltar Local File is required where the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions (raw polymer resin purchases from the Bermuda-affiliated supplier, management fees to the Bermuda parent, IP licences to the Gibraltar branch, and intercompany financing) exceeds GBP 2,000,000 in a single accounting period; a Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above GBP 750,000,000. Below the Local File threshold, contemporaneous economic benchmarking must still be retained to justify the arm's length outcome.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations 2019 (ESR) apply to every Gibraltar-registered entity that performs a relevant activity, including distribution and service centres, financing, leasing, and intellectual property holding. A Bermuda Exempted Company managed and controlled in Bermuda but operating commercially from Gibraltar may claim non-residence in Gibraltar; it must then file an annual ESR return, demonstrate that core income generating activities (CIGA) are performed in Bermuda (substance: physical premises, full-time employees, strategic meetings), and either meet the local CIGA test in Gibraltar or rely on the no-tax-residence rule. Failure to satisfy ESR results in financial penalties from GIP 10,000 to GIP 100,000, and the Registrar will compulsorily exchange the file with HM Revenue & Customs and the Bermuda Registrar.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Under-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Tax-geared penalty of up to 200% of the evaded tax; civil fine up to GIP 200,000; statutory interest at 8% per annum; criminal prosecution under the Gibraltar Criminal Justice (Revenue Offences) framework with a maximum custodial sentence of 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign knowingly false returns are personally liable for the penalty amount and the evaded tax; courts may issue disqualification orders for up to 15 years, travel bans, and asset freezing orders; on conviction, directors face imprisonment of up to 7 years and personal criminal fines.
Shell Company / Nominee Structure Abuse

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar ESR penalty regime: initial penalty of GIP 10,000, escalating to GIP 100,000 for continued non-compliance; the Commissioner may strike the Gibraltar branch registration off the register and report the arrangement to HMRC, the Bermuda Registrar of Companies, and EU financial intelligence units as a high-risk cross-border structure.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors face personal civil liability for abuse of office; Gibraltar courts will pierce the corporate veil to render directors personally liable for all unpaid Gibraltar tax, penalties, and interest; travel bans under the Proceeds of Crime Act and director disqualification orders are routinely imposed.
AML/CFT Deficiencies on Digital Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and Sanctions Act 2019 of up to GIP 500,000; forfeiture of the gross proceeds collected through Stripe and PayPal; referral to the Gibraltar Commission for Combating Crime and Corruption; criminal prosecution for money laundering with imprisonment of up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and any designated compliance officer are personally exposed to confiscation orders under POCA 2015, individual fines up to GIP 100,000, imprisonment up to 14 years, and restriction orders preventing departure from Gibraltar pending asset tracing.
Tax Residence Mismatch / Permanent Establishment Fraud

Statutory sanction: On audit, the Gibraltar Income Tax Office will reassert chargeability at 12.5% on the worldwide profits of the resin statue activities, add a penalty of up to 100% of the evaded tax, charge statutory interest, and compel re-filing of six prior years of returns, with an extended look-back period in cases involving deliberate concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors who certified the false non-residence declarations are jointly and severally liable for the assessed tax; serious cases trigger criminal prosecution, travel bans, seizure of personal assets, disgorgement of dividends paid while the company was knowingly non-compliant, and piercing of the corporate veil.
