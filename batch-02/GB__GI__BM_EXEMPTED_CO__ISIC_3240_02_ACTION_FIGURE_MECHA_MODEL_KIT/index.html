Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, HM Government of Gibraltar, Income Tax Office, 6th Floor, Meridian House, 3 Harbour Walk, Gibraltar; online portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation

E-government filing portal

For the Bermuda Exempted Company ('BM_EXEMPTED_CO') wishing to operate in Gibraltar, the foreign company must first complete Form F with the Companies House of Gibraltar to register as an overseas company. After obtaining the Gibraltar Company Number, it must apply to the Income Tax Office for a Tax Reference Number (TRN) using the Composite Tax Registration Form for non-resident companies. Filing may be done electronically via the Integrated Tax System (ITS) portal, and requires certified constitutional documents, certificate of incorporation from Bermuda, certificate of good standing, evidence of registered office in Bermuda, and passport copies of directors. Once registered, the TRN is used for all income tax filings and corresponds with the entity's tax status as a non-resident or managed-and-controlled resident company.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box election comparable to US Subchapter K. Instead, classification follows the territoriality principle under the Income Tax Act 2010. A Bermuda Exempted Company that keeps central management and control outside Gibraltar will be treated as non-resident for Gibraltar tax purposes, and its offshore income will remain outside the charge to Gibraltar tax. If central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, the company becomes resident and subject to tax only on locally accruing or arising income. No hybrid mismatch election exists; however, the entity's Bermuda exempt status is recognised, and existing exemptions may be preserved only where expressly grandfathered by transitional provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

VAT

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT or GST is levied in Gibraltar. The territory maintains a distinct import and excise regime. There is no general consumer tax registration threshold. However, persons importing alcoholic beverages, tobacco, petroleum oils and other dutiable goods into Gibraltar must register with Her Majesty's Customs (Gibraltar) as importers under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance. For this purpose, there is no turnover threshold; registration is mandatory upon importation of dutiable goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

As Gibraltar imposes no VAT, there is no VAT zero-rating mechanism in the traditional sense. Export of goods from Gibraltar is outside the scope of Gibraltar's import purchase tax and excise duties. To preserve this treatment, the exporter must prove that goods physically departed Gibraltar and were not consumed locally. Offshore services supplied to non-resident recipients, including the realm of injection-moulded toy assembly and technical design services, are treated as territorial scope income and are not assessed to Gibraltar tax. Therefore, no input VAT refund exists because no output VAT is charged.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Gibraltar has not introduced a real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate. Import/export declarations must be filed electronically through the Customs National System (CNS) integrated with the UK-Gibraltar Single Trade Window and the EU Import Control System (ICS2) where applicable. Digital fiscal stamping is required for alcohol and tobacco products. There is no continuous transaction control (CTC) API obligation for standard commercial invoices.

Fraudulent export declarations or misrepresentation intended to obtain exemption from duty trigger penalties under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance (Cap. 116) and the Importation and Exportation Ordinance. Civil penalties range from outright forfeiture of goods, duty evaded up to three times, and monetary fines of GBP 500 to GBP 1,000 per transaction. Criminal prosecution may lead to imprisonment of up to seven years. HM Customs may retrospectively claw back duty-free treatment and disallow export records if the specified documentation or the 180-day repatriation requirement is not fulfilled. The Complete Inward Remittance Certificate is strictly scrutinised during audit, and missing certificates are treated as prima facie evidence that the export proceeds were not repatriated as declared.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full current and capital account convertibility. The Gibraltar pound is pegged to the British pound sterling at par, and there are no exchange controls, no central bank approval requirements, and no restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds for a non-resident-owned Bermuda Exempted Company operating from Gibraltar.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds, no mandatory conversion of foreign currency into GBP/GIP, and no obligation to hold a minimum balance in a Gibraltar-denominated account. Exporters may retain 100% of receipts in freely convertible foreign currency accounts at any GFSC-licensed bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for the sterling backing of the Gibraltar pound), HM Government of Gibraltar (Gibraltar pound issuance), and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the banking conduct and AML/CTF supervisor.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for offshore digital EMIs. A Gibraltar-licensed electronic money institution is viable, but foreign (e.g., UK or EU) EMIs cannot passport automatically into Gibraltar post-Brexit. Any fintech/EMI must hold a locally issued payment-services or e-money authorisation from the GFSC, or operate through a GFSC-licensed bank with a fully compliant agent arrangement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

10% flat CIT (Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010); no municipal surcharges. Large MNE groups within OECD Pillar 2 may incur a 15% jurisdictional top-up tax on residual excess profit.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% – Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividends under the Income Tax Act 2010; distributions are paid gross.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 12 months after the end of the basis period (calendar year for Gibraltar); a company with a 31 December year-end must file no later than 31 December of the following year. Payment of self-assessed tax is due 9 months after year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: EUR 750,000,000 (GIP equivalent) consolidated group revenue triggers mandatory Country-by-Country Report filing; no separate Local File/Master File statutory threshold exists in Gibraltar, but contemporaneous TP documentation must be produced on ITO notice for any controlled transaction involving the Bermuda Exempted Company.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar ESR (Taxation Economic Substance Act 2019): relevant activities (financing/leasing, HQ, distribution/service centres, IP) require premises, full-time employees, expenditure and board management in Gibraltar. Manufacturing of toy model kits is not per se 'relevant', but the distribution service-centre and IP elements of this supply chain are captured; the Bermuda Exempted Co must also file Bermuda ESR declarations. Failure exposes the structure to shell-company countermeasures under EU and OECD listings.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Tax-geared civil penalty up to 100% of the additional tax (200% for concealment), interest, and criminal prosecution: fines plus imprisonment up to 7 years on indictment under the Income Tax Act 2010.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns face joint and several liability for restitution of the tax loss, disqualification, and custodial sentences; travel bans may be imposed pending prosecution.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR penalties up to GIP 100,000 per failure, daily continuing penalties, exchange of information with UK/EU tax authorities, and compulsory winding-up or strike-off of the Gibraltar registration.

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified for up to 15 years, made personally liable for debts incurred after the strike-off, and placed on immigration watchlists where AML-linked substance failures are identified.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Commissioner's arm's-length reallocation of profit, additional 10% CIT, interest at 4% plus base rate, and penalties up to 100% of the under-declared tax; CbCR non-filing penalties for groups above threshold.

Director personal liability: Where the pricing arrangement is a sham or designed to defraud, directors face piercing-of-the-veil claims, disgorgement of diverted profits, and criminal penalties for fraudulent trading.
AML / Money Laundering / Sanctions

Statutory sanction: Money-laundering offenses under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015: up to 14 years' imprisonment and unlimited fines; GFSC AML enforcement penalties; freezing and forfeiture of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors personally prosecutable for failure to submit Suspicious Activity Reports and for willful blindness; civil recovery orders, travel bans, and extradition within the UK's Gibraltar framework follow.
UK CFC / Place of Effective Management (POEM)

Statutory sanction: UK Corporation Tax at 25% on worldwide profits, Diverted Profits Tax at 25% on the shifted profits (Finance Act 2015), plus interest and penalties; loss of Gibraltar residence and all treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: UK directors jointly liable for filing failures; HMRC may pursue civil penalties and disqualification; migration of management back to Gibraltar or re-domiciliation of the structure is the only mitigation.
