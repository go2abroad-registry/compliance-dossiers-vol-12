Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

His Majesty's Revenue and Customs (HMRC); digital portal: HMRC Online Services (https://www.tax.service.gov.uk/) and Companies House for entity registration

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity (including a BVI Business Company) with a UK VAT registration obligation must apply for a UK VAT number via form VAT1 (or VAT1G for groups/non-established taxable persons) through HMRC's Online Services. Before VAT registration, the foreign entity should obtain a UK Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) if it has UK source income or corporation tax liabilities; for VAT-only purposes, HMRC issues a separate VAT registration number. The application must provide entity constitutional documents, evidence of economic activity in the UK, UK establishment details (if any), and a UK or EEA address for correspondence unless an agent is appointed. HMRC must issue the VAT registration certificate within 30 days of a complete application, and the effective date of registration is generally the date when the taxable turnover threshold was exceeded or, for voluntary registration, the date requested.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (as to BVI BC's BVI-source income and passive worldwide income; however, UK-indirect-tax nexus applies separately based on UK VAT registration thresholds and the place-of-supply rules for digital services). — A non-resident entity (including a BVI Business Company) with a UK VAT registration obligation must apply for a UK VAT number via form VAT1 (or VAT1G for groups/non-established taxable persons) through HMRC's Online Services. Before VAT registration, the foreign entity should obtain a UK Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) if it has UK source income or corporation tax liabilities; for VAT-only purposes, HMRC issues a separate VAT registration number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (as to BVI BC's BVI-source income and passive worldwide income; however, UK-indirect-tax nexus applies separately based on UK VAT registration thresholds and the place-of-supply rules for digital services).

Available election forms

No UK general check-the-box election exists for foreign entities; a BVI BC may elect to be treated as a 'non-established taxable person' for UK VAT if it has no UK establishment, or may apply for VAT group registration under the Value Added Tax Act 1994, Schedule 1, if it has a UK establishment with a UK-established entity. Corporation Tax (if a PE arises in the UK) follows the OECD Model Tax Convention attribution rules and UK CTA 2010 Part 3; no hybrid mismatch election is available, but UK anti-hybrid rules (TIOPA 2010 Part 6A) may apply to instrument or entity mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 90,000 (UK taxable turnover threshold for established businesses); for non-established overseas businesses, there is no threshold and registration is required from the first supply of UK taxable services/goods, subject to the place-of-supply rules for Business-to-Consumer digital services under the Overseas Business Scheme.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

UK VAT zero-rating applies to exports of goods if (1) the exporter retains evidence of export within specified time limits (usually 3 months from the time of supply; extended cases allowed), (2) the goods are supplied to a customer outside the UK and are exported from the UK, and (3) the supplier obtains and preserves 'valid evidence of export' as required by VAT Notice 703 (e.g., shipping documents, customs entries, or authenticated commercial documents). For digital services (including game sales via Steam/PlayStation online stores), exports of electronically supplied services to non-UK recipients are generally 'outside the scope' of UK VAT rather than zero-rated if the place of supply is outside the UK; for B2C supplies of digital services to UK consumers, the UK place of supply rule applies and UK VAT is chargeable at 20%, with the overseas seller using the VAT Mini One Stop Shop (MOSS) or UK non-established taxable person registration. Offshore supplies of digital games to non-UK consumers do not enjoy a refund mechanism because no UK VAT is chargeable; input VAT recovery is available under the 13th Directive procedure for non-established businesses only if the supplies are taxable UK supplies.

  • Bill of Lading or airway bill / consignment note
  • Proof of export (carrier's certificate, shipping note, or HM Revenue & Customs export receipt)
  • Commercial invoice and sales contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (only where separately required by banking or anti-money-laundering controls; not a statutory VAT evidence requirement)

No real-time fiscal clearance or mandatory e-invoicing clearance regime (as of 2026); taxpayers may use standard VAT invoicing. HMRC operates Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT requiring digital record-keeping and submission of VAT returns via functional compatible software (API-based) for VAT-registered businesses with taxable turnover above GBP 10,000, with mandatory MTD for all VAT-registered businesses from April 2022. There is no digital stamping; MTD APIs (HMRC VAT API v1.0) transmit nine-box VAT return data to HMRC with a 'declaration' timestamp; invoice stamping is not required.

HMRC may deny zero-rating and assess VAT at 20% plus interest if the exporter fails to obtain valid evidence of export within the prescribed period. Penalties include: (i) a VAT evasion civil penalty of up to 100% of the VAT evaded for dishonest conduct (Finance Act 2007, Schedule 24), (ii) failure-to-notify penalties of up to 100% of the tax (for deliberate non-notification), (iii) accuracy penalties up to 100% for careless/deliberate inaccuracies (Schedule 24 FA 2007), (iv) late submission penalties GBP 5-15 per day under the default surcharge regime (replaced by points-based late payment regime), and (v) criminal prosecution under the VAT Act 1994 Section 72 and the Fraud Act 2006 for fraudulent evasion, carrying imprisonment up to 7 years and unlimited fines. HMRC has retrospective assessment powers of up to 20 years for deliberate non-disclosure (TMA 1970, Schedule 1A), and may issue assessments for four years for ordinary carelessness or six years for careless errors. Subject to the rights of third-party ownership, HMRC may also issue a joint and several liability notice to directors or shadow directors under Finance Act 2007, Schedule 24, paragraph 17C.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom maintains full and unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital accounts under the IMF Article VIII obligations; there are no exchange controls, no requirement for HM Treasury approval on inbound equity injections, no restrictions on dividend/royalty/profit remittances, and no obligation to channel transactions through a licensed domestic bank, though banks apply standard OFSI sanctions screening and source-of-funds verification; the 1979 abolition of UK exchange controls plus the Code for Capital Movements under OECD liberalisation codes ensure free capital movement within OECD and EU (post-Brexit, a unilateral liberal regime remains)

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline, no minimum mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to surrender export proceeds into GBP; the BVI company may retain, bank, and settle 100% of Steam/PlayStation sales proceeds in USD or any freely convertible currency; for statistical purposes HMRC may record the BOP classification, but no haircut, conversion, or central bank settlement window applies

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority) in conjunction with HM Treasury (macro-financial policy) and the Financial Conduct Authority (bank conduct and AML supervision); exchange-control and sanctions enforcement is administered by HM Treasury's Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI)
