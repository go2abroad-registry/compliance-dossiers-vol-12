Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC); online portal: GOV.UK (www.gov.uk/topic/business-tax)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (including a BVI Business Company) without a UK establishment may be required to register for UK corporation tax only if it carries on a trade of property income in the UK through a permanent establishment. For VAT, a non-UK business must register for VAT (and receive a UK VAT registration number) if it supplies taxable goods or services in the UK and its UK taxable turnover exceeds the VAT registration threshold, or if it supplies digital services to UK consumers under the VAT Mini One Stop Shop scheme. Registration is performed online via HMRC's 'Register for VAT' service, or by completing form VAT1 and posting it to the HMRC VAT registration unit. The entity must provide proof of identity of directors and beneficial owners, evidence of business activity (e.g. EOR client contracts and payroll records), a UK postal address or agent details, and bank account information. A UK establishment address may be required for certain registrations; where none exists, a tax representative may be required for non-established taxable persons under certain circumstances. For PAYE, if the BVI BC acts as an employer with UK employees or a UK payroll, it must register as an employer and obtain a PAYE reference number before the first payday, using the HMRC 'Register as an employer' service.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

For UK corporation tax, no 'check-the-box' election exists; UK domestic entities are taxed at entity level on worldwide profits, subject to double tax relief. A non-UK resident company (including a BVI BC) is taxed only on UK-source income and gains attributable to a UK permanent establishment. The entity may elect to be treated as a 'non-UK resident company' under CTA 2009 s.5 and may claim relief under the UK's double taxation treaties. For UK VAT, a BVI BC may register under the 'non-established taxable person' procedure. Under the UK's hybrid mismatch rules (TIOPA 2010 Part 6), an entity may make an election to adjust the treatment of a hybrid entity or to bring into account amounts under the 'taxpayer choice' provisions; such elections are notified to HMRC in the company tax return or by separate election forms, and may be irrevocable. No general pass-through election is available for a BVI BC for UK tax purposes; a foreign entity is not automatically treated as transparent.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£90,000 (UK taxable turnover over a 12-month rolling period; reduced to £0 for non-established taxable persons supplying digital services to UK consumers under the UK VAT MOSS scheme and for certain distance selling supplies).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from the UK qualify for VAT zero-rating if the supplier retains evidence that goods have been removed from the UK to a destination outside the UK (or, in Northern Ireland, to the EU) within specified time limits and the goods are supplied to a person who is neither in the UK nor a UK-established taxable person, in accordance with VATA 1994 s.30(6) and SI 1995/2518. Offshore services to non-UK business customers are generally outside the scope of UK VAT or subject to the reverse charge where the recipient is in another country, provided the place-of-supply rules are met. For an EOR agency, the supply of personnel to UK third-party clients is subject to UK VAT; zero rating or outside-scope treatment is available only where the staff secondment is supplied to a non-UK business customer or the underlying services are physically performed entirely outside the UK and the recipient is outside the UK.

No mandatory real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance regime currently exists in the UK. VAT invoices may be issued in paper or electronic format, provided all required VAT invoice content is included. Mandatory digital record keeping applies under 'Making Tax Digital for VAT': VAT-registered businesses must maintain digital VAT records and submit VAT returns using HMRC-recognised software via the HMRC Application Programming Interface (API). No digital stamping or pre-clearance is required.

HMRC may conduct export evidence checks and demand repayment of VAT and interest for any zero-rated export where the evidence of removal is insufficient. Penalties for dishonest evasion or fraudulent zero-rating claims may include a penalty of up to 100% of the VAT evaded, daily default penalties, criminal prosecution under VATA 1994 s.72 (unlimited fine and/or imprisonment up to 7 years), and potential liability under the failure-to-prevent-facilitation-of-tax-evasion offences (FA 2017 ss.44-47) with unlimited fines. HMRC can also issue a joint and several liability notice under FA 2019 where the entity knew or should have known of an upstream VAT fraud in the supply chain. Retrospective clawback periods are generally up to 4 years for deliberate inaccuracies, and up to 20 years for intentional non-compliance involving a non-UK matter, in addition to enhanced interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GBP (ISO-4217: GBP) is fully convertible on a freely floating exchange rate basis. The UK maintains no distinction between capital-account and current-account transactions for exchange control purposes; both are completely liberalized. Profit remittance from a UK-managed BVI BC requires no central bank or HM Treasury authorization; the only statutory gates are the UK Companies Act 2006 distributable profits test for dividends and UK tax clearance routines. Residual frictions are limited to OFSI sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering counterparty due diligence, and client-money protection rules under the Conduct of Employment Agencies and Employment Businesses Regulations 2003.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. The UK imposes no mandatory surrender timeline or conversion ratio for export proceeds or service receipts. Following repeal of the Exchange Control Act in 1979, exporters and service providers (including EOR platforms) may retain, hold, or disburse foreign currency indefinitely outside the UK. The only applicable obligations are: (i) tax disclosure to HMRC on worldwide income if UK tax-resident; (ii) OFSI sanctions screening; and (iii) reporting to the ONS for balance-of-payments surveys if transactions exceed GBP 20 million per quarter. No mandatory conversion applies to receipts under ISIC 7810 payroll services.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (central bank and Prudential Regulation Authority supervisor); HM Treasury retains residual powers over exchange control and financial sanctions administration via the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI). The Exchange Control Act 1947 was repealed with full effect by the Exchange Control (Repeal) Act 1979, abolishing all UK foreign exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High viability as a supplementary liquidity rail; low-medium viability as a sole primary banking relationship. FCA-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the E-Money Regulations 2011, such as Wise Business (Wise Payments Ltd), Revolut Business (Revolut Ltd), and Modulr (Modulr FS Ltd), support BVI-incorporated companies subject to enhanced due diligence. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not authorised deposit-takers; client funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts at partner banks (ClearBank, Barclays, Standard Chartered), with no Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS) protection; (ii) transaction volume and value caps apply for cross-border flows; (iii) EMIs must perform OFSI sanctions screening identical to Tier-1 banks; (iv) BVI is treated as a higher-risk offshore jurisdiction, so documentary evidence must include apostilled incorporation documents, beneficial ownership extracts, and economic substance declarations. EMIs are recommended for sub-GBP 500,000 monthly payroll disbursements and multi-currency payouts via local clearing rails (ACH, SEPA, FPS).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: shareholder capital is transmitted from the BVI money manager or shareholder's account via SWIFT MT103 to the UK clearing bank GBP account (sort code and account number). The payment narrative must identify 'Capital Contribution - BVI BC share allotment' or 'Intercompany Loan - agreement dated [date]'. UK law does not mandate statutory purpose codes; banks apply SWIFT field 72 (Sender to Receiver Information) for regulatory classification, and no HM Treasury approval is required. OFSI sanctions screening and MLR 2017 source-of-funds checks apply. For injections above GBP 50,000, the bank will request the evidentiary dossier described above. For operational revenue from non-UK clients, inbound SWIFT MT103 with narrative 'Payment for Global EOR and Contractor Payroll Services' is sufficient; UK banks apply no FX conversion mandate, and funds may remain in the original currency in a multi-currency account. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High viability as a supplementary liquidity rail; low-medium viability as a sole primary banking relationship. FCA-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the E-Money Regulations 2011, such as Wise Business (Wise Payments Ltd), Revolut Business (Revolut Ltd), and Modulr (Modulr FS Ltd), support BVI-incorporated companies subject to enhanced due diligence. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not authorised deposit-takers; client funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts at partner banks (ClearBank, Barclays, Standard Chartered), with no Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS) protection; (ii) transaction volume and value caps apply for cross-border flows; (iii) EMIs must perform OFSI sanctions screening identical to Tier-1 banks; (iv) BVI is treated as a higher-risk offshore jurisdiction, so documentary evidence must include apostilled incorporation documents, beneficial ownership extracts, and economic substance declarations. EMIs are recommended for sub-GBP 500,000 monthly payroll disbursements and multi-currency payouts via local clearing rails (ACH, SEPA, FPS).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed published UK statutory threshold for a Local or Master File; HMRC expects OECD-aligned transfer pricing documentation for cross-border controlled transactions exceeding GBP 1,000,000. Mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting applies where a UK-parented group has consolidated group revenue of GBP 750,000,000 or more. A BVI BC acting as an EOR must maintain contemporaneous TP documentation demonstrating arm's length payment for services.

Economic substance scope: The BVI Business Company is subject to BVI Economic Substance Regulations unless it is tax resident in the UK and pays UK corporation tax. In the UK, substance is determined by central management and control; the entity must demonstrate actual operational presence, including physical office, employment contracts, payroll and management functions. Failure to show UK substance may cause HMRC to attribute profits under the offshore transfer-of-profit rules and may also trigger BVI ESR scrutiny.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Common law offence of cheating the public revenue: imprisonment up to 12 years and an unlimited fine; HMRC civil penalties up to 200% of the tax evaded, plus interest, daily penalties and negligence penalties

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly concerned in the evasion may be personally assessed for penalties, restrained by POCA confiscation orders, issued with travel bans and disqualified under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986
Shell Company / Artificial Tax Residence

Statutory sanction: HMRC may assert UK tax residence based on central management and control and tax worldwide profits; penalties under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 up to 100% of the additional deemed tax, plus interest and offshore asset-transfer penalties; the BVI may also impose financial penalties for failure to satisfy ESR economic substance if a genuine exemption is not established

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for company PAYE and NIC debts, wrongful trading claims by HMRC in insolvency, disqualification for failing to maintain statutory records, and criminal sanction if they knowingly facilitated the artificial arrangement
Employer Non-Compliance / PAYE Fraud

Statutory sanction: Penalties equalling up to 100% of the PAYE income tax and NICs not accounted for; monthly default penalties for late RTI filings up to GBP 400 per 10 employees; interest on all unpaid deductions; criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion of income tax

Director personal liability: Under the PAYE Regulations, senior directors may be personally liable for unpaid PAYE and NICs when they were knowingly responsible for the non-payment; HMRC may wind up the company on an expedited basis and seek a director disqualification order
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: HMRC transfer pricing adjustments re-determining taxable profits; denial of deductions and interest-charge adjustments; penalties under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 of up to 100% of the additional tax due; accelerated payment notices for contested TP adjustments

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for adjustments if they signed inaccurate CT600 returns, are involved in fraudulent TP schemes, or participate in wrongful trading after the business becomes insolvent; HMRC may also issue personal contribution notices under the Criminal Finances Act 2017 where the company is used to facilitate offshore tax evasion
