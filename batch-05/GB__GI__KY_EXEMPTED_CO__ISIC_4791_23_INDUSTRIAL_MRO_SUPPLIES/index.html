Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar: HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax) and Companies House Gibraltar; parent-state fiscal authority: HM Revenue & Customs (UK) for UK VAT and corporation tax nexus. Online portals: Gibraltar Income Tax Office e-services (https://eservices.gibraltar.gov.gi), Companies House Gibraltar (https://www.csra.gi), and UK HMRC online services (https://www.gov.uk).

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company receives no Cayman income-tax registration and no income-tax TIN because the Cayman Islands impose no direct taxes; a 20-year Tax Exemption Certificate is obtained from the Cayman Competent Authority immediately after incorporation. If the company creates a Gibraltar nexus, it must register as an overseas company with Companies House Gibraltar within 21 days of establishing a place of business, obtaining a Gibraltar overseas company number, and must file an initial corporation tax return with the Income Tax Office within 30 days of commencing activity, which triggers issuance of a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number. For UK VAT, the non-established overseas seller must register with HMRC via the non-UK VAT registration application from the first taxable supply of goods located in the UK at the point of sale (no de minimis threshold); HMRC issues a UK VAT registration number within 5 to 10 working days and back-dates it to the date of the first supply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Cayman). Under UK and Gibraltar analysis, the composite is a foreign offshore company which does not become UK tax resident if central management and control is not exercised in the UK; it becomes subject to Gibraltar corporation tax at 12.5% only on profits attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment or on Gibraltar-source income; it is outside UK corporation tax unless UK-resident under s.14 CTA 2009 or trading in the UK through a UK permanent establishment.

Available election forms

Election A - Cayman Tax Exemption Certificate under s.6 Tax Concessions Act: renewable, guarantees exemption from future Cayman direct taxes for 20 years. Election B - Gibraltar Qualifying Company top-up assessment election under the Income Tax (Amendment) Regulations 2020: an eligible trading company with profits not exceeding GBP 100,000 may elect for deemed taxable profits equal to 10% of payroll plus 10% of social security contributions, producing an effective tax rate well below the 12.5% headline. Election C - CFC non-residence and business establishment election for UK CFC rules: the company must affirm that it is not managed and controlled in the UK, and that no UK CFC attribution applies. Election D - Postponed VAT Accounting election on UK customs declarations: permits the registered importer to account for import VAT on its VAT return instead of paying cash at the border. Election E - Hybrid mismatch designation: under the UK hybrid mismatch rules (CTA 2010 s.259), the Cayman vehicle is treated as opaque for UK/Gibraltar tax purposes while disregarded for Cayman direct-tax purposes; no matching adjustment is required where the entity is not in a hybrid mismatch arrangement.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar levies no VAT: it is excluded from the UK VAT area under s.16 VATA 1994 and was never within the EU VAT territory. Local indirect taxes are limited to Community Duties on imported goods, excise duties on alcohol, tobacco and hydrocarbon oils, and premiums tax on insurance. UK VAT at 20% becomes relevant only when the MRO supply chain creates a UK taxable supply, namely sale of goods located in the UK, importation of goods into the UK, or certain B2B services supplied to UK business customers.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: no VAT registration threshold because VAT is not in force; any business importing industrial hardware into Gibraltar for commercial purposes must register with HM Customs for Community Duties, with no de minimis limit on commercial consignments. UK: non-established overseas sellers of goods physically located in the UK at the point of sale must register for UK VAT from the first sale; the previous GBP 70,000 distance-selling threshold for overseas sellers was abolished effective 1 July 2021. Where goods are sent from outside the UK directly to UK customers in separate consignments, the seller must account for 20% import VAT on the sale through whichever applicable import or marketplace regime arises.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A UK VAT-registered supplier may claim 0% VAT on export sales of industrial MRO hardware provided all of the following conditions are met: (1) the goods are physically removed from the UK to a destination outside the UK within 3 months of the time of supply, subject to HMRC tolerance of up to 6 months where business circumstances justify, (2) the supplier obtains and retains both official customs evidence (a valid export declaration entry accepted on CDS/CHIEF) and commercial evidence (customer order, commercial invoice, contract of carriage, freight payment) proving that the goods left the UK, (3) the customer belongs outside the UK for VAT purposes, which is satisfied for the Cayman/Gibraltar B2B counterparty, and (4) the supply is not part of an abusive chain. Ancillary electronic catalogue and platform services rendered to overseas B2B customers fall outside the scope of UK VAT under the place-of-supply rule when the recipient is established outside the UK. Movements of goods between the UK and Gibraltar are post-Brexit inside the UK customs territory for goods but remain outside the UK VAT area, so they are outside the scope of UK VAT rather than zero-rated exports.

  • Customs export declaration (UK Single Administrative Document or electronic CDS/CHIEF export entry with Movement Reference Number) evidencing export from the UK
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR or CIM consignment note proving physical removal of goods from the UK
  • Commercial invoice and purchase order from the overseas B2B customer, including the Incoterms rule applied to the export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT wire credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign-currency sale proceeds into the seller's bank account
  • Packing list, container manifest or cargo tracking records from the freight forwarder specific to the consignment
  • Certificate of Origin, EUR.1 movement certificate or ATA Carnet where preferential tariff or temporary export treatment is claimed

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing pre-clearance regime exists in Gibraltar or the UK. UK Making Tax Digital requires digital record-keeping and API-based VAT return submission through HMRC-approved MTD-compatible software; late-filing digital penalties apply after the soft-landing period. Gibraltar mandates electronic filing of annual company tax returns and confirmation statements through its e-services portal, and customs declarations must be lodged electronically via the customs single-window platform. B2B invoices issued to UK VAT-registered customers must contain the supplier name/address/VAT number, the customer VAT number, invoice date, sequential invoice number, goods description, quantity, tax point and rate; no government clearance or digital stamp is required.

HMRC applies enhanced scrutiny to export chains; if zero-rating evidence is not perfect and contemporaneous, the supply is reclassified as standard-rated and assessed at 20% VAT plus statutory interest. Clawback periods run up to 4 years for innocent error and 6 years for deliberate non-disclosure; penalty rates range from 30% for failure to notify, up to 70% for deliberate but not concealed behaviour and 100% for deliberate and concealed behaviour. Fraudulent export zero-rating constitutes a criminal offence under VATA 1994 Schedule 24 and FA 2007, attracting unlimited fines, asset forfeiture and director disqualification. In Gibraltar, misdeclaration to the Income Tax Office carries penalties of up to 100% of the tax under-assessed, plus strike-off risk. In the Cayman Islands, failure to file the Economic Substance return may result in penalties of up to KYD 10,000 per offence and, for materially false statements, potential imprisonment of the responsible officer. Any UK CFC attribution triggered by the export profits is assessable to UK corporation tax at rates between 19% and 25%, with late-payment interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full current and capital account convertibility. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is freely convertible at par with the pound sterling. Profit and dividend remittance faces no exchange control approval hurdles; however, standard corporate documentation, beneficial ownership verification, and counter-financing-of-terrorism clearance are required. There are no IMF Article XIV restriction remnants, and offshore entities such as Cayman Exempted Companies can freely move capital, subject to bank-level compliance and substance expectations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar imposes no mandatory export proceeds surrender, no repatriation timeline, and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in any convertible currency, held in offshore or Gibraltarian bank accounts, and utilised for global trade settlements without prior approval.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC); the Government of Gibraltar Financial Secretary administers exchange control; monetary policy follows the Bank of England as the territory uses the Gibraltar pound pegged at 1:1 to GBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Restricted Feasibility. Gibraltar-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and fintech challenger banks can onboard Cayman Exempted Companies if the beneficial ownership chain is fully documented and the e-commerce B2B MRO business model is substantiated with transactional evidence. However, leading cross-border digital EMIs (e.g. Wise, Revolut Business) generally deprioritise or reject Gibraltar-domiciled offshore companies for regulatory and perceived money-laundering risk, making feasibility a Low-to-Medium rating. A locally licensed EMI with a physical Gibraltar office is the only viable fintech route.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for shareholder capital injections uses SWIFT MT103 or MT202 wires with unambiguous purpose codes: 'EQUI' for equity share capital, 'INTC' for intercompany loans, and 'GIFT' for non-repayable support. Each transfer must be accompanied by the underlying board resolution, executed subscription or loan agreement, and UBO confirmation. Beneficiary bank details must match the Gibraltar account name exactly. Any injection exceeding GBP 12,000 triggers enhanced due diligence in line with the UK/Gibraltar money laundering regulations, and funds are subject to sanctions screening across UK, OFAC, and global embargo lists. Each outbound wire must carry full beneficiary bank BIC/IBAN, legal entity identifiers (LEI), and transaction codes aligned to the ISIC 4791 catalogue sales ledger. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File required for enterprises resident or having a PE in Gibraltar with turnover exceeding GIP 60 million; Master File required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding GIP 600 million. Small and medium enterprises with turnover below GIP 10 million are exempt from mandatory documentation, although arm's length principle still applies.

Economic substance scope: Applicable under the Gibraltar Economic Substance (Requirements) Act 2020. A Cayman Exempted Company tax resident in Gibraltar or carrying on a relevant activity such as a distribution and service centre must demonstrate economic substance in Gibraltar, including core income-generating activities locally directed and managed, having appropriate qualified employees, and incurring adequate operational expenditure. Annual substance reporting is compulsory.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fine up to GIP 50,000 or 200% of the tax evaded, and imprisonment up to five years. The Commissioner may assess omitted profits for up to six years (unlimited in cases of fraud).

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the company's tax liabilities and penalties if the company becomes insolvent. Travel bans and asset freezing orders may be issued during criminal tax fraud investigations.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic substance assessment penalty of GIP 60,000, plus daily penalties for continued non-compliance; the Gibraltar Companies Registry may strike off the company or revoke its tax status.

Director personal liability: Directors face disqualification under the Companies Act and personal civil penalties for knowingly filing false economic substance declarations. In cross-border enforcement, directors may also face extradition if fraud is alleged.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act, a finding of money laundering may lead to imprisonment up to fourteen years and asset confiscation; the GFSC may revoke any relevant EMI licence and impose unlimited fines.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be criminally liable if they knowingly or negligently fail to implement AML controls. Personal assets may be confiscated, and travel bans may be imposed pending proceedings. Dissolution of the Cayman vehicle is not a defence.
