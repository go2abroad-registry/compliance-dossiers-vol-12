Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office (ITO), HM Government of Gibraltar, established under the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended); official e-filing and taxpayer portal via the Gibraltar TAM (Tax Administration Management) system at https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax. Indirect tax and customs competence rests with HM Customs Gibraltar (Customs and Excise Department), operating the Gibraltar Trader electronic declarations portal.

E-government filing portal

For the Cayman Exempted Company establishing a place of business in Gibraltar: (i) register as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Companies House under the Companies Act 2014 (form OC-1, certified Cayman certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, register of directors and secretary, Gibraltar registered office, and local authorized representative) within 30 days of establishing a place of business; (ii) apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) using the standard Registration of a Company form, furnishing the companies-house certificate, constitutional documents, PE details, and a declaration of projected Gibraltar-source activity; (iii) file the Economic Substance Return (GES-1) identification form under the Income Tax (Economic Substance) Act 2020 if the entity becomes a Gibraltar tax resident engaged in manufacturing; and (iv) obtain a Gibraltar Customs Trader Number (EORI-style) from HM Customs for the import of raw inputs and export of finished sutures. Statutory issuance of the TRN typically occurs within 2–4 weeks of complete filing; the first annual Corporate Income Tax Return is due by 30 November following the relevant tax year end.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — the Cayman Exempted Company retains Tax_Exempt_Zone status in the Cayman Islands (no Cayman income tax), but upon registration in Gibraltar it becomes an entity-level taxpayer solely in respect of profits 'accruing in or derived from Gibraltar' under section 5 of the Income Tax Act 2010. A permanent establishment comprising the surgical suture and needle assembly plant will be assessed on arm's-length attributable trading profits at the standard corporate rate of 12.5%; foreign-source income not derived from the Gibraltar PE and not remitted to or used in Gibraltar falls outside the charge.

Available election forms

No US-style 'check-the-box' or Cayman-style pass-through election exists in Gibraltar; a Cayman company is unambiguously treated as a body corporate. Available elections include: (i) group relief and consortium loss-surrender elections under the Income Tax (Groups) Regulations; (ii) an advance agreement with the Commissioner of Income Tax on the attribution of OECD arm's-length profits to the Gibraltar PE; (iii) mandatory self-disclosure filings under the Income Tax (Hybrid Mismatch Arrangements) Regulations 2020 to neutralize double non-taxation; and (iv) elections on the apportionment of common overheads between Gibraltar and foreign operations, subject to ITO acceptance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST is levied in Gibraltar, which is expressly outside the EU VAT area. The only indirect taxes are (i) Purchase Tax imposed on a closed list of imported goods for local consumption (alcoholic beverages, tobacco products, petroleum, and specified luxuries) and (ii) Customs and import duties under the Gibraltar Customs Tariff. Surgical sutures and needles (ISIC 3250 / HS 9018–9021) are outside the Purchase Tax net and generally attract 0% import and export duty.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No Purchase Tax registration threshold applies to this business scope because surgical suture and needle assembly is not a taxable commodity. Conversely, all commercial importers and exporters must obtain a Gibraltar Customs Trader Number (EORI-equivalent) before the first customs declaration; there is no de minimis threshold for commercial entities (the EUR 150 duty-free consignment rule applies only to low-value non-commercial parcels consigned to private individuals).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

As no VAT exists, the functional equivalent of zero-rating is the statutory exemption of exports from Purchase Tax and customs export duty. Eligibility requires: (i) possession of a valid CE certificate / EU Declaration of Conformity under MDR 2017/745 for the absorbable sutures and needles; (ii) completion of an electronic customs export declaration (customs status 10/1000) evidencing physical departure of the goods from Gibraltar to a foreign destination within the declared time; (iii) sale to a non-resident buyer or into an approved export warehouse; and (iv) contractual conclusion outside Gibraltar if territorial exclusion from corporate income tax is also asserted. Profits attributable to the Gibraltar PE remain taxable at 12.5% regardless of destination.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD/DAE, customs status code 1000)
  • Commercial Invoice specifying ISIC 3250 / HS 9018–9021 description, lot numbers, quantities, unit prices, and Incoterms
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill dated and consigned to the overseas buyer
  • Packing List referencing sterile suture/needle configurations and batch/lot numbers
  • Certificate of Origin (non-preferential, or EUR.1/ATR if claiming applicable preference)
  • Medical Device CE Certificate / EU Declaration of Conformity under MDR 2017/745
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export proceeds

Gibraltar imposes no mandatory B2B e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API-based invoice clearance, or digital stamping. Customs clearance is fully electronic: export declarations must be lodged as electronic SAD/DAE returns through the Gibraltar Customs Trader Portal, with safety and security declarations (EXS/ENS) transmitted via the applicable ICS2/EU-UK gateways. Imports of raw polyglycolic acid braided suture material require an electronic import entry, optional duty deferment, and submission of CE technical documentation under EU MDR 2017/745 where needed for subsequent re-export.

Fraudulent export declarations or misuse of the export exemption trigger retrospective clawback of any Purchase Tax/customs duty avoided, a pecuniary penalty of up to 300% of the duty value under the Customs and Excise Ordinance, forfeiture of the goods, and criminal liability (fines up to GBP 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years). In the income tax field, failure to substantiate the export destination or foreign buyer permits the ITO to re-attribute the export profit to the Gibraltar PE; the Income Tax Act 2010 penalty regime imposes up to 200% of the tax difference for negligence, GBP 100 per day for continuing non-filing, and GBP 5,000 plus up to 2 years' imprisonment for fraudulent evasion. Additional fixed penalties of GBP 3,000 per return plus GBP 600 per day apply for hybrid mismatch misreporting.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Cayman Islands is a jurisdiction with no central bank and maintains an open exchange control regime. The Cayman Islands Dollar (KYD) is pegged to USD at 0.82 KYD = 1 USD; the currency is fully convertible for current account transactions, capital transactions, and profit remittances. The Cayman Exempted Company, as a non-resident controlled entity, is classified as a 'non-resident company' for exchange control purposes and is exempt from all restrictions on holdings of foreign currency, and the issuance of shares, loan capital, and distribution of dividends to non-resident shareholders requires no exchange control approval. Gibraltar likewise enjoys full current account and capital account convertibility for the GIP, which is freely exchangeable at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP, and profit remittance to a UK parent or Cayman shareholder is unrestricted provided standard corporate books and returns are filed. There is no substantive profit-remittance hurdle or dividend repatriation restriction for the Cayman Exempted Company operating through Gibraltar bank accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Neither Gibraltar nor the Cayman Islands imposes any mandatory surrender of export proceeds to the central authority. The United Kingdom's Exchange Control Act 1947 is repealed in its relevant application; there is no statutory requirement to convert export earnings into local currency, no prescribed timeline for repatriation or conversion, and no mandatory conversion ratio. A Cayman Exempted Company settling export proceeds in USD or GBP through Gibraltar-based accounts may retain the entire gross proceeds in a foreign-currency account, remit surplus treasury to the Cayman parent or UK treasury, and effect payment to suppliers without conversion. Utilisation of export receipts for trade settlements is unrestricted, subject only to the anti-money laundering and financial sanctions monitoring of the GFSC.

Central bank supervision: For Gibraltar: Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar, with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) supervising banks and the Bank of England maintaining the monetary anchor for the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) which circulates at parity with GBP. For the Cayman Islands Exempted Company: Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) under the Exchange Control Regulations of the Cayman Islands, with the Cayman Islands Dollar (KYD) pegged to the US Dollar at a fixed rate of 0.82 KYD = 1 USD, and the UK Crown Dependencies and Overseas Territories are subject to the Bank of England's oversight only for note issuance interoperability; the pertinent exchange control authority for Cayman is the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority and the Financial Secretary.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for the treasury blueprint. A Cayman Exempted Company may lawfully open accounts with UK-authorized or Gibraltar-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions, such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, Currencycloud (Visa Cross-Border), and Ebury, provided the EMI can complete due diligence on the Cayman-incorporated beneficiary. Legal constraints include: (i) many EMIs do not accept Cayman Islands jurisdictions as a standard onboarding jurisdiction unless the entity provides a Cayman Certificate of Good Standing, register of directors, and a UK or Gibraltar nexus letter; (ii) EMIs are not full credit institutions and cannot issue commercial letters of credit or provide documentary trade finance; (iii) EMI safeguarding rules under the UK Payment Services Regulations 2017 and the E-Money Regulations 2011 require client funds to be held in a segregated safeguarding account at a Tier-1 bank, thereby retaining the need for a physical banking relationship; (iv) the GFSC requires any entity conducting banking-type activity through Gibraltar to hold a banking licence, but holding an account with an EMI is permissible as the EMI is not conducting deposit-taking; (v) where the entity's export trade involves surgical sutures (medical device category), enhanced EDD may be triggered by the regulated activity of dealing in medical devices; (vi) as a practical matter the use of EMIs is limited to corridor accounts, vendor payments and treasury sweeps below GBP 250,000 per single transaction, and the ultimate clearing rail is still SWIFT MT103 through the EMI's commercial bank partner.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High for the treasury blueprint. A Cayman Exempted Company may lawfully open accounts with UK-authorized or Gibraltar-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Payment Institutions, such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, Currencycloud (Visa Cross-Border), and Ebury, provided the EMI can complete due diligence on the Cayman-incorporated beneficiary. Legal constraints include: (i) many EMIs do not accept Cayman Islands jurisdictions as a standard onboarding jurisdiction unless the entity provides a Cayman Certificate of Good Standing, register of directors, and a UK or Gibraltar nexus letter; (ii) EMIs are not full credit institutions and cannot issue commercial letters of credit or provide documentary trade finance; (iii) EMI safeguarding rules under the UK Payment Services Regulations 2017 and the E-Money Regulations 2011 require client funds to be held in a segregated safeguarding account at a Tier-1 bank, thereby retaining the need for a physical banking relationship; (iv) the GFSC requires any entity conducting banking-type activity through Gibraltar to hold a banking licence, but holding an account with an EMI is permissible as the EMI is not conducting deposit-taking; (v) where the entity's export trade involves surgical sutures (medical device category), enhanced EDD may be triggered by the regulated activity of dealing in medical devices; (vi) as a practical matter the use of EMIs is limited to corridor accounts, vendor payments and treasury sweeps below GBP 250,000 per single transaction, and the ultimate clearing rail is still SWIFT MT103 through the EMI's commercial bank partner.
