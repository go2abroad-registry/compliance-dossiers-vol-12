Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Commissioner of Income Tax, Gibraltar Income Tax Office (direct taxes) and the Customs and Excise Department, HM Government of Gibraltar (Purchase Tax); HMRC (HM Revenue & Customs) is the competent authority for UK VAT where B2C digital supplies are made to UK consumers outside the marketplace deemed-supplier rules. Official portals: www.gibraltar.gov.gi (Gibraltar government, Companies Registry and Income Tax Office) and www.gov.uk/hmrc (UK VAT registration and Making Tax Digital).

E-government filing portal

The Cayman Exempted Company first secures its Certificate of Incorporation and annual Tax-Exempt Certificate from the Cayman Islands Registrar of Companies. To operate through Gibraltar as the pan-European gateway, the company must: (1) register as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry using the certified constitutional documents and register of directors/members, while appointing a resident agent under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014; (2) obtain the Gibraltar company registration number, which serves as the local TIN; (3) apply to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office for corporate tax registration only upon creation of a Gibraltar taxable nexus, namely central management and control in Gibraltar, a Gibraltar permanent establishment, or Gibraltar-source income; (4) optionally obtain a Gibraltar Certificate of Tax Residence if treaty-benefit proof is required; (5) for UK VAT, first verify whether Apple IAP and Google Play Billing act as deemed supplier/merchant of record for UK consumer supplies; if the entity remains the supplier of record, it must register with HMRC as a non-established taxable person via form VAT1 (and VAT1A for a UK tax representative where security is required), with no £85,000 deregistration threshold applying to non-established suppliers of B2C digital services; (6) for EU consumer sales made outside the app-store rails, elect the Non-Union VAT OSS scheme in one EU Member State or register for EU VAT in each relevant state.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Cayman Exempted Company exempt from Cayman Islands income tax while holding its Tax-Exempt Certificate; under Gibraltar territorial taxation, foreign-source digital-app income of a non-resident offshore entity remains outside Gibraltar charge unless managed and controlled in Gibraltar, in which case the 12.5% Gibraltar corporate rate applies only to Gibraltar-source profits).

Available election forms

Gibraltar Income Tax Act elections: election between the Allowances-Based System and the Gross Income-Based System for companies; group loss-relief and capital-allowance pooling elections. Cayman election mechanics: annual renewal of the Tax-Exempt Certificate and filing of the Economic Substance return with the relevant-activity designation. No US-style check-the-box election exists for UK or Gibraltar purposes; instead, UK TIOPA 2010 Part 6A hybrid-mismatch rules and Gibraltar's ATAD2-aligned anti-hybrid rules require disclosure and election-based neutralisation of any hybrid financial instrument or hybrid entity mismatch, including designation of the lead taxing jurisdiction for mismatch offset.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Purchase Tax (not an EU VAT system and inapplicable to pure services); UK VAT applies to B2C digital supplies to UK consumers only where the platform is not the deemed supplier. Qualifying exported offshore digital supplies carry a 0% effective indirect-tax rate.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: Purchase Tax registration is mandatory only for importers, manufacturers and excise operators; there is no Gibraltar VAT and no turnover threshold for digital services. UK VAT: the £85,000 registration threshold does not apply to non-established suppliers of B2C digital services; registration is mandatory from the first UK consumer supply unless Apple/Google is the deemed supplier under marketplace rules. EU VAT: the Non-Union OSS regime permits pan-European B2C digital-service compliance without multiple member-state registrations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under UK place-of-supply rules, B2B supplies of mobile-utility software to overseas customers fall outside UK VAT (reverse charge on the recipient), and B2C supplies are deemed made in the customer's jurisdiction; no UK zero-rating needs to be claimed where the consumer belongs outside the UK. Where the entity is itself the merchant of record, zero-rating/outside-scope treatment requires: (i) two or more non-conflicting items of customer-location evidence (billing address, IP address, mobile country code, bank or payment-card country); (ii) demonstrable export intent and purely offshore supply; (iii) executed Apple/Google distributor or commissionaire agreements; and (iv) classification of the revenue as an electronically supplied service under ISIC 6201/NAICS 511210 rather than a goods transaction. Gibraltar Purchase Tax never applies to services, so exported app-store revenues benefit from a 0% effective indirect-tax rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (goods-only evidence; not produced for digital app-store supplies)
  • Bill of Lading (goods-only evidence; replaced for digital services by Apple/Google consolidated sales reports and platform export logs)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank credit evidence (wire/EMI statement evidencing FX conversion and inward receipt)
  • Apple App Store Connect monthly sales/earnings reports and Google Play financial reports

Gibraltar imposes no real-time e-invoicing, fiscal-clearance, digital-stamping or API clearance mandate. UK VAT: Making Tax Digital for VAT requires VAT-registered entities to keep digital records and file VAT returns through HMRC-recognised software using HMRC APIs. Apple App Store Connect and Google Play Console issue merchant invoices and consolidated sales reports; these platform records, commission invoices and payment-rail settlement files are the primary transactional fiscal evidence and must be retained for HMRC and Gibraltar inspection.

HMRC may assess unpaid UK VAT for up to 4 years, extended to 20 years for deliberate, careless or fraudulent conduct, with Schedule 24 FA 2007 penalties up to 100% of potential lost revenue (up to 200% under Schedule 24A FA 2021 for deliberate dishonesty) plus interest, and criminal liability under VATA 1994 s.72 for fraudulent evasion; retrospective clawback of wrongly zero-rated output VAT and any deducted input VAT arises where the supplier cannot substantiate customer location or the 180-day repatriation benchmark is missed. Gibraltar Customs and Excise enforces Purchase Tax evasion through forfeiture and fines under the Customs Ordinance, and Gibraltar Income Tax penalties for false returns reach 100% of tax plus late-payment interest. Cayman authorities may revoke the Tax-Exempt Certificate and impose Economic Substance penalties up to KYD 120,000 plus imprisonment for false filings, while UK CFC and hybrid-mismatch rules may attribute app income to UK shareholders if central management and control is exercised from the UK, producing full retrospective loss of the offshore tax-exempt treatment.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar and the UK operate a freely convertible current and capital account regime with no exchange controls and no central-bank approval requirement for capital inflows, profit repatriation, dividend remittance, intercompany loan servicing, or liquidation proceeds paid to non-resident shareholders. The Cayman Exempted Company layer adds no FX restriction because the Cayman Islands likewise impose no exchange controls. Practical hurdles are limited to documentary evidence: board resolutions, certified incorporation and good-standing certificates, beneficial-ownership declarations, audited or management accounts, a clear tax-residence explanation, and source-of-funds verification. Apple IAP and Google Play Billing revenue may be settled in USD, EUR, or GBP and freely converted at commercial market rates without surrender or conversion obligation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no export-proceeds surrender rules applicable to a Gibraltar/UK treasury platform or a Cayman Exempted Company operating through Gibraltar. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%. There is no statutory 30/60/90-day timeline for repatriating or converting export receipts. Export and app-store service proceeds may be retained indefinitely in a multi-currency account, held as bank deposits, or converted through a spot or forward transaction. The only reporting burden is routine bank-level balance-of-payments statistical reporting, which does not require corporate entity filing.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for UK sterling system) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (statutory regulator for banking, investment, payment and e-money activity in Gibraltar); no separate Gibraltar central bank exists, and currency issuance is delegated to HM Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability: HIGH for operational liquidity clearing and low-value outbound supplier disbursements through a UK FCA-authorised EMI or a Gibraltar-registered payment institution; LOW for capital preservation, escrow, or large capital injection use cases because an EMI is not a licensed bank and cannot accept deposits. Legal constraints arise under UK E-Money Regulations 2011, Gibraltar Financial Services Act 2019, the Payment Services Regulations 2017, and safeguarding rules requiring ring-fencing of customer funds at an authorised credit institution. A non-bank EMI can manage App Store payouts and FX conversion only if it implements bank-grade KYC/AML systems and shares transaction data with the primary bank; it cannot provide credit, overdraft protection, or commercial letters of credit. Offshore digital EMIs with no EEA or UK passport are not viable for this structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing is a direct SWIFT MT103 from the named shareholder's or UBO's bank account into the Gibraltarian/UK treasury account. The wire reference must state Capital Subscription / Share Premium, and if the receiving bank uses ISO 20022 external purpose codes, the remitter should use an equity-capital or foreign-direct-investment purpose code, alternatively the MT103 free-text must state CAYMAN EXEMPTED COMPANY CAPITAL INJECTION. The bank will require board resolution approving the share allotment, certificate of incorporation/good standing, UBO and sanctions screening, source-of-funds declaration, bank statements or audited financials proving the remitter's legitimate funds, and a written instruction that third-party payments are prohibited. The capital injection should be received only on a fully matched bank account and never mixed with app-store collections or customer funds.The settlement rails are SWIFT MT103 for non-EEA cross-border vendor and supplier payments, SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant Credit for EUR-denominated EEA disbursements, CHAPS and Faster Payments for domestic UK/Gibraltar GBP transactions, and commercial letters of credit (MT700/MT707) only where physical infrastructure procurement requires a documentary credit. App-store platform commissions are settled by Apple/Google payout rails into the operating account, while outbound royalty, licence, hosting, cloud-infrastructure, and contractor payments must be matched to signed agreements and valid invoices. Where a commercial LC is needed, the Gibraltar bank may issue or confirm it through a correspondent bank; this is not a primary rail for a pure software and mobile utility app model. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% for the qualifying non-resident Cayman Exempted Company operations in Gibraltar; 12.5% standard Gibraltar corporate income tax rate for a Gibraltar-resident trading company or for a non-resident company carrying on trade through a Gibraltar permanent establishment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. Gibraltar does not impose any withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar-resident subsidiary to a non-resident shareholder, including a Cayman Exempted Company. The Cayman entity will, however, need to support an exemption from any UK source tax where the dividend onward stream is derived from UK app-store profits under controlled-company rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions in a fiscal year. Above this threshold, the Gibraltar taxpayer must prepare a Local File and a summary Master File detailing the Cayman parent's global business, intangible ownership and transfer pricing methods. Transactions below the threshold must still be disclosed in the annual return and supported by at least a one-page arm's-length memorandum.

Economic substance scope: The structure is subject to the Gibraltar Economic Substance (Companies and Partnerships) Act 2019. A mobile utility app distributor or service business is a 'Distribution and Service Centre' relevant activity; where the undertaking provides management or intellectual property support, it may also constitute 'Headquarters' or 'Intellectual Property' activity. The Gibraltar entity must demonstrate core income-generating activities in Gibraltar: physical office or server presence, appropriately qualified employees, board decision-making, operational expenditure and local bank accounts. In parallel, because the top entity is a Cayman Exempted Company, the Cayman Islands Economic Substance regime requires the pure equity holding exemption or a substantive CIGA test in Grand Cayman for any income streams not subject to Gibraltar substance. App-store revenues booked as passive royalties to the Cayman company are vulnerable unless real development and management assets exist in Cayman or Gibraltar.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — False or Understated Return

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, fraudulent or negligent returns attract tax-geared penalties of up to 100% of the tax evaded, late-payment interest at 8% per annum, a fine of up to GIP 100,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years upon conviction in the Magistrates Court or Supreme Court.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where a director signs a false COT1. Directors are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and penalties; they may be disqualified from acting as directors for up to 15 years; travel bans and surrender of passports are obtainable pending criminal proceedings; fraud proceeds are subject to restraint and confiscation orders.
Transfer Pricing Understatement / Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: The Income Tax Commissioner can re-price the transaction under the arm's-length standard, disallow the excess deduction, assess additional tax, apply a transfer pricing penalty of up to 200% of the tax deficiency and charge interest from the original filing date.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve a non-arm's-length royalty may be made personally liable for the additional tax under anti-avoidance provisions; they can be ordered to make equitable restitution to the company; intentional participation in a scheme to strip taxable profits from Gibraltar constitutes tax evasion and carries imprisonment.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Economic Substance Act 2019 and the Cayman International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act 2018, the tax authority may levy an initial penalty of GIP 10,000, a daily default penalty of GIP 1,000 per day, require the disqualification of the corporate service provider, and ultimately strike the company off the register or issue a notice of automatic exchange of information with the UK.

Director personal liability: Directors of a non-compliant shell company face disqualification and a travel ban. Directors who allow the company to carry on business with no real economic nexus are personally liable for the company's tax liabilities, and the Registrar may issue a restoration order requiring the directors to pay all penalties and creditor costs from their personal assets.
AML and Digital Payment Channel Offences

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2011 and Anti-Money Laundering Regulations, unauthorised money service activity carries a fine up to GIP 500,000 and imprisonment for up to 14 years; failure to report suspicion is punishable by a fine and up to 5 years' imprisonment; the digital assets and corporate bank accounts are subject to freezing and forfeiture.

Director personal liability: AML liability attaches to each director and compliance officer individually. Directors can be made subject to travel bans, asset freezing orders and confiscation orders; disqualification is automatic upon conviction; the Cayman corporate veil provides no protection because AML law imposes a personal statutory duty on every officer concerned in the management of the company.
