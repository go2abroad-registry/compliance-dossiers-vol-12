Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under s.14 of the Cayman Companies Act; authorized capital is conventionally declared at USD 50,000, which may remain unissued and no escrow, deposit, or paid-up requirement applies at incorporation; Gibraltar imposes no additional capital requirement for digital service branches.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and civil defense clearance by the Gibraltar Fire and Rescue Service covering annual fire risk assessment, detection systems, and escape routes; structural load inspection certificate for office premises; Health and Safety at Work Act compliance including display screen equipment and first-aid certification; no hazardous chemicals or explosive materials, therefore no special civil defense license is required.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under s.14 of the Cayman Companies Act; authorized capital is conventionally declared at USD 50,000, which may remain unissued and no escrow, deposit, or paid-up requirement applies at incorporation; Gibraltar imposes no additional capital requirement for digital service branches.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft
The First Board Resolution of the Exempted Company: (1) the founder's share subscription is approved, and the authorized capital of USD 50,000 is allotted in accordance with the register; (2) the registered office address of the licensed corporate services provider is adopted, and filing with the Registrar is directed; (3) the initial directors and officers are appointed for the first governance year; (4) the directors are authorized to register the company as a foreign company in Gibraltar and to open a segregated client funds account; (5) the common seal is approved and adopted.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office, iTax online portal (https://www.gibraltartax.gov.gi)

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated Cayman Exempted Company must file a company registration application with the Gibraltar Companies House if it establishes a place of business or a permanent establishment in Gibraltar. To obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number, submit Form T1 (Company Tax Registration), the certified Cayman certificate of incorporation and memorandum/articles, evidence of a licensed Gibraltar registered office or physical presence, and names of directors and shareholders. The Income Tax Office issues the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (GTIN) within 15-30 business days; a local tax representative may be required for non-resident entities.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign-incorporated Cayman Exempted Company must file a company registration application with the Gibraltar Companies House if it establishes a place of business or a permanent establishment in Gibraltar. To obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number, submit Form T1 (Company Tax Registration), the certified Cayman certificate of incorporation and memorandum/articles, evidence of a licensed Gibraltar registered office or physical presence, and names of directors and shareholders. The Income Tax Office issues the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (GTIN) within 15-30 business days; a local tax representative may be required for non-resident entities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not offer US-style check-the-box elections. The Cayman Exempted Company is treated as a tax-exempt foreign entity absent a Gibraltar permanent establishment; upon acquiring territorial nexus it becomes subject to Gibraltar entity-level income tax under the Income Tax Act 2019. No hybrid mismatch or fiscal unity elections apply at present.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST levied in Gibraltar; limited indirect tax arises only through Gibraltar Customs and Excise duties on physical importation

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; Gibraltar does not impose VAT/GST on services or most sales. Customs duty registration is required only when importing physical goods into Gibraltar, and an economic turnover threshold does not exist for indirect tax purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT/GST to zero-rate. For physical goods exported from Gibraltar, zero/default duty treatment applies upon proof of removal outside Gibraltar. For digitally delivered EdTech subscription services, no Gibraltar indirect tax is imposed; the supplier should document the customer's residence and place of consumption to satisfy destination-state VAT rules (e.g., EU OSS, UK OSS, or local sales/GST regimes).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar SAD / C88)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (original signed carrier document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank payment confirmation showing funds received in Gibraltar)

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping for digital service suppliers. Physical import/export declarations are processed through the Gibraltar Customs electronic system, while commercial invoices are required for recordkeeping but are not cleared through a national tax clearance portal.

Fraudulent export declarations are prosecuted under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance and the Income Tax Act 2019, attracting fines up to 100% of the goods value or duty evaded, imprisonment of up to seven years, and retrospective clawback of any export-related tax advantage with statutory interest. The Income Tax Office may apply anti-abuse rules to deny refunds or impose 20% late-payment penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar has full capital and current account convertibility. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged 1:1 with the British pound (GBP) and is freely exchangeable within the sterling zone. No exchange control legislation restricts inbound capital, dividend distributions, or profit remittances. Profits of the Cayman Exempted Company may be repatriated to shareholders subject to Gibraltar Companies Act solvency requirements and standard AML clearing. No specific hurdles or approvals apply to EdTech subscription revenues collected via App Store payment processors.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Neither the United Kingdom nor Gibraltar imposes mandatory surrender of export proceeds or any minimum conversion percentage. All export/current account receipts may be retained, netted, hedged, or converted at freely market-determined rates. FX settlement follows standard SWIFT practices and the GFSC AML framework.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (banking and regulatory supervision)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility with legal constraints. A Cayman Exempted Company may open settlement accounts with GFSC-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) or UK-regulated EMIs that support multi-currency IBANs and SWIFT access. Key constraints: (i) the EMI must hold a valid e-money licence; (ii) the Cayman Exempted Company must present a commercial substance narrative covering the Gibraltar/UK nexus; (iii) EMI onboarding requires a video or in-person interview for the ultimate beneficial owner; (iv) the EMI must confirm that Apple IAP / Google Play Billing proceeds are treated as low-risk subscription income derived from digital language-learning services.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections from the Cayman entity into Gibraltar/UK accounts must use SWIFT MT103 payments with transparent purpose codes. For equity contributions, use a standard corporate investment purpose description (e.g., 'Investment of capital in subsidiary') and provide a board resolution plus source-of-funds letter. For shareholder loans, attach the executed loan agreement and the correct ISIC/settlement descriptor. Prior to transfer, compliance must receive evidence of source of funds: audited financial statements, asset disposal contract, or verified shareholder wealth. Routing must avoid intermediary banks in high-risk jurisdictions; the sender should use a UK clearing correspondent (e.g., Lloyds, HSBC, Barclays) and maintain a complete audit trail.Outbound disbursements covering Apple/Google platform fees, cloud hosting, content licensing, and third-party subscriptions must be executed via SWIFT MT103 for wire settlements or commercial LC (MT700) where documentary credit is contractually required. For recurring digital-service fees the preferred rail is: (a) pool GBP in an operational account; (b) spot-convert at market rates with targeted FX forward cover; (c) disburse via MT103 with invoice numbers and payment references; (d) if a counterparty requires an LC, issue an irrevocable LC at sight confirmed by a UK/EEA bank. Because the underlying activity is digital subscription services, cheque and paper-based instruments are not appropriate; use FPS/SEPA for small-value European payments and SWIFT for cross-border USD/EUR wires.Outright GBP/USD and GBP/EUR FX forwards (up to 12 months)Over-the-counter vanilla currency options on GBP, USD, and EURCross-currency swaps between GIP/GBP and USD/EUR for balance-sheet matchingNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only where the offshore settlement requirement is demonstrated and the Gibraltar bank permits the product; otherwise NDFs are restricted for EdTech cash-flow hedging
