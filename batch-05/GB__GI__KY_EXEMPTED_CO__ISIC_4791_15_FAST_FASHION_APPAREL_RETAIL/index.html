Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office, Ministry of Finance, Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar; HM Customs Gibraltar for customs, excise and consumption duties; official e-service portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi (Income Tax Office and Companies House Gibraltar).

E-government filing portal

A Cayman exempted company is not automatically subject to Gibraltar income tax. The entity is treated as non-resident unless management and control are exercised in Gibraltar. If no Gibraltar permanent establishment or taxable nexus exists, no Gibraltar tax reference number is required. If a taxable nexus is created (e.g., a branch, a place of effective management, or a Gibraltar agent concluding e-commerce sales), the company must register with the Income Tax Office by filing the prescribed corporate registration application together with: (1) the Cayman Islands Certificate of Incorporation; (2) the Memorandum and Articles of Association; (3) a Certificate of Good Standing and the Cayman Tax Exemption Certificate (where applicable); (4) a certified register of directors and shareholders; (5) a declaration of the Gibraltar registered office or agent; and (6) evidence of economic substance or, where absent, a Cayman tax residence certificate. Concurrently, an overseas company register filing is required at Companies House Gibraltar under the Companies Act. The Income Tax Office issues the Gibraltar tax reference number and the company registration number. Separate customs registration must be obtained through HM Customs Gibraltar when physical goods are imported, exported or warehoused.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box election nor an elective hybrid classification regime. A Cayman exempted company is classified under Gibraltar law as a non-resident foreign company with entity-level limited liability. Its profits are within the Gibraltar tax charge only where management and control is in Gibraltar or where a permanent establishment exists. The tax-exempt-zone status is not an elective classification but results from the entity being non-resident in Gibraltar and holding a Cayman tax exemption certificate. For UK/Gibraltar hybrid mismatch purposes, the entity remains a separate taxable person and no pass-through election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

VAT / GST / Sales and Use Tax — Not applicable in Gibraltar; Gibraltar imposes no general VAT, GST or sales and use tax. The relevant indirect tax nexus is UK VAT, which applies to cross-border supplies of goods sold to UK consumers through the e-commerce channel.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in Gibraltar; no Gibraltar VAT/GST threshold exists. For UK VAT, a non-UK established retailer is required to register when taxable supplies to UK consumers exceed £90,000 in a rolling 12-month period, or earlier when using the UK VAT Mini One Stop Store (VATOSS) for distance sales. Online marketplace facilitators may be deemed suppliers and require UK VAT registration irrespective of the seller's turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

No Gibraltar export zero-rating exists because Gibraltar does not levy VAT or a general consumption tax. Where the Cayman exempted company is registered for UK VAT and exports goods from Great Britain or Northern Ireland to a place outside the UK, zero-rating is available under VAT Act 1994, section 30 and HMRC VAT Notice 703 if the goods are physically exported by the supplier or on its behalf and conclusive export evidence is retained. For fast-fashion e-commerce supplies made to UK consumers, the supply is taxable in the UK at the standard rate of 20%; zero-rating applies only where physical export to an address outside the UK is established. Refund claims for UK input VAT can be made on the VAT return / VAT126 form by non-established traders; Gibraltar has no VAT refund mechanism because it has no VAT.

  • Customs export declaration and export acceptance message generated through HMRC's Customs Declaration Service (CDS) or the legacy CHIEF system
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, consignment note, or courier shipment manifest proving physical departure from the UK
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment evidence showing receipt of foreign currency export proceeds within the applicable currency repatriation period
  • Commercial invoice, sales contract, packing list, and proof of non-UK delivery destination
  • Where applicable, a signed export authority letter if the export is made through a third-party logistics provider or consolidated freight forwarder

Gibraltar does not impose real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping. There is also no UK continuous transaction controls (CTC) or HMRC e-invoicing pre-clearance requirement. However, UK VAT-registered businesses must maintain digital records and submit VAT returns using functional compatible software under Making Tax Digital (MTD) via HMRC's API. Invoices must contain the standard VAT invoice data set: date, supplier and customer VAT numbers, tax point, description, quantity, rate, VAT amount, and total value. No pre-clearance or digital stamping is required.

Where export zero-rating is claimed without admissible evidence or where refund claims are fraudulent, HMRC may deny the zero-rating and assess under-accounted UK VAT retrospectively. Assessment periods extend to up to 4 years for careless errors and 20 years for deliberate concealment. Civil penalties apply under Finance Act 2007, Schedule 24 (careless up to 30%; deliberate up to 70-100%) and Finance Act 2008, Schedule 41 for failure to register, plus interest. Deliberate evasion may lead to prosecution under VAT Act 1994, section 72 with unlimited fines. Gibraltar customs and income tax authorities may also impose penalties under the Customs Duties Ordinance and the Income Tax Act 2010 for false declarations, including monetary penalties, seizure and confiscation. Input VAT may be clawed back where the exporter fails to meet the export substantiation requirements in VAT Notice 703 within the required period.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar Pound is pegged at 1:1 parity to the Pound Sterling under the Currency Notes Act. Gibraltar maintains a fully liberalised current and capital account with no foreign exchange controls, no approval requirement for dividend remittances, and no restrictions on the repatriation of profits. Cayman exempted companies may hold and convert GBP, EUR, USD, and other trading currencies freely, provided tax arrears in Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands are nil and the economic substance posture does not trigger UK anti-abuse review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Gibraltar and the United Kingdom abolished exchange controls in 1979. Export proceeds arising from fast-fashion e-commerce sales may be retained indefinitely in multi-currency accounts, offset against supplier payments, or converted to Gibraltar Pounds without statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: Ministry of Finance of Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar as currency issuer in conjunction with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission as the prudential banking authority

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% (Gibraltar corporation tax on taxable income of companies; a Gazetted rate of 20% applies only to licensed utilities). The Cayman Exempted Company itself is chargeable at 0% in the Cayman Islands under the Tax Concessions Act (Cap. 60) upon grant of a Tax Exemption Certificate, so the combined statutory exposure is 12.5% solely on Gibraltar-source or Gibraltar-remitted profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. Gibraltar imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions to non-resident shareholders, and the Cayman Islands imposes no dividend withholding tax of any kind. A dividend paid by the Cayman Exempted Company to its UK or Gibraltar parent is therefore made without deduction at source.

Within six months after the end of the accounting period. For a 31 December year-end, the return is due on or before 30 November of the following year. Payment of the self-assessed corporation tax falls due on the same date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 500,000. A Local File must be prepared and filed with the return where the aggregate value of controlled transactions (goods, services, intangibles and financing) exceeds GIP 500,000 in the year of assessment. A Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds GIP 25 million and intra-group transactions exceed GIP 1 million. The arm's-length principle under section 12A of the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 applies to all controlled transactions regardless of threshold.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019 (as amended) and the Cayman Islands Economic Substance Law apply. Fast-fashion online retail (ISIC 4791) constitutes a 'distribution and service centre business' in both Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands, which is a relevant activity for economic substance purposes. The entity must demonstrate, in each jurisdiction where it claims tax residence or tax exemption: (a) direction and management exercised locally through board meetings at adequate intervals; (b) core income-generating activities such as inventory management, order fulfilment logistics, pricing strategy, risk-taking and supplier contracting performed in the jurisdiction; (c) an adequate number of full-time employees with appropriate qualifications physically present; (d) adequate operating expenditure incurred and physical premises maintained. Annual filings are compulsory: the Gibraltar Economic Substance Return (Form ESR) and the Cayman annual economic substance return, both accompanied by supporting records. Failure of the test in either jurisdiction recharacterizes profits and activates graduated financial penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Avoidance (Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010; Criminal Justice Act 2015)

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction: a fine up to the statutory maximum and imprisonment for up to 2 years. On indictment: an unlimited fine and imprisonment for up to 7 years. Civil penalties of up to 100% of the understated tax plus interest at 8% per annum are imposed concurrently.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in the fraudulent return are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, penalties and interest. The court may order civil recovery of assets, issue travel bans, and disqualify directors under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 for up to 15 years.
False Tax Residence and Sham Foreign Entity (Cayman Tax Concessions Act; Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, sections 3 and 8)

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment of the entity as UK tax resident or Gibraltar tax resident on worldwide profits for six years; revocation of the Cayman Tax Exemption Certificate; penalties of up to GIP 100,000 for false declarations and UK CFC or diverted-profits tax of 25% on reallocated profits.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil, rendering directors personally liable for three years of underpaid tax and interest. Directors face disqualification orders under section 108 of the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014, immigration stop-lists, and extradition exposure to UK HMRC criminal prosecutions.
Economic Substance and Shell Company Non-Compliance (Gibraltar ESR 2019; Cayman ES Law)

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar: financial penalties up to GIP 100,000 plus GIP 1,000 per day of continued non-compliance, and the Registrar may strike off the company. Cayman Islands: penalties escalating from CI$12,500 for a first contravention to CI$1,000,000 for a second or subsequent contravention, plus CI$10,000 per day; failure to satisfy the substance test also triggers automatic exchange of information with the UK.

Director personal liability: Officers who knowingly cause or permit the contravention are personally liable for the penalties and may be ordered to resign. Enforcement authorities may prohibit directors from leaving the jurisdiction and may impose personal fines equivalent to the corporate penalty.
Anti-Money Laundering and Financial Crime (Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar); AML/CFT Regulations 2019; UK AML Regulations 2019)

Statutory sanction: On conviction in Gibraltar: an unlimited fine and imprisonment for up to 14 years for the entity's senior officers; regulatory penalties from the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission of up to GIP 500,000 for systemic AML failures. The UK parent may be separately fined under the UK Money Laundering Regulations 2019.

Director personal liability: Criminal liability extends to the Money Laundering Reporting Officer and each director who consented to or connived in the offence. Penalties include personal fines, confiscation orders, director disqualification for up to 15 years, and travel bans pending investigation.
Permanent Establishment and Diverted Profits Tax (UK CTA 2010; UK Finance Act 2015)

Statutory sanction: Re-attribution of profits to the UK permanent establishment and assessment to UK corporation tax at 25%; application of the UK Diverted Profits Tax at 25% on the diverted profit; potential inheritance of UK VAT registration obligations and penalties for unregistered taxable supplies.

Director personal liability: A UK-resident director who is the Senior Accounting Officer may face personal penalties of GBP 5,000 per accounting period for failure to maintain accurate tax records; deliberate facilitation of PE avoidance triggers criminal referral to HMRC's Fraud Investigation Service and potential extradition proceedings.
