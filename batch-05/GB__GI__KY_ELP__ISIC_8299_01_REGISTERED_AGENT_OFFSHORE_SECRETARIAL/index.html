Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership; the Limited Partnership Agreement exclusively determines capital commitments and drawdown procedures. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Her Majesty's Treasury of Gibraltar); online filing portal: www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation

E-government filing portal

A Gibraltar-incorporated Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) is registered for tax purposes by its general partner acting through a licensed tax agent. The agent submits the partnership deed, Certificate of Incorporation/Limited Partnership Registration from the Companies House Gibraltar, a registered office address, and the appointment of the local general partner. The Income Tax Office issues a Tax Reference Number (TRN) and, where required, a separate Corporate Tax Identifier for the partnership. Registration must be completed within 30 days of the date of the limited partnership registration. Foreign partners with Gibraltar-source income are also required to file individual tax returns and obtain their own TRNs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box system applies in Gibraltar. The ELP is treated as tax transparent under the Income Tax Act 2010 and the Limited Partnerships Act. The General Partner may elect to file an annual 'Partnership Information Return' instead of a full tax return, unless the partnership has elected, by agreement of all partners, to be taxed as an entity under the 'Transparent Entity (Election) Regulations'. This election is rarely made for offshore secretarial vehicles operating under ISIC 8299. Hybrid mismatch elections under UK/Gibraltar domestic anti-avoidance rules are not available to ELPs that do not carry on an economic activity in the British Isles.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 85,000 annual taxable turnover of taxable supplies made into Gibraltar; voluntary registration is permitted below this threshold. For offshore secretarial and registered agent services supplied to non-residents, no GST registration arises unless the supplier makes substantial local supplies in Gibraltar.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Gibraltar-registered ELP may zero-rate its export of corporate secretarial, company administration, nominee directorship, and registered agent services when it is satisfied that the recipient is located outside Gibraltar and the direct benefit of the services is received outside Gibraltar. Supporting evidence includes a current Certificate of Business/Residence of the client, a services agreement expressly territorial, and the absence of any local use of the services. Zero-rating also applies to services directly related to the export of goods, to services performed outside Gibraltar for non-resident persons, and to incidental supplies of less than 10% of total consideration in a quarter. A zero-rated output entitles the provider to recover any GST incurred on attributable inputs (no refund is available for business entertainment).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar Customs C88 / Single Administrative Document) applicable to any physical goods
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing the overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice evidencing receipt of payment from the non-resident client

Gibraltar does not operate a real-time fiscal electronic clearance or e-invoicing stamping system. Registered businesses must submit quarterly GST returns electronically through the Government of Gibraltar's online GST portal. Standard invoices must be issued within 21 days of supply and must contain the GST registration number, date, description of services, and applicable GST rate. Electronic invoices are accepted without prior approval, provided they are retained for six years and can be produced to the Commissioner on demand. No digital stamping or continuous control (CFI) system is in force.

Gibraltar tax law treats false zero-rating claims as fraudulent evasion. Where the mandatory export documentation is not produced before the return is filed, the GST is immediately repayable with interest at 8% per annum. Deliberate misdeclaration, backdated contractual amendments, or fabricated remittance certificates attract penalties up to 150% of the under-declared tax and imprisonment on indictment for up to 7 years. In addition, the Commissioner may require a retrospective clawback of any GST repaid to the exporter, issue a tax assessment based on a deemed margin of 25% of turnover, and the taxpayer must also pay a customs administrative fine of up to GIP 10,000 per false declaration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar features unencumbered full convertibility under both current and capital accounts. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) circulates solely as sterling-equivalent legal tender, and there are no exchange control restrictions on profit remittances, capital withdrawals, or partnership distributions to non-resident partners. An Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) may freely receive partner capital contributions in any designated currency and repatriate profits without withholding tax, prior exchange-control approval, or compelled conversion into GIP. Compliance obligations extend only to AML/CTF transaction reporting, CRS/FATCA reporting on partner identity, and record-keeping for the GFSC-regulated business scope.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no time limit on the retention of export/service proceeds in foreign currency. Cross-border service receivables generated by the registered-agent and secretarial business may be retained in original denomination or converted at the entity's discretion. The only statutory requirements are accurate accounting, evidence of economic substance, and supportable source-of-funds documentation in line with Gibraltar AML/CTF legislation.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), acting as the financial regulator and banking conduct supervisor; given Gibraltar has no independent central bank, the Government of Gibraltar's Ministry of Finance oversees currency issuance (Gibraltar pound pegged 1:1 to GBP sterling), with operational alignment to the Bank of England's monetary framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium. UK/EU-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut, or Airwallex will generally accept a Gibraltar ELP as a client for day-to-day payment transaction accounts, but their regulatory licences do not automatically extend to Gibraltar under any passport right after the UK's exit from the EU. Such EMIs typically impose a non-EEA corporate onboarding path with strengthened due diligence and may reject the account if the ELP is treated as a high-risk 'company formation agent'. A GFSC-licensed EMI or a UK FCA-authorised EMI with an explicit cross-border servicing clause is preferable. For any escrow, client-money, or partner-fund holding function, only a fully licensed Gibraltar deposit-taking bank is viable.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Entity classified as a 'registered agent / company formation provider' engaged in offshore secretarial services, which is a heightened money-laundering risk category.

Root cause: Entity classified as a 'registered agent / company formation provider' engaged in offshore secretarial services, which is a heightened money-laundering risk category.

Defense evidence: Provide the GFSC-regulated TCSP licence (where held) or equivalent professional membership, the AML Compliance Manual approved for the Gibraltar jurisdiction, implemented client-due-diligence files for each partner, sworn declarations that client monies are never co-mingled with operating funds, and a board-approved risk assessment demonstrating robust source-of-funds verification.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For compliant inbound partner capital injections, use a SWIFT MT103 from the partner's own bank to the ELP's operational bank account in Gibraltar. The wire instruction must include the specific payment purpose code, typically 'CAPT' (capital transfer) or 'GDSV/INTP' for service-related contributions if structured as advance fees; in the absence of standard purpose codes, use the ISO 20022 purpose 'COLI' for capital contributions. A fully compliant reference must read: 'PARTNER CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION – [ELP name]/[date]/[partner UBO]' and, where applicable, be accompanied by a signed board/GP capital contribution resolution, an updated capital account statement, source-of-funds evidence (bank statements, audited accounts, tax returns), and a completed AML KYC declaration (including the origin of assets for the funding bank).For outbound liquidity and trade disbursements, the ELP uses SWIFT MT103 for GBP, USD, and other foreign currency payments (lawyer, registry, tax and administrative fees) from its Gibraltar bank. For euro-denominated trading within the EEA, SEPA Credit Transfer is the preferred clearable rail. Commercial letters of credit (MT700 issuance from a Gibraltar bank) are permissible and can support the ELP's ancillary client-facilitation activities, especially where the registered-agent business coordinates escrowed payments for goods or services; the documentation bundle includes the draft, transport document, insurance certificate and commercial invoice. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% (standard Gibraltar corporate tax rate for tax-resident companies; an Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) is generally tax-transparent and, if validly exempt and deriving only non-Gibraltar source profits, is chargeable at 0%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividend or partnership distribution payments made by a Gibraltar-resident company or an ELP to any shareholder or partner, whether resident or non-resident. Distributions of an ELP are treated as allocations of profit-sharing under the Limited Partnerships Act 2020 rather than dividends, and no Gibraltar statutory withholding mechanism exists.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Gibraltar Year of Assessment (YoA) runs 1 July to 30 June. The CTR-1 return and self-assessment for a YoA must be filed on or before 30 November immediately following the close of that YoA (e.g., YoA 2024/25 return due 30 November 2025). A company whose accounting period ends on 31 December is assessed on the profits of the basis period ending in the YoA. Balancing payments are due with the return; non-payment attracts statutory interest.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be maintained and filed with CTR-1 where the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds GIP 500,000 per Year of Assessment; Master File is required where the consolidated group revenue exceeds GIP 50,000,000; Country-by-Country Report is required where the group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000, with automatic exchange through the Gibraltar Competent Authority.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar has enacted Economic Substance Regulations under the Income Tax Act 2010, aligned with the EU Code of Conduct Group and OECD BEPS. Tax-resident entities, including ELPs with separate legal personality or partnerships that are otherwise in scope, carrying on any 'relevant activity' (banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, finance and leasing, headquarters, distribution and service centre, pure equity holding, and intellectual property) must satisfy an economic substance test: management and direction in Gibraltar, adequate physical premises and employees, and core income-generating activities performed in Gibraltar. A corporate secretarial or registered agent business is within scope only if it constitutes a 'distribution and service centre' for related parties or acts as a pure equity holding vehicle; provision of secretarial services to unrelated non-resident clients does not, by itself, trigger the full relevant-activity substance requirements, but a low-risk notification via Schedule ESR-1 is still required. The exempt ELP must additionally confirm that no ESR-relevant activity is carried on in Gibraltar; failure to do so invalidates the exemption and exposes the entity to additional assessments and regulatory action.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Territoriality Abuse

Statutory sanction: Additional assessments for up to six years (unlimited in fraud), 100% of the avoided tax as a penalty, statutory interest at 12.5% per annum, and criminal prosecution on indictment with an unlimited fine and imprisonment of up to 7 years for fraudulent evasion of income tax.

Director personal liability: The general partner, its directors, and any shadow controller are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; the Gibraltar courts may impose civil restitution orders, freeze personal assets, issue travel bans, and disqualify individuals from acting as company directors or licensed registered agents for up to 15 years.
Shell Company and False Substance Declaration

Statutory sanction: Strike-off and dissolution by the Gibraltar Registry under the Limited Partnerships Act 2020; retrospective withdrawal of the exemption; taxation on the entire worldwide profits (including foreign-source income) at 12.5% for up to six years; revocation of the registered agent's licence by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission under the Financial Services Act 2019; administrative fines of up to GIP 500,000 per breach.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the partnership veil to hold the general partner and the principals of the corporate secretarial provider personally accountable for unpaid tax, strike-off costs, and regulatory fines; travel bans, freezing orders, and disqualification orders are routinely sought, and extradition may be pursued under mutual legal assistance treaties where offshore evasion of a foreign tax is involved.
AML/CFT Breach by the Registered Agent and Secretarial Provider

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar) and the Financial Services Act 2019 (Gibraltar) for money laundering with imprisonment up to 14 years and an unlimited fine; the GFSC may impose administrative penalties of up to 5% of annual group revenue or GIP 2,000,000, suspend or revoke the licence, and issue public censures; the ELP's assets are subject to confiscation proceedings.

Director personal liability: Individual directors, the MLRO, and the compliance officer face personal criminal liability, civil restitution to victims, prohibition orders from regulated activity, travel bans pending investigation, and imprisonment for up to 14 years; the corporate veil of the registered agent will be disregarded where it is found to be a facade for the partners' unlawful conduct.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of up to GIP 200,000 for missing documentation; a secondary penalty of 10% of the value of the undisclosed controlled transactions; transfer pricing adjustments that increase taxable profits are assessed with interest; deliberate or careless mispricing attracts a further penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax; transactions may be recharacterised where economic substance is absent.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner are jointly and severally liable for the additional tax and penalties where they knowingly participated in the mispricing or failed to cause the ELP to comply; the Commissioner may obtain personal bankruptcy or winding-up orders, asset freezing injunctions, and court-imposed travel restrictions; criminal prosecution for tax evasion is available where the mispricing was intended to evade tax.
