Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar: Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar, and Customs Division for indirect/import duties (portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax); Cayman Islands: Tax Information Authority / Department of International Tax Cooperation for exchange of information (portal: https://www.tia.gov.ky) and General Registry for exempted-company incorporation (portal: https://www.ciregistry.gov.ky); United Kingdom: HM Revenue & Customs for any UK VAT registration or UK taxable nexus (portal: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc).

E-government filing portal

Cayman Exempted Company: (1) incorporate via an approved registered office provider at the Cayman Islands General Registry; (2) obtain Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, and Register of Directors and Officers filings; (3) apply for a statutory Tax Exemption Undertaking under section 6 of the Tax Concessions Act, which provides contractual assurance of no Cayman income, corporate, capital gains, estate, inheritance or withholding taxes for 20 years, extendable by 10 years. Gibraltar: registration with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office is not automatic for a foreign exempted company; it is required only if central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar (making the company Gibraltar tax-resident) or if Gibraltar-source income arises; the company then obtains a Gibraltar Income Tax Reference Number. UK: no automatic UK TIN; HMRC VAT registration is required only where UK VAT liability arises, typically via the online non-established taxable person registration process, with no threshold for digital B2C supplies to UK consumers.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Cayman) combined with Territorial_Scope analysis for Gibraltar. In the Cayman Islands the exempted company is statutorily tax-exempt and is not subject to entity-level income tax. In Gibraltar the entity is considered non-resident unless managed and controlled in Gibraltar; as non-resident it is within Gibraltar tax jurisdiction only for Gibraltar-source income and no capital gains tax or inheritance tax generally applies. The entity is not treated as Pass_Through_Eligible in Gibraltar or the UK for corporate tax purposes; the UK and Gibraltar apply entity-level taxation concepts and anti-hybrid rules may reclassify the entity if structured to exploit mismatches.

Available election forms

Cayman Islands: the only material election is the Tax Exemption Undertaking under section 6 of the Tax Concessions Act, chosen at incorporation or by undertaking filed by a Cayman Exempted Company; it creates a binding assurance that no tax is imposed in Cayman for up to 20 years, with possible extension to 30 years. Gibraltar: no UK-style check-the-box election exists; Gibraltar tax law applies its own rules based on residence and source, and a foreign exempted company may elect to be treated as a non-resident company or may be taxed as resident if management and control occurs in Gibraltar. United Kingdom: no check-the-box election; hybrid mismatch rules under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A and UK CFC rules may disregard or recompute the tax-exempt status of the Cayman entity where an actual or deemed mismatch arises.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST or Sales and Use Tax applies in Gibraltar or in the Cayman Islands. The only relevant indirect-tax system for this structure is UK VAT, which arises only if the exempted company creates a UK taxable nexus (e.g., a UK establishment, UK B2C digital-service supplies, or a UK business customer in specified reverse-charge cases). Gibraltar applies Customs import duties and excise taxes on physical goods, but these are not turnover taxes and are not triggered by payroll-SaaS digital services.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in Gibraltar or Cayman: neither territory imposes VAT or GST, so there is no economic turnover threshold triggering registration. For UK VAT, the standard registration threshold is GBP 90,000 of taxable turnover; however, a non-established overseas entity supplying digital services (payroll SaaS) to UK consumers must register for UK VAT from the first supply and has no turnover threshold. UK B2B supplies to a UK VAT-registered customer may fall under the reverse charge, shifting accounting to the UK customer and reducing or eliminating the supplier's UK registration obligation. Gibraltar import-duty obligations arise on physical imports with no trader turnover threshold, under the Customs Ordinance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For the payroll SaaS digital service, export zero-rating as understood for physical goods is not applicable because Gibraltar and Cayman have no VAT. If the entity becomes UK VAT registered, the following substantive rules apply: (i) physical goods exported from the UK to addresses outside the UK are zero-rated at 0% where the supplier keeps the required export evidence and the goods leave the UK within the statutory period; (ii) electronically supplied services such as payroll SaaS supplied to a non-UK customer are outside the scope of UK VAT (rather than zero-rated), provided the place of supply is outside the UK; (iii) B2C digital services supplied to UK consumers remain subject to UK VAT at 20%; (iv) no zero-rating can be claimed for services consumed inside the UK or in Gibraltar. Where a claim is made for UK VAT zero-rating on physical export, the supplier must prove that the goods have left the UK and that title/ownership passed to the overseas customer, with transport documentation and payment evidence retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (UK CDS electronic declaration or Gibraltar customs declaration if physical goods are moved via Gibraltar)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export movement and destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 credit evidence showing settlement of the export consideration
  • Commercial invoice and packing list consistent with the export declaration
  • Customs duty/tax receipts or C88/SAD proof where applicable
  • For digital payroll SaaS: contractual service agreement and customer establishment evidence demonstrating place of supply outside the UK

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government-authorised electronic invoicing mandate exists in Gibraltar or the Cayman Islands. If UK VAT registration becomes applicable, the company must comply with UK Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT: VAT returns must be submitted digitally via HMRC-approved software through MTD application programming interfaces; there is no per-invoice clearance or pre-clearance stamping. The company must retain transactional records in a functional compatible format and file quarterly VAT returns using MTD-compatible bridging software. No B2B e-invoicing mandate applies to UK supplies.

HMRC and Gibraltar Customs apply strict documentary proof standards in zero-rating and export claims. Missing or inconsistent export evidence causes the zero-rating to be denied, output VAT to be added, and input VAT previously deducted to be clawed back; this can be retrospective for the full vulnerable period. Civil penalties under the UK Finance Act 1994 for careless inaccuracies and deliberate VAT evasion can reach 30% and 70-100% of the tax, respectively; fraudulent evasion of VAT is a criminal offence punishable by up to 7 years imprisonment and unlimited fines. HMRC may issue assessments up to 20 years for deliberate non-notification or concealment, and may invoke Contractual Disclosure Facility / Code of Practice 9 for civil-fraud investigations. In Gibraltar, customs false declarations and duty evasion carry penalties up to 100% of the duty, forfeiture of goods, and potential criminal prosecution. In Cayman, no indirect-tax clawback arises, but a material misstatement may affect the validity of the Tax Exemption Undertaking and trigger enhanced anti-tax-evasion due-diligence under international exchange-of-information obligations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains a fully liberalised exchange-control regime; there are no residual UK or GIP restrictions on current-account flows (SaaS licence fees, payroll processing, service fees) or capital-account flows (equity injection, shareholder loans, dividend remittance, capital redemption). The GIP is fully and unconditionally convertible into GBP at par, and the Cayman Exempted Company may freely repatriate profits and capital without exchange-control approval, withholding, or scheduled conversion hurdles. The only practical constraints are bank-level CDD/EDD and reporting duties under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the AML Regulations 2019.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion obligation in the United Kingdom or Gibraltar. Exporters and SaaS licensors may retain 100% of foreign-currency export receipts indefinitely in any currency; the mandatory conversion ratio is nil and the statutory retention timeline is unlimited. The only administrative reporting requirement is the standard suspicious-transaction reporting threshold and the CRS/FATCA classification of the receiving account.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (HM Treasury) acting as the Sterling-area monetary authority, with the Government of Gibraltar issuing the Gibraltar pound (GIP) under a 1:1 GBP currency-board peg; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory supervisor for banking, foreign-exchange dealing and AML/CFT compliance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Conditional viability (Medium). GFSC-licensed electronic money institutions and payment institutions, aligned with PSD2-equivalent Gibraltar legislation, can onboard a Cayman Exempted Company only under Enhanced Due Diligence; the tax-exempt offshore classification triggers an automatic high-risk score at most UK/EU digital EMIs. A GFSC-licensed EMI is recommended solely as a disbursement aggregator for global payroll if the EMI maintains settlement balances at a Tier-1 Gibraltar bank and the company never takes possession of third-party client funds. Fully offshore digital EMIs without a physical licensed banking partner or without a Gibraltar/GBP settlement backbone are rated Low and should be excluded from the treasury spine.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: shareholder banks remit by SWIFT MT103 for direct payment, or MT202 for correspondent routing, using purpose code S010 (capital contribution) or L200 (shareholder loan) and a remittance narrative carrying the beneficiary entity name and 'Capital Injection – Exempted Co'. UK domestic GBP funding uses CHAPS or Faster Payments; EUR funding uses TARGET2/SEPA Credit Transfer; USD funding uses Fedwire or CHIPS via a correspondent. Before value date, the receiving Gibraltar bank requires the Cayman registered agent's confirmation of allotment, a board resolution, an executed subscription deed and a complete source-of-funds pack. GIP/GBP conversion is executed at par under the currency-board peg; other-currency conversions are at bank spot. Any inbound payment at or above GBP 10,000 / EUR 10,000 triggers automatic CDD refresh, so the full evidentiary dossier must be pre-positioned with the relationship manager.Payroll disbursements: SWIFT MT103 for cross-border non-UK/non-EEA salaries, SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant for EUR payees, Faster Payments / BACS / CHAPS for UK-based employees, and GFSC-licensed EMI or ACH aggregators for United States payroll via ACH. Software-licence and platform-trade settlements use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code S182 (software licence fees) or G100 (IT and technology services). Commercial Letters of Credit are not a natural instrument for SaaS intangibles and are used only as stand-by or performance LC facilities where a client demands documentary security. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (Cayman Exempted Company statutory tax exemption; Gibraltar's ordinary non-qualifying corporate rate is 10% on Gibraltar-source trading profits attributable to a permanent establishment)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% in Gibraltar and 0% in the Cayman Islands; no Gibraltar withholding tax is imposed on dividends paid by a Gibraltar-resident company or a Cayman Exempted Company to its non-resident shareholder

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 (aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions); a Gibraltar Local File/Master File must be prepared and retained when the group attains GIP 1,000,000 in controlled transactions or GIP 75,000,000 consolidated turnover, with files produced within 30 days of the Commissioner's written notice

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The Cayman Exempted Company is subject to the Cayman Islands Economic Substance Regulations if it carries on a relevant activity such as a distribution and service centre, financing business, headquarters business or intellectual property business. A Gibraltar qualifying company is subject to Gibraltar's economic substance test requiring local office premises, qualified employees, board meetings and decision-making in Gibraltar. The payroll-automation entity must demonstrate genuine business substance in the jurisdiction of claimed residency or face loss of the 0% exemption, reclassification of income and automatic exchange of information
