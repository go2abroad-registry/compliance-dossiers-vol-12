Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), portal: taxoffice.gov.gi; Gibraltar Companies House, portal: companieshouse.gi. Cayman Islands – Registrar of Companies, General Registry (registry.gov.ky) and Department for International Tax Cooperation (DITC, ditc.ky) for economic substance administration. UK HMRC (hmrc.gov.uk) is not the direct filing authority for a Gibraltar-registered foreign company, but UK CFC and anti-hybrid rules may apply to UK parent groups.

E-government filing portal

Sequence: (1) Incorporate the Cayman Exempted Company under the Cayman Companies Act with a registered office and licensed registered agent; obtain the Cayman company number. (2) If the Exempted Co establishes a place of business in Gibraltar, register as an external/overseas company with the Gibraltar Companies House within one month under the Companies (External Companies) Regulations; no minimum issued capital applies. (3) Apply to the Gibraltar Commissioner of Income Tax for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number on the prescribed registration form, attaching the certified certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, director/secretary particulars, and evidence of a Gibraltar tax representative. (4) File the annual Gibraltar corporation tax return (Form 7) even where nil local profits arise, applying the territorial source rule. (5) File the annual Cayman Economic Substance Return (ES-IP) with DITC. (6) No VAT/GST registration is possible or required in Gibraltar; a separate HM Customs Trader Registration is triggered only where physical import/export occurs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: the Cayman Exempted Company bears no corporate income tax, capital gains tax, withholding tax, or indirect tax in the Cayman Islands. Territorial_Scope (Gibraltar): as an external company it is chargeable to Gibraltar income tax at the 10% standard corporate rate solely on profits accruing in or derived from Gibraltar under s.5 Income Tax Act 2010; worldwide IP royalty income managed and controlled outside Gibraltar, with no Gibraltar permanent establishment, falls outside the Gibraltar charge. Entity_Level_Taxation at 0% effective rate describes the Cayman result.

Available election forms

Neither Gibraltar nor the Cayman Islands operates a US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. Statutory mechanisms available: (1) Cayman ES Law tax-residency election – the Exempted Co may declare tax residence in Gibraltar in its annual DITC filing and furnish a Gibraltar Certificate of Tax Residence, substituting foreign tax residence for Cayman substance testing; (2) Gibraltar group taxation election under the Income Tax Act 2010 group relief provisions where a 75% qualifying relationship exists with a Gibraltar-resident member; (3) election for the external company to be treated as non-Gibraltar resident where central management and control is exercised abroad, preserving the territorial exclusion; (4) election to apply BEPS Action 2 anti-hybrid alignment rules where a counterpart jurisdiction treats the Cayman entity as tax-transparent.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar: import/customs duty and excise duty regime only – no VAT, no GST, no sales tax, and no services tax; Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area. Cayman Islands: import duty on physical goods only – no VAT, no GST, no sales tax, and no tax on intangibles or royalty flows.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: no VAT/GST registration threshold applies because the system is absent; royalty and service exports bear 0% indirect taxation. Physical goods importers must obtain a Customs Trader ID from the first import, with de minimis relief for low-value non-commercial consignments. Cayman: commercial importers require an importer licence from the first import (effective threshold CI$0); cross-border IP licensing payments are wholly outside the scope of Cayman import duty.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar: no VAT mechanism exists, so the equivalent benefit is the territorial exclusion of offshore royalty income from the 10% corporate tax. To qualify the Exempted Co must prove: (i) no Gibraltar fixed establishment or dependent-agent PE; (ii) central management and control exercised outside Gibraltar; (iii) IP assets registered, protected, and exploited outside Gibraltar; (iv) licensing agreements negotiated and executed offshore; (v) no Gibraltar-linked royalty collection or servicing activity. The Cayman Islands automatically apply a 0% rate to all Exempted Co income; the company must nonetheless satisfy the Cayman ES Law IP-business reduced-substance test and file the annual ES-IP return. The 180-day repatriation window reflects intercompany licensing norms rather than a Gibraltar or Cayman statute, as neither territory imposes exchange controls.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar SAD/ASYCUDA World entry; required only where physical media or prototypes accompany the licensing flow)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (physical-goods conduit; for pure IP licensing a signed master-license acceptance certificate is substituted)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 evidencing royalty receipt into the company's Gibraltar or Cayman bank account
  • Executed patent master licence and sub-licence agreements stipulating royalty rates, territory, patent numbers, and withholding-tax allocation
  • Patent registration certificates and full assignment/prosecution chain from UKIPO, EPO, or WIPO/PCT registries
  • Cayman ESR-IP annual return acknowledgement and Gibraltar external-company registration certificate

Neither Gibraltar nor the Cayman Islands imposes real-time fiscal clearance, transactional tax e-invoicing, digital stamping, or an API clearance mandate for IP licensing. Gibraltar customs declarations are processed electronically via ASYCUDA World; Cayman customs imports are declared through the Cayman iDeclare system. Standard commercial e-invoicing (XML/PEPPOL/PDF) is accepted; no fiscal validation stamp is required.

Gibraltar: the Commissioner may re-assess and re-characterise royalty flows found to accrue in or derive from Gibraltar, apply GAAR and dependent-agent PE rules, extend assessments up to 20 years in fraud or wilful default, and impose interest at 10% p.a. plus civil penalties of 50%-150% of the undercharged tax; criminal prosecution may lead to fines up to GBP 5,000 and imprisonment. BEPS Action 2 anti-hybrid provisions may claw back deductions achieved through jurisdiction mismatch. Cayman: false or late ES-IP filings attract CI$10,000 initial penalty, continuing monthly penalties up to CI$100,000, imprisonment up to 2 years for wilful non-compliance, and potential strike-off of the Exempted Company; fraudulent claims of Gibraltar tax residence also expose the structure to Gibraltar retrospective clawbacks and substance-related penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility, consistent with UK exchange-control abolition in 1979. The GIP is freely convertible into GBP/EUR/USD with no central-bank approval required for royalty, dividend, interest, or principal remittances by non-resident companies. A Cayman exempted company holding global IP may freely convert and repatriate licensing income without exchange-control authorization, provided that bank-required beneficial ownership, source-of-funds, and economic-substance documentation is maintained. There are no restrictions on holding offshore accounts or pricing intercompany royalty transactions beyond normal transfer-pricing rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline for export or royalty proceeds. Receipts from global patent and trademark sub-licensing may be retained in any currency indefinitely. The Cayman IP HoldCo must nonetheless document receipt dates, invoice references, and ultimate remittance to the Gibraltar treasury account for audit, tax-residence, and AML traceability purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for pound sterling); the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the local banking and AML supervisor and administers exchange-control policy, although Gibraltar has no independent central bank or autonomous monetary policy. The Gibraltar pound is issued by the Government of Gibraltar at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. Gibraltar-licensed electronic money institutions are limited in number, and UK FCA-authorized EMIs no longer passport into Gibraltar post-Brexit. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Mercury, Airwallex, Wise Business) generally decline tax-exempt offshore IP HoldCos as a matter of internal risk policy or require a physical Gibraltar registered office, a locally appointed director, and an active operating history. Legally, remote-only onboarding without a Gibraltar-licensed introducer or notary is disfavored by the GFSC AML framework, and the Cayman exempted company must first be registered as an Overseas Company in Gibraltar before any EMI or bank account can be opened.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from the parent or ultimate beneficial owners should be executed via SWIFT MT103 for wire transfers (or MT202 for interbank/treasury settlement) using the bank's correspondent network: CHAPS for GBP, TARGET2 for EUR, and Fedwire/CHIPS for USD. The payment must carry the corporate beneficiary name, the Gibraltar Overseas Company registration number, and a compliant ISO 20022 purpose code: 'INTC' for intra-group loans or equity contributions, and 'ROYL' or 'LIC' for royalty advances. Complete supporting documentation — board resolution authorizing the borrowing, the executed loan or subscription agreement, and the beneficial-ownership register — must be pre-delivered to the receiving Gibraltar bank to avoid freezing or rejection of the inbound credit.Outbound royalty and trade settlements should be cleared through SWIFT MT103 over the Gibraltar bank's correspondent network: UK clearing banks for GBP, continental European correspondents for EUR, and New York correspondents for USD. For high-value or documentary trade transactions, commercial letters of credit (UCP600) confirmed by a top-tier UK bank are the preferred rail. Intercompany royalty disbursements to the parent or licensor should be executed with purpose code 'ROYL' plus the underlying license agreement reference; treasury netting may be applied for intra-group flows, with daily liquidity positions settled across CHAPS/TARGET2/Fedwire. All outbound payments must align with the approved transfer-pricing schedule to preserve tax integrity.Outright deliverable FX forwards in GBP, EUR, and USD; the GIP is pegged at 1:1 to GBP and freely convertible, so deliverable forwards eliminate exchange risk without basis adjustment.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted currencies (e.g., CNY, INR, or other non-convertible licensee-territory currencies) to hedge royalty receivables, executed under an ISDA 1992/2002 master agreement with a GFSC- or FCA-regulated counterparty and subject to documented underlying commercial exposure.Vanilla FX options and FX swaps to hedge quarterly royalty cash-flow mismatches and rebalancing requirements.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCS) for long-dated, fixed-rate IP licensing fee streams denominated in EUR or USD, matched against GBP operating expenses.

Due within six months after the end of the company accounting period; a 31 December year-end therefore requires filing by 30 June of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where the aggregate value of controlled transactions equals or exceeds GIP 1,000,000 per accounting period; Master File is mandatory where group consolidated revenue equals or exceeds GIP 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations classify IP ownership and patent licensing as a relevant activity. A Gibraltar-resident IP HoldCo must demonstrate core income-generating activities in Gibraltar, including strategic decision-making, active management and exploitation of the patents and licences, and adequate qualified staff, premises and expenditure proportionate to the level of profits. The annual Economic Substance Return must be filed regardless of the Cayman Exempted Co governance label.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / deliberate understatement of royalty income

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax, daily default fines, criminal fines of up to GIP 1,000,000 and imprisonment of up to seven years; an estimated assessment may be raised on the best available information.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise the concealment are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, may be disqualified for up to 15 years, subjected to travel bans and asset freezing, and may face imprisonment.
Economic Substance Regulations breach (shell IP company)

Statutory sanction: A significant fixed penalty for first non-compliance, daily penalties for continued failure, a secondary penalty of 10% of relevant turnover for egregious breaches, possible strike-off from the Gibraltar Companies Register, and automatic exchange of information with HMRC and foreign tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors can be made personally liable for the unpaid corporate penalty; the court may issue a disqualification order, impose travel restrictions, and order confiscation of property.
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: A transfer-pricing adjustment to the arm's length profit, disallowance of the corresponding deduction, statutory late-payment interest, and a default penalty of up to 100% of the additional corporation tax.

Director personal liability: If the TP failure is attributable to a director's deliberate or reckless conduct, the director is personally liable for the additional tax, penalties and interest; a travel ban and imprisonment may follow.
AML / financial sanctions / shell company abuse

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fines of up to 10% of annual turnover or GIP 2,000,000, asset freezing orders, confiscation orders, and criminal sentences of up to five years for natural persons under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.

Director personal liability: A director who directs, consents to, or facilitates the prohibited payment is personally liable, subject to arrest, extradition, travel bans, and criminal confiscation of benefits traced to the breach.
