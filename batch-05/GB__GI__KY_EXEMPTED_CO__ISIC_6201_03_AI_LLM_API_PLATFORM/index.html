Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — Gibraltar does not levy VAT or GST; therefore no zero-rating mechanism exists for export of goods or offshore AI API services. Digital API supplies are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation regardless of customer location.

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping regime applies in Gibraltar. Standard commercial invoices are sufficient for straight taxable supplies.

No export zero-rating claim arises for clawback or penalty purposes due to the absence of VAT. Failure to satisfy the Commissioner of Income Tax may result in reassessments at the 10% rate, interest at the statutory rate, civil penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded, and criminal sanctions for fraudulent misstatements.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar is part of the United Kingdom for monetary and financial stability purposes but does not automatically adopt the euro; the official currency is the Gibraltar pound (GIP), pegged at parity with the British pound sterling (GBP). Both GIP and GBP circulate freely. Capital account convertibility is unrestricted under the UK-Gibraltar joint financial framework; there are no surrender requirements, no exchange controls, and no restrictions on current account transactions for non-resident owned exempted companies. Profit remittance hurdles are effectively nil for a Cayman exempted company operating through a Gibraltar-licensed or Gibraltar-banked vehicle, provided the entity maintains substantive economic presence in Gibraltar or uses Gibraltar solely as a treasury booking centre with appropriate substance and AML/KYC documentation. The Bank of England does not administer exchange controls; Gibraltar's Financial Services Commission implements EU-derived anti-money laundering directives and UK sanctions regimes. A practical constraint is that the GIP is not freely traded internationally; therefore, settlement in GBP or USD via UK clearing banks or global correspondent banks is the standard commercial approach. The FX regime is fully liberalised for both current and capital transactions, with no mandatory conversion obligations, no repatriation-related tax clearances, and no central bank prior approval for cross-border payments. The only regulatory obstacle is the need to evidence beneficial ownership and source of funds under the GFSC AML/CFT framework and UK economic substance requirements where applicable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules exist for Gibraltar. Export proceeds and inbound service revenues (including AI LLM API licensing fees and token sale proceeds) are fully retainable in any currency. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no timeline for repatriation, and no requirement to exchange export receipts into GIP or GBP. The Cayman exempted company, as the ultimate parent, may hold revenues in USD, EUR, GBP, or USDC via a Gibraltar corporate bank account or a UK correspondent account, provided the funds are routed through a regulated financial institution and proper commercial documentation is maintained. For tax treaty and substance purposes, the Gibraltar operating entity should ensure that economic substance tests are met by maintaining management, employees, and revenue-generating activities in Gibraltar, since the absence of substance may invite UK CFC or Gibraltar anti-abuse scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Prudential Regulation Authority) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the Gibraltar competent authority for banking and payment services; Her Majesty's Treasury (HM Treasury) retains ultimate sanctions and financial crime policy authority over Gibraltar as a British Overseas Territory.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High subject to structural optimisation. Offshore digital EMI issuance directly for a Cayman exempted company is legally possible for account services with the following conditions: the EMI is licensed under the UK FCA Electronic Money Regulations 2011 or the Gibraltar Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act, or is authorised under a recognised EEA-equivalent regime (e.g., Lithuanian or Maltese EMI) that permits cross-border payment services into Gibraltar under financial services passporting or third-country equivalence rules. The Cayman exempted company may open an EMI account remotely, but the EMI is prohibited from providing payment services that touch UK banking infrastructure unless it has an agency relationship with a UK clearing bank. For the target AI LLM API Platform business (USDC-denominated and Stripe-flowing revenues), a dual-rail structure is recommended: (1) a Gibraltar GIB corporate account for USD/GBP fiat settlements, and (2) an FCA-authorised EMI account (e.g., a tier-1 EMI such as Wise/Revolut Business or a direct GFSC-licensed EMI) for inbound customer API subscription collections and outbound disbursements. The EMI viability is High for payment collection from customers in EU/UK/APAC and Low for capital escrow and long-term treasury custody because EMIs do not provide lending, prime brokerage, or complex FX hedging. The legal constraint: EMIs cannot hold crypto-assets unless dual-licensed under the UK/GFSC regime. Since the target platform includes USDC payments, the rUSD stablecoin issued by Gibraltar's regulated DLT framework can be used within scope; however, custody of USDC must be maintained either with an FCA-registered crypto-asset firm or a GFSC Distributed Ledger Technology (DLT) provider, not the EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Cayman exempted company is the parent and the Gibraltar operating entity (if any) is the regulated/operational subsidiary. For inbound capital injection from the Cayman parent into the Gibraltar treasury account, the compliant protocol is a share subscription wiring from the Cayman bank account or its legal counsel trust account into the Gibraltar bank's operational account using SWIFT MT103 with purpose code GBBP (Business and Personal Purposes) or code 'E011' for intra-group services under UK reporting guidelines. The wire instruction should include: full legal names of both entities; Cayman reference number; invoice or shareholder resolution reference; and a narrative string matching the corporate banking mandate (e.g., 'SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION 201/2024'). Anti-money-laundering verification should include a certified board resolution of the Cayman company authorising the injection, the share register entry, and a certified copy of the Gibraltar entity's certificate of incorporation. For the target AI LLM API Platform business (USDC-denominated and Stripe-flowing revenues), a dual-rail structure is recommended: (1) a Gibraltar GIB corporate account for USD/GBP fiat settlements, and (2) an FCA-authorised EMI account (e.g., a tier-1 EMI such as Wise/Revolut Business or a direct GFSC-licensed EMI) for inbound customer API subscription collections and outbound disbursements. The EMI viability is High for payment collection from customers in EU/UK/APAC and Low for capital escrow and long-term treasury custody because EMIs do not provide lending, prime brokerage, or complex FX hedging. The legal constraint: EMIs cannot hold crypto-assets unless dual-licensed under the UK/GFSC regime. Since the target platform includes USDC payments, the rUSD stablecoin issued by Gibraltar's regulated DLT framework can be used within scope; however, custody of USDC must be maintained either with an FCA-registered crypto-asset firm or a GFSC Distributed Ledger Technology (DLT) provider, not the EMI.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File) is required in Gibraltar where aggregate controlled transactions of the Gibraltar PE exceed GGP 1,000,000 (approximately GBP 1,000,000 / USD 1.25 million) per tax year; the Master File and Country-by-Country Reporting threshold is group consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more (OECD/G20 BEPS Action 13 threshold).

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply, implementing EU and OECD harmful-tax-practices criteria. The Cayman Exempted Company carrying on 'relevant activities' in Gibraltar — including 'distribution and service centre' or 'intellectual property' (AI/ML algorithms and LLM platform intangibles) — must demonstrate in each reporting period: (i) premises in Gibraltar; (ii) qualified full-time employees in Gibraltar proportionate to the AI API activity; (iii) core income-generating activities performed in Gibraltar; and (iv) annual expenditure adequate to the activity. A pure equity-holding company may rely on the reduced substance test. Failure is progressively penalised.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Deliberate Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Assessment on a best-of-judgment basis with the understated tax payable; a penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded plus statutory interest; criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion with an unlimited fine and/or imprisonment up to 7 years; confiscation proceedings under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the misdeclaration are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax; the court may make personal restitution orders, authorise confiscation of personal assets, issue director disqualification orders, impose travel bans and passport surrender, and pass custodial sentences up to 7 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance (ESR) Failure

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty up to GGP 100,000 per accounting period of non-compliance, with daily additional penalties for continuing default; the Commissioner may re-tax the entity as Gibraltar-resident on worldwide income at 12.5% and disregard artificial arrangements; exchange of information with Cayman and UK authorities under CRS and the Exchange of Information framework.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal civil restitution to the Crown for the tax shortfall, asset freezes and travel restrictions under AML/terrorist-financing measures, director disqualification, and criminal conviction for knowingly furnishing false ESR declarations carrying imprisonment up to 10 years.
Transfer-Pricing Distortion / Abusive Intra-Group Payments

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer-pricing adjustment under the Income Tax Act 2010 restoring arm's-length profit with 12.5% tax on the uplift; secondary adjustment treating the excess as a deemed distribution or loan; penalties equal to the additional tax plus interest; mandatory disclosure escalation and audit referral.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign returns relying on fictitious transfer prices are personally accountable for the additional tax and penalties; courts may require civil compensatory damages to the company; persistent abuse results in disqualification, financial restraint orders, and criminal fraud liability added to the corporate debt.
