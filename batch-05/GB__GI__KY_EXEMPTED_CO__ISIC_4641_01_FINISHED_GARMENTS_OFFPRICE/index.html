Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital, authorized capital ceiling, or escrow deposit requirement under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014; a single subscriber share of GBP 1.00, fully paid, suffices. The exempted-company structure (non-resident ownership, no local capital funding condition) is preserved under the Qualifying Company regime, and capital duty is abolished by the Finance Centre Act 2011.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar — Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax) and HM Customs Gibraltar (Customs & Excise); central portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi. For United Kingdom-linked VAT and customs: HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) via https://www.tax.service.gov.uk and https://www.gov.uk/eori. The Gibraltar Reference Number (GRN) serves as the entity's Gibraltar TIN.

E-government filing portal

1) Appoint a licensed Gibraltar corporate services provider (CSP). 2) File Form RO-1 (overseas company registration) with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies to obtain the Gibraltar company number. 3) Apply to the Income Tax Office for the Gibraltar Reference Number (GRN) - the statutory TIN used for all filings, CRS/FATCA reporting and the annual Economic Substance return; no VAT ID is issued in Gibraltar as no VAT exists. 4) Register with HM Customs Gibraltar for a customs declarant/trader identifier; for goods cleared through Great Britain, obtain a GB EORI from HMRC. 5) File the Cayman Economic Substance notification with the Cayman Tax Information Authority (wholesale distribution is a relevant activity); if UK supplies commence, register for UK VAT under VATA 1994 Sch 3A (overseas person) once the GBP 90,000 threshold is exceeded.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Cayman Tax Exemption Certificate under s.6 of the Companies Act - a statutory 20-year, renewable guarantee against future Cayman direct taxation; obtained by filing an application with the Cayman General Registry. Gibraltar non-resident election under the Income Tax Act 2010 - the entity elects to maintain central management and control outside Gibraltar and confirms no Gibraltar-source income, so only non-resident tax treatment applies (12.5% corporate rate applies to resident companies, and only on Gibraltar-source income); a tax treatment certificate may be sought from the Commissioner. Hybrid mismatch election - Gibraltar Taxes (Hybrid Mismatch) Rules and UK CTA 2010 Pt 6TA allow a formal joint election with counterparties to align opaque status and prevent deduction/non-inclusion mismatches. UK VAT election - the entity may join a VAT group (VATA 1994 s.43) or elect overseas-person registration (VATA 1994 Sch 3A) without a UK establishment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Import Duty (Gibraltar Customs Ordinance) - no VAT, GST or sales tax applies in Gibraltar. UK VAT (Value Added Tax Act 1994) governs only supplies linked to Great Britain, including export zero-rating and import VAT.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in Gibraltar (no VAT/GST regime exists). For UK VAT, mandatory registration arises when taxable turnover exceeds GBP 90,000 over a rolling 12-month period (VATA 1994 Sch 1); voluntary registration, B2C distance-selling registration and overseas-person registration begin at GBP 0.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT, so exports of finished garments from Gibraltar are inherently untaxed (no Gibraltar GST/export duty and no zero-rating application is required). For UK VAT exposure, exports from Great Britain to a non-UK destination qualify for the 0% rate under VATA 1994 s.30(6) and Group 8, Schedule 8 where: (1) the goods are physically exported by the supplier or its agent; (2) export occurs within the statutory time limits (normally one month from supply; extended rules for freight-forwarders); (3) the supplier retains full export evidence (CDS export declarations, bills of lading, port departure records); and (4) the customer is outside the UK. Offshore B2B services (liquidation brokerage, sourcing commissions) are outside the scope of UK VAT under the place-of-supply rules (VATA 1994 s.7A; SI 1992/3121) when the customer belongs in a non-UK country and the customer's foreign VAT/TIN is evidenced; the supplier must retain a service contract and the customer's TIN to substantiate the cross-border treatment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (HMRC CDS electronic SAD export declaration or Gibraltar Customs declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / CMR) evidencing the export movement and destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103/MT202 or ACH credit confirmation evidencing export proceeds credited to the entity's account)
  • Commercial Invoice (buyer, HS code, values and incoterms)
  • Packing List
  • Proof of Physical Export (port exit stamp, pre-lodged GVMS departure notification or destination landing confirmation)

Gibraltar imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no e-invoicing API mandate and no digital stamping. UK VAT-registered entities must file quarterly returns via HMRC's Making Tax Digital (MTD) API using compatible software. Customs declarations are lodged electronically through HMRC's Customs Declaration Service (CDS); roll-on/roll-off freight uses the Goods Vehicle Movement Service (GVMS) for pre-lodgement. No SAF-T or Continuous Transaction Controls (CTC) obligation applies to this ISIC 4641 scope.

HMRC may retrospectively assess a wrongly zero-rated export for up to 4 years (ordinary error), 6 years (careless) or 20 years (deliberate/concealed) under FA 2008 Sch 39; penalties reach 100% of the VAT lost (FA 2007 Sch 24 for inaccuracies; Sch 25 for deliberate wrongdoing). Fraudulent evasion of VAT is a criminal offence under VATA 1994 s.72 (up to 7 years' imprisonment and an unlimited fine). False exports or missing-trader involvement trigger disallowance of input VAT, refund clawbacks, Customs civil penalties (TA 1979; up to the higher of £3,000 or 30% of duty plus forfeiture) and POCA 2002 confiscation. Gibraltar Customs Ordinance penalties include forfeiture of goods and fines up to 100% of duty evaded; failure to evidence FX repatriation within 180 days renders the export refund claim incomplete and subject to denial.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP is fully convertible and circulates freely alongside GBP at a fixed 1:1 parity. No exchange controls are imposed on current account or capital account transactions. Profit remittance by a Cayman Exempted Company registered as an external company in Gibraltar is unrestricted and free of any Gibraltar withholding tax, given Gibraltar's territorial taxation basis and the absence of dividend withholding. Capital injections from the Cayman parent, intercompany loan repayments, and trade settlements can proceed without central bank approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory surrender, mandatory conversion, or holding-period requirement for export proceeds. Receipts from off-price apparel liquidation sales may be retained in GBP, USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency. Only statistical ITRS reporting is required for qualifying transactions; no regulatory clearance is needed.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no central bank. Authority is tripartite: (i) Financial Services Commission (GFSC) licenses and supervises banks; (ii) the Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) under the Currency Notes Act 1934; (iii) the Bank of England provides the monetary anchor, with GIP pegged at 1:1 to GBP. For cross-border statistical reporting, the Bank of England's International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) applies through the GFSC.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High viability with clear legal constraints. UK-authorized e-money institutions and digital cross-border payment providers (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, Wise Business, Intergiro) can passport into Gibraltar under the preserved UK-Gibraltar financial services passporting framework (FCA-GFSC arrangements post-Brexit). These EMIs enable multi-currency virtual IBANs, SWIFT gpi, and fast GBP settlement via UK FPS. However, Gibraltar is outside SEPA, so EUR clearing requires SWIFT or correspondent banking. Cayman-incorporated legal persons trigger enhanced due diligence at any reputable EMI, requiring the same UBO/tax-residency evidence as a licensed Gibraltar bank. EMIs are suitable as secondary payment rails, not as the primary treasury account for a liquidating trader.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% at source in Gibraltar; no withholding tax is imposed on dividends paid by a Gibraltar company or a Gibraltar PE. Dividend distributions from the Cayman Exempted Co are also subject to zero Cayman withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 6 months after the end of the accounting period; for a calendar-year company this is 30 June of the following year. Payment of the final tax liability must be made by the same deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where annual gross trading turnover exceeds GBP 10 million or related-party transactions exceed GBP 1 million. Master File: mandatory where group consolidated turnover exceeds GBP 50 million. Country-by-Country Report: group consolidated turnover exceeding EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations 2019 apply. Distribution and Service Centre is a relevant activity; off-price wholesale liquidation of finished garments through a fixed place of business in Gibraltar therefore requires core income-generating activities, physical premises, qualified full-time employees, and an annual Economic Substance Return. The Cayman parent must comply in parallel with the Cayman Islands ESR or prove Gibraltar tax residence and obtain a Gibraltar TRC to avoid adverse deeming provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Sham Permanent Establishment / Fictitious Gibraltar Presence

Statutory sanction: Re-assessment of full 12.5% Gibraltar tax on deemed PE profits; statutory interest; tax-geared penalties up to 100% of understated tax; ESR penalty up to 10% of relevant expenditure; possible strike-off of the Gibraltar branch.

Director personal liability: Gibraltar courts may pierce the corporate veil under the Companies Act 2014 for fraudulent trading, imposing civil restitution and director disqualification for up to 15 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Arm's length re-adjustment of PE taxable profits; additional 12.5% tax on the adjusted profit; interest; penalties up to 100% of the tax shortfall; daily penalties for failure to deliver TP documentation under the Income Tax Act 2010.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false TP calculations are personally liable for restitution orders, director disqualification of up to 15 years, and travel bans.
Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act 2010: fine up to GBP 50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 7 years, plus civil recovery of unpaid tax and interest.

Director personal liability: Each director and compliance officer may be prosecuted as principal or accomplice; travel bans, asset seizures, and extradition proceedings are available to the Gibraltar authorities.
Trade-Based Money Laundering in Liquidation Channels

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar): unlimited fines, confiscation orders, and imprisonment up to 14 years; civil AML penalties from the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission; revocation of branch permissions.

Director personal liability: Directors face criminal liability where they knew or suspected the invoices represented criminal property; courts may impose international travel bans, freezing orders, and personal confiscation.
Shell / Tax-Exempt Misclassification and ESR Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR default: fixed and daily penalties capped by regulation, automatic information exchange with HMRC, fines up to GBP 10,000 per ESR infraction, and strike-off; the tax exemption is void in Gibraltar so the 12.5% rate applies.

Director personal liability: Court-ordered winding up for failure to satisfy economic substance; directors are liable for the company's unpaid tax and ESR fines to the extent of distributions made to place assets beyond the reach of creditors.
AML / Sanctions Compliance Failure on Branded Overstock

Statutory sanction: Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act (Gibraltar): fines up to 4% of turnover, bank account freezes, correspondent banking restrictions, debarment from public procurement, and public reprimands by the GFSC.

Director personal liability: The Money Laundering Reporting Officer and all directors are individually prosecutable; unlimited fines, custodial sentences, and personal travel bans are statutory remedies.
