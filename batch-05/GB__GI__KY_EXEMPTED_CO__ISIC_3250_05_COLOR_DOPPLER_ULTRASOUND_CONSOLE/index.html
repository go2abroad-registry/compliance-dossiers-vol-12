E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — under Cayman law the exempted company is legally tax-exempt in its home jurisdiction. In Gibraltar, which applies a territorial-source system, a non-resident foreign company is chargeable only on trading income arising in or derived from Gibraltar through a branch or permanent establishment; the standard corporate income-tax rate on such Gibraltar-source profits is 10%, with a qualifying company meeting the Gibraltar Economic Substance rules (Income Tax Act 2010, Third Schedule) eligible for an effective 0% rate on relevant activities such as holding and export IP functions. Pure offshore export sales of the colour Doppler ultrasound console with no Gibraltar permanent establishment, no management-and-control exercised in Gibraltar and no Gibraltar source of income remain entirely outside the Gibraltar tax charge.

Available election forms

Gibraltar law does not contain a US-style check-the-box classification, but the following statutory elections are available: (i) application for Qualifying Company status (effective 0% corporate rate) with annual certification to the Income Tax Office; (ii) group relief and loss-surrender elections for 75%-group companies under sections 41-43 of the Income Tax Act 2010; (iii) rollover relief election on replacement of business assets; (iv) where a US owner exists, a US federal Form 8832 check-the-box election may be filed to treat the Cayman entity as opaque or disregarded for US tax classification purposes; (v) hybrid and branch-mismatch alignment elections under UK TIOPA 2010 Part 6A and the equivalent Gibraltar provision to prevent deduction/non-inclusion asymmetries; and (vi) the first-year accounting-period-end election made by the foreign company upon its Gibraltar income-tax registration, which fixes filing and payment due dates for that period.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar_Import_Duty_and_Purchase_Tax_Regime_no_VAT_or_GST — Gibraltar does not levy VAT, GST or a general sales/use tax; the relevant indirect system is the Import Duty and Purchase Tax regime under the Customs Ordinance (Cap. 111) with ad valorem duties, excise duties for alcohol, tobacco and hydrocarbon oils, and a purchase tax on specified locally supplied goods.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory monetary threshold triggers an indirect-tax registration in Gibraltar because VAT/GST does not exist (registration threshold: GIP 0 / not applicable). Registration as importer/exporter with the Gibraltar Border and Coastguard Agency is compulsory from the first customs transaction with no de minimis turnover. For comparison, a UK taxable establishment would trigger UK VAT registration only when taxable supplies reach GBP 90,000 per annum (VATA 1994, Sch. 1); export sales of the medical-imaging console to an overseas buyer normally remain outside the scope of UK VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because Gibraltar levies no VAT/GST, EU-style 0% export VAT does not exist; the correct characterisation is that goods physically exported from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar import duty and purchase tax. The substantive criteria for export relief/out-of-scope treatment are: (i) the colour Doppler ultrasound console (ISIC 3250 / NAICS 334510) is physically removed from Gibraltar within the applicable transit, temporary-admission or customs-warehousing time limits; (ii) title, risk and control transfer to the non-Gibraltar purchaser; (iii) the exporter holds a valid Gibraltar customs operator registration; (iv) the proceeds are received from a non-Gibraltar buyer into a Gibraltar-licensed bank account via SWIFT LC or TT; and (v) the entire export documentation bundle is retained and internally matched to the customs declaration. On import into the destination jurisdiction, including the UK, the buyer accounts for local import VAT and no Gibraltar input-VAT refund arises because no input VAT is ever incurred in Gibraltar; consequently no VAT refund mechanism is available from or required before the Gibraltar tax administration.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD/declaration lodged with the Border and Coastguard Agency) as the master export evidence
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the goods from Gibraltar
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List identifying the portable phased-array ultrasound console (ISIC 3250 / NAICS 334510), including value, quantity, HS classification, serial numbers and country of origin
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank FX credit confirmation issued by a Gibraltar-licensed bank evidencing timely receipt of the LC/TT proceeds into the exporter's account
  • Certificate of Origin or non-preferential origin statement together with applicable medical-device packaging and end-user declarations

Gibraltar does not operate a continuous-transactions-control e-invoicing clearance mandate and imposes no national real-time fiscal stamping or API-based invoice clearance duty. Mandatory controls are: (i) serial-numbered invoices containing the statutory particulars, retained for 10 years under section 32 of the Income Tax Act 2010; (ii) electronic customs declarations through the Border and Coastguard Agency e-SAD / single-window clearance platform for goods entering or leaving Gibraltar; (iii) electronic loss-monitoring and traceability records for excise-controlled goods; and (iv) automatic exchange of information and Country-by-Country reporting through the designated government portal, which is unrelated to invoice clearance.

Misdeclaration, false export documentation and fictitious outward shipments are punishable under the Customs Ordinance (Cap. 111): goods are liable to forfeiture, financial penalties may reach three times the duty or CIF value, and the customs operator authorisation can be suspended or revoked. Under the Income Tax Act 2010 (ss. 62-65), fraudulent or negligent tax returns attract penalties of up to 100% of the undercharged tax plus interest, and the assessment window is extended to 6 years, or 20 years where fraud involves a non-resident or offshore conduit. Abusive claims to Qualifying Company status are clawed back prospectively and retrospectively, including loss of the 0% rate and disqualification from the Third Schedule regime. The Gibraltar Economic Substance Act 2019 imposes escalating fines and potential striking-off for failures to satisfy core-income-generating-activity requirements. Where a UK ultimate parent controls the structure, HMRC may additionally apply Diverted Profits Tax at 25%, CFC charging provisions and, for deliberate UK VAT or customs wrongdoing, penalties of up to 100% of the tax plus criminal referral.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains a fully convertible currency regime. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at 1:1 with the pound sterling and circulates alongside GBP as legal tender. There are no exchange controls on current account or capital account transactions. A Cayman Exempted Company may freely repatriate dividends, pay intercompany fees, and remit liquidation proceeds without prior central bank approval. The only practical hurdles are AML/CFT source-of-funds verification and Statutory Record-keeping obligations under Gibraltar law. There is no differential treatment between resident and non-resident accounts for a properly registered and substance-justified trading entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar imposes no mandatory surrender or conversion requirement on export receipts. A company may hold 100% of export proceeds in any permitted foreign currency (USD, EUR, GBP) within an authorized bank account in Gibraltar. Inward remittances may be retained indefinitely. The Cayman Exempted Company is not a Gibraltar tax resident, and therefore no local tax-based forced currency conversion or export proceed liquidation timetable applies. Customary banking practice, however, requires a board resolution and underlying trade contract for any inbound export proceeds to support the payment narrative in SWIFT MT103 messages.

Central bank supervision: Financial Services Commission, Government of Gibraltar (Exchange Control Authority)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Moderate. Gibraltar-licensed Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the Financial Services Act 2019 may accept a Cayman Exempted Company after enhanced due diligence, but are operationally constrained. Major pan-EU and UK digital EMIs generally reject non-EEA offshore companies because of policy-based geography restrictions and the absence of local physical nexus. Legally, there is no absolute prohibition; however, EMIs cannot issue documentary letters of credit, provide formal cross-currency swaps, or operate as correspondent clearing members in CHAPS/TARGET2/Fedwire. Consequently, an EMI is only feasible for low-value trade collections and salary payments, not for comprehensive treasury or LC-based medical-imaging export transactions.

Preferred clearing rail: SWIFT MT103 from the Cayman registered bank to the Gibraltar settlement bank. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required where the Gibraltar PE annual turnover exceeds GBP 10 million or cross-border related party transactions exceed GBP 1 million; Master File required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or above (Gibraltar Tax (Transfer Pricing) Regulations 2015, OECD-aligned).

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance (Compliance) Regulations 2019 mandate local employment, premises, expenditure and board direction for relevant activities; the manufacture, distribution and post-sales servicing of colour Doppler ultrasound consoles in Gibraltar constitutes a physical activity requiring a real permanent establishment. The Cayman parent remains within the Cayman International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Law, 2018 for pure equity holding, requiring registered office, minutes, and compliant books. Absence of both creates twin ESR disclosure obligations and penalty exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Incorrect Returns

Statutory sanction: Income Tax (Compliance) Act: civil penalty of up to 200% of underpaid tax; evasion conviction punishable with a fine of up to GBP 100,000 and imprisonment of up to 7 years; automatic exchange of information with HMRC and the Cayman Tax Information Authority.

Director personal liability: Directors may be served personal notices, subjected to Gibraltar Supreme Court travel bans and asset freezing orders, and disqualified from managing a company for up to 15 years; the Cayman parent's directors are jointly liable in Gibraltar civil proceedings where the PE is a mere facade.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Regulations: fixed penalty of up to GBP 100,000, daily penalties of GBP 10,000, automatic information exchange with HMRC and Cayman authorities; the Registrar may strike off the PE.

Director personal liability: Personal fines of up to GBP 50,000 per director, disqualification orders, and personal contribution orders where substance failures caused creditor loss; insolvency office-holder actions can pierce the corporate veil.
AML / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar) and AML/CFT Regulations: fines of up to GBP 250,000 per breach; confiscation orders of the full laundering transaction value; custodial sentences of up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors face up to 14 years imprisonment, personal confiscation, travel bans, and extradition under UK-Gibraltar cooperation; bank relationships are withdrawn and the beneficial owner's identity is disclosed through the Gibraltar Beneficial Ownership Register.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failures

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to Gibraltar taxable profits plus a 20% penalty on the additional tax notified; public taxpayer rulings under the Gibraltar self-assessment regime; interest accrual increases total exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false TP declarations are personally liable for the penalty portion where fraud is established; civil claims by group creditors and commercial counterparties may attach to the directors' personal assets.
