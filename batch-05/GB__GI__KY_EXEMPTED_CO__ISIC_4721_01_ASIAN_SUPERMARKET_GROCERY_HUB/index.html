Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
HM Government of Gibraltar Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), official portal: www.tax.gov.gi; customs and excise matters administered by HM Customs of Gibraltar (www.gibraltar.gov.gi/customs)

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign-incorporated Cayman Exempted Company that commences a physical grocery retail trade in Gibraltar must apply for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) by filing Form GX-TIN-REG with the Income Tax Office before commencing business. The application requires: (i) certified certificate of incorporation and memorandum and articles of association from the Cayman Islands Registrar, (ii) the Companies House (Gibraltar) overseas company registration confirmation, (iii) the Gibraltar registered office or local business address, (iv) particulars of directors and shareholders, and (v) a brief description of the local business activity. The Commissioner issues a Gibraltar TIN within 10–15 working days. Should the entity earn certain Gibraltar-source income without a permanent establishment, the Income Tax Office may require a non-resident tax registration or withholding registration rather than a full company TIN

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Tax_Exempt_Zone

        
Available election forms
Gibraltar has no check-the-box or disregarded-entity election. The Cayman Exempted Company is treated by Cayman law as tax-exempt in the Cayman Islands; Gibraltar taxation depends on the place of effective management, the creation of a permanent establishment in Gibraltar, and the source of trading profits. The company may elect (where eligible) to apply for a Gibraltar tax residence certificate under the Income Tax Act for treaty purposes, and may seek a private ruling from the Commissioner on the application of the territorial principle to its offshore income. No hybrid mismatch election is available in Gibraltar domestic law, but the UK-parented OECD hybrid mismatch rules may require consideration in the broader consolidated group

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
Gibraltar has no general VAT or GST; the applicable regime is Import Duty and Excise Duty under the Customs (Import Duty) Act and the Excise Act, supplemented by a small number of selective consumption taxes

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Not applicable - no turnover-based VAT/GST registration threshold exists; all importers and excise-licensed traders must register with HM Customs irrespective of turnover. For selected alcoholic beverages, tobacco, fuel, and motor vehicles, separate excise duty registration is mandatory before the first import or sale

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Gibraltar does not operate a zero-rated export VAT regime. Because no VAT/GST is charged on domestic supplies or exports, export sales are simply outside the scope of Gibraltar consumption tax. A Cayman Exempted Company exporting goods from Gibraltar to a destination outside Gibraltar may treat those sales as outside the scope of Gibraltar duties provided (i) the goods physically depart Gibraltar within the prescribed period, (ii) the exporter holds a valid customs export declaration, and (iii) the billing entity is not a registered Gibraltar excise or import-duty payer for those goods. If the entity sources goods from overseas into Gibraltar for re-export, import duty may be suspended only under a customs warehousing or inward processing regime and will be released upon proof of export

No real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or electronic invoicing mandate applies to retail grocery sales in Gibraltar. Ordinary commercial invoices and till receipts satisfy local record-keeping rules. Imported goods must be declared through the Gibraltar Customs Single Administrative Document (SAD) system (electronic submission via the local Customs Office); no digital tax stamp is required for grocery items, but excise goods require authorised customs warehouse and movement control procedures

      
HM Customs may conduct retrospective audits of export evidence for up to six years. In the absence of the complete statutory documentation bundle, any previously suspended import duty becomes immediately payable, plus default interest of 8% per annum. Knowingly or fraudulently claiming export treatment with forged or false documentation attracts penalties of up to GBP 10,000 per consignment, forfeiture of the goods, and in egregious cases referral to the Director of Public Prosecutions for criminal proceedings under the Customs (Import Duty) Act and the Criminal Offences (Misuse of Documents) Act

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Gibraltar maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at 1:1 to GBP, and there are no exchange-control restrictions or profit-remittance barriers for non-resident corporate account holders. A Cayman exempted company, being tax-exempt in the Cayman Islands and not automatically Gibraltar tax-resident, may remit dividends, repay shareholder loans, and convert GIP/GBP to any foreign currency without government approval. Practical obstacles are limited to bank-level KYC/AML comfort, commercial substance evidence, and source-of-wealth documentation.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Gibraltar has no statutory export-proceeds surrender regime; exporters are not required to convert foreign-currency receipts into GIP/GBP, and no surrender timeline applies. For this Asian supermarket grocery hub, trade flows are import-heavy with local in-store and card-acquirer receipts in GIP/GBP; any incidental export earnings from wholesale re-export to EU/UK counterparties may be retained in the original currency and converted at market rate through the licensed clearing bank.

      

      
Central bank supervision: No domestic central bank exists in Gibraltar. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory financial services regulator including banking, payments, and e-money; the Bank of England acts as the monetary anchor for sterling, and the Government of Gibraltar issues Gibraltar pound banknotes at par with GBP.

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
Cross-border digital EMI accounts such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, and Airwallex are viable only as secondary collection or FX-spot execution rails, not as the primary treasury clearing backbone. Gibraltar is outside the EEA, so an EU/EEA EMI cannot passport into Gibraltar automatically; any EMI must hold a GFSC payment-services/e-money licence or be a licensed Gibraltar branch of an authorised non-EEA EMI. The offshore Cayman exempted company will be treated as enhanced-risk by most digital EMIs, triggering additional UBO, source-of-funds, and operating-substance checks. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound shareholder capital should be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 from the originating bank in Hong Kong, Singapore, or the UK to the Gibraltar treasury account. The payment remittance line must identify the Cayman exempted company by full legal name, account number, and purpose code 'Capital Contribution – Issue of Share Capital – Non-Resident Exempted Company'. Supporting verification must include the Cayman board resolution authorising the share issue, updated register of members, share certificate, bank credit advice, and a funds-sourcing statement from the investing shareholder. No Gibraltar exchange-control approval is required, but the receiving bank will require a capital clear-letter in its onboarding file to prevent recharacterisation as taxable revenue.Supplier payments for imported Asian condiments, packaged groceries, and fresh vegetables should be cleared via SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary bank BIC/IBAN details and charge code 'SHARED' or 'OUR' to avoid beneficiary deductions. For bulk high-value container shipments, the Gibraltar bank's trade-finance desk should issue an irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit (sight or usance) with Incoterms 2020, cargo inspection certificates, ocean or air waybill, commercial invoice, packing list, and insurance certificate. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory GIP threshold is published for a standalone Local File. In practice, a Gibraltar taxpayer must maintain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation, including a Local File, when aggregate controlled transactions with associated enterprises exceed GIP 1,000,000 in any annual accounting period. A Master File is legally expected where the multinational group to which the Gibraltar entity belongs has consolidated group revenue equal to or greater than EUR 750,000,000, consistent with Gibraltar's Country-by-Country Reporting Regulations. For the Asian supermarket hub, if the Cayman parent and affiliated procurement companies in Asia sell Indonesian or Japanese products into Gibraltar exceeding that threshold, a full OECD-compliant Local File is mandatory.

      
Economic substance scope: Applicable. The Economic Substance (Implementation) Act 2020, aligned with the EU Code of Conduct and OECD BEPS Action 5, requires a Gibraltar-resident or tax-resident company carrying on a relevant activity to demonstrate substantial activity, physical premises, qualified employees, and locally directed core income-generating activities. Operating a physical grocery store in Gibraltar inherently satisfies the substance requirement for the retail trade. However, if the Cayman Exempted Company is held to be a pure holding or IP-owning company, or if procurement and financing functions are performed outside Gibraltar while intercompany invoicing is booked in Gibraltar, the substance test may fail. The tax authority will reclassify the company as Gibraltar tax resident if management and control are exercised in Gibraltar, regardless of Cayman incorporation.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion / Fraduent Filing
        
Statutory sanction: A civil penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded plus 8% per annum interest. On criminal conviction in the Supreme Court of Gibraltar, fines may reach GIP 1,000,000, and a principal director or manager may be imprisoned for up to 7 years under the Income Tax Act 2010.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns or direct the concealment are personally liable for the extra tax, penalties and costs. The court may order civil restitution and asset freezes; a travel ban may be issued before or during proceedings. The corporate veil is pierced where the company is used as a vehicle for fraudulent trading.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Sham Substance
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance (Implementation) Act 2020, first-year non-compliance attracts a penalty of GIP 10,000; a second consecutive year attracts GIP 50,000 and mandatory notification to the United Kingdom tax authorities. The Commissioner may assess the company on its worldwide income as a Gibraltar tax resident and impose a further 100% penalty for failure to file retrospective returns.

        
Director personal liability: Directors can be disqualified for up to 15 years under the Companies Act 2014. The court may issue a travel restriction order preventing the director leaving Gibraltar until tax arrears are secured. The tax liability may be recovered directly from a director who caused the statement of non-residence to be filed.

      

      
      

        Anti-Money Laundering / Cash Handling
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the Gibraltar AML/CFT Regulations 2019, an authorised officer may impose a civil penalty of up to GIP 500,000, revoke the retail business licence, and refer the matter for criminal prosecution. Money laundering carries a maximum sentence of 14 years' imprisonment and unlimited fines; asset forfeiture of retail proceeds is mandatory.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and the designated Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) face personal criminal liability for failing to disclose suspicious activity. Personal assets can be frozen under a restraint order. Travel bans are routinely imposed, and directors may be charged as primary offenders even if the company is sanctioned.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing / Associated Enterprise Non-Arm's-Length
        
Statutory sanction: The Gibraltar Income Tax Officer may recalculate profits under the arm's-length principle and assess additional tax at 12.5% plus 8% interest. A tax penalty of 100% of the underpaid tax applies for deliberate transfer-pricing misdeclaration. Failure to deliver a required Local File or Master File attracts a fixed penalty of GIP 10,000 and an additional GIP 100 per day of continuing non-compliance.

        
Director personal liability: Officers who knowingly prepare or approve false TP documentation are treated as responsible persons for the tax debt. Directors may be disqualified and excluded from serving on any Gibraltar company for up to 10 years. Where the TP scheme is a sham to evade tax, the court may order repatriation of profits, impose travel bans to secure restitution, and, in egregious cases, impose custodial sentences under the Criminal Justice (International Cooperation) Act.
