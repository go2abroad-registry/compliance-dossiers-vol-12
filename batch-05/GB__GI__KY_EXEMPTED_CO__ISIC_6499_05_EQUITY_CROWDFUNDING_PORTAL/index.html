Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

H.M. Government of Gibraltar Income Tax Office, 2-6 John Mackintosh Square, Gibraltar; online portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation (Income Tax Online Services / Company Taxpayer Web Portal). Customs and excise matters are administered separately by H.M. Customs Gibraltar via the ASYCUDA World electronic declaration system.

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company carrying on an equity crowdfunding portal business in Gibraltar must: (1) obtain a licence from the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services (Financial Mutuals) Act / E-money and Crowdfunding Regulations; (2) register the offshore entity as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry under the Companies Act 2014; (3) apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (GTIN) by submitting the Company Registration Form (G-1) together with the Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, memorandum and articles, audited financial statements, details of place of effective management, a Gibraltar-registered office address, and the GFSC license; (4) obtain a Gibraltar EORI number from H.M. Customs if any goods movement or duty-suspended import arises; (5) register with the Gibraltar Social Security Authority as an employer if any directors, employees, or service providers are remunerated from Gibraltar. Non-resident companies without permanent establishment may alternatively file an annual non-resident declaration and will not be issued an active GTIN unless subject to withholding or reporting obligations under CRS/FATCA.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Cayman Exempted Company is statutorily exempt from Cayman Islands income, corporate, capital gains, and withholding taxes under the Tax Incentives Act (long-term tax undertaking, normally lasting 20 years from incorporation). In Gibraltar, the default classification is that of a non-resident company under the Income Tax Act 2010, which taxes only Gibraltar-source income unless the central management and control test is met. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Cayman Exempted Company is statutorily exempt from Cayman Islands income, corporate, capital gains, and withholding taxes under the Tax Incentives Act (long-term tax undertaking, normally lasting 20 years from incorporation). In Gibraltar, the default classification is that of a non-resident company under the Income Tax Act 2010, which taxes only Gibraltar-source income unless the central management and control test is met. If management and control of the crowdfunding portal is effectively exercised in Gibraltar (e.g., board meetings in Gibraltar, strategic investment decisions taken there, or a dependent agent concluding contracts), the company becomes Gibraltar-resident and subject to 12.5% corporate income tax on worldwide chargeable profits (subject to territorial-source reliefs).

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate an IRC §7701 or check-the-box election regime. Elections available include: (a) notice of self-assessment election under the Income Tax (Self-Assessment) Rules, permitting the company to self-compute profits; (b) group relief election under s.91 of the Income Tax Act 2010 for loss surrender between Gibraltar-resident group companies; (c) loss carry-forward election under s.93; (d) the UK-Gibraltar Double Taxation Agreement claims route, electing to attribute business profits to a Gibraltar permanent establishment rather than to a deemed resident presence; (e) hybrid mismatch neutralizing elections, including alignment of payment characterisation (equity vs debt) for instruments issued by the platform; and (f) a non-resident company election to apply the remittance basis to bank interest and offshore services fees, where the sole shareholder is not Gibraltar-resident. No entity classification election exists for the Cayman vehicle itself, which retains its legal personality and limited-liability status in all Gibraltar filings.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar operates no VAT or GST; its indirect tax system consists of (i) customs and excise duties under the Customs Duties Ordinance, (ii) import duty on goods entering Gibraltar from outside the customs territory, (iii) payroll-linked levies (Social Security contributions and the Training Levy), and (iv) stamp duty on transfers of Gibraltar land. Equity crowdfunding facilitation services are outside the scope of any Gibraltar turnover-based indirect tax; however, the supply of regulated intermediary services to investors established in EU member states may create foreign VAT registration obligations in the investor's jurisdiction, and platform fees received from UK-resident investors may be assessable to UK VAT only if the supply is treated as made where the customer belongs and the platform holds a UK VAT registration.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A - No VAT/GST turnover registration threshold exists because Gibraltar does not impose a general turnover tax. Employers must register with the Income Tax Office for PAYE-style deduction from the date the first employee is engaged, regardless of salary level. Importers of any goods for the portal's operations (e.g., hardware, office equipment) must obtain a Gibraltar EORI number before the first customs declaration; there is no de minimis customs-registration threshold, although goods valued below £25 may be relieved of import duty under the General Facilities and Tariff Arrangements. For non-EU crowdfunding activities, no indirect-tax registration is required in Gibraltar.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is not applicable because Gibraltar has no VAT regime. For income tax purposes, the equivalent relief is the non-assessment of offshore profits earned by a non-resident or non-Gibraltar-managed company. A Cayman Exempted Company operating the crowdfunding portal will be exempt from Gibraltar income tax on its platform fees where: (i) the services (arranging equity offers, investor due diligence, escrow settlement) are performed wholly outside Gibraltar; (ii) contracts are concluded and negotiated outside Gibraltar; (iii) no dependent agent or permanent establishment exists in Gibraltar; (iv) all board decision-making occurs outside Gibraltar; and (v) the investor-facing payment rails (ACH/Escrow and Bank Wire) clear through a non-Gibraltar settlement bank, or through a Gibraltar bank solely as a correspondent without decision-making authority. The exemption is not automatic and must be claimed by filing a protective non-resident tax return with the Income Tax Office together with the Schedule B offshore income exclusion statement.

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping of invoices. Customs entries must be filed electronically through ASYCUDA World, with item-level data transmitted to H.M. Customs before release of goods. The Income Tax Office operates an electronic self-assessment filing gateway for company returns; payment of tax is via bank transfer or Gibraltar-licensed payment processors. Crowdfunding platforms are, however, subject to the GFSC's Electronic Money and Crypto-Asset record-keeping rules, requiring end-to-end transaction audit trails, investor identity verification, and export of transaction data to the Financial Intelligence Unit upon request. Classified as a High_Risk_Special_License, the platform must also submit monthly transaction reports in a machine-readable format (CSV/XML) through the GFSC's myGFSC portal, and must retain e-invoices and investor transaction ledgers for 6 years from the date of each offer.

Gibraltar's Income Tax Office, in cooperation with HMRC under the UK-Gibraltar Double Taxation Agreement (in force as of 1 January 2021) and the OECD Multilateral Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance, may reopen assessments for 6 years in cases of negligent misstatement and without time limit in cases of fraudulent conduct. Where the offshore-services exemption is misclaimed, the entire platform fee income is treated as Gibraltar-source, subject to income tax at 12.5%, plus late-payment interest at the statutory rate of 8% per annum, plus a penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded for error without reasonable excuse, or 200% for deliberate concealment under the Income Tax (Interest and Penalties) Rules 2015. Contracting parties relying on non-existent zero-rating may face GFSC enforcement: fines up to GBP 1,000,000 per breach of the Financial Services Act and revocation of the crowdfunding licence. Company officers risk disqualification under the Companies Act 2014 and, in egregious cases, criminal prosecution for tax evasion carrying imprisonment up to 7 years. The Cayman tax undertaking will not be affected by Gibraltar clawbacks, but false declarations to the Cayman Registry could attract separate civil penalties of USD 10,000 for inaccurate filings and deregistration of the exempted status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both the United Kingdom and Gibraltar maintain full current-account and capital-account convertibility under Articles VIII and XIV of the IMF Articles of Agreement; no post-Bretton Woods exchange-control restrictions exist for purchases, remittances, dividends, proceeds of liquidation, or portfolio flows. The Gibraltar pound is legal tender in Gibraltar, is pegged at par to GBP Sterling, and carries no convertibility wall. A Cayman exempted company holding investor funds in a Gibraltar or UK bank account may freely convert GBP, USD, EUR, and other major currencies at spot or via wholesale forward markets. Profit remittance to Cayman shareholders is legally unhindered; however, banks will apply FATCA, CRS, sanctions, source-of-funds and economic-connectivity checks, and may require a tangible-services invoice or board resolution to evidence the real economic activity and ultimate beneficial owner line-up. Practical hurdles are not statutory exchange controls but bank de-risking, counterparty due diligence, and the absence of documentary evidence that the crowdfunding platform is regulated or operating through an authorised payment chain.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. The United Kingdom and Gibraltar do not operate export-proceeds surrender requirements, compulsory repatriation timelines, or mandatory conversion ratios under the Exchange Control Act 1947 or any successor regulations. A Cayman exempted company receiving third-party crowdfunding subscription payments or platform fees is not deemed to be earning export proceeds in the customs sense. The company may retain balances in any currency of account at a licensed banking institution, may determine its own hedge policy, and has no obligation to convert USD or EUR receipts into GBP. If the entity were to extend trade finance or merchant advances to underlying portfolio companies, the applicable FX margin and clearing fees would remain a contractual and treasury-policy matter rather than a statutory surrender requirement.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar does not maintain a separate central bank; the Bank of England, as the United Kingdom central bank, remains the monetary and wholesale clearing authority for sterling, while the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory bank and payment-services supervisor for Gibraltar. The Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) retains oversight of the Cayman exempted company at its domicile but is not a Gibraltar exchange-control authority. For the proposed structure, the operative central-bank authority is the Bank of England for GBP clearing and real-time settlement, with HM Treasury responsible for sanctions administration and any residual Exchange Control Act oversight.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Limited-to-moderate feasibility. Authorised UK and Gibraltar electronic-money institutions such as Revolut Ltd, Wise Europe, Modulr, Transact Payments, and CurrencyCloud can generally provide SWIFT, SEPA, and Faster Payments rails with strong user interfaces and lower onboarding cost; however, their risk-acceptance engines classify crowdfunding portals, digital capital-raising, and offshore Cayman exempted companies as high-risk sectors. As a result, most EMIs will decline the account where the entity is not already licensed by the GFSC or FCA, where no Gibraltar/UK resident compliance officer exists, or where the KYC file lacks a full regulatory licence, substance evidence, and an audited client-money segregation framework. Legal constraints include the Payment Services Regulations 2017 in the UK, Gibraltar's Financial Services (E-Money) framework, Payment Services Directive (PSD2) safeguarding requirements, and the obligation to maintain any safeguarded funds on the EMI's own balance sheet with a UK or Gibraltar regulated credit institution. Even when an EMI accepts the company, it will typically impose: (i) no payment cards; (ii) a pre-approved beneficiary-list only; (iii) mandatory transaction-per-wire supporting documentation; (iv) monthly audited reconciliation of investor money; and (v) no credit or F/X leverage products. EMIs therefore are viable only as an ancillary collector account feeding a commercial bank escrow account, not as the main treasury for a regulated crowdfunding portal.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound investor capital must be transferred by SWIFT MT103 from a licensed bank or authorised EMI into the designated Gibraltar client-money escrow account. The beneficiary bank must be a GFSC-licensed credit institution; the beneficiary account must be labelled as an escrow/client-money account in the name of the Cayman exempted company as trustee or agent. Wire instructions must include the beneficiary BIC/IBAN exactly matching the bank's SWIFT directory, and the remittance information field (tag 70) must contain ISO 20022 Purpose Code EQUI for equity subscription or ESCR for escrow deposit, followed by the unique investor identification reference and the offering security identifier. SEPA/EUR inbound transfers must use SEPA Credit Transfer rules with the same EQUI purpose category. The receiving bank must verify SWIFT RMA (Relationship Management Application) keys, conduct sanctions screening against HM Treasury/Sanctions and OFAC lists, and confirm the remitter's bank and the economic substance of the order. The portal's backend must reconcile each inbound message through a two-factor queue that matches wire value, investor ID, offering token, and bank reference before funds are released from the escrow control account. A minimum 48-hour hold for new investors and negative-media re-screen is recommended.Outbound disbursements to portfolio companies and trade-closing payments should default to SWIFT MT103 single customer credits for same-day GBP settlement through CHAPS, with F/X converted at a competitively quoted bank rate. Euro-denominated payments should clear via SEPA Credit Transfer; US-dollar disbursements via FedWire or SWIFT MT103 to the beneficiary correspondent account. When the treasured transaction involves physical goods or services supplied by a portfolio company, an irrevocable SWIFT MT700 Commercial Letter of Credit (at sight or usance) may be used, but for crowdfunding investments the standard rail is MT103 with full remittance data. Each outbound transaction must include purpose code EQUI, DISB or TRADE as appropriate and be accompanied by the underlying disbursement certificate. The portal must maintain a disbursement authorisation order: (i) refunds to investors are always senior; (ii) platform service fees next; (iii) portfolio funding after close; and (iv) dividends or redemptions accrue only from surplus funds outside the client-money account. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Limited-to-moderate feasibility. Authorised UK and Gibraltar electronic-money institutions such as Revolut Ltd, Wise Europe, Modulr, Transact Payments, and CurrencyCloud can generally provide SWIFT, SEPA, and Faster Payments rails with strong user interfaces and lower onboarding cost; however, their risk-acceptance engines classify crowdfunding portals, digital capital-raising, and offshore Cayman exempted companies as high-risk sectors. As a result, most EMIs will decline the account where the entity is not already licensed by the GFSC or FCA, where no Gibraltar/UK resident compliance officer exists, or where the KYC file lacks a full regulatory licence, substance evidence, and an audited client-money segregation framework. Legal constraints include the Payment Services Regulations 2017 in the UK, Gibraltar's Financial Services (E-Money) framework, Payment Services Directive (PSD2) safeguarding requirements, and the obligation to maintain any safeguarded funds on the EMI's own balance sheet with a UK or Gibraltar regulated credit institution. Even when an EMI accepts the company, it will typically impose: (i) no payment cards; (ii) a pre-approved beneficiary-list only; (iii) mandatory transaction-per-wire supporting documentation; (iv) monthly audited reconciliation of investor money; and (v) no credit or F/X leverage products. EMIs therefore are viable only as an ancillary collector account feeding a commercial bank escrow account, not as the main treasury for a regulated crowdfunding portal.
