However, the bonded road-feeder operation requires a customs guarantee (bank bond, insurance-bond, or cash collateral acceptable to HM Customs Gibraltar), typically set at no less than EUR 10,000 or the quantum of customs duty and VAT exposure on the bonded cargo, whichever is higher.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at parity with the British pound (GBP); both capital and current account transactions are fully convertible with no exchange-control approval requirements. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and capital repayments from a Gibraltar-resident Exempted Company are freely transferable, subject only to sanctions screening, AML/CFT compliance, and verification of the beneficial ownership register. No central-bank surrender or conversion approval applies to inward or outward payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no prescribed timeline for repatriation of export receipts. Proceeds of bonded road-feeder services may be retained in any freely convertible currency, including USD or EUR, and settled through Gibraltar-based licensed banks or UK clearing networks without a forced local conversion.

Central bank supervision: HM Treasury, acting as the monetary and exchange-control authority for the Gibraltar Overseas Territory, in coordination with the Bank of England as the ultimate issuer of pound sterling and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the local licensed-banking regulator

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Post-Brexit, Gibraltar is outside the EEA, so EEA-authorised electronic money institution (EMI) passports do not extend to Gibraltar. UK FCA-authorised EMIs and digital cross-border platforms can service ancillary GBP collection and disbursement flows, but they are not acceptable as primary clearing rails for bonded customs operations because they lack direct CHAPS/Faster Payments membership in Gibraltar and may be rejected by tier-1 correspondent banks for high-volume airport feeder settlement. GFSC-licensed DLT/e-money providers remain subject to segregated-fund and client-asset rules, making their use suitable only for low-value non-core payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from the Cayman Exempted Company to the Gibraltar operating bank account should be executed via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code CAPT (Capital Transfer) or the equivalent non-trade capital remittance classification. The payment instruction must reference the share subscription agreement or loan agreement under which funds are received, and the receiving Gibraltar bank will verify the Cayman entity’s resolution, beneficial ownership disclosure, source-of-funds evidence, and the absence of any UK or EU sanctions nexus. Funds must be routed only through a licensed Gibraltar bank or a UK clearing bank with corresponding relationship to the Gibraltar beneficiary; cash passes are prohibited. The capital escrow account must be earmarked until the compliance officer completes the onboarding KYC and the corporate bank account is formally activated.Outbound trade and operational disbursements for the bonded truck fleet will be cleared through: (i) SWIFT MT103 for international supplier payments, customs fees, tolls, and cross-border service settlements; (ii) UK Faster Payments and CHAPS for domestic GBP liquidity and time-critical fuel or payroll payments within the UK clearing system; (iii) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700) for any major acquisition of trailers, tractors, or GPS-sealed equipment requiring documentary trade finance; and (iv) SWIFT MT760 Standby Letters of Credit to support customs-bonded guarantees and airport access agreements. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% standard Gibraltar corporate income tax rate under the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended); 15% Multinational Top-up Tax effective 1 January 2025 applies to groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividend distributions under the Income Tax Act 2010; the Cayman Islands equally impose no dividend withholding tax on distributions by an Exempted Company.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return is due within 12 months after the end of the accounting period (e.g., accounting year ending 31 December 2024 is due by 31 December 2025). Payments on account of estimated current-year tax are due in the first month of the YOA; a balancing payment is due with the return.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box A1 - Tax Status and Residence Declaration of whether the company is tax-resident in Gibraltar (central management and control exercised in Gibraltar) or non-resident with a permanent establishment in Gibraltar. A Cayman Exempted Company not managed and controlled in Gibraltar must elect non-resident status and attach a computation of Gibraltar-source trading profits attributable to its permanent establishment (bonded truck fleet operations). Late filing attracts a fixed penalty up to GIP 300 plus daily default penalties up to GIP 60 per day (maximum 100 days); tax-geared penalties up to 100% of the additional tax for careless error and up to 200% for deliberate concealment; interest on unpaid tax at 10% per annum; deliberate defaulters are publicly named; fraudulent non-filing is a criminal offence.

Capital allowances are permitted only against Gibraltar PE profits; assets must be physically deployed in Gibraltar operations to qualify. Box C - Related Party Transactions (Transfer Pricing Declaration) Schedule of controlled transactions with associated enterprises (intercompany management fees, treasury interest, GPS licence fees, fleet leasing, shared dispatch services). Attach contemporaneous OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines documentation evidencing arm's length pricing; a Cayman affiliate must not strip profit from Gibraltar below arm's length value. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar does not codify a Local File / Master File monetary threshold in the Income Tax Act 2010; transfer pricing documentation is required on a proportionate OECD-TPG basis for all controlled transactions with associated enterprises, irrespective of value, and must be contemporaneous where aggregate annual controlled transactions exceed GIP 250,000. Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for Gibraltar-resident ultimate parent entities of multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million (GIP equivalent), filed within 12 months of the group year-end through the e-Services portal.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The Gibraltar Economic Substance (Companies and Foreign Companies) Regulations 2019 require tax-resident companies carrying on relevant activities (financing and leasing, distribution and service centres, transport and ancillary logistics, headquarters) to maintain central management and control, employees, premises, and income-generating activity in Gibraltar proportionate to their activity. A Cayman Exempted Company operating a GPS-sealed bonded road feeder fleet must (a) satisfy the Gibraltar substance test if tax-resident in Gibraltar; and (b) if non-resident in Gibraltar, file the Cayman Islands Economic Substance annual notification/return declaring its tax residence outside Cayman to avoid Cayman penalties up to KYD 10,000 plus KYD 10,000 per month of continued default.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Offshore Facade (Management and Control)

Statutory sanction: Deemed Gibraltar tax residency with assessment of worldwide income at 12.5%; back taxes plus interest at 10% per annum; civil penalty up to 200% of the tax for deliberate non-disclosure; striking off of the company under the Companies Act; refusal or revocation of AEO and bonded carrier licensing.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties where they knowingly maintain the offshore facade; travel bans and disqualification orders under the Companies (Disqualification) Act; Cayman-domiciled directors are enforceable against in Gibraltar under reciprocal enforcement of judgments.
Customs / Bonded Transport Licence Breach

Statutory sanction: Forfeiture of goods and vehicles; civil penalty up to three times the value of the goods or GIP 3,000 per occurrence; suspension or revocation of the bonded operator licence; AML/TF breach under the Proceeds of Crime Act adds corporate fines up to EUR 500,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the unlawful movements are personally liable for customs penalties; imprisonment up to 7 years for fraudulent evasion of customs or excise duty; revocation of airport traffic rights destroys going-concern value.
Transfer Pricing and CFC Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Primary TP adjustment increasing Gibraltar taxable profits; secondary adjustment or notional dividend treatment where funds remain outstanding; tax-geared penalties of 100% to 200% of understated tax; Gibraltar CFC rules (Income Tax Act 2010, Part VI) attribute the Cayman affiliate's passive income to the Gibraltar taxpayer; statutory interest at 10% per annum.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false TP declarations are jointly liable for the restitutionary tax; deliberate price manipulation is prosecutable as tax evasion with imprisonment up to 7 years; advisers face regulatory sanction and suspension.
Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Fraudulent evasion of income tax: fine up to twice the tax evaded plus forfeiture of assets derived from the evasion; assessments may be raised up to 20 years after the relevant year of assessment; publication on the deliberate defaulters list.

Director personal liability: Imprisonment up to 7 years; confiscation orders under the Criminal Law (Confiscation) Act; travel bans pending extradition; disqualification as a director in Gibraltar and the United Kingdom for up to 15 years; directors remain personally liable for restitution and penal surcharges.
