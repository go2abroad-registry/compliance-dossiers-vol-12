Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar: HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (ITO) and HM Customs Gibraltar; United Kingdom: HM Revenue & Customs for UK VAT and customs matters. Portals: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation and https://www.gov.uk/hmrc

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Cayman Exempted Company must obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (GTRN) from the Income Tax Office before commencing any economic activity in Gibraltar. The application is submitted by a Gibraltar registered agent through the ITO eServices portal or Form TRN, accompanied by an apostilled Cayman certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, certificate of good standing, director and UBO identification, and a declaration of expected Gibraltar-source income. HM Customs separately issues a Gibraltar Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number for import/export activity. Gibraltar does not issue VAT registration numbers because Gibraltar does not operate a VAT system.

Available election forms

Not applicable under Gibraltar law for a Cayman Exempted Company: there is no check-the-box or equivalent election to treat the entity as fiscally transparent for Gibraltar tax purposes. Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Customs and Excise Duty regime (no VAT/GST)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST and therefore has no economic turnover registration threshold. All entities importing or exporting goods must hold a Gibraltar Customs EORI account from the first transactional activity. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from Gibraltar to any destination outside Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxes and therefore attract a 0% effective rate; Gibraltar operates no VAT refund system. UK VAT zero-rating may apply only if the entity is voluntarily VAT-registered in the UK and exports goods from the UK to a place outside the UK, with legal title passing to a non-resident customer and statutory export evidence retained. The exporter must substantiate physical removal, export destination, and receipt of foreign sales proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Gibraltar has no B2B e-invoicing mandate and no real-time fiscal clearance or API-based VAT stamping regime. Import and export declarations must be filed electronically through ASYCUDA World, the customs single-window system used by HM Customs Gibraltar. Invoices may be issued in paper or PDF form and must be retained for six years.

HM Customs Gibraltar and the UK tax authority may audit export zero-rating claims for up to six years, with an unlimited time limit in cases of fraud. False or unsupported export claims trigger retrospective assessment of the full duty or VAT, statutory interest, and penalties up to 100% of the tax or duty. Criminal prosecution may follow under Gibraltar customs legislation and UK VAT law, with potential fines and imprisonment where fraudulent misrepresentation or dishonest conduct is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully liberalised in Gibraltar for non-resident corporate entities. There are no centralised FX licensing requirements, no surrender obligations, and no statutory restrictions on conversion of GIP/GBP, USD, EUR, or other freely tradable currencies. The Cayman Exempted Company, being tax-transparent for Gibraltar purposes and non-resident absent a permanent establishment, may repatriate profits, dividends, interest, repayment of intercompany loans, and liquidation proceeds without exchange-control approval. Practical hurdles arise only from bank-level AML/KYC, CRS/FATCA self-certification, economic substance evidence, and UK/EU financial sanctions screening. Capital movements into Gibraltar for equity subscription and intercompany funding are permitted and will be verified by the receiving bank against board resolutions and source-of-funds documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Gibraltar or the Cayman Islands. Proceeds from sales of Ultra-HD 4K surgical laparoscopy towers may be credited in the original invoice currency (USD, EUR, GBP, or GIP) and retained in the operational bank account. There is no obligation to convert export receipts into GIP or KYD. The Cayman Exempted Company should, however, maintain a complete export ledger, underlying purchase orders, bills of lading/airway bills, and documentary evidence for bank transaction monitoring, Cayman economic substance filings, and any CFC reporting in the ultimate parent jurisdiction. Proceeds involving sanctioned jurisdictions or persons must be blocked and reported to the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit where required.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) and, for the Cayman Exempted Company, Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA). Gibraltar has no independent central bank; the Gibraltar pound is issued under the Government of Gibraltar Currency Notes Act at a fixed 1:1 parity with sterling, with monetary anchoring to HM Treasury and the Bank of England. CIMA administers the Cayman Islands Exchange Control regime and issues the necessary non-resident permissions for the Exempted Company.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium as an auxiliary FX and receivables/payables workbench; Low as the sole treasury and clearing rail. UK-authorised EMIs and EU/EEA fintechs can in many cases onboard a Cayman Exempted Company remotely via video CDD, but post-Brexit they do not passport e-money licences into Gibraltar. A UK FCA EMI can provide virtual IBANs and cross-currency settlement, but cannot provide GIP local clearing, CHAPS/TARGET2 direct participation, commercial letter-of-credit issuance, or deposit safeguarding equivalent to a bank. For a low-risk medical-device exporter, a reputable EMI may be used for sub-threshold vendor payments and quick FX conversion, with balances capped and funds swept daily to a Gibraltar tier-1 bank. The main legal constraints are safeguarding-account status, absence of financial-services compensation protection, contractual choice-of-law, and the requirement for the EMI to satisfy Gibraltar anti-money-laundering rules when servicing a Cayman structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the Cayman Exempted Company's settling bank to the Gibraltar receiving bank. The receiving bank will credit a dedicated Capital Escrow Account pending verification of the board resolution, share-subscription agreement, and CRS self-certification. Field 70/Purpose of Payment should state 'CAPITAL INJECTION / SUBSCRIPTION FOR SHARES / MEDICAL DEVICE MANUFACTURING'. The sending bank should use the appropriate direct-investment purpose code (e.g., UK BoP code 110 for direct investment) where required. For USD wires, use a correspondent bank in New York via CHIPS/Fedwire; for GBP, use CHAPS if same-day value; for EUR, use TARGET2. No third-party pooled remittances are acceptable; the ordering customer must be the named Cayman entity or a documented direct parent/group treasury entity. For amounts above USD 250,000, provide pre-delivery board minutes, source-of-funds letter, and evidence of economic substance before the bank will release escrowed funds to the operational account.Primary rail for high-value settlement is the Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700/MT710) issued by the Gibraltar bank to the contract manufacturer or component supplier, subject to UCP600. Documents required under the LC include commercial invoice, packing list, transport document (Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill), inspection certificate, insurance certificate, and CE/ISO 13485 compliance certificates. For urgent and low-value disbursements below USD 50,000, use TT wire via SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary details, purchase-order reference, and purpose narrative 'TRADE PAYMENT / MEDICAL EQUIPMENT COMPONENTS'. Interbank cover positions may be settled via SWIFT MT202COV. The receiving bank will match trade disbursements to the approved vendor list, historical purchase orders, and sanctions-screening results. No cash payments, shell-company payees, or third-party settlement are permitted.Outright deliverable FX forwards (fixed, flexible, and forward-extended) in USD, EUR, GBP/GIP, with tenors up to 24 months, limited to documented contract-manufacturing payables and hospital-tower receivables.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted currencies such as CNY, KRW, INR, and BRL where the underlying trade flow is evidenced by invoices and purchase orders, settled under a signed ISDA Master Agreement.FX swaps (spot/forward and forward/forward) for temporary conversion of export proceeds into the operational currency without taking speculative open positions.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps between USD and GBP/EUR for intercompany capital and project financing, executed under a board-approved treasury policy with genuine hedge documentation and independent valuation.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% flat corporate income tax rate (Income Tax Act 2010 (Gibraltar), s.15, as amended with effect from 1 January 2021); multinational groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million are additionally within the 15% Pillar Two domestic top-up tax under the Multinational Groups (Pillar Two) Taxation Act 2023.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividends under the Income Tax Act 2010, so dividends paid by a Gibraltar-resident company to its Cayman Exempted Company shareholder are distributed gross with no Gibraltar tax at source.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The CT01 must be submitted within 12 months of the end of the chargeable accounting period; for a 31 December year-end the return is due by 31 December of the following calendar year. The resulting corporation tax is payable no later than the following 1 February (or in the instalment pattern prescribed by the GTA for the relevant year of assessment). The Commissioner may issue a formal notice to file, in which case the deadline is the later of 12 months after the period end and 30 days after the notice.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation is required in Gibraltar where aggregate controlled transactions with associated enterprises exceed EUR 1,000,000 in a tax year; a Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000 (aligned with OECD BEPS Action 13). The GTA may in any case call for documentation below the monetary threshold where the arm's length outcome is implausible.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar ESR applies to every Gibraltar tax-resident company carrying on a relevant activity, including IP holding, headquarters, distribution and service centre functions. A Cayman Exempted Company managed and controlled from Gibraltar must satisfy the CIGA test: sufficient physical premises, adequate operating expenditure, qualified employees in Gibraltar, and core strategic decision records (board minutes, IP ownership registers, manufacturing oversight logs) kept on-island. A pure shell or letterbox endoscope 'manufacturer' fails the test and is exposed to exchange-of-information measures and administrative penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Civil: 100% tax-geared penalty on the understated tax plus statutory interest capped at 8% per annum. Criminal: on indictment a fine at the court's discretion and imprisonment up to 3 years; on summary conviction a fine up to £10,000 and imprisonment up to 6 months under Part XIX of the Income Tax Act 2010.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause or permit the fraud are joint and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax through responsibility notices; the Gibraltar court may issue travel bans and disqualify directors for up to 15 years under the Companies Act 2014.
Transfer Pricing / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Compensating transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's length result, a penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax attributable to deliberate error, and accrued interest; the GTA may re-characterize the arrangement where it lacks commercial rationale under the anti-artificiality provisions.

Director personal liability: Personal liability attaches where a director signs false transfer pricing documentation or knowingly procures the underpayment; prosecution for producing false evidence attracts imprisonment up to 2 years, together with a personal restitution order for the tax deficit.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar ESR non-compliance attracts fines up to £100,000 and default fines of £10,000 per month, with strike-off risk under the Companies Act 2014; if the Cayman parent itself remains subject to Cayman Economic Substance Law, Cayman penalties of USD 10,000 plus USD 10,000 per month apply to non-compliant relevant activities.

Director personal liability: Upon strike-off, directors become personally liable for company debts incurred after default; the GTA may register a beneficial ownership charge over the directors' personal assets and apply to the Supreme Court of Gibraltar for a travel ban where unpaid tax exceeds £50,000.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar), money laundering is punishable on indictment by imprisonment up to 14 years and an unlimited fine; a compliance officer's failure to report suspicious activity attracts imprisonment up to 5 years; the Financial Intelligence Unit may impose a monetary penalty of up to 10% of annual turnover for AML programme deficiencies.

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to confiscation orders and disqualification under the Financial Services Act; deliberate failure to register the ultimate beneficial owner with the Gibraltar Registry can trigger personal fines up to £500,000, particularly where structuring is designed to evade beneficial ownership transparency.
