E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Register the Cayman Exempted Company as an overseas company at the Gibraltar Companies Registry (Companies Act 2014) when establishing a branch or place of business in Gibraltar; file the certified Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, appoint a Gibraltar registered office and a local process agent; obtain the Gibraltar Companies Registry number. Step 2 — Register for direct tax at the Gibraltar Income Tax Office under section 84 of the Income Tax Act 2010 as a person chargeable to tax; submit the Companies Registry number, principal business activity (ISIC 3250 — manufacture of biocompatible dental ceramic blocks and milling discs), Gibraltar business address, and central management and control details; obtain the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TIN). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Cayman Islands: the Exempted Company obtains a statutory Tax Undertaking under section 6 of the Tax Concessions Act (20-year exemption from any future Cayman taxes) and may file a Certificate of Tax Residence with the Cayman Tax Information Authority to evidence non-residence in Cayman for CRS purposes. Gibraltar: no US-style check-the-box election exists; classification follows the Income Tax Act 2010, under which a foreign-incorporated company becomes Gibraltar tax resident when central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, while a non-resident company is taxed only on Gibraltar-source profits attributable to a Gibraltar trade or permanent establishment. The group may therefore organize central management and control outside Gibraltar so that the Gibraltar manufacturing branch is taxed on a territorial-source basis only; a Gibraltar-resident company is otherwise taxed at the standard 12.5% corporation tax rate, with the statutory participation exemption available by claim for qualifying dividends and capital gains. Hybrid mismatch: Gibraltar provides no hybrid-election mechanism; the structure must avoid deductible/non-includible mismatches and comply with OECD CRS and mandatory disclosure rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST in Gibraltar; indirect taxation comprises Gibraltar Customs import duties, excise duties, and stamp duty. UK VAT under VATA 1994 applies only to supplies made in or from the United Kingdom territory, which does not include Gibraltar.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable (GBP) — Gibraltar does not levy VAT or GST, so no indirect tax turnover threshold exists; customs EORI registration and import duty apply from the first import transaction. Should the group register a UK VAT presence for UK supplies, the £90,000 UK VAT registration threshold applies only to UK-established businesses, while non-established businesses must register from their first taxable supply; qualifying exports of dental zirconia blocks from the UK may be zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar: exports of dental CAD/CAM zirconia blocks made from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation because Gibraltar has no VAT or GST; no output tax is charged and no VAT refund claim mechanism applies. UK VAT: where the export is made by a UK VAT-registered person and the goods are physically removed from the UK customs territory to a final destination outside the UK, zero-rating is available under section 30(8) VATA 1994 and HMRC Notice 700, provided valid official export evidence (customs export declaration and transport documents) is obtained and the VAT return is corrected within the prescribed time. Supplies of services or UK domestic supplies that do not involve export of goods remain taxable at the standard UK VAT rate (20%) unless another relief applies.

  • Gibraltar or UK Customs Export Declaration (electronic customs entry evidencing departure from the customs territory)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exportation of the dental zirconia blocks
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank certificate proving receipt of export proceeds by SWIFT Wire or TT Payment, used where duty/tax relief is claimed or where bank financing requires proof of repatriation)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the goods, quantities, and export terms
  • Certificate of Origin (non-preferential or preferential as required by the destination market)

Gibraltar imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, mandatory e-invoicing, digital stamping, or SAF-T filing. Invoices may be issued on paper or PDF without fiscal authorization. Customs declarations must be lodged electronically through the Gibraltar Customs declaration system (interoperable with the UK customs process) for imports of zirconia raw materials and exports of finished blocks; invoices, customs entries, and export evidence must be retained for at least six years for Customs and Income Tax Office audit.

UK export zero-rating: HMRC may raise assessments for 4 years (10 years for deliberate non-disclosure), impose misdeclaration penalties of up to 100% of the tax lost under VATA 1994 sections 67 to 71, charge interest, and pursue criminal penalties under CEMA 1979, including forfeiture of goods. Gibraltar customs: false declarations attract penalties under the Customs Ordinance, including seizure and forfeiture, fines up to the value of the goods, and criminal conviction; the Income Tax Office may impose late-filing and failure-to-notify penalties under the Income Tax Act 2010 and may retrospectively claw back any duty relief (for example inward-processing relief on imported zirconia material) where export documentation cannot be substantiated within the statutory retention period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound is fully convertible at a fixed 1:1 parity to GBP. The Cayman Exempted Company can denominate operating, treasury and trade settlement accounts in GBP, USD or EUR without exchange-control approval. Current-account payments for dental consumables, raw-material imports and service fees are unrestricted; capital-account transactions, including equity injections, intercompany loans and profit remittances, are likewise unrestricted. Dividends paid by the Cayman Exempted Company are not subject to Gibraltar withholding tax, and there is no statutory exchange-control permission threshold. Convertibility is limited only by UK/Gibraltar sanctions lists, AML freezing orders or FATF-related enhanced due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement applies in Gibraltar. Trade invoices for dental CAD/CAM zirconia blocks may be collected in the currency of the sales contract, and the full amount may be retained, credited to a multi-currency operating account, or converted voluntarily through a licensed Gibraltar bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation timeline and no requirement to deposit export proceeds with any designated central bank account.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (United Kingdom), anchored by HM Government of Gibraltar's Currency Commissioner under the Currency Notes Act 1934 and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the licensing and prudential supervisor of Gibraltar banks. There is no separate Gibraltar central bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for cash collection and low-value supplier payments, but Low for full treasury operations. Offshore digital EMIs licensed in the UK, Gibraltar or another FATF-compliant jurisdiction may accept the Cayman entity for virtual GBP/EUR/USD accounts; however they are not deposit-taking banks, do not hold funds as protected bank deposits, cannot issue commercial letters of credit, and rarely provide FX forwards or trade loans. They may be used as a preliminary receiving account while physical bank onboarding is in progress, but material trade settlement, capital escrowing and hedging must be handled by a GFSC-licensed commercial bank. Legal constraint: the EMI must comply with UK Electronic Money Regulations 2011 or equivalent, client funds must be held in a segregated safeguarding account, and the Cayman entity's beneficial ownership pack must be acceptable to the EMI's compliance department.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use an SWIFT MT103 wire from the originating Cayman bank to the beneficiary Gibraltar bank, with precise field 50K for the ordering institution and field 59 for the beneficiary account, and field 72 free-text 'CAPITAL INJECTION / CAYMAN EXEMPTED CO / SHARE PREMIUM / ZIRCONIA BLOCK MANUFACTURING'. Include the Gibraltar IBAN or account number and the bank BIC code. The purpose must be recorded on the bank mandate as 'equity contribution for working capital', and the remitter must provide a source-of-funds pack showing the origin of subscribed capital. For transfers routed through UK clearers, the final leg can be executed via CHAPS; for EUR, Target2 or SEPA may be used once the Gibraltar bank provides a correspondent account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions with associated enterprises triggers the mandatory Local File. A Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated turnover is equal to or greater than GIP 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply. A tax-resident company carrying on manufacturing of dental zirconia blocks must demonstrate in Gibraltar: physical premises, full-time core staff, locally directed decision-making, and adequate operating expenditure. A Cayman Exempted Company that is centrally managed from Gibraltar must satisfy these tests locally. Failure triggers penalties, possible strike-off, and treatment as a shell company for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Income Tax Act: civil penalty up to 150% of the additional tax; on indictment, a fine at the court's discretion and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly concerned in the offence are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax; court may disqualify directors or impose travel bans; corporate veil may be pierced to recover assets.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Penalty of GIP 10,000 per failure; additional tax at 10% plus interest on any upward TP adjustment; higher penalty of 25% of the undercharged tax for careless or deliberate errors.

Director personal liability: Directors approving non-arm's length pricing may be personally assessed for the TP penalty if they were responsible for compliance; disqualification orders possible for repeated serious non-compliance.
Economic Substance Deficiency / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Under Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations, a penalty of up to GIP 10,000 may be imposed per financial year; continued deficiency can lead to a compulsory winding-up of the company or a notice of strike-off.

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to personal liability if they knowingly present the company as having substance while failing to implement it; they may be disqualified for up to 15 years and be required to bear the penalty.
AML / Proceeds of Crime Act (POCA)

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act: imprisonment up to 14 years and/or an unlimited fine for each offence; criminal confiscation orders can seize bank balances, dental manufacturing equipment, and trade receivables.

Director personal liability: Directors who aided, abetted, or assisted under POCA are personally criminally liable; they may be prohibited from leaving Gibraltar pending investigation; the corporate veil will be disregarded for confiscation purposes.
Persistent Non-Filing / Non-Payment of Tax

Statutory sanction: Daily surcharges accumulate; the Commissioner may raise estimated assessments and commence distress proceedings; contempt of court for failing to answer a court order can result in imprisonment of up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: Negligent directors can be fined personally; if the corporate failure to file amounts to wrongful trading, the court may require directors to personally contribute to the company's assets.
