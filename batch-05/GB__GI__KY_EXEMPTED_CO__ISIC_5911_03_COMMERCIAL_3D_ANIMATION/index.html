Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (ITO), 223 Main Street, Gibraltar GX11 1AA (portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation/income-tax-office). Company registration is administered separately by Gibraltar Companies House; the GFSC supervises AML but not direct tax.

        
E-government filing portal
Step 1: A Cayman Exempted Company (KY Exempted) is itself tax-exempt in the Cayman Islands under s.65 Companies Act and holds a Tax Exemption Certificate; no Cayman tax registration is required beyond the beneficial-ownership register. Step 2: For the Gibraltar nexus, file Form QFC-1 at Gibraltar Companies House to register the Cayman company as a Qualifying Foreign Company, producing certified incorporation documents, a UK-recognised notarised director resolution, and a Gibraltar registered-office appointment letter. Step 3: Upon QFC issuance, submit ITO Form 1-A (Non-Resident Company Branch Return) to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN); the ITO will issue a Statement of Non-Residence where central management and control remains outside Gibraltar. Step 4: Appoint a Gibraltar-resident company secretary and registered agent at a licensed CSP. Step 5: File annual confirmation statements, audited financial statements (Companies Act 2014 ss.241–242), and a nil/limited Gibraltar tax return (Form 10) each year; register as an employer only if local staff are engaged.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — the Cayman Exempted Company benefits from full Caymanian direct tax exemption for 20 years (extendable to 30); its Gibraltar QFC branch, being non-resident if managed and controlled abroad, is taxed solely on Gibraltar-source profits at the territorial 12.5% rate (Income Tax Act 2010 s.15). Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Tax_Exempt_Zone — the Cayman Exempted Company benefits from full Caymanian direct tax exemption for 20 years (extendable to 30); its Gibraltar QFC branch, being non-resident if managed and controlled abroad, is taxed solely on Gibraltar-source profits at the territorial 12.5% rate (Income Tax Act 2010 s.15). The composite structure therefore achieves an effective exempt-zone outcome for foreign-origin 3D animation production income.

        
Available election forms
Election 1: 'Non-Resident Declaration' filed annually with the ITO, restricting assessment to Gibraltar-source income; Election 2: Hybrid mismatch entity designation under the Gibraltar Taxation (Hybrid Mismatch) Rules 2021, permitting the Cayman exempt company to elect Transparent or Non-Transparent treatment of its Gibraltar branch for BEPS Action 2 alignment; Election 3: UK–Gibraltar DTA 2019 application (Form GB-GI Treaty) to protect business profits from UK taxation if UK clients or a UK deemed PE arise; Election 4: Economic Substance (Geographically Mobile Activities) Rules 2019 declaration categorising the branch as a pure-holding/low-substance activity while the Cayman parent retains core VFX IP title.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
Gibraltar Purchase Tax and Customs Duties (no VAT; no sales and use tax). Exports of services are outside the scope of any Gibraltar indirect tax.

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
GIP 0 for services — no economic-activity threshold applies. Any qualified foreign company establishing a branch in Gibraltar must register with Companies House and the ITO irrespective of turnover. For physical goods imports (e.g. rendered deliverables on media), a customs entry is required only where the goods consignment exceeds GIP 10,000 CIF; import duties/purchase tax (0–25% per tariff heading) apply at the positive rate only.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Gibraltar has no VAT, so the express category is 'out of scope' rather than zero-rating: B2B exports of commercial CGI, VFX and 3D character animation services supplied to EU, UK, or third-country clients are exempt from any Gibraltar indirect tax, provided the recipient is not established in Gibraltar. If the same services were treated as UK supplies (e.g. due to a UK fixed establishment), they would be zero-rated under VATA 1994 Sch.8 Gp.16 only where copyright or IP rights in the finished animation are transferred outright; otherwise they would fall to the positive rate inside the UK subject to reverse charge—an outcome avoided by maintaining the Gibraltar-only supply chain.

Gibraltar operates no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for B2B services; e-invoicing is voluntary (EN 16931 format adopted for public-procurement suppliers above EUR 5,000). Customs transactions are mandatorily electronic via the Gibraltar Customs Electronic Clearance System (Single Administrative Document / SAD-ICTS). Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 ss.93–95: where an out-of-scope export position is founded on false documentation, the ITO may make assessment for 20 years back and impose a civil penalty up to 200% of the tax lost, rising to criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 3 years for fraudulent declarations. Customs Duties Act 2013 s.50 permits forfeiture of goods plus a penalty of 150% of duty evaded for misdeclared SADs. If UK reverse-charge errors are made by the client-supplier nexus, HMRC VATA 1994 Sch.24 penalty applies at 70% for deliberate inaccuracies, with retrospective VAT assessments on the Gibraltar entity if a UK permanent establishment is found.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Gibraltar maintains fully liberalised capital and current account convertibility with no exchange restrictions, no surrender requirements and no offshore premium mechanism. The Gibraltar pound is fixed 1:1 to GBP and freely exchangeable into major reserve currencies. Profit remittances, dividends and royalties may be transferred without prior central bank approval; only standard anti-money laundering and economic substance verifications apply. Cayman Exempted Company shareholders are free to receive distributions in any currency via licensed financial intermediaries.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Gibraltar law does not impose any statutory export proceeds surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or retention limit. Export receipts from animation and VFX services may be held in the currency of receipt indefinitely, and corporate treasury may convert funds at market rates through authorised banks.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Financial Services Commission of Gibraltar (GFSC) combined with HM Treasury supervision; the Bank of England serves as the monetary authority for sterling, as the Gibraltar pound is pegged at par to the British pound (£).

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
12.5% effective Gibraltar corporate income tax rate on chargeable Gibraltar-source profits; 0% in the Cayman Islands for the Exempted Company head office

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
0% in Gibraltar on dividends paid to the Cayman parent (Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividend distributions); 0% in the Cayman Islands on dividends paid by the Exempted Company (no corporate income tax regime exists)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar: Local File required where the Gibraltar entity or branch conducts related-party transactions exceeding GBP 2,000,000 in aggregate annual value, or where the Gibraltar operation holds intercompany balances exceeding GBP 10,000,000; Master File required where the global group consolidated turnover exceeds GBP 60,000,000. Cayman: no formal transfer pricing documentation regime, but the TIA may request benchmarking studies where the Exempted Company engages in cross-border services or IP licensing arrangements; contemporaneous OECD-aligned documentation is strongly advised.

      
Economic substance scope: The Cayman Exempted Company must comply with the International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act: for ISIC 5911 'Commercial CGI Visual Effects and 3D Character Animation', the activity is characterized as a geographically mobile service business. Core income-generating activities — creative direction, 3D modeling, lighting, rendering management, client liaison, and post-production QA — must be physically performed in a jurisdiction with genuine substance; if the substance is located in Gibraltar, the entity must (a) declare Gibraltar tax residence on the Cayman ESN, (b) hold a Gibraltar Form C assessment, and (c) maintain in Gibraltar leased offices, a minimum of 2 full-time senior animators and 1 production accountant, and quarterly board meetings with substantive quorum. Failure to demonstrate directed and managed presence results in deemed Cayman tax residence, automatic exchange of information with Gibraltar, and re-characterization of all Gibraltar-source profits as taxable at 12.5% in Gibraltar with interest and penalties.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion — Deliberate Omission of Gibraltar-Source Profits
        
Statutory sanction: Assessment for the omitted profits at 12.5% plus interest at 8% per annum compounded daily; a civil penalty of up to 100% of the understated tax; criminal conviction under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 sections 146–150 with a fine not exceeding GBP 150,000; in the Cayman Islands, disclosure to the TIA may trigger the removal of the Exempted Company's tax exemption certificate.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are criminally liable for aiding tax evasion and face imprisonment up to 7 years, personal and unrestricted civil restitution of the underpaid tax, a travel ban preventing departure from Gibraltar pending trial, and disqualification as directors under the Gibraltar Companies Act for up to 15 years; assets traced to the Cayman head office are subject to freezing orders.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Sham Substance Arrangement
        
Statutory sanction: Gibraltar: the ITO disallows the foreign-source exclusion, taxes the entire profit at 12.5%, and imposes a penalty of up to GBP 250,000; Cayman: the TIA strikes the company off the Register under the Economic Substance Act, cancels the Exempted Company's tax exemption status, and issues a monetary penalty of CI$20,000 plus CI$1,000 per day of continued non-compliance.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for the full amount of evaded tax under the principle of piercing the corporate veil; they face criminal prosecution for fraudulent trading, imprisonment up to 5 years, mandatory restitution of misappropriated funds, a 10-year travel ban across the UK, Gibraltar, and Overseas Territories, and confiscation of personal assets under the Proceeds of Crime Act.

      

      
      

        AML / Beneficial Ownership Violation
        
Statutory sanction: Gibraltar: under the Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Act 2017 and AML Act 2019, fines up to GBP 300,000 or 5% of annual turnover, whichever is higher, plus confiscation of the funds involved in suspicious transactions; Cayman: penalties up to CI$25,000 for incomplete or false BO returns and imprisonment up to 2 years; the entity may be added to the public register of non-compliant entities

        
Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability with imprisonment up to 4 years, disqualification from holding corporate office for 10 years, and travel restrictions; where the concealed principal is identified, the directors are jointly liable for the tax consequences of the entire Gibraltar-source profit, and the corporate veil is pierced for both civil restitution and criminal sanction.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Stripping
        
Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Transfer Pricing Regulations 2014: the ITO makes an arm-length adjustment, recharacterizing the transactions, disallowing the excessive deductions, and assessing additional tax at 12.5% plus a transfer pricing penalty of up to 100% of the adjustment where documentation is absent or contemporaneously prepared; the case is automatically referred to HMRC through the UK–Gibraltar tax information exchange protocol

        
Director personal liability: Directors who approved the non-arm-length margins are personally liable for the additional tax and penalty under Gibraltar's Fiscal Responsibility provisions; they may be subject to summary proceedings, personal travel bans pending assessment, disqualification orders, and criminal prosecution for making false statements in the Form C, with imprisonment up to 5 years.
