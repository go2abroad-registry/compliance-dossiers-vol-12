Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar, Income Tax Office. Official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation; e-Services portal: https://tax.gov.gi. Statutory basis: Income Tax Act 2010.

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company ('KY_EXEMPTED_CO') that will carry on regulated multi-family office / discretionary portfolio advisory services through a Gibraltar branch or place of business must first obtain a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number ('TIN') from the Income Tax Office. Procedure: (1) confirm that the GFSC licence or expression of support is in hand, given the High_Risk_Special_License classification; (2) submit the prescribed Company Tax Registration application through the Income Tax Office, attaching the Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, Certificate of Good Standing, directors and ultimate beneficial owners register, Gibraltar service address, and details of place of effective management and control; (3) receive a Gibraltar TIN within approximately 5–15 business days. No VAT/GST identifier is issued because Gibraltar does not operate a general consumption tax. The Gibraltar TIN is used for income tax, employer/PAYE returns, and customs/statistical declarations.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Territorial_Scope for a non-resident company; worldwide taxation if Gibraltar management and control is established). The Cayman Exempted Company is Tax_Exempt_Zone in its domestic jurisdiction, but Gibraltar does not recognise pass-through treatment; it is taxed as a body corporate. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Territorial_Scope for a non-resident company; worldwide taxation if Gibraltar management and control is established). The Cayman Exempted Company is Tax_Exempt_Zone in its domestic jurisdiction, but Gibraltar does not recognise pass-through treatment; it is taxed as a body corporate. Non-resident status limits liability to Gibraltar-source profits; Gibraltar-resident status extends liability to worldwide profits.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate check-the-box elections or elective entity classification. Available statutory elections include group relief/consortium relief among 75% group companies and joint loss surrender elections. Hybrid mismatch and anti-avoidance rules under OECD BEPS Action 2 apply automatically; there is no election to alter the entity's tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None applicable — Gibraltar does not levy VAT, GST, or sales and use tax. Indirect taxation is limited to customs and excise duties on the importation of certain scheduled goods (alcohol, tobacco, fuel, and related products).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable (GIP). Gibraltar imposes no economic-turnover threshold for VAT/GST/GCT registration because no general consumption tax is in force. A customs registration may be required for importers of dutiable goods, but no turnover threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — Gibraltar has no VAT/GST regime, so there is no export zero-rating claim. For Gibraltar corporate income tax purposes, fees from discretionary portfolio advisory services supplied to non-resident private family clients may fall outside Gibraltar-source taxation only if the services are actually performed outside Gibraltar and the profits are not derived from Gibraltar. The territorial analysis is factual and not evidenced by export documentation.

  • If a physical export were relevant, the standard evidence bundle would be: (i) Customs Export Declaration (CDS), (ii) Bill of Lading/Airway Bill, (iii) Commercial Invoice, and (iv) Inward Remittance Certificate/SWIFT payment evidence.

None. Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping. Businesses must preserve invoices, contracts, and accounting records for six years under the Income Tax Act. Customs declarations are filed electronically through Gibraltar/UK customs processes where goods are imported.

There is no VAT zero-rating fraud clawback because no VAT/GST is chargeable. However, false declarations or artificial arrangements intended to mischaracterise Gibraltar-source profits as offshore or non-resident will be reopened by the Income Tax Office under anti-avoidance powers. The Commissioner may assess tax for up to six years (20 years for fraud or wilful default), add statutory interest, and impose tax-geared penalties of up to 100% (or 200% in cases of fraudulent or negligent returns). Criminal prosecution, GFSC disciplinary measures, and AML/CFT sanctions may also apply to a licensed multi-family office.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound is exchanged at par with GBP and is fully convertible. Gibraltar has no exchange-control restrictions on current-account payments, profit remittances, dividends, or capital movements. A Cayman Exempted Company may therefore hold and repatriate USD/GBP/EUR balances freely; remittances to Cayman or family-office beneficiaries require only CRS/FATCA reporting, standard AML verification, and, where applicable, evidence of Cayman economic substance. No Bank of England or GFSC conversion permit is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Gibraltar does not impose statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirements on export receipts or service income. Foreign-currency proceeds earned by the multi-family office may be retained indefinitely, credited to operational or escrow accounts, and converted only on a transactional/voluntary basis; settlement follows standard T+2 spot practice.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (de facto monetary authority; Gibraltar pound is backed by Bank of England notes) / Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the licensing and supervisory authority for banks and FX businesses in Gibraltar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. Offshore digital EMIs (UK, EEA or third-country) are not authorised by GFSC to take deposits, hold client money for a Cayman Exempted Company, or execute SWIFT interbank/custodial settlement. Post-Brexit EEA passporting into Gibraltar is unavailable, and the MFO's ISIC 6430 / NAICS 523920 activity is classified high-risk. Digital EMIs may be used only as a supplementary FX/expense conduit, never as the primary treasury or custody clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: SWIFT MT103 (customer transfer) or MT202 COV (interbank cover) from the contributing family member/trust to the Gibraltar-based account of the Cayman Exempted Company, with beneficiary field showing the operational account and reference 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION / CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION'. For GBP, CHAPS same-day settlement is available; for USD/EUR, use SWIFT with the correspondent banking routing designated by the receiving bank. Remittance information must cite the entity's CRS/FATCA registration number and clearly state 'Capital Contribution - no underlying goods or services'. The receiving bank applies the relevant statistical purpose code, typically an inward-investment code; ISO 20022 ExternalPurposeCode 'INVC' should be used where supported. Verification dossier: board resolution approving the capital injection, signed capital subscription/issuance document, source-of-funds evidence, and exchange-control nil declaration. Funds may remain in a capital escrow account until the investment advisory mandate requires liquidity.Clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 for routine client and vendor settlements; MT202/COV for interbank/high-value obligations; CHAPS for urgent GBP; Fedwire/CHIPS plus SWIFT for USD/EUR. Commercial letters of credit are legally permissible but are not a standard MFO rail; if the family office finances cross-border trade assets, a licensed Gibraltar bank may issue/confirm a documentary credit only against valid title documents, underlying trade contracts, and within its credit and AML appetite. Box 4 Allowable deductions: employment costs, office rent, professional fees, compliance costs, utilities, interest, and any other revenue expenses wholly and exclusively incurred for the Gibraltar operation. Deduct only expenses attributable to the Gibraltar branch. Intercompany allocations must be supported by a contemporaneous transfer pricing analysis. Late filing attracts a fixed penalty of GBP 300 plus a daily surcharge of GBP 60 for each day of continued non-compliance, capped at GBP 3,000. Late payment interest accrues at 8% per annum, and unpaid tax may attract a 10% collection surcharge.
Box 5 Trading losses and loss relief: declaration of current-year losses or carried-forward losses used to reduce taxable profits. Non-resident branch losses are limited to Gibraltar-source losses and may only be carried forward against future Gibraltar-source trading profits. Late filing attracts a fixed penalty of GBP 300 plus a daily surcharge of GBP 60 for each day of continued non-compliance, capped at GBP 3,000. Late payment interest accrues at 8% per annum, and unpaid tax may attract a 10% collection surcharge.
Box 6 Tax computation: taxable profits at 10%, tax credits, payments on account, and final tax liability / repayment. Non-resident companies are not entitled to Gibraltar personal credits; include payments of Gibraltar branch tax made at the time of payment. Late filing attracts a fixed penalty of GBP 300 plus a daily surcharge of GBP 60 for each day of continued non-compliance, capped at GBP 3,000. Late payment interest accrues at 8% per annum, and unpaid tax may attract a 10% collection surcharge.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar Local File is mandatory where annual Gibraltar gross revenue exceeds GBP 10,000,000; Master File is mandatory where the consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 750,000,000. All entities must nevertheless retain supporting valuations and arm's-length evidence insofar as intercompany transactions materially affect the Gibraltar tax base.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations apply to every tax-resident entity undertaking a relevant activity, including investment and asset management services. A Multi-Family Office operating through a Cayman Exempted Company must demonstrate adequate full-time employees, physical premises, management direction, and operating expenditure in Gibraltar. Annual substance reporting is mandatory. Failure renders the entity subject to financial penalties, deregistration, and exchange of information with foreign competent authorities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction exposes the entity to a fine of up to GBP 250,000 plus confiscation of the tax benefit and a maximum imprisonment term of 7 years for individuals involved. Civil tax penalties may reach 200% of the tax understated.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns are personally liable for the penalty and may be disqualified as directors for up to 15 years. Travel bans and personal bankruptcy proceedings may be invoked, and the corporate veil is pierced for fraud.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Breach of the Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations attracts penalties from GBP 10,000 to GBP 100,000 per year of default, possible striking-off from the Companies Registry, and automatic reporting to the Court of Justice exchange mechanisms.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally penalised for failing to ensure substance reporting compliance. In insolvency proceedings, they can be held personally liable for fraudulent or wrongful trading where the corporate form was used to conceal lack of substance.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: The firm may be fined up to GBP 500,000 per breach, its financial services licence suspended or revoked, and monies confiscated under the Proceeds of Crime Act. Responsible individuals may be imprisoned for up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who consented to or knowingly turned a blind eye to money laundering activities are criminally liable in their individual capacity. Corporate privilege is unavailable; personal travel bans, asset freezes, and extradition may follow.
Transfer Pricing Adjustment and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner of Income Tax may upwardly adjust taxable profits to reflect arm's-length pricing, disallow the excessive interest deductions, and impose penalties equal to up to 100% of the resulting tax deficiency plus interest at 8% per annum.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve sham intercompany arrangements or sign incorrect tax returns may be subject to negligence penalties and joint and several liability for the underpaid Gibraltar corporation tax.
