Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar; official e-portal: Gibraltar Tax e-filing system at https://www.gibraltartax.gov.gi

E-government filing portal

A non-Gibraltar incorporated foreign entity, such as a Cayman Exempted Company, must first register a branch or place of business with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act. Upon completion of commercial registration, the entity must apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) using the prescribed application form, providing certified constitutional documents (certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles), details of directors and beneficial owners, and a Gibraltar-registered address. The ITO issues a unique TIN/tax reference number within 5–10 business days. VAT registration is not applicable because Gibraltar does not impose VAT.

Not applicable because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST; the concept of 0% export VAT does not arise. For customs duty drawback or temporary export relief, the exporter must substantiate physical exportation of the goods within 12 months of importation and maintain full customs entry records.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Certificate of Origin (if required)

Gibraltar does not mandate electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance for indirect tax purposes. Customs entries are filed electronically through the Gibraltar Customs automated entry-processing system, but there is no fiscal stamping, no tax-authority API clearance requirement, and no mandatory certified e-invoicing software.

Since no VAT zero-rating refund exists, the main compliance risk concerns customs drawback and export declaration accuracy. Fraudulent or incomplete export proof triggers retrospective clawback of duty relief, monetary penalties of up to five times the duty under the Customs and Excise Act, forfeiture of goods, and potential criminal liability for directors and officers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar Pound (GIP) is pegged at 1:1 parity with UK Sterling (GBP) and both currencies are fully convertible for current account transactions (freight invoices, disbursements, commissions) and capital account transactions (equity injections, loan repayments) without any quantitative restriction or prior Central Bank approval. Gibraltar has no exchange-control legislation. Profit remittances from a Gibraltar branch or from a non-resident Cayman Exempted Co are free of dividend withholding and capital controls; however, if the Cayman entity carries on a trade in Gibraltar through a permanent establishment, the profits are subject to Gibraltar taxation under the Income Tax Act at the prevailing corporate rate. All outward remittances are screened only under AML/CFT reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Neither the United Kingdom nor Gibraltar imposes any surrender, conversion, or repatriation requirement for export proceeds. The GIP is freely usable without mandatory conversion of hard-currency earnings. For an NVOCC ocean freight forwarder, receipts in USD/EUR may be retained in foreign-currency accounts. Under the Gibraltar Anti-Money Laundering Regulations 2020 and the UK Proceeds of Crime Act 2002, cash receipts or any suspicious payment exceeding EUR 10,000 must be reported to the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU). The exporter must maintain contemporaneous evidence of the underlying freight contract, signed bills of lading, and commercial invoices to demonstrate the trade-based nature of each flow.

Central bank supervision: The Government of Gibraltar does not maintain a central bank; the Bank of England acts as the monetary authority for the Sterling Zone, while the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the full licensing and prudential regulator for all banks operating in Gibraltar. No separate foreign exchange administration exists; exchange-control monitoring is exercised through the GFSC and the UK Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) for sanction-related matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low-Medium. Offshore digital EMIs without a Gibraltar or UK licence are not permissible as primary treasury rails. A GFSC-licensed E-Money Institution or a UK-authorised EMI with a valid Gibraltar top-up licence may be used for ancillary, low-value settlement only, but the same corporate KYC pack, source-of-funds evidence, and regulatory domiciliation checks apply. Unregulated virtual IBAN providers and non-EEA/non-UK EMIs should be entirely rejected for any account class of a Cayman Exempted Co.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File/Master File arises where the multinational group reports consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more and the Gibraltar PE records related-party transactions exceeding GBP 1 million in annual turnover; below that threshold, a concise OECD-compliant transfer pricing documentation file must be kept in Gibraltar covering freight commission, container charges, and cost-contribution arrangements.

Economic substance scope: A Cayman Exempted Company claiming tax residence other than Gibraltar must demonstrate core income-generating activities and operational management in the claimed jurisdiction (Cayman) through the Cayman International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Law; if it is managed and controlled in Gibraltar, Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations apply to any 'relevant activity'. Ocean freight forwarding/NVOCC is treated as a service business rather than pure shipping and therefore must pass the syndicated economic-substance test by proving physical presence, full-time employees, and board meetings in the claimed jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Understatement of Gibraltar PE Income

Statutory sanction: Prosecution for tax evasion under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 and Criminal Justice Act 2015; a fine not exceeding the amount of tax evaded plus interest and costs; imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax, may be disqualified as directors, have travel restrictions imposed pending criminal proceedings, and face imprisonment.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may disregard the circular transaction, impose a 100% adjustment plus a penalty of up to 100% of the artificial tax advantage, and apply to strike off the Gibraltar registered overseas company under the Companies Act 2014.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: directors and shadow directors are liable for civil restitution of diverted corporate assets, are subject to disqualification orders and travel bans, and may suffer criminal fine or imprisonment for fraudulent trading.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustment to the arm's length consideration, an additional 20% transfer-pricing penalty on adjusted tax, and, in serious cases, referral to the Gibraltar Commissioner's Anti-Avoidance unit.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve the mispricing may be personally assessed for the additional tax where the company is insolvent or liquidated, and can be prohibited from managing a company for up to 15 years.
AML / Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the AML Regulations 2019, fines up to GBP 2,000,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years for money-laundering facilitation; under the ESR, daily fines of up to GBP 10,000 and loss of tax transparency status.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for civil restitution and criminal penalties, are subject to travel bans and overseas asset freezes, and may receive custodial sentences of up to 7 years.
Unlicensed NVOCC / Unauthorised Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Revocation of trading registration, seizure of freight documents, monetary penalties under the Gibraltar Customs Duties Act and Financial Services Act 2019, and prohibition of further cargo consolidation.

Director personal liability: Directors carry criminal liability for conducting unlicensed regulated business, face fines of up to GBP 500,000, integrity-based disqualification, and courts may impose travel bans while restitution claims are assessed.
