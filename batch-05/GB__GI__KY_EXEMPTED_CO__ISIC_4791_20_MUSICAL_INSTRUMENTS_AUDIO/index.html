Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to the Cayman exempted company for registration in Gibraltar; shares are issued per the articles (here 10,000 ordinary shares of US$1.00 each). No escrow account is required solely for the Gibraltar overseas-establishment registration, but subscription proceeds must be recorded in the statutory share register and supported by a bank receipt.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety statement certified by the Gibraltar Fire and Rescue Service for the registered office and any dispatch warehouse; annual workplace risk assessment and occupational health clearance under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2018; structural load and electrical installation clearance certificate issued by a licensed civil/electrical engineer in accordance with the Occupational and Fire Safety Regulations 2015.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax); corporate registry portal: Gibraltar Companies House (https://www.csra.gov.gi); tax portal: https://www.income-tax.gov.gi

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company managing and controlling its business from Gibraltar must be registered with the Gibraltar Companies House under the Companies Act 2014 (either as a foreign company or a Gibraltar-established company) and must apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN). The application requires: certified certificate of incorporation from the Cayman Islands Registrar; memorandum and articles of association; register of directors and members; evidence of the Gibraltar-registered office address and licensed company manager/agent; and a written declaration of the intended territorial source of profits. If taxable Gibraltar-source trading income is anticipated, the company must file a corporate tax return (Form C1) annually and pay advance instalments of corporation tax at 12.5%. If no Gibraltar-source income arises, a nil-return filing and annual confirmation of tax status is still required. The TIN is issued by the Income Tax Office typically within 7–10 working days. Non-resident Cayman exempted companies that do not exercise management and control in Gibraltar and derive no Gibraltar-source income are generally outside the charge to Gibraltar income tax and need no TIN, but the online trading of physical goods into Gibraltar customers may trigger registration if turnover arises from a Gibraltar permanent establishment.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone / Territorial_Scope. The Cayman Exempted Company is tax-exempt in its place of incorporation (Cayman Islands, no income tax) and, if centrally managed and controlled in Gibraltar, it is treated as a Gibraltar-resident company for tax purposes but is taxed only on profits arising in or derived from Gibraltar (territorial principle). Pure foreign-source trading income of a non-resident company is outside the jurisdiction. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone / Territorial_Scope. The Cayman Exempted Company is tax-exempt in its place of incorporation (Cayman Islands, no income tax) and, if centrally managed and controlled in Gibraltar, it is treated as a Gibraltar-resident company for tax purposes but is taxed only on profits arising in or derived from Gibraltar (territorial principle). Pure foreign-source trading income of a non-resident company is outside the jurisdiction. Gibraltar corporation tax rate applies at 12.5% only to Gibraltar-source trading profits; passive investment income may be taxed at 10% or exempt under specific provisions.

Available election forms

Under the Income Tax Act 2010 (Gibraltar), available elections include: (1) election for territorial-source treatment under Section 12A for companies that are centrally managed and controlled in Gibraltar, restricting tax to Gibraltar-source profits; (2) election to be treated as a non-resident company for tax purposes if the central management and control test is not satisfied, subject to the anti-avoidance rules; (3) Qualifying Company status under the Companies Act for offshore-incorporated entities redomiciling to Gibraltar; (4) election under the International Headquarters (IHQ) regime for qualifying headquarters activities; (5) hybrid mismatch elections are not generally available because Gibraltar follows the OECD hybrid mismatch framework via the Income Tax (Hybrid and other Mismatch Arrangements) Regulations, requiring companies to disclose and align tax treatment of hybrid entities and instruments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

VAT / GST / Sales and Use Tax – Not applicable. Gibraltar has no VAT, GST, or general sales tax. A narrow set of specific consumption duties is imposed under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance on imported excise goods only (alcoholic beverages, tobacco products, and mineral oils). Musical instruments and audio gear (ISIC 4791 / NAICS 459140) are not excisable goods and therefore bear no indirect tax on import, sale, or export.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – no VAT/GST registration threshold exists because no such tax is levied. A Customs/Excise Dealer's Licence is required only for persons importing or dealing in excise goods; online retailers of musical instruments and studio audio equipment are outside this licensing scope.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – Gibraltar levies no VAT, so no zero-rating mechanism exists. Exports of musical instruments and audio equipment from Gibraltar are outside the scope of any indirect tax. For income tax purposes, export trade profits are taxable only if the profit source is territorial to Gibraltar; the Cayman Exempted Company may rely on territorial sourcing if the sale negotiation, contract conclusion, and shipping terms are structured offshore, and export sales to non-Gibraltar purchasers are generally treated as foreign-source profits exempt from Gibraltar tax.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (electronic single administrative document via the GibTrade customs platform)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Gibraltar
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List matching the customs declaration value
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/MT103 evidence from the overseas buyer via Stripe/PayPal settlement
  • Certificate of Cayman Tax Exempt Status and Gibraltar trade certificate (if claiming treaty or territorial relief)

Not applicable – Gibraltar does not operate any real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing clearance, digital stamping, or API-based tax reporting regime. Standard commercial invoices, payment records from Stripe/PayPal, and conventional accounting records satisfy tax verification requirements. Cross-border B2B transactions to EU customers remain outside Gibraltar indirect tax; the customer's import VAT is settled in the destination jurisdiction.

Under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended), any artificial or fictitious export arrangement, false territorial-source claim, or misdeclaration enabling tax avoidance may be recharacterised by the Commissioner of Income Tax under anti-avoidance provisions (Sections 89–91). Culpable penalties range from 100% to 200% of the tax undercharged, plus interest at 8% per annum. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export documentation or false statements can result in fines up to £50,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years. The Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit and the Customs and Excise Department may impose additional civil penalties of up to 250% of the duty or rebate falsely claimed and may seize goods and conveyances. Retrospective clawback is permitted without a general time limit in cases of fraud, and a 6-year limitation applies in cases of careless underdeclaration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates full current-account and capital-account convertibility with no exchange-control licence. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is legal tender pegged at 1:1 to GBP, and USD/EUR/GBP receipts may be held freely. A Cayman Exempted Company may remit profits from its Gibraltar treasury without exchange-control approval and without Gibraltar withholding tax, provided it is not managed-and-controlled in Gibraltar and does not carry on regulated activity under the Gibraltar Financial Services Act. Practical hurdles are documentary rather than statutory: banks will require proof of Cayman tax exemption, economic substance or passive-entity status, source of funds from musical-instrument/audio marketplace sales, and clear UBO information before enabling dividend or shareholder distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts in Gibraltar. Proceeds from online sales of musical instruments and studio audio gear may be retained 100% in the original settlement currency for any period; no forced repatriation, no surrender, and no exchange-control filing applies. The only procedural requirement is prompt bank classification of inward remittances as trading receipts, supported by marketplace settlement statements and invoices.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (United Kingdom) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) — the Bank of England provides the sterling monetary anchor, while the GFSC is the Gibraltar-licensed banking and foreign-exchange oversight authority; HM Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar pound. No separate Central Bank of Gibraltar exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-Medium as a standalone clearing rail; High only as a supplementary routing layer. A foreign offshore EMI not licensed by the GFSC or FCA cannot open a Gibraltar bank account, cannot access SWIFT/CHAPS as principal, and cannot lawfully hold or transmit funds for a Gibraltar-linked business without a Gibraltar electronic-money licence. Stripe and PayPal can operate as payment facilitators for the online audio-instrument store, but final settlement must be drawn down into a GFSC-licensed bank. Post-Brexit, EEA EMI passports are no longer automatically valid in Gibraltar, so a sponsored bank-licensed arrangement or a separately authorized Gibraltar EMI is required.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.008 with a clear remittance narrative 'Shareholder capital contribution — Cayman Exempted Co — Gibraltar Escrow'. The receiving Gibraltar bank will require its internal purpose-code field to be marked as capital transfer, commonly the ISO 20022 code 'CAPT' or bank-specific equivalent; the underlying board resolution, share allotment/payment notice, and Cayman register update must be delivered before value is booked. For GBP funds, route via CHAPS or Faster Payments; for EUR, via TARGET2; for USD, via CHIPS/Fedwire using the receiving bank's correspondent. Final verification matches the MT103 field 50a/59a details to the corporate name and account number; any mismatch with UBO documentation causes automatic suspension.Direct supplier payments should be released using SWIFT MT103 with trade-invoice reference and purpose code 'TRAD' or 'INTC'. For large-risk purchase orders of musical instruments or audio gear, a documentary collection or commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700, irrevocable sight or deferred) is permissible with the LC issued through the Gibraltar bank's trade-finance desk. Interbank cover for cross-currency payments uses SWIFT MT202 COV. E-commerce refunds are cleared through Stripe/PayPal and card-rail reversals; high-value inventory replenishment can be supported by a receivables-backed supply-chain finance line documented under English/Gibraltar law.Deliverable FX forwards in GBP/EUR/USD with GFSC-licensed banks or EU/UK authorised counterparties, documented under an ISDA Master Agreement, no speculative gearing.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) cash-settled in GIP/GBP or EUR where physical delivery of a restricted currency is impossible, supported by documented trade settlement exposure.Cross-currency swaps (FX swaps/CCSs) to hedge USD/EUR procurement costs against GBP-denominated merchant settlement income, subject to EMIR transaction reporting and internal hedging-policy limits.Plain vanilla FX options and FX collars for capped forward purchase/sale of major currencies, permitted only where the underlying inventory/sales exposures create economic exposure.
