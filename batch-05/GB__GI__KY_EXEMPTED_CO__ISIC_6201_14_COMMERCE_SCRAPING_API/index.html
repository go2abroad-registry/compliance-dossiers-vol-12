Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office of HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax), Portland House, Glacis Road, Gibraltar GX11 1AA; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation. Company registration: Registrar of Companies, Companies House Gibraltar. Customs: HM Customs Gibraltar. Data protection: Gibraltar Regulatory Authority.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: If the Cayman Exempted Company establishes a physical place of business in Gibraltar, it must register as an 'external company' with the Registrar of Companies within 30 days, filing certified copies of the Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, register of directors and secretary, and a Gibraltar registered address and process agent. Step 2: The Income Tax Office then issues a Gibraltar company reference number (TIN), either automatically on companies-registry data exchange or on filing Form IT-R1 under the Income Tax Act; this TIN governs corporation tax, PAYE, and all fiscal filings. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the Cayman Exempted Company holds a 30-year Cayman Tax Exemption Certificate (Tax Concessions Act) and is tax-exempt in the Cayman Islands; in Gibraltar it is treated as a non-resident company where central management and control is exercised outside Gibraltar. Only Gibraltar-source income is taxable at the 10% corporate rate; no capital gains tax, no net-worth tax, no withholding tax on dividends, interest, or royalties, and no branch remittance tax.

Available election forms

Gibraltar provides no US-style 'check-the-box' election. Relevant mechanisms: (1) transition from Gross Income Based Tax (GIBT) to standard chargeable-profits assessment — GIBT is being phased out and the standard 10% corporation tax on chargeable profits applies from financial year 2025; (2) non-resident companies require no election — automatic taxation on Gibraltar-source income only; (3) hybrid mismatch designations under the Income Tax (Hybrid Mismatch) Regulations for structured UK/EU financing and IP arrangements; (4) optional UK VAT registration under the non-UK-established business rules where UK B2C digital-services turnover is below the mandatory threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST — Gibraltar applies no general value-added, goods-and-services, or sales tax. Indirect levies are limited to import duty, purchase tax on a closed list of goods (alcohol, tobacco, fuel, luxury vehicles), and stamp duty. Cross-border digital services (web-scraping API subscriptions, proxy-gateway usage fees) are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation; UK VAT may apply where private consumers resident in the UK are supplied.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A in Gibraltar pounds (GIP) — there is no VAT, therefore no turnover threshold for indirect-tax registration. Mandatory triggers are: (i) external-company registration with Companies House Gibraltar within 30 days of establishing a Gibraltar place of business (no turnover threshold); (ii) HM Customs registration for importers, arising at first importation with no de-minimis turnover limit; (iii) UK VAT registration once UK-resident private-consumer digital-service sales exceed GBP 85,000 in any rolling 12-month period; voluntary registration is permitted below that threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — Gibraltar imposes no VAT, so there is no export zero-rating or refund mechanism to claim. Exports of services, including web-scraping API access and proxy-gateway subscriptions, are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax regardless of customer location; no output tax arises and no input tax is recoverable. For UK VAT purposes, if the company registers, cross-border B2B supplies remain outside the scope of UK VAT under place-of-supply rules, and UK zero-rating applies only to physically exported goods, not to these intangible services.

  • Commercial invoice and packing list

No real-time e-invoicing, continuous transaction control (CTC), fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate exists in Gibraltar. Invoices must contain standard commercial particulars (supplier and customer identity, description, consideration, date) but require no government clearance or cryptographic stamp. Customs declarations are submitted electronically via HM Customs Gibraltar's single administrative entry system. UK Making Tax Digital (MTD) requirements are irrelevant unless the company registers for UK VAT, in which case MTD-compatible digital record-keeping applies only to UK VAT returns.

No Gibraltar VAT export clawback exists. Scrutiny instead targets income characterisation, substance, and cross-border compliance: (i) incorrect Income Tax returns attract default interest up to 8% per annum and penalties up to 100% of the tax undercharged; (ii) false customs declarations result in forfeiture of goods and fines up to three times the duty evaded; (iii) failure to satisfy the Genuine Link economic-substance return can trigger residence recharacterisation, information exchange with the Cayman Islands Tax Information Authority, and retrospective assessment at 10% on worldwide income; (iv) UK HMRC may assess and penalise undeclared UK VAT registration (up to 100% of the VAT due plus daily penalties) if the GBP 85,000 threshold is crossed; (v) USDC or other cryptocurrency settlement does not alter the tax position and must be supported by invoices and blockchain records evidencing consideration and customer identity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account. No exchange-control statute restricts capital injections, profit repatriation, dividend distributions, or cross-border service-fee receipts in Gibraltar. The Cayman exempted company may remit profits to shareholders through Gibraltar correspondent banking without prior regulatory approval, subject only to bank-level source-of-funds documentary verification. No Gibraltar withholding tax applies to outbound dividends or interest, easing the profit-remittance path; the tax-exempt Cayman status of the entity itself does not obstruct conversion but elevates the documentary burden at bank onboarding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Gibraltar maintains a zero-surrender regime: no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate foreign-currency export or service-export proceeds. USD and EUR receipts may be retained in original currency, converted to GIP/GBP at market rate, or routed onward for trade settlement at the company's discretion without central-bank registration.

Central bank supervision: Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Currency) issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP), pegged at 1:1 to GBP; prudential regulation and AML/CTF supervision are executed by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC). The Bank of England constitutes the de facto monetary anchor through the GIP/GBP parity, and HM Treasury retains ultimate sovereign authority over the British Overseas Territory.

Equity injections from members must be routed via SWIFT wire from the shareholder's home jurisdiction into the Gibraltar operational or escrow account denominated in GBP, EUR, or USD. Viable alternatives: (1) a Gibraltar-licensed EMI route conditioned on enhanced due diligence, a registered Gibraltar agent, and a full source-of-funds pack; (2) USDC stablecoin treasury rails executed through regulated custodians (Coinbase Prime or Circle account) for Stripe-paired merchant settlements and supplier payouts. No Gibraltar statute prohibits EMI onboarding of a Cayman vehicle, but each EMI's risk appetite is proprietary and a legal opinion plus verifiable operational substance is effectively mandatory.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GBP 1,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions requiring a Gibraltar Local File; Master File required when group consolidated global turnover exceeds GBP 50,000,000; Cayman Economic Substance Return required for every relevant activity irrespective of thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Cayman ESR applies to the API distribution and IP holding functions; a Cayman tax-resident company must maintain Cayman office, full-time staff, and local operating expenditure, or, if claiming Gibraltar tax residence, file the Cayman economic substance return with a Gibraltar TRC and demonstrate board meetings, staff, and decision-making in Gibraltar. Gibraltar applies economic substance/substance-over-form to the small-profits rate and patent box; artificial arrangements with no real revenue-producing activity will be disregarded.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 100% of the underpaid tax, plus interest at 8% per annum; criminal conviction carries an unlimited fine and imprisonment for up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who know of or cause the false return are jointly and severally liable for penalties; courts may pierce the corporate veil, impose travel bans, freeze assets, and issue extradition requests through the UK-Gibraltar criminal assistance framework.
Transfer Pricing Mismatch

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar transfer-pricing adjustment increases taxable profit to arm's-length; additional 12.5% CIT, compound interest at 8%, and tax-geared penalties up to 100% of the adjustment; secondary adjustment may deem a dividend or loan to the Cayman parent.

Director personal liability: Directors approving undocumented or unrealistically priced intercompany transactions are personally liable for penalties and disgorgement; in egregious cases, the court lifts the corporate veil and applies director disqualification orders and travel restrictions.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Cayman ESR fines: initial CI$12,000, then CI$12,000 per month, up to CI$120,000; on indictment, fine up to CI$120,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years for false declarations; Gibraltar may impute tax residency and levy 12.5% CIT on all worldwide profits.

Director personal liability: Directors signing false economic substance returns are personally liable; Cayman courts may impose travel bans, asset freezes, and director disqualification; UK authorities can use the 'offshore entity' provisions to recover taxes from controlling persons.
AML / Unlicensed Payment Handling

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission penalties up to GBP 1,000,000 per breach or 5% of annual turnover; criminal fines for sanctions/anti-terrorism lapses are unlimited with imprisonment up to 7 years; Stripe/merchant accounts may be suspended and funds confiscated.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and directors face personal criminal liability for failures to submit SARs and for AML programme deficiencies; travel bans, confiscation orders, and extradition to UK courts can be imposed.
