Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar, Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax); official portal: www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax; customs portal: www.gibraltar.gov.gi/customs

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (including a Cayman Exempted Company) conducting business or maintaining a permanent establishment in Gibraltar must apply to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (GRN/TIN). The application requires a duly completed Form C+I (Company Information), certified constitutional documents from the Cayman Registry (Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles, Certificate of Good Standing), a Gibraltar registered business address, and particulars of the local authorised representative. If the foreign entity is not tax resident in Gibraltar and does not have a permanent establishment, no Gibraltar TIN is required; however, an EORI number must be obtained from Gibraltar Customs for import/export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope - a non-resident Cayman Exempted Company with a Gibraltar permanent establishment is taxed only on Gibraltar-source profits attributable to that PE. A company that is managed and controlled in Gibraltar becomes tax resident and is taxed on worldwide profits. The Cayman entity itself retains its tax-exempt status under Cayman Islands law but is subject to Gibraltar territorial taxation on local-source income.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' election. However, a foreign company may formally elect to be treated as a Gibraltar tax resident company by establishing management and control in Gibraltar. Conversely, a Gibraltar-incorporated company may elect non-resident status if it has no local management and control, subject to the Income Tax Act transitional rules. The former 'Exempt Company' regime has been abolished; no new exempt-status elections are available. Hybrid mismatch and intra-group financing elections are governed by the Intra-Group Finance (Tax) Act, preventing double-dip deduction arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST; Gibraltar imposes selective Purchase Tax on specific goods (e.g. alcohol, tobacco, fuel) and customs/excise duties on imports. There is no value added tax system.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable (no VAT/GST registration regime or turnover threshold). All importers and exporters of goods must obtain a Gibraltar Customs EORI registration regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and imposes no VAT, therefore no export zero-rating or VAT refund regime exists. Exports from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation. For customs purposes, exported pet food products may be entitled to relief from any local customs duties where the goods are in free circulation or for re-export; no VAT claim or refund can be filed.

  • Customs Export Declaration (EX1/SAD)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing payment receipt
  • Veterinary/Health Certificate issued by a recognised Gibraltar veterinary authority for animal consumption
  • Certificate of Origin
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List

Gibraltar does not operate a continuous transaction control (CTC) system, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital tax stamping. Electronic customs declarations are filed via the UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS) under the UK-Gibraltar customs arrangement. There is no government-linked e-invoicing clearance mandate.

In the absence of VAT, export zero-rating clawback is not applicable. However, customs fraud penalties apply under the Customs (Import and Export) Regulations 2020, including fines up to £10,000 per offence or 150% of the dutiable value, forfeiture of goods, and criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 5 years. Misdeclaration of export destination, false health certificates, or failure to repatriate export proceeds within the prescribed 180-day period may trigger investigation by the Gibraltar Customs and Revenue Department. Cross-border cooperation with UK authorities may extend penalties under UK VAT or customs legislation if the goods enter the UK market.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both capital and current account. GIP is a sterling proxy, pegged at par to GBP and freely convertible at commercial banks without Bank of England authorisation. Profit remittance from the Gibraltar operating subsidiary to the Cayman Exempted Company shareholder is freely permitted subject to three ordinary conditions: (i) the Gibraltar entity has filed its annual corporate tax return and economic substance declaration with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office; (ii) the distribution satisfies the solvency-test rules of the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 (positive net worth and a directors' solvency statement); and (iii) the paying bank completes standard source-of-funds due diligence on the Cayman shareholder. There are no withholding taxes on dividends in Gibraltar, no capital controls, no advance approvals, and no documentary exchange licences. The practical bottleneck is banking-layer KYC friction imposed on the Cayman nexus, not any statutory FX law constraint.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline exists in Gibraltar or the UK. Exporters may retain 100% of proceeds in any convertible currency (USD/EUR/GBP/CHF) for an unlimited period. Commercial banks nevertheless apply their own trade-compliance screen: inbound export collections must be matched against the underlying sales contract, invoice, packing list, health certificate, and transport document, and each receipt is tagged under ISO 20022 purpose code 'TRAD' on ISO-migrated rails. Best practice is to hold export proceeds in segregated USD/EUR accounts at the Gibraltar bank and remit to suppliers on a back-to-back basis, which yields the cleanest audit trail and avoids triggered enhanced due diligence.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (central bank for the United Kingdom and monetary backstop for the Gibraltar pound); Government of Gibraltar (Currency Commission) (issuer of the Gibraltar pound, GIP, under a statutory 1:1 currency-board peg with sterling); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) (prudential regulator and licensing authority for all Gibraltar-incorporated banks, EMIs, and payment institutions). HM Treasury exercises no exchange-control power because the UK abolished exchange controls in 1979; neither the UK nor Gibraltar operates a foreign-exchange administration, surrender, or central-approval regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Aggregate feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH, with a structural caveat. GFSC-licensed Gibraltar EMIs and payment institutions (regulated under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2020) can onboard the Cayman-owned Gibraltar company normally because the GFSC accepts Cayman Companies Registry and Cayman Beneficial Ownership Register evidence as compliant customer due diligence. By contrast, leading UK FCA-regulated and EU EEA digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, Nium, Intergiro) frequently decline or hard-restrict accounts where the ultimate beneficial owner is a Cayman tax-exempt company because their automated risk engines flag the 'Tax_Exempt_Zone' classification and the offshore parent jurisdiction. Recommended overlay: (1) primary clearing at Gibraltar International Bank and Trusted Novus Bank; (2) supplier-payout and marketplace-collection overlay via Airwallex or Nium once the Gibraltar entity has accumulated 12 months of audited accounts, a CRS registration, and a physical payroll record; (3) Platform_Pay settlements routed direct to the Gibraltar bank feed. Legal constraints: EMIs may not take deposits or extend credit; client funds must be safeguarded in segregated accounts at prudentially regulated credit institutions; and any onward transfer to the Cayman shareholder will be profile-reviewed under sanctions and anti-bribery/anti-corruption policy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the Cayman Exempted Company maintains its treasury at a Cayman-licensed bank (e.g. Butterfield Bank (Cayman), Fidelity Bank (Cayman), or NCB (Cayman)); the injection is wired via SWIFT MT103 with gpi tracking to the Gibraltar operating account at Gibraltar International Bank or Trusted Novus Bank, settled in GBP (the GIP clearing peg renders GBP receipt functionally identical to GIP). No government-mandated purpose code exists in Gibraltar, but the paying bank must carry an ISO 20022 purpose category 'EQUI' (equity earnings or capital contribution) and free-text remittance referencing the Cayman board resolution. Inbound tranches above GBP 25,000 are quarantined pending completion of the enhanced due diligence file (source of funds, source of wealth, and deployment plan). Recommended engineering: route both the Cayman and Gibraltar accounts through a shared US or UK correspondent corridor (e.g. Bank of America Merrill Lynch or Lloyds) to collapse intermediary fees and accelerate clearing from T+2 to T+0; deploy initial capitalisation in two tranches (GBP 50,000 escrow, then GBP 100,000 upon evidence of premises and licence) to retain negotiating leverage during onboarding.Three-tier disbursement architecture: (1) SWIFT MT103 with gpi end-to-end tracking for routine supplier payments below GBP 250,000, particularly EU raw-meat vendors; each instruction must reference the matching invoice, packing list, and health certificate to survive the bank's trade-finance screen, and the beneficiary must be pre-validated and UBO-consistent; (2) irrevocable, confirmed Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) issued via Banco de Sabadell (Gibraltar) for large-block raw-meat purchases from third-country exporters (e.g. South America, Oceania), payable at sight against presentation of bills of lading, veterinary certificates, and insurance documents; (3) instant rails for domestic and regional settlements: GBP via Faster Payments (under GBP 1 million) or CHAPS (urgent, above GBP 1 million), EUR via TARGET2 correspondents (Sabadell or Trusted Novus), and Platform_Pay API-driven settlement from the marketplace with T+0/T+1 sweep into the Gibraltar operating account. Controls: each outward instruction requires a referenced purchase order and approved invoice; cash withdrawals are prohibited; a 10% merchant reserve is maintained on Platform_Pay; quarterly reconciliation is submitted to the board.Outright deliverable FX forwards (GBP/USD, GBP/EUR, GBP/CHF) with tenors up to 12 months under ISDA or bilateral FX master agreements with UK/EU counterparties (Lloyds, HSBC, NatWest, Banco de Sabadell); no GFSC licence is required for treasury self-hedging.Vanilla FX options and zero-cost collars (purchase of puts and calls) to protect budget exchange rates on USD-denominated raw-meat purchase contracts.Cross-currency swaps (USD on the Cayman side against GBP/EUR on the Gibraltar side) to manage the intercompany shareholder loan portfolio between the Cayman parent and the Gibraltar operating subsidiary with documented arm's-length terms.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in USD and GBP referencing emerging-market currencies (LATAM and APAC feed-commodity supplier currencies) where convertibility obstacles prevent deliverable settlement; NDFs are lawful in Gibraltar and must be booked under an executed ISDA with credit-support annex.Natural hedge overlay via multi-currency omnibus accounts at Trusted Novus Bank, pairing EUR/USD payables with USD/EUR receivables to minimise open exposure; a board-approved hedging policy caps coverage at 80% of forecast exposure with a maximum 12-month horizon.
