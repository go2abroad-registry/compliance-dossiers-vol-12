Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the Cayman Exempted Company is exempt from Cayman Islands direct taxation (s.6 Companies Act (Cayman)); for Gibraltar purposes it is an entity-level taxed corporation under the territorial-source regime: Gibraltar-source profits are taxable at 12.5%, while income from services performed wholly outside Gibraltar is outside the charge. Pass_Through_Eligible does not apply; the entity is a non-transparent juridical person.

Available election forms

(i) Non-resident election: file an election/notice with CITD to be treated as tax non-resident by evidencing central management and control outside Gibraltar. (ii) Territorial-source exclusion: elect on the CIT-1 to exclude foreign-source service income and maintain contemporaneous documentary proof of offshore performance. (iii) Group relief election: available under the Income Tax Act 2010 for loss surrender should a Gibraltar-resident subsidiary later be added. (iv) No Gibraltar 'check-the-box' or hybrid-mismatch election exists; the entity is treated as a corporation for Gibraltar and UK/HMRC purposes, and the ultimate parent may separately consider a US 7701 election where US-taxable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST on services in Gibraltar; Import Duty regime applies only to physical goods entering Gibraltar; UK VAT does not extend to Gibraltar

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A — no indirect-tax registration threshold exists for services in Gibraltar. Importers of goods must hold a CITD duty deferment/import account from the first consignment and pay Import Duty under the Gibraltar tariff (0%–22.5% depending on tariff line); services are outside the scope of any registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

No VAT zero-rating applies because Gibraltar levies no VAT/GST on services; the income-tax analogue is the territorial-source exemption. Foreign fee income is excluded from Gibraltar corporation tax where: (i) services are performed and delivered outside Gibraltar; (ii) the client is outside Gibraltar; (iii) invoicing and fee receipt occur through the Cayman entity without a Gibraltar PE; and (iv) central management and control remains in the Cayman Islands. Invoices should state 'no VAT/GST — Gibraltar has no indirect tax on services'; no input refund mechanism exists for services.

  • Signed virtual CFO service agreement identifying the foreign client and offshore delivery model
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Cayman entity marked outside the scope of Gibraltar taxation (no VAT/GST)
  • Bank documentation (SWIFT credit advice, Wise or Stripe settlement statements) evidencing inward remittance of fees

No e-invoicing, digital stamping, real-time fiscal clearance, or API clearance mandate in Gibraltar. B2B cross-border CFO invoices may be issued in standard PDF/paper format and must be retained for 7 years. For imported goods only, an electronic customs import entry (Single Administrative Document) is lodged with CITD; services fall entirely outside the customs clearance chain.

CITD may raise assessments within 4 years of the chargeable period, extended to 20 years where fraud or negligent conduct is established. Penalties: up to 100% of tax undercharged for deliberate evasion, up to 75% for negligent inaccuracy, and fixed penalties up to £300 plus continuing daily penalties for non-filing or missing records (7-year retention). The Gibraltar general anti-abuse rule can cancel any artificial offshore-exemption advantage and impose substantial penalty charges; criminal tax evasion carries fines and/or imprisonment up to 5 years. Cross-border exchange of information (UK–Gibraltar agreement and OECD CRS) enables HMRC and Gibraltar to challenge the substance of outsourced CFO structures and to claw back abused exemptions with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is freely convertible into United Kingdom sterling at par and is accepted interchangeably within Gibraltar. Gibraltar maintains fully open current-account convertibility and a de facto open capital account for corporate treasury operations; there are no exchange-control approvals, profit-remittance licences or dividend-blocking restrictions imposed on the GIP/GBP, EUR, or USD clearing channels. A Cayman Exempted Company holding funds in Gibraltar accounts may remit profits to its shareholders by board-approved dividend or capital distribution without Gibraltar withholding tax or FX approval, provided the underlying funds are demonstrably derived from bona fide service receipts and the client/UBO/KYC documentation is in order. Sanctions screening and anti-money-laundering controls remain the sole practical friction points; FATCA, CRS and economic substance disclosure may be required rather than exchange-control permission.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no export-proceeds surrender requirement, no statutory conversion ratio, no minimum holding period, and no mandatory repatriation deadline for service-company receipts. Invoices raised by the Virtual CFO consultancy may be maintained in any combination of GIP/GBP, EUR and USD. The bank may require transaction-level commercial documentation and a rationale for retaining foreign currency, but no regulatory order compels conversion into sterling or a Gibraltar-dollar equivalent.

Central bank supervision: Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar acting through the Currency Notes Commission (the note-issuing and Gibraltar pound monetary authority), with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (FSC) as the prudential banking and foreign-exchange licensing regulator. There is no separate independent central bank in Gibraltar; monetary policy is anchored by the Bank of England through the Gibraltar pound's 1:1 parity with sterling.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for Gibraltar-licensed electronic-money institutions and UK-FSCA-authorised payment institutions; LOW to MEDIUM for offshore digital EMIs that refuse Cayman-situs entities or require a European establishment. Wise Business can be used as a disbursement/settlement aggregator where the Cayman Exempted Company is able to satisfy proof-of-business and source-of-wealth checks, but Wise is a payment institution and not a bank; its safeguarding model does not carry deposit-protection insurance. Stripe Billing is viable as the client-invoicing and card-acceptance gateway, but settlement must route onward to the Gibraltar operational bank account. Legal constraints: EMIs may not provide credit, overdrafts, investment products or true escrow protection; they are subject to client-fund safeguarding and e-money segregation rules enforced by the Gibraltar FSC or FCA. Funds held with an EMI are not deposits and are exposed to insolvency risk of the institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for shareholder capital or subordinated loan funding into the Cayman Exempted Company's Gibraltar treasury account: (i) execute SWIFT MT103/MT202 transfers from the shareholder's bank with a cleared remitter reference; (ii) use the corporate account IBAN/BIC and exact beneficiary name matching the Cayman certificate; (iii) include a purpose reference in field 70 such as 'Equity subscription pursuant to Cayman board resolution' or 'Shareholder loan under loan agreement dated [date]'; (iv) upload to the receiving bank a board resolution or loan agreement, audited or notarised certificate of incumbency, and source-of-funds evidence; (v) for GBP use CHAPS or Faster Payments, for EUR use SEPA CT, for USD use Fedwire via a UK/US correspondent; (vi) ensure the sending bank is not in a sanctioned/high-risk country unless a valid OFAC/FSC licence is presented. The receiving Gibraltar bank will verify the funds against expected turnover, UBO profile and the declared purpose before posting; any inbound capital used for client-fee settlement must be separated on ledger to avoid commingling.Trade settlements for Virtual CFO services are executed on open account rather than documentary L/C because the underlying supply is advisory and cloud-based. Preferred rails: SWIFT MT103 for cross-border EUR/USD, SEPA Instant or SEPA Credit Transfer for periodic European subcontractor payments, Faster Payments/CHAPS for sterling obligations, and Fedwire for US-denominated disbursements. Commercial letters of credit are relevant only where the CFO contract bundles hardware, software licences or physical goods procurement; in those cases an irrevocable, confirmed LC governed by ICC UCP600 may be arranged through Barclays or Bank of China, with MT700 issuance and MT710/MT720 negotiation. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH for Gibraltar-licensed electronic-money institutions and UK-FSCA-authorised payment institutions; LOW to MEDIUM for offshore digital EMIs that refuse Cayman-situs entities or require a European establishment. Wise Business can be used as a disbursement/settlement aggregator where the Cayman Exempted Company is able to satisfy proof-of-business and source-of-wealth checks, but Wise is a payment institution and not a bank; its safeguarding model does not carry deposit-protection insurance. Stripe Billing is viable as the client-invoicing and card-acceptance gateway, but settlement must route onward to the Gibraltar operational bank account. Legal constraints: EMIs may not provide credit, overdrafts, investment products or true escrow protection; they are subject to client-fund safeguarding and e-money segregation rules enforced by the Gibraltar FSC or FCA. Funds held with an EMI are not deposits and are exposed to insolvency risk of the institution.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% standard Gibraltar corporation tax (Income Tax Act, single flat rate applicable to all corporate sectors from year of assessment 2020-21). The Cayman Exempted Company itself bears 0% Cayman corporate income tax; its Gibraltar branch/residence triggers the 12.5% charge on Gibraltar-source profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — neither the Cayman Islands nor Gibraltar imposes dividend withholding tax on distributions by the Exempted Company or its Gibraltar branch/PE; dividends may be declared unconditionally subject only to solvency and AML verification.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: any single controlled transaction with an associated enterprise exceeding GIP 100,000, or aggregate related-party gross revenue/cost exceeding GIP 500,000 per accounting period. Master File and Country-by-Country Report: groups with consolidated revenue ≥ EUR 750 million (OECD-aligned threshold).

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar ESR: applies to relevant activities (banking, insurance, fund management, financing/leasing, headquarters, shipping, IP, distribution/service centres, holding entities). Outsourced virtual CFO services to third parties are not a statutorily listed relevant activity in Gibraltar or Cayman; however, if the Cayman exempted company renders services to related parties, it constitutes a 'distribution and service centre' and must file the Cayman ESR return, prove 'directed and managed' in Cayman (or, where the activity is performed in Gibraltar, show the GT jurisdiction actually exercises supervision), and maintain adequate physical substance, skilled employees, and premises proportionate to the service income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 100% of tax underpaid, daily interest at 8% p.a., seizure of records, criminal conviction with fines of up to GIP 100,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 5 years; the Commissioner may assess on a best-of-judgment basis and enforce by warrant.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who 'knowingly concerned' in the fraud are jointly and severally liable for the tax, penalties and costs; courts may issue travel bans, disqualification orders under the Companies Act, and custodial sentences.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Cayman ESR penalties up to CI$ 10,000 plus CI$ 5,000 per month of default; enforced deregistration from the Cayman Register; automatic re-taxation in Gibraltar with penalties for late registration of a PE.

Director personal liability: Officers are personally liable for ESR filing obligations; the Cayman court may strike off the company, freezing bank accounts (Stripe/Wise) and restraining directors from acting; persistent default can lead to personal fines and imprisonment for contempt.
Permanent Establishment / UK CFC Attack

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the arrangement: UK PE assessment on profits attributable to UK activity, plus interest and penalties up to 100% of tax; UK CFC charge under TIOPA 2010 Part 9A if a UK resident holds the Cayman entity; Gibraltar 12.5% assessment on the Gibraltar PE.

Director personal liability: UK HMRC may issue personal liability notices and disqualification orders; directors can be pursued for penalties where they caused the PE registration failure; travel restrictions may be imposed to secure unpaid liabilities.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar: fines up to GIP 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years for contravention of the Beneficial Ownership regulations; Cayman: fines up to CI$ 100,000 and 5 years' imprisonment for false beneficial ownership declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for each day of default; nominee directors risk criminal prosecution, civil restitution of undeclared proceeds, asset freezes, and travel bans pending investigation.
