Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A Gibraltar company must maintain a registered office in Gibraltar and maintain statutory records at the location required by the Companies Act 2014 and applicable Registry rules. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on the Cayman Exempted Company (Companies Act (Cayman), s. 44), nor for the Gibraltar external-company registration; share capital may be nominal (e.g., US$1) and denominated in any currency. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope / Tax_Exempt_Zone dual classification: (a) Cayman layer - the Cayman Exempted Company carries a statutory tax undertaking (Cayman Companies Law s.6) and is exempt from income, corporation, capital gains and withholding taxes; (b) Gibraltar layer - the company is a non-resident foreign company chargeable to Gibraltar corporation tax at 12.5% (Income Tax Act 2010, as amended) only on profits derived from Gibraltar-source trading or attributable to its Gibraltar permanent establishment (the tea-salon branch). Pass-through is not available; entity-level taxation applies to the Gibraltarian branch.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. Statutory elections available include: (i) direct-assessment election treating the branch as a permanent establishment taxed on arm's-length attributable profits; (ii) capital-allowance pooling election for tea-lounge furniture, ceramic stock, plant and fixtures; (iii) group-relief/loss-surrender election where the Gibraltarian branch sits in a 75% UK/Gibraltar group; (iv) rollover/business-asset disposal elections under the Income Tax Act 2010 where eligible; (v) UK-side hybrid-mismatch analysis (Finance Act 2016 Part 6A) applies only where a UK/foreign structure produces a deduction-without-inclusion outcome - the combination of a Cayman exempted parent and a Gibraltarian 12.5% chargeable branch does not ordinarily trigger such an election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar: no VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax (Gibraltar sits outside the UK VAT area and the EU VAT area). UK Value Added Tax (VATA 1994), standard rate 20%, is relevant only if the same business acquires a UK establishment, imports goods from Great Britain, or makes distance sales to UK consumers; it does not attach to the Gibraltarian retail tea service.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: not applicable - no indirect tax regime exists. United Kingdom (where a UK VAT nexus arises): GBP 90,000 annual taxable turnover threshold under VATA 1994, Schedule 1, paragraph 1 (2024/25 rate; Gibraltar pound at parity with GBP).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% UK VAT under s.30 VATA 1994 applies only to exports of goods from Great Britain to destinations outside the UK (e.g., loose-leaf tea and ceramic wares exported from a GB warehouse to EU/offshore customers), provided that (i) the supplier holds valid evidence of removal from the UK, (ii) the goods leave the UK within the statutory dispatch window, (iii) the commercial invoice states export terms, and (iv) the counterparty is not a UK VAT-identical person. The Gibraltarian tea-salon's local food-and-beverage retail service is outside UK VAT altogether, so no zero-rating claim arises. Services: tea-salon or tea-culture consultancy supplied to overseas business customers is generally outside the scope of UK VAT under B2B place-of-supply rules (recipient's jurisdiction). Gibraltar applies no VAT, so no Gibraltarian refund mechanism exists; UK VAT refunds for an exporting UK VAT-registered trader are recovered through the UK VAT return.

  • Commercial invoice and packing list stating quantity, value, Incoterms and export destination
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing actual removal of goods from the UK

Gibraltar: no real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction controls, no e-invoicing scheme and no digital stamping; statutory books, invoices and records must be retained for at least six years and income-tax returns are filed through the Income Tax Office e-filing portal. United Kingdom (if the entity becomes UK VAT-registered): Making Tax Digital requires functional-compatible software and HMRC-approved API-based submission of quarterly VAT returns; there is no real-time pre-clearance, e-invoicing mandate or digital stamping.

United Kingdom: HMRC may verify the export evidence dossier for six years; insufficient or fictitious evidence triggers assessments under VATA 1994 s.73, statutory interest under s.74, penalties under FA 2008 Schedule 24 (up to 100% for deliberate/dishonest conduct), possible criminal proceedings, and retrospective clawback of refunds with a denial of future zero-rating. Gibraltar: false branch returns, understated PE profits or overstated losses invite best-of-judgment assessments, penalties up to double the omitted tax, daily default fines and interest under the Income Tax Act 2010; fraudulent customs declarations on tea imports attract seizure/confiscation and fines under the Customs Ordinance. Cayman Islands: failure of the exempted company to comply with Economic Substance notification/record obligations may expose the entity to administrative penalties notwithstanding its tax-exempt status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at par 1:1 to GBP, is 100% sterling-backed, and is fully convertible through Gibraltar commercial banks; GBP is also legal tender alongside GIP. There are no exchange-control approvals for current or capital account transactions in Gibraltar, the UK, or the Cayman Islands (the UK Exchange Control Act 1947 was repealed in 1979; Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands never re-imposed such controls). Profit remittance hurdles are nil: Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividends or interest paid to non-residents and no capital duty on share issuance, so the Cayman Exempted Company may freely repatriate operating profits after settling Gibraltar corporation tax on any Gibraltar-source trading profits and after filing the annual Cayman Economic Substance return (Form ES) with CIMA.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Gibraltar, the UK, and the Cayman Islands impose no mandatory surrender timelines, conversion ratios, or forced repatriation of export or service proceeds. The specialty tea salon's revenue is predominantly domestic GIP/GBP retail receipts; any incidental cross-border e-commerce tea sales may be retained in the original currency (GBP/EUR/USD) without conversion. Only routine AML/CTF transaction monitoring and statistical balance-of-payments purpose-coding obligations apply (e.g., ISO 20022 purpose code EQUI, CAPT, or TRADE).

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no independent central bank. Banking supervision is exercised by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019 (Gibraltar) and the Banking Act 2012 (Gibraltar). Currency issuance rests with HM Government of Gibraltar (Treasury) under the Currency Notes Act 1934, with statutory 100% sterling backing held at the Bank of England. The Bank of England does not directly supervise Gibraltar-licensed banks. For the Cayman Exempted Company, the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) supervises the registered office and registered agent, but CIMA approval is not required for ordinary capital injections or dividend flows into Gibraltar.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 250,000 per related-party transaction category (goods, services, intangibles, financing) triggers the mandatory preparation of contemporaneous Local File documentation; Master File documentation is required where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds GIP 750,000,000, in alignment with OECD/EU documentation standards.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations 2019 require any entity tax resident in Gibraltar that carries on a 'relevant activity' (banking, insurance, ship management, fund management, finance and leasing, headquarters, holding company, distribution and service centre, or intellectual property business) to demonstrate adequate premises, employees, expenditure, and core income-generating activities in Gibraltar. A directly operating specialty tea salon is a local consumer-facing trade ordinarily outside the schedule of relevant activities; however, the Cayman Exempted Company must also satisfy the Cayman Islands Economic Substance Rules for any holding, distribution, or service-centre functions it purports to perform, and any artificial shifting of Gibraltar operating profits to the Cayman tax-exempt zone without real substance in either jurisdiction will be challenged by the GRA and by the Cayman competent authority.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and failure to file or register

Statutory sanction: The GRA may issue default assessments at 12.5% on estimated chargeable profits plus interest, impose late-filing penalties of up to GIP 1,000 and daily penalties of GIP 100 per day, levy tax-geared penalties of up to 100% of the understated tax for careless or deliberate conduct, and pursue criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion of Income Tax with fines at the discretion of the court and imprisonment for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors, shadow directors, and the de facto manager of the Gibraltar branch may be personally assessed for the company's unpaid tax where failure is attributable to their fraud or neglect; the corporate veil will be pierced where the Cayman Exempted Company is used as a facade, exposing directors to civil restitution, asset freezing orders, disqualification for up to 15 years, travel bans, and extradition proceedings from the United Kingdom.
Transfer pricing and artificial profit shifting

Statutory sanction: The GRA may recharacterize intercompany transactions, adjust consideration to arm's length, disallow deductions for absent or inadequate documentation, impose secondary adjustments, charge interest, and apply penalties of up to 100% of the deficiency; general anti-abuse powers and controlled-foreign-company principles may reattribute the shifted profits to Gibraltar with immediate assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false transfer pricing returns or direct the artificial pricing arrangements can be joined to civil penalty proceedings, held personally liable for the additional tax, prosecuted for tax evasion where the documentation is knowingly false, and subjected to disqualification orders; professional advisers and nominee directors of the Cayman entity face regulatory sanctions for facilitating the scheme.
Shell company misuse and economic substance failure

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar may treat the Cayman company as tax resident on the basis of management and control, assess all Gibraltar-source and worldwide profits at 12.5%, disregard the Cayman structure under the general anti-abuse rule, and impose penalties and interest; in parallel, the Cayman Islands Tax Information Authority may impose administrative fines under the Economic Substance Rules and apply to strike the exempted company off the register.

Director personal liability: Nominee shareholders and corporate directors of the Cayman vehicle are exposed to regulatory penalties and disqualification for failure to discharge substance obligations; once the structure is characterized as a sham, the true beneficial owner is treated as the principal trader, with the corporate veil pierced to impose personal tax liability, civil restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment for deliberate evasion.
AML, counter-terrorist financing, and statutory licensing breaches

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to GIP 3,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover for serious and systematic AML compliance failures under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; revocation of the business and premises licences; confiscation orders against assets derived from undeclared trading receipts; and prosecution for failure to report suspicion, carrying custodial sentences and unlimited fines.

Director personal liability: The money laundering reporting officer and each director who consented to or permitted the laundering of undeclared tea salon proceeds are criminally liable and may be imprisoned for up to fourteen years; senior managers face personal financial penalties, disqualification, and civil recovery proceedings, and the Cayman parent's directors can be reached through mutual legal assistance and international freezing orders.
