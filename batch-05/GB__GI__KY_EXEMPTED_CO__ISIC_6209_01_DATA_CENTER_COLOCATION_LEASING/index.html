Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), e-filing via the Gibraltar Government e-services portal (gibraltar.gov.gi); indirect and customs administration: HM Customs Gibraltar, using the Gibraltar Customs electronic single-window

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Cayman Exempted Company carrying on data center activities in Gibraltar must: (i) register the branch/overseas company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies if the activities constitute a trade or permanent establishment; (ii) file an entity tax registration application with the Income Tax Office to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN), attaching certified constitutional documents (MEMART), Cayman certificate of incorporation and certificate of good standing, registers of directors and members, appointment of a local registered office/agent, and a written statement of the proposed Gibraltar-source activities; (iii) register as an employer for PAYE and social insurance if local staff are engaged; and (iv) obtain an importer code from HM Customs Gibraltar before importing servers, racks, cabling, or cooling infrastructure into the Gibraltar customs territory

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A non-resident Cayman Exempted Company carrying on data center activities in Gibraltar must: (i) register the branch/overseas company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies if the activities constitute a trade or permanent establishment; (ii) file an entity tax registration application with the Income Tax Office to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN), attaching certified constitutional documents (MEMART), Cayman certificate of incorporation and certificate of good standing, registers of directors and members, appointment of a local registered office/agent, and a written statement of the proposed Gibraltar-source activities; (iii) register as an employer for PAYE and social insurance if local staff are engaged; and (iv) obtain an importer code from HM Customs Gibraltar before importing servers, racks, cabling, or cooling infrastructure into the Gibraltar customs territory

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory elections and mechanisms include: (a) group relief elections to surrender losses within a Gibraltar tax group under the Income Tax Act 2010; (b) capital allowance pooling and balancing-charge elections for plant and machinery deployed in the data center; (c) advance tax ruling/fiscal clearance applications to confirm territorial-source treatment, non-residence, and permanent establishment scope for the Cayman entity; (d) elections and notifications under the GloBE/Pillar Two top-up tax framework for multinational groups above the relevant consolidated revenue threshold; and (e) anti-hybrid and controlled-foreign-company elections applicable to the Cayman intermediate holding structure

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Import Duty and Excise Duty (no VAT/GST; Gibraltar is excluded from the UK/EU VAT system and does not apply VAT to B2B services)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – no VAT/GST registration threshold exists because Gibraltar levies no VAT. Import duty registration is compulsory for every importer/consignee of goods into Gibraltar irrespective of turnover or value. Data center servers, racks, cabling, and cooling equipment generally qualify for a 0% commercial import duty rate, creating no measurable indirect tax burden on the colocation operation

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not a VAT zero-rating claim because Gibraltar imposes no VAT. The operative reliefs are: (1) goods physically re-exported from Gibraltar are outside the scope of import duty, supported by an electronic customs export declaration and proof of physical departure; and (2) cross-border colocation/cloud services supplied to non-resident clients are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation and, under the territorial source rules of the Income Tax Act 2010, may be excluded from the Cayman entity's chargeable profits where no permanent establishment exists or the profits are not attributable to a Gibraltar-source trade. Eligibility is evidenced by the non-resident recipient test, offshore contract execution, and payment received from abroad; inward remittance evidence within the 180-day benchmark completes the substantiation file

  • Electronically lodged Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (electronic Single Administrative Document with entry and movement reference number)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of re-exported equipment/infrastructure from Gibraltar customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of sales proceeds from the non-resident client within the 180-day repatriation benchmark

No domestic real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal stamping, or digital clearance mandate applies to B2B data center service invoices. Import consignments must be cleared electronically via HM Customs Gibraltar's approved e-customs interface using an electronic Single Administrative Document with digital risk assessment and customs release stamping. The Cayman entity should retain standard commercial invoices, contractual tax references, and customs entry records for audit substantiation

HM Customs Gibraltar may assess unpaid import duty with interest, impose penalties of up to three times the duty evaded, forfeit or seize goods, and pursue criminal prosecution for fraudulent export misdeclaration. The Income Tax Office may reopen assessments within six years (indefinitely for fraud or neglect), withdraw territorial-exemption treatment where documentation is incomplete or inconsistent, and apply tax-geared penalties and interest under the Income Tax Act 2010. Untraceable FX repatriation or mismatched export records will trigger retrospective clawback, penalty assessments, and potential referral for financial compliance review

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar has substantially unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility. Sterling is freely transferable, and there is generally no local exchange-control approval requirement for receiving foreign currency, converting operating receipts, making intercompany payments, repatriating profits, or returning capital. Banks may impose transaction limits, enhanced due-diligence requirements, sanctions screening, source-of-funds checks, beneficial-owner verification, and documentary requirements. A Cayman exempted company must separately assess whether its management, banking activities, income, or personnel create Cayman, Gibraltar, United Kingdom, or other jurisdictional tax residence, permanent-establishment, economic-substance, transfer-pricing, or withholding-tax exposure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or prescribed repatriation deadline for export receipts. The relevant business is principally a cross-border digital infrastructure and hosting-services business rather than a goods exporter. Service receipts may be retained in foreign currency or converted at the company's discretion, subject to bank policies, contractual settlement terms, accurate books and records, tax reporting, sanctions controls, and evidence supporting the commercial purpose of material receipts.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no separate central bank or general foreign-exchange administration. Gibraltar uses sterling under the monetary framework of the Bank of England. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission regulates Gibraltar financial services firms, while the Government of Gibraltar administers applicable local monetary and exchange-control legislation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for payment collection and treasury execution, but not a substitute for a fully serviced domestic bank account. A foreign electronic-money institution may provide services only within the scope of its home-state authorization, applicable cross-border permissions, contractual reach, and local regulatory perimeter. Gibraltar is outside the European Union and European Economic Area after Brexit; EU passporting cannot be assumed. An offshore EMI without applicable authorization or a lawful cross-border basis should not market regulated payment services directly in Gibraltar. EMI balances are generally safeguarded funds and are not ordinary bank deposits. Reliance on a fintech should be limited by counterparty, jurisdiction, safeguarding, sanctions, account-closure, operational-resilience, and funds-access risk.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GBP 5,000,000 in aggregate intercompany transactions; below this threshold, a simple TP policy and supporting records must be kept.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar ESR applies if the company is tax resident or has an activity in Gibraltar; Cayman ESR requires an annual return only if the entity is tax resident in Cayman or carries out one of the nine relevant activities; a pure colocation leasing company managing physical assets outside Gibraltar/Cayman may not fall within ESR if no core income-generating activity is local.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

UK Corporate Tax Residence

Statutory sanction: UK corporation tax at 25% on worldwide profits; penalties up to 100% of tax; notification failure penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly permit UK management without registration may be personally assessed for tax and penalties; up to 7 years' imprisonment under the Fraud Act.
Transfer Pricing Mismatch

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of profits; late filing penalties; any tax understated bears interest and penalties up to 100%.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign incorrect TP documentation are jointly liable for penalties; in severe cases, confiscation orders and director disqualification.
Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Automatic penalty of GBP 300; daily penalties up to GBP 3,600; material penalty of up to GBP 100,000 in Gibraltar; equivalent Cayman penalties of up to CI$120,000.

Director personal liability: Directors of record may be liable for the company's penalties if they signed false declarations; potential disqualification and 2 years' imprisonment in Gibraltar.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Cayman: fine up to CI$25,000 and 2 years' imprisonment; Gibraltar: fine up to GBP 500,000 and director disqualification; asset freeze and international sanctions liability.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors who allow false beneficial ownership filings are personally criminally liable; personal assets can be subjected to confiscation or freezing orders.
Tax Evasion or Fraudulent Avoidance

Statutory sanction: HMRC can assess the company as UK-resident and apply criminal fines, civil monetary penalties, and interest; Government can strike off the Gibraltar or Cayman trading entity.

Director personal liability: In cases of fraudulent evasion of tax, directors and shadow directors face imprisonment of up to 7 years; civil restitution and travel bans issued by the Gibraltar Supreme Court.
