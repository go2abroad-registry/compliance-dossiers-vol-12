Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone – the Cayman Exempted Company is exempt from tax in its place of incorporation. In Gibraltar it is treated as a non-resident offshore company unless central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar; if such management and control is found, the entity would be subject to Entity_Level_Taxation on its world-wide income at the Gibraltar corporate rate. A non-resident Cayman Exempted Company is taxable only on Gibraltar-source income attributable to a permanent establishment.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' entity classification election. The Income Tax (Attribution of Profits) Regulations permit an election to apply the authorised OECD approach for permanent establishment profit attribution, and the Economic Substance (Company and Private Foundations) Act 2020 permits a foreign-resident entity to file a non-residence certificate to be excluded from Gibraltar economic substance reporting where tax residency and substance in another jurisdiction are proven. Hybrid mismatch standards under the OECD BEPS regime are adopted, and the Commissioner may accept an election to classify an entity as transparent or opaque for cross-border tax purposes where the underlying foreign tax treatment is properly evidenced.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 100,000 annual taxable turnover; however, a non-resident entity importing goods into Gibraltar for onward distribution is required to register for GST at the first import regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of IVD diagnostic reagents and related clinical assay materials to destinations outside Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar GST and qualify for zero-rating where the supplier can prove that the goods physically departed Gibraltar within 30 days of the taxable supply and were received by a customer not established in Gibraltar. Offshore services, including cross-border distribution logistics services ancillary to the reagent supply, are zero-rated if the place of supply is outside Gibraltar and the recipient is non-resident. The Cayman Exempted Co will claim the zero rate by showing a valid export declaration, transport documentation and bank evidence of export proceeds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic GICS declaration or CUS-EXP form)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of lading or air waybill, including consolidated transport documents
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit evidence for the exported goods
  • Certificate of origin for trade preference computation
  • GMP/CE-IVD conformity certificate and free-sale certificate for diagnostic medical devices

All imports and exports must be declared electronically through the HM Customs of Gibraltar Integrated Customs System (GICS); the system performs real-time risk scoring, duty/GST calculation and digital clearance release. There is no mandatory continuous transaction control or digital fiscal stamp for wholesale invoices, but every GST invoice must include the supplier's GST registration number and be retained in electronic form for six years for audit purposes.

HM Customs and the Commissioner of Income Tax may audit export or zero-rating claims for up to six years. A false or inflated export zero-rating claim attracts a penalty up to 150% of the tax avoided and potential criminal liability under the Customs Ordinance and Income Tax Act. Where exported proceeds are not repatriated to a Gibraltar bank account within 180 days, the zero-rating is automatically reversed, the GST becomes immediately payable with interest at 8% per annum, and the non-repatriation creates a conclusive presumption of undeclared taxable profit unless rebutted by the taxpayer.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP is fully convertible at par to GBP, with no managed float, no convertibility license, and no differential treatment between current and capital account transactions. Both current-account payments (trade in ELISA assay reagents, freight, insurance, professional fees) and capital-account transactions (equity subscription, shareholder loans, debt repayment, dividend remittances) are free of exchange-control approval in Gibraltar and the United Kingdom. The practical constraint is not regulatory permission but bank-level compliance: the relevant bank must confirm that the Cayman exempted company is not tax-resident in Gibraltar (central management and control located outside Gibraltar) and that it is not a UK tax resident via the shadow-director or permanent-establishment tests, otherwise a 12.5% Gibraltar corporate tax exposure and the UK diverted-profits-tax scrutiny could arise on remitted profits. Dividend and profit repatriation from the Gibraltar bank account to Cayman or to ultimate shareholders is unrestricted once the bank holds a board resolution, a tax-residence profile, and evidence that the company has satisfied the Cayman Economic Substance notification obligations. For USD settlements the bank may require conversion via the London wholesale market at the prevailing interbank rate without any mandatory surrender or conversion ratio.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules and no mandatory conversion ratios in Gibraltar or the United Kingdom. Exporters may retain 100% of export receipts in any currency and may hold them indefinitely in GIP, GBP, USD, or EUR. The only compliance requirements are (i) the international-transaction reporting threshold: all cross-border payments above GBP 10,000 (or the EUR equivalent) reported by the bank to the Bank of England / GFSC for balance-of-payments statistics; (ii) the suspicious-transaction reporting duty under Gibraltar's Proceeds of Crime legislation and the GFSC AML/CFT Rules; and (iii) the customer-due-diligence expectation that the source of export proceeds (commercial invoices for diagnostic reagents) be retained on file for five years. No timeline applies to repatriation of export receipts, and no approval is needed for holding them offshore.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (London) acts as the UK central monetary and exchange-control authority and, together with HM Treasury, governs all UK and Gibraltar-related cross-border payment reporting, sanctions, and financial-stability policy; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory prudential and conduct supervisor for banks and payment institutions licensed in Gibraltar. The Government of Gibraltar, rather than the Bank of England, issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) under the Currency Notes Act of Gibraltar (enacted 1934), maintaining a hard 1:1 peg to GBP. There is no separate Gibraltar foreign-exchange administration; exchange-control functions are absorbed into the UK/Gibraltar integrated AML/CFT and balance-of-payments reporting framework led by the Bank of England for the UK and by the GFSC for Gibraltar-licensed firms.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Legal constraints: Gibraltar is outside the EEA, so post-Brexit passporting by EEA electronic-money institutions is no longer available; an EMI serving the Cayman exempted company must be either a GFSC-licensed electronic-money institution under the Gibraltar electronic-money licensing regime or a UK FCA-authorised EMI operating through a permitted Gibraltar branch or cross-border supervision arrangement. In practice, mainstream UK EMIs will decline a Cayman Tax-Exempt company for a high-volume operating account because the corporate structure is offshore and lacks UK/Gibraltar tax residence, even though the product line is Low_Risk_Standard. A workable blueprint is a dual-rail structure: (i) a GFSC-licensed bank (Gibraltar International Bank plc or Trusted Novus Bank) for capital escrow, GBP clearing, and documentary trade settlements; and (ii) a UK FCA-authorised EMI (for example, a regulated payment-account provider such as Wise Platform or ClearBank-connected API banks) for low-value EUR/USD supplier payouts, using the parent undertaking and named trading activity 'wholesale distribution of IVD diagnostic reagents (NAICS 424690)'. Xapo Bank (Gibraltar) and other digital-first firms accept the Cayman company only when there is a genuine trade flow, a Gibraltar-licensed TCSP introduction, and audited accounts; no fully unregulated online account route exists in Gibraltar.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for the initial and subsequent capital injections: the shareholder issues a SWIFT MT103 or RTGS transfer to the Gibraltar beneficiary bank, addressing field 56A (intermediary) through the bank's London or New York correspondent only when the beneficiary bank provides such routing instructions, field 59 with the exact legal name and account number of the Cayman exempted company exactly as stated on the Certificate of Incorporation, and field 70 with the remittance reference 'CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION AGAINST SHARES – NO LOAN'. The payment-purpose declaration for the Bank of England / GFSC reporting must be the IMF BPM6 category 'Direct investment - equity capital (excluding reinvestment of earnings)'; for SEPA/CT euro-denominated injections, the EPC category purpose code 'CABD' (capital payments and debts) must be used. There is no statutory minimum capital for a Cayman exempted company, but the Gibraltar bank may internally impose a minimum opening balance of GBP 1,000 to GBP 5,000 for an operational account and may hold the capital in escrow until the source-of-funds file is fully cleared. Verification steps: the bank requires (i) the board resolution approving the contribution, (ii) a share-certificate or paid-up capital ledger entry post-receipt, (iii) a letter from the receiving officer confirming no third-party beneficial interest, and (iv) a pre-notification to the bank's compliance team at least two business days before the transfer to avoid an automatic return of funds on name-mismatch.Clearing rails for trade settlements: (1) High-value supplier payments above USD 100,000 or GBP/EUR 50,000 must use SWIFT MT103 via the Gibraltar bank's London (for GBP), New York (for USD), or Frankfurt correspondent (for EUR) with full 50K/59 fields and purpose code 'Trade in goods - medical devices and reagents' under the BPM6 balance-of-payments classification. (2) Documentary trade settlements against shipping documents use an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit opened through an MT700 issued by the Gibraltar bank and advised through the supplier's confirming bank, with the compliant documents being the certificate of analysis, commercial invoice, port-to-port bill of lading, insurance certificate, and the MHRA/CE registration copy for each reagent lot. (3) Low-value European supplier payouts are paid by SEPA Credit Transfer (SEPA/CT) settled through STEP2, or SEPA Instant for urgent sub-EUR-10,000 transactions, in each case with the purpose code 'TRAD' or the more specific ISO purpose 'GDSV' (purchase of goods - services) if the bank's payment gateway supports it. (4) For same-day GBP domestic disbursements, use CHAPS; for batched non-urgent payments, BACS. The recommended treasury design is a dedicated USD collection account at a New York or London correspondent to receive laboratory customer receipts from the United States, with a zero-balance sweep into the Gibraltar operational account at end-of-day to maximise interest and concentration; all disbursements must be backed by a purchase order and the bank's automated anti-money-laundering engine must see a clear goods-versus-funds narrative to prevent a trade-based money-laundering referral.FX outright forwards: permitted for GBP/USD and GBP/EUR trade payables and receivables with GFSC-authorised bank counterparties under an ISDA 2002 or single-bank treasury master agreement; GIP forwards are unnecessary because GIP is pegged at par to GBP.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs): not permissible or commercially relevant for GIP because the Gibraltar Government maintains a fixed parity to GBP; NDFs are restricted to hedging exotic-currency exposures only where the underlying trade flow involves a genuinely non-convertible settlement currency and the GFSC bank has explicitly approved the product.Cross-currency swaps (FX swap and cross-currency interest-rate swap): permitted for converting USD shareholder capital into EUR/GBP procurement currency and for medium-term treasury rebalancing, subject to the Gibraltar EMIR-equivalent margining rules (initial and variation margin posting under the CEM/VM regime with a GFSC-licensed counterparty).European-style vanilla FX options (call/put on GBP/USD, GBP/EUR, or USD/EUR): permitted for contingent trade flows such as confirmed purchase orders or capital-raise timing, written by a GFSC-licensed investment firm and collateralised within the bank's market-risk credit line; exotic or barrier options should be avoided because they attract enhanced product-governance review and heightened capital charges.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Legal constraints: Gibraltar is outside the EEA, so post-Brexit passporting by EEA electronic-money institutions is no longer available; an EMI serving the Cayman exempted company must be either a GFSC-licensed electronic-money institution under the Gibraltar electronic-money licensing regime or a UK FCA-authorised EMI operating through a permitted Gibraltar branch or cross-border supervision arrangement. In practice, mainstream UK EMIs will decline a Cayman Tax-Exempt company for a high-volume operating account because the corporate structure is offshore and lacks UK/Gibraltar tax residence, even though the product line is Low_Risk_Standard. A workable blueprint is a dual-rail structure: (i) a GFSC-licensed bank (Gibraltar International Bank plc or Trusted Novus Bank) for capital escrow, GBP clearing, and documentary trade settlements; and (ii) a UK FCA-authorised EMI (for example, a regulated payment-account provider such as Wise Platform or ClearBank-connected API banks) for low-value EUR/USD supplier payouts, using the parent undertaking and named trading activity 'wholesale distribution of IVD diagnostic reagents (NAICS 424690)'. Xapo Bank (Gibraltar) and other digital-first firms accept the Cayman company only when there is a genuine trade flow, a Gibraltar-licensed TCSP introduction, and audited accounts; no fully unregulated online account route exists in Gibraltar.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar has no codified GIP monetary threshold for mandatory Local File or Master File. Administrative practice and OECD alignment require contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation where annual connected-party turnover exceeds GIP 1,000,000, or where the taxpayer applies for an Advance Pricing Arrangement. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more; reports are filed with the Commissioner and exchanged with the United Kingdom (HMRC) under the Competent Authority Agreement.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations 2019 apply to every company tax-resident in Gibraltar carrying on a 'relevant activity', expressly including 'distribution and service centre business'. A Cayman Exempted Company that is managed and controlled in Gibraltar and wholesales IVD reagents through Gibraltar must demonstrate in the annual ESR return that: (i) board meetings and senior management decisions occur in Gibraltar; (ii) core income-generating activities (negotiating order terms, maintaining inventory, controlling distribution risk, invoicing customers, arranging logistics) are performed in Gibraltar; and (iii) adequate full-time employees, physical premises, and operating expenditure are maintained in Gibraltar proportionate to the distribution activity. Cayman's parallel ESR regime will accept Gibraltar as the jurisdiction of tax residence and substance only if the Gibraltar presence is genuine and documented; mere letterbox arrangements fail both regimes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Management and Control Arbitrage

Statutory sanction: Reassessment of worldwide profits at 12.5%; interest at 1% per month; penalty up to 100% of the understated tax; criminal fine up to GIP 50,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years for deliberate fraudulent evasion; automatic information exchange with Cayman (TIA) and HMRC.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly caused or acquiesced in the invalid tax position are jointly and severally liable for restitution of unpaid corporate tax; civil recovery may be pursued against Cayman-held assets; a departure prohibition order (travel ban) can be imposed; imprisonment up to 7 years for fraudulent evasion where consent or neglect is proven.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty up to GIP 100,000 under the Economic Substance Regulations; continuing default penalties; substituted penalty of up to 10% of Gibraltar-source gross revenue for egregious cases; mandatory exchange of information with Cayman/HMRC; compulsory strike-off and loss of redomiciliation privileges in Gibraltar.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for penalties where they signed a false ESR declaration; director disqualification orders up to 15 years; personal fines up to GIP 30,000 per offence; custodial sanction of up to 6 months for knowingly filing a false substance return.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment by the Commissioner with interest; penalty up to 100% of the adjusted tax liability for deliberate understatement (75% for careless error); publication in the list of defaulting taxpayers; withholding or denial of cross-border deductions.

Director personal liability: Directors can be made personally liable for the unpaid tax debt where the company is struck off or dissolved before payment; personal liability attaches where the director knowingly signed false intercompany agreements; travel restrictions and disqualification orders are available to enforce collection.
AML / Proceeds of Crime and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and AML Regulations 2019: on conviction on indictment, an unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering; civil forfeiture of the assets in Gibraltar; regulatory penalties up to GIP 100,000 for reporting failures and FIU enforcement action.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers commit an offence where the breach is committed with their consent or connivance, or through their negligence; absence of adequate AML procedures is an aggravating factor; confiscation orders and travel bans can be enforced against personal assets located both in Gibraltar and elsewhere through mutual legal assistance.
Unlicensed Regulated Product Distribution

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Medicines Act: a fine up to GIP 50,000 per prohibited sale and/or imprisonment up to 2 years per offence; product recall and stop notices; revocation or suspension of any existing trading licence; forfeiture of non-compliant stock.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers are personally liable for offences proven to have been committed with their consent or neglect; civil restitution may be owed to affected hospitals or laboratories; exclusion from public procurement and signage of the responsible person disqualification are additional consequences.
