Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar — Office of the Commissioner of Income Tax (administering the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010); indirect taxes and customs are administered separately by HM Customs & Excise Department, Gibraltar. Primary portals: Gibraltar Income Tax e-Filing (Government of Gibraltar Income Tax Portal at www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax-office), Gibraltar Companies Registry (www.gibraltar.gov.gi/companies-registry), and HM Customs & Excise Gibraltar (www.gibraltar.gov.gi/customs-and-excise). For UK parent-sovereign linkages, HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) remains competent for UK VAT and customs matters.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — the Cayman Exempted Company is exempt in the Cayman Islands from income, corporate and capital gains taxes under a Tax Concessions Act statutory undertaking (20 years, renewable up to 30 years); for Gibraltar purposes the same entity is re-characterised under Territorial_Scope, as the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 charges tax only on profits arising in, derived from, or attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment or Gibraltar-source trading activity. Worldwide passive income of a non-resident Cayman company lacking Gibraltar management and control remains outside the Gibraltar charge, and no entity-level UK corporation tax applies unless the entity is UK tax resident or carries on a UK trade through a UK permanent establishment.

Available election forms

Cayman Islands: statutory irrevocable election to obtain a 20-year (renewable to 30-year) Tax Exemption Certificate and tax undertaking under section 6 of the Tax Concessions Act. Gibraltar: formal notifications/elections under the Income Tax Act 2010 to fix territorial scope and deduction entitlements, including the Notional Interest Deduction on qualifying new equity, the domestic Intellectual Property regime election for patented income, and the application of OECD/G20 hybrid-mismatch and base-erosion rules; no US-style check-the-box regime exists, but the jurisdictions permit alignment of entity characterisation under the Multilateral Instrument to prevent both double taxation and double non-taxation of the exempted company's cross-border e-commerce income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar: no VAT/GST or general sales tax — the regime comprises targeted Import Duty (mirrored on the UK Global Tariff under the UK-Gibraltar Customs Union) and a limited Purchase Tax on designated goods and services; parent United Kingdom sovereignty: standard Value Added Tax under VATA 1994 at 20% for supplies made in or from Great Britain.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general Gibraltar turnover threshold is prescribed: customs import registration is mandatory from the first commercial importation of goods into Gibraltar (no de minimis exemption for business importers), and Purchase Tax attaches from the first import or supply of designated items (e.g., liquid fuels, alcohol, tobacco, certain services). In the parent UK sovereign, the statutory VAT registration threshold is GBP 85,000 of annual taxable turnover; for cross-border distance sales into the UK or EU, the entity must also monitor the UK distance-selling threshold and the EU One-Stop-Shop non-union registration conditions for online sales of custom mechanical keyboard keycaps.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT, so classic export zero-rating does not arise in the fiscal sense; goods exported from Gibraltar to non-Gibraltar destinations are outside the scope of Gibraltar Purchase Tax and free of import duty at origin, provided the exporter retains conclusive evidence of physical departure from Gibraltar customs territory; any imported input duty is not clawed back if the goods are re-exported in the same condition under customs duty-suspension/end-use rules. For goods or services routed via Great Britain, exports of custom mechanical keyboard keycaps from the UK to non-UK destinations qualify for zero-rated VAT under section 30 VATA 1994 where the goods physically leave the UK within statutory time limits and the prescribed commercial and transport documentation is obtained before the relevant VAT return is submitted; offshore supplies of design and digital services to non-UK consumers are outside the scope of UK VAT under the place-of-supply rules.

  • Gibraltar/UK electronic customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document/Export Entry) using the correct commodity classification for plastic keycaps (HS heading 3926 or 8501/8471 parts, per ISIC 3240 scope)
  • Signed commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading or House/Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch from the exporting customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 evidence of receipt of export proceeds in the entity's bank account
  • Carrier's certificate for consolidated or groupage freight where applicable
  • Certificate of Origin or dual-use licence declaration if required by the destination market (not applicable to standard polycarbonate/ABS keyboard keycaps)

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal e-invoicing, government clearance, or digital stamping; invoices must satisfy the evidentiary requirements of the Income Tax Act 2010 (supplier identification, date, description, consideration, and buyer identification). Customs import and export declarations are filed electronically through HM Customs & Excise Gibraltar's automated declarations processing system using the UK Global Tariff nomenclature under the UK-Gibraltar Customs Union. No SII/FatturaPA-style continuous transaction controls or Arabic/Indian-style fiscal stamping obligations apply to the jurisdiction.

HM Customs & Excise Gibraltar (and HMRC for UK-linked exports) conduct post-clearance documentary audits cross-matched against shipping manifests, courier records, payment rails (Shopify Payments, Stripe) and bank statements. Misuse of export zero-rating, false export declarations, or overclaimed refunds attract: denial/withdrawal of zero-rating or duty relief, assessment of the full VAT/duty evaded, interest, civil penalties of up to 100% of the tax, customs seizure and forfeiture of goods with penalty up to double the duty (Gibraltar Customs & Excise Ordinance and UK CEMA 1979), retrospective clawback generally up to 4 years and up to 20 years in fraud cases (UK FA 2007, VATA 1994 Sch. 24; Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010), criminal prosecution of directors, and joint and several liability of shareholders and beneficial owners for wrongly recovered amounts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GBP is fully convertible with no capital or current account restrictions; GIP is freely convertible within Gibraltar but must be exchanged via authorised banks at par to GBP for external settlement. KYD is pegged at 0.80 to USD. As a CIMA non-resident designated Exempted Company, the entity may freely hold USD, GBP, EUR, and CNH accounts with no repatriation or conversion approvals. Profit remittance is hurdle-free in both jurisdictions; Cayman levies zero withholding tax on dividends and Gibraltar imposes no remittance tax. The only practical hurdle is the bank's demand for proof of economic substance under the Cayman Economic Substance (International Tax Compliance) Act and a Gibraltar management-services agreement evidencing central management and control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. The Cayman Exchange Control Regulations 1959 exempt non-resident designated companies from surrender obligations. Gibraltar has maintained no exchange controls since 1979. Export sales proceeds in USD, GBP, EUR, and CNH may be retained offshore indefinitely; there is no statutory conversion ratio or mandatory repatriation timeline for this entity structure.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England for sterling monetary policy; Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) as Exchange Control Administrator for the Cayman Exempted Company; Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as prudential and AML supervisor of Gibraltar banks and EMIs. The Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at a 1:1 peg to GBP, which circulates alongside sterling throughout the jurisdiction.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: High for ancillary aggregation and payment routing, Medium as a settlement engine, Low as sole treasury repository. Gibraltar-licensed EMIs such as Transact Payments Limited operate under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Regulations 2019 and can provide GBP/EUR virtual IBANs, SEPA/CHAPS access, and segregated client funds. Legal constraints: (1) the EMI must independently complete AML/KYC on the Cayman Exempted Company and will demand the same substance file as a Tier-1 bank; (2) Gibraltar EMIs are not members of the Gibraltar Deposit Guarantee Scheme, so balances above the GBP 85,000 coverage equivalent must be swept daily to a commercial bank; (3) Shopify Payments and Stripe preferentially settle via their acquiring banks to a real corporate IBAN in the legal entity name, and EMI virtual IBANs may be rejected for route-1 merchant settlement; (4) custody risk on card float requires a board-approved sweep policy and monthly reconciliation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital injection is executed by SWIFT MT103 with CHAPS for same-day GBP settlement above GBP 1 million, or via USD CHIPS correspondent routing for USD injections. Field 70 remittance information must state SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION with board resolution date, and the ISO 20022 purpose code must be INVE for investment or EQUI for equity-like instrument. The receiving Gibraltar bank requires pre-credit notification comprising the Cayman certificate of incorporation, the capital-increase board resolution, the updated register of members, and the CIMA Exchange Control non-resident designation letter. Funds are credited to the Capital Escrow Account and released to operations only upon a dual-signatory escrow release instruction. Missing purpose-code alignment or absent board documentation triggers a 3-10 business day compliance hold.Supplier payments for PBT plastic pellets, mould tooling, and keycap production are cleared as follows: (1) ordinary overseas suppliers via SWIFT MT103 with GPI tracking from the Gibraltar Tier-1 bank; (2) large Asian procurements via irrevocable documentary letter of credit under UCP600 confirmed by the beneficiary's bank, or CNH-denominated MT103 routed through CIPS where onshore CNY settlement is commercially preferred; (3) EU suppliers via T2 or SEPA credit transfer from the EUR account; (4) freight and customs duties via the EMI virtual IBAN payer. Every outbound wire must be backed by a commercial invoice and transport document (bill of lading or waybill); the bank will block any transfer failing shipper and consignee sanctions screening. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: High for ancillary aggregation and payment routing, Medium as a settlement engine, Low as sole treasury repository. Gibraltar-licensed EMIs such as Transact Payments Limited operate under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Regulations 2019 and can provide GBP/EUR virtual IBANs, SEPA/CHAPS access, and segregated client funds. Legal constraints: (1) the EMI must independently complete AML/KYC on the Cayman Exempted Company and will demand the same substance file as a Tier-1 bank; (2) Gibraltar EMIs are not members of the Gibraltar Deposit Guarantee Scheme, so balances above the GBP 85,000 coverage equivalent must be swept daily to a commercial bank; (3) Shopify Payments and Stripe preferentially settle via their acquiring banks to a real corporate IBAN in the legal entity name, and EMI virtual IBANs may be rejected for route-1 merchant settlement; (4) custody risk on card float requires a board-approved sweep policy and monthly reconciliation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local currency is GBP. A mandatory Local File and Master File are required if (i) the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds GBP 1,000,000 in the tax year, or (ii) the enterprise's total consolidated group turnover exceeds GBP 20 million and it participates in a controlled cross-border transaction.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to tax-resident companies undertaking relevant activities (including manufacturing, distribution, and service provision). The entity must demonstrate (i) board meetings held in Gibraltar maintaining minutes, (ii) an adequate local employee headcount, (iii) premises or physical presence, and (iv) that core income-generating activities are undertaken in Gibraltar. An annual Economic Substance Return is mandatory.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Fixed fines not exceeding GBP 100,000; daily default fines up to GBP 100; surcharge of 100% of tax underpaid; criminal indictment subject to imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors may be personally liable for the unpaid tax where evasion is knowingly practised; company dissolution may be severed from the balance sheet; court may impose travel bans and interfere with bank mandates.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR penalties: GBP 10,000 fixed penalty plus GBP 100 per day of continued non-compliance; strike-off from the Companies Registry; denial of tax residence certificate.

Director personal liability: The director may be disqualified for up to 15 years, prohibited from managing any Gibraltar company, and compelled to reimburse the revenue losses from personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner of Income Tax may adjust profits under the Transfer Pricing Rules; penalties up to 100% of the additional tax, plus 10% daily interest from the due date.

Director personal liability: Controllers may be jointly and severally liable for the adjusted corporate tax, and the beneficial owner's CAYMAN Exempted Company status may be disregarded for distributions.
Money Laundering / AML Secrecy

Statutory sanction: Failure to file UBO and register a beneficial owner statement leads to fines of up to GBP 500,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors or corporate secretaries who knowingly permit the omission will face personal fines and potential extradition on fraud charges; travel restrictions may be applied.
