Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar - Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax); official portal: https://www.taxes.gov.gi. Corporate registry: Gibraltar Registrar of Companies, Companies House Gibraltar. Customs authority: Gibraltar Customs and Border Control Agency.

E-government filing portal

For a Cayman Exempted Company establishing a place of business or central management and control in Gibraltar: (1) register as an overseas company with the Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 2014 within one month of establishing a place of business or commencing local operations; (2) within 30 days of commencing taxable activity, register for corporate tax with the Income Tax Office and obtain a Gibraltar tax reference number via the GibTax e-filing portal; (3) before any import or export of goods, obtain a Gibraltar EORI / customs registration identifier from the Customs and Border Control Agency; (4) file Forms OC/CT with certified constitutional documents, deed of incorporation or certificate of good standing from the Cayman Islands, Gibraltar registered office address, and details of the local manager or representative. No minimum capital requirement applies and 100% non-resident ownership is permitted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not apply US-style check-the-box elections; entity classification and source rules are statutory under the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended). Available mechanisms include: (i) application to the Commissioner for a formal advance ruling or status determination confirming residence, non-residence, and source of profits for a foreign-incorporated company; (ii) elective Simplified Payroll-Based Tax Regime for small trading companies with fewer than five employees and annual payroll normally not exceeding GBP 500,000, taxing 16% of payroll instead of the standard 10% corporate rate on profits; (iii) no preferential exempt-company regime remains available. Hybrid mismatch and controlled foreign company rules apply mandatorily under OECD BEPS Action 2 / EU ATAD alignment, so no elective hybrid deduction or arbitrage benefit is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST applies in Gibraltar. Operative indirect taxes: Gibraltar Import Duty under the Customs Ordinance (tariff-aligned with the EU Common Customs Tariff) and Excise Duty under the Excise Ordinance on alcohol, hydrocarbon oil, and tobacco. There is no sales and use tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT/GST registration threshold exists because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST regime. For customs purposes, every trader moving goods into or out of Gibraltar must hold a Gibraltar EORI / customs registration identifier irrespective of value or volume, with no economic threshold. For corporate income tax, any foreign company with Gibraltar-source taxable profits must register with the Income Tax Office without a turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT, so exports are not 'zero-rated' in the VAT sense. The equivalent relief is the absence of export taxation: goods physically exported from Gibraltar, including electric cargo e-bikes, are entirely outside the charge to Gibraltar import duty and excise duty, provided a valid customs export declaration is lodged. Imported raw materials and components used in the manufacturing process and subsequently exported may qualify for full duty relief under inward-processing or end-use authorisations. The 10% corporate tax rate applies only to Gibraltar-source profits; title and export proceeds attributable to sales made and performed outside Gibraltar can be excluded from the Gibraltar tax charge where the situs of the trading operations is outside Gibraltar, subject to transfer pricing and controlled foreign company rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD - Single Administrative Document filed and cleared electronically with HM Customs Gibraltar)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill for airfreight exports)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank or fintech certificate evidencing foreign currency proceeds repatriated to the Gibraltar operating account)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (where preferential tariff treatment or trade defence measures are claimed)
  • Supplier and raw-material import declarations under inward-processing authorisation (where duty relief is claimed for inputs incorporated into exported e-bikes)

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping. Ordinary electronic or paper invoices are accepted for tax and customs evidentiary purposes. Customs declarations (Single Administrative Document / SAD) must be submitted electronically through HM Customs Gibraltar's electronic declaration system before export release; no tax-authority pre-clearance of individual invoices is required.

HM Customs Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office conduct joint post-clearance audits of export claims. Under the Customs Ordinance, a false or fraudulent export declaration can trigger forfeiture of goods, a civil penalty of up to three times the value of the goods plus the evaded duty, and criminal liability including imprisonment. Under the Income Tax Act 2010, incorrect export-related returns attract a penalty of up to 100% of the underpaid tax, daily default fines of up to GBP 50 per day, and criminal prosecution. Retrospective clawback of any customs duty relief, tax advantage, or exemption is imposed where goods are re-imported, diverted in transit, or the stated export destination and proceeds are not substantiated. Transfer pricing adjustments may also be made where the export price to the Cayman parent or related buyers does not comply with the arm's-length standard.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Current- and capital-account convertibility is unrestricted in Gibraltar. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) maintains a de jure one-to-one parity with pound sterling under the Currency Notes Act, with both GIP and GBP circulating freely. There are no exchange controls, prior approval licences, or repatriation limits affecting dividends, interest, loan repayments, or capital proceeds payable to a Cayman exempted company. The only practical profit-remittance hurdles are bank-level source-of-funds verification and economic-substance scrutiny, not statutory FX restrictions. A Cayman exempted company can freely receive and transfer USD, EUR, GBP, GIP, and other convertible currencies through its Gibraltar treasury account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar law does not impose any statutory surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or prescribed timeline for export receipts. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in the currency of receipt or converted at the company's discretion. The only applicable obligations are accurate statutory recordkeeping, submission to the Gibraltar Statistics Office if the business is selected for trade data collection, and compliance with AML/CFT transaction reporting thresholds. No conversion to sterling or Gibraltar pound is ever mandated.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the licensing, conduct and AML/CFT supervisory authority; the Government of Gibraltar's Currency Section as the statutory issuer of Gibraltar pound banknotes; and Her Majesty's Treasury / Bank of England as the provider of one-for-one sterling backing and monetary policy anchor for the Gibraltar pound.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium (constrained by commercial risk appetite). A Cayman exempted company is generally treated by offshore digital EMIs as a high-risk jurisdiction structure, and fully digital onboarding without a Gibraltar/UK operating subsidiary or a locally engaged director is frequently declined. EMIs with a UK or EEA licence may operate under Gibraltar's Temporary Permission Regime or the UK's Gibraltar Authorisation Regime, but local GFSC licensing is required for a substantive Gibraltar-based EMI. Legal constraints include the need for the EMI to apply enhanced due diligence, verify physical trading substantiation, and prove that the account is not used as a shell conduit. Wise Business, Airwallex, and Revolut Business may open treasury accounts only where the Cayman structure is complemented by a Gibraltar branch registration and demonstrable local management.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Defense evidence: Cayman Islands Companies Registry Certificate of Incorporation (Form Ex/Cr), Certificate of Good Standing issued not more than 30-90 days prior, registered office certificate and letter from the licensed Cayman corporate services provider (registered agent), Memorandum and Articles of Association, the revolving Tax Exemption Certificate under the Cayman Islands Income Tax Act (or equivalent current legislation), Cayman Economic Substance Notice confirming exempted company treatment for the relevant financial year, and a written business rationale memorandum explaining why the Gibraltar treasury account is used for the e-bike OEM manufacturing and trade flows.
Non-transparent beneficial ownership chain or nominee shareholder structures

High-volume international trade flows to Southeast Asia/China for OEM e-bike components

Root cause: High-volume international trade flows to Southeast Asia/China for OEM e-bike components

Lack of physical presence or economic substance in Gibraltar or Cayman

Root cause: Lack of physical presence or economic substance in Gibraltar or Cayman

Defense evidence: If management and control is exercised from Gibraltar, provide a Gibraltar office lease or premises confirmation, employment contracts for local management and staff, payroll records, board minutes and tax/economic-substance documentation showing where key decisions are taken, and a Gibraltar foreign-company registration certificate if the Cayman entity has established a place of business. If the entity remains purely a Cayman holding company, provide the Cayman economic substance return and a transfer-pricing policy proving that any Gibraltar treasury function is supported by at least a registered branch, professional management, and real decision-making autonomy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the investor's own account, with the beneficiary legal name exactly matching the Cayman exempted company name and the Gibraltar bank account title. The wire must carry the appropriate cross-border purpose code consistent with the sending bank's taxonomy, typically '1100' or '7011' for equity capital contribution, or a shareholder-loan code if the injection is structured as debt. Field 70 should state 'CAYMAN EXEMPTED CO — CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / WORKING CAPITAL' and reference the board resolution number. The receiving Gibraltar bank must be provided with a signed subscription agreement, board resolution approving the injection, capital contribution ledger entries, a source-of-wealth memorandum for the funding party, and confirmation that the sender is not in a sanctioned jurisdiction. For amounts above EUR/USD/GBP 50,000, the bank will require a further trail to audited financial statements, a statement of net worth, or an independent asset/equity valuation.Outbound trade disbursements are cleared primarily through SWIFT MT103 black-and-white telegraphic transfers for direct supplier settlements, and via SWIFT MT700-series commercial letters of credit for large OEM component purchase orders. Direct wire payments must match the beneficiary name and account number on the underlying supplier invoice and include the purpose code '2031' (trade credit) or '9040' (payment for goods) as required by the sending bank's payment oracle. For letters of credit, the issuing Gibraltar bank will open irrevocable, at-sight or 30-60 day terms under UCP600, with presentation documents comprising commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading/air waybill, and any inspection certificate. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% standard Gibraltar corporate income tax on the Gibraltar branch/profits of the Cayman exempted company; the Cayman parent entity itself is subject to 0% Cayman corporate income tax under the Companies Act (As Revised) exemption

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%; Gibraltar does not impose withholding tax on dividends or distributions and the Cayman exempted company is not subject to Cayman withholding tax on distributions made to its shareholders

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Gibraltar Local File is required where the Gibraltar entity's cross-border related-party transactions exceed GBP 1,000,000 per annum and the worldwide group consolidated turnover exceeds GBP 50,000,000; a Master File is required at group level where consolidated worldwide turnover exceeds GBP 50,000,000; documentation must be prepared before the filing deadline and produced within 30 days of a Commissioner's notice

Economic substance scope: The Cayman parent is subject to the Cayman economic substance regime under the International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act (As Revised) and must file an annual economic substance notification within 12 months of its fiscal year-end; pure manufacturing is not a Cayman relevant activity, but holding-company, IP-business and distribution-and-service-centre categories triggered by the e-bike brand and parts flows must be assessed. Gibraltar has no separate Economic Substance Regulations statute; however, substance is policed indirectly through the source rules and the central management and control doctrine: if the Cayman parent's real board decisions and management functions are performed in Gibraltar, the Commissioner may deem the Cayman exempted company Gibraltar tax resident and assess its worldwide or Gibraltar-managed profits at 12.5%

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - deliberate suppression of Gibraltar-source profits

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties of up to 100% of the additional tax, fixed penalties up to GBP 300 plus daily default penalties, interest on underpaid tax, and criminal conviction for fraudulent evasion of income tax with unlimited fines and imprisonment of up to five years

Director personal liability: Directors and persons concerned in management are jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax where the company defaults; the Supreme Court may impose travel bans, the court may order civil restitution of the evaded amount, and imprisonment is available for fraudulent trading and for money laundering of the evaded proceeds
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization of the structure under the central management and control doctrine, retroactive Gibraltar tax residence assessment at 12.5%, penalties for failure to notify chargeability of up to GBP 3,000, interest on the assessed underpayment, and potential strike-off of the company or revocation of its banking and EMI licenses by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission

Director personal liability: Directors incur personal penalties for failure to comply with filing and notification obligations, civil liability for misfeasance or fraudulent trading, personal restitution orders, travel bans, and disqualification orders of up to 15 years; imprisonment of up to 10 years may be imposed for fraudulent trading under Gibraltar company law
AML / Proceeds of Crime - misuse of the EMI bank account

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties of up to GBP 2,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover under the anti-money laundering regime, confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, unlimited criminal fines, imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering, and suspension or revocation of the EMI license by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission

Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal prosecution, personal confiscation orders, disqualification, travel bans, and custodial sentences; directors and beneficial owners may also be held civilly liable by injured counterparties, and banks that process the flows without filing suspicious activity reports are subject to parallel enforcement
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Upward transfer pricing adjustment of the Gibraltar taxable profits at 12.5%, a penalty of up to 100% of the adjustment, fixed documentation penalties of up to GBP 5,000 per failure, daily default penalties, and interest on the understated tax

Director personal liability: If the TP adjustment renders the Gibraltar PE insolvent, directors are liable for wrongful trading and civil restitution; deliberate manipulation of inter-company prices is prosecuted as tax evasion carrying imprisonment, and personal guarantees given to the EMI or trade creditors may be enforced to collect the tax debt
