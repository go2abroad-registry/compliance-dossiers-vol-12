Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar — official name: 'The Commissioner of Income Tax'; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax. For indirect tax and customs matters: Gibraltar Customs (HM Customs Gibraltar), official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/customs; for UK VAT: Her Majesty's Revenue and Customs (HMRC), official portal: https://www.gov.uk/vat

E-government filing portal

For a Cayman Exempted Company operating from Gibraltar, tax-registration duty depends on de facto nexus. Step 1 — determine whether the company is centrally managed and controlled in Gibraltar or has a permanent establishment (PE) there; if neither, no Gibraltar income-tax registration is required, and the company retains its Cayman 'tax-exempt zone' status. Step 2 — if Gibraltar nexus exists, register the overseas company at the Gibraltar Companies House (Companies Act 2014) within one month of opening a place of business, stating the Cayman registered number. Step 3 — apply to the Commissioner of Income Tax on the prescribed form (TRN application) delivering the Cayman certificate of incorporation, certificate of good standing, constitutional documents, board resolution appointing a Gibraltar tax representative, and the Gibraltar registered-office address. Step 4 — the Income Tax Office issues the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN), used for corporation-tax returns, PAYE if employees are hired, and customs/warehouse authorisations. Step 5 — file annual company tax returns with audited financial statements by the statutory filing date (generally 30 November after the relevant tax year) and pay any Gibraltar corporation tax due at 10% (SME) or 12.5% (non-SME). Step 6 — where no PE or management/control in Gibraltar exists, the company should still obtain a written non-resident/no-nexus confirmation from the Commissioner if requested by a bank or EMI.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Cayman Islands — the exempted company may apply under s.6 of the Tax Concessions Law (2021 Revision) for a tax-exemption undertaking from the Governor in Cabinet, guaranteeing exemption for up to 20 years from all Cayman income, profit and capital gains taxes. Gibraltar — no check-the-box election exists, but a foreign-incorporated entity may elect to be treated as Gibraltar tax-resident by filing a notice and accepting the charge to Gibraltar income tax, thereby accessing the UK–Gibraltar double-taxation arrangement; conversely, it may elect to be treated as non-resident if it has no PE and no central management/control. UK hybrid — under Part 6A TIOPA 2010 the entity must elect or document transparent/non-transparent treatment for any hybrid or reverse-hybrid arrangement with a UK permanent establishment; such election must be made within the statutory disclosure window and maintained in the corporate accounting records.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

United Kingdom: GBP 90,000 annual taxable turnover for UK-established businesses (2024/25); non-UK-established businesses, including a Cayman/Gibraltar entity, are registrable from the first UK taxable supply (no threshold), and voluntary registration is available to obtain a UK VAT number and recover input VAT. Gibraltar: no VAT registration threshold exists because no VAT is levied; any commercial importer must register for a customs business code with Gibraltar Customs regardless of value.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exports arises under s.30 VATA 1994 and HMRC VAT Notice 703. The Cayman Exempted Company may zero-rate a supply of acetate eyeglass frames when (i) the goods are exported to a destination outside the UK, including Gibraltar; (ii) the goods leave the UK within 3 months of the time of supply; (iii) the company (or its UK agent) holds official and commercial evidence of export, such as the CDS customs declaration, a Bill of Lading or airway bill, a commercial invoice with Incoterms showing overseas delivery, and an inward remittance certificate; (iv) the company is a taxable person at the time of supply. For purchases from UK manufacturers, the UK supplier may zero-rate the supply only if the Cayman company gives the supplier the export evidence and its VAT number. Sales that never enter the UK (drop-shipments from non-UK factories to non-UK customers) are outside the scope of UK VAT, and only UK input VAT on UK costs can be recovered under a VAT registration.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice with Incoterms
  • Packing List
  • Export Licence / Certificate of Origin (if required)

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping is used in the UK or Gibraltar. In the UK, Making Tax Digital (MTD) requires VAT returns to be submitted using MTD-compatible software through HMRC's API; invoice data must be preserved in digital form, but e-invoicing is not mandatory. For exports, UK customs declarations must be lodged electronically via the Customs Declaration Service (CDS), and the 'Export Accompanying Document/Electronic Export System' release is required before departure. Gibraltar uses its own customs electronic declaration platform; physical stamps are not required, but an electronic audit trail and, where requested, a certified 'Gibraltar Customs Clearance Certificate' must be retained.

HMRC may audit the export evidence trail retrospectively and will deny zero-rating if the goods are not proven to have left the UK. If the claim fails, output VAT is chargeable at 20%, import VAT becomes reclaimable only if correctly declared, and any incorrectly repaid input VAT is recovered with interest. Penalties apply under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 (up to 100% of VAT lost for deliberate inaccuracy and up to 30% for careless errors), and dishonest conduct can trigger a further penalty and prosecution. For MTIC/carousel fraud, s.16A VATA 1994 imposes joint and several liability on a trader who knew or should have known that the transaction was connected with fraudulent evasion, extending to the full amount of VAT. Gibraltar customs false declarations are subject to seizure, condemnation and confiscation of goods, plus fines of up to three times the value of the goods; all records must be kept for at least 6 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands maintain fully liberalised capital and current account convertibility. Gibraltar, as a UK Overseas Territory, applies no exchange-control restrictions on inward or outward capital flows; GIP is freely convertible into GBP at par, and transactions in USD, EUR, CNH and other major currencies are unrestricted (the only minor regulation is a GBP 2,000 limit on physical export of GIP notes and coins). The Cayman Islands Currency Law (2023 Revision) imposes zero convertibility restrictions; the Cayman dollar (KYD) is pegged at 1 KYD = 1.20 USD, and an exempted company may lawfully fund, hold, and distribute entirely in USD, EUR, or CNH without CIMA approval. Profit remittance: neither Gibraltar (territorial income tax; generally 0% for non-resident-owned or qualifying exempt structures) nor the Cayman Islands (no income or withholding tax) imposes withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, or management fees. The only practical hurdles are (i) GFSC and correspondent-bank conduct rules requiring economic-substance and beneficial-ownership disclosure; (ii) Gibraltar Economic Substance (Companies) Rules applying to companies that are tax-resident in Gibraltar; and (iii) OECD CRS, CbC reporting, and the Gibraltar Beneficial Ownership Register, which bind the company regardless of its Cayman tax-transparent character. A wholesale optical-frames distribution company conducting genuine third-party purchasing, warehousing, and international resale from Gibraltar is expected to remain fully outside any controlled-foreign-company or anti-abuse challenge.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Neither Gibraltar nor the Cayman Islands imposes statutory surrender, mandatory repatriation, or forced conversion of export proceeds. There is no statutory deadline for collecting receivables in foreign currency and no requirement to convert USD, EUR, or CNH export receipts into GIP or KYD. Export invoices may be settled directly into offshore USD clearing accounts and retained in USD. GFSC AML/CFT Guidance and banking practice nonetheless require that export proceeds be reconciled to the underlying trade file within a commercially reasonable period (typically 60 to 120 days from bill-of-lading date), supported by commercial invoice, packing list, ocean/air waybill, certificate of origin, and customs declaration where applicable. For ISIC 4646 acetate eyewear-frame wholesale, the bank will require ERP extracts or platform settlement statements evidencing the physical flow of goods; no conversion ratio applies and no CIMA or GFSC approval is required for retention of export earnings in hard currency.

Central bank supervision: Primary supervisor: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), the integrated financial regulator responsible for banking conduct, AML/CFT supervision, and licensing of banks and e-money institutions in Gibraltar. Monetary anchor: Bank of England (BoE) sterling framework, given the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued by the Government of Gibraltar and legally pegged at par (1:1) to GBP; Gibraltar has no separate central bank. For the residing Cayman Exempted Company, the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) exercises registration-level oversight under the Cayman Islands Companies Act (Cap. 22) and the Money Laundering Regulations (2023 Revision), and acts as the counterpart authority for any Cayman-side licensing or exchange-control-adjacent matters. Actual FX custody, clearing, and trade settlement are Gibraltar-banking functions subject to GFSC conduct rules and BoE payment-systems standards.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM, with material legal constraints. Gibraltar has a statutory e-money regime (Gibraltar E-Money Regulations 2011, as amended, supervised by GFSC), but no domestic EMI has the clearing depth required for a primary treasury account. UK FCA-regulated EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business, Modulr, Currenxie, Airwallex) frequently decline direct onboarding of a Cayman Exempted Company because of its offshore tax-transparent status, even when effective management sits in Gibraltar. Some EMIs (notably Currenxie for Gibraltar-managed structures, and Airwallex for UK/EU-group entities) may accept the account, but they impose (i) USD/EUR conversion spreads of 0.3% to 3%, (ii) daily transaction caps, (iii) restrictive settlement thresholds for Platform_Pay payouts, and (iv) enhanced beneficial-ownership screening against the Cayman Islands ICL Register and the Gibraltar BOR. Post-Brexit, UK EMI passporting into Gibraltar is not automatic; reliance is on the UK-Gibraltar Treasury MoU and FCA/GFSC cooperation arrangements, meaning a UK-licensed EMI must rely on per-user sub-licences or manual approval. Legal verdict: an EMI may be used as a secondary collection and Platform_Pay sweep account only after a Tier-1 Gibraltarian bank account is fully operational; using an EMI as the sole or primary treasury rail is not compliant with prudent GFSC expectations and will raise correspondent-bank concerns for subsequent Tier-1 onboarding.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM, with material legal constraints. Gibraltar has a statutory e-money regime (Gibraltar E-Money Regulations 2011, as amended, supervised by GFSC), but no domestic EMI has the clearing depth required for a primary treasury account. UK FCA-regulated EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business, Modulr, Currenxie, Airwallex) frequently decline direct onboarding of a Cayman Exempted Company because of its offshore tax-transparent status, even when effective management sits in Gibraltar. Some EMIs (notably Currenxie for Gibraltar-managed structures, and Airwallex for UK/EU-group entities) may accept the account, but they impose (i) USD/EUR conversion spreads of 0.3% to 3%, (ii) daily transaction caps, (iii) restrictive settlement thresholds for Platform_Pay payouts, and (iv) enhanced beneficial-ownership screening against the Cayman Islands ICL Register and the Gibraltar BOR. Post-Brexit, UK EMI passporting into Gibraltar is not automatic; reliance is on the UK-Gibraltar Treasury MoU and FCA/GFSC cooperation arrangements, meaning a UK-licensed EMI must rely on per-user sub-licences or manual approval. Legal verdict: an EMI may be used as a secondary collection and Platform_Pay sweep account only after a Tier-1 Gibraltarian bank account is fully operational; using an EMI as the sole or primary treasury rail is not compliant with prudent GFSC expectations and will raise correspondent-bank concerns for subsequent Tier-1 onboarding.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% (the single Gibraltar corporation tax rate under the Income Tax Act 2010, as amended; non-resident companies are charged at 12.5% on Gibraltar-source profits attributable to a permanent establishment in Gibraltar).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (Gibraltar does not impose any withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar-resident company, regardless of the shareholder's residence; distributions to a Cayman Exempted Company are paid without Gibraltar tax deduction).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File / Master File is triggered when the Gibraltar group member's aggregate controlled transaction value exceeds GIP 1,000,000 in a tax year, or where consolidated group annual turnover exceeds GIP 50,000,000. All other taxpayers must attach a simplified transfer-pricing declaration (Form TP-C) and retain benchmark data for arm's-length prices.

Economic substance scope: Applicable under the Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2020 and the ESR guidance issued by the Gibraltar Commissioner of Companies. Wholesale distribution of acetate ophthalmic frames falls within 'Distribution and Service Centres' as a relevant activity when income arises from the distribution of goods supplied by group companies. A Cayman Exempted Company that is tax resident in Gibraltar must demonstrate all core income-generating activities - procurement negotiation, inventory risk, supply-chain management and customer invoicing - are performed in Gibraltar with a physical office, full-time employees and adequate operating expenditure; an annual ESR return must be filed with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / sham structure

Statutory sanction: Assessment on the true Gibraltar-source worldwide profits at 12.5%; civil penalty up to 150% of the tax; criminal conviction under the Income Tax Act 2010 carries fines up to GBP 500,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, personally penalised, subjected to travel bans pending cross-border proceedings, and disqualified from acting as directors for up to 15 years.
Filing of knowingly false tax return

Statutory sanction: In addition to the unpaid tax, a penalty of 100% of the understated tax and a fine up to GBP 250,000; repeated conduct is an indictable offence leading to imprisonment for up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: The director who signs the false return may be criminally prosecuted irrespective of corporate capacity, with liability for interest and penalties and a travel restriction order.
Transfer pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment under the arm's-length principle, additional tax at 12.5%, penalty of 100% of the extra tax, interest, and automatic exchange of information with the UK tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the non-arm's-length pricing are liable as tax avoidance enablers, subject to personal financial penalties of up to 100% of the tax evaded and disqualification from managing an exempted or limited company.
AML / proceeds of crime and sanctions breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the Financial Services Act, the company may be fined up to 10% of annual turnover or GBP 500,000, bank accounts frozen, and the EMI/banker relationship terminated; criminal exposure is imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally accountable for AML system failures, can be made subject to confiscation orders and travel bans, and are barred from banking in Gibraltar or any DPA-regulated territory.
