Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax - Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar; online filing via the Gibraltar e-Tax portal (https://etax.gibraltar.gov.gi) and corporate registry filings via Companies House Gibraltar (https://www.companieshouse.gi).

E-government filing portal

Foreign companies (including a Cayman Exempted Company) commencing activity or registering an establishment in Gibraltar must: (1) register as an 'external company' at Companies House Gibraltar under Part XXI of the Companies Act 2014 and obtain a Gibraltar company number; (2) file a written application for tax registration with the Commissioner of Income Tax within 30 days of commencement of activity, accompanied by a certified Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, register of directors and shareholders, evidence of central management and control, and bank references; (3) for ISIC 5012 RoRo maritime operators, produce the vessel's flag-state registry certificate, IMO tonnage certificate, and a Gibraltar port or business licence; (4) upon verification, the Commissioner issues a unique Gibraltar Tax File Number (TFN) to be quoted on all Corporation Tax returns, customs declarations, and payment instructions. Within the same registration packet, the taxpayer elects its filing currency, accounting reference period, and applicable tax regime.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the Cayman Exempted Company's home tax-exempt status (Tax_Exempt_Zone) is not recognised in Gibraltar; the company is a separate Gibraltar-chargeable person on profits attributable to its Gibraltar trade or permanent establishment, subject to 10% corporation tax and payroll/social insurance obligations, unless it elects into the shipping Tonnage Tax regime.

Available election forms

Statutory elections available: (i) election into the Tonnage Tax regime for qualifying international RoRo shipping operators under the Income Tax Act 2010, replacing ordinary corporation tax with a notional profit determined by net registered tonnage; (ii) application for non-resident company treatment where central management and control lies outside Gibraltar, limiting tax to Gibraltar-source income; (iii) joint elections for group relief under the Income Tax Act 2010; (iv) binding advance rulings from the Commissioner of Income Tax; and (v) OECD-aligned hybrid mismatch elections for cross-border instruments. Gibraltar does not provide a US-style check-the-box or per se entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar is not a VAT/GST jurisdiction. The applicable indirect tax system is 'Customs Import Duty and Excise Duty', administered under the Gibraltar Customs and Excise statutes and aligned with the UK customs tariff framework.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST registration threshold exists in Gibraltar. Businesses engaging in any economic activity in Gibraltar must register for corporate tax with the Commissioner of Income Tax; importers, producers, and warehousekeepers of dutiable/excise goods must obtain a Customs and Excise Dealer registration from HM Customs (Gibraltar) irrespective of turnover; ships calling at Gibraltar must obtain port and cargo clearance from the Gibraltar Port Authority.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating is inapplicable by law because Gibraltar has no VAT system. For direct-tax equivalence, income derived by a qualifying international deep-sea RoRo carrier may be relieved either by election into the Tonnage Tax regime, where a notional profit based on net tonnage replaces actual charter earnings, or as wholly offshore shipping income where the vessel is operated outside Gibraltarian waters by a non-resident company. For customs duty relief, goods exported out of the Gibraltar customs territory, including RoRo vehicles transshipped through the Port of Gibraltar, qualify for export relief provided duty-paid import evidence and proof of physical export are retained; no refund claim is required because exports fall outside the duty charging event.

  • Customs Export Declaration - SAD / UK CDS Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Original Ocean Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, and RoRo cargo manifest
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign-currency bank credit advice evidencing repatriation of freight earnings
  • Vessel Certificate of Registry (flag state) and IMO International Tonnage Certificate
  • Voyage statement, charterparty, or slot-charter contract allocating freight income

No real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or certification-API mandate applies. Customs formalities are fully digitized: export and import entries are lodged through the Gibraltar/UK SAD framework on the UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS) or the Gibraltar Trade Information System, and RoRo cargo manifests are submitted by EDI to the Gibraltar Port Authority. Ordinary B2B/B2C invoicing requires only the statutory invoice content; there is no government-operated e-invoicing clearance ledger.

Gibraltar tax audits under the Income Tax Act 2010 may review export relief, Tonnage Tax qualification, and repatriation records. Inaccurate returns attract default penalties of up to 150% of undercharged tax for negligence and up to 200% for deliberate wrongdoing, with additional continuing daily penalties; fraudulent export or duty-relief claims trigger criminal prosecution and asset seizure. Tonnage Tax entrants losing 'qualifying ship' status face exit from the regime with retrospective re-computation at the standard 10% corporation tax rate for up to six years plus compensatory interest. Customs export-duty relief fraud is penalised by forfeiture of goods and fines of up to three times the duty value, in addition to director disqualification and criminal sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at 1:1 to GBP with full current and capital account convertibility. No exchange controls restrict profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan repayments, or capital repatriation for non-resident offshore entities. The Cayman Islands' currency, the Cayman Islands dollar (KYD), is pegged to USD at 0.8333 per USD, also with no convertibility restrictions. Both jurisdictions operate as zero-exchange-control regimes, with compliance limited to AML/CFT, sanctions screening, and beneficial-ownership reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules in Gibraltar or the Cayman Islands. Ro-Ro shipping revenue, charter hire, and associated freight receipts may be retained in any freely convertible currency, including USD, EUR, or GBP, without mandatory conversion into local currency or any prescribed repatriation timeline. Export invoicing must align with applicable transfer pricing documentation, and e-invoicing/records must be retained for the required statutory periods.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the integrated financial regulator; the Government of Gibraltar (Currency Commissioners) issues Gibraltar pound. The Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) retains home-state supervision of the Cayman Exempted Company.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-to-MEDIUM. Gibraltar-licensed electronic money institutions are regulated under the Financial Services Act 2019 and may serve local/EU/UK corporates; however, most premier cross-border EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business) decline Cayman-exempted companies due to perceived offshore jurisdiction risk and lack of substantive presence. Niche B2B EMIs may provide multi-currency IBANs for low-volume operating expenses after enhanced due diligence, but they are not acceptable for high-value Ro-Ro charter payments, escrowed capital injections, or letters of credit. Tier-1 commercial banks remain the mandatory clearing and liquidity backbone for this ISIC 5012 shipping treasury.
