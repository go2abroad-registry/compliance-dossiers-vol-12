Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The Commissioner of Income Tax, HM Government of Gibraltar, Customs and Income Tax Department (CITD), administered via the MyTax digital portal at www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation and supported by Companies House Gibraltar for corporate/branch filings.

E-government filing portal

Stage 3 SOP for a foreign Cayman Exempted Company establishing presence in Gibraltar: (1) File a Branch Registration Form (Form BR) with the Gibraltar Companies Registry within 7 days of establishing a place of business pursuant to the Companies Act (Gibraltar) 2014. (2) Submit Form GIB-01 (Non-Resident Company Registration) to the CITD together with certified constitutional documents (Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, registered office certificate, beneficial ownership register extract) and a valid passport/director due-diligence pack. (3) Obtain a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (GTIN), being a 7-digit numeric code issued within 5-10 working days, evidenced by a Letter of Registration from the CITD. (4) Register for the MyTax portal to enable electronic filing of the annual Corporation Tax Return (Form CIT-200), employee payroll (PAYE) returns, and Gibraltar Social Insurance declarations. (5) If business activity is expected to create a permanent establishment or branch, apply simultaneously for a customs ID (Trader Identification Number) for export-import declarations. Legal-form registration is separate from tax registration; tax registration is mandatory regardless of anticipated taxable profit and must be renewed or de-registered only with CITD approval.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - The Cayman Exempted Company is domiciled in a zero-tax jurisdiction and, as such, is exempt from Cayman Islands income, corporate, capital and wealth taxes. For Gibraltar purposes, the default classification is that of a non-resident foreign company; however, if the company carries on a trade in Gibraltar through a branch, agency or permanent establishment, the Gibraltar-source attributed profits become subject to the territorial Income Tax Act 2010 regime at a flat rate of 12.5%. The entity itself is eligible for the status of an unregulated exempted foreign company for Companies Act purposes, subject to filing an annual return and declaration of its tax-exempt activity.

Available election forms

Available elections: (i) Qualifying Company election under s.26B of the Income Tax Act 2010 enabling exemption from withholding tax on qualifying interest or royalty payments and confirmation of reduced-rate treatment. (ii) Election for tax-exempt status from shipping, insurance and certain qualifying intellectual property revenue under the Gibraltar Exempt Organisations and Incentive Regulations, where approved in writing by the Minister of Finance. (iii) Hybrid mismatch disjunction elections under the Gibraltar transposition of EU ATAD (Taxation (Hybrid Mechanisms) Rules), allowing the designation of a specific branch as tax-transparent or opaque for mismatch avoidance. (iv) Check-the-box election is not applicable: Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box classification; the tax character of the entity follows its legal form and tax residence. (v) Re-election and revocation require CITD approval and are effective from the start of the following calendar year.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST - Gibraltar is outside the UK VAT area and does not levy VAT or GST. The applicable indirect tax system is the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Regime (Import Duties and Consumption Tax) administered under the Customs and Excise Ordinance, supplemented by the Gibraltar Consumption Tax (on locally consumed alcoholic beverages, tobacco and fuel) and, for international trade, the UK-Gibraltar Trade Continuity Agreement formalities.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A - Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST and therefore there is no general economic turnover threshold for VAT registration. However, an entity that imports controlled goods (including pharmaceutical-grade heparin sodium APIs) must register as an Importer/Declarant with the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Department, obtain a Customs Trader Identifier, and provide a customs bond or security for duty liabilities. Excise registration applies only to alcohol, tobacco and fuel. A de minimis customs value threshold applies to non-commercial samples only; no such threshold applies to commercial pharmaceutical exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because Gibraltar does not impose VAT, zero-rating in the Gibraltar domestic context refers to exemption from customs/reimport duty on exported goods. The export of heparin sodium anticoagulant (ISIC 3250/NAICS 325412) from Gibraltar to a destination outside the UK/Gibraltar customs territory is eligible for 0% duty treatment where: (i) the goods have been definitively exported within the statutory period without subsequent re-entry; (ii) the exporter holds a valid Customs Export Declaration (SAD) accepted by the CITD; (iii) title and risk have passed to a non-resident customer; (iv) the goods leave the jurisdiction through a registered customs port or approved airport; and (v) the overseas customer is documented on the commercial invoice. For value-added tax purposes, if the exempted company separately registers for UK VAT (optional for non-resident companies), supplies of heparin sodium exported from a UK establishment can claim zero-rating under s.30 VAT Act 1994 and VAT Notice 703, provided the evidence of export is held within 3 months of the supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar SAD Form C88 / G-Export electronic declaration) with confirmed Goods Movement Reference
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing consignment to the international buyer and showing consignee, number of packages, weight and port of discharge
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or FX repatriation confirmation from an authorised Gibraltar bank verifying the receipt of export proceeds in a matched currency
  • Certificate of Origin (for preferential tariff claims under the UK-Gibraltar trade continuity agreements and in support of EU/SAA-eligible origin)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List with dual HS Code classification (heparin sodium API) and stated export value
  • Statement on Export Valuation (customs value declaration) confirming the transaction value of the fractionated heparin crude
  • Certificate of Analysis (CoA) and Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) certificate issued by the manufacturing facility, together with the European Directorate for the Quality of Medicines (EDQM) or US FDA reference documentation where applicable

No statutory real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping regime is currently enacted in Gibraltar for B2B/B2C invoices. The CITD does not mandate XML/EDI e-invoicing. Nevertheless, customs export declarations for heparin sodium must be transmitted electronically through the Gibraltar Systematic Export Control application (G-Export) using the Single Administrative Document (SAD, Form C88) format, and compliance with the UK-Gibraltar mutual recognition of the Goods Movement Reference (GMR) applies for movements to/from the UK, CE marking and controlled pharma watch-list alerts. Invoices issued to overseas customers are not subject to any clearing or Continuous Transaction Controls (CTC) requirement; however, accepted digital invoices must be retained in accordance with the CITD e-record keeping guidance for a minimum of 6 years.

Export zero-rating and duty exemption is subject to intensive post-clearance audit emphasis on (i) genuine movement of goods, (ii) timing of physical export, and (iii) matching of INR receipts. Fraudulent or negligent claims trigger: (a) in Gibraltar, civil assessment for lost duty plus interest from the date of export; default penalties under the Customs and Excise Ordinance of up to 200% of the duty evaded, and in the case of summary conviction a fine not exceeding £3,000 per count; (b) cancellation or suspension of the customs trader identifier and withdrawal of any customs bonding facilities; (c) retrospectively withdrawn zero-rating with reassessment of 12.5% Gibraltar corporation tax on deemed undeclared business income where receipts are not repatriated or remain undocumented; (d) criminal liability for false declarations and fraud by false representation under the Criminal Offences (Gibraltar) Act 2007, punishable by imprisonment up to 7 years; and (e) where the EU or UK VAT interest is involved, HMRC may issue assessments for up to 20 years on deliberate non-disclosure and impose civil penalties of 30% to 100% of the VAT lost. All penalties apply jointly and severally to the Cayman Exempted Company and its holding/operating officers in Gibraltar.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The UK and Gibraltar have no general exchange controls; both current and capital account transactions are freely convertible. Profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriation are unrestricted subject to anti-money laundering (AML) and sanctions screening. Legal obligations under sanctions regulations (e.g., UK Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018, Gibraltar Sanctions Regulations) may block transfers involving sanctioned counterparties. A Cayman exempted company is not subject to currency surrender requirements in Gibraltar.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds in Gibraltar. Export receipts may be retained in original currencies or freely converted. However, for pharmaceutical trade involving heparin sodium, the paying and receiving banks require supporting documentation (commercial invoice, bill of lading, customs declarations, and product licence) to satisfy transaction-monitoring and sanctions checks.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (ultimate monetary authority); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) acts as financial regulator and supervises banks; HM Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at a 1:1 peg to GBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-viability for offshore Cayman exempted companies. EU-licensed fintech/electronic money institutions (EMIs) generally decline non-resident offshore-incorporated entities, especially in absence of local economic substance. UK-authorised EMIs accept Gibraltar business only with strong beneficial ownership disclosure and a tactical operational presence. Legal constraints arise from UK and Gibraltar AML directives (including EMD and PSD2) requiring robust source-of-funds evidence; many digital EMIs consider a Cayman exempted company as a high-risk jurisdiction and refuse onboarding.

Legal constraints arise from UK and Gibraltar AML directives (including EMD and PSD2) requiring robust source-of-funds evidence; many digital EMIs consider a Cayman exempted company as a high-risk jurisdiction and refuse onboarding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where the aggregate annual value of controlled transactions with associated enterprises equals or exceeds GIP 1,000,000. Master File and Country-by-Country Report mandatory where the worldwide group has consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 (approximately GIP 680,000,000).

Economic substance scope: The Cayman Exempted Co remains within the scope of the Cayman Islands Economic Substance Law (ESL) if it is tax-resident in the Cayman Islands and carries on a relevant activity. For an active heparin fractionation business operated through a Gibraltar PE, the annual Cayman ES Return (Form ES) should assert either (i) 'relevant activity: distribution and service centre business' where the entity purchases and resells the crude heparin, or (ii) 'no relevant activity' where manufacturing is the sole function; in both cases the entity must evidence sufficient employees, premises and decision-making in Gibraltar for the PE, while the Cayman board must hold substantive meetings in the Cayman Islands. If central management and control are in fact exercised in Gibraltar, the company becomes Gibraltar tax-resident and must file Gibraltar returns on worldwide income, and the Cayman Form ES must report tax residence in Gibraltar supported by a GITO tax residence certificate.
