Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar Customs and Income Tax Department (CITD); corporate registry filings through Gibraltar Companies House; online filing and taxpayer portal at https://portal.citd.gov.gi

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company becomes liable to register with the CITD if it is managed and controlled from Gibraltar (statutory tax residence) or carries on a trade through a Gibraltar permanent establishment. Registration is effected by filing the Corporate Taxpayer Registration Form together with the Cayman certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association (certified), a Gibraltar registered-office address, and a local tax representative appointment letter when required. The CITD issues a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number that mirrors the Gibraltar Companies House registration number; non-resident companies deriving Gibraltar-source income must appoint a resident tax representative and obtain a tax reference before opening any operational bank account in Gibraltar. Registration must be completed within 31 days of commencing the relevant activity; late registration attracts fixed penalties of up to GBP 1,000 plus further daily penalties.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Cayman Exempted Company is tax-exempt in its home jurisdiction (Cayman Income Tax Act and Companies Act) and may preserve full exemption in Gibraltar only by qualifying under the Qualifying Companies (Tax Exemption) Regulations 2019, which requires that central management and control are not exercised in Gibraltar. If central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, the entity is reclassified as Entity_Level_Taxation: it becomes Gibraltar tax-resident under s.5 Income Tax Act 2010 and is taxed at the 12.5% headline rate on worldwide income, subject to territorial relief for overseas land and qualifying foreign-source profits. — A Cayman Exempted Company becomes liable to register with the CITD if it is managed and controlled from Gibraltar (statutory tax residence) or carries on a trade through a Gibraltar permanent establishment. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Cayman Exempted Company is tax-exempt in its home jurisdiction (Cayman Income Tax Act and Companies Act) and may preserve full exemption in Gibraltar only by qualifying under the Qualifying Companies (Tax Exemption) Regulations 2019, which requires that central management and control are not exercised in Gibraltar. If central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, the entity is reclassified as Entity_Level_Taxation: it becomes Gibraltar tax-resident under s.5 Income Tax Act 2010 and is taxed at the 12.5% headline rate on worldwide income, subject to territorial relief for overseas land and qualifying foreign-source profits.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box regime; entity classification is determined exclusively by the place of effective management test under s.5 Income Tax Act 2010, applied consistently with OECD/UK case principles. Available statutory elections and reliefs include: (i) Qualifying Company election under the Qualified Companies (Tax Exemption) Regulations 2019, conferring confirmed tax-exempt status on companies that are not tax-resident in Gibraltar; (ii) group relief election permitting surrender of losses within a 75% group under s.40 Income Tax Act; (iii) election to apply the transitional capital-allowances pooling rules for assets held before 1 January 2019; (iv) capital-gain deferral election on intra-group asset transfers; (v) hybrid mismatch alignment election to neutralise deductions in accordance with OECD BEPS Action 2 principles as transposed via the UK TIOPA 2010 Rules; and (vi) election to designate a representative member for joint filing where the entity forms part of a Gibraltar fiscal unity/grouping arrangement. All elections require prior CITD Revenue approval and must be submitted on Form CITD-E within the statutory return deadline.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar operates no VAT and no GST; indirect taxation is limited to import duty under the Customs Duties Ordinance 1964, excise duties on alcohol, tobacco and fuel, and a narrow Purchase Tax on specified goods. The territory is outside the EU VAT area and outside the EU Customs Union, with its own independent customs regime administered by the CITD Customs Division.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold exists because Gibraltar has no VAT. For customs purposes, there is no de minimis turnover threshold: every person or entity importing goods into Gibraltar, including the REIT's fixtures, fittings and capital equipment, must be registered with the CITD Customs Division as an importer/exporter and hold a Gibraltar EORI (Economic Operators Registration and Identification) number commencing with the prefix 'GI'. A Customs Agent licence is required where the entity does not lodge declarations itself.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating is not applicable because Gibraltar levies no VAT. Export relief is instead granted under the Customs Duties Ordinance 1964: goods physically exported from Gibraltar to third-country destinations are relieved of import duty and excise duty provided the exporter holds a customs export declaration accepted by GECS and can substantiate physical departure of the goods within the declaration validity window. Export of services — including offshore REIT management, property administration, and investor-dividend processing services to non-resident beneficiaries — falls outside the scope of Gibraltar import/excise duties entirely and is chargeable only to Gibraltar corporate income tax where the services are performed through a Gibraltar trading permanent establishment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Single Administrative Document lodged via GECS, endorsed with the unique movement reference number)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exportation and confirming the foreign destination port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or corresponding bank credit advice evidencing receipt of the foreign-sale proceeds into the entity's Gibraltar or Cayman bank account

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous controls (no SAF-T, no PEPPOL BIS, no VAT transaction controls). Tax invoices must satisfy only the minimum content requirements of the Commercial Information (Legislation) Ordinance and be retained for six years. Import and export declarations must be lodged electronically through the Gibraltar Electronic Customs System (GECS), with pre-arrival ICS2 datasets required for goods entering the territory; post-Brexit protocol applies routine customs supervision and authorised economic operator arrangements. The mandatory annual corporate tax return and qualifying-company renewal are filed exclusively through the CITD online portal using the entity's tax reference.

Fraudulent or erroneous export duty-relief claims expose the entity to: (i) criminal prosecution before the Magistrates' Court under the Customs Duties Ordinance 1964 (as amended) with fines of up to GBP 10,000 and imprisonment of up to two years; (ii) civil penalties of up to 200% of the duty evaded or amount falsely claimed; (iii) retrospective clawback of every export relief previously granted, with statutory interest charged at 8% per annum compounded; (iv) arrest of the goods and forfeiture of the export proceeds; (v) director disqualification orders under the Companies Act and adverse findings referenced to the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission for AML breaches; and (vi) public naming of the taxpayer on the CITD defaulters register. The CITD audit window is six years from the end of the relevant tax year, with no statutory time limit in cases of dishonesty or fraudulent misrepresentation.

10% (Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010; 20% top-up rate applies only to utility, telecommunication and fuel distribution licensees)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form G and supporting computations must be filed within 18 months after the end of the accounting period; the related tax liability falls due not later than the following 30 September, and interest and instalment rules apply to late payment.

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Gross trading and business income, including Gibraltar-source rental income from the REIT property portfolio. Box 3 Capital gains on disposal of shares, securities or real estate interests. Non-residents are generally outside the charge on share disposals; property gains must be reported and treaty relief claimed. Late filing attracts an automatic GIP 100 penalty, a GIP 50 per month continuing default penalty (maximum GIP 600), and a further GIP 1,000 penalty for a return outstanding more than six months after notice; inaccurate returns attract a penalty of up to 100% of the tax understated, plus interest at 5% per annum; persistent failure leads to estimated assessment and enforcement action.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 0 — no statutory de minimis threshold: all controlled transactions must be supported by contemporaneous arm's length documentation; the OECD Country-by-Country reporting threshold of EUR 750 million consolidated group revenue (approximately GIP 660 million) triggers the Master File and CbC report in Gibraltar.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance (Companies) Regulations 2020 apply to resident companies conducting relevant activities, including holding company, distribution and service centre, financing and leasing, and investment fund management; a pure equity-holding Cayman Exempted Company must satisfy the reduced substance test by maintaining a registered office, adequate board meetings and records in Gibraltar, and must file an annual economic substance return; remote control with all directors outside Gibraltar and no physical presence is a compliance failure attracting sanctions and potential recharacterization of profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Substantive Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 300% of the tax undercharged, fixed fine up to GIP 100,000, interest at 5% per annum, estimated assessments, and criminal fines up to GIP 500,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in or approve false returns are jointly and severally liable for the tax and penalty, and are exposed to imprisonment of up to 7 years, travel bans and disqualification orders under Gibraltar company law.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR penalty of GIP 10,000 fixed plus GIP 500 per day of continuing default; deregistration of the company; cancellation of tax residence certification; exchange of information to the Cayman Registrar.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines up to GIP 50,000, exclusion from management positions, and civil confiscation of benefits; the corporate veil is pierced for sham arrangements.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Criminal fine up to GIP 500,000 or twice the value of the gain, and imprisonment up to 5 years under the Proceeds of Crime Act and the Beneficial Ownership Act; freezing orders may be issued.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated compliance officer are personally liable; restriction and seizure of assets, travel bans, and custodial sentences of up to 10 years for money laundering are available.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment with interest at 3% above the Gibraltar base rate, penalty of up to 100% of the resulting under-declared tax, and a fixed GIP 50,000 penalty for non-production of documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false TP computations are subject to civil restitution in the amount of the tax and penalties, disqualification for up to 15 years, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent tax evasion.
