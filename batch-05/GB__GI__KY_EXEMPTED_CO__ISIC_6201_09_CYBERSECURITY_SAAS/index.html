Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum authorized or paid-up capital for a Cayman exempted company; model constitutional provision is US$50,000 authorized capital divided into 50,000 ordinary shares of US$1.00 each, with no escrow or paid-up deposit imposed by the Registrar. Gibraltar external registration likewise applies no minimum capital condition.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate and annual inspection for the Gibraltar registered office under the Fire Safety (Commercial Premises) Regulations; structural load notification to the Building Control Office for any server rack or equipment room; employer's liability insurance and a workplace risk assessment under the Health and Safety at Work Act (Gibraltar) for local staff.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax) — official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation-and-tax-allowances; e-filing portal: https://e-tax.gibraltar.gov.gi

E-government filing portal

1) Test Gibraltar tax residence: a Cayman Exempted Company is Gibraltar-resident only if central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar or it is incorporated/continued in Gibraltar. 2) If a physical place of business is established, register the Cayman entity as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry (Companies Act, Cap. 100). 3) Submit the corporate tax registration application to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office with certified Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, memorandum and articles, Certificate of Good Standing (dated within 3 months), director/shareholder registers, Gibraltar registered office details, and appointment of a Gibraltar tax representative. 4) Obtain the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) in approximately 5–10 working days. 5) Thereafter file the annual Gibraltar company income tax return (Form IT), the Economic Substance Return under the Economic Substance (Companies) Act 2019, and renew the Cayman Tax Exemption Certificate (TEC) annually with the Cayman Registrar of Companies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — Cayman Exempted Company holding a Tax Exemption Certificate (TEC) under section 6 of the Cayman Tax Concessions Act: no Cayman income/capital tax applies; no Gibraltar VAT/GST applies; Gibraltar corporate income tax at 12.5% is triggered only for Gibraltar-source income or if the entity is managed and controlled in Gibraltar.

Available election forms

Cayman TEC election granting a 20-year exemption (renewable up to 30 years); Gibraltar has no check-the-box election — entity taxation follows statutory residence; optional election to apply the UK–Gibraltar Double Taxation Arrangement (2019) via HMRC/Commissioner claim to avoid double taxation of SaaS fees; annual Economic Substance Return classification must elect the relevant activity category (e.g., 'distribution and service centre' — not 'pure equity holding company').

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax in Gibraltar; Gibraltar sits outside the EU VAT area and its indirect impositions are limited to customs/excise duties and stamp duty. Cross-border SaaS cybersecurity subscriptions are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in Gibraltar — no VAT registration threshold or registration mechanism exists for digital services. Once customers are in the UK: mandatory UK VAT registration arises for B2C digital supplies exceeding £10,000 per annum (reverse charge for B2B customers, no registration required). For EU customers: because Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area, the supplier must register via EU OSS from the first euro of B2C supplies; B2B customers self-account under reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — Gibraltar levies no VAT, therefore there is no 0% export VAT rate, no export zero-rating claim, and no VAT refund mechanism for SaaS exports. Digital service exports from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax; any UK/EU VAT chargeable at destination is accounted for by the customer (B2B reverse charge) or via UK VAT/OSS registration, with corresponding input VAT recovery in the relevant foreign VAT filing.

  • Gibraltar Corporate Income Tax Return (Form IT) and Economic Substance Return demonstrating territorial-source computation and nil Gibraltar indirect tax charge
  • Cayman Tax Exemption Certificate (TEC) with annual Cayman registration fee receipts evidencing the entity-level exempt status
  • Export revenue substantiation file: signed SaaS subscription contracts, invoicing ledger, and payment settlement records (Stripe/wire transfers with inward remittance advices)
  • Place-of-supply evidence: customer VAT identification numbers (where held), IP/API access logs, and service-rendering verification records

Gibraltar imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, API integration, e-invoicing mandate, or digital stamping requirement; invoices must be retained for 6 years. When exporting into EU member states, customer-side e-invoicing frameworks (Italian SDI, Polish KSeF, French Factur-X) may apply to the Gibraltar supplier where the local reverse-charge or OSS records are submitted electronically.

Gibraltar Income Tax Act penalties: late filing fines up to £5,000 plus daily defaults up to £500/day; negligent/deliberate under-declaration attracts up to 100% of the tax undercharged plus statutory interest; tax fraud is a criminal offence carrying fines and imprisonment of up to 7 years. Retrospective reassessment may extend up to 20 years in cases of fraud or abusive arrangements (Gibraltar has no general statute of limitation bar in such cases). Artificial reliance on the Cayman TEC, diversion of Gibraltar management-and-control, or defective Economic Substance filings trigger clawback of concessions, loss of the TEC benefit for Gibraltar activities, and HMRC enquiry under the UK–Gibraltar DTA exchange-of-information provisions (with penalty exposure for facilitating tax evasion).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The UK, Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands each maintain full current and capital account convertibility; there are no exchange control permissions for SaaS subscription receipts, profit remittances or intercompany equity injections. GBP and USD are fully convertible Article VIII currencies; GIP is pegged at 1:1 to GBP and KYD at 1.20 to USD. Profit remittance hurdles are documentary only: annual Cayman Economic Substance Return (Form ES), an active and compliant Cayman registered agent record, and, where the Cayman entity maintains a Gibraltar office to evidence substance, registration as an overseas company with Companies House Gibraltar. Dividends paid by a Gibraltar-resident company carry no Gibraltar withholding tax; a Cayman Exempted Company which is managed and controlled outside Gibraltar is not Gibraltar tax resident and faces no Gibraltar tax on foreign-source revenue.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio apply in the UK, Gibraltar or the Cayman Islands. Export receipts from cloud vulnerability scanning and penetration-testing SaaS subscriptions (ISIC 6201) may be retained indefinitely in any convertible currency in a Gibraltar or UK bank account. Incidental physical exports of security appliances attract only statistical reporting (UK Customs Declaration Service export declarations, and Gibraltar trade-in-goods declarations for movements between Gibraltar and the EU/UK); none of these regimes imposes FX conversion or repatriation deadlines.

Central bank supervision: Monetary authority for the United Kingdom: Bank of England. Gibraltar has no separate central bank; the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is legal tender issued by the Government of Gibraltar and pegged at par to GBP, while the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) supervises banking and FX conduct. Home-jurisdiction supervisor for the Cayman Exempted Company: Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The UK parent executes a CHAPS transfer in GBP to its UK account, followed by a SWIFT MT103 credit to the Cayman Exempted Company's Gibraltar operational account at Banco Santander Totta (or Trusted Novus where the escrow account is opened). The wire narrative must cite the Bank of England Balance of Payments purpose code for outward direct investment equity (or outward intercompany lending if structured as a shareholder loan) and the reference 'EQUITY SUBSCRIPTION - CAYMAN EXEMPTED CO KY_EXEMPTED_CO'. The onboarding bank requires the UK parent board resolution, Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, executed share allotment, Cayman Form ES acknowledgment, and the Gibraltar overseas-company registration where a Gibraltar place of business is established. No central bank or CIMA pre-approval is required for equity injection into a non-regulated Cayman Exempted Company; funds are credited to the operational account upon matching of purpose code and reference narrative.Operating payables are predominantly digital and settle via: (i) SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for USD and EUR B2B invoices (cloud infrastructure, cybersecurity research subcontractors, legal and compliance services); (ii) SEPA Credit Transfers for all EUR-denominated payees inside the SEPA zone; (iii) UK Faster Payments and CHAPS for domestic GBP payroll, tax and supplier payments; and (iv) Wise Business or Currencycloud batch APIs for contractor and micro-vendor disbursements below GBP 10,000. A commercial documentary Letter of Credit (UCP 600, MT700) is used only for bundled hardware deliveries such as on-premise vulnerability scanning appliances, supported by commercial invoice, packing list and clean transport document. Recurring USD outflows to cloud providers are paid from a segregated USD operational account using host-to-host ISO 20022 pain.001/pain.002 file transmissions to eliminate unnecessary spot FX conversion on each disbursement.Deliverable FX forward outrights (FXA) on USD/GBP, EUR/GBP and USD/EUR with tenors up to 12 months, documented under an ISDA 1992 Master Agreement or a bank-standard FX Master Agreement plus credit support annex.Rolling extendable FX forwards permitting early delivery, extension or cancellation of the forward within a defined reference period.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCS) on USD/GBP and EUR/GBP for intercompany loans or financed security-appliance capital expenditure, with notional tenors up to five years and GBP/USD cash collateral.Vanilla European-style FX options (puts and calls) on USD/GBP and EUR/GBP, including zero-cost collar structures, maximum 12-month tenor, premium settled in GBP.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are assessed as unnecessary and unavailable for this treasury profile because GBP, EUR and USD are fully deliverable and because GIP and KYD are pegged currencies with no meaningful NDF market.

Economic Substance Notification (ESN) Within 12 months after the end of the financial year; for a calendar-year reporting period ending 31 December, the ESN must be filed no later than 31 December of the following calendar year. $0 USD Failure to file the ESN carries a financial penalty of KYD 12,000 (USD 14,634); deliberate or persistent non-compliance may result in enhanced administrative penalties up to KYD 50,000 (USD 60,976) and criminal liability for officers.
Beneficial Ownership Register Annual Confirmation (BOR Confirmation) Within 60 days after each anniversary of the company's incorporation; the confirmation must attest that the Beneficial Ownership Register on file is accurate and updated, including any changes caused by share transfers. $0 USD Failure to file or filing an inaccurate confirmation is punishable by a fine of KYD 10,000 (USD 12,195), potential forfeiture of corporate benefits, and grounds for strike-off if the default persists.
