Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar, 1-2 Parliament Lane, Gibraltar; official departmental portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/departments/income-tax

E-government filing portal

A Cayman exempted company that is not incorporated in Gibraltar must obtain a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (GTIN) only when it has a Gibraltarian filing obligation, branch registration, or opens a Gibraltar bank/custodian account requiring CRS/FATCA tax-residence evidence. The procedure is: (1) register any trade-carrying presence at the Gibraltar Companies Registry under the Companies (Foreign Companies) Regulations 2014; (2) submit to the Income Tax Office a completed Form TIN-1 executed by a director, accompanied by certified memoranda and articles of association of the Cayman entity, the Cayman certificate of incorporation, the Cayman tax-exemption certificate, a certificate of good standing issued within the last three months, and the Gibraltar-registered branch certificate (where applicable); (3) the Income Tax Office verifies the applicant's status, issues a GTIN, and where central management and control is outside Gibraltar, issues a Notice of Non-Resident Status; (4) in the absence of any Gibraltar taxable presence or regulated account, no GTIN is mandatory, but a Cayman Tax Information Authority TIN should be furnished for global FATCA/CRS reporting; (5) the standard processing window is 5 to 15 business days, subject to KYB/KYC vetting.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box election and has no hybrid transparent filing for foreign corporations. A Cayman exempted company is, by default, a non-resident foreign entity taxed in Gibraltar on a territorial basis, meaning only income sourced within Gibraltar is chargeable to income tax. If central management and control is exercised from Gibraltar, the entity becomes Gibraltarian tax-resident and is then subject to entity-level corporate tax at the standard rate of 12.5% on worldwide profits. No elective under Gibraltar law permits the company to claim Cayman-style exemption; a 'qualifying company' designation is available only for non-resident controlled investment-holding entities on prior written application to the Commissioner, and it is not available for active wholesale trading in semiconductors. UK income tax and check-the-box classifications are irrelevant to Gibraltar, but for UK corporate tax purposes the Cayman entity would remain an non-transparent company unless a UK domestic election were made under CTA 2010 rules, which do not apply to non-UK resident companies not trading in the UK.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/general sales and use tax in Gibraltar; Gibraltar imposes only narrow customs, excise, import and stamp duties, with no broad-based consumption tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 0 - there is no turnover threshold for indirect tax registration in Gibraltar because no VAT/GST exists. Any person importing or exporting physical goods must nevertheless obtain a Gibraltar Customs Trader Registration number (customs identifier) through HM Customs Gibraltar; no economic turnover conditions apply. The United Kingdom VAT registration threshold (GBP 85,000) is not applicable because Gibraltar is outside the UK VAT territory and does not fall within VATA 1994 for VAT equality purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

In Gibraltar the export of SSD and CPU chips is outside the scope of any Gibraltar VAT/GST, so there is no zero-rating claim to make. Where specific goods were imported into Gibraltar and duty was paid at importation, re-export may qualify for customs-drawback treatment under the Customs Duty Ordinance if the identity and export of the goods are evidenced and application is made within the statutory limitation period. If the Cayman entity later registers for UK VAT, UK zero-rating under VATA 1994, section 30 may be available on exports of qualifying goods physically removed from the United Kingdom to a third country, provided the supplier holds valid commercial and transport evidence, the goods leave within three months of the supply, and sale proceeds are received within the six-month export-payment window.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/electronic export entry lodged through the UK/Gibraltar customs single window)
  • Bill of Lading or House/Master Air Waybill evidencing dispatch to the foreign buyer
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List identifying type, quantity, value and tariff classification of the SSD/CPU chips
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank Swift/TT payment advice confirming receipt of sale proceeds
  • Certificate of Origin where a preferential origin or tariff treatment is claimed
  • Delivery Confirmation or Port/Container Tracking Evidence showing goods left Gibraltar customs territory

None. Gibraltar has not implemented real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing licences, digital stamping, or an API-based tax-return interface. Electronic customs declarations must be filed through the Gibraltar electronic customs gateway / UK Single Window infrastructure for goods entering or leaving the jurisdiction; this is a customs-compliance requirement, not an indirect-tax clearance mandate, and commercial invoices need not be digitally signed or fiscally sealed.

Under UK VAT, false zero-rating claims attract retrospective HMRC assessment up to four years, clawback of all input VAT previously recovered, a penalty of up to 100% of the VAT evaded for deliberate or concealed conduct, and criminal prosecution with up to seven years' imprisonment for fraudulent evasion. In Gibraltar, false customs declarations and forged export evidence are dealt with under the Customs Duties Ordinance and Criminal Code; penalties include forfeiture of the goods, customs fines of up to twice the value of the goods or three times the duty liable, civil restitution orders, and directors' personal criminal liability in cases of fraudulent misrepresentation or money laundering.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates a sterling-pegged currency board system with unrestricted current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no exchange control laws, no prior-approval requirements for profit remittances, and no restrictions on holding, transferring, or converting foreign currency within the banking system. Practical remittance hurdles are limited to bank-level AML/source-of-funds checks, sanctions screening, and proof of beneficial ownership. A Gibraltar exempt company must maintain its qualifying status and avoid local trading activities to preserve its tax-exempt profile; profit repatriation to a Cayman parent is otherwise freely permissible via dividend, loan repayment, or liquidation distribution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements or mandated conversion ratios. Export receipts arising from the wholesale distribution of SSDs and server processors may be retained in foreign currency or converted at the corporate treasury's discretion, subject only to the reporting and record-keeping obligations of the receiving bank under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and GFSC AML/CFT rules.

Central bank supervision: HM Government of Gibraltar (Currency Notes Act 1934) and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC); monetary policy and sterling liquidity are effectively delegated to the Bank of England, with the Gibraltar pound pegged at 1:1 to GBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium. Gibraltar-incorporated companies are generally outside the automatic client-eligibility scope of UK and EU digital EMI platforms post-Brexit. Offshore digital EMIs typically reject or heavily restrict Gibraltar tax-optimised structures unless there is clear local substance, a demonstrable trade rationale, and a physical or local registered office. GFSC-regulated Gibraltar EMIs are viable but form a limited market; unregulated offshore payment facilitators must be avoided because they fail safeguarding, sanctions, and audit-trace requirements. For this Cayman-owned Gibraltar wholesale vehicle, a digitally opened EMI is feasible only if licensed in Gibraltar and only after full corporate KYC, UBO identification, and a board resolution approving account usage.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cayman exempted company as ultimate beneficial owner and tax-haven nexus

Root cause: Cayman exempted company as ultimate beneficial owner and tax-haven nexus

Defense evidence: Provide an unbroken legal entity chart, Cayman certificate of incorporation, certificate of good standing, register of directors and members or certified UBO declaration, audited or management accounts of the Cayman parent, and a written commercial substance memorandum explaining why the Gibraltar company exists as the European trading and treasury hub. Include local office lease, employment or outsourced management agreements, and future board-meeting minutes evidencing active commercial decision-making in Gibraltar.
Wholesale semiconductor and SSD flows involving multi-jurisdiction counterparties and third-party escrow payments

Root cause: Wholesale semiconductor and SSD flows involving multi-jurisdiction counterparties and third-party escrow payments

Defense evidence: Maintain a complete trade-file per transaction: signed purchase and sale contracts, pro-forma and final commercial invoices, packing lists, shipping bills of lading or airway bills, customs declarations (EORI and Intrastat where applicable), inspection certificates, end-user statements for restricted server processors, and a reconciliation of each invoice to the corresponding incoming and outgoing bank transfer. Provide pre-vetted counterparty KYC files and bank references for all recurring suppliers and customers.
Tax-exempt or zero-tax jurisdiction status of the Gibraltar company

Root cause: Tax-exempt or zero-tax jurisdiction status of the Gibraltar company

Defense evidence: Submit the Gibraltar qualifying/exempt company certificate or a tax-residence and tax-status letter from Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar, together with an Economic Substance return or demonstrable compliance narrative. Show payroll, corporate-services invoices, local bank statements, and board minutes proving the company is not dormant or a pure shell. If activities are genuinely outsourced, provide the licensed management company's credentials and a formal services agreement describing day-to-day treasury, logistics, and compliance controls.
Large inbound capital injections from the Cayman parent combined with rapid outbound disbursements to suppliers

Root cause: Large inbound capital injections from the Cayman parent combined with rapid outbound disbursements to suppliers

Defense evidence: Prepare a comprehensive source-of-funds package: audited Cayman financial statements, board resolutions authorising each equity injection or shareholder loan, executed loan agreements, treasury cash-flow forecasts matching inventory purchases with confirmed customer orders, and corresponding bank transaction statements. Use consistent remittance reference codes such as 'CAPITAL INJ', 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN', or 'TRADE SETTLEMENT' to allow the bank to map the end-to-end circular flow and verify commercial logic.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from the Cayman parent to the Gibraltar company should be executed via SWIFT MT103 with full remitter and beneficiary details, using the company's GI IBAN and the bank's BIC. Recommended wire field 70 narratives: for equity, 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION — CAYMAN INJ'; for shareholder loans, 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN — INTERCOMPANY'. The sending Cayman bank must apply target-country, sanctions, and source-of-funds screening; the receiving Gibraltar bank will validate the IBAN/BIC, confirm the legal entity's name matches the account, and may require a board resolution from the Cayman entity and the Gibraltar company before crediting. For amounts exceeding agreed thresholds, obtain a pre-advice from the receiving bank and provide supporting corporate authorisations to minimise holds.
