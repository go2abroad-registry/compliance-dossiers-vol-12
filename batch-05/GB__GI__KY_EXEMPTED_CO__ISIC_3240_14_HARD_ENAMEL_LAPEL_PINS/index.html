Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax), Gibraltar; e-filing portal via www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation; customs and export entries filed through the Gibraltar Customs Department Trade Interface. For any separately triggered UK VAT exposure, the competent authority is Her Majesty's Revenue and Customs (HMRC) via its MTD API and EORI systems.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the Cayman Exempted Company constitutional pack (Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles, Certificate of Good Standing) with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the External Companies Act, appointing a Gibraltar service provider as registered office. Step 2: Submit the non-resident/external company registration particulars (directors, shareholders, Etsy trading activities, and physical premises) to the Income Tax Office, which issues the Gibraltar Tax File Number (TFN/IT number) for corporate income tax self-assessment. Step 3: Obtain a Gibraltar Customs Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number with prefix GIB for customs export declarations. Step 4: If the entity later sells into the UK through its own channel, register under HMRC's Non-Established Taxable Person (NETP) route without a threshold and obtain a GB VAT registration number; no separate Gibraltar VAT ID is available because Gibraltar is outside the UK VAT area.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Cayman or Gibraltar law. For US federal tax purposes, the wholly owned Cayman Exempted Company may file Form 8832 to elect disregarded-entity or corporate status. In Gibraltar, elections are limited to reliefs under the Income Tax Act 2010 (group relief, share reorganisations, roll-over relief) with no hybrid entity classification; where the entity is a CFC of a UK parent, the UK CFC 'excluded activities' or trading profits low-profit condition must be certified. A Gibraltar 'qualifying company' tax-exemption election is no longer available; all companies are charged at the flat 10% rate on Gibraltar-source profits.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar: no VAT/GST or general sales tax (outside EU VAT territory); supplies are 'outside the scope' and subject only to limited Gibraltar import/excise duties. UK VAT (VATA 1994, standard rate 20%) attaches solely as a destination-based import/reverse charge tax when goods enter Great Britain or Northern Ireland; online marketplace deemed-supplier rules apply for consignments valued at or below GBP 135.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: nil threshold and no consumption-tax registration requirement; only the corporate tax TFN and customs EORI are needed. UK: GBP 90,000 taxable turnover threshold for UK-established VAT registration; for non-established overseas sellers there is no threshold and registration is due before the first taxable supply; for goods in consignments up to GBP 135 sold through Etsy to UK consumers, Etsy is the deemed supplier and the seller does not require UK VAT registration on those sales; postponed import VAT accounting (PVA) applies to imports above this level.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar-origin export supplies are not subject to VAT and therefore need no technical zero-rating; they are simply outside the scope of Gibraltar and UK VAT, with no refund claim required on the Gibraltarian leg. UK VAT zero-rating at 0% under VATA 1994 s.30 and Schedule 4 applies only if the Exempted Co holds UK stock and dispatches hard-enamel lapel pins to customers outside the UK, the Isle of Man, and the EU, provided the goods physically leave Great Britain/Northern Ireland within 3 months of supply and the prescribed export evidence is held. Where the sale is made through Etsy/Stripe into the UK, zero-rating is unavailable because the deemed-supplier rules treat the supply as made in the UK at the point of sale; supporting evidence and any reclaim are effected through the UK VAT return (Box 4 and Box 7) rather than a Gibraltar refund mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar SAD/C88 or UK Customs Declaration Service entry) showing HS tariff 9617.10 and ISIC 3240 classification for die-struck brass cloisonné lapel pins
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill endorsed to the non-UK consignee with named port/terminal of outward departure
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT/MT103 credit record) matching the commercial invoice value
  • Commercial invoice and packing list stating unit quantities, Incoterms 2020, ex-works value, enamel pin composition, and destination
  • Proof of final destination (Etsy/Stripe order log, DDP customs clearance receipt, or written non-UK consumer instruction)
  • Where UK zero-rating is claimed: certified shipment reference (C&E 132), booking receipt, and evidence of consignee non-UK status
  • Gibraltar EORI and UK EORI (if applicable) exporter identification recorded on the transport document

Gibraltar has no real-time electronic invoice clearance, digital stamping, or XML fiscal API mandate; compliance is documentary, requiring SAD/C88 export declarations and commercial invoices. If UK VAT registration is held, HMRC 'Making Tax Digital' requires VAT records to be kept digitally and submitted via functional-compatible software through the HMRC MTD VAT API; no live fiscal clearance or stamping exists in either territory.

HMRC may deny or retrospectively claw back zero-rated export VAT on missing, defective, or fraudulent evidence, assessing under VATA 1994 s.73 with penalties up to 100% of the VAT amount under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 for careless or deliberate errors, plus default surcharge and interest. Fraudulent export claims attract criminal sanctions under CEMA 1979, joint-and-several liability for directors/officers, and unlimited time limits for dishonest conduct. Gibraltar law imposes additional tax of up to 100% for omission or understatement linked to the export trade and can suspend customs approval or seize the covered goods. Records, including the inward remittance certificate, must be retained for at least 6 years, but both Gibraltar and UK authorities may extend the audit window without limit where evasion is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility exists across the United Kingdom, Gibraltar, and the Cayman Islands. GIP is freely convertible into GBP at par, and USD, EUR, GBP, and KYD are unrestricted. No exchange-control permissions, surrender requirements, or retention limits apply to commercial trading receipts. Profit remittance is friction-free: Gibraltar levies no withholding tax on dividends or branch remittances to Cayman, and Cayman imposes no tax on inbound distributions. A Cayman Exempted Company carrying on trade through Gibraltar must nevertheless maintain genuine economic substance in Gibraltar (via the International Tax Compliance (Economic Substance) Regulations) to avoid adverse reporting and to prevent re-characterisation of profits as UK-source trading income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Neither the United Kingdom, Gibraltar, nor the Cayman Islands imposes a statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export proceeds. Etsy and Stripe settlement inflows may be retained in the invoicing currency (GBP, USD, or EUR) or converted at the operator's discretion. Standard AML/CTF record-keeping obligations (5 years) apply to all FX conversion and cross-border receipts under GFSC guidance.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as banking supervisor and financial regulator; Bank of England (BoE) as the sovereign monetary authority for GBP; Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) as the home-state regulator of the Cayman Exempted Company. Gibraltar does not operate an independent central bank; the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued by the Government of Gibraltar at a fixed 1:1 peg to GBP.
