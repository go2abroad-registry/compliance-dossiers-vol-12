Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Department of Tax & Customs (DTC), official portal tax.gov.gi; connected single window for customs is Gibraltar Customs at HM Customs, Waterport, Gibraltar

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities (including Cayman Exempted Companies) must apply to the DTC for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) and a Customs Economic Operator Identifier (EOI) using Form TRN-501/EOI-9. The application requires a certified copy of the Certificate of Incorporation and Certificate of Good Standing, CIPC or Cayman Registry extract, constitutional documents, a completed Beneficial Ownership Review under the Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Regulations 2017, a notarised board resolution confirming the registered office and Gibraltar Principal Place of Business (PPB), powered letters of authority for directors. Submission is made electronically via the DTC Customer Portal (sopra financial system) followed by hard-copy authentication. Issuance of the TRN is generally completed within 15-25 business days without an advance ruling. A non-resident Cayman Exempted Company with a Gibraltar PPB or permanent establishment must also file a non-resident company registration with Form P92. The annual compliance package confirms the TRN, tax grouping status, and qualifies for territorial tax assessments.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not have an elective check-the-box classification; foreign entity classification is determined factually under the Income Tax Act 2010 (ss. 7-10) and the Companies Act 2014. Available mechanisms include (a) s. 72 election for loss surrender in a qualifying Gibraltar group; (b) continuation of the Cayman company into Gibraltar as a Gibraltar company under Part XIV of the Companies Act 2014, which creates Gibraltar tax residence; (c) election under the Gibraltar Taxation (International Co-operation) Act to align hybrid entity treatment with the constituent-parent OECD BEPS standards, thereby avoiding hybrid mismatch deductions; and (d) voluntary submission to the DTC's advanced confirmation, binding for two years, that the entity's central management and control (COMI) remains in Cayman and therefore no Gibraltar source income is assessed unless a Gibraltar permanent establishment arises.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Customs and Excise Duty system (no VAT/GST; import and excise duties only)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 0.00; no consumption tax registration threshold is applicable as Gibraltar levies no VAT/GST. However, any person importing or exporting goods must hold a Customs Economic Operator ID and EORI; manufacturers of excisable goods require the manufacturing licence under the Excise Duties Act. Business turnover does not create a registration threshold, but customs signature authority requires a financial guarantee of up to 20% of annual import value.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST, the term 'zero-rating' technically does not apply; the substantiation criteria for obtaining a 0% customs duty rate upon export of medical examination gloves are: (i) the gloves are classified under HS 4015.12 (synthetic rubber) or HS 3926.20 (disposable PPE); (ii) the product is certified as a sterile medical device Class I under the Gibraltar Medical Devices Regulations; (iii) the goods physically depart Gibraltar by sea or air within 28 days of the stated export date; (iv) a valid Certificate of Origin (EUR.1 or ATR) is presented where preference is claimed; and (v) the export sale is evidenced by a complete contractual chain with FOB/CIF documentary obligations. Purchases that are exported under inward processing relief (IPR) also qualify, provided the raw nitrile latex was imported and the finished gloves exported without authorised destruction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Form C88/SAD) bearing the signed electronic signature of the clearing agent and the export reference number
  • Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill or Air Waybill matched to the declared gross weight and number of packages
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 with the BIC of the receiving bank confirming credit within 180 days)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List certified by the exporter and importer
  • Certificate of Origin / Eur.1 or GSP Form A (when the destination state accords preferences)
  • Sterility certificate and Batch Certificate conformance with ISO 13485

Gibraltar does not mandate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system. Invoices may be issued in paper or PDF, provided they satisfy the GST-1 (Anti-Money Laundering) record retention rules. Customs declarations are filed electronically through the Gibraltar Customs Digital System (GDS), using the CHIEF migration engine and a completed C88/SAD form or with a GD-Ex/EORI link for environmental goods. There is no continuous VAT API clearance; risk-targeted datasets are transmitted to the DTC ERP via a secure SFTP on a nightly cycle; a blockchain-based 'GI-TRACE' pilot applies for high-risk medical textiles from January 2025.

The DTC may open a customs/income duty audit under section 64 of the Customs & Excise Act 1990 for 6.5 years after the export declaration. If export evidence is missing, the low-risk rate is withdrawn and the goods become liable to import duty plus 50% clawback. When fraudulent zero-rated paperwork is used, the penalty regime provides for an amount equal to three times the duty evaded, or GIP 500,000, whichever is greater. Continuing non-compliance can trigger a management-licence revocation under medical device statutory controls, disqualification of directors for up to 15 years, and publication of criminal consequences under the Corporations (Criminal Liability) Order. The 180-day FX benchmark is monitored by the DTC against the central banking system; receipts not credited within 180 days are prima facie treated as a deemed distribution and taxed at 10% unless the exporter can evidence force majeure or without-fault foreign exchange denial.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar is a British Overseas Territory in sterling monetary union; there are no exchange controls under the Currency Act (Cap 62) and no restrictions on current-account conversion, capital-account repatriation, or profit remittance for non-resident shareholders. A Cayman exempted company may freely convert GBP, USD, and EUR through Gibraltar's banking system without central bank approval; the only practical hurdle is documentary evidence of economic substance and beneficial ownership transparency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement. Gibraltar does not impose mandatory repatriation, retention timelines, or conversion ratios on export receipts. The Cayman exempted company may retain export proceeds indefinitely in any foreign-currency account held with a Gibraltar bank, subject only to the bank's internal AML risk rating and documentation of commercial purpose.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) supervises all licensed banks, electronic money institutions, and payment service providers; the Bank of England is the currency authority responsible for GBP issuance. The Cayman exempted company is not a regulated Gibraltar entity, but the GFSC is the competent authority for any Gibraltar-based treasury management office, intermediary, or EMI arrangement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: B+ (conditional). Gibraltar licenses EMIs under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2020. A Cayman exempted company may open an EMI account with a licensed Gibraltar EMI often without physical branch presence, but the EMI must be GFSC-licensed and is generally not permitted to provide interbank clearing or commercial LC services. Offshore digital EMIs incorporated outside Gibraltar are not passportable into Gibraltar for core banking; they may serve only as collection rails, not as tier-1 treasury settlement counterparties.
