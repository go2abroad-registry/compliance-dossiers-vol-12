Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar — Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax); official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation. Customs and import-duty matters: HM Customs Gibraltar, Gibraltar Border and Coastguard Agency; DG port control: Gibraltar Port Authority.

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company establishing a branch, agency, or place of effective management in Gibraltar must apply in writing to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (GRN) using the corporate registration form prescribed under the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended). The filing package must include: (i) authenticated extract of the Cayman Companies Register showing incorporation and exempted status; (ii) Memorandum and Articles of Association; (iii) details of directors, shareholders, and ultimate beneficial owners for AML/beneficial-ownership registration with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies; (iv) evidence of a Gibraltar registered office and licensed agent; (v) substance documentation (lease, employment records, Gibraltar bank account where a PE is claimed); and (vi) a declaration of the place of central management and control. There is no VAT identification number because Gibraltar does not operate a VAT/GST regime. Non-resident companies are assessed on Gibraltar-source profits only; the Income Tax Office issues a tax-status determination on the application.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope: non-resident Cayman Exempted Company taxable in Gibraltar only on profits arising in or derived from Gibraltar (source-based taxation); worldwide profits are not subject to Gibraltar corporation tax unless central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, in which case residence-based taxation applies. The entity remains tax-exempt in the Cayman Islands concurrently.

Available election forms

(i) Election to be treated as tax-resident in Gibraltar where central management and control is exercised there; (ii) election of basis-period dates via the annual tax status return; (iii) allocation of profits between a Gibraltar permanent establishment and the foreign head office under the Gibraltar Transfer Pricing Rules 2019 adopting the Authorised OECD Approach; (iv) application of the 'locally generated profits' regime requiring satisfaction of the six statutory substance conditions (employees, premises, management decisions, records, bank account and tax returns); (v) there is no check-the-box election in Gibraltar law, but a Cayman Exempted Company may elect in its Cayman filing to be treated as tax-transparent for Cayman purposes while remaining subject to Gibraltar territorial assessment; (vi) Pillar 2 global minimum tax election and safe-harbour reporting elections for in-scope MNE groups under the GloBE implementation in Gibraltar.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax: Gibraltar levies no general indirect tax on goods or services. Indirect taxation is limited to customs import duties and excise duties on selected goods under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance, plus port and dangerous-goods safety charges imposed by the Gibraltar Port Authority.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT or GST registration threshold exists in Gibraltar. A DG freight forwarder acting as customs declarant for dutiable imports must register with HM Customs Gibraltar for a declarant code regardless of turnover; no de minimis exemption is available for licensed DG operators.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — with no VAT/GST in Gibraltar, there is no export zero-rating relief and no input VAT refund mechanism. Tax neutrality for cross-border DG freight-forwarding services is instead achieved through the territoriality principle: profits derived from services performed wholly outside Gibraltar, from carriage between non-Gibraltar ports, or from contracts concluded and executed abroad by a non-resident Cayman company without a Gibraltar PE are not within the charge to Gibraltar income tax. Where a Gibraltar PE exists, attributable profits are computed under the separate-enterprise principle with a commensurate arm's-length return, and no indirect-tax refund substantiation is required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar electronic customs entry, C88/SAD format where UK or EU movement applies)
  • Bill of Lading or House Air Waybill evidencing export of dangerous goods
  • Multimodal Dangerous Goods Declaration (IMDG/IATA DGR compliant, signed by competent consignor)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT bank confirmation evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Certificate of Origin where preferential tariff treatment is claimed
  • Gibraltar Port Authority movement permit / port marine licence for hazardous cargo
  • Cargo insurance policy or broker cover note for DG marine/aviation liability

Gibraltar does not impose real-time fiscal e-invoicing clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping. Customs declarations are filed electronically through the Gibraltar Customs electronic clearance system and, where goods move to or from the United Kingdom under the UK-Gibraltar trade arrangements, through the UK Customs Declaration Service. Dangerous goods consignments must also be declared electronically to the Gibraltar Port Authority in accordance with the IMDG Code and IATA DGR, including pre-arrival hazard notification in the port community system.

No VAT zero-rating clawback is possible. However, HM Customs Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office jointly audit DG freight forwarders for customs fraud, undeclared hazardous cargo, and artificial profit diversion. Consequences include: (i) retrospective customs duty assessment of up to 100% of evaded duty plus penalty interest; (ii) monetary penalties under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance and forfeiture of goods; (iii) tax-geared penalties of up to 100% of underpaid corporation tax plus late-payment interest at 10% per annum for false returns or fraudulent statements under the Income Tax Act 2010; (iv) retrospective recharacterisation of profits and disallowance of foreign tax credits where the Gibraltar PE lacks economic substance; (v) suspension or revocation of the DG forwarding licence and port access rights; and (vi) criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion of duty or tax, carrying fines and imprisonment of up to 7 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP and GBP are fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no exchange-control permits, no differentiation between offshore and domestic accounts, and no statutory impediments to profit or capital remittance from Gibraltar to the Cayman parent. The only operational hurdles are documentary: evidence of economic substance, a live CRS/FATCA tax classification, and standard AML source-of-wealth review by the correspondent banking chain. A Cayman Exempted Company shareholder may receive dividends, interest, or loan repayments without prior regulatory approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar does not impose any surrender, repatriation, or conversion timeline on export proceeds. DG forwarding receipts in EUR or USD may be retained in nostro or current accounts, used to match supplier disbursements, or swapped freely, subject only to GFSC-mandated transaction recording and sanctions-screening duties.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no independent central bank. The Government of Gibraltar (Ministry of Finance) holds note-issuing authority for the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) under the Currency Notes Act, while the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) performs the integrated supervisory, banking-licensing, and AML/CFT oversight function. As GIP is pegged at par to GBP, monetary-policy anchoring and lender-of-last-resort capacity are effectively provided by the Bank of England.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Constrained. Gibraltar-licensed EMIs under the Financial Services Act 2019 can provide low-cost multicurrency IBANs, SEPA/SWIFT rails, and a competitive FX layer, and are feasible for outgoing payments under GBP 50,000. However, for ISIC 5229 DG/hazmat forwarding, EMIs are commercially and legally constrained: they cannot issue or confirm Letters of Credit, cannot extend trade credit, cannot accept client funds as deposits, and most risk-committee policies auto-decline any hazmat sector classification. Feasibility rating: C+/Conditional, only as a Tier-2 payment overlay backed by a Tier-1 licensed bank relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound injections from the Cayman Exempted Company to the Gibraltar operating account are executed via SWIFT MT103 using purpose or transaction code 'CFA' (equity capital in an incorporated enterprise) for equity injections or 'CL' (intercompany credit extension) for loan advances, as mapped by the receiving bank's statistical reporting. Supporting pack: Cayman board resolution, signed share-subscription or loan agreement, source-of-funds letter, and a Cayman-licensed counsel certificate. The Gibraltar receiving bank applies standard source-of-funds verification and a 2–5 business day internal clearing hold for amounts exceeding GBP 50,000; no statutory exchange-control approval or central bank certificate is required.Primary outbound rail is the SWIFT MT103 for supplier and carrier payments, supported by MT202 COV where correspondent cover is necessary. Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700 issue, MT720 transfer) must be used for hazardous-cargo charter settlements and high-value DG shipments; MT798 documentary-collection requests are acceptable for smaller LCL DG consignments. Clearing settlement uses UK CHAPS for GBP, TARGET2 for EUR, and US Federalwire for USD. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% effective rate for qualifying Exempted Company foreign-source income; 12.5% standard Gibraltar corporation tax rate applicable to Gibraltar-source trading profits

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

On or before 31 December immediately following the end of the chargeable period (fiscal year ends 31 December); e.g., 2024 assessment period must be filed by 31 December 2025

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File where aggregated controlled transactions with a single related party exceed GBP 1,000,000 per year, or where a Gibraltar entity claims a deduction for management fees or royalties exceeding GBP 250,000; Master File and CbC Report required for multinational groups with consolidated group revenue above GBP 200,000,000 (aligned with Gibraltar's CbC Notification threshold of EUR 750,000,000 for ultimate parent reporting); lower threshold of GBP 500,000 applies where the related party is resident in a non-DTA jurisdiction or a no/low-tax territory such as the Cayman Islands

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's Taxation (Economic Substance) Act and ESR Regulations apply to every Exempted Company carrying on a relevant activity, which includes 'shipping' and 'freight forwarding services as a transport support activity' (ISIC 5229, high-risk DG class); the entity must demonstrate in Gibraltar: (i) board meetings held at a registered physical office in Gibraltar and strategic decisions made there, (ii) an adequate and proportionate number of full-time employees physically present in Gibraltar, (iii) sufficient annual operating expenditure incurred in Gibraltar for DG compliance, dangerous-goods safety management, and transport coordination, and (iv) absence of outsourcing of core income-generating activities to a substitute jurisdiction; a completed ESR-1 self-assessment return with supporting substance evidence must be filed annually with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies, and the Dangerous Goods (Transport) Licence under the Ports and Transport Act must remain current to corroborate real activity

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 200% of the evaded tax; criminal conviction under the Income Tax Act and Criminal Justice Act punishable by fine up to GBP 300,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; asset forfeiture proceedings under the Proceeds of Crime Act

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns are personally assessed for the restituted tax, subject to disqualification under the Companies Act for up to 15 years, travel-banned pending criminal investigation, and imprisoned for fraud offences
Shell Company / Deficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Fixed financial penalty of GBP 10,000 for first non-compliance and GBP 100,000 for continuing breach; second-year failure leads to company de-registration, mandatory exchange of information with HM Revenue & Customs, and disclosure to the OECD pursuant to the Code of Conduct Group

Director personal liability: Directors face disqualification orders, personal penalty orders up to GBP 50,000, and personal liability for all unpaid corporate taxes or penalties where the court finds deliberate stripping of economic substance through a shadow-director arrangement
AML / Sanctions / Dangerous Goods Licence Breach

Statutory sanction: Criminal money-laundering conviction under the Criminal Justice (Proceeds of Crime) Act punishable by fine up to GBP 500,000 and/or imprisonment up to 14 years; immediate revocation of the DG freight forwarding licence; freezing injunctions over bank accounts and freight receivables

Director personal liability: Senior officers personally liable for money-laundering and sanctions-evasion offences; personal confiscation orders equal to the value of assets laundered; international travel bans during the investigation; extradition exposure to the UK under the Commonwealth and bilateral mutual legal assistance framework
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of the impugned transactions by the Gibraltar Income Tax Office; assessment of additional tax at 12.5% plus a negligence adjustment of up to 25%; penalty equal to 100% of the under-declared tax, abateable only if a qualifying OECD-aligned benchmarking study is produced within 60 days

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structured round-tripping transactions can be joined to the transfer pricing adjustment demand, personally fined up to GBP 100,000, and subjected to disqualification under the Companies Act where the mispricing was concealed from the auditors
