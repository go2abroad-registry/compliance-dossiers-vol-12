E-government filing portal

A non-resident Cayman exempted company obtains a Gibraltar Income Tax Reference Number by submitting the Gibraltar Income Tax form IT 1, certified constitutional documents, beneficial ownership register, directors' and shareholders' particulars, and written evidence of place of central management and control to the Income Tax Office. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar has full and unrestricted currency convertibility. There are no exchange-control statutes, no central-bank approval requirements for capital-account transactions, no surrender obligations, and no restrictions on converting GIP into GBP, EUR, USD or any other freely traded currency. The GIP trades legally only within Gibraltar but is redeemable at par with GBP by authorised banks, and all international conversion is effected through GBP/sterling corridors. Capital and current account flows for a non-resident Cayman Exempted Company through a Gibraltar permanent establishment are entirely free; profit, dividend and capital remittances are not subject to withholding taxes, prior notification, or exchange-control permission, provided the transaction is compliant with GFIU record-keeping rules, the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018 (Overseas Territories) Order and the company's Economic Substance filing position in Cayman.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rule exists in Gibraltar. Because Gibraltar imposes no exchange controls and no foreign-currency surrender ratios, marketplace payouts from UberEats, DoorDash, Just Eat or any delivery-aggregator settlement engine may be retained indefinitely in the original settlement currency (GBP, EUR or USD) or converted at the corporate treasury's discretion. There is no mandated timeline for conversion and no state-administered channel for foreign-exchange surrender. The only operational discipline is a contractual/AML-driven one: the Gibraltar licensed bank will require the operating entity to maintain segregated multi-currency accounts and provide quarterly MT940/MT950 statement reconciliation to verify that platform payouts correspond to real meal-delivery sales rather than third-party fund passing.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar does not maintain a central bank; currency issuance is the statutory responsibility of the Government of Gibraltar, which issues Gibraltar pound (GIP) banknotes and coins pegged at parity 1:1 to pound sterling. Monetary anchor, lender-of-last-resort and UK wholesale payment infrastructure functions are ultimately provided by the Bank of England, while licensing, prudential supervision and market-conduct regulation of banks, foreign-exchange dealers, money-service businesses and authorised payment institutions are vested in the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC). Cross-border payments compliance and sanctions screening are jointly governed by the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit (GFIU), HM Treasury's UK financial sanctions regime as extended to Gibraltar, and the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and Money Laundering Regulations 2019.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium — permissible only as a complement to, not a substitute for, a locally licensed Tier 1 banking relationship. UK-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) may passport payment services into Gibraltar under the preserved UK–Gibraltar passporting framework (Financial Services (Gibraltar) (Amendment) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019); EEA-authorised EMIs (e.g. Dutch, Lithuanian or Irish licensed entities) no longer hold automatic EU passport entry into Gibraltar after Brexit and would require a full GFSC EMI licence or a Gibraltar-incorporated branch. For the ghost-kitchen delivery model, Wise Business, Payoneer and Revolut Business are operationally viable for collecting UberEats and DoorDash settlement batches in EUR/USD and for paying small-volume local food suppliers, provided the platform payout accounts are held in the legal entity's own name. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections from the Cayman parent to the Gibraltar operational bank account must be executed as SWIFT MT103 wires bearing purpose codes 'OCAT' (other capital transfers), 'GIFT' (non-repayable contribution) or 'RECDS' (operating equity injection). No exchange-control approval is required in either Cayman or Gibraltar; the receiving bank requires a dated Board Resolution, an instruction letter from the Cayman account signatory, and a standard F7/GL1 indemnity. Injections above GBP 20,000 are systematically recorded in the bank's CDD source-of-funds register; injections above GBP 50,000 require a full source-of-wealth disclosure from the remitting Cayman bank, HM Treasury sanctions screening of all parties, and a direct confirmation call with the Cayman compliance officer. Inward wires must be prefunded in the Cayman entity's own treasury account; third-party funding, crypto-ramp transfers or unbacked promissory instruments are categorically rejected.Outbound trade disbursements flow through four clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for international supplier wires, cross-border royalties and Cayman-parent management fees; (2) CHAPS Sterling Settlement Services for same-day GBP payments to UK food wholesalers and packaging vendors; (3) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for EUR payments to EU-based food-service groups and platform settlement entities; and (4) local direct credits through the Gibraltar Automated Clearing House (GACH) for Gibraltar staff salary and public-utility payments. For high-value frozen or ambient food procurement contracts exceeding EUR 50,000, a Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) issued by the Gibraltar Tier 1 bank with sight drafts against transport and customs documents is the recommended trade-finance rail, providing supplier security and bank-side documentary compliance. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar Local File: mandatory for taxpayers with annual turnover exceeding GIP 5,000,000 or intra-group transactions exceeding GIP 1,000,000; Cayman entities with cross-border related-party transactions must prepare OECD-compliant documentation at group level when group turnover exceeds EUR 750,000,000 (Master File).

Economic substance scope: Cayman Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to Cayman tax-resident entities carrying on relevant activities, including headquarters, distribution and service centre, IP holding and pure equity holding. Ghost-kitchen and virtual-brand groups supporting affiliated delivery brands with procurement, technology, and logistics functions fall within 'distribution and service centre' and must demonstrate adequate employees, premises, decision-making and expenditure in the Cayman Islands unless tax resident elsewhere (typically Gibraltar/UK). Pure equity holding companies benefit from reduced substance but must still file annually. Gibraltar applies its own economic employer concept for permanent establishment purposes and follows OECD BEPS Action 5 substance-over-form requirements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion (Gibraltar)

Statutory sanction: A tax-geared penalty up to 200% of understated tax plus daily late-filing surcharges and interest; criminal conviction for evasion under Gibraltar Income Tax Act with fines and imprisonment up to 7 years; a freeze order over bank accounts including payment facilitator balances.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors may be personally assessed for penalties where false declarations are signed; courts may issue travel bans, seize passports, impose disqualification as director, and pierce the corporate veil to recover unpaid tax from personal assets.
Cayman Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Initial failure fines up to CI$12,000 increasing to CI$120,000 for continued non-compliance; the Cayman Registrar may strike off and dissolve the exempted company; directors may be ordered to restore the company and pay the tax authority's investigation costs.

Director personal liability: Courts may impose personal fines, corporate dissolution, travel restrictions, and, in egregious cases of fraudulent insolvency, criminal imprisonment; directors are barred from acting as directors of Cayman entities without permission.
Transfer Pricing Abuse / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar tax authority adjusts profits under arm's-length principle, imposes interest and penalties up to 100% of resulting tax, and extends assessment periods to 7 years where documents are wilfully withheld; under OECD Pillar Two rules, related top-up tax can also arise.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed intercompany agreements without economic justification are jointly and severally liable for penalty assessments; travel bans and asset freezing orders may be issued pending appeal; personal liability extends to tax-geared penalties where they knowingly procured an improper transfer-pricing benefit.
AML / Payment-Compliance Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Under Gibraltar AML/CFT legislation, regulatory fines of up to GBP 5 million or 10% of turnover, public censure, suspension of payment institution licences, and criminal prosecution for money laundering carrying sentences up to 14 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and MLROs bear personal criminal liability for consent failures, suspicious activity report failures, and providing false information to the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit; director disqualification and travel bans routinely accompany enforcement actions, and courts may pierce the corporate veil to confiscate hidden assets.
