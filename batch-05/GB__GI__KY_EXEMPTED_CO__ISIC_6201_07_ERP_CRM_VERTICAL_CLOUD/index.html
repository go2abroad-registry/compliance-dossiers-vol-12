Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar — Commissioner of Income Tax (Income Tax Office), https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (including a Cayman exempted company) must apply for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) by submitting: (1) certified copy of the Cayman Islands Certificate of Incorporation and Memorandum/Articles of Association; (2) certificate of good standing / exemption certificate; (3) board resolution appointing a Gibraltar resident representative and registered office; (4) details of all directors, shareholders, and ultimate beneficial owners; (5) completed application forms prescribed by the Commissioner of Income Tax. Once the entity has a permanent establishment, a place of management, or derives taxable income in Gibraltar, it must file a corporate tax return and obtain a TIN. No VAT ID is applicable as Gibraltar does not levy VAT.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign entity (including a Cayman exempted company) must apply for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) by submitting: (1) certified copy of the Cayman Islands Certificate of Incorporation and Memorandum/Articles of Association; (2) certificate of good standing / exemption certificate; (3) board resolution appointing a Gibraltar resident representative and registered office; (4) details of all directors, shareholders, and ultimate beneficial owners; (5) completed application forms prescribed by the Commissioner of Income Tax. Once the entity has a permanent establishment, a place of management, or derives taxable income in Gibraltar, it must file a corporate tax return and obtain a TIN. No VAT ID is applicable as Gibraltar does not levy VAT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Gibraltar applies a territorial corporate tax system with a single headline rate; there are no check-the-box elections. A foreign company may elect to be treated as tax-transparent under the Tax Partnerships (Election) Regulations if it meets specified conditions, subject to anti-avoidance review. Hybrid mismatch rules under OECD BEPS Action 2 are incorporated through domestic anti-avoidance provisions and the VAT/import duty framework is administered by the Customs and Excise Authority.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Purchase Tax and Import Duty (no VAT/GST)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 0 — no VAT registration threshold applies because Gibraltar does not impose VAT. Purchase tax is levied at the point of importation or supply of specified goods (e.g. alcohol, tobacco, fuel) regardless of local turnover. A business making taxable supplies of goods into Gibraltar must register with HM Customs for duty purposes; there is no minimum annual turnover relief.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT system, so no export VAT zero-rating is available. Exports of goods from Gibraltar to third countries are outside the scope of Gibraltar purchase tax and import duty provided the goods leave Gibraltar customs territory under customs control. Offshore services (including vertical ERP/CRM cloud software delivered to non-Gibraltar clients) are not subject to Gibraltar indirect tax, as Gibraltar does not levy VAT on services. Any claim for export exemption must be substantiated by contemporaneous customs evidence and commercial contracts.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (SAD form)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill evidencing departure from Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT remittance evidence for the exported service
  • Commercial invoice and export contract (with jurisdiction and delivery terms)

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping. Importers must file customs declarations (SAD/electronic single administrative document) via the Gibraltar Customs electronic cargo system. B2B invoicing is subject to standard commercial record-keeping rules; no API-based clearance is required.

Fraudulent or unsupported export exemption claims attract penalties under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance and Income Tax Act. Penalties may include a fine of up to 150% of the evaded duty or tax, a fixed penalty of up to GIP 5,000 per offence, and/or imprisonment for up to two years. The Commissioner may reopen assessments for up to six years (or without time limit in cases of deliberate fraud), retrospectively claw back any duty or tax relief previously granted, and impose interest at the statutory rate of 8% per annum.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom, Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands maintain full current-account and most capital-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. Sterling, US dollars and euros are freely transferable; a Cayman Exempted Company trading via Gibraltar bears no restriction on the conversion of export proceeds or the conversion of GBP into USD/EUR for trade settlements. The only statutory hurdle is the prospective bank's evaluation of ultimate beneficial ownership and source of funds; profit remittance to shareholders is unrestricted and subject to zero withholding tax because the entity is non-resident in the UK/GI with no permanent establishment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. There is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds, no mandated conversion ratio, no waiting period, and no central-bank approval for retaining export receipts in foreign currency. Receipts from worldwide cloud ERP/CRM subscriptions may be held indefinitely in the original invoicing currency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England for Sterling; Gibraltar Financial Services Commission for Gibraltar-licensed banks and EMIs; Cayman Islands Monetary Authority as the home supervisor of the Cayman Exempted Company. Her Majesty's Treasury retains residual competence under the Exchange Control Act 1947, but no administrative exchange control is materially applicable to this non-resident software-trading entity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High. A GFSC-licensed Gibraltar EMI or an FCA-authorised UK EMI can onboard the Cayman Exempted Company fully remotely if the UBO has adequate photo identity and proof of address, and can issue local GBP and EUR IBANs for e-money safeguarding. Legal constraints: EMIs are not credit institutions, cannot extend credit, must segregate client funds in a safeguarded account with an authorised bank, and cannot provide access to CHAPS unless they hold a sponsoring arrangement with a credit institution. A third-country EMI without British or European licensing should be rejected because it lacks direct access to UK Faster Payments, CHAPS and SEPA interoperability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory for any Gibraltar-resident company or PE whose cross-border related-party transactions exceed GBP 250,000 per annum; Master File: mandatory where the consolidated group turnover exceeds GBP 750 million or where intra-group services exceed GBP 10 million; all documentation is prepared in GBP.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's Economic Substance Requirements (ESR), enacted following the EU Code of Conduct Group and embedded in the Income Tax Act 2010, apply to relevant activities including software/IP holding, financing, and holding-company operations. A Cayman Exempted Company operating the vertical ERP/CRM cloud must demonstrate in Gibraltar: (i) a physical office, (ii) adequate qualified full-time staff, (iii) board meetings with real decision-making, and (iv) proportionate local revenue expenditure. Failure to satisfy ESR rebuts a Gibraltar residence claim and triggers anti-avoidance recharacterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded plus interest at 8% per annum; criminal fine of up to GBP 500,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years under the Income Tax Act 2010; confiscation of proceeds under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar).

Director personal liability: Directors face joint and several civil restitution, asset freezes, travel bans and passport surrender orders, disqualification from acting as directors for up to 15 years, and imprisonment for up to 7 years where the evasion is attributable to personal dishonesty.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Disregard of the corporate entity, reallocation of profits to the Gibraltar place of management, additional assessments with a six-year look-back, and a penalty of up to GBP 100,000; the Registrar of Companies may strike off the branch/PE registration.

Director personal liability: Court-ordered restitution of diverted profits, mandatory confiscation and disgorgement, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to 5 years for directors who certified false substance declarations.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment to arm's-length profit, secondary adjustment recharacterising excess payments as constructive dividends (non-deductible), interest and a penalty of up to 50% of the additional tax where the Local File was not produced; failure to file the Local File is itself a strict liability offence.

Director personal liability: Civil liability for the full penalty amount, disqualification from holding financial services licences, and criminal exposure for the company's tax agent; directors who knowingly sign false TP documentation face extradition procedures between Gibraltar, the UK, and the Cayman Islands.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Criminal fines up to GBP 500,000, imprisonment up to 7 years, daily default penalties for continuing non-disclosure, freezing orders initiated by the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit, and revocation of the company's banking relationships.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability irrespective of corporate capacity, confiscation orders covering the beneficial owners' assets, INTERPOL travel bans, and extradition exposure across Gibraltar, the UK, and the EEA.
