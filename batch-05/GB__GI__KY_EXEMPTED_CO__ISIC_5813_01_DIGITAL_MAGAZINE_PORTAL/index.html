Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound is pegged at parity with, and is freely convertible into, Pound Sterling (GBP). Gibraltar imposes no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. A Cayman Exempted Company maintaining bank accounts in Gibraltar may hold, remit, and convert GIP, GBP, EUR, and USD freely. Capital injection, intercompany lending, dividend distributions, and profit repatriation are not subject to exchange-control approval; the only relevant constraints are Cayman solvency tests under the Cayman Companies Act and UK/Gibraltar AML record-keeping requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar does not impose statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timelines on export proceeds, including receipts from digital magazine subscriptions, sponsored media placements, and advertising platforms. There is no mandatory conversion ratio for foreign currency receivables. However, all export invoices, platform settlement statements, and currency conversion records must be retained to satisfy GFSC AML/CTF due-diligence expectations and to demonstrate source of funds in the event of clearing bank reviews.

Central bank supervision: HM Government of Gibraltar, Ministry of Financial Services, Currency and Banknotes Division, in coordination with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC); Gibraltar does not maintain an independent central bank and operates a currency board-style issuance regime for the Gibraltar pound (GIP).

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% (Gibraltar corporate income tax rate; the Cayman Islands Exempted Company layer is tax-exempt at 0%). The Gibraltar rate applies to the digital magazine portal's Gibraltar-source trading income where central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, or to income attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% in Gibraltar; 0% in the Cayman Islands. Gibraltar does not levy withholding tax on dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, and the Cayman Exempted Company may distribute profits free of any Cayman tax, dividend withholding, or stamp duty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Gibraltar: the tax year runs to 31 December; the Form C for each year of assessment must be filed by 30 November of the immediately following tax year, i.e. a 31 December fiscal-year-end return is due by 30 November of the next calendar year. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar: a written Transfer Pricing Local File and Master File are mandatory where cross-border controlled transactions exceed GBP 1,000,000 per annum or where Gibraltar turnover exceeds GBP 10,000,000; a simplified TP notification suffices below those thresholds. OECD-aligned documentation is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000. All transactions between the Cayman exempted parent and the Gibraltar operation or its shareholders must be documented on an arm's-length basis and retained in the Gibraltar books.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply at both tiers. In the Cayman Islands, the International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act classifies the digital magazine portal as a distribution and service centre business with potential intellectual-property elements; the entity must demonstrate core income-generating activities in Gibraltar, premises reasonably adequate for the scale of activity, a substantive local workforce and director presence, and local expenditure proportionate to the income. Gibraltar applies parallel substance requirements to companies managed and controlled on the Rock, evidenced by board minutes, payroll records, and physical premises. If the entity is tax-resident in Gibraltar, it is automatically excluded from Cayman ESR filing under the non-residence ground; failure to prove residence or substance renders the structure a shell company with loss of tax-exempt status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax undercharged, plus monthly interest at 1%; criminal conviction carries fines up to GBP 250,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years; Cayman tax information non-disclosure attracts fines up to CI$25,000 and 5 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate returns are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt; the court may pierce the corporate veil, issue travel bans, freeze bank accounts, and disqualify directors for up to 15 years.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Cayman ESR penalties: CI$10,000 initial, CI$10,000 per month of default, and strike-off; Gibraltar Companies Act penalties of GBP 300 to GBP 1,000 plus daily default fines and compulsory strike-off for failure to file returns and accounts.

Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalisation Abuse

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment recomputing taxable profits at arm's length, with interest; penalties up to 100% of the tax on the adjusted profit; the deliberate-default regime extends the assessment window to 6 years and imposes fixed penalties for each omitted TP return.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the non-arm's-length pricing may be personally assessed for the corrected tax; criminal prosecution can impose travel bans, extradition between the UK and Gibraltar, and disqualification under the Company Directors Disqualification Act.
Anti-Money Laundering, Beneficial Ownership and Economic Crime
