Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Revenue and Customs Department (Income Tax Office), 15 John Mackintosh Hall, Gibraltar; taxpayer portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company conducting trade through a Gibraltar branch, agency, or permanent establishment must first file a branch registration notice with the Registrar of Companies under the Gibraltar Companies (Foreign Companies) Regulations. The branch must then submit a Taxpayer Registration Application using the statutory corporate registration form, providing the certified certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, list of directors, Gibraltar registered business address, and details of the local representative. The Commissioner of Income Tax will issue a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) upon verification. Registration must be completed before the commencement of revenue-generating activities, typically within 30 days of the Gibraltar establishment becoming effective.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box election mechanism. A Cayman Exempted Company is treated as a legal entity subject to territorial corporate taxation. Under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act, a company is Gibraltar tax resident only if its central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar. The default election available is to remain non-resident and taxable solely on income arising in Gibraltar or attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment. Conversely, the company may elect in writing on the corporate tax return to be treated as Gibraltar tax resident and thereby subject to Gibraltar Income Tax on worldwide income. Hybrid mismatch elections under the Gibraltar Taxation (Hybrid Mismatches) Rules 2020 may be made in respect of instruments and entities falling within the OECD BEPS Action 2 framework, subject to filing with the Revenue and Customs Department.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No Gibraltar VAT or GST system; indirect taxation consists of Import Duties, Excise Duties, and Purchase Tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A – Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST and therefore prescribes no economic turnover registration threshold. Any person importing goods into Gibraltar must obtain an Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number irrespective of transaction value; exporters of designer blind box toys from Gibraltar must likewise hold an EORI reference, obtainable through the Gibraltar Revenue and Customs Department at zero registration cost.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of vinyl designer blind box toys manufactured or finished in Gibraltar and dispatched to a destination outside Gibraltar fall outside the scope of state purchase tax and are effectively zero-rated. There is no VAT refund mechanism because no VAT was payable at input stage. To preserve statutory recognition of an export sale, the exporter must prove that title to the goods passed outside Gibraltar and that the goods were irrevocably exported without diversion into the Gibraltar domestic market.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / C88 SAD) endorsed by the Revenue and Customs Department
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing departure from Gibraltar to the final foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice stating the FOB or EXW value, HS Code 9503 or 9504 classification, and the statement 'Goods exported outside Gibraltar'
  • Packing List detailing the number of units, unit weights, and container markings of each shipment
  • Proof of Export Movement and Receipt in the destination jurisdiction (Delivered Duty Paid or customs entry in country of import)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency consideration from the foreign buyer

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal electronic invoicing, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control clearance. Export and import clearances are processed electronically via the Gibraltar Customs Declaration System, using the Single Administrative Document (SAD) format. For excise goods monitoring, the Excise Movement and Control System (EMCS) is used where applicable. Commercial invoices must contain the issuer's Gibraltar TIN and EORI number, buyer details, Incoterms, description of goods, HS classification, and value in GBP for customs support.

Although Gibraltar does not levy VAT and therefore cannot claw back input VAT, the submission of false or incomplete export evidence exposes the Cayman Exempted Company to serious sanctions. The Commissioner may deny the export exemption, subject the goods to immediate customs seizure, issue a protective purchase-tax assessment at the applicable domestic purchase-tax rate, and impose monetary penalties up to 100% of the duty evaded plus interest from the date of export. Knowingly providing fraudulent customs declarations constitutes an offence under the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Regulations, punishable on conviction by fines not exceeding £10,000 and imprisonment for up to two years. The exporter must also retain all documentation for at least six years; failure to meet the 180-day foreign exchange repatriation deadline may trigger a presumption that the trade was carried on through the Gibraltar establishment, thereby exposing the proceeds to Gibraltar corporate taxation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The UK and Gibraltar maintain fully convertible currency regimes under both current and capital account components for non-resident and resident entities. There are no exchange control approvals for profit remittance, dividend payments, royalty distributions, or liquidation proceeds of a Cayman exempted company banked in Gibraltar, provided the beneficial ownership chain and source of funds pass standard AML/KYC. The Gibraltar Pound is pegged at 1:1 to Sterling, and USD/EUR conversion is freely available through GFSC-licensed banks. The Cayman Exempted Company itself is not subject to exchange controls in the Cayman Islands, which have no exchange control legislation. Practical hurdles are limited to sanctions screening, CRS/FATCA reporting, and the bank's internal de-risking policies, not regulatory convertibility limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement in the United Kingdom, Gibraltar, or the Cayman Islands. Firms may retain export receipts in the original currency (USD, EUR, GBP, or GIP), hold offshore operating accounts, and repatriate or leave funds abroad at their election. For payments routed through Gibraltar banks, the bank may request underlying trade documentation and a UNSPSC/HS code declaration for Part VI of the Bank of England reporting framework, but no conversion mandate or minimum local retention percentage applies. Receipts from designer blind-box toy sales are therefore freely mobilizable without forced conversion or deemed remittance rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England is the ultimate monetary authority for Sterling, with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) supervising licensed banks in Gibraltar under the Financial Services Act 2019. For the Cayman Exempted Company, the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) is the home-state regulator, and HM Treasury/Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) oversees sanctions enforcement across UK and Gibraltar. There is no separate central bank in Gibraltar; Sterling/Gibraltar Pound banknotes are issued under the Government of Gibraltar Currency Notes Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility is High for UK/EU/EEA-licensed electronic money institutions such as Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Payoneer for inbound collection and outbound disbursement in low-risk, e-commerce-only flows. However, an offshore digital EMI that is not licensed in Gibraltar or the UK will not be viable as a primary treasury or liquidity rail because it cannot provide guaranteed SWIFT/CHAPS/Faster Payments access, cannot offer Fx forwards or NDFs, and is immediately flagged as high-risk if used to concentrate substantial turnover of a Cayman exempted company. A Gibraltar customer relationship is best structured with a tier-1 licensed bank as the master account and an EMI used only as a settlement sub-rail for Shopify, Stripe, and local POS card captures, with daily sweeps to the licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to the Cayman Exempted Company should be cleared by SWIFT MT103 from the subscribing shareholder's maintained bank account, reaching a Gibraltar-licensed master account if the funds are for European operations. The payment field must include the legal entity identifier (LEI) of the Cayman company, the exact corporate shareholder name, and a purpose code consistent with the UK BOP reporting taxonomy, e.g., 'D.3 Direct Investment Capital Contribution' or equivalent BOX70/Credit Instruction narrative. Verification packets must include the board resolution approving the share issuance, the subscription agreement, the updated register of members, confirmation that no share premium is being injected above book value, and source-of-funds statement. Since the Cayman Exempted Company does not have a minimum capital requirement, capital can be injected as shareholder loans or paid-up share capital; all loan-on-demand advances must evidence non-residency and be ring-fenced from sales receivables. For deposits in USD or EUR, the Gibraltar bank will effect a spot Fx conversion at the institution's closing rate, applying an interbank spread plus correspondent charges, and will issue a CRS/FATCA attribution record to the Cayman company.
