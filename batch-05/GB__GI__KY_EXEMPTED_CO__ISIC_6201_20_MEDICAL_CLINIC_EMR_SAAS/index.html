Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) for UK VAT; for Gibraltar, the Commissioner of Income Tax / Gibraltar Tax Office (GTO) and HM Customs Gibraltar (Gibraltar Customs) administer indirect taxes under the UK-Gibraltar VAT arrangements as part of the EU Customs Union transitional framework; online portal: HMRC online services for VAT registration and the Gibraltar Tax Office e-services for local tax registration.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident entity deriving taxable income in Gibraltar or making taxable supplies registered for VAT must apply for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) through the Gibraltar Tax Office (GTO) using Form GTO/REG/01, accompanied by constitutional documents, register of directors/members, proof of registered address in Gibraltar (required for a Gibraltar company), and evidence of economic substance or the relevant permit/licence for regulated healthcare software activity; for VAT purposes, the entity must register with HM Customs Gibraltar / HMRC via the Gibraltar VAT registration process using form VAT1 (application for registration) or the online VAT registration service, providing its legal entity identifier, banking details, compliance contact, and evidence of business activity transacted in or from Gibraltar; for a Cayman Exempted Company with no physical presence in Gibraltar, the appointment of a Gibraltar resident representative agent is required to hold the compliance file and receive service of process for tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone; the Cayman Exempted Company as a non-resident owner is classified for Gibraltar tax purposes as a foreign entity with territorial scope; it is not ordinarily tax resident in Gibraltar absent central management and control being exercised in Gibraltar; however, because the composite structure uses a Gibraltar-registered presence and UK/Gibraltar market access, the operating branch or Gibraltar permanent establishment would be subject to entity-level taxation on Gibraltar-source profits only; the Cayman parent itself remains within its domestic Tax_Exempt_Zone classification.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; however, under Gibraltar's Income Tax Act the entity can make an election to be treated as a tax-transparent partnership for certain collective investment or joint venture arrangements, subject to Commissioner approval; for UK/Gibraltar hybrid mismatch purposes, the Cayman Exempted Company may be treated as opaque and any interest, royalty, or SaaS fee payments are subject to transfer pricing and diverted profits tax scrutiny; an election can be made to apply the Gibraltar domestic participation exemption on capital gains from qualifying shareholdings; for VAT, a Gibraltar established entity may elect to join a UK VAT group with a UK-established member under section 43A VATA 1994 as extended to Gibraltar, subject to HMRC approval and the 'relevant association' control tests; a territorial group VAT registration election can be made to centralize compliance where multiple Gibraltar entities exist.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

UK/Gibraltar VAT registration compulsory where taxable supplies exceed £90,000 (GBP) in rolling 12 months; where the entity is not established in the UK/Gibraltar but supplies digital services to non-business consumers, a non-UK supplier must register for VAT from the first supply via the Non-Established Taxable Person (NETP) scheme with no threshold; for Gibraltar-specific supplies, the threshold is aligned with UK VAT law, and a KPMG or GTO administrative notice requires registration at £90,000 or within 30 days of exceeding

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Gibraltar/UK VAT-registered entity may zero-rate exports of goods if it retains evidence of physical export outside the UK/Gibraltar within statutory timeframes; for SaaS and electronic medical record software supplied to overseas customers in the form of intangible digital services, the place of supply rules determine that B2B supplies of digital services are deemed supplied where the customer belongs, reverse-charged to the customer, and outside the scope of UK/Gibraltar VAT (effectively 0% with no output tax) provided the supplier holds valid evidence of the customer's business status and place of establishment; B2C supplies of digital services to non-UK consumers are outside scope if the customer is not in the UK/Gibraltar; supplies to UK healthcare authorities or UK-established medical clinics may be taxable at 20% unless the supply is covered by the VAT relief for medical care (Group 7 Schedule 9 VATA), but EMR software alone typically does not qualify as medical care and is taxable; zero-rating claims require contractual documentation, IP logs, and customer eligibility evidence; intra-Gibraltar or UK supplies of SaaS to non-business consumers are subject to 20% VAT through the Gibraltar/UK return.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar/UK Single Administrative Document or electronic Customs Handling of Import and Export Freight (CHIEF)/Customs Declaration Service export record)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill or other proof of physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Statement showing payment from overseas customer
  • Customer contract and purchase order identifying the recipient and place of establishment
  • Digital service delivery record or server logs evidencing offshore use of the EMR SaaS platform
  • VAT certificate of registration and if applicable a Certificate of Origin or Export/Import Health Documentation for regulated medical software components

No real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) clearance or mandatory digital stamping applies in Gibraltar or the UK for domestic VAT; however, e-invoicing is standard for B2B compliance and must satisfy the legal invoice content requirements of VATA 1994 Schedule 11 and the VAT (Account) Regulations; MTD (Making Tax Digital) requires all VAT-registered businesses to maintain digital records and submit VAT returns via Functional-Compatible Software using the HMRC API (MTDfB); real-time HMRC VAT API clearance is not applicable, but the software must digitally link records to the VAT return; for the HealthTech SaaS export model, invoices issued by the Gibraltar entity to UK/offshore customers will need to carry the UK or Gibraltar VAT number, and where required by the customer, be exchanged through validated Peppol e-invoicing networks; there is no mandatory third-party fiscal stamp or government portal pre-approval for invoices.

HMRC/Gibraltar Customs may disallow zero-rating and issue assessment within 4 years (extendable to 20 years for fraud or deliberate non-disclosure), with a penalty of up to 100% of the VAT understated; fraudulent zero-rating claims involving forged export documents, circular transactions, or fake overseas customers trigger civil penalties under the Criminal Finances Act and VATA 1994 s.13A and, in Gibraltar, the Customs Ordinance and Criminal Justice offences, with criminal liability including imprisonment up to 7 years and unlimited fines; clawbacks include interest from the due date and joint and several liability for directors and the compliance agent where false evidence is knowingly provided; the Cayman parent and Gibraltar branch may also face a UK HMRC cross-border information notice or a Gibraltar TMA 2010 discovery assessment if repatriation of export proceeds does not occur within 180 days, creating a presumption of UK/Gibraltar taxable profits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account. The United Kingdom and the Cayman Islands have accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, and Gibraltar, as a British Overseas Territory, is covered by the UK's position. There are no profit-remittance restrictions, no prior-exchange-control approvals for inward equity investment into Gibraltar, no withholding tax on dividends or royalties paid by a Gibraltar branch or Cayman holding company, and no restrictions on repatriating EMR SaaS subscription revenue. The only structural friction is the absence of a local RTGS: GIP/GBP high-value settlement is executed via UK CHAPS and Faster Payments correspondents, EUR is cleared via SEPA through an EEA correspondent, and USD uses the SWIFT correspondent network. GIP is pegged at par to GBP, so convertibility risk is negligible and no GIP-specific hedging is needed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar: none. The UK Exchange Control Act 1947 was repealed and Gibraltar has no statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds, including EMR/software subscription fees received in GIP, GBP, USD or EUR. Cayman Islands: the exempted company, upon obtaining CIMA non-resident status, is exempt from the Exchange Control Regulations' obligation to repatriate and surrender foreign-currency export earnings within six months; if the company were inadvertently treated as resident, foreign-currency receipts from software exports would have to be sold to an authorized dealer within six months of receipt. In practice, the standard Notice of Non-Resident Status issued by CIMA eliminates this requirement.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the licensing and prudential supervisory authority for banks, payment institutions and e-money institutions under the Financial Services Act 2019; Government of Gibraltar as issuer of the Gibraltar pound (GIP) under the Currency Notes Act 1934, operating a sterling-backed currency board mechanism with 100% backing by Bank of England notes and coin; Bank of England (United Kingdom) and HM Treasury as the ultimate monetary anchor of the GBP/GIP 1:1 peg; Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) as the exchange-control administrator for the Cayman exempted company under the Exchange Control Regulations 1959 (Revised).

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Cayman Exempted Company: 0% on Cayman-source profit and on all income booked through the exempted company while the Tax Exemption Certificate is in force; if the company establishes a taxable Gibraltar branch or permanent establishment, Gibraltar corporate income tax applies at 10% on profits derived from or attributable to Gibraltar.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% in the Cayman Islands and Gibraltar. The Cayman Exempted Company may pay dividends to shareholders, including non-resident shareholders, without Cayman or Gibraltar dividend withholding tax, and no Gibraltar withholding tax arises when a Gibraltar-registered entity distributes profits to a Cayman parent or foreign shareholder.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year accounting period, the Gibraltar CT-01 is due no later than 30 November of the following year unless the Commissioner has served a notice requiring filing within 30 days; the Cayman Exempted Company Annual Return is due on or before 31 January following the year covered by the return. Where the entity is not Gibraltar tax resident and holds a valid Cayman Tax Exemption Certificate, no Gibraltar CIT return is required for foreign-source profits absent a Gibraltar PE or taxable activity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Gibraltar Local File documentation is required where aggregate controlled transactions exceed GIP 1,000,000; a Master File is required where the worldwide consolidated group revenue exceeds GIP 100,000,000. Where the underlying Cayman books are used, the practical accounting threshold is KYD 500,000 for intercompany service, licence, or financing transactions; even below these thresholds, inconsistently documented related-party flows create audit risk in Gibraltar, UK, and Cayman ESR review.

Economic substance scope: The Cayman Economic Substance Regulations and Gibraltar economic substance rules apply in parallel. The medical EMR SaaS entity must demonstrate core income-generating activities in the jurisdiction where it claims tax residence: clinical EMR software development, contract negotiation, data hosting, customer support, and board oversight. Merely holding a Cayman Exempted Company registration or a Gibraltar agent address without full-time qualified staff, premises, substantial expenditure, and regular decision-making will fail the ESR and invalidate the 0% tax treatment; IP-holding activities are the highest-risk category and require active substance and demonstrable risks and opportunities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 100% of the tax lost, daily late-filing surcharges of GIP 30 per day up to GIP 1,200, interest on unpaid tax, and criminal fines on conviction; HMRC or Gibraltar authorities may re-open all previous years and impose a senior accounting officer penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or cause the submission of false returns are exposed to property confiscation orders, travel bans, director disqualification for up to 15 years, joint and several liability for unpaid taxes and penalties, and imprisonment of up to 7 years.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and IP Migration

Statutory sanction: Tax authority may reattribute profits to the UK/Gibraltar developer entity, apply the arm's-length adjustment, disallow deductions, and impose interest and penalties of up to 50%–100% of the adjustment; the GAAR or anti-abuse rules may deny the Cayman exemption for the entire SaaS revenue stream.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve artificial IP transfers face personal assessments for the tax shortfall, civil fraudulent trading liability, and criminal prosecution for cheating the public revenue where a deliberate sham is shown; disqualification of the director and seizure of personal bank accounts are sanctions in practice.
Profit Repatriation and Artificial Debt

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of debt as equity; disallowance of UK interest deductions under the Corporate Interest Restriction; hybrid mismatch counteraction in the UK, Gibraltar, and any EU source jurisdiction; interest plus penalties up to 100% of the avoided withholding or income tax; HMRC can issue prompt corrective notices to the corporate service provider.

Director personal liability: Directors who facilitate shareholder loan-stripping may incur joint and several liability for the recovered tax and be prosecuted under the UK Criminal Finances Act 2017 for facilitating tax evasion outside the UK; travel restrictions and bank account freezes can be imposed during the investigation.
