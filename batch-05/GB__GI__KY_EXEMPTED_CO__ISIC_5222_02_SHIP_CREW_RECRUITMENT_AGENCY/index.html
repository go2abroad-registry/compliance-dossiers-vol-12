Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (ITO), Ministry of Finance; online filing via the ITO e-Returns portal and Companies House Gibraltar electronic registry. Gibraltar is a self-governing UK Overseas Territory with an autonomous tax administration; HMRC cooperation occurs only via UK/Gibraltar Exchange of Information and TIEA instruments.

E-government filing portal

(1) Register the Cayman Exempted Company as an 'overseas company' with the Gibraltar Companies Registry within 30 days of establishing a registered office (Companies Act s.341), accompanied by certified Cayman incorporation documents and a Gibraltar-resident company secretary. (2) Apply to the Income Tax Office on Form TRO-1 for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN), stating the business as ISIC 5222 crew manning. (3) Submit beneficial ownership particulars to the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission under the Beneficial Ownership (Companies) Regulations. (4) Obtain the seafarer recruitment agency licence from the Gibraltar Maritime Administration / Department of Employment before any crew payroll activity. (5) Register as an employer for PAYE and Social Security contributions and open a Gibraltar bank account for multi-currency payroll clearing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone – the Cayman Exempted Company is recognised as not subject to Gibraltar corporate profits, capital gains or withholding taxes provided its central management and control and any permanent establishment remain outside Gibraltar. If board meetings and crew payroll management are carried on in Gibraltar, the entity is reclassified as Entity_Level_Taxation and taxed on territorial income at the standard 12.5% (15% effective under Pillar Two for groups above EUR 750m revenue).

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Gibraltar; classification is determined by factual management/control. Available elections under the Income Tax Act include: (i) group relief election to surrender losses among Gibraltar-resident group members; (ii) election for GloBE safe-harbour treatment for qualifying MNE groups; and (iii) a territorial-scope notification election for foreign-source income derived from non-resident shipowners, submitted to the ITO with supporting payroll and invoicing records. Taxpayers may also elect the simplified deemed-profits basis for crew supply contracts where commercial accounts are unavailable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

VAT – not in force. Gibraltar is outside the UK VAT area; indirect taxation is limited to customs/excise duties on imported goods and a purchase tax on luxury retail. Services, including crew recruitment and payroll management, are outside the scope of VAT and no input tax recovery mechanism exists.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – no VAT registration threshold. Employers must register for PAYE and Social Security at the first payment to crew members (no minimum turnover threshold); importers of physical goods require a customs registration for consignments above GIP 150 de minimis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Structural not applicable – no VAT exists in Gibraltar, therefore no 0% export VAT rate or VAT refund mechanism applies to crew recruitment services. For income tax purposes, fees charged to non-resident shipowners for crew placement are treated as foreign-source only where recruitment, payroll and dispatch activities are performed and managed outside Gibraltar; if a Gibraltar office performs any part of the crewing value chain, the entire fee income is considered to accrue in Gibraltar and is taxable at 12.5% with no export exemption.

  • Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a Gibraltar-licensed bank for foreign-currency crew management fees
  • Signed Crew Agency Service Agreement between the Cayman Exempted Co and the non-resident shipowner
  • MLC 2006 Crew List and Crewing Licence showing statutory compliance of the recruitment agency

The ITO may open audit on the territorial-source filing within 6 years (extendable to 20 years for fraudulent failure); an unfounded claim of foreign-source treatment triggers assessment at 12.5%, late-payment interest at 8% per annum, and penalties up to 100% of the tax avoided plus GIP 100,000 per false return. The General Anti-Abuse Rule (Income Tax Amendment Act 2020) allows retrospective clawback of any tax advantage from artificial offshore structuring, and TIEAs with the UK/Cayman permit automatic exchange of the audit file.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility exists throughout the United Kingdom, Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands; there are no exchange-control barriers to profit repatriation, dividend distribution, management-fee payments, payroll funding or repayment of shareholder loans routed through a Gibraltar treasury account of a Cayman Exempted Company to non-resident members. The Gibraltar pound is freely convertible into sterling at par without margin or delay; KYD is freely convertible into USD at the statutory peg. Practical hurdles are bank-level rather than statutory: (i) a Cayman offshore vehicle automatically attracts enhanced due diligence irrespective of the legitimate maritime staffing business; (ii) banks require demonstrable Gibraltar economic substance (registered office, resident director or controller, or licensed crewing presence) to avoid a shell-conduit classification; and (iii) each outward crew-wage payment must carry documentary transparency satisfying the flag-state maritime authority and the International Transport Workers' Federation (ITF). No Gibraltar or UK withholding tax, remittance fee, or prior regulatory approval is imposed on profit distribution, and no Bank of England consent is required for outward dividends.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation exists in the United Kingdom, Gibraltar or the Cayman Islands. Following the repeal of UK exchange controls in 1979 and the parallel liberalisation in Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands, all forced-sale and quota-based conversion mechanisms were eliminated. The group may retain 100% of crew-management fees, placement commissions and vessel-payroll funding indefinitely in any nominated currency (GBP, GIP, EUR, USD, PHP, INR); there is no 30-, 60- or 90-day repatriation timeline and no mandated conversion ratio into GIP/GBP or KYD/USD. Reporting obligations are limited to: (i) GFSC-licensed banks filing suspicious-activity reports and monitoring cash-intensive inbound flows; (ii) the company maintaining accurate Cayman annual-return and beneficial-ownership filings under the Cayman Companies Act and Beneficial Ownership Transparency Regulations; and (iii) external-statistics capture by the Bank of England when payments are made in sterling — none of which impose surrender, conversion or approval duties.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (Sterling Monetary Authority and lender of last resort for GBP); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC, the statutory banking and AML/CFT supervisor in Gibraltar under the Financial Services Act 2019); Government of Gibraltar (issuer of Gibraltar pound (GIP) notes and coin pursuant to the Currency Notes Act 1934, pegged at par with GBP); and the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) as the residual administrator of the Cayman Islands Exchange Control Regulations (1959), with the Cayman Islands dollar (KYD) pegged at USD 1 = 0.83333 KYD.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: High as a secondary disbursement and payroll rail; Low-to-Medium as the sole corporate treasury provider. GFSC-licensed Gibraltar electronic-money institutions under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Regulations 2019 (notably PSI Pay) and FCA-authorised UK EMIs (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Ebury) will onboard a Cayman Exempted Company, provided the company supplies a GFSC-style EDD file containing a natural-person UBO, apostilled Cayman incorporation extracts, and a Gibraltar-licensed agent or registered-office appointment. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot offer GIP/GBP Faster Payments or CHAPS settlement, with safeguarding limits typically restricting pooled balances to GBP/EUR 85,000; (ii) Revolut Business and Wise Business frequently decline Cayman-incorporated entities unless third-country economic-substance evidence is filed; (iii) an EMI cannot satisfy an MLC 2006 A1.4 crewing-licence auditor's requirement that payroll funds be segregated through a full-reserve regulated credit institution; and (iv) outbound seafarer wages to the Philippines, India and Indonesia demand SWIFT MT103 counterparties, which EMIs route through sponsor banks at higher cost and slower confirmation. Any EMI selected must be wholly owned and regulated in Gibraltar (GFSC) or the United Kingdom (FCA); use of an unregulated third-country EMI is inadvisable and may breach the Gibraltar AML/CFT Handbook.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity or loan funding enters the Gibraltar operating account via SWIFT MT103 (ISO 20022 pacs.009.001.08) using external-purpose code SUBS for share subscriptions or INTE for intra-group loans, with the remitting bank verifying source of funds. The receiving GFSC-licensed bank requires a verification bundle comprising: (i) a certified board resolution authorising the share issue or loan (Cayman Companies Act, s. 45); (ii) an updated register of members and the relevant share certificates; (iii) the beneficial-ownership declaration filed under the Gibraltar Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Regulations 2019; (iv) a signed shareholder-loan agreement where the remittance is debt rather than equity; and (v) a Gibraltar tax-residence or economic-substance certificate to prevent shell-company classification. For amounts above GBP/EUR/USD 50,000, the bank applies a source-of-wealth backstop: audited group accounts, the UBO's personal tax returns, or asset-disposal contracts must be furnished. No Gibraltar Exchange Control permission or conversion ticket is required, and incoming funds remain in the remitted currency until redeployed for payroll or trade settlement.Seafarer wages are settled by SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary name and address under the information-on-the-payer rules, transmitted in USD, EUR, GBP, PHP, INR or IDR to each mariner's designated account. Low-value repetitive payroll is consolidated into a bulk ISO 20022 pain.001 file lodged by the treasury platform to the Gibraltar bank; high-value tranches above GBP 1 million are cleared through CHAPS via the London banking system. Vessel-supply and crew-change disbursements of material commercial value are effected under an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP 600) opened by a Gibraltar or UK clearing bank against the crewing agency's commercial invoice, the delivery certificate for crew services, and the Master's receipt; standby LCs secure the agency's payroll-prepayment obligations to the vessel owner. UK and EEA operating expenses settle through SEPA Credit Transfer or Faster Payments where VAT-compliant, but all cross-border payments to shipowners, manning principals and international seafarers remain MT103-based to preserve an unbroken audit trail for the flag-state maritime administration, the MLC 2006 licensing authority and the ITF.Outright delivered forward foreign-exchange contracts in GIP/GBP, EUR and USD for crew-payroll and vessel-supply settlement exposures up to 12 months, executed on an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or a Foreign Exchange Global Code-compliant execution venue.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in PHP, INR, IDR, UAH and TRY, permissible only where the seafarer wage currency faces home-country convertibility restrictions and the notional exactly matches the underlying payroll exposure, booked through a GFSC-authorised MiFID II professional-client desk.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCIRS) converting a USD-denominated intra-group shareholder loan into a GIP/GBP floating-rate liability of the Gibraltar treasury vehicle, subject to board-approved hedge-policy documentation and derivative governance review.GBP/EUR and GBP/USD rolling spot-forward swap structures for monthly wage-funding cash-flow management, provided the aggregate notional does not exceed 100% of projected 90-day payroll obligations.European-style vanilla FX options (calls and puts) on EUR/USD and GBP/USD for protection against adverse payroll-cost movements in the crew nationalities' remittance corridors, purchased only with CRO sign-off and never used for speculative open positions.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: High as a secondary disbursement and payroll rail; Low-to-Medium as the sole corporate treasury provider. GFSC-licensed Gibraltar electronic-money institutions under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Regulations 2019 (notably PSI Pay) and FCA-authorised UK EMIs (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Ebury) will onboard a Cayman Exempted Company, provided the company supplies a GFSC-style EDD file containing a natural-person UBO, apostilled Cayman incorporation extracts, and a Gibraltar-licensed agent or registered-office appointment. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not credit institutions and cannot offer GIP/GBP Faster Payments or CHAPS settlement, with safeguarding limits typically restricting pooled balances to GBP/EUR 85,000; (ii) Revolut Business and Wise Business frequently decline Cayman-incorporated entities unless third-country economic-substance evidence is filed; (iii) an EMI cannot satisfy an MLC 2006 A1.4 crewing-licence auditor's requirement that payroll funds be segregated through a full-reserve regulated credit institution; and (iv) outbound seafarer wages to the Philippines, India and Indonesia demand SWIFT MT103 counterparties, which EMIs route through sponsor banks at higher cost and slower confirmation. Any EMI selected must be wholly owned and regulated in Gibraltar (GFSC) or the United Kingdom (FCA); use of an unregulated third-country EMI is inadvisable and may breach the Gibraltar AML/CFT Handbook.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 10,000,000 aggregate related-party turnover per accounting period triggers the mandatory Local File; Master File is mandatory where the ultimate parent group reports consolidated annual revenue equal to or exceeding GIP 50,000,000; below these thresholds, a simplified contemporaneous transfer pricing disclosure is filed within Form 600 Box 8.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply fully to Gibraltar-resident companies and foreign companies operating in Gibraltar under the Income Tax (Substance Requirements) Regulations 2019 as amended. A ship crew recruitment agency engaged in crew sourcing, seafarer payroll, manning and vessel crew management carries on a relevant activity classified as shipping business or distribution and service centre business. The company must meet the CIGA test in Gibraltar: strategic and operational decisions, seafarer recruitment and contract negotiation, payroll execution and compliance management must be performed by adequate staff with premises and proportionate expenditure in Gibraltar. Annual filing on Form ESR-1 is mandatory; the evidential burden is on the company, and a Cayman Exempted Company structure cannot rely on foreign outsourcing arrangements to satisfy the substance test. Failure triggers penalties, automatic exchange of information to the UK and partner jurisdictions, and potential deregistration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 200% of the tax undercharged under the Income Tax Act 2010 as amended; interest at 8% per annum on the unpaid tax; criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion with unlimited fines and imprisonment of up to 7 years on indictment.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where directors sign false returns or direct the evasion; directors are jointly and severally liable for restitution of tax, penalties and costs; the court may impose travel bans, passport surrender, disqualification orders, and imprisonment for up to 7 years.
Shell Company and Economic Substance (ESR) Failure

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of GIP 10,000 and continuing daily penalty of GIP 200 under the ESR Regulations; automatic exchange of substance information with the UK tax authorities and partner states; deregistration of the company under Gibraltar Companies Act powers for persistent non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal daily fines for ongoing default, disqualification from acting as directors for up to 15 years, travel bans pending audit completion, and civil restitution orders for penalties incurred during their tenure.
Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Breach in Seafarer Payroll

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the AML Regulations 2011 as amended: imprisonment up to 14 years and unlimited fines; freezing and forfeiture orders by the Gibraltar Sanctions Unit; withdrawal or refusal of the crew agency licence; inclusion on the public AML defaulters register.

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to personal asset freezing, travel bans, extradition proceedings, disqualification for up to 15 years and civil recovery of tainted payroll funds under the Proceeds of Crime Act; personal criminal responsibility attaches where the director knowingly directed the payment flows.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Record Failure

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment of chargeable income by the Commissioner of Income Tax; penalty from 50% to 150% of the additional tax assessed; interest at 8% per annum computed from the original due date of the corporation tax return; public disclosure of the adjustment in serious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed recklessly priced intercompany agreements may be personally assessed for the penalty portion; the court may issue travel bans pending resolution of the audit; deliberate destruction of transfer pricing records carries imprisonment of up to 2 years and personal civil restitution of the tax shortfall.
