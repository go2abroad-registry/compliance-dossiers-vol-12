Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (untax authority for income tax; www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation); Customs & Excise Department, HM Government of Gibraltar (port of entry for import duty and TIR/customs procedures).

E-government filing portal

1. Register the Cayman Exempted Co as a foreign company (or branch) at Companies House Gibraltar under the Companies Act 2014. 2. File the company income tax registration form (GI-1) with the Income Tax Office within 30 days of commencing economic activity in Gibraltar. 3. Obtain the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (G-TIN), which is a 6-digit code issued by the Income Tax Office. 4. Register separately with the Customs & Excise Department to obtain a Gibraltar customs registration code for import/export and TIR operations. 5. Provide the G-TIN and customs code to the local bank/EMI for account opening. No minimum capital, notarisation, or local beneficial owner pre-approval is statutorily required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box election. A Cayman Exempted Co is tax-exempt in its home jurisdiction but becomes a Gibraltar tax resident if its central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, resulting in entity-level income tax under the Income Tax Act 2010 (standard 12.5% rate). The company may file a written declaration to be assessed as non-resident if central management and control is demonstrably outside Gibraltar; anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the OECD/G20 BEPS framework apply. A US Form 8832 election (if made in the Cayman entity's US return) has no effect in Gibraltar.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT and no GST in Gibraltar; Gibraltar operates an import duty and excise regime under the Customs Ordinance. Goods placed under TIR transit are outside the scope of import duty; trucking services are outside the scope of any Gibraltar consumption tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover-based registration threshold applies (no VAT regime). Any person importing goods into Gibraltar must hold a Gibraltar customs registration code; TIR transit movements that remain in bond do not by themselves trigger customs duty registration. Amounts are recorded in Gibraltar pounds (GIP), pegged 1:1 to GBP.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is not applicable because Gibraltar imposes no VAT. Instead, export and TIR transit relief is granted as an exemption from import duty when goods are physically removed from Gibraltar customs territory under a TIR carnet or an equivalent export procedure. Cross-border trucking services supplied to non-resident customers are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax; no VAT refund mechanism exists.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronically lodged exit declaration / SAD-equivalent)
  • TIR Carnet (red cover with counterfoils validated at each customs office)
  • Bill of Lading or CMR Waybill evidencing the international carriage
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT wire confirmation evidencing receipt of freight proceeds

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or B2B/B2G electronic invoicing. Customs import/export and TIR declarations are lodged electronically via the Gibraltar Customs electronic system; paper and hybrid TIR carnets remain accepted. Authorised Economic Operator (AEO) and authorised consignor statuses are available for accelerated clearances. No API-based continuous transaction control (CTC) regime exists.

Misuse of the TIR regime or false export duty-relief claims triggers penalties under the Customs Ordinance, including fines of up to three times the value of the goods or GIP 10,000, forfeiture of the vehicle and goods, invalidation of the TIR guarantee, and suspension or revocation of authorised consignor status. Retrospective clawback of import duty reliefs is available for up to four years from the date of export; fraudulent TIR carnet use constitutes a criminal offence. No VAT clawback applies. Remittance and contract documents supporting freight proceeds must be retained for at least six years for trade-finance and tax-audit inspection.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both Gibraltar and Cayman Islands operate fully convertible currency regimes with unrestricted capital account transactions; profit remittances face no capital controls but require standard beneficial ownership and tax compliance verification

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds required; full repatriation and convertibility permitted for cross-border trucking revenues under TIR Carnets upon submission of supporting documentation including freight contracts and insurance certificates

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) administers a liberalised FX regime with no central bank; Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) oversees a free FX regime with no central bank

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No codified monetary threshold; OECD-aligned enforcement expects contemporaneous Local File documentation where aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed GBP 250,000 per annum (Master File at GBP 50 million group turnover/GBP 10 million local revenue), or when the taxpayer is selected for a TP audit.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations 2019 apply to every 'relevant entity' carrying on a 'relevant activity', including distribution/service centres, headquarters, financing and leasing. A Cayman Exempted Co operating TIR trucking or providing treasury/routing services from Gibraltar must demonstrate directed and managed in Gibraltar, adequate employees, premises, physical assets and annual spending. An annual ESR return is mandatory; shell/letterbox companies not satisfying class tests are subject to enhanced surveillance and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (deliberate under-declaration)

Statutory sanction: Civil assessment to tax plus penalty up to 100% of undercharged tax; criminal conviction punishable by a fine of up to GBP 10,000 per offence, confiscation of fleet proceeds, and dereferencing of the regulated transport licence.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil may be pierced; directors are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution, subject to travel bans, asset freezing orders, and imprisonment for up to seven years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty of GBP 50,000 for non-compliance with ESR; five-year strike-off/deregistration, cancellation of the Gibraltar external company registration, and forfeiture of benefits of the 12.5% regime.

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified for up to 15 years, made personally liable for the company's unpaid tax in an amount proportional to distributions received, and detained pending creditor restitution.
