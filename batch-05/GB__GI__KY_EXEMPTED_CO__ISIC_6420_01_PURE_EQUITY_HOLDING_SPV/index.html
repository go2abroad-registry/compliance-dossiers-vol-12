Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office ('Commissioner of Income Tax'), Ministry of Finance, HM Government of Gibraltar — official portal: www.gibraltar.gov.gi; corporate registration is effected at Companies House Gibraltar (Registrar of Companies) under the Companies Act 2014. Gibraltar is a separate fiscal territory from the UK: no UK HMRC or UK VAT registration arises from Gibraltar incorporation. Tax returns are submitted through the Income Tax Office e-services portal, and annual corporate filings are due per the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended).

E-government filing portal

1. Register the Cayman exempted company as an overseas company at Companies House Gibraltar (Part 22, Companies Act 2014): file a certified copy of the Cayman certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, particulars of directors/members, a Gibraltar service address, and (where required) a Gibraltar resident representative. 2. Apply to the Income Tax Office for the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) within 30 days of registration/commencement of activity: submit the corporate tax registration form, certified constitutional documents, overseas-company registration certificate, UBO particulars, and a statement of tax-residence position; the ITO issues the TRN after determining whether central management and control (and hence Gibraltar tax residence) exists. 3. File the annual corporate income tax return (with audited financial statements where required); a non-resident SPV files a limited territorial return claiming the participation exemption on qualifying dividends and disposal gains. 4. Register beneficial owners with the Gibraltar Registry of Beneficial Ownership and maintain economic-substance records (registered office, board minutes, statutory registers in Gibraltar) under the Economic Substance Regulations. 5. Retain Cayman formalities in parallel: annual return filing and Cayman tax exemption certificate under section 6 of the Companies Act (Cayman); no Cayman TIN is required for an exempted company with no reportable income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no US-style check-the-box regime; a Cayman exempted company is per se a juridical person subject to Gibraltar corporate law. Available statutory claims/elections: (i) the participation exemption — exemption from Gibraltar tax on qualifying dividend income and disposal gains where the SPV holds a participating interest (typically ≥10%) for at least 12 months in a subsidiary that is not resident in a non-qualifying jurisdiction; (ii) group relief/group taxation election for losses and intra-group transfers within a 75% group; (iii) foreign tax credit election for inbound withholding tax; (iv) OECD BEPS hybrid-mismatch alignment, restricting deduction/non-inclusion outcomes in cross-border structures; (v) reduced economic-substance compliance for pure equity holding SPVs. OECD Inclusive Framework/Pillar Two minimum-tax rules (15% top-up) apply to in-scope large MNE groups in Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands from 2025 and should be modelled for this intermediate holding structure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax (Gibraltar levies only customs/excise import duty on specific categories of goods under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST registration regime exists in Gibraltar. Customs/excise licensing applies only to importers or manufacturers of dutiable commodities (e.g., alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbons, vehicles); a pure equity holding SPV transacting no goods or services has no registration threshold to observe.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — Gibraltar has no VAT system, so there is no 0% export VAT rate, no input-VAT refund mechanism, and no VAT invoice requirement. Goods exported from Gibraltar are outside the customs duty charge; import duty drawback/re-export relief is available only where dutiable imported goods are subsequently exported under customs supervision. The SPV's passive equity-holding activity is neither a supply of goods nor services and therefore falls outside Gibraltar's indirect tax net; the operative tax benefit is the corporate participation exemption on dividends and capital gains.

None — Gibraltar operates no real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate. Commercial invoices in English are sufficient; records must be retained for tax audit (generally six years).

Gibraltar imposes no exchange controls and has no statutory FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark represents the pipeline's internal substantiation standard for cross-border dividend and receivable remittances. Misstatements attract: penalties under the Income Tax Act (up to 100% of tax undercharged plus interest), customs penalties up to three times the duty evaded with forfeiture and imprisonment under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance, and economic-substance penalty regimes for missing substance filings. Participation-exemption claims are retrospectively clawed back with interest where the SPV fails the qualifying-holding conditions (minimum holding period, qualifying residence, and trading/taxed-profits tests) or lacks commercial substance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar has no general exchange controls restricting conversion or movement of sterling, euros, United States dollars, or other major currencies. Current-account payments, including genuine dividends, interest, service fees, trade payments, and ordinary operating transfers, are generally transferable through the banking system without a statutory conversion quota. Capital-account transfers, including equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, acquisitions, disposals, and intercompany funding, are not subject to a general Gibraltar exchange-control approval regime, but banks must verify source of funds, beneficial ownership, economic purpose, sanctions exposure, and tax residence. A Cayman exempted company with no Gibraltar substance, no Gibraltar operating activity, and a passive holding purpose may face enhanced onboarding, correspondent-bank scrutiny, and requests for evidence that distributions are legally declared under Cayman law and are not disguised payments, sanctions evasion, or unlawful tax avoidance. Gibraltar banking access does not eliminate Cayman corporate, economic-substance, beneficial-ownership, tax-reporting, or withholding obligations. Dividend remittances should be supported by board resolutions, financial statements or distributable-reserves evidence, dividend vouchers, shareholder records, and underlying subsidiary distribution evidence. Gibraltar generally does not impose withholding tax on ordinary outbound dividends, but the tax treatment of interest, royalties, management fees, and payments involving related parties must be separately reviewed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory repatriation period, or prescribed conversion percentage for export receipts. Export receipts may generally be retained in the currency received or converted at the account holder's instruction, subject to bank documentation, sanctions screening, anti-money-laundering controls, and any contractual requirements of a lender, insurer, payment provider, or trade-finance bank. The Cayman exempted company must maintain transaction-level records demonstrating the commercial basis of receipts and payments, reconcile receipts to invoices or distribution documents, and comply with applicable accounting, tax, economic-substance, beneficial-ownership, and automatic-exchange-of-information requirements. Any payment involving a jurisdiction with separate exchange controls must also satisfy that jurisdiction's rules.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar does not operate a separate statutory central bank or a general exchange-control administration. The principal financial-services regulator is the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC). Monetary and currency matters are administered under Gibraltar legislation and by the Government of Gibraltar, with the Gibraltar pound maintained at parity with sterling and issued by the Government of Gibraltar. The Bank of England is not Gibraltar's central bank and does not supervise Gibraltar-incorporated companies as their primary prudential regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for limited payment functionality and Low to Medium for a passive Cayman holding company seeking unrestricted multi-currency treasury access. A Gibraltar-authorised electronic money institution or payment institution may provide safeguarded payment accounts, foreign-exchange services, and payment initiation, but an EMI account is not a bank deposit, does not provide ordinary deposit protection as a bank account, and may restrict investment-company, holding-company, high-value dividend, shareholder-loan, or pass-through activity. UK or European Economic Area EMI authorisation does not automatically confer Gibraltar passporting rights after the United Kingdom's withdrawal from the European Union. The provider must be authorised to serve Gibraltar or rely on a legally valid local framework. Offshore digital EMI onboarding remains subject to identity verification, source-of-wealth and source-of-funds review, UBO verification, sanctions screening, transaction monitoring, and restrictions on third-party payments. The company should not use an EMI as a substitute for a regulated bank where it requires commercial letters of credit, borrowing, escrow, custody, or stable long-term deposit services.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route equity subscriptions and shareholder loans from the investor's verified corporate bank account directly to the Cayman company's designated Gibraltar bank account or another account expressly approved by the receiving bank. Use a payment narrative identifying the legal entity, funding type, agreement date, and purpose, such as "equity subscription under [agreement]" or "shareholder loan under [agreement]". Gibraltar generally has no statutory FX purpose-code regime; banks instead require accurate payment descriptions, supporting corporate documents, and transaction-level source-of-funds evidence. Before remittance, provide the receiving bank with the subscription or loan agreement, board approvals, UBO information, originating-bank details, expected amount and currency, and any required tax or regulatory analysis. Avoid cash, personal remitters, unexplained third-party transfers, split payments designed to evade review, and circular funding. Reconcile the incoming wire to the capitalisation ledger, issue or register shares where applicable, record the loan and interest terms where applicable, and retain SWIFT or ISO 20022 payment evidence. Any capital injection into a subsidiary located in a separate exchange-control jurisdiction must also comply with that jurisdiction's registration, reporting, and conversion rules.For ordinary cross-border payments, use bank-to-bank SWIFT MT103 where still supported or the equivalent ISO 20022 customer-credit-transfer message, with accurate originator, beneficiary, invoice, contract, currency, and purpose information. Use documentary collections under URC 522 only where the counterparty and country risk are acceptable. For dividend, interest, management-fee, and intercompany payments, retain board approval, agreement, invoice or distribution notice, withholding-tax analysis, transfer-pricing support, and evidence of distributable profits.
