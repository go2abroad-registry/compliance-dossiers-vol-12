Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar. Official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation-and-financial-services. Indirect tax (Purchase Tax and customs duties) is administered by the Customs and Excise Department, HM Government of Gibraltar (https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/customs). The Cayman Islands General Registry is the company registrar in the home jurisdiction; no Cayman direct tax authority exists because the Cayman Islands imposes no income tax, corporation tax, or capital gains tax.

E-government filing portal

The KY_EXEMPTED_CO, as a Cayman Exempted Company, receives no Cayman TIN because Cayman has no direct tax registration. To operate in or from Gibraltar with a statutory tax presence, the entity must first register as an oversea company with Companies House Gibraltar using the Overseas Company Registration application (Form F-0). The submission must include the Cayman certificate of incorporation, certified memorandum and articles of association, a current certificate of good standing from the Cayman Registrar of Companies, particulars of directors and secretary, a Gibraltar registered office address, and the appointment of a Gibraltar-licensed corporate or individual agent. Upon acceptance, the Income Tax Office assigns a Gibraltar Tax Returns Number (TRN). For Gibraltar Purchase Tax, a separate Customs registration is required only for importation of physical goods into Gibraltar; cross-border remote software services do not trigger Purchase Tax registration. No Gibraltar VAT identification number can be obtained because Gibraltar is not within the EU or UK VAT area. If the entity uses Paddle or Stripe as merchant of record, those intermediaries may hold the global VAT/GST registration obligations for the underlying supply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or comparable entity classification election exists under Gibraltar or Cayman law. The former Gibraltar 'Qualifying Offshore Company' election was abolished; the current default is entity-level taxation at 10% corporation tax for companies resident and managed or controlled in Gibraltar, with non-resident companies taxed only on Gibraltar-source income. The Cayman Exempted Company cannot elect to be transparent or disregarded for Gibraltar tax purposes. Hybrid mismatch principles under Gibraltar's Income Tax Act, aligned with OECD and UK rules, treat the Cayman company as a separate opaque legal entity, not as a pass-through.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

VAT/GST Not Applicable; Gibraltar Purchase Tax (tax on physical goods only)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for Gibraltar Purchase Tax: registration is event-driven on the first import of chargeable goods into Gibraltar. For UK VAT, if the Cayman entity itself makes taxable supplies of remote digital services to UK consumers through a UK establishment or at source, the then-current UK VAT registration threshold of £85,000 (2024/25) applies only to UK-established businesses; non-established suppliers generally have no threshold and must register for UK VAT from the first supply unless using an eligible non-UK OSS scheme or relying on the merchant of record.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable to the core software supply: Gibraltar Purchase Tax and Cayman customs duty apply only to physical goods. Electronically supplied remote screen-share and IoT telemetry software is an intangible service outside the scope of Gibraltar Purchase Tax; therefore no zero-rating claim exists. If the Cayman entity becomes UK VAT registered, supplies of digital services to non-UK business customers are outside the scope of UK VAT under place-of-supply rules, while supplies to UK consumers are standard-rated. Export zero-rating under UK VAT is reserved for tangible exported goods and requires proof of physical removal from the UK within the statutory time limit.

  • Customs Export Declaration/Entry (electronic Single Administrative Document or export declaration reference)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (proof of physical removal from the UK/Gibraltar for tangible exports)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/FX receipt evidencing receipt of export consideration from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial Invoice (for exported goods or the invoiced service)
  • Packing List (for physical consignments)

Gibraltar imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, tax API, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate. Purchase Tax is declared through customs entries; corporate income tax is filed annually via the Income Tax Office. If the entity later registers for UK VAT, it must maintain digital VAT records and submit returns through Making Tax Digital (MTD)-compatible software using HMRC APIs; electronic invoicing is permitted but not mandatory. Third-party merchant-of-record providers (Stripe, Paddle) may issue tax-compliant VAT/GST invoices on behalf of the supplier in foreign jurisdictions.

Fraudulent zero-rating or export VAT refund claims, if the entity has UK VAT exposure, are prosecuted by HMRC under the Value Added Tax Act 1994: civil penalties up to 100% of the tax at risk for deliberate inaccuracy, fraud penalties, and criminal liability for dishonest evasion are possible. Retrospective recovery generally extends to 4 years for careless inaccuracies and 20 years for deliberate concealment. In Gibraltar, evasion of Purchase Tax may result in Customs seizure, monetary penalties up to twice the duty, and criminal prosecution; no Purchase Tax refund mechanism exists for exported goods because the tax is charged at the import stage, and the Cayman entity has no entitlement to export refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full current and capital account convertibility. The Gibraltar Pound is freely convertible at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP, and there are no restrictions on the conversion of USD, EUR, or other major currencies. Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and capital repatriation are permitted without prior regulatory approval or withholding in Gibraltar. For the Cayman Exempted Company, no Cayman Exchange Control restrictions apply; however, the entity must avoid management-and-control being exercised from Gibraltar to prevent inadvertent Gibraltar tax residence. The key profit remittance hurdle arises from UK anti-money laundering and economic substance documentation expectations, not from FX restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists for export proceeds in Gibraltar. Export receipts can be retained in any convertible currency, and there is no requirement to surrender foreign currency to the Government of Gibraltar or the Bank of England. The Cayman Islands also impose no export surrender rules. The only applicable obligations relate to record-keeping and AML transaction reporting for cross-border receipts routed through UK or EU payment systems, which require end-to-end beneficiary and purpose-code transparency.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for Sterling) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the local banking and forex supervisory authority; Gibraltar has no separate central bank and the Gibraltar Pound is issued by the Government of Gibraltar under a currency board arrangement pegged at par to GBP.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection from the Cayman parent to the Gibraltar operating account must follow a strictly documented wire protocol: (i) adopt a board resolution in Cayman authorising the share capital contribution and identifying the exact amount in GBP or USD; (ii) open a segregated Capital Escrow Account in Gibraltar in the name of the Cayman Exempted Company; (iii) transmit via SWIFT MT103 from a licensed Cayman bank (e.g., Butterfield Bank (Cayman) or Cayman National Bank) to the Gibraltar receiving bank, using SWIFT purpose code CAIT (Capital Investment) or their operational equivalent for direct investment; (iv) ensure the payment narrative includes the corporate registration number, beneficiary name, and explicit phrase 'Capital Injection - Unpaid Capital under Cayman Companies Act (Exempted Company)'; (v) provide the Gibraltar receiving bank with a copy of the resolution, a source-of-funds statement showing the Cayman bank account history, and confirmation that no funds originate from the sale of negotiable bearer instruments. All funds should clear via the correspondent banking network: GBP via CHAPS in London, USD via CHIPS/Fedwire, EUR via SEPA or TARGET2.Trade disbursements from the Gibraltar operating account to global suppliers, contractors, tax authorities, and the Cayman parent must use the following clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for high-value or one-off invoices, with proper purpose codes (e.g., 'Software licensing fees', 'Subcontractor services', 'Dividend distribution') and the beneficiary bank's BIC; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary UCP 600) for hardware procurements connected to IoT telemetry devices, issued by the Gibraltar bank against pro-forma invoices and transport documents; (iii) SEPA credit transfers for low-value EUR obligations within the EEA; (iv) intercompany profit distributions to the Cayman parent via MT103 using purpose code 'DIV' and accompanied by a distribution certificate.
