Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital: a single subscriber share at nominal value (e.g. GBP 0.01) satisfies Companies Act s.10, and capital may lawfully remain uncalled until demanded. Banking and EMI AML policies normally expect an initial declared paid-up capital of approximately GBP 1-10. Where capital is parked in escrow, a licensed corporate service provider holds the funds in a client account and releases them only after the Registry filings and bank/EMI onboarding are complete.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Precautions (Workplace) Regulations 2015: annual fire risk assessment and GFRS issuance of the Fire Certificate confirming means of escape, first-aid firefighting appliances, and emergency lighting. Structural load inspection by the DPC Building Control Office covers floor loading for racking systems and any mezzanine installation. Occupational clearance under the Gibraltar Health and Safety at Work etc. Act and the Workplace (Health, Safety and Welfare) Regulations includes PAT testing of electrical installations, first-aid provision, staff fire marshal training, and designation of an occupational safety officer for dispatch operations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, HM Government of Gibraltar Income Tax Office, 8 Midtown, Gibraltar GX11 1AA; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation; e-services and company self-assessment returns are submitted through the Government of Gibraltar tax e-filing platform.

E-government filing portal

Within 21 days of commencing business in Gibraltar, a Cayman Exempted Company must first register as an external company under the Gibraltar Companies Act with a Gibraltar registered office and a local process agent, then submit an electronic company tax registration application to the Commissioner of Income Tax attaching the Cayman certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, Gibraltar registered-office certificate, register of directors and members, an application for a Gibraltar EORI number (format XEGI) for customs purposes, and an estimate of Gibraltar-source assessable profits. The Income Tax Office issues a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) electronically, normally within five business days of a complete filing, and the company becomes a reporting taxpayer required to file an annual company tax return by the statutory filing date (generally 30 November) with payment due by 31 December of the following year.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar law provides no check-the-box or entity-classification election comparable to the United States Internal Revenue Code section 7701 regime. A Cayman Exempted Company becomes a Gibraltar tax resident automatically when central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, and may not elect out of that status. Taxpayers may, however, apply in writing to the Commissioner for an apportionment election allocating mixed-source trading profits between Gibraltar-source and foreign-source components, supported by transfer-pricing documentation and contemporaneous records; the election is discretionary and must be approved before the filing of the affected return. The entity may also elect into the reduced corporate-tax computation applicable to small companies only if it satisfies the statutory gross-assets and employee thresholds; no hybrid mismatch, territorial remittance, or offshore-relief elections are otherwise available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar has no VAT or general sales tax; it is outside the EU VAT area, and indirect taxation is limited to import duties under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance and excise duties on specified goods. For e-commerce into the EU, the relevant indirect tax is the destination Member State's VAT, administered through the Import One-Stop Shop (IOSS) or ordinary non-Union VAT registration; for e-commerce into the United Kingdom, UK VAT applies under the Overseas Goods Regime.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in Gibraltar: there is no VAT/GST registration threshold because no VAT/GST regime operates. Export-orientated e-commerce into the EU from Gibraltar triggers VAT registration obligations in the destination state: the EUR 10,000 EU OSS distance-selling threshold is available only to EU-established businesses and cannot be used by a Gibraltar seller, so the Cayman Exempted Company must either (i) register for VAT in each EU Member State once sales to that state begin or exceed that state's non-Union distance-selling rules, or (ii) use IOSS for low-value consignments with an intrinsic value not exceeding EUR 150 and charge VAT at the destination-state rate. For consignments above EUR 150, the buyer is liable to EU import VAT and a customs declaration at the first point of entry unless the seller uses a DDP model with a fiscal representative. For deliveries into the United Kingdom, an overseas seller must register for UK VAT from the first sale because the UK distance-selling threshold for overseas sellers was abolished; UK VAT is chargeable at 20% on goods valued at GBP 135 or less, whereas goods above GBP 135 are instead subject to import VAT collected at the UK border unless a licensed fulfilment business is used.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT system, and therefore no statutory export zero-rating or VAT refund mechanism operates at Gibraltar level: a physical export of hydroponics and gardening equipment from Gibraltar to a third country is outside the scope of Gibraltar import duty and is relieved through the customs export procedure. If the same goods are exported from the United Kingdom through a UK VAT registration, a 0% rate may be claimed under VATA 1994, Schedule 8, Group 2, provided the supplier exports the goods outside the UK within the statutory time limit (generally three months from the time of supply), retains the approved export evidence, and receives payment in foreign currency or by an approved payment method within 180 days. Offshore services supplied from Gibraltar to overseas customers are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax; however, electronically supplied B2C services to EU final consumers are taxed at the customer's location, requiring VAT registration in that Member State unless the customer is a business that self-accounts under the reverse-charge mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar Tradeport / UK CDS submission) containing the correct HS classification for hydroponics equipment (typically headings 8419, 3926, or 9406)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment from Gibraltar or a United Kingdom port or airport to the overseas destination
  • Commercial Invoice stating Incoterms 2020, unit prices, currency, and country of origin
  • Packing List with weights, package marks, and container numbers
  • EORI certificate of the Gibraltar establishment or UK establishment, as the case may be
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign-currency bank credit advice evidencing receipt of sales proceeds from the overseas buyer

Gibraltar does not operate a national e-invoicing clearance platform, real-time fiscal-tax reporting API, or digital tax-stamping system analogous to Spain's SII, Italy's fattura elettronica, or Poland's KSeF. Paper and PDF Commercial invoices remain valid for Gibraltar income tax and customs purposes. However, all goods movements into and out of Gibraltar must be declared electronically through the Gibraltar Tradeport system (functionally aligned with the UK Customs Declaration Service), and an EORI number is mandatory prior to any declaration. If the company makes EU distance sales, it must maintain an electronic sales record and submit periodic IOSS or OSS returns in the destination Member State; where VAT registration is obtained in an EU state, that state's applicable invoicing and digital-reporting regulations govern those records.

A false declaration or omission made to obtain any Gibraltar export relief or duty suspension attracts forfeiture of the goods and a monetary penalty of up to three times the duty evaded, with criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance. For any UK VAT zero-rating claimed on the UK leg, HMRC may impose a penalty of up to 100% of the tax under VATA 1994, section 60 (deliberate inaccuracy) or section 61 (dishonest understatement), together with daily penalties, statutory interest, and a dishonesty penalty; assessments and clawback of the export zero-rating may be made retrospectively up to 6 years for careless claims and up to 20 years for deliberate concealment under FA 2007, Schedule 24. If export proceeds are not received or repatriated within the 180-day deadline, or if the approved export evidence is missing, the tax authority is entitled to recharacterise the supply as domestic, deny the export relief, and assess VAT or import duty retroactively at the statutory rate with interest and, in serious cases, refer the matter to the Gibraltar Attorney General or HMRC Prosecutions for criminal proceedings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Cayman Islands law imposes no exchange controls; current and capital account transactions are freely convertible, and dividends/redemptions may be remitted without prior regulatory consent. Gibraltar likewise maintains a liberalised FX environment with no profit-remittance restrictions for foreign-owned companies, although the company must maintain tax-residence separation (Cayman economic substance) to avoid being deemed UK/Gibraltar tax-resident, which would trigger domestic corporate tax obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Neither Cayman nor Gibraltar imposes surrender or conversion ratios for export proceeds. The e-commerce operator may retain 100% of sales revenue in foreign currency; no statutory timeline or percentage must be repatriated into GBP/GIP. Bank account statements and payment processor records must evidence the transaction trail.

Central bank supervision: Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) for the Cayman Exempted Company; Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) for Gibraltar banking and payment services; Gibraltar's currency issuance is administered by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act, with the Gibraltar pound pegged 1:1 to GBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-High for UK/FCA-licensed EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business) when used as auxiliary turbo-settlement rails, but not as the primary bank account. Gibraltar has a thin domestic EMI-licensing stock; therefore, most e-commerce accounts settle via a regulated Gibraltar bank integrated with card processors. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File arise where the Gibraltar entity's annual revenue exceeds GIP 10,000,000 or same-year intra-group controlled transactions exceed GIP 1,000,000; below these thresholds, although no filing is mandatory, a concise TP report must be retained for the Income Tax Office on request.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies Economic Substance (ES) guiding rules under the Income Tax Act: a resident entity performing any relevant activity—including distribution/service centre or financing—must maintain appropriate premises, employees, expenditure, and board decision-taking in Gibraltar. An online hydroponics e-tailer that books revenue in Gibraltar must establish that strategic commercial decisions, inventory risk, and sales functions occur on-island; absence of substance can reclassify profits as Cayman-source and trigger tax assessments, penalties, or exchange of information reports.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / failure to file

Statutory sanction: Civil financial penalty up to 100% of the unpaid tax; fine up to GIP 100,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years on conviction; any DTA relief is revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in fraudulent tax suppression are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debt; the court may issue travel bans, asset freezes, disqualification as a director for up to 15 years, and restitution orders requiring personal repayment.
Shell company and false economic substance

Statutory sanction: Reassessment on deemed Gibraltar profits, penalty of GIP 50,000 to GIP 500,000 for substance misstatement, cancellation of the company's registration or licence, and automatic referral to the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission for AML/EU transparency enforcement.

Director personal liability: Officers may face civil liability for the entire tax under-assessment and director disqualification; deliberate false substance statements are an either-way offence attracting 5 years' imprisonment.
Money laundering (AML/CTF)

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Criminal Justice Act, penalty on indictment is unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 14 years; confiscation orders may seize not only tainted funds but all assets obtained with the profits.

Director personal liability: Senior officers conducting or approving such transactions are deemed principal offenders; personal assets can be restrained, travel bans imposed during investigation, and directors barred from future corporate appointments unless a full defence of no knowledge or reasonable mitigation is proven.
