Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office of HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax) for direct taxation and HM Customs Gibraltar for indirect taxation; official government portal: www.gibraltar.gov.gi

E-government filing portal

As a non-resident offshore entity, the Cayman Exempted Company is not required to incorporate in Gibraltar. If it establishes a taxable nexus or engages in regulated import activity in Gibraltar, the sequence is: (1) register as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 2014 and appoint a Gibraltar-resident process agent, obtaining a Gibraltar Company Reference Number; (2) file a protective application with the Income Tax Office and obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number on first becoming assessable or when appointed as a withholding agent; (3) produce the Cayman Tax Exemption Certificate (issued under s.6 of the Cayman Companies Act) as evidence of entity-level tax-exempt status; (4) register with HM Customs Gibraltar as Importer of Record if the company controls the import chain, holds inventory in Gibraltar, or operates a customs warehouse; (5) complete the Gibraltar beneficial-ownership register filing with the Financial Services Commission. No minimum capital filing is triggered by the Cayman constitutive documents, and no statutory audit is imposed solely on the basis of foreign incorporation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime; classification follows legal form under the Income Tax Act 2010, and hybrid mismatch arbitrage is counteracted by ATAD-derived rules transposed into Gibraltar law. Relevant statutory elections and positions include: (i) group relief election for intra-group loss surrender between qualifying Gibraltar companies; (ii) determination of non-residence where central management and control is exercised outside Gibraltar, confining liability to Gibraltar-source income on a territorial basis; (iii) relief-at-source and treaty election procedures under Gibraltar's Double Taxation Agreements and Tax Information Exchange Agreements; (iv) election of accounting period and reporting currency aligned to the Cayman statutory accounts, subject to Commissioner approval.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Purchase Tax — single-stage duty on importation of goods into Gibraltar; no VAT or GST regime applies

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in Gibraltar Pounds (GIP): purchase tax is collected at the customs border at importation and is not triggered by economic turnover or distance-sales thresholds. Any business controlling the import chain, holding stock in Gibraltar, or operating a customs warehousing facility must obtain an approved importer/warehousekeeper authorization from HM Customs Gibraltar. Where goods are sold remotely into the UK or EU from Gibraltar, the destination jurisdiction's import VAT or distance-selling registration thresholds may apply as a supply-side obligation, but these are not Gibraltar fiscal thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable by way of VAT zero-rating because Gibraltar levies no VAT/GST. Exports and re-exports of portable solar generators and battery packs from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Purchase Tax. Eligibility criteria: (i) goods must be physically exported under an approved Gibraltar customs export declaration; (ii) imported goods that are re-exported without consumption in Gibraltar may be removed from duty-suspension regimes (bonded warehouse, transhipment, or outward processing) free of purchase tax; (iii) the export evidence must demonstrate physical departure from Gibraltar, a non-Gibraltar destination, and receipt by an overseas consignee; (iv) purchase tax mistakenly paid at importation can be reclaimed from HM Customs Gibraltar against satisfactory proof of export; no standing refund mechanism comparable to a VAT refund pool exists.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration / Exit Summary Declaration lodged electronically through a licensed customs agent
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical removal of goods from Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice showing export proceeds credited to the entity's UK EMI or Gibraltar bank account
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List with Incoterms and non-Gibraltar destination details
  • Bonded Warehouse / Re-export Entry records where duty-suspension relief is claimed
  • IATA/IMDG Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods (UN 3480 / UN 3481) applicable to lithium-ion battery cargo

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), digital stamping, or mandatory e-invoicing API regime is in force in Gibraltar for private-sector B2B/B2C invoicing. Standard PDF/EDI invoicing is accepted, and fiscal records must be retained for the Income Tax Act 2010 limitation period, normally six years. If the entity directs sales into UK/EU markets, destination-market e-invoicing and real-time reporting requirements may apply in the customer's jurisdiction, which are separate compliance obligations rather than Gibraltar clearance mandates.

HM Customs Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office jointly scrutinize export claims. False or overstated export declarations, fictitious bills of lading, circular remittances, and fabricated Inward Remittance Certificates expose the taxpayer to: (i) seizure and forfeiture of the goods; (ii) immediate assessment and recovery of the unpaid purchase tax with statutory interest; (iii) civil penalties of up to three times the duty under the Customs and Duties Ordinance; (iv) income tax penalties of up to 100% of the tax avoided where the same records affect Gibraltar assessments; and (v) criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion of duty or tax, carrying unlimited fines and imprisonment of up to seven years. Retrospective clawback applies within the full assessment window, which is extended where fraud or wilful default is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar Pound (GIP) is pegged at full parity 1:1 with GBP and is freely convertible. Neither the UK nor Gibraltar maintains exchange control legislation (Exchange Control Act 1947 repealed), so current account and capital account transactions are fully liberalised. Dividend distribution, profit remittance, and loan servicing from Gibraltar to a Cayman Exempted Company shareholder require no central bank approval or licensing. The only practical hurdles are bank-level AML/KYC documentary satisfaction, CRS/FATCA reporting on beneficial owners, and Gibraltar's annual substance reporting under the Taxation (Companies - Economic Substance) Act 2019.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender obligation for export or retail sales proceeds held in a Gibraltar corporate account. Receipts in GBP, GIP, USD or EUR may be retained indefinitely. Compliance discipline requires that proceeds remain fully traceable through Stripe and Affirm settlement reports to satisfy audit trails and tax-file integrity.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of England exercises monetary authority for the Sterling Area; the Government of Gibraltar issues Gibraltar Pound (GIP) notes under the Currency Notes Act 1934; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) supervises all banking, payment and AML conduct under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; HM Treasury retains jurisdiction over UK-wide sanctions and counter-terrorist financing regimes that bind all Gibraltar firms.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar: contemporaneous Local File is expected when cross-border related-party transactions exceed GIP 1,000,000 in a fiscal year; a Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least GIP 50,000,000 or EUR 750,000,000 under OECD standards.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Under Cayman ESR, the exempted company must show substance if carrying on a relevant activity such as ‘distribution and service centre’; the Gibraltar ESR similarly requires management, staff, premises, and core income-generating activities in the claimed jurisdiction. A Cayman company claiming Gibraltar tax residence must be fully managed there; otherwise both jurisdictions can apply penalties and recharacterise profits as attributable to shell operations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to 100% of the tax lost; criminal fines up to GIP 20,000 per count; imprisonment up to 7 years; direct tax assessments reopened for 20 years in fraud cases.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for unpaid corporate tax, ordered to make restitution, disqualified from management for up to 15 years, and subject to travel bans and extradition on fiscal offences.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Cayman ESR: KYD 10,000–KYD 100,000 fines; failure notices, striking-off, and compulsory winding up. Gibraltar: fines up to GIP 10,000 plus daily default penalty, tax residence revocation, and loss of treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve false ESR notifications are jointly and severally liable for the company's fines; non-compliance can trigger personal contribution orders, disqualification, and seizure of personal assets.
AML / Money Laundering and Sanctions Evasion

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act: on indictment, a fine up to GIP 5,000,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years; civil freezing orders; suspicious activity report failure fines; company restoration or liquidation may be ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and even beneficial owners can face personal criminal liability, travel bans, banking prohibition, and mandatory compensation orders equal to the value laundered.
