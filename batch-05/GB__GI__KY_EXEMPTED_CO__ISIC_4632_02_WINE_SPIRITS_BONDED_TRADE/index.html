Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

United Kingdom: HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) via https://www.gov.uk/hmrc. Gibraltar: HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax) and Customs & Excise Division via https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/tax

E-government filing portal

The Cayman Exempted Company does not receive a UK TIN automatically. To operate a bond warehouse in Gibraltar, the entity must first register as a foreign company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act, obtaining a Gibraltar Company Number. The branch must then file an income tax registration return with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office, which issues a Gibraltar Tax Reference (TIN). Because Gibraltar has no VAT system, no Gibraltar VAT ID is issued; a separate UK VAT registration would only be required if taxable supplies are made in the UK mainland.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Cayman exempted company; for Gibraltar purposes the branch is a non-resident territorial candidate, taxable only on Gibraltar-source trading profits attributable to the branch unless centrally managed and controlled in Gibraltar, in which case entity-level Gibraltar tax may apply).

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not recognise US-style check-the-box elections. The Cayman Exempted Company can obtain a Cayman tax-exemption certificate under section 6 of the Cayman Companies Act. Under Gibraltar law, the company may elect to file as a resident or non-resident company for Income Tax Act purposes. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; OECD BEPS Action 2 is the default framework.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Excise Duty and Customs Duty regime; no VAT/GST is levied in Gibraltar.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold applies. The authorised excise warehousekeeper must hold a licence granted by the Customs & Excise Commissioner before any duty-suspended receipt, storage or export of wine/spirits occurs; any importation into Gibraltar also requires customs registration regardless of volume or value.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Since Gibraltar does not levy VAT, no VAT zero-rating applies. Exports from Gibraltar may be relieved from excise duty under the duty-suspension mechanism if: (i) the wine/spirits are supplied to a customer outside Gibraltar; (ii) they are physically exported from Gibraltar under an approved customs procedure; and (iii) the export is evidenced by compliant documentation. No offshore services zero-rating relief exists for the wine/spirits wholesale business.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar Customs electronic system)
  • Bill of Lading / CMR / air waybill evidencing physical removal from Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank payment evidence for the export sale
  • Bonded Warehouse Removal Warrant and excise-duty suspension documentation
  • Certificate of origin (for documentary audit and tariff preference)

Gibraltar Customs and Excise operates an electronic declaration platform for customs and excise movements; declarations must be lodged electronically and invoices must be retained in a structured machine-readable format for inspection. There is no general real-time fiscal clearance/e-invoicing stamping requirement beyond warehouse entry/exit control and customs declaration processing.

Gibraltar Customs may reopen export files for up to six years and claw back unpaid excise duty if goods are shown not to have left Gibraltar. Penalties include a civil penalty of up to 100% of the duty evaded, fixed penalties for false declarations, forfeiture of goods and means of transport, and criminal prosecution with unlimited fines and imprisonment for fraudulent duty evasion. Bonded warehouse records must be retained for at least six years and are subject to unannounced physical stock audits.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current- and capital-account convertibility is available in both the United Kingdom and Gibraltar; no resident or non-resident exchange control permissions are required for capital injections, profit distributions, or dividend repatriation. The Gibraltar pound is pegged at par to GBP and is freely convertible. Profit remittance from a Gibraltar-operating Cayman exempted company can be made through ordinary interbank transfers without prior regulatory consent, subject only to transaction-amount reporting thresholds under the Financial Action Task Force standards and periodic CIMA filing obligations in the Cayman Islands.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds receivable in Gibraltar or the wider UK for bonded-warehouse wine and spirits trade. Export receipts may be retained indefinitely in any convertible currency, with no obligation to surrender into Sterling or Gibraltar pounds. Only standard anti-money laundering transaction monitoring and suspicious activity reporting obligations apply to the collecting bank.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (ultimate monetary authority for Sterling) and HM Treasury (exchange-control and sanctions oversight); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) exercises day-to-day prudential and AML supervision of licensed banks and payment institutions in Gibraltar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium. FCA-authorised UK electronic money institutions may offer multi-currency payment wallets to a Gibraltar-based Cayman exempted company, but most onshore EMIs reject offshore vehicles due to beneficial-ownership opacity. GFSC-licensed Gibraltar EMIs are preferable for local Sterling clearing; unlicensed offshore digital EMIs are legally unavailable for both GBP and cross-border EUR/USD transfers. Any EMI must be used as an ancillary, low-balance clearing rail while the primary licensed bank remains the treasury anchor.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% standard Gibraltar corporate income tax rate, applied to the Cayman Exempted Company solely on profits attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment or where management and control is exercised in Gibraltar; Cayman domestic corporate income tax is nil.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% – Gibraltar does not levy a dividend withholding tax on distributions by a Gibraltar resident company or on profits remitted by a Gibraltar branch to its foreign head office; dividends are paid gross to UK, EU or Cayman shareholders and are taxed only in the recipient's country of residence.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations 2019 (ESR) classify bonded wine/spirits trading as a 'distribution and service centre'; the Cayman Exempted Company must demonstrate that core income-generating activities, adequate staff, qualifying premises and operating expenditure are present in Gibraltar, and must file an annual ESR return; the Cayman entity remains concurrently subject to Cayman Islands Economic Substance filings with the Department for International Tax Cooperation. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Excise Duty Fraud

Statutory sanction: Tax-geared penalties up to 200% of the tax understated plus fixed penalties of £100-£5,000 under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act; under the Customs and Excise statutes, confiscation of spirits, duty not less than three times the excise evaded, and imprisonment for up to 7 years for fraudulent evasion of spirit duty.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for fraudulent trading under the Gibraltar Companies Act; courts may impose travel bans, freezing and confiscation orders, and terms of imprisonment up to 7 years; the corporate veil is pierced where the Cayman Exempted Company is operated as a sham to mask taxable bonded trade.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR penalties of GIP 10,000 to GIP 100,000 per year of non-compliance, mandatory exchange of information with UK HMRC and EU/OECD authorities, strike-off of the Gibraltar branch, and reallocation of profits to the actual place of effective management outside Gibraltar.

AML/CFT Compliance Failure (Medium_Risk_Enhanced)

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar AML Regulations 2023 and Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 impose fines up to GIP 1,000,000 per breach on legal persons, imprisonment up to 5 years for money laundering, licence suspension of the bonded warehouse activity by the GFSC, and civil penalties proportionate to the transaction turnover.
