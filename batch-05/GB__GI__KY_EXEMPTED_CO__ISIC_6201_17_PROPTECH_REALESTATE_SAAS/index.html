Competent tax authority

Legal entity and foreign-branch registration: Registrar of Companies, Companies House Gibraltar. Customs: Commissioner of Customs, Gibraltar Customs Department. The United Kingdom (HMRC) has no direct registration authority over Gibraltar entities; Gibraltar is outside the UK VAT area and enjoys fiscal autonomy as a British Overseas Territory.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with territorial sourcing. The Gibraltar branch of the Cayman Exempted Company is an opaque corporation for Gibraltar tax purposes and is chargeable to Gibraltar corporate income tax at the standard rate of 12.5% on profits accruing in or derived from Gibraltar; pass-through treatment is not available. The Cayman entity itself is Tax_Exempt_Zone in the Cayman Islands under the Tax Concessions Act (tax exemption certificate), but that status does not extend to Gibraltar. Branch profits are computed as if the branch were a separate enterprise under the arm's-length principle, with allocation of capital, risk, and functions. Multinational groups with consolidated turnover above EUR 750 million are also within the Gibraltar Pillar Two global minimum tax legislation aligned to the OECD 15% rate.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; the Income Tax Act 2010 defines a company to include any body corporate, so the Cayman Exempted Company is classified as opaque and may not elect transparency. Available elections and mechanisms: (1) group relief election for surrender of losses within a 75% group under the Income Tax Act 2010 group-relief provisions, subject to anti-loss-buying and hybrid-loss limitations; (2) the Taxation (Anti-Hybrid) Rules 2018/2020 (ATAD-aligned) governing hybrid and reverse-hybrid mismatches, which are neutral here unless another jurisdiction treats the Cayman vehicle or Gibraltar branch as transparent; (3) binding tax rulings and advance pricing agreements from the Commissioner of Income Tax, valid up to five years, for territorial sourcing, GST classification of SaaS, and transfer pricing; (4) elective transitional CbC reporting safe harbour under the Gibraltar global minimum tax rules; and (5) voluntary GST registration election for zero-rated exporters to recover input tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates as a fully convertible, no-exchange-control economy. The Gibraltar Pound (GIP) circulates at strict 1:1 legal tender parity with GBP, and commercial banks accept GBP and GIP interchangeably without any statutory limit or surrender duty. Both capital-account transactions (equity injections, intercompany loans, treasury deposits) and current-account transactions (subscription receipts, licensing fees, rent and management fees) are entirely free of quantity, price, maturity or beneficiary restrictions. Profit remittance by a non-resident Cayman Exempted Company is not subject to any Gibraltar withholding tax, prior-authorisation or central-bank licence. The only practical limitations are (a) UK/Gibraltar/national sanctions screening of counterparties and (b) potential correspondent-bank de-risking in the US/EU wholesale payment chain; neither constitutes a legal convertibility barrier.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender rule, mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation deadline or foreign-currency balance quota in Gibraltar. A PropTech SaaS exporting ISIC 6201 / NAICS 531390 services may retain 100% of its USD, GBP and EUR export proceeds offshore in either the Cayman treasury account or a Gibraltar bank account, with no obligation to convert into GIP. Export receipts from recurring software subscriptions and rental-screening data services are classified as current-account receipts; although banks may require a documented commercial invoice or service agreement to satisfy wire-transfer rules, no revenue is required to be routed through the Bank of England or GFSC.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar does not maintain an independent central bank; monetary policy is anchored by the Bank of England (United Kingdom), while the issue of Gibraltar Pounds is vested in HM Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory regulator for banking, e-money, payments and AML/CFT supervision, operating in tandem with the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High-Medium. Gibraltar-licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs), authorised under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Regulations 2014 and the Financial Services (Payment Services) Regulations 2018, may lawfully issue e-money, hold safeguarded client funds, process card payments and route SEPA/UK/US disbursements for a Cayman Exempted Company. A new-generation EMI is strongly viable as an acquisition rail for Stripe_Billing and ACH_Direct collections because it avoids the heavy onboarding of a tier-1 bank. Critical legal constraints: (i) EMIs may not accept deposits or extend credit; surplus treasury liquidity must be held in a segregated safeguarding account or with a tier-1 commercial bank; (ii) client funds must be protected via segregation or an equivalent insurance/guarantee mechanism under GFSC rules; (iii) following Brexit, Gibraltar EMIs no longer possess automatic EEA/UK passporting, so UK/EEA payment reach is achieved through an FCA-registered UK branch, EU agent or licensed processor; (iv) ACH Direct requires a US sponsor bank, imposing a correspondent-bank overlay; and (v) although the Cayman class is 'Low_Risk_Standard' on the AML tier matrix, the offshore corporate form will still trigger enhanced due diligence and a commercial-substance questionnaire at the EMI onboarding desk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an investor/VC capital injection into the Cayman Exempted Company, route via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 COVER credit transfers only. The sender must be the named investor with the beneficiary account held under the Cayman company's legal name at Jyske Bank (Gibraltar) or the Gibraltar International Bank. The narrative/purpose reference must recite the corporate authorisation, e.g., 'Capital contribution — Board Resolution 2024-07-I01'. Gibraltar law does not require a capital-control permission, but GFSC-sanctioned banks will verify: (i) the capital-call notice or subscription agreement; (ii) investor KYC packs; (iii) the Cayman registered-agent confirmation; and (iv) economic-substance notification. No CHAPS/SEPA threshold applies to inbound equity; however, US/UK sanctions screenings and the Wire Transfer (Information on the Payer) Regulations make it imperative to avoid omnibus or intermediary-chain ambiguity. Thereafter, funds may be downstreamed to a Gibraltar operating account or to the Stripe treasury via an integrated FX sweep.Outbound clearing rails in Gibraltar: (i) SWIFT MT103 for vendor payments, contractor settlements and landlord disbursements; (ii) commercial letter of credit (MT700/MT707) issued at Jyske/Fideuram only if a property or tech supply counterparty insists on LC terms — rare for SaaS but available; (iii) SEPA Credit Transfer / SEPA Instant for EEA payees following GBP-to-EUR conversion; (iv) CHAPS same-day Sterling for UK settlement and TARGET2 for EUR; and (v) US ACH Direct for payees in the United States, executed through a US sponsor bank or payments partner connected to the Gibraltar account. The treasury policy should order the rails by cost: ACH/domestic rails first, then SEPA, then SWIFT MT103, with LC reserved for high-value obligational transactions. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% standard corporate profits tax rate for all Gibraltar-resident companies and for Gibraltar-source profits of non-resident companies; the rate applies from year of assessment 2016/17 under the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Gibraltar levies no withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar-resident company to its Cayman exempted parent, and Gibraltar does not tax dividend income received by non-resident shareholders; there is no Gibraltar capital duty, no branch remittance tax, and no additional profits tax on distributions; Cayman likewise imposes no dividend withholding tax upon the ultimate receipt.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Gibraltar year of assessment runs from 1 July to 30 June; a company is assessed on the profits of its accounting period ending in the relevant year of assessment; the return and any payment of tax are due within 30 days of the date of the notice of assessment issued by the ITO; for a 31 December year-end, the return attributable to year of assessment 2024/25 is typically assessed and payable in the calendar quarter following the filing of the statutory accounts; separately, the Cayman Exempted Company annual return must be filed within 60 days of each anniversary of incorporation and the Cayman ESR return by 31 December each year; a Gibraltar non-resident company with a permanent establishment is required to file the same return limited to Gibraltar-source trading income.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Gibraltar Local File obligation is triggered where aggregate related-party revenue or costs exceed GIP 500,000 (approximately USD 620,000 or EUR 550,000) per accounting period; a Master File is required where the multinational enterprise group has consolidated revenue above GIP 750 million (approximately EUR 750 million) and includes a Gibraltar constituent entity; Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) notification and filing obligations apply to ultimate parent entities above the EUR 750 million consolidated revenue threshold, with the Cayman exempted parent treated as the reporting entity where required; for the present PropTech SaaS structure with intercompany licence and payment-processing arrangements, the recommended standard is a medium-size enterprise three-tier file consisting of an intercompany contract, a TP policy paper, and a functional/credit rating analysis sufficient to support the arm's length royalty and ACH/Stripe fee allocation.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies the Taxes (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019, which mirror the OECD and EU Code of Conduct substance standard; a PropTech SaaS company exploiting proprietary IP is an intellectual property business and a distribution and service centre relevant activity; the entity must show: (i) boards of directors meeting and taking strategic decisions in Gibraltar at least twice per financial year; (ii) core income-generating activities performed in Gibraltar, including software development control, licensing negotiation, contract management, and direction of the global bank and payment accounts; (iii) an adequate number of full-time qualified employees in Gibraltar proportionate to the SaaS revenue; (iv) suitable physical premises and local IT infrastructure; (v) local outsourced accounting and legal providers engaged under wholly owned commercial contracts; simultaneously, the Cayman Exempted Company must satisfy the International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act by either demonstrating reduced substance in Cayman as a pure holding entity or, if tax-resident in Gibraltar, filing the Cayman ESR annual return with a declaration of tax residence outside Cayman and attaching the Gibraltar TRC; failure of either substance test is treated as a fatal compliance breach for the entire structure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, deliberate and concealed under-declaration attracts a penalty of up to 100% of the tax undercharged plus interest at the prescribed rate, and upon criminal conviction a fine of up to GIP 100,000 per count and imprisonment for up to two years; continuation of non-payment may attract a further 50% surcharge on the outstanding assessed tax and seizure of bank accounts through the Tax Collection and Recovery Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who sign or cause a false return are personally liable to criminal prosecution, confiscation of personal assets under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, director disqualification for up to ten years, and travel bans preventing departure from Gibraltar while an investigation is pending; courts may pierce the corporate veil and require directors to satisfy the company's unpaid tax liabilities where the company has been stripped of funds by intercompany dividends or loans.
Shell Company / Sham Substance (Cayman-Gibraltar Hybrid)

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations and the Cayman International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act, the authorities may impose information penalties escalating to GIP 250,000 or KYD 120,000 (approximately USD 144,000) with daily default penalties applied for continued non-compliance; the Cayman Registrar may strike the exempted company off the register, invalidate its banking facilities, and compel deregistration; the Gibraltar ITO may re-assess the full SaaS profit at 12.5% plus a 200% civil penalty under the anti-avoidance provisions and initiate exchange-of-information requests with HMRC and the Cayman Tax Information Authority.

Director personal liability: Officers who sign false economic substance declarations face both Cayman and Gibraltar personal penalties of up to KYD 120,000 and imprisonment for up to five years in the Cayman courts; civil asset freezing orders and travel bans are routinely granted to prevent flight; where the company is struck off before tax liabilities are settled, directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid Gibraltar tax and may be pursued personally through the Gibraltar Supreme Court.
AML / Financial Crime and Payment Processing

Statutory sanction: The Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the Financial Services (DNFBP) framework impose summary penalties of up to GIP 50,000 and imprisonment for up to two years for failure to register as a relevant business or to file a SAR; upon indictment there is an unlimited fine and imprisonment for up to 14 years for money laundering; the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit may issue enforcement notices, suspend payment service contracts, and require termination of the merchant relationship with all Gibraltar-licensed electronic money institutions; the UK National Crime Agency may open a parallel investigation through the Joint Money Laundering Intelligence Taskforce because the parent jurisdiction is the UK.

Director personal liability: Any director who consented to or connived in laundering proceeds faces personal prosecution, an unlimited fine, up to 14 years' imprisonment in Gibraltar, property confiscation or civil recovery orders, director disqualification, and inclusion on the public sanctions list; with a UK nexus, extradition between the UK and Gibraltar may be sought, and the Court may impose a travel ban and surrender of passports pending final adjudication.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and CbCR Failure

Statutory sanction: The ITO will make a transfer pricing adjustment re-determining profit at the arm's length rate, impose tax at 12.5% on the adjusted profit, and charge statutory interest from the date the tax would originally have been payable; documentation penalties reach up to GIP 100,000 for first offences and GIP 250,000 for deliberate irregularities in the files; CbCR notification failure triggers penalties of up to GIP 50,000 plus a daily default of GIP 5,000; the re-assessment is automatically exchanged with HMRC under the UK-Gibraltar DTA and with the Cayman Tax Information Authority under the OECD country-by-country framework, leading to high-probability joint audit.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign an incomplete or false Local File, Master File, or CbCR are exposed to personal penalty assessments of up to GIP 50,000 per false document and director disqualification; where the transfer pricing mispricing is shown to be deliberate and concealed for tax evasion purposes, criminal fines of up to GIP 100,000 and imprisonment for up to two years apply; if the Gibraltar OpCo cannot settle the adjusted tax liability, the directors are jointly and severally liable to the extent of dividends and management fees distributed during the assessed period.
