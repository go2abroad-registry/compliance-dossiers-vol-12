Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Income Tax Office (HM Government of Gibraltar); official e-services portal: https://www.income-tax.gov.gi. Supporting registries: Gibraltar Companies Registry (Gibraltar Financial Services Commission) and HM Customs Gibraltar. Underlying Cayman entity status: Cayman Islands General Registry and the Department for International Tax Cooperation (DITC), Cayman Islands.

        
E-government filing portal
Step 1 - Cayman leg: confirm the Cayman Exempted Company's registered number, Certificate of Incorporation, and Tax Exemption Certificate (Section 6, Companies Act undertaking) in the Cayman General Registry; those identifiers constitute the entity's base TIN-equivalent. Step 2 - Gibraltar presence: if the exempted company establishes a place of business, management presence, or registered office in Gibraltar, file the prescribed Overseas Company registration application with the Gibraltar Companies Registry and obtain a Gibraltar Overseas Company Number. Step 3 - Gibraltar tax registration: apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) by submitting the certified constitutional documents, Overseas Company registration certificate, directors' particulars, and the Cayman Tax Exemption Certificate. Step 4 - Customs and ancillary: register with HM Customs Gibraltar for an Importer/Exporter Reference if any physical engine movements into or out of Gibraltar are planned; no separate VAT ID exists because Gibraltar has no VAT regime. Step 5 - Beneficial ownership: file the Gibraltar Beneficial Ownership return with the Registry of Beneficial Ownership as required under the Beneficial Ownership (Companies) Regulations 2018.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Tax_Exempt_Zone

        
Available election forms
Cayman leg: election under Section 6 of the Companies Act to hold a Tax Exemption Certificate guaranteeing exemption from all Cayman income, corporate, capital gains, and withholding taxes for 20 years (renewable; no economic-substance condition beyond standard regulatory filings). Gibraltar leg: no check-the-box or look-through election exists; a foreign company becomes Gibraltar-resident and taxable at 12.5% corporation tax if centrally managed and controlled in Gibraltar, notwithstanding its Cayman exempted status. Available Gibraltar elections are limited to the Qualifying Company regime (banking, insurance, funds, and shipping - not applicable to aircraft-engine leasing), the 10% qualifying-retailer band on the first GBP 500,000 of retail profits, and the OECD Pillar Two top-up tax elections for in-scope multinational groups with revenue of at least EUR 750 million. Hybrid-mismatch and territorial elections are not available. If the company is managed from the Cayman Islands and has no Gibraltar permanent establishment, no Gibraltar corporate tax nexus arises.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
Gibraltar operates no VAT or GST; it is expressly excluded from the EU VAT territory. The only indirect taxes are Gibraltar customs import duties levied on goods imported under the UK Combined Nomenclature, together with a residual purchase tax confined to specified locally traded goods. Cross-border aircraft-engine dry-leasing supplies are B2B services and fall entirely outside any Gibraltarian indirect tax, irrespective of the lessee's jurisdiction.

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Not applicable - no VAT/GST turnover threshold exists in Gibraltar; there is no indirect-tax registration obligation for engine-leasing turnover. A customs registration (Importer/Exporter Reference) is instead required for any physical movement of goods into or out of Gibraltar, with no monetary threshold for that registration duty.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Not applicable as a VAT mechanism: Gibraltar has no VAT/GST, hence no 0% export VAT rate and no input VAT refund claim exists; cross-border engine dry-leasing rentals are outside the scope of Gibraltarian indirect tax. For physical goods movements, Gibraltar customs duty at 0% may apply to temporary exports and re-imports of engines installed on internationally registered aircraft, but that is customs-duty relief, not VAT zero-rating. Any non-aviation physical transfer of the engine into Gibraltar for storage or MRO is treated as an import subject to customs-duty procedures, which may be suspended or relieved under temporary-importation rules.

HM Customs Gibraltar may demand customs-duty recovery plus penalties of up to 100% of the duty evaded, forfeiture of goods, and criminal sanctions for false declarations under the Customs and Excise Duties Act. The Income Tax Office may raise retrospective assessments beyond the standard six-year period where fraud or wilful default is established, with penalty surcharges of up to 100% for negligent error and up to 200% for deliberate concealment or fraudulent documentation. Misuse of the Cayman Tax Exemption Certificate to conceal Gibraltar central management and control triggers clawback of the exemption, full 12.5% corporation tax plus interest, financial penalties, director disqualification, and criminal liability; the Cayman Registrar may also strike off the exempted company for false undertakings. Cross-border exchange under CRS, FATCA, and the UK-Gibraltar-Cayman tax information agreements can expose the structure to joint audits; sanctions for fraudulent export documentation may include fines of up to GBP 1,000,000 and imprisonment. Professional practice requires either documented substance in the Cayman Islands or, alternatively, voluntary Gibraltar registration and full 12.5% compliance.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The UK abolished exchange controls in 1979, and neither the UK, Gibraltar, nor the Cayman Islands operates exchange control legislation restricting capital or current account transactions. GIP is fully convertible into GBP at parity and is freely usable across the UK clearing system. Aircraft engine dry-leasing rental receipts are classified as current account services income under the IMF BPM6; profit remittances, dividends, and capital repayments to the Cayman shareholder are unrestricted and require no central bank approval. Any observed friction arises solely from bank-level AML/EWS evidence, not from legal convertibility limits. Functional-currency exposure is material because aviation leases are predominantly USD-denominated, producing natural GBP/USD translation and cash flow risk.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
There is no statutory requirement to surrender export or lease proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriation in the UK, Gibraltar, or the Cayman Islands. Lease receipts may be retained in USD or any freely convertible currency. Enterprises are only subject to statistical reporting obligations (e.g., Bank of England/ONS direct investment and BoP surveys, GFSC regulatory returns) and to normal transfer-pricing documentation where intercompany cross-border flows are involved.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of England (sovereign monetary authority for the United Kingdom); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the designated financial services regulator for Gibraltar; the Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at a fixed 1:1 peg to GBP. The Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) supervises the Cayman exempted company's regulatory status and any licensed activity within the Cayman Islands. No separate Gibraltar central bank sets independent monetary policy.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Compliant routing: shareholder's originating bank initiates SWIFT MT103 (or MT101 if batch-initiated) to the Gibraltar receiving bank, crediting the Cayman exempted company's account at Gibraltar International Bank or NatWest Gibraltar. The remittance field must identify the payer's legal name, country, and account number exactly as they appear on the share subscription or loan agreement. Recommended ISO 20022 purpose codes: 'EQTI' for equity investment; 'INTC' for intercompany funding; 'LOAN' for shareholder loan drawdowns. The receiving bank will typically tag the payment under BoP direct-investment statistical code 8846 (investment in business concerns). The full evidentiary file, comprising the board resolution, subscription agreement, source-of-funds statement, notarized identification of ultimate beneficial owners, and bank reference, must be pre-positioned with the Gibraltar bank before first credit; any material deviation between the MT103 text and the corporate records will trigger enhanced review.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for operational lease payments such as maintenance reserves, insurance premiums, management fees, and security-deposit returns; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (sight or usance) opened through the Gibraltar or UK clearing bank for engine acquisitions and title-transfer settlements; (iii) standby letters of credit for lease-performance guarantees; (iv) TARGET2/CHAPS for GBP/GIP-denominated settlements with UK counterparties; (v) escrow account clearing at Gibraltar International Bank for simultaneous engine delivery-versus-payment transactions. USD disbursements should be routed via international correspondents accepting Cayman corporate clients, commonly JP Morgan Chase, Citibank, and BNY Mellon. Dividend repatriation to the shareholder is executed by SWIFT MT103 using purpose code 'DIVI' with audited accounts appended for bank sign-off.Deliverable GBP/USD and GBP/EUR forward contracts with tenors of 1-12 months executed with the Gibraltar clearing bank or its UK affiliate.USD/GBP cross-currency interest-rate swaps hedging the financing of aviation asset acquisitions.European-style GBP/USD FX options for protection on lease-revenue spikes while retaining upside participation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible or necessary in the GIP/GBP pegged regime because GIP trades at parity with GBP and no independent NDF market exists.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium overall. FCA-regulated or GFSC-regulated electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, Currencycloud) will generally onboard a Gibraltar-incorporated company with genuine substance, but almost all decline a Cayman exempted company whose sole purpose is aviation asset leasing because of the offshore nexus, enhanced due diligence burden, and restrictions on high-value SWIFT MT103 correspondent chains. EMIs cannot provide credit, overdrafts, or custodial safeguarding equivalent to a banking deposit; their product policies frequently exclude aircraft leasing, and they are unsuitable as the primary clearing rail for engine acquisitions or lease settlement. Where accepted, EMIs should be limited to low-value operating disbursements such as security deposits, consultant fees, and expense reimbursements.

Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
12.5% for all trading, investment and passive income of companies tax resident in Gibraltar; a qualified domestic minimum top-up tax raises the effective minimum to 15% for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million, aligned with OECD Pillar Two effective 1 January 2024.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
0% — Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar-resident company to its shareholders, whether the shareholder is resident in Gibraltar or offshore. The Cayman Exempted Company is likewise exempt from Cayman dividend tax; therefore distributions of the engine leasing profits to the ultimate beneficial owners flow free of any Gibraltar or Cayman statutory withholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar's transfer pricing regime, enacted under the Income Tax Act 2010 as amended and aligned with OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and BEPS Action 13, requires mandatory Country-by-Country Reporting, Master File and Local File for multinational enterprise groups whose consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000 (approximately GBP 645,000,000). Below that threshold, a Gibraltar-resident company or a Gibraltar PE of a Cayman Exempted Company must nonetheless prepare and retain contemporaneous arm's length documentation where its connected-party transactions in a chargeable period exceed GBP 1,000,000 in aggregate, or earlier where the taxpayer reasonably expects that a transfer pricing adjustment could exceed GBP 250,000. Documentation must be delivered to the Income Tax Office within 45 days of a formal demand.

      
Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations, in force from 30 June 2019 under the Income Tax Act 2010, apply to every Gibraltar tax-resident company and to every Gibraltar PE of a foreign company that carries on a relevant activity. The relevant activities expressly include financing and leasing; aircraft engine dry leasing therefore falls within scope. The entity must demonstrate and report annually: (i) direction and management exercised through board meetings held in Gibraltar at adequate frequency, with the board's minutes retained in Gibraltar; (ii) physical presence consisting of a rented or owned office, facilities and equipment in Gibraltar; (iii) qualified full-time employees and senior management resident in Gibraltar, proportionate to the scale of the engine leasing portfolio; (iv) core income-generating activities performed in Gibraltar, including lease negotiation, engine technical oversight, re-marketing, risk assessment, invoicing and treasury management; and (v) an annual Economic Substance Form (ESF) filed with the Income Tax Office within 12 months of the financial year end. A Cayman Exempted Company is not exempt: if tax resident in Gibraltar or operating through a Gibraltar PE, it must satisfy the ESR test in full; the company's separate Cayman economic substance filings do not discharge the Gibraltar obligation.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Accounting
        
Statutory sanction: Upon conviction, a fine of up to GBP 1,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 7 years per count; civil tax-geared penalties of 100% for deliberate understatement and 200% for deliberate and concealed understatement, plus statutory interest; forfeiture of the turbofan engines and leasing receivables as proceeds of crime under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; public naming in the Tax Defaulters Gazette.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors may be held personally liable for civil restitution of the whole unpaid tax where the company is insolvent; automatic director disqualification for up to 15 years; travel bans, freezing orders and asset restraint orders; imprisonment of up to 5 years for directors who consented to or connived in the evasion.

      

      
      

        Offshore Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure
        
Statutory sanction: Financial penalty of up to GBP 10,000 per chargeable offence plus up to GBP 1,000 per day for each continuing day of non-compliance without upper limit; deregistration and striking-off of the company from the Gibraltar register; withdrawal of the tax residence certificate; referral to the EU Code of Conduct Group and automatic exchange of information with the Cayman Islands under the Tax Information Exchange Agreement, exposing the group to Cayman ESR penalties and to withholding tax exposure in lessee countries that deny the DTA benefits.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who sign false economic substance declarations face individual fines and disqualification orders; directors of the Gibraltar corporate services provider also face professional sanction; shareholders may be compelled to unwind lease transactions; non-cooperation with Commissioner's enforcement enquiries can lead to travel bans and custody for contempt.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Tax Base Erosion
        
Statutory sanction: Primary arm's length adjustment increasing Gibraltar taxable profits, secondary adjustment recharacterising the excess interest or price as a deemed distribution; interest on the underpaid tax from the original due date; transfer pricing penalty of 50% of the adjustment where contemporaneous documentation was absent, increasing to 100%-200% where documents were false or destruction of evidence occurred; CbCR non-compliance fines of GBP 5,000 per unfiled report and loss of safe-harbour reliefs.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's-length lease pricing bear personal liability for the adjusted tax if the company cannot pay and they knew of the undervaluation; disqualification for up to 10 years; criminal liability for fraudulent transfer pricing evasion where deliberate falsification is proven, carrying imprisonment and travel bans.

      

      
      

        Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Violation
        
Statutory sanction: GFSC administrative penalty of up to GBP 2,000,000 or 10% of the worldwide turnover of the licensed entity; unlimited criminal fines and confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; revocation of any authorisations affecting the escrow payment rails; prohibition from holding AML-regulated funds; referral to the Gibraltar police and to overseas authorities for mirror penalties in the lessees' jurisdictions.

        
Director personal liability: Directors must demonstrate 'appropriate consent' was obtained from the Commissioner before transactions proceeded; failure creates individual criminal exposure with fines, travel bans and imprisonment of up to 2 years on summary conviction or up to 14 years on indictment; civil liability to third parties for any losses caused by laundering of lease proceeds; statutory disqualification from acting as a director of any regulated Gibraltar company.
