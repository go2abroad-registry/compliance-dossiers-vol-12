Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar - Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), e-filing portal via www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation; import/export customs matters handled by HM Customs Gibraltar through the Gibraltar Customs Management System (GCMS).

E-government filing portal

A foreign company (e.g., Cayman Exempted Co) carrying on a trade in Gibraltar must register for corporation tax within 30 days of branch opening by filing the Application for Registration and the Company Details Form together with certified certificate of incorporation, memorandum, and appointment of a resident general agent/representative. The Commissioner issues a Gibraltarian TIN. Separate EORI registration with HM Customs Gibraltar is required for customs transactions; social insurance/payroll registration applies if staff are employed.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign company (e.g., Cayman Exempted Co) carrying on a trade in Gibraltar must register for corporation tax within 30 days of branch opening by filing the Application for Registration and the Company Details Form together with certified certificate of incorporation, memorandum, and appointment of a resident general agent/representative. The Commissioner issues a Gibraltarian TIN. Separate EORI registration with HM Customs Gibraltar is required for customs transactions; social insurance/payroll registration applies if staff are employed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections. The foreign entity may make an election to be assessed on a territorial basis by submitting a notice requiring the Commissioner to confirm that non-Gibraltar-source income is disregarded; alternatively, prior-clearance applications may be made to establish whether the restaurant's income constitutes Gibraltar-source profits. Taxpayer may also apply for a unilateral credit or treaty relief under applicable double taxation arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST; Gibraltar indirect taxation via customs import duty and excise duties

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A - no VAT turnover threshold; any person importing or exporting goods must register as an importer/exporter with HM Customs, and a business must register for income tax from the first day of trading, irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - Gibraltar does not levy VAT or GST, and thus there is no mechanism for zero-rating exports or claiming an input VAT refund. Exports of condiments from Gibraltar to other territories are outside the scope of Gibraltar's indirect tax system; customs duty may be relieved or exempted on goods temporarily exported or re-exported.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / e-DEC submitted through GCMS)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or overseas bank receipt evidencing the export proceeds

No electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate is in force. Customs entries are submitted electronically through the GCMS; direct taxes are filed online via the Income Tax Office e-Portal. Invoices may be issued in standard commercial format; no digital stamping or real-time API clearance is required.

There are no VAT clawback or zero-rating fraud penalties given the absence of VAT. However, false declarations to HM Customs for duty relief may lead to forfeiture of goods, monetary penalties not exceeding £5,000 per declaration, or criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion of duty (up to 7 years' imprisonment). For income tax purposes, manipulation of transfer pricing or deliberate under-declaration of Gibraltar-source branch profits may be corrected by reassessment with late payment interest and a penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax under the General Anti-Abuse Rule.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure and de facto convertibility on both current account and capital account. GIP is freely convertible into GBP at par with no commission, approval, or declaration. Profit remittances from the hotpot restaurant to the Cayman Exempted Company are unrestricted and subject only to standard bank-level source-of-funds verification; Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax, exchange-control quota, or prior-notification duty on dividend or branch-profit repatriation. Capital-account transactions such as Cayman share-subscription capital injections and intercompany loans are equally free. Practical hurdles are limited to KYC documentation: audited financial statements, Gibraltar overseas-company registration, restaurant operating licence, and UBO source-of-wealth evidence for wires above GBP 100,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline applies in Gibraltar. The Cayman entity may lawfully retain hotpot dining revenue and condiment-supply export proceeds in GIP, GBP, EUR, USD, or CNH at commercial bank discretion. No GFSC notification is required for maintaining multicurrency accounts. The Business (Licensing) Regulations and the Income Tax Act impose no forex-repatriation quota on the restaurant trade; any internal treasury policy requiring weekly conversion from card-settlement currency into GIP is purely a commercial governance choice, not a regulatory mandate.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) acting as the integrated banking, payments, and AML regulator; no independent Central Bank of Gibraltar exists. Currency issuance is performed by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934 for the Gibraltar Pound (GIP), pegged at par with GBP. Monetary policy and emergency liquidity functions effectively align with the Bank of England (BoE) because GIP and GBP circulate interchangeably at par. Exchange controls have been abolished; the GFSC holds no approval power over FX transactions, only AML/CFT supervisory authority over banks and payment institutions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for auxiliary treasury functions such as FX conversion and cross-border supplier disbursements; Low-to-Negative as a primary or settlement account for the physical hotpot restaurant. Post-Brexit, UK FCA-authorised e-money institutions (Wise, Revolut Business, Modulr) may continue serving Gibraltar entities under the transitional financial-services regime only as unlicensed passported providers; EEA EMIs (Lithuanian, Cypriot, Polish) can no longer passport into Gibraltar and generally decline restaurant merchants due to GIP-gap and clearing friction. Legal constraint: under the Gibraltar Financial Services Act 2019, money transmission and merchant acquiring require a GFSC licence or valid passport; offshore EMIs cannot supply in-territory POS/EFTPOS merchant IDs. Optimal architecture: keep GIB or Banco Sabadell as the designated settlement bank for card receipts and local payroll; use a UK EMI solely for low-value FX conversions and payments to non-EU condiment vendors.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Accounting Returns

Statutory sanction: Assessed tax plus a civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded; criminal conviction for tax fraud carries a fine of up to GIP 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 5 years; the company faces strike-off from the Gibraltar register and automatic freezing of operational bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax; the Supreme Court may issue travel bans, disqualification orders (up to 15 years), personal asset seizure, and criminal imprisonment where fraud is proven. Directors cannot rely on Cayman exemption or Gibraltar 0% distribution rules once tax evasion is established.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Denial of non-resident PE treatment; the Gibraltar Income Tax Officer may deem 100% of the Cayman company's global hotpot revenue as Gibraltar-source; penalty of up to GIP 100,000 for ESR non-compliance; compulsory strike-off and disqualification of the overseas company registration.

Director personal liability: Directors receive formal notices requiring full disclosure of the ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) or face personal fines up to GIP 500,000. Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Royalty Rigging

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act to set an arm's-length TNMM margin (typically 3–8% for restaurant distribution); the royalty deduction is disallowed entirely in the absence of valid business purpose; a penalty of 50% of the additional assessed tax and interest from the original due date is imposed.
