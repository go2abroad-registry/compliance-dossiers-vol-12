Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar - Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), policy portal https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/ and e-filing portal https://www.ito.gov.gi/; HM Customs (Gibraltar) is the indirect tax authority for import and export declarations.

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company commencing any economic activity in Gibraltar must notify the Commissioner of Income Tax within 30 days and obtain a Gibraltar Reference Number (GRN), the sole statutory tax identification used for all tax, customs and payroll filings; no VAT ID is issued in Gibraltar because the territory is outside the EU/UK VAT area. The application dossier must contain (i) notarized Cayman certificate of incorporation and constitutional documents, (ii) Cayman Certificate of Good Standing, (iii) the Cayman tax exemption certificate (20-year exemption) issued by the Tax Information Authority, (iv) acceptance of a Gibraltar registered office or appointed agent, (v) full particulars of directors and ultimate beneficial owners, and (vi) an economic substance declaration confirming that no activities are managed and controlled from Gibraltar or, if they are, quantifying the attendant permanent establishment exposure. After registration, the Income Tax Office issues the GRN electronically; separate payroll/employer registration is triggered only if staff are engaged in Gibraltar. Payment settlement via Stripe/PayPal does not itself create registration, but foreign payment service provider data is exchangeable under OECD AEOI/DAC7 reporting.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation on a territorial basis for Gibraltar-source income only; the Cayman Exempted Company is a Tax_Exempt_Zone entity in its home state and remains outside Gibraltar tax unless it is managed and controlled in Gibraltar or maintains a permanent establishment there.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box election. Available statutory mechanisms include: (i) group relief election to surrender trading losses between qualifying Gibraltar-resident group companies (Income Tax Act 2010), (ii) election for qualifying company status, where applicable, to benefit from simplified annual compliance and reduced direct-tax exposure under Gibraltar's qualifying company regime, (iii) request for an advance ruling/clearance from the Commissioner confirming non-residence and absence of a Gibraltar permanent establishment, and (iv) election for hybrid mismatch neutrality under Gibraltar's OECD BEPS Action 2 and EU ATAD aligned rules, including deemed treaty anti-abuse declarations. A redomiciliation election is also available under the Companies (Redomiciliation) Regulations 2020 if the Cayman company wishes to convert into a Gibraltar company.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Not_Applicable_No_VAT_GST_or_Sales_and_Use_Tax_in_Gibraltar; the only indirect taxes are Import Duty (Gibraltar Integrated Tariff) and excise duties; destination-state VAT applies to jewellery and watch distance sales in the UK/EU.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None in Gibraltar - no economic turnover threshold triggers a VAT/GST registration because the system does not exist; import duty is triggered by physical importation at customs value. For UK distance selling of goods not exceeding GBP 135 per consignment, the overseas seller is accountable for UK VAT from the first sale without a turnover threshold; for EU consumers the Import One-Stop Shop applies above EUR 150.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Domestically not applicable: Gibraltar levies no VAT or output tax on exported jewellery or watches, so no zero-rating claim or refund procedure arises. Where goods are sold to UK/EU consumers and exported into those jurisdictions, eligibility for any zero-rating (e.g., UK exports where shipped from the UK) depends on retaining proof of physical export within the statutory deadlines; alternatively, marketplace/OMP liability rules apply. Offshore services rendered from Gibraltar likewise carry no Gibraltar output tax.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD-C88A) filed via the Gibraltar Integrated Tariff System
  • Commercial invoice with HS 7113 (jewellery) or HS 9102 (watches), quantity and origin of goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement from Gibraltar
  • Certificate of Origin and, where applicable, import documentation for onward EU/UK clearance
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT payout records from Stripe or PayPal settlement
  • Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, Good Standing and Tax Exemption Certificate

Gibraltar imposes no real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping mandate; ordinary commercial invoicing suffices. HM Customs Gibraltar requires SAD-C88A customs declarations processed through the Gibraltar Integrated Tariff System for import/export clearance, with a six-year record-keeping obligation; payment service providers (Stripe/PayPal) are not linked to any Gibraltar tax clearance API.

Misdescription of goods or customs value in Gibraltar may lead to seizure/forfeiture, penalty notices up to GBP 5,000 per declaration and criminal fiscal proceedings; in the UK, a fraudulent export zero-rating claim (where goods never physically leave or are diverted) triggers reassessment of 20% VAT plus interest and a tax penalty of up to 100% of the VAT, with retrospective clawback of any input VAT recovered, and, for dishonest conduct, criminal liability under VATA 1994 s.72; a Cayman exempt certification can additionally be revoked in material-abuse cases involving misrepresentation to the Tax Information Authority.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility applies in Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands. The Gibraltar pound is fixed to GBP at parity, and transactions in USD, EUR, CHF and other major currencies require no governmental approval and are not subject to quantitative limits, remittance authorizations, or profit-conversion permits. Dividend and capital distributions from Gibraltar to the Cayman Exempted Company are unrestricted, subject only to GFSC-mandated AML/CFT client due diligence and standard corporate formality documentation. UK HM Treasury financial sanctions screening remains a practical compliance hurdle for counterparties connected to sanctioned jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or retention timeline exists under Gibraltar law, Cayman Islands law, or UK overseas-territory legislation. Online retail sales proceeds in USD and EUR received via Stripe and PayPal may be retained in the original settlement currency, converted freely to GBP or GIP, or remitted cross-border as treasury policy dictates, without central-bank reporting thresholds or surrender deadlines.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no sovereign central bank; the relevant monetary and supervisory authorities are: (i) Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), which licenses and supervises deposit-taking institutions and enforces AML/CFT compliance; (ii) the Government of Gibraltar Ministry of Finance, which issues Gibraltar pound banknotes and coinage at a 1:1 fixed peg to GBP Sterling; (iii) the Bank of England, which provides the underlying Sterling settlement infrastructure for GBP clearing; and (iv) the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA), home-state regulator of the Cayman Exempted Company, which exercises no foreign-exchange control powers.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: the Cayman holding account initiates a SWIFT MT103 equity remittance to the Gibraltar bank account with remittance field 70 coded as 'Share Capital Injection / Direct Investment' and classified under the IMF BPM6 financial-account direct-investment equity category. Operational e-commerce inflows arrive as Stripe and PayPal scheduled payouts through local UK or Gibraltar clearing into the same GIP/GBP account. The verification chain requires a signed board resolution, updated share register, UBO source-of-wealth form, and the receiving bank's MT103 credit confirmation; all credits are booked with GL attribution tags to maintain a complete audit trail.Primary clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for supplier prepayments, freight, and logistics invoices; (2) irrevocable, confirmed commercial letters of credit issued through a UK or EU clearing bank for fine-timepiece and high-value inventory procurement; (3) UK Faster Payments or CHAPS transfers executed via agency arrangements through the Gibraltar Interbank Payment System (GIPS) for vendor disbursements below 1 million GBP; and (4) EUR payments settled via SWIFT correspondent accounts with Frankfurt or Paris clearing banks, as Gibraltar holds no TARGET2 membership.Outright deliverable FX forwards in GIP/GBP, USD, and EUR executed under ISDA or EMTA master agreements with GFSC-licensed or FCA-authorized counterparties.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on USD/EUR crosses referencing non-convertible end-customer settlement currencies, executed under EMTA documentation and cash-settled in GBP or USD.Cross-currency swaps (XCCY) aligning USD revenue streams against GBP/EUR operating-cost liabilities, subject to UK EMIR trade-reporting obligations.Vanilla FX call and put options used to hedge known seasonal campaign capex in EUR and USD procurement cycles, subject to bank credit-appetite approval.

Post-Brexit, EEA electronic-money-institution (EMI) passporting into Gibraltar has ceased; an FCA or EEA-authorized EMI requires GFSC recognition or a distinct Gibraltar electronic-money licence to serve the Gibraltar trade platform. Leading digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex) commonly decline Cayman Exempted Companies because of offshore-jurisdiction classification and the high-value goods typology of fashion jewelry and fine timepieces. Where an EMI accepts the entity, Enhanced Due Diligence, reduced transaction thresholds, and elevated compliance fees are imposed. The defensible sequencing is: (i) establish a primary licensed bank account in Gibraltar; (ii) route Stripe and PayPal aggregated payouts into that account; and (iii) deploy an FCA-authorized EMI solely for cross-currency working-capital drawdowns under a documented treasury policy.
