E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Cayman Exempted Company at the Registrar of Companies, Cayman Islands, under the Companies Act (2023 Revision) and engage a licensed registered office service provider. Step 2: Register with the DITC portal to obtain a Cayman Tax Reference Number (TRN); file the FATCA/CRS registration and, where relevant, the Economic Substance annual return under the International Tax Cooperation (Economic Substance) Law if the company carries on a relevant activity such as legal services, headquarters, or service business. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: the Cayman Exempted Company is exempt from Cayman Islands income tax, capital gains tax, and withholding tax under the Tax Concessions Law; it is not subject to entity-level taxation in the Cayman Islands. For Gibraltar territorial-source purposes, it may become subject to Gibraltar corporate income tax (presently 12.5%) only if managed and controlled in Gibraltar or if it carries on a trade through a permanent establishment in Gibraltar.

Available election forms

None applicable in Cayman or Gibraltar: no local 'check-the-box' election is available for exempted companies. Under Gibraltar's Income Tax (OECD Hybrid and Branch Mismatch Rules) Act, the entity may be treated as a reverse hybrid for tax-mismatch analysis if it has non-resident owners and is disregarded by the investor jurisdiction; but there is no elective domestic classification regime. The entity's default classification is a taxable corporate person in Gibraltar only upon establishment of territorial nexus.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales-and-Use-Tax regime in either the Cayman Islands or Gibraltar; indirect tax exposure is limited to customs and excise duties, none of which are triggered by legal-services supply.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Neither Cayman Islands nor Gibraltar imposes a VAT/GST/SUT turnover threshold. No local-currency indirect-tax registration threshold is prescribed. For UK VAT, an overseas business with no UK establishment has no mandatory registration unless it generates more than GBP 90,000 of UK taxable supplies, which is not the expected profile of this offshore litigation-services entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable for a Cayman/Gibraltar nexus because there is no VAT/GST/SUT rate to zero-rate. If the company were separately registered for UK VAT as a UK-established business, offshore legal and arbitration services supplied to a non-UK business customer would be outside the scope of UK VAT under the place-of-supply rules; tangible goods exported from the UK could qualify for zero-rating under section 30, Value Added Tax Act 1994, but only if the exporter retains removal and export evidence and the recipient is a non-UK person. The zero-rating claim therefore requires documented proof of both the non-UK status of the recipient and the actual export/offshore supply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD C88 or electronic customs record) evidencing physical export from the UK where tangible goods are involved
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill, or alternative objective export movement evidence
  • Contract or engagement letter identifying the non-UK recipient of legal services, the foreign place of supply, and the recipient's valid VAT identification number where available
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and bank/FX statement demonstrating payment from outside the UK, Gibraltar, and Cayman Islands

None. The Cayman Islands and Gibraltar operate no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory e-invoicing API, and no digital stamping regime. Ordinary professional invoices in PDF format are acceptable for accounting, audit, and customs purposes; Gibraltar Customs/Excise uses electronic declarations only for physical import/export controls.

In the Cayman/Gibraltar no-VAT regime there is no export-zero-rating refund to claw back. Where a UK VAT zero-rating claim is made by or for a UK-registered establishment of this entity and is later disallowed, HMRC may assess a civil penalty under sections 67/73, Value Added Tax Act 1994 of up to 100% of the lost VAT, issue a customs debt assessment under section 159(3), Customs and Excise Management Act 1979, and pursue a dishonest-evasion criminal penalty. Retrospective assessment can extend six years for careless inaccuracies and twenty years for deliberate concealment. In Gibraltar, false customs or indirect-tax declarations attract forfeiture of goods and penalties up to GBP 10,000 plus a three-year retrospective assessment under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates a fully liberalised exchange-control system with no restrictions on either current account or capital account convertibility. There are no exchange-control permissions required for capital contributions, fee remittances, settlement proceeds or profit repatriation in GBP, EUR, USD or any other freely convertible currency. A Cayman Exempted Company receives profits without Cayman income tax and Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, management fees or litigation settlements paid to non-residents. The only practical hurdles are documentary: source-of-wealth verification, FATCA/CRS reporting and economic-substance demonstration for the Gibraltar-linked activity, but none is a currency-convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar maintains no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio and no central-bank deadline for repatriating export receipts. Trade and services proceeds, including legal-fee income and cross-border settlement recoveries in the ISIC 6910 commercial litigation model, may be retained in the original invoiced currency or converted freely. The applicable compliance duties are limited to accurate GIP-equivalent bookkeeping, AML transaction reporting under the GFSC Suspicious Activity Reporting regime, and FATCA/CRS classification. Therefore the effective surrender ratio is zero per cent and the statutory timeline is indefinite.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no independent central bank. Monetary authority is anchored to the Bank of England through a fixed 1:1 peg of the Gibraltar pound (GIP) to pound sterling (GBP), with the Government of Gibraltar issuing GIP notes and coin. Banking conduct, foreign-exchange services and payment institutions are regulated by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act and the Financial Services (Markets in Financial Instruments) Act. In relation to UK-led financial sanctions and exchange accountability, HM Treasury and OFSI exercise relevant extraterritorial prohibitions only where sanctions jurisdiction applies.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary operational accounts, but Low for client trust or IOLTA balances. A GFSC-licensed electronic money institution may accept a Cayman Exempted Company if it passes enhanced digital KYC and meets acceptable business-activity criteria. However, a non-bank EMI cannot legally hold statutory client trust money under Gibraltar client-money rules; IOLTA funds must be held with an authorised Gibraltar credit institution in a separately designated trust account. Many offshore digital EMIs also decline legal-services and asset-tracing activity as an unacceptable business model. The EMI channel should therefore be limited to non-client disbursement accounts, foreign-exchange execution and low-value operational payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from the Cayman Exempted Company to the Gibraltar treasury account must be wired by SWIFT MT103 for customer-level transfers or MT202 COV for interbank/treasury movements. The remittance field must carry the ISO 20022 purpose code CTRA (treasury operation) or TRST (trust services), the LEI 549300KY_EX, and the identifier 'Capital Contribution / Trust Deposit'. Verification on receipt is made against the GFSC AML database, OFSI and UN sanctions lists, the Cayman Corporate Registry, and the bank's internal economic-substance matrix. Supporting documents for each injection include the board resolution authorising the transfer, a certificate of incumbency, a source-of-funds declaration signed by the UBO, and, if the funds originate from a Cayman bank account, a bank statement evidencing the same stream of funds.Settlement and litigation disbursements are executed through four clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 for international counsel, expert witnesses, mediators and foreign court fees; CHAPS for urgent GBP payments within the UK and Gibraltar; SEPA INST for EUR disbursements to counterparties within the European Economic Area; and USD via Fedwire or CHIPS through a US correspondent bank. Where an external arbitration or litigation lodgement requires a guarantee, bond or bank undertaking, an MT700 commercial letter of credit is issued by the Gibraltar bank on behalf of the Cayman Exempted Company. All outbound disbursements must be preceded by a written payment instruction containing the matter reference, payee name, amount, supporting invoice or court order and a client-ledger reconciliation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A contemporaneous Local File is required where the aggregate annual value of controlled cross-border transactions equals or exceeds GIP 250,000. A Master File is required where the multinational group consolidated revenue exceeds GIP 150 million. For a typical litigation chambers billing GIP 2 million in fees with recharge to Cayman, a full TP Study may be required.

Economic substance scope: Cayman Exempted Company engaging in legal practice (ISIC 6910) is within the scope of both Cayman's Economic Substance Regulations and Gibraltar's Economic Substance (Legal Services) Rules. To avoid a shell-company finding, the operation must demonstrate physical office in Gibraltar, at least one part-time executive managing litigation, employed legal and paralegal staff performing core income-generating activities, and local expenditure proportional to gross revenue. A Cayman tax residence certificate must be filed with Cayman's Competent Authority to shift ESR compliance to Gibraltar; failure triggers KYD 10,000–120,000 penalties and potential strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act, a fine of up to GIP 5,000 plus an additional amount equal to 200% of the tax evaded; imprisonment not exceeding five years on conviction on indictment.

Director personal liability: Courts will lift the corporate veil where a director knowingly signed a false return; the director is personally liable for the company's unpaid tax, restitution and costs, and may be subject to a travel ban and disqualification from acting as a director for 15 years.
Transfer Pricing and Artificial Recharging

Statutory sanction: Tax authority adjusts gross income to arm's length, disallows the deduction, levies penalties up to 100% of the additional tax, and charges interest at 12% per annum; the company can be struck off for non-filing.

Director personal liability: Directors who personally directed the mispricing are jointly and severally liable for the additional tax, interest and penalties; the Gibraltar Civil Court may issue a compensation order requiring the director to repay diverted profits.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Gibraltar ESR, an automatic penalty of GIP 10,000 per breach plus GIP 1,000 for each month of continuing default, and deregistration by the Registrar of Companies. Under Cayman ESR, a penalty of KYD 10,000 per breach; KYD 120,000 for repeated default.

Director personal liability: Directors may be made personally liable to contribute to restitution of the company's unpaid tax and penalties under insolvency law; they face travel restrictions preventing departure from Gibraltar until the company's ES return and financial penalties are settled.
AML / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act (Gibraltar) and AML/CFT regulations, penalties may reach GIP 10 million or 20 years' imprisonment for money laundering; the trust account funds are also liable to civil forfeiture.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for money laundering: directors incur personal liability to forfeit their own assets traced to the laundering scheme, are banned from holding fiduciary office, and may be remanded in custody pending trial.
