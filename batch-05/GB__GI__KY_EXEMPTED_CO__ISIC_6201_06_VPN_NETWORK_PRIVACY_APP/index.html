Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar - Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax); corporate tax and self-assessment e-filing portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/tax-and-fees/income-tax-office. Indirect tax and customs matters: Gibraltar Customs & Excise Department (customs.gov.gi). Gibraltar is outside the UK VAT system and outside the EU VAT area.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: A foreign Cayman Exempted Company intending to carry on business in Gibraltar must first file Form OV-1 with the Gibraltar Companies Registry to register as an 'Overseas Company' (fee approx. GBP 45), or, if commercially preferred, elect to 'Continue' into Gibraltar under the Companies (Transfer of Domicile) Regulations, providing a certificate of continuance, memorandum/articles, and a Gibraltar registered office. Step 2: Within 15 days of commencing business, submit the Corporate Income Tax Registration Application (Form IT-REG-CO) to the Income Tax Office together with: (i) certificate of incorporation; (ii) certificate of good standing from the Cayman Islands Registrar; (iii) Gibraltar registered office confirmation; (iv) board resolution on the location of central management and control; and (v) audited or management accounts if an existing business. Step 3: The Commissioner issues the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) in the format XXXXX-XX, normally within 10 business days. Step 4: For physical imports of network equipment or marketed hardware, obtain an Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number via Gibraltar Customs to file electronic customs declarations. Step 5: If hiring local staff, register separately with the Gibraltar Social Security Office. No VAT identification number exists because Gibraltar has no VAT regime. Digital services (VPN SaaS) do not require a specific e-commerce tax licence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

1) Qualifying Company Election under the Companies (Qualifying Company) Regulations 2011: an elective status permitting a company to be taxed only on Gibraltar-source income and profits, providing confirmation that non-Gibraltar export income is outside the tax base. 2) Transfer of Domicile / Continuation Election under the Companies (Transfer of Domicile) Regulations: converts a Cayman Exempted Company into a continuing Gibraltar company while preserving legal personality. 3) Non-Resident Company Election: if central management and control is exercised wholly outside Gibraltar, the company may disclose that status on its tax return and is taxable only on Gibraltar-source income. 4) Hybrid Mismatch Self-Notification: Gibraltar has adopted OECD BEPS-aligned rules; taxpayers with hybrid or branch-payment mismatches must notify the Commissioner on the annual corporate return; no unilateral 'check-the-box' election exists in Gibraltar law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST or Sales and Use Tax. Gibraltar imposes Import Duty (customs tariff) and Excise Duty on the importation of goods only. Digital services and intangible supplies, including VPN network privacy software, are outside the charge to any Gibraltar indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Gibraltar has no VAT/GST and therefore no economic turnover threshold triggering indirect tax registration. A customs registration (EORI) is required on the first import transaction without any monetary threshold. Import Duty rates apply ad valorem from 0% to 10% depending on tariff classification, with excise duty on alcohol, tobacco, and fuel.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT, so export transactions are ipso facto outside the scope of indirect tax; no zero-rating claim or VAT refund mechanism exists. For direct corporate tax, export-derived profit from VPN/SaaS services will be outside the Gibraltar territorial tax base where: (i) the company is not managed and controlled in Gibraltar; (ii) the digital infrastructure (servers, encryption key management and staff) is located outside Gibraltar; and (iii) the customers and revenue-generating activities are non-Gibraltarian. A Qualifying Company may also rely on the statutory territorial limitation without needing a formal zero-rating certificate. Physical exports of goods are free of export duty.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD/CDS message) for any physical network hardware exported
  • Commercial invoice and VPN software licence or SaaS supply agreement
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill for physical cargo; for digital services, a service completion certificate or server-location attestation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 evidencing foreign-currency collection of subscription fees
  • Board minutes and management-and-control evidence showing that strategic decisions are made outside Gibraltar
  • IP address geolocation logs and customer residency records demonstrating the export nature of the revenue

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing mandate applies to Gibraltar domestic transactions or software exports. For goods movement, mandatory electronic import/export declarations are filed via the UK-Gibraltar customs interchange (CHIEF/CDS compatible platforms); for goods entering or transiting the EU, the Import Control System 2 (ICS2) pre-arrival/pre-departure security declarations are mandatory. No cryptographic tax seal or continuous VAT transaction control is implemented.

As no VAT/GST applies, there is no VAT zero-rating fraud or clawback regime. However: (i) Customs misdeclaration of exported/imported goods triggers seizure, fines up to GBP 500 or three times the value of the goods, and retrospective reassessment of import duty with no time limit in cases of fraud or wilful neglect. (ii) Income Tax: if a company falsely characterises Gibraltar-managed digital service income as foreign-source, the Commissioner may raise assessments up to six years after the year of assessment (unlimited for fraudulent or negligent conduct), charge interest at 8% per annum, and impose penalties up to 100% of the tax underpaid, with criminal sanctions for knowingly making false declarations. (iii) Transfer-pricing, CFC, and BEPS-aligned anti-avoidance rules may re-characterise VPN subscription income and deny territorial exemption. (iv) OECD Pillar Two global minimum top-up tax applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more for fiscal years beginning on or after 1 January 2025.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility applies in both Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands. The GIP is pegged at par (1:1) to GBP with unrestricted convertibility into sterling, and transactions in USD, EUR, and other major currencies are freely permitted without prior central-bank approval. The Cayman Exempted Company (KY_EXEMPTED_CO) is subject to zero corporate income tax, zero withholding tax on dividends, and zero exchange controls; profit repatriation by dividend, share redemption, or liquidation distribution is unrestricted subject only to the solvency test under the Cayman Islands Companies Act (as revised). The only practical hurdles are documentary: source-of-funds verification, economic-substance evidence, and standard anti-money-laundering (AML) scrutiny by the GFSC-licensed receiving bank in Gibraltar.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable — 0% mandatory surrender, no statutory conversion ratio, and no fixed repatriation timeline for export proceeds. Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands both operate a fully liberalised regime with no requirement to convert foreign-currency export receipts into local currency. Export earnings may be held indefinitely in USD, EUR, or GBP; the only reporting obligation is the bank's routine balance-of-payments statistical return to the Government of Gibraltar / Bank of England settlement framework.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the prudential and AML/CFT supervisor, acting with the Ministry of Finance, Government of Gibraltar, which issues the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) under a 100% sterling currency-board backing arrangement; the Bank of England (United Kingdom) provides the monetary anchor and settlement infrastructure. There is no independent Gibraltar central bank and no dedicated foreign-exchange control administration beyond the GFSC's licensing and monitoring powers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol is a SWIFT MT103 from the parent/UBO's account to the Cayman Exempted Company's GIP/GBP account held at the Gibraltar tier-1 bank, using SWIFT purpose code 'CAPA' (capital account transfer) or 'INVS' (investment) with a memo line referencing 'Subscription for shares / APIC'. No exchange-control approval is required in Gibraltar or the Cayman Islands; the bank will require, before credit, a board payment resolution, updated share register, share certificates, source-of-funds documentation for the remitting account, and a confirmation that the Cayman company meets the economic-substance benchmark. Sterling legs settle over UK Faster Payments via the Bank of England; USD legs settle via Fedwire/SWIFT through the correspondent network. The account-opening bank must record the transaction in its balance-of-payments return to the Government of Gibraltar.Primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for cross-border infrastructure and supplier payments (AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, advertising networks); SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant for EU-based contractors and licensors; CHIPS/Fedwire for US-denominated disbursements; and a Commercial Letter of Credit (UCP 600) for material white-label VPN licensing arrangements requiring secured payment performance. Each outward MT103 must carry the correct purpose code — e.g., 'ROYC' or 'TECH' for software licensing, 'GOOD' for procurement, 'ADVT' for advertising services — and be matched against an underlying invoice and purchase order to satisfy GFSC transaction-monitoring and sanctions-screening requirements. The recommended legal structure is to establish or identify a Gibraltar-incorporated qualifying company to hold the EMI account under a treasury services agreement with the Cayman parent, with the Cayman entity acting solely as parent guarantor. Legal constraints include the absence of UK/EU passporting rights for the Cayman vehicle, EMI restrictions on commingling client funds, and enhanced FCA/Gibraltar AML obligations triggered by the VPN-privacy business classification.
