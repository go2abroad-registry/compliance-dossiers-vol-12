Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Cayman Exempted Company. The standard authorised capital is USD 50,000 (50,000 ordinary shares of USD 1.00 each); shares may be issued partly or fully paid at the board's discretion. No escrow account is mandated under the Companies Act; subscription funds may be held in the licensed corporate administrator's client-money account (in Gibraltar or the Cayman Islands) pending share issuance.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Gibraltar Income Tax Office), official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company that carries on a trade in Gibraltar, has a permanent establishment in Gibraltar, or derives Gibraltar-source income must register for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN). Procedure: (1) appoint a Gibraltar-domiciled tax representative; (2) submit Form GTO/REG, including certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, Cayman registered office details, and proof of beneficial ownership for due diligence; (3) disclose the Nature of trading-card publishing activities and target payment rails (Stripe, Authorize.Net); (4) obtain the Gibraltar TIN within 5–10 business days. If no Gibraltar-source income and no permanent establishment exists, registration is not mandatory, but a voluntary application for non-resident status may be filed.

Tax ID type & application path: Territorial_Scope (non-Gibraltar incorporated entity not managed or controlled in Gibraltar; taxation limited to Gibraltar-source income, chargeable gains, and certain Gibraltar-source property; no worldwide taxation in default circumstances). — A Cayman Exempted Company that carries on a trade in Gibraltar, has a permanent establishment in Gibraltar, or derives Gibraltar-source income must register for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope (non-Gibraltar incorporated entity not managed or controlled in Gibraltar; taxation limited to Gibraltar-source income, chargeable gains, and certain Gibraltar-source property; no worldwide taxation in default circumstances).

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no US-style check-the-box classification. Available elections are: (a) hybrid entity election under the Income Tax (International Tax Compliance) Regulations to disapply or apply anti-hybrid matching rules; (b) election under section 19A of the Income Tax Act to treat a non-resident company as resident for treaty/relief purposes upon central management and control being exercised in Gibraltar; (c) Qualifying Company status election for finance licensees, subject to conditions. No election can confer tax-exempt status on a Cayman Exempted Company in Gibraltar.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar does not impose VAT or GST. Indirect taxation is limited to import duties, excise duties on fuel, alcohol and tobacco, and customs levies. Sales of collectible trading cards from Gibraltar to foreign buyers are not subject to a turnover-based indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No statutory threshold. There is no VAT/GST registration threshold in Gibraltar because no VAT/GST exists. Customs registration may be required for commercial movement of goods, but this is a customs procedure, not an indirect tax registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Gibraltar's own indirect tax system because no VAT/GST is levied. For UK VAT purposes, if the Cayman Exempted Company is separately registered for UK VAT through a UK establishment, exports of collectible trading cards to non-UK destinations qualify for zero-rating under the Value Added Tax Act 1994, s.30(6), provided that: (i) the goods are exported from the UK within three months of sale; (ii) evidence of physical export is retained; (iii) the export is to a destination outside the UK. For Gibraltar, exports are outside the scope of any indirect tax and do not generate refunds.

  • Gibraltar export declaration transmitted via the Gibraltar Electronic Customs System (GECS)
  • Commercial invoice (three copies) evidencing ISIC 3240 trading-card classification and declared value
  • Packing list detailing card subjects, quantities and cardboard/foil composition
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (master and house)
  • Certificate of Origin (only when preferential tariff treatment is claimed)
  • Outward proof of physical export (shipping log, airway certificate, carrier receipt) for export status substantiation

No real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate applies in Gibraltar. E-invoicing is voluntary. Customs declarations must be filed electronically through the Gibraltar Electronic Customs System (GECS) / UK National Export System, with Entry/Exit summary declarations (ENS/EXS) for UK-EU movements. Digital stamping applies only to excise goods (alcohol, tobacco, fuel) and not to trading cards.

False export claims or false customs declarations constitute fraudulent evasion. Under Gibraltar customs law, penalties include forfeiture of goods, fines of up to three times goods value, and imprisonment up to two years, with retrospective claw-back of any exemption or refund obtained. For UK VAT, HMRC may issue an assessment covering a period of up to four years, apply penalties up to 100% of the VAT (mitigable on disclosure), and pursue criminal conviction for fraud, with a maximum sentence of seven years' imprisonment. A falsely claimed zero-rating refund would be clawed back in full, with interest and statutory penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains no exchange-control restrictions; the Gibraltar pound is pegged at parity to GBP and is fully convertible. Current-account and capital-account transactions for non-resident entities such as a Cayman exempted company are unrestricted, and dividend or profit remittance is permitted without special regulatory approval. Banking practice still requires source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership verification to satisfy AML/CFT obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement or forced conversion of export receipts in Gibraltar. A collectible trading-card exporter may retain foreign-currency proceeds indefinitely in any domestic or offshore settlement account, subject only to standard bookkeeping and GFSC/FCA-aligned record-keeping rules.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (residual UK central bank for sterling-area monetary policy) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the lead financial regulator for licensing, AML/CFT, and exchange-control administration in Gibraltar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium with substantial legal constraint. A UK or EEA-authorised e-money institution does not have automatic EU passporting rights into Gibraltar. An offshore digital EMI may be used as a non-core receivables collection rail only if it holds or works through a GFSC-licensed payment institution or an appointed Gibraltar corporate-services/licensed agent. For stable multi-tier treasury operations, a GFSC-licensed bank remains the primary clearing route.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% flat rate on chargeable profits of every Gibraltar-resident company (Corporation Tax Act 2010, as amended; flat rate in force since 1 January 2015).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. Gibraltar imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions paid by a Gibraltar-resident company to any shareholder, including a Cayman Exempted Company parent. There is no secondary corporate tax and no remittance charge on distributions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are mandatory where the aggregate value of connected-party transactions exceeds GIP 1,000,000 per chargeable period (approximately GBP 1,000,000). A Country-by-Country Report and notification are required where the group's consolidated annual revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000. Transactions below the threshold must still be conducted at arm's length and a contemporaneous benchmarking summary should be retained to withstand challenge.

Economic substance scope: Cayman Exempted Company receives primary substance discipline under the Cayman Islands International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act 2018. A trading-card publishing operation that engages in distribution, service-centre activity, or the holding/exploitation of intangibles (holographic cold-foil security know-how) falls within 'relevant activity' and must either: (i) satisfy substantial economic presence in the Cayman Islands (directed and managed in Cayman, adequate premises, full-time employees, and core income-generating activities executed there); or (ii) demonstrate tax residency in Gibraltar by obtaining an annual Gibraltar TRC and filing the Gibraltar CIT-CT. A Gibraltar-resident Cayman company must also maintain real substance in Gibraltar: local operating office, locally engaged management, executed contracts, risk-bearing capacity, and actual printing or publishing functions sufficient to prevent round-tripping and BEPS re-characterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Gibraltar Corporation Tax Evasion and Failure to File

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2010 and the Companies Act 2014: late filing penalties escalating from GIP 100 to GIP 3,000; tax-geared penalties up to 100% of the tax understated for deliberate and concealed non-compliance; interest on unpaid tax at the statutory daily rate; GITO may strike the company off and direct the GFSC to terminate or suspend its payment institution and EMI arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly concerned in the default are jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall; the court may issue civil restitution orders, freezing orders, and travel bans with passport surrender; disqualification as a director for a substantial period; criminal conviction for cheating the public revenue may result in imprisonment and unlimited fines.
Cayman Economic Substance (ESR) Failure and Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Cayman Islands International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act: initial penalty CI$12,000 for failing the economic substance test; CI$10,000 for each month of continued failure; CI$10,000 for failure to file the ESR annual notification; intentional false declarations are punishable by fines and imprisonment up to 5 years. The Registrar of Companies may strike off the Cayman entity and the Gibraltar branch registration will be revoked by the Gibraltar Registrar, causing the Stripe/Authorize_Net merchant accounts and any Gibraltar EMI bank account to be closed.

Director personal liability: Individual directors and officers are personally liable for assessed penalties where the default is attributable to their negligence; knowing misstatements in ESR declarations carry criminal liability with custodial imprisonment; the court may make disqualification orders, asset preservation orders, and travel bans to secure attendance before the Cayman Tax Information Authority or the Gibraltar courts.
AML and KYC Deficiency on Payment Rails (Stripe, Authorize_Net, and Gibraltar EMI)

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the Financial Services Act 2019: the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission may impose a civil penalty of up to GIP 100,000 or, in serious cases, 10% of group annual turnover; the GFSC can order a compliance report, suspend the EMI license, or revoke it; merchant processing agreements with Stripe and Authorize_Net will be terminated and the funds held under indemnity; repeat offenders are published on the GFSC enforcement page.

Director personal liability: Directors, MLROs, and compliance officers are personally liable for failing to file SARs or for tipping-off; civil liability includes unlimited fines and compensation to victims; criminal money-laundering convictions carry custodial sentences; travel bans, economic restriction orders, and confiscation of foreign assets may be imposed under proceeds-of-crime legislation.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Documentation Deficiency

Statutory sanction: The Gibraltar Income Tax Office may re-compute taxable profits applying the OECD arm's length standard, disallow the excessive deductions, and impose an inaccuracy penalty of up to 100% of the additional corporation tax at 12.5%; failure to notify CbC-relevant presence incurs a penalty of up to GIP 5,000 per default; in egregious cases the arrangement is recharacterised and the Cayman entity is treated as a Gibraltar-resident taxpayer on worldwide profits.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve inaccurate returns in bad faith are jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax; civil proceedings may pierce the corporate veil to recover transferred sums from shareholders; fraudulent trading findings trigger disqualification; criminal fraud convictions may lead to imprisonment and travel bans while the enforcement judgment is executed.
