Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum authorized or paid-up share capital applies for a Gibraltar private company limited by shares or for its Cayman Exempted Company parent; at least one share must be allotted to the subscriber and fully paid or credited as paid. Share subscription funds are not subject to statutory escrow, but any voluntary paid-up capital deposit is held in the licensed corporate services provider's client trust account and released to the Company only after issuance of the certified share certificate.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate under the Fire and Rescue Services Act 1981 verifying compartmentation, hydrant coverage, and fire annunciation for the UHT tanks and packaging hall; structural load inspection and building completion certificate from the Development and Planning Commission for cold-stores and heavy filling-line footings; occupational clearance from the Health and Safety Inspectorate covering pressure equipment (PED certification of retorts and aseptic vessels), confined-space protocols for sterilization tanks, and temperature/noise exposure controls.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum authorized or paid-up share capital applies for a Gibraltar private company limited by shares or for its Cayman Exempted Company parent; at least one share must be allotted to the subscriber and fully paid or credited as paid. Share subscription funds are not subject to statutory escrow, but any voluntary paid-up capital deposit is held in the licensed corporate services provider's client trust account and released to the Company only after issuance of the certified share certificate.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Commissioner of Income Tax (Income Tax Office, Government of Gibraltar); online e-Tax portal: https://etax.gibraltar.gov.gi. Foreign-company registration is filed with the Registrar of Companies (Companies House Gibraltar); goods movements are administered by HM Customs of Gibraltar.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Cayman Exempted Company as a foreign (overseas) company under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 (s.322) at Companies House Gibraltar, lodging certified constitutional documents, register of directors and shareholders, a Gibraltar registered office, and appointing a licensed Gibraltar resident agent; obtain the Gibraltar Foreign Company Registration Number. Step 2: Submit the completed Application for Company Income Tax Registration to the Income Tax Office together with the Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, Gibraltar registration certificate, and registered-office confirmation; the Commissioner issues the permanent Tax Reference Number (TIN). Step 3: If employing staff in Gibraltar, register for PAYE; if the plant imports packaging materials or exports finished oat-milk goods, obtain an HM Customs importer/exporter code. Step 4: The TIN is quoted on all statutory returns, import/export declarations, invoices, and banking records relating to the Gibraltar branch.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — The Cayman Exempted Company is Tax_Exempt_Zone in its home jurisdiction, but if it carries on trade in Gibraltar through the ISIC 1050 UHT plant or otherwise has a permanent establishment, Gibraltar taxes exclusively profits arising in or derived from Gibraltar at 10% under the Income Tax Act 2010 on an activity/territorial-sourcing basis. Capital gains of a capital nature are outside the scope of Gibraltar income tax unless they arise as trading receipts; a PE profit attribution follows arm's-length transfer pricing principles.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Gibraltar law. Available elections include: (i) joint filing and group relief for companies in a 75% group; (ii) capital-allowances pooling elections and surrender of losses between group members; (iii) an election to adopt a remittance/receipt basis for qualifying foreign trading income where statute permits; and (iv) elections relevant to the OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch rules and the Global Anti-Base Erosion (Pillar Two) top-up tax of 15% applicable to MNE groups exceeding EUR 750m consolidated revenue. The former Qualifying Company and tax-exempt statuses have been abolished.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax — Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area. A limited customs and excise duty regime applies to specific goods (alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oils, and certain luxury/imported items) together with a frontier import-duty system for goods entering Gibraltar; exports from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST turnover threshold exists in Gibraltar. Any entity import/exporting commercial goods, including UHT packaging laminates and finished plant-based beverage products, must obtain an HM Customs importer/exporter code; there is no de minimis turnover exemption, although occasional non-commercial movements may be relieved administratively.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar levies no exit VAT and no export duty on goods manufactured or re-packed at the Gibraltar UHT plant; exports of aseptic hydrolyzed oat milk and packaging are outside the scope of Gibraltar customs duty. Eligibility conditions: (i) goods must physically leave Gibraltar within 3 months of despatch; (ii) the taxpayer must hold a certified export declaration, transport contract, and proof of delivery outside Gibraltar; (iii) full consideration must be received via SWIFT/TT wire or letter of credit before the claim is finalised; and (iv) for duty-paid imported inputs used in exported goods (e.g., packaging laminate), a customs duty drawback may be claimed supported by the original import declaration and evidence of export.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate; supplementary evidence: Commercial Invoice, Packing List, Certificate of Origin (UK-Gibraltar), Duty Drawback Application, and proof of physical departure from the port/airport frontier

None. Gibraltar imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no digital stamping, and no mandatory B2B e-invoicing or API interconnection with the tax authority. Standard commercial invoices and EDI formats accepted by buyers are sufficient; invoices issued by the Gibraltar branch must quote the Gibraltar TIN and, for exports, reference the corresponding customs export declaration number.

HM Customs and the Income Tax Office conduct joint post-clearance audits comparing export declarations, bills of lading, letters of credit, and FX inward remittances. If an export zero-rating/duty-free claim is fraudulent, or export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day deadline absent an approved deferral, the Commissioner may: (i) withdraw the export relief and assess the evaded duty plus interest retrospectively; (ii) impose civil penalties of up to 200% of the tax or duty evaded; (iii) initiate criminal proceedings with fines at large and imprisonment for up to 10 years under the Income Tax Act 2010 and the Customs Ordinance; and (iv) claw back input-duty drawbacks previously refunded on the exported goods. Directors of the Cayman Exempted Company may incur personal liability for fraudulent export documentation and false declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Gibraltar Pound is fixed at parity with GBP and may be freely transferred, exchanged and remitted abroad. A Cayman Exempted Company is permitted to hold and remit multi-currency balances without exchange-control approval. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is unrestricted under Gibraltar exchange rules, but must satisfy standard corporate-law distribution tests under the Gibraltar Companies Act, pass the bank's KYC/source-of-funds verification, and comply with UK/Gibraltar sanctions. Gibraltar imposes no withholding taxes on dividends, interest or royalties paid to foreign shareholders. If the company is managed and controlled from the UK, additional UK tax-residence and permanent-establishment risks must be analysed separately to avoid unintended UK corporation tax exposure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline exists for surrender, conversion or repatriation of export proceeds. No mandatory conversion ratio applies. Export receipts for the UHT oat milk plant may be retained in any convertible currency, held in a Gibraltar bank account, or offset directly against import payments. For banking compliance, each export receipt must be matched with the underlying commercial invoice and bill of lading, and all receipts above the statutory AML threshold must be supported by source-of-funds documentation and sanctions screening.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for the Pound Sterling and the Gibraltar Pound parity); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) (licensing, prudential supervision and AML/CFT enforcement authority for banks and payment institutions operating in Gibraltar).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for auxiliary euro and US dollar collection accounts; Low for the principal treasury function. A UK or EEA-regulated EMI may provide transaction banking and payment initiation services, but a Cayman Exempted Company without an operating track record will face heightened due-diligence rejections. Gibraltar law requires any entity providing e-money or payment services in or from Gibraltar to hold GFSC authorisation or a valid UK-Gibraltar passporting arrangement; the Cayman company itself cannot act as an EMI. EMIs do not support documentary letter-of-credit issuance, large capital-injection escrow facilities, or short-term liquidity buffers. A primary commercial banking relationship with Barclays or Gibraltar International Bank remains mandatory.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required where annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed GBP 1,000,000; Master File required where the MNE group consolidated turnover exceeds GBP 50,000,000 and the Gibraltar entity engages in controlled transactions.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) and Cayman Islands ESR apply to distribution and manufacturing relevant activities. The UHT oat milk packaging business must demonstrate direction and management in Gibraltar, proportionate employees and premises, and core income-generating activities performed in Gibraltar; failure triggers financial penalties, information exchange with Cayman, and potential recharacterisation of income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Liability to underpaid tax, penalties up to 200% of tax, daily surcharges, criminal prosecution under Gibraltar's Income Tax Act and Crime (International Co-operation) Act; imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of unpaid tax; travel bans; disqualification from acting as director for up to 15 years; personal criminal imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar ESR penalties up to GBP 10,000 per infringement and possible strike-off; Cayman ESR penalties up to KYD 20,000, information exchange and dissolution; recharacterisation of profits as Gibraltar-chargeable.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally fined, disqualified, or have property seized; beneficial owners exposed to asset freezes and extradition proceedings in serious cases.
Anti-Money Laundering / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 14 years under Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act; Cayman AML regulations impose fines, suspension of registration and criminal sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and MLROs personally liable for money laundering offences; assets subject to confiscation; travel restrictions and cross-border arrest possible.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment to arm's length, penalties up to 100% of adjustment, interest at 8% per annum, and potential sham transaction recharacterisation.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false TP documentation are personally liable for civil penalties and may be disqualified; criminal liability arises if documents are used to conceal tax evasion.
