Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Ministry of Finance) - administrator of Gibraltar corporate income tax registration, annual returns and the Qualifying Company regime; HM Customs Gibraltar - customs and excise duty administration, EORI facilitation and import/export clearance; Gibraltar Companies House - registration of overseas companies; Cayman Islands General Registry - incorporation, annual return and exemption undertaking administration of the Cayman Exempted Company. The official electronic filing channel is the Gibraltar e-Government tax portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Cayman Exempted Company under the Cayman Islands Companies Act, file the s.6 exemption election and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation and the 20-year tax exemption undertaking (renewable to 30 years). Step 2: Register the overseas company with Gibraltar Companies House within one month of establishing a place of business, obtaining the Gibraltar company registration number. Step 3: File the corporate registration form with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office within 30 days of commencing economic activity to obtain the Gibraltar corporate tax reference (TIN); there is no minimum capital requirement and no turnover threshold for registration. Step 4: If goods movements, warehousing or customs duty matters arise, register with HM Customs Gibraltar, obtain an EORI number and align the entity's customs profile with the HMRC Customs Declaration Service for UK/EU duty arrangements. Step 5: Appoint a Gibraltar-resident registered-office service provider and, where applicable, an auditor, and file annual electronic income tax returns and Cayman annual returns/fees to preserve the exempted status by conducting activity wholly outside the Cayman Islands.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

(i) Cayman s.6 exemption undertaking - statutory election guaranteeing 20-30 years of immunity from Cayman income tax, capital gains tax and stamp duty; (ii) Gibraltar Qualifying Company election under Part 10 of the Income Tax Act 2010 - Commissioner-approved status charging 2% on qualifying profits up to a cap anchored to Gibraltar social-security contributions where local substance is maintained; (iii) Residence/territorial election - the foreign-incorporated exempted company may remain non-resident by keeping central management and control outside Gibraltar, leaving only Gibraltar-source profits within the charge; (iv) ATAD-driven anti-hybrid alignment for royalty and revenue-based financing instruments to prevent deduction/non-inclusion mismatches; no US check-the-box or transparency election is available, and the exempted company is a corporate person taxable at entity level if it becomes Gibraltar-resident.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None applicable - Gibraltar has no VAT, GST or sales/use tax; the indirect tax regime consists solely of customs duties and excise duties on imported goods, aligned with the UK/Common Customs Tariff and administered as importation charges rather than a consumption tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable: because no VAT system exists, no VAT registration threshold in Gibraltar Pounds (GIP) arises. Income Tax registration is triggered irrespective of turnover (threshold: zero, upon commencement of economic activity). HM Customs Gibraltar requires registration and an EORI for any importer/exporter of goods, with no de minimis value threshold for dutiable transactions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar does not operate VAT and therefore has no 0% export VAT rate. For goods, exportation beyond the customs territory removes the goods from the customs duty charge; duty paid on temporary importation or inward processing may be relieved or drawback-claimed upon documented export. For services, revenue-based financing, royalty monetisation and non-dilutive digital-brand financing supplied by the non-resident Cayman Exempted Company to non-Gibraltar counterparties are outside the scope of Gibraltar income tax where not attributable to a Gibraltar trade, permanent establishment, or Gibraltar-source royalties/interest; such offshore income is similarly excluded from the 2% Qualifying Company charge where a QR election is in force.

  • Electronic Customs Export Declaration (CDS/SAD C88) evidencing departure of goods from the customs territory
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or CMR/rail consignment note providing proof of export
  • Certificate of Origin (EUR.1 / Statement on Origin / Form A) where preferential tariff treatment or duty relief is claimed
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds for duty-relief and fund-covenant substantiation
  • Cayman exemption undertaking, Gibraltar EORI number and corporate TIN evidencing the exporting entity's tax identity

Gibraltar does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance regime, digital stamping, or a government-validated B2B e-invoicing mandate. Tax administration is electronic: income tax returns, payments and assessments are processed through the Gibraltar e-Government tax portal; customs declarations are lodged electronically through the HMRC Customs Declaration Service (CDS) aligned with Gibraltar customs procedures, requiring EORI validation and pre-arrival/export clearance checks. Invoices must be issued within one month of supply and retained for six years for income tax, customs and anti-money-laundering substantiation.

Gibraltar has no statutory 180-day FX repatriation rule for customs duty claims, but a 180-day inward-remittance benchmark is applied by the Income Tax Office and financial counterparties as documentary substance; failure to bring export or financing proceeds into the books within that period may trigger an economic-substance enquiry. False declarations, export duty-relief abuses and fraudulent zero-rating attempts expose the fund to clawback of all unpaid duty or drawback, penalties of up to three times the value of the goods, forfeiture, and imprisonment of up to two years under the Customs Ordinance; income-tax error penalties may reach 100% of the tax where concealment or fraud is established, with interest on underpaid tax and assessments reopened for six years (20 years for fraudulent or negligent conduct). The fund may also lose Qualifying Company or non-resident treatment with retrospective withdrawal of the 2% rate, and is subject to enhanced AML scrutiny consistent with the Medium_Risk_Enhanced tier applicable to revenue-based financing structures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates a currency-board arrangement with the GIP pegged at par to GBP. There is no exchange-control legislation; both current and capital account transactions are freely convertible. Royalty remittances and profit distributions from a Cayman Exempted Company administered through Gibraltar are not subject to prior approval, conversion quotas, or withholding constraints. Treasury planning should nonetheless maintain transfer-pricing documentation and substance records to preserve the corporate veil and avoid adverse tax characterization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory FX conversion requirements exist in Gibraltar. All export receipts, financing inflows, and royalty/fee collections may be retained in the receipt currency, swapped through a licensed financial institution, or remitted offshore without any forced conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: HM Government of Gibraltar (Gibraltar Currency Board) as currency issuer for the Gibraltar pound (GIP); prudential and financial-conduct regulation by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High for operational settlement and low complexity disbursements; medium for capital escrow and core treasury. A GFSC-licensed EMI is authorised to deliver payment services and clearing in Gibraltar. UK or EU/EEA EMIs do not have post-Brexit passporting rights into Gibraltar; such offshore EMIs may only serve ancillary flows and cannot replace a Gibraltar-licensed bank/EMI for statutory treasury, capital base, or audit trail integrity. The Cayman parent may maintain a UK EMI for non-Gibraltar disbursements, but all Gibraltar substance transactions should flow through a GFSC-authorized institution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GBP 10,000,000 aggregate intercompany revenue for Local File; Master File required where the Gibraltar group has turnover exceeding GBP 50,000,000; threshold recomputed at the level of cross-border controlled transactions.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 2019 apply to a company carrying on a finance and leasing or holding activity; a financing fund must maintain premises, full-time employees, and board meetings in Gibraltar, and conduct its core income-generating activity from Gibraltar; failure to satisfy the ESR test will result in the income being recharacterised as taxable in Gibraltar at 10% and subject to penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: up to £250,000 fine and/or 10% daily surcharge; civil penalties up to 100% of the underpaid tax; criminal prosecution with a maximum term of 10 years.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors and shadow directors can be personally liable for all unpaid tax plus interest where they knew or caused the filing; travel bans applied under the Income Tax Act; disqualification for up to 15 years and imprisonment for up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: notification of non-compliance and financial penalties up to £1,000 in the first non-compliance year, £10,000 for the second year and either strike-off or restriction from carrying on the relevant activity; officers may be prosecuted.

Director personal liability: Personal liability to pay the penalty if at fault; in addition, a court may order a ban from acting as a director for up to 15 years; the entity veil is pierced where the board has not exercised independent judgement; in egregious cases, assets of directors are frozen pending conclusion of an investigation.
AML / Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act, failure to report suspicious activity carries a penalty of up to £500,000 and/or imprisonment up to 7 years; regulated entities are subject to AML supervision fines, suspension or revocation of the financial services license.

Director personal liability: Company directors are personally liable for breaches by senior officers and can be required to settle confiscation orders; seizure of passports and travel bans may be imposed; personal criminal liability may attach where directors conceal transactions; piercing the corporate veil is available when the fund assets are used to disguise a money-laundering scheme.
