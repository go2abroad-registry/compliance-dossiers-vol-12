UBO Ultimate Beneficial Owner Screening Rules Official Registry Only

>=25% ownership / control

Applicable AML statute: UBO piercing is mandatory through all corporate, trust, partnership and foundation layers to the ultimate natural-person controllers. Governing statutes: (i) Cayman Islands: Companies Act (2023 Revision), Proceeds of Crime Act (2023 Revision), Anti-Money Laundering Regulations (2023 Revision), Beneficial Ownership Transparency Act 2021, Beneficial Ownership (Companies) Regulations 2017, and International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act (2023 Revision); (ii) Gibraltar: Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, Criminal Justice Act 2015, GFSC AML/CFT Guidance Notes and Financial Services Commission Rules, Companies Act 2014 (Register of Persons with Significant Control), and the UK Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018 as extended to Gibraltar by Order in Council; (iii) UK Money Laundering Regulations 2017 used as benchmarking standards for enhanced due diligence. The statutory ownership/control threshold is 25%+1; where no natural person is identifiable at that threshold, indirect interest holders, controllers and senior managing officials must be disclosed and verified.
Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A Cayman Exempted Company must maintain a registered office at a licensed corporate services provider in the Cayman Islands; a virtual or serviced address satisfies the statutory registered-office test only where the provider maintains a physical office and accepts service of process. However, for the licensed physical wholesale brokerage of marine fuel oil and distillates operating through Gibraltar, pure virtual-office status is not acceptable: the GFSC-licensed trading activity and the Gibraltar-licensed bank/EMI relationship require a physical operating address, staffed premises, local substance and demonstrable decision-making presence in Gibraltar. Virtual arrangements materially increase licensing refusal, bank-de-risking and GFSC non-substance enforcement risk.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at 1:1 to GBP and is fully convertible; GBP is itself freely convertible under IMF Article VIII. Both capital and current account transactions are unrestricted. There are no exchange controls, no profit-remittance hurdles, and no requirement for central bank approval to pay dividends, management fees, or intercompany repayments from Gibraltar to the Cayman shareholder. Multi-currency accounts (GBP, EUR, USD) are permissible for a marine-fuel wholesale broker, and USD trade receivables may be held offshore without conversion obligation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. No statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or export-proceeds repatriation requirement exists under Gibraltar law, UK law as extended to Gibraltar, or Cayman Islands law. The Cayman exempted company may retain USD sales proceeds in external accounts or convert at its chosen bank's discretion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (sovereign monetary authority for GBP) in conjunction with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), which acts as the local prudential, AML/CFT, and conduct-of-business supervisor; Gibraltar has no independent central bank or separate foreign-exchange administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium legal viability. Offshore digital EMI providers are unsuitable as primary banking rails for an ISIC 4661 energy commodity broker classified as high risk. Post-Brexit, a UK FCA-authorised EMI requires a separate GFSC authorisation under Gibraltar's E-Money Regulations; an overseas EMI incorporated in low-tax jurisdictions (e.g., BVI, Belize, or other offshore centres) will not be accepted by tier-1 correspondent banks for SBLC or documentary-credit flows. A GFSC-licensed EMI may be used only for collection of sub-threshold brokerage commissions, never for cargo settlement or capital structuring.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from the Cayman parent is injected via SWIFT MT103 corporate credit to the Gibraltar operational account, with remittance instructions reading 'Capital Contribution — Marine Fuel Wholesale Brokerage, ISIC 4661'. The wire should carry the UK/Gibraltar balance-of-payments purpose code for a non-resident equity contribution (capital account category C.1 as advised by the receiving bank). The bank onboarding file must contain a certified Cayman board resolution approving the contribution, a source-of-wealth certificate, and a forward-looking 12-month funds-flow projection to satisfy the account's expected-activity profile.Trade settlements clear through SWIFT MT103 for direct payments and MT202COV for lender-side cover payments via London or New York correspondent nostros. Cargo and bunker purchases above USD 500,000 are settled by irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit advised through a Gibraltar-licensed branch, typically on 30-60 day usance; Standby Letters of Credit provide performance security to suppliers. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% (flat corporate income tax rate under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act; no progressive bands for companies)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar imposes no statutory withholding tax on dividends; dividends paid by the Gibraltar permanent establishment or broker to the Cayman Exempted Company shareholder are paid gross; the Cayman side also levies no dividend withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be delivered electronically no later than 12 months after the end of the accounting period; for a 31 December accounting year-end the filing deadline is 31 December of the following calendar year; payment of any resulting tax liability is due separately by 30 June following the end of the Year of Assessment (1 July to 30 June).

Deadline: · Frequency:

The ESR1-GI must be certified and filed within 12 months of the financial year end (e.g., a 31 December year end requires filing by 31 December of the following year); the filing must be executed by a Gibraltar tax representative or a licensed professional.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filed simultaneously with the CTR1, due 12 months after the accounting period end; supporting Local File and Master File documentation must be fully prepared within the same deadline and produced within 30 days of a written enquiry notice; records must be retained for six years.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GBP 750,000 aggregate net value of controlled cross-border transactions in the accounting period triggers mandatory Local File preparation and electronic filing; group consolidated turnover above GBP 50 million additionally triggers a Master File; marine fuel brokerage commissions, SBLC fees and intercompany financing above these thresholds must be benchmarked to the OECD six-step approach.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance regime mirrors EU and OECD standards; physical wholesale brokerage of crude oil and refined marine fuel products qualifies as 'trading in commodities', which is a designated relevant activity. The company must (i) direct and manage the trading activity in Gibraltar; (ii) perform the core income-generating activities locally, namely contract negotiation, purchase and resale decisions, logistics, index pricing and risk management; (iii) maintain adequate physical premises and at least one full-time qualified employee in Gibraltar; (iv) file ESR1-GI annually; and (v) where the same activity is carried on through the Cayman Exempted Company, satisfy the parallel Cayman Economic Substance test to preserve the Tax_Exempt_Zone classification.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 200% of the tax undercharged, daily interest at 8% per annum, criminal fines up to GBP 500,000, and imprisonment for up to 7 years under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act; a fraudulent return also triggers an unlimited enquiry window with no limitation period.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or caused the fraudulent filing are jointly and severally liable for the full tax debt; the Commissioner may issue personal recovery orders against their bank accounts; civil restitution orders and disqualification from holding directorships apply; a custodial sentence is imposed in a personal capacity for counseling and assisting tax fraud.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Shortfall

Statutory sanction: ESR penalty of GBP 10,000 for the first year and GBP 40,000 for each subsequent year of default; automatic strike-off and deregistration of the Gibraltar trading licence; freezing of brokerage bank accounts; exchange of information with UK HMRC and the Cayman tax authorities; forfeiture of profits derived from the non-substantiated activity.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: directors are personally liable for the unpaid ESR penalties and any tax due on the repatriated commissions; personal travel bans, passport surrender and asset freezing orders are available; directors face disqualification under the Gibraltar Companies Act and joint and several liability to third-party bunker suppliers who suffered loss through the sham structure.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Mispriced Intercompany Trading

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment to 100% of the underdeclared profit plus a secondary adjustment; penalty of 35% of the adjusted tax deficiency; 8% per annum interest from the original due date; denial of the participation exemption on the artificially shifted profits; referral to the international tax authority network under the multilateral framework.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve unreasonable transfer prices without a benchmarking study face personal penalty orders of up to GBP 150,000; a direction order may enforce personal recovery of the adjusted tax; in egregious cases the arrangement is recharacterised as a sham, exposing directors to fraud-based civil damages and extradition risk where counterparties are in the UK or EU.
AML Violation and Unlicensed High-Risk Energy Trading

Statutory sanction: On indictment: unlimited fine and imprisonment of up to 14 years under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act and Anti-Money Laundering legislation; revocation of the banking relationship and of any special licence; escalating fines of up to GBP 500,000 per violation; asset freezing and confiscation orders; blacklisting by correspondent banks terminates all SWIFT wire and SBLC facilities.

Director personal liability: The accountable officer and each director are criminally liable where AML controls failed; courts may impose travel bans, passport surrender and freezing of personal assets; directors face unlimited personal compensation orders to defrauded counterparties; the Cayman corporate veil is lifted on a 'sham' determination, leaving the directors exposed for the full trading liabilities.
