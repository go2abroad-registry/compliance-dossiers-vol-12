Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar, Income Tax Office (ITO) – official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation-and-duties

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Cayman Exempted Company ('KY_EXEMPTED_CO') that establishes a branch, agency or other permanent establishment in Gibraltar must apply for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) within 30 days of commencing business. Submit Form TR-1 to the Income Tax Office with certified copies of the Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, a register of directors and members, and a written declaration confirming that central management and control is located outside Gibraltar. The ITO issues the TRN through the e-Tax Portal. No VAT ID is required because Gibraltar levies no VAT/GST; if the entity imports goods, it must separately register with the Gibraltar Customs and Borders Department for customs duties.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box election comparable to the US. A foreign entity may make a written election under the Income Tax Act 2010 to be treated as a Gibraltar-resident company where central management and control is in Gibraltar, or a negative non-residence election confirming the opposite. For group relief, 75% group companies may jointly elect to surrender tax losses. Anti-hybrid provisions under the Taxes (Anti-Hybrid) Rules 2019 allow election-driven alignment with OECD BEPS Action 2, and advance pricing agreements are available for cross-border transactions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST – Gibraltar applies import and excise duties only; digital services are outside the local indirect tax base

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT/GST registration threshold exists; no consumption tax registration is required. Importers of dutiable goods must register with Gibraltar Customs when single-consignment value exceeds GIP 135 (non-dutiable goods remain below threshold). Digital streaming services have no indirect-tax registration trigger.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – Gibraltar does not levy VAT or GST, so there is no export zero-rating or refund-substantiation procedure. Physical goods exported from Gibraltar are free of export duty, and digital streaming services supplied to non-Gibraltar consumers are outside the indirect tax charge.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping. Taxpayers must retain valid electronic invoices for 7 years and file income tax returns through the ITO e-filing portal. Physical exports/imports require electronic pre-notification via the Gibraltar Single Window environment.

As no VAT/GST zero-rating system exists, retrospective zero-rating clawbacks are not applicable. Nonetheless, false declarations for customs duty relief, export drawback or treaty foreign-tax credits trigger reassessment by Gibraltar Customs and the Income Tax Office. Penalties include up to 150% of the tax/duty understated, civil fines up to GIP 50,000, imprisonment up to 3 years, assessments made within 6 years (20 years for fraud), interest at the statutory 8% per annum, and transfer-pricing adjustments on rerouted streaming revenues.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates a full convertibility regime with the Gibraltar pound (GIP) pegged at 1:1 to GBP. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted. The Cayman Exempted Company parent is domiciled in a no-exchange-control jurisdiction; profit remittance by dividend or loan repayment from Gibraltar to the Cayman shareholder is permitted without prior approval. No withholding tax on dividends or royalties arises in Gibraltar or the Cayman Islands for non-resident recipients under normal circumstances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement exists in Gibraltar or the Cayman Islands. Export receipts, including streaming-platform settlements, distribution royalties and licensing fees, may be retained in USD, EUR, GBP or any other convertible currency without time-bound repatriation or conversion ratios.

Central bank supervision: HM Treasury (UK) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), which acts as the banking, capital markets and foreign-exchange regulatory authority in Gibraltar; the Bank of England issues Gibraltar pound notes under the currency board arrangement with full convertibility to sterling.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for operational settlement only; Low for capital escrow or regulated treasury purposes. Gibraltar-licensed and UK-authorized Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) can provide GBP/EUR virtual IBANs and faster-payment access for streaming-platform settlements, but they are not full banks, do not extend deposit protection, and are subject to safeguarding rather than lending. Legal constraints require that any EMI used must be fully FCA or GFSC authorized, must maintain segregation of customer funds, and must not be used as the primary custodian of shareholder capital or statutory escrow balances.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% (Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, as amended); Cayman Islands direct statutory rate 0% for an exempted company duly holding a Tax Exemption Certificate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - neither Gibraltar nor the Cayman Islands imposes dividend withholding tax on distributions to non-resident shareholders. Distributions from a Cayman exempted company are exempt from Cayman tax and free of Gibraltar WHT even if received by a Gibraltar resident shareholder (as the shareholder is assessable, not the company).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010: tax-geared penalties of up to 100% of the evaded tax, interest at 10% per annum, and a fine and/or imprisonment for up to 7 years upon conviction on indictment.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who signed or caused the false return to be submitted are jointly and severally liable for the penalty and may face criminal prosecution; the Commissioner may issue a direction preventing departure from Gibraltar where tax liabilities remain unpaid.
Economic Substance Failure - Cayman ESR

Statutory sanction: International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act: CI$ 5,000 initial penalty, escalating to CI$ 10,000 per enforcement notice, information exchange with HMRC, and potential strike-off of the company; knowingly providing false information attracts CI$ 10,000 and up to 2 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: The Register of Directors is publicly searchable; directors may be personally liable for unpaid penalties and disqualified from serving in any Cayman or Gibraltar company, plus a court-imposed travel ban and seizure of foreign assets.

Statutory sanction: Cayman Companies (Amendment) Act: daily default fine for non-compliance with the beneficial ownership register; Gibraltar (companies incorporated or registered there) imposes a fine of up to GIP 100,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 2 years for failing to maintain accurate PSC records.

Statutory sanction: Finance Act 2016, Part 2: unlimited fine on the company; HMRC may issue a winding-up petition; details are published on the HMRC named-tax-defaulters panel.

Director personal liability: Senior officers face personal liability for money-laundering offences under the Proceeds of Crime Act, and a managing director who failed to implement adequate prevention procedures is disqualified from acting as a director for up to 15 years.
