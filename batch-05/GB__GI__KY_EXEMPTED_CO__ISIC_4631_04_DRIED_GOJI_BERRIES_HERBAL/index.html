Official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi (Income Tax section); customs e-services and tax filing are processed through the Gibraltar government electronic-service portal.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. A foreign-incorporated entity may elect to be treated as a Gibraltar tax resident by exercising central management and control within Gibraltar, thereby becoming subject to the standard 12.5% corporate income tax on a worldwide basis. A foreign company may also seek recognition under the Gibraltar Companies Act as a Qualifying Recognised Company, aligning its local commercial-law status with standard Gibraltar treatment. For hybrid arrangements, the Income Tax (Hybrid Mismatch) Regulations, mirroring EU ATAD 2017/952, require taxpayers to elect the governing-jurisdiction rule for structured hybrid mismatches. For groups above the EUR 750 million consolidated-revenue threshold, OECD Pillar Two GloBE elections, including the qualified domestic minimum top-up tax and transition safe-harbour elections, are available. Absent an election, the default statutory territorial rules apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No Gibraltar VAT/GST: Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT and customs-union territory; local indirect taxation consists of customs duty and purchase tax applied to selected imported goods (principally alcohol, tobacco, fuel, and luxury items). Dried goji berries and herbal botanicals in transit or re-export are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax. UK VAT (standard 20%) or EU member-state VAT arises only at the destination import point.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No Gibraltar VAT registration threshold exists because Gibraltar does not levy VAT/GST. Operative thresholds: (a) customs registration is mandatory for all economic operators moving goods through Gibraltar with no turnover threshold; (b) UK VAT registration is required when UK taxable turnover exceeds GBP 90,000 (GIP 90,000); (c) EU One-Stop-Shop distance-sales registration is required when cross-border distance sales to a member state exceed EUR 10,000 per annum. For B2B herbal/botanical export supplies, the reverse-charge mechanism generally avoids seller-side VAT registration in the EU member state.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because Gibraltar imposes no VAT, exports of dried goji berries and herbal botanicals leaving Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax, and no Gibraltar VAT refund mechanism applies. Where the Cayman Exempted Company is UK VAT registered, zero-rating is available under VATA 1994 s.30 for goods physically exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK, provided that: (a) the goods are exported within three months of supply (or such longer period as HMRC allows); (b) the exporter holds contemporaneous commercial and customs evidence of export; and (c) the supply is made to an overseas customer. For EU member-state movements, the zero-rating/intra-community reverse-charge exemption applies where the customer's valid EU VAT number is obtained and proof of cross-border transport is retained. No Gibraltar-specific zero-rating certificate exists or is required.

Gibraltar has not introduced a real-time fiscal clearance, Continuous Transaction Control, e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping mandate. Standard electronic commercial invoices in PDF or PEPPOL/UN-CEFACT formats are accepted for customs and banking evidence. Export declarations must be lodged electronically through HM Customs Gibraltar's electronic customs system; cargo entering the UK requires an HMRC Customs Declaration Service (CDS) declaration, and cargo entering the EU requires an Entry Summary Declaration (ENS/ICS2) filed electronically before arrival. No government invoice-validation API is required in Gibraltar, but the underlying invoice-and-export evidence file must be retained for UK/HMRC assessment periods.

Under UK/EU audit practice, the tax authority may require the full export-evidence pack within 30 days; failure to substantiate the export triggers a retrospective VAT assessment with interest, penalties under Finance Act 2007 Schedule 24 (up to 100% for deliberate and concealed inaccuracies), and criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion of VAT under VATA 1994 s.72, carrying imprisonment of up to seven years and unlimited fines. False customs declarations attract civil penalties of up to GBP 3,000 per declaration in the UK and forfeiture of goods; in Gibraltar, misdeclarations under the Customs Ordinance expose the goods to seizure, administrative penalties of up to three times the value of the goods, and criminal prosecution with imprisonment of up to seven years. Assessment time limits in the UK extend to four years for careless errors and 20 years for deliberate or concealed conduct. The Cayman tax exemption does not extinguish directors' personal liability, and the receiving bank may reject or reverse TT payments if the 180-day inward-remittance evidence is not produced.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at par to GBP within a de facto currency board relationship, and the Cayman dollar (KYD) is pegged at 1 KYD = 1.20 USD. Both the Gibraltar and Cayman regimes impose zero statutory exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. Current account items including trade settlement, service fees, dividends, interest, and intercompany charges are freely remittable without central bank approval or declarations, subject only to CRS/FATCA reporting and sanctions screening. Capital account flows such as share capital injection, subordinated loans, and intercompany funding are likewise unrestricted. Profit remittance hurdles are materially absent: Gibraltar levies no withholding tax on dividends, interest, or royalties, and the Cayman exempted company pays no Gibraltar or Cayman tax on offshore trading income; the only administrative step is a board resolution declaring a dividend and the corresponding CRS self-certification of the recipient's tax residence. Banks will apply routine spread-based conversion at interbank reference rates and may require a standard FX agreement and purpose code documentation before executing conversion related to trade remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Gibraltar, the Cayman Islands, or the United Kingdom. Export proceeds from dried goji berry and herbal botanical wholesale sales may be retained indefinitely in any freely convertible currency, including GBP, USD, EUR, CNH, or CNY as permitted by the settling bank. The effective compliance obligation is documentary rather than regulatory: the Cayman exempted company must maintain underlying economic substance records in the Cayman Islands or Gibraltar, including invoices, bills of lading, and bank statements showing the physical flow of goods and payments, to rebut any shell-company classification. The mandatory conversion ratio is therefore 0%, and the statutory retention timeline is unlimited.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) for banking and AML supervision; the Government of Gibraltar Commissioner of Currency is responsible for Gibraltar pound note and coin issuance; the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) supervises the Cayman exempted company home jurisdiction. Neither Gibraltar nor the Cayman Islands maintains an exchange-control authority; the Bank of England anchors monetary conditions via the Gibraltar pound parity peg at 1 GIP = 1 GBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material legal limits. Gibraltar has no distinct e-money institution licensing class under the Financial Services Act, so a UK Financial Conduct Authority authorised EMI or payment institution, or a licensed Gibraltar payment service provider, would be required for any digital-bank-style layer. Practical viability for a Cayman exempted company is constrained because most EMIs apply enhanced KYC scrutiny to offshore entity structures, require the same beneficial ownership disclosure as banks, and refuse to provide documentary credit or complex treasury products. EMIs can serve as an additive collection and small-value disbursement rail for B2B receivables using partner banking rails equivalent to SWIFT, but they are not viable as the primary escrow or trade-settlement platform for commercial letters of credit or high-value bulk goji berry shipments. The recommended architecture is a Tier 1 Gibraltar bank as the master treasury node with an EMI used separately for low-value collections only after obtaining a full legal opinion on the EMI's regulatory passport into Gibraltar.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol requires a two-step wire structure: first, the ultimate beneficial owner's bank sends SWIFT MT103 to the Cayman exempted company's corporate account at a Cayman bank such as Butterfield Bank (Cayman) Limited or Cayman National Bank, with purpose code such as 'SHARECAP' for share subscription or 'DIRIN' for director current account funding; second, the Cayman account sends a SWIFT MT103 GPI wire to the Gibraltar operating account with purpose code 'CAPITAL INJECTION' or 'INTRACOMPANY LOAN', explicitly identifying the underlying share subscription agreement or signed intercompany loan agreement. The pre-wire compliance package must include a board resolution approving the capital movement, certificate of incumbency, source-of-wealth documentation, CRS/FATCA self-certification for the Gibraltar account, and evidence that the Cayman entity has filed its economic substance return. Funds may be injected in GBP, USD, EUR, or CNH; any conversion is executed at the receiving bank's prevailing interbank rate under the account's standing FX instruction. The Gibraltar bank reserves the right to hold funds in a suspense escrow account for up to five business days pending completion of enhanced due diligence on the Cayman structure.The primary clearing rails for trade settlements are: (a) SWIFT MT103 for open-account telegraphic transfer settlements to dried goji berry and herbal botanical suppliers, with purpose code 'TILGO' for goods and the associated commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading retained for compliance matching; (b) commercial documentary letters of credit issued on SWIFT MT700 and amended via MT707, using the Gibraltar account-holding bank as issuing bank and a Chinese correspondent bank such as Bank of China or ICBC as advising and confirming bank for high-value shipments; (c) MT202 and MT202COV for correspondent bank cover in multi-tier USD or CNY clearing; (d) CHAPS and Faster Payments for domestic UK settlement; (e) TARGET2 for EUR-denominated disbursements; and (f) CIPS or offshore CNH clearing for RMB-denominated supplier payments. Each outbound disbursement must be matched against the corresponding import or export declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading, weight certificate, certificate of origin, and phytosanitary certificate. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium feasibility with material legal limits. Gibraltar has no distinct e-money institution licensing class under the Financial Services Act, so a UK Financial Conduct Authority authorised EMI or payment institution, or a licensed Gibraltar payment service provider, would be required for any digital-bank-style layer. Practical viability for a Cayman exempted company is constrained because most EMIs apply enhanced KYC scrutiny to offshore entity structures, require the same beneficial ownership disclosure as banks, and refuse to provide documentary credit or complex treasury products. EMIs can serve as an additive collection and small-value disbursement rail for B2B receivables using partner banking rails equivalent to SWIFT, but they are not viable as the primary escrow or trade-settlement platform for commercial letters of credit or high-value bulk goji berry shipments. The recommended architecture is a Tier 1 Gibraltar bank as the master treasury node with an EMI used separately for low-value collections only after obtaining a full legal opinion on the EMI's regulatory passport into Gibraltar.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 (or equivalent on annual aggregate cross-border related-party transactions) for mandatory transfer pricing documentation. Master File and Local File must be available within 30 days of a request. Even below threshold, all transactions must conform to arm's length principle.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance (ESR) Regulations 2020 impose substance requirements on Gibraltar-resident companies engaged in 'relevant activities', including distribution and service centre activities, which covers bulk wholesale of dried goji berries and herbal botanicals. For such an activity, the company must demonstrate: (i) core income-generating activity performed in Gibraltar; (ii) hiring of adequate employees; (iii) physical premises in Gibraltar; and (iv) direction and control exercised in Gibraltar. Non-resident companies not tax resident in Gibraltar are not automatically within ESR unless they have permanent establishment activity. Failure to meet ESR triggers a financial penalty equal to 10% of the company's relevant income or GIP 100,000 (whichever is higher), with continued failure leading to strike-off. The Cayman exempted company must be careful to align its substance profile with either genuine Gibraltar activity or a valid non-resident position.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Offshore Artificiality

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Income Tax (Assessments and Appeals) Rules allow assessments to be reopened for up to 25 years in fraud or negligent conduct; penalties up to 200% of tax evaded; interest at up to 1.5% monthly. UK HMRC may apply the controlled foreign company rules or General Anti-Abuse Rule; civil penalty plus potential criminal prosecution for cheating the public revenue.

Director personal liability: Directors can face personal civil liability for dishonest assistance or breach of fiduciary duty; UK Criminal Finances Act 2017 creates a corporate offence for failure to prevent the facilitation of tax evasion, with unlimited fines and personal prosecution of directors. If a director signs false returns, imprisonment up to 7 years, asset freezing orders, and travel bans (including restriction on leaving Gibraltar) can be imposed.
Shell Company Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar ESR penalties: up to GIP 100,000 for the first failure; strike-off of the company. Under UK-DTA rules, the entity may be treated as UK-resident due to central management and control, exposing it to 25% UK corporation tax. HMRC may also apply the 'offshore receipts in respect of intangible property' or transfer pricing adjustments.

Director personal liability: A director knowingly allowing the company to operate without substance can be disqualified up to 15 years; personal penalties; in appropriate cases criminal proceedings for tax evasion and money laundering. Travel bans are used to secure attendance; assets may be subject to restraint orders if the accused has left the jurisdiction.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar tax authorities may adjust profits using the arm's length standard; a penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax liability; interest on the underpayment; for multinational groups, the UK Diverted Profits Tax at 31% may apply to arrangements with low economic substance exploiting tax asymmetries.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable in negligence to discharge company tax debts in certain wind-up scenarios; if they knowingly implement sham transfer pricing, creditors or the tax authority can seek contribution; criminal liability arises for false statements; UK CDDA/Director Disqualification may be triggered; travel bans and depersonalised international arrest warrants may follow.
AML and Sanctions Failure in International Herbal Trade

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act penalties: unlimited fines and imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering; GFSC administrative penalties up to GIP 5 million for AML failures; freezing of bank accounts; cancellation of the company's beneficial ownership registrations. International sanctions violations invoke UK Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors have personal criminal liability if they know or suspect laundering but fail to submit a suspicious activity report (SAR). Imprisonment up to 14 years, personal fines, confiscation orders on all assets, travel bans, and extradition treaties allow cross-border enforcement.
false DTA Benefit and UK Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: HMRC can apply the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) under the UK-Gibraltar DTA to deny all treaty benefits; reassess UK income tax and NIC for the individual, plus inheritance tax concerns; penalties up to 200% of the tax; criminal prosecution lies for benefit fraud and tax evasion.

Director personal liability: The de facto director in the UK faces personal income tax liability for undeclared profits, court restitution orders, and a maximum term of imprisonment of 7 years under the UK Fraud Act 2006. HMRC can obtain a travel prohibition order and may freeze personal assets in the UK and Gibraltar via mutual assistance.
