Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar, Commissioner of Income Tax - Income Tax Office, 2nd Floor, 5-9 Flaxmill Road, Gibraltar; e-filing portal: https://etax.gibraltar.gov.gi (Gibraltar e-Tax); Companies House Gibraltar for business registration: https://www.companieshouse.gi

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company with a physical or administrative presence in Gibraltar must first register as a foreign company at the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies (Companies House) under the Companies Act 2014, receiving a Company Number and Certificate of Registration. Within 30 days of commencement of business the company must file an Application for Tax Registration with the Commissioner of Income Tax, under which the Company Number is converted to a 9-digit Gibraltar TIN/reference. The company must then file an annual Income Tax Return even if profits are wholly foreign-sourced, and may apply for a Certificate of Residence or a letter of non-liability where no Gibraltar-source profit is derived.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box regime. The Income Tax Act 2010 taxes all companies at 12.5% solely on profits accruing in or derived from Gibraltar (territoriality principle, s. 18 ITA 2010). The Cayman Exempted Company holds a statutory 30-year Tax Exemption Certificate issued under the Cayman Islands Tax Exemption Law; in Gibraltar the only relevant written election is the application to the Commissioner for exclusion of foreign-source profits supported by OECD transfer pricing and substance documentation, and the transitional election for grandfathered exempt-company status available only to companies exempt before 31 December 2010 (new applicants are ineligible).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

VAT - Not applicable in Gibraltar

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area (Protocol 3 to the Act of Accession) and levies no VAT or GST; no VAT registration threshold exists. Import duties apply only to physical goods entering Gibraltar at rates set by the Customs Duties Ordinance; for pure film-copyright licensing (no physical medium) no indirect-tax registration event is triggered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export VAT zero-rating is not available because Gibraltar imposes no VAT or consumption tax. Independent film copyright licensing is intangible; the income falls outside Gibraltar taxation only if (i) the licensee is outside Gibraltar and exercises no local exploitation, (ii) management and control of the Cayman Exempted Company remains in the Cayman Islands (or another substantive business location) meeting BEPS/economic-substance requirements, and (iii) all contracts, sub-licences and payment flows demonstrate a non-Gibraltar source. Physical exports of films/merchandise are duty free on export and no refund procedure is required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (required only where physical film prints or merchandise cross the Gibraltar frontier; not applicable to pure IP licensing - a Self-Declared Export Invoice with ISIC 5911 description suffices for tangible goods)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (physical goods only; for intangible licensing the corresponding evidence is the signed copyright licence agreement and payment clearing advice)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT credit or Letter of Credit negotiation instrument evidencing receipt of foreign-sourced royalty income to substantiate the non-Gibraltar-source position)

Gibraltar has no mandatory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping regime. Taxpayers are free to issue commercial invoices; original records must be kept for 6 years. Income Tax returns must be submitted electronically via the e-Tax portal, and bank confirmations of SWIFT/LC inward remittances must be retained to substantiate the foreign source of licensing royalties.

Gibraltar imposes best-of-judgment assessments under s. 87 Income Tax Act 2010 where substance and source documentation is deficient; penalties range up to 150% of the tax undercharged plus daily interest, and fraudulent claims expose directors and the company to criminal fines and up to 3 years imprisonment under s. 88 ITA 2010. Where a Cayman tax-exemption certificate is used to shield profits that in reality arise in Gibraltar, the Commissioner may retrospectively claw back tax for up to 6 years, and the UK-Gibraltar Double Taxation Arrangement plus the OECD BEPS Substance Rules permit exchange of information and secondary adjustments for mispriced film-fee transactions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both the United Kingdom and Gibraltar maintain full current-account and capital-account convertibility without exchange control legislation. The Gibraltar Pound (GIP) is pegged at parity to the British Pound (GBP) and is freely substitutable; non-resident Cayman Exempted Companies may hold GIP, GBP, EUR, or USD multi-currency accounts and remit film licensing royalties, capital injections, dividends, and trade payments without prior central-bank approval or conversion restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion timelines, or repatriation quotas applicable to film-licensing export receipts. Royalties and license fees derived from international film distribution may be retained in any currency, freely reinvested, or converted at prevailing market rates in accordance with standard treasury policy.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (BoE) for UK monetary and GBP stability, in conjunction with the Government of Gibraltar's Note Issuance Office and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) for regulatory supervision of the Gibraltar financial sector

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium with substantial compliance overlay. Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise and Revolut Business may offer agile multi-currency account structures and lower onboarding friction, but they generally restrict onboarding to entities with genuine UK/EEA nexus. A Cayman Exempted Company can pass only where it presents a registered agent attestation, economic substance evidence, and a robust filmed-rights chain. Legal constraints under the Gibraltar Financial Services (Electronic Money) Regulations 2011 and UK EMI regulations render such accounts subject to discretionary risk-based closure or transaction holds if the licensing company lacks clear business purpose.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar Local File is required where related-party transactions equal or exceed GBP 1,000,000 per chargeable accounting period; Master File is required where the multinational group has consolidated revenue above EUR 50,000,000; SME groups (fewer than 250 employees, turnover below EUR 50,000,000 or balance sheet total below EUR 43,000,000) are relieved of mandatory documentation but must still meet the arm's-length standard.

Economic substance scope: The Income Tax (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019 apply to relevant activities, and independent film licensing is treated as an intellectual-property business. A tax-resident compnay or PE must demonstrate that core income-generating activities (CIGA) are directed and managed in Gibraltar, with adequate physical premises, full-time employees, and operating expenditure proportionate to licensing activity. An annual ESR notification must be filed with the GITO ESR desk; breach attracts penalties up to GBP 100,000, automatic exchange of information, denial of tax residence certificates, and potential compulsory strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 200% of the tax evaded, interest at 8% per annum, unlimited criminal fine, imprisonment up to 7 years; automatic exchange of information with the UK and Cayman under the OECD MCAA and bilateral arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause a false return are jointly and severally liable; civil restitution orders, seizure of film-asset proceeds, travel bans, and disqualification as a director for up to 15 years.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic substance penalties up to GBP 100,000; loss of the 0% non-resident concession with deemed profits assessed at 12.5%; compulsory strike-off; exchange of information with Cayman and the UK.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the company is a mere façade, exposing the beneficial owner and nominee directors personally; director disqualification, asset-freezing orders, and travel bans against controlling officers.
