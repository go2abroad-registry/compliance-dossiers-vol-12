Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone. The Cayman Exempted Company is tax-transparent in Cayman with no corporate profit tax; in the UK it will normally be treated as a separate non-UK resident company subject to corporation tax only if it carries on a trade in the UK through a permanent establishment; in Gibraltar, non-resident companies are not subject to Gibraltar income tax on foreign-source profits.

Available election forms

No Gibraltar or UK check-the-box regime exists for a foreign exempted company. The entity may, however, elect to register as an external company under Gibraltar Companies (Registration of Overseas Companies) Act; for tax purposes it may make an overseas company declaration under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act and may rely on UK anti-hybrid mismatch provisions if the Cayman entity is treated as transparent in Cayman and opaque in the UK. The UK will apply its hybrid mismatch legislation if an election creates a double deduction or deduction-with-custom-exemption.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 90,000 annual UK VAT registration threshold; however, a non-established overseas supplier must register for UK VAT immediately upon making taxable supplies in the UK where the goods are located in the UK at the time of sale, and must appoint an established tax representative in the UK if outside the EU.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available under VATA 1994, Schedule 8 and Section 30(6) when goods are exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK (including Gibraltar and the Channel Islands) and the exporter retains positive evidence that the goods were physically removed from the UK within a specified period. Offshore services in connection with exported goods may be zero-rated when supplied to an overseas person who belongs outside the UK. The seller must issue a valid VAT invoice and hold incontrovertible transport documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CDS/SAD/EAD)
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill or freight receipt showing port of departure outside the UK
  • Signed proof of delivery from overseas consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT/confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the seller's bank account

UK VAT returns must be submitted digitally under Making Tax Digital (MTD) via HMRC-recognised software and APIs; real-time invoice clearance and fiscal stamping are not mandatory. For export and customs declarations, the UK uses the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) and the Single Administrative Document (SAD), with electronic filing, customs agent access via CHIEF replacement APIs, and no requirement for digital stamping.

HMRC may retroactively withdraw export zero-rating if the statutory export evidence is not timely or is false; the taxpayer may be assessed for any unpaid VAT within 6 years, or 20 years for deliberate or concealed conduct, with interest. Penalties under Finance Act 2007 range from 30% to 100% of the VAT lost for careless or deliberate inaccuracies, and facilitation of fraudulent zero-rating may trigger civil penalties and criminal prosecution. Funds not repatriated within the 180-day requirement can lead to clawback of the export relief and denial of future zero-rating claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar has no exchange controls. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued by the Government of Gibraltar and is fixed at parity with GBP under a currency board-like arrangement; sterling and GIP circulate interchangeably. The Cayman Exempted Company operates with a fully convertible Cayman Islands financial account subject to no capital controls; dividends, intercompany loan repayments and invoice settlements may be freely remitted to or through Gibraltar. Profit repatriation from Gibraltar to the Cayman entity is unrestricted and does not require consent from any exchange control authority. The only practical hurdles are documentary: evidence of beneficial ownership, source of funds, economic substance status in Cayman, and compliance with UK/Gibraltar sanctions lists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules or mandatory conversion ratios in the United Kingdom or Gibraltar. Export and subscription-box service receipts may be retained in any freely convertible currency. No requirement exists to convert receivables into GIP or GBP within any timeline. This also applies to the Cayman Exempted Company: Cayman Islands laws impose no surrender or repatriation quota. A commercial bank may nevertheless impose internal contract-based expectations that receivables are settled in major currencies and that supporting invoices are retained for anti-money-laundering review.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the territorial banking, payments and anti-money laundering supervisor; Bank of England as the United Kingdom's central monetary authority maintaining sterling stability and acting as lender of last resort for GBP-denominated clearing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High. Reputable Gibraltar-licensed or UK FCA-authorized electronic money institutions supporting cross-border multi-currency IBANs can rapidly onboard the Cayman Exempted Company through remote video identity verification and automated document checks. However, many EMIs treat a Cayman exempted entity as an offshore-zone/non-tax-resident structure, resulting in initial manual underwriting, enhanced source-of-funds scrutiny, and possible rejection where the company lacks demonstrated non-Cayman economic substance. Structurally permissible, but treasury policy should rely on a hybrid model: a domestic Gibraltar bank for batched high-value trades, plus a regulated EMI for low-value recurring card collection and rapid supplier disbursements in regional currencies.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant capital flows into the Gibraltar operating treasury are structured as equity injection or shareholder intercompany lending from the Cayman Exempted Company. Routing uses SWIFT MT103/ISO 20022 corporate-to-bank payment instructions from the Cayman bank through correspondent banking to the Gibraltar account. The payment purpose code must identify capital transfer or intercompany financing, e.g. UK/Gibraltar BOE_EI code for equity injection or code 9900/INTERCOMPANY where required; supporting documents must include a board resolution, a signed loan facility agreement or share subscription agreement, and contemporaneous source-of-funds evidence from the Cayman entity. The receiving Gibraltar bank will execute QI/QDD CDD and confirm the ultimate beneficial ownership before crediting; all funds must be reflected in the entity's capital contribution or shareholders' loan register with no cash-back layering.Subscription-box trade settlements use SWIFT MT103 for standard supplier payments and MT700 documentary letters of credit for high-value bulk procurement of physical box contents. Operational disbursements from Gibraltar may be made in GIP, GBP, EUR, USD, CAD, AUD or regional currencies via SWIFT or the SEPA/CHAPS clearing network, depending on the supplier's clearing jurisdiction. Consumer refunds and chargeback recompense are executed through Stripe and Authorize.Net by originating settlement credits from the Gibraltar collection account, with clearing rails configured under Visa/Mastercard scheme rules. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No local statutory threshold is codified in the Income Tax Act, but the Gibraltar Revenue expects OECD-aligned transfer pricing documentation for all connected-party transactions. As a practical compliance floor, prepare a Local File whenever the Gibraltar PE or resident company turnover exceeds GIP 5,000,000, or aggregate connected-party transactions exceed GIP 500,000, per accounting period.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance (Companies) Act applies to all resident companies and to registered foreign companies with a relevant activity in Gibraltar. A recurring subscription-box distribution and service-centre operation is a relevant activity. The company must maintain adequate physical presence, qualified employees, expenditure, and core income-generating activities in Gibraltar, and must file the annual Economic Substance Notification/Return. A Cayman Exempted Company claiming exemption in its home jurisdiction must nevertheless demonstrate substance in Gibraltar or be able to prove that all strategic activities are genuinely conducted in Cayman and not in Gibraltar.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent misstatement

Statutory sanction: On indictment, corporations face unlimited fines and directors face imprisonment of up to 7 years. Civil penalties may reach 100% of the tax lost plus statutory interest from the original due date. Offences may be pursued under the Income Tax Act and the Criminal Justice (Money Laundering) Act where money derived from evasion is concealed.

Director personal liability: Directors and company officers who signed or caused the false return may be personally prosecuted. Courts may impose compensation orders, freeze personal assets, grant travel bans, order passport surrender, and disqualify directors from acting as company officers for up to 15 years.
Shell company / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to GIP 100,000 per infringement under the Economic Substance (Companies) Act; possible compulsory strike-off from the Gibraltar Register; denial of tax residence status; and re-assessment of profits at the standard 12.5% rate on the full income attributable to the de facto management in Gibraltar.

Director personal liability: A director who permits nominee or sham administration may be held personally liable for unpaid taxes, be subject to a director disqualification order, and face a financial services investigation. Travel restrictions and seizure of personal assets are available where the corporate veil is pierced for fraud or evasion.
Transfer pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may recalculate taxable profits at arm's length, assess additional tax at 12.5%, and impose interest and penalties up to 100% of the additional tax. Deliberate TP manipulation with false documents may be prosecuted as tax evasion, attracting unlimited fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: CFOs and tax directors who sign transfer pricing declarations or knowingly process artificial intercompany invoices may be personally liable for the additional tax, subject to disqualification, and may face travel bans pending tax recovery.
AML and unlicensed payment intermediation

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed money service business activity attracts regulatory action by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission. Principal liability under the Criminal Justice (Money Laundering) Act includes unlimited fines and imprisonment of up to 14 years; a Proceeds of Crime confiscation order may be made against all traced assets.

Director personal liability: Directors may be prosecuted as principals if they knew or deliberately ignored suspicious payment flows. Personal civil liability includes restitution of laundered funds, confiscation of personal property, travel bans, and disqualification from holding a financial services position.
