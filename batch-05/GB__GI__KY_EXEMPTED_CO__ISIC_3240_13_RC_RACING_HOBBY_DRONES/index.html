Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Gibraltar is a separate tax jurisdiction from the United Kingdom and is outside the UK and EU VAT systems.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Cayman Exempted Company is exemption-certificated under Cayman law (no income, corporate, or capital tax in Cayman); in Gibraltar it remains outside the corporate tax net unless central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar (creating residence) or sales generate a taxable permanent establishment under Income Tax Act 2010, s.11; territorial scope applies to non-residents, so only Gibraltar-source profits are chargeable.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Gibraltar. The Cayman exempted company may elect to continue/redomicile into Gibraltar (Companies (Transfer of Domicile) Act 2014), thereby becoming Gibraltar-resident and chargeable at 10%; alternatively, it may preserve non-resident status by keeping all management-and-control functions offshore. ATAD hybrid-mismatch rules (in force 2020) apply automatically and prohibit the use of the Cayman entity's exempt status to create double deductions or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes; group-relief elections under the Income Tax Act 2010 are limited to Gibraltar-resident group members.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or general sales tax in Gibraltar; indirect taxation consists of import duties and excise duties under Gibraltar customs legislation. Gibraltar is excluded from the UK VAT area and the EU VAT area, so no UK-style VAT registration applies to Gibraltar domestic or export supplies; retail sales of FPV drones to consumers in Great Britain/Northern Ireland may separately trigger UK VAT obligations above the £90,000 statutory registration threshold.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 0 — no turnover threshold applies because no VAT/GST is levied. Any person importing goods into Gibraltar must nonetheless obtain an Importer/Exporter Code from HM Customs Gibraltar and file customs declarations; no economic-activity threshold for direct-tax registration exists, as all present/operating companies must register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — Gibraltar has no VAT, so there is no export zero-rating claim or refund mechanism. Exports of FPV racing drones, carbon-fiber airframe components, and brushless-motor assemblies from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation and may be effected duty-free upon lodgment of an export declaration. For direct-tax purposes, export profits of the Cayman exempted company are not Gibraltar-source if sale contracts are negotiated and concluded outside Gibraltar and shipments are fulfilled without a Gibraltar permanent establishment; any Gibraltar warehouse, stockholding, or sales personnel would create a PE chargeable at 10%.

  • Gibraltar Customs export declaration (electronic Single Administrative Document filed via the Gibraltar Customs declaration system)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (CN22/CN23 postal customs form for small-value consignments)
  • Inward remittance evidence (bank SWIFT MT103 credit advice or PayPal/Stripe settlement reports evidencing receipt of export proceeds)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list

Gibraltar imposes no real-time e-invoicing, fiscal-clearance, API, or digital-stamping mandate. Traders must retain ordinary commercial invoices (paper or PDF) and supporting records for six years (Income Tax Act 2010, s.51). Customs declarations for imports and exports are filed electronically through the Gibraltar Customs authorities' declaration system (Single Administrative Document format).

Gibraltar imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline; the 180-day benchmark is applied as documentary-consistency evidence for export proceeds. Fraudulent evasion of customs duty attracts civil penalties of up to 100% of evaded duty, forfeiture of goods, and imprisonment for up to 7 years under Gibraltar customs law. The Income Tax Act 2010 general anti-abuse rules (ss.63–65) and the 2020 ATAD hybrid-mismatch provisions allow the Commissioner to counteract artificial structures and make retrospective assessments for up to 21 years in cases of fraud or negligent misstatement. Gibraltar-resident companies failing economic-substance requirements face fines of up to GIP 10,000 and possible compulsory striking-off.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The GIP is fully convertible at par into GBP and, through the floating GBP, into USD, EUR, CNY and all major international currencies. Gibraltar operates no exchange-control regime: current-account (trade and service) and capital-account (share subscription, intercompany funding, profit remittance) transactions are unrestricted, require no central-bank approval and are not subject to mandatory conversion. The Cayman Exempted Company is treated as non-resident for Gibraltar corporate income tax purposes; profit remittance to Cayman shareholders carries zero Gibraltar withholding tax, and Cayman imposes no income, capital or distribution tax on an exempted company. Residual practical hurdles are procedural only: (i) GFSC-aggravated enhanced due diligence applied to the offshore ownership structure; (ii) demonstration of economic substance in Gibraltar before a licensed bank will open multi-currency USD/EUR position capacity; and (iii) correspondent-bank confirmation of the GIP/GBP offshore clearing route. None constitutes a convertibility restriction within the meaning of IMF Article VIII obligations. Funds may be held indefinitely in GIP, GBP, USD or EUR multi-currency accounts without a repatriation timeline.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Gibraltar and the United Kingdom impose no statutory timeline for repatriation, no mandatory surrender, and no conversion ratio for export receipts of a non-bank exporter. The company may retain export receipts in their original currency indefinitely. Residual compliance obligations: (i) GFSC AML/CFT record-keeping under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 requires full reconciliation of incoming beneficiary funds within five business days and retention of underlying trade documentation (commercial invoice, customs declaration, transport document) for five years; (ii) merchant settlement flows from PayPal and Stripe arrive gross of acquiring fees in GBP and must be swept to the licensed operational account; (iii) if the company elects to net quarterly treasury positions, the bank will require an internal FX policy evidencing that no speculative trading posture is held.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the competent financial services regulator and foreign-exchange administration for the jurisdiction. There is no independent Gibraltar central bank; the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued by HM Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934 with a fixed 1:1 parity and 100% backing by Bank of England (BoE) sterling. The Bank of England therefore constitutes the monetary anchor for the GIP/GBP currency union, and the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) is the home-state supervisor for the Cayman Exempted Company for licensing and economic-substance filings.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a secondary collection or disbursement rail; Low as a sole or primary operational treasury account. Legal constraints: (i) under the Gibraltar Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act, as modified by the UK-Gibraltar post-Brexit financial services arrangements, only GFSC-authorised electronic-money institutions or UK FCA-authorised firms that have completed cross-border passporting notice into Gibraltar may lawfully hold and transmit funds for this entity; an unauthorised offshore digital EMI would be acting without a licence; (ii) e-money is not a bank deposit and carries no GFSC depositor-protection or FSCS-equivalent guarantee; (iii) EMIs cannot provide commercial letters of credit, bank guarantees, documentary collections, interest-bearing escrow, or FX derivatives, and therefore cannot satisfy the trade-disbursement and hedging rails required by the import-heavy FPV drone supply chain; (iv) PayPal and Stripe remain the target retail acquiring rails, but their payouts should be settled directly into the licensed operational bank account and swept centrally rather than held at an EMI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing: the Cayman shareholders transmit USD via SWIFT MT103 into the Gibraltar Capital Escrow Account, using the ISO 20022 ExternalPurpose code 'INTC' (intra-company payment) and including in field 70 the narration 'Share Capital Subscription - Cayman Exempted Company 549300KY_EX'. The receiving Gibraltar bank applies source-of-funds verification on the ultimate remitter within two banking days, records the transaction in its CDD files, and releases funds to the escrow ledger. Verification bundle to accompany the wire and the subsequent escrow release: (i) apostilled board resolution authorising the issuance of ordinary shares and naming the escrow account; (ii) updated share certificate and register of members; (iii) notarised signature mandate; (iv) Cayman registered agent certificate of good standing; (v) CRS and FATCA self-certification. (1) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for pre-payment deposits (typically 20-30% of purchase-order value) to Chinese and Asian suppliers, using the ISO 20022 ExternalPurpose code 'TRDP' (trade payment) and referencing the contract, the pro-forma invoice and the HS classification of the drone components. (2) Irrevocable sight commercial letters of credit issued by NatWest International (Gibraltar) and advised through a Southern Chinese correspondent bank, settling against clean-on-board bills of lading, packing lists and supplier quality certificates. (3) Documentary collections (documents against payment, sight) as a transitional mechanism while supplier credit-standing is being established. (4) UK and Gibraltar domestic clearing - CHAPS, Faster Payments and cross-guaranteed sterling settlement - for payroll, office rent, EU wholesale invoices and re-import duty payments, executed through the Gibraltar branch's UK sterling clearing membership. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as a secondary collection or disbursement rail; Low as a sole or primary operational treasury account. Legal constraints: (i) under the Gibraltar Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act, as modified by the UK-Gibraltar post-Brexit financial services arrangements, only GFSC-authorised electronic-money institutions or UK FCA-authorised firms that have completed cross-border passporting notice into Gibraltar may lawfully hold and transmit funds for this entity; an unauthorised offshore digital EMI would be acting without a licence; (ii) e-money is not a bank deposit and carries no GFSC depositor-protection or FSCS-equivalent guarantee; (iii) EMIs cannot provide commercial letters of credit, bank guarantees, documentary collections, interest-bearing escrow, or FX derivatives, and therefore cannot satisfy the trade-disbursement and hedging rails required by the import-heavy FPV drone supply chain; (iv) PayPal and Stripe remain the target retail acquiring rails, but their payouts should be settled directly into the licensed operational bank account and swept centrally rather than held at an EMI.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar follows OECD-aligned transfer-pricing principles under the Income Tax Act. Local File documentation is administratively expected where cross-border related-party transactions exceed GIP 1,000,000 in an accounting period; Master File is expected where the worldwide group (including the Cayman parent) has consolidated turnover above GIP 45,000,000. The FPV racing-drone retail operation will normally fall below these thresholds, but contemporaneous benchmarking evidence should be retained whenever related-party purchases of motors, carbon-fibre, or intellectual property occur.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to companies tax-resident in Gibraltar carrying on relevant activities, including distribution and service centre activity, holding companies, and intellectual-property holding. The company must be directed and managed in Gibraltar and maintain adequate physical premises, qualified staff, and proportionate operating expenditure in Gibraltar. A Cayman Exempted Company that is merely registered in Gibraltar but has no substance and claims non-residence is also separately exposed to the Cayman Islands Economic Substance Law if it fails to satisfy the Cayman substance tests.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Upon conviction, an unlimited fine or a fine of up to £250,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years; civil penalties up to 100% of the tax understated plus interest at 8% per annum; forfeiture of assets connected to the evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow officers who knowingly procure or connive in evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. Travel bans, asset freezing orders, disqualification from acting as a director for up to 15 years, and imprisonment are available.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Enforcement under the Economic Substance Regulations may result in financial penalties up to GIP 25,000 per offence, compulsory strike-off of the company, and deemed-profit assessments based on the offshore activity; late filings carry additional GIP 100 daily penalties.

Director personal liability: A director knowingly making a false economic-substance declaration is personally liable for the penalty and costs. The court may order restitution of profits extracted, impose travel bans, and disqualify directors from managing Gibraltar or Cayman companies.
Anti-Money Laundering / False Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Breach of the Proceeds of Crime Act and Gibraltar AML framework: unlimited fines, seizure and confiscation of bank accounts, reporting to the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit, and imprisonment for up to 7 years; regulated payment processors may close accounts and file suspicious activity reports.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false beneficial-ownership statements are personally liable for criminal penalties and civil compensation to defrauded creditors. The corporate veil is pierced where the Cayman entity is used as a sham; personal bank accounts, domicile and travel movements become subject to restraint orders and cross-border arrest warrants.
Transfer Pricing / Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: The Gibraltar tax authority may re-characterise non-arm's length payments under transfer-pricing rules, disallow the deduction, assess additional corporation tax at 10%, and apply interest at 8% per annum and penalties of up to 100% of the tax avoided; failure to produce documentation shifts the burden of proof against the company.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or cause artificial-pricing arrangements without a valid commercial purpose are personally liable for fraudulent or wrongful trading. Courts may issue compensation orders in favour of the revenue authority, injunctive asset freezes, and travel bans pending full restitution.
