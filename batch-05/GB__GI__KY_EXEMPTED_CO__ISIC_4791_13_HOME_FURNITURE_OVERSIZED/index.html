Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Commissioner of Income Tax, Gibraltar (portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax); HM Revenue and Customs for UK VAT and customs export/import matters (portal: https://www.gov.uk/hmrc).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Confirm the Cayman Exempted Company's Certificate of Incorporation and Cayman Tax Exemption Certificate issued by the Cayman Islands Registrar of Companies; Cayman does not issue a TIN or VAT ID because it imposes no direct taxation. Step 2: If the company establishes a place of business in Gibraltar, register as an overseas company with Gibraltar Companies House, then apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar TIN using the overseas registration certificate, constitutional documents, and evidence of central management and control; a TIN is issued only if the company is resident in Gibraltar or has Gibraltar-source income. Step 3: Gibraltar has no VAT, so no Gibraltar VAT ID is obtainable; register for customs identification as importer/exporter with Gibraltar Customs for physical goods movements. Step 4: If the company imports goods into the UK or makes taxable supplies from UK stock, register for UK VAT through HMRC Form VAT1; a non-established taxable person must register without regard to the standard UK turnover threshold.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Cayman: file for and maintain a Tax Exemption Certificate under the Tax Concessions Act, confirming a 20-year exemption from direct taxation on profits, income, gains, and appreciation. Gibraltar: no US-style check-the-box election exists; company residence follows central management and control, and non-residence may be evidenced by absence of Gibraltar board/corporate decisions. UK/Gibraltar hybrid mismatch rules apply to cross-border instruments and entities; where a mismatch is identified, contemporaneous elections or documentation may be required to align tax treatment, but no elective elimination of a covered mismatch is generally available to third-party investors.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT or GST in Gibraltar; specified goods are subject to Gibraltar customs/excise duties and purchase tax. UK VAT at the standard rate may apply where goods are imported into or supplied from the UK.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: no VAT registration threshold because no VAT is levied; customs registration is mandatory for importers/exporters. If UK VAT registration is triggered, a non-UK-established business must register from the first taxable supply, whereas a UK-established business making domestic taxable supplies has a statutory threshold of GBP 85,000 unless making zero-rated or intra-EU supplies that remove the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For UK VAT, zero-rating applies to goods physically exported from the UK by the seller, on the seller's behalf, or by the customer to a destination outside the UK, provided removal is evidenced within the prescribed period (normally 3 months from supply). Eligibility criteria include: the goods leave the UK; the seller retains and records legal title at the time of export; the customer is outside the UK; and commercial proof of export is obtained and retained for six years. Certain offshore services directly connected to exported goods may also qualify for zero-rating. Because Gibraltar has no VAT, no Gibraltar zero-rating refund procedure exists; exports from Gibraltar must instead follow UK customs and VAT rules if goods enter the UK supply chain.

  • Customs Export Declaration (UK CDS/SAD or Gibraltar export declaration showing Movement Reference Number)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing physical removal from the UK/Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/SWIFT payment evidence showing final settlement by the non-resident buyer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, freight contract, and insurance certificate
  • Retained evidence log for six years: export declaration, MRN, carrier manifest, and proof of destination-country import

Gibraltar imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction control, no government-mandated e-invoice API, and no digital stamping of invoices. UK VAT-registered businesses must maintain digital records and submit VAT returns through HMRC-recognized software under Making Tax Digital for VAT using approved APIs; this does not require full e-invoicing clearance or fiscal stamping.

HMRC may retrospectively revoke zero-rating and issue assessments for unpaid VAT, interest, and penalties where export evidence is missing, incomplete, or false. For deliberate fraud, HMRC can apply extended assessment periods, impose penalties up to 100% of the tax, issue personal liability notices on directors and responsible officers, disqualify directors, and pursue criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion of VAT or customs duty. Gibraltar Customs may impose forfeiture, civil penalties, or criminal sanctions for false export declarations. Export proceeds not repatriated within the 180-day deadline or mismatched with supporting FX evidence will heighten audit scrutiny and may trigger deemed-local-supply clawbacks, economic-substance reviews, and reclassification of the Cayman Exempted Company as taxable in Gibraltar or the UK.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at 1:1 to GBP and is freely convertible. Gibraltar maintains an open, liberalised capital account with no exchange-control restrictions, no profit-remittance licensing, and no prior central-bank approval for current-account trade settlements, dividends, interest, or liquidation distributions. Practical convertibility is assured for GIP, GBP, EUR, USD, and other deliverable currencies through GFSC-licensed banks; sanctions and OFSI screening remain the only meaningful constraint.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirements or mandatory conversion ratios in Gibraltar. Gibraltar-resident entities and offshore exempted companies may retain export receipts in the currency of denomination. Banking practice requires underlying commercial documentation, including invoices, contracts, bills of lading, and logistics proofs, for the credit of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for GBP and GIP currency peg) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the domestic conduct, prudential, and AML/CFT supervisory authority for Gibraltar-licensed banks. Exchange-control functions over Gibraltar are exercised through HM Treasury's reserved monetary and sanctions powers; there is no separate Gibraltar central bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-High with legal constraints. UK FCA-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) and Gibraltar-licensed DLT and payment providers can onboard the Cayman Exempted Company remotely and offer multi-currency omnibus accounts, particularly for merchant settlement, FX conversion, and low-value supplier payments. However, EMIs are not full commercial banks: they do not provide credit, are not within the Gibraltar deposit compensation scheme, and may de-risk cross-border e-commerce flows involving volumetric customer refunds, BNPL schemes, or freight-heavy transactions. Where an EMI is used, it should be a Tier-1 UK-regulated principal with segregated e-money funds and a clear sub-account structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing utilises SWIFT MT103 with narrative 'Inward equity subscription / shareholder loan – Cayman Exempted Co' and the UK/Gibraltar purpose code for intercompany financing or direct investment. The originating bank should receive a board-approved capitalisation memorandum, a corporate registry extract of the Cayman entity, FATCA/CRS attestation, and beneficial-ownership evidence. Settlement into the Gibraltar operational account is cleared through the UK Faster Payments/CHAPS network for GBP, TARGET2 for EUR, or SWIFT correspondent banking for USD. For amounts above typical high-value thresholds, pre-verification and sanctioned-sector screening are applied.Trade settlements are executed primarily by SWIFT MT103 single or batch wire transfers for direct supplier payments; commercial letters of credit (MT700) are used for high-value, large-batch furniture purchase orders where transport documents and inspection certificates must be exchanged. Low-value refunds and BNPL payouts to UK consumers are cleared via Faster Payments and card rails (Mastercard/Visa) through acquiring partners. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar Transfer Pricing Rules (effective 1 January 2020, aligned with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines): Master File and Local File are required where the multinational group has consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million (approximately GIP 750 million). A Local File and contemporaneous benchmarking documentation are strongly advised for any related-party transaction exceeding GIP 100,000 per annum; failure to maintain or produce the file attracts documentation penalties.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar ESR (Economic Substance Regulations) 2018/2019 apply. The online retail of oversized furniture and home decor constitutes a 'Distribution and Service Centre' relevant activity. The entity must (i) be directed and managed in Gibraltar, (ii) maintain adequate employees, premises and operating expenditure in Gibraltar, (iii) perform core income-generating activities (order acceptance, logistics and warehouse contracting, customer service, inventory risk management) in Gibraltar, and (iv) file an annual ESR return with the Commissioner by 30 September. A Cayman Exempted Company operating through a Gibraltar PE is equally caught. Non-compliance triggers financial penalties of up to GIP 100,000, possible strike-off from the Companies Register, and loss of UK-Gibraltar DTA benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2010 ss.155-160: tax-geared penalty of up to 100% of the underpaid tax, interest at 8% per annum compounded, unlimited criminal fine upon conviction, and seizure of assets.

Director personal liability: Fraudulent evasion carries imprisonment of up to 5 years; directors face civil restitution of diverted profits, freezing orders and travel bans under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR penalty of up to GIP 100,000 per failure, daily default penalties, strike-off from the Gibraltar Companies Register, denial of UK-Gibraltar DTA benefits, and prohibition on opening or maintaining accounts with Gibraltar-licensed banks.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for wrongful trading under Companies Act 2014 s.136; disqualification as a director for up to 15 years; personal assessment for unreported tax under the Income Tax Act.
