Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

If the Cayman Exempted Company is managed and controlled from Gibraltar, it must obtain a Gibraltar Tax Assessment Number (TAN): (1) appoint a Gibraltar-authorised tax agent; (2) file the ITO Corporate Registration Form attaching the Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, Memorandum & Articles, register of directors, UBO declarations, evidence of the place of board meetings and, if available, first-year accounts; (3) submit through the ITO e-portal; (4) receive the TAN in approximately 5-10 working days; (5) thereafter file annual Self-Assessment returns and settle any gross-return tax at 12.5%. If the company has no Gibraltar central management and control, registration is optional and the company may instead obtain a Certificate of Non-Residence for bank/EMI onboarding.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: the Cayman Exempted Company is liable to no Cayman income, corporate, capital gains, or withholding taxes. In Gibraltar, entity-level taxation at 12.5% on gross return applies only if central management and control is exercised there; if non-resident, worldwide e-commerce income falls outside Gibraltar taxation. UK taxation arises solely upon creation of a UK permanent establishment or UK VAT-registrable presence.

Available election forms

Cayman: the company may elect to obtain a Tax Exemption Certificate under section 6(2) of the Cayman Companies Act granting a 20-year (renewable) no-tax guarantee. Gibraltar: no US-style check-the-box or fiscal-transparency election exists; classification follows factual central management and control. Structuring options include (i) holding board meetings and key decisions outside Gibraltar to preserve non-residence, (ii) applying for an ITO Certificate of Residence or Non-Residence, and (iii) invoking tie-breaker/MAP procedures under the applicable UK-Gibraltar double taxation arrangement and the UK-Cayman exchange-of-information framework. The former Gibraltar Qualifying Company e-commerce 10% regime was abolished effective 1 July 2021 with no new registrations. UK anti-hybrid rules (TIOPA 2010 Part 6A) are non-elective and require review.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

3.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: no GCT turnover registration threshold; GCT is border-collected on imports at rates of 0% (basic foodstuffs, books), 3.5% (standard goods such as apparel and housewares) and 10% (luxury goods, electronics, jewellery), plus higher specific duties on alcohol, tobacco and fuel. Goods shipped directly from suppliers to non-Gibraltar customers are outside the GCT net. UK VAT: GBP 90,000 annual taxable turnover threshold (2024/25); EU distance-selling and OSS/IOSS thresholds are unavailable to a non-EU/non-UK company absent a UK or EU establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Gibraltar because Gibraltar has no VAT. UK 0% export VAT may be claimed only if the Cayman company is UK VAT-registered (through a UK establishment) and exports goods from the UK outside the UK: the goods must be exported within three months of the time of supply, the company must retain HMRC-approved export evidence, and the supply must be a direct export. Offshore digital/e-services supplied by a non-UK company to EU consumers are outside UK zero-rating and do not qualify for OSS/IOSS unless the company is UK/EU established.

Gibraltar: no real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, digital stamping, or government API mandate; commercial invoices suffice. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains a fully open capital and current account regime with no exchange control legislation, no restrictions on dividend, interest or royalty remittance, and no statutory withholding taxes for non-resident recipients. The GIP is freely convertible at par to GBP, while USD and EUR are commonly accepted as clearing currencies by Gibraltar banks and payment processors. For a Cayman Exempted Company operating an online DTC brand, profit repatriation to Cayman or ultimate UK shareholders can be executed without regulatory approval, subject only to bank-level verification of the legality of the distribution and clearance of the payer's AML/KYC file.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export or e-commerce receipts under Gibraltar law. Receipts generated via Shopify Payments, PayPal and Stripe may be retained in the original currency, swept into a GIP/GBP base account, or converted through approved corporate FX lines without any obligation to surrender foreign currency to the authorities; a prudent treasury policy retains 70% GBP, 20% USD and 10% EUR operating balances to match supplier and settlement liabilities.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the prudential and conduct regulator of all licensed banks, e-money institutions and payment service providers in Gibraltar; the Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) through the Currency Notes Ordinance, fully backed by Bank of England sterling reserves held under a statutory 1:1 peg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Moderate overall feasibility. The GFSC licenses a small but credible set of e-money and payment institutions in Gibraltar, and UK/EU EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud and Payoneer will generally accept Gibraltar-incorporated corporate clients; however, most mainstream EMIs decline a pure Cayman Exempted Company as account holder absent a Gibraltar nexus, substantive local activity, and a bank reference from a licensed bank. Recommended structuring: hold the merchant settlement accounts in the name of the Gibraltar operating branch or a Gibraltar-resident agent entity, with the Cayman Exempted Company as the licensed IP owner and intercompany treasury counter-party, thereby preserving tax efficiencies while satisfying EMI and correspondent-bank due diligence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route the Cayman parent's capital injection via SWIFT MT103/MT203, or via GBP Faster Payments exploiting the 1:1 GIP/GBP peg through a UK correspondent bank, directly into the Gibraltar operating account. Use ISO 20022 purpose code 'PURP:TRAD' for working capital transfers and reference 'Share Capital - [Entity Name]' for equity injections, attaching the Cayman board resolution and share subscription documents to the paying bank before release to prevent name-mismatch holds; for intercompany loans, execute a formal loan agreement, use purpose code 'PURP:INTC', and document an arm's-length interest-rate schedule. Because Gibraltar has no exchange controls, no prior central-bank approval is required, but the receiving Gibraltar bank will evaluate the ultimate origin of funds and requires the sending Cayman account to bear the exact legal entity name matching the account title; consider a pre-notification cover letter to the bank's compliance desk.Employ SWIFT MT103 with GPI tracking for direct supplier and logistics payments, and Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) confirmed through a Gibraltar bank for large-value or first-time high-risk inventory orders with 90- to 180-day tenors. For rapid brand refunds, affiliate payouts and Shopify Balance operations, use Stripe Connect and Shopify Payments native payout rails in USD and GBP, while urgent GBP obligations clear via CHAPS or Faster Payments through a UK correspondent. Implement a centralized treasury FX layer using Wise Business or Currencycloud balance-clearing with pool conversion, rather than permitting each processor to convert independently, to reduce aggregate FX spread leakage; maintain multi-currency concentration accounts at Gibraltar International Bank or Barclays to enable SEPA EUR settlement at domestic cost and to recycle USD receivables into supplier payments without double conversion.Outright FX forwards (GBP/USD, GBP/EUR, EUR/USD) with tenors up to 12 months under the GFSC-licensed bank's ISDA 2002 or master forward confirmation, used strictly to hedge projected Shopify and PayPal settlement flows.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on USD and EUR where settlement against GIP is standardized; available through the Gibraltar bank's international broker desk or an authorized EMI, permitted only where counterparty credit limits are pre-approved by the board.FX swaps and cross-currency swaps for matched USD-receivable and EUR-payable inventory cycles, executed under ISDA/CSA documentation with a GFSC-licensed banking counter-party.Plain-vanilla European FX options for one-off brand-launch marketing budgets in new geographies, approved as treasury instruments under a board-adopted derivative-risk policy; speculative uncovered option writing is contractually prohibited in the compliance manual.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory transfer pricing documentation is triggered where aggregate controlled transactions exceed GIP 1,000,000 per annum or where the Gibraltarian entity's turnover exceeds GIP 10,000,000; group entities above these thresholds must maintain a Master File, Local File, and Country-by-Country report where required by OECD-aligned rules.

Economic substance scope: Cayman Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to the Cayman Exempted Company because it is incorporated in the Cayman Islands. If the company is tax resident in Gibraltar, it may be excluded from Cayman ESR only by evidencing a valid Gibraltar tax residence certificate and the corresponding tax treatment. In Gibraltar, the economic substance doctrine requires that the management and control, core income-generating activities (CIGA), employees, premises, and board meetings are proportionally located in or performed from Gibraltar. For a Shopify DTC brand, the distribution and service-centre activity must be demonstrated by actual staff, inventory control, logistics decisions, and customer-support functions in Gibraltar, or the entity risks reclassification as a shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Non-Declaration of Gibraltarian PE

Statutory sanction: A penalty of up to 100% of the tax undercharged, daily default penalties, interest on overdue tax, and criminal fines not exceeding GIP 250,000. In serious cases, prosecution may result in imprisonment for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for civil restitution of unpaid tax, subjected to travel bans and asset freezes, and imprisoned for deliberate fraud; piercing the corporate veil is available to the tax authority where the company is a mere façade.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Cayman law, a fixed penalty of CI$10,000 plus CI$10,000 per month for continued default and possible strike-off. Under Gibraltar ESR, financial penalties of up to EUR 10,000 per offence plus daily penalties and a reassessment to tax at 12.5% on an estimated basis.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for substance declaration failures; enforcement measures include disqualification orders, freezing orders, and personal penalties. Directors may also be barred from acting as company directors in any reporting jurisdiction.
Anti-Money Laundering / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: In Gibraltar, failure to file beneficial ownership information attracts a fine of up to £5,000 and/or imprisonment for up to two years, with the Registrar able to suspend or strike off the company. In Cayman, failure to file or update the beneficial ownership register may result in fines up to CI$25,000 per offence.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers face personal criminal exposure for AML breaches, including asset seizure and up to five years' imprisonment. The corporate veil is pierced to impose joint liability on any individual controlling or receiving the illicit proceeds.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Inadequate Documentation

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar tax authorities may adjust the taxable profits, assess additional tax, apply interest, and impose penalties up to 100% of the additional tax. Deliberate TP documentation gaps may trigger criminal fines and public disclosure orders.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false TP certificates can be jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax underpayment, subject to civil recovery, travel restrictions, and disqualification; in aggravated instances, imprisonment up to seven years.
Failure to File Annual Returns / Registry Striking Off

Statutory sanction: The Gibraltar Commissioner may make a best-of-judgment assessment and aggressively enforce collection, with late penalties up to £1,000 and daily surcharges. The Cayman Registrar may strike the company off after compounded non-compliance, causing all rights and assets to be forfeited to the Crown.

Director personal liability: Once struck off, the directors become personally liable for the company's debts incurred after the strike-off and may be disqualified. Civil restitution orders may authorise additional asset recovery actions in both Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands.
