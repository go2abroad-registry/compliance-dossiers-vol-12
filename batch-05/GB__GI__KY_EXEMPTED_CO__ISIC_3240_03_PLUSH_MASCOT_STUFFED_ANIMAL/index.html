Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Gibraltar Tax Registry; https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/tax)

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company that is not centrally managed and controlled in Gibraltar and has no permanent establishment is not ordinarily required to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number. If the entity establishes a branch, acquires Gibraltar situs land, or becomes tax resident, it must first register with the Gibraltar Companies House as a foreign company, appoint a Gibraltar resident agent and registered office, file Form 100 (Company Return) together with certified constitutional documents and a Cayman Certificate of Good Standing, and thereafter receive a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number. The application is processed by the Income Tax Office within approximately 10 working days of submission; no minimum capital requirement applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under Gibraltar domestic law. A Cayman Exempted Company relies on its inherent Cayman exemption undertaking and does not benefit from any Gibraltar elective classification. Anti-hybrid rules derived from OECD BEPS Action 2 are mandatory under the Gibraltar Finance Act and cannot be displaced by taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST (Gibraltar applies only customs and excise duties)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – Gibraltar does not impose VAT or GST and has no indirect tax registration threshold. Import duty obligations arise on each consignment entering Gibraltar, and there is no economic turnover threshold triggering registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – Gibraltar does not levy VAT or GST, therefore no 0% export VAT rate and no VAT refund mechanism exist. Exports of plush mascots and stuffed animals (ISIC 3240; HS Chapter 95) from Gibraltar are outside the scope of any local indirect tax, and goods definitively exported under the Customs Ordinance are relieved from import or excise duty upon production of proof of export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/C88)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing repatriation of export proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, API-based fiscal data submission, or digital stamping system is mandated for business-to-business sales in Gibraltar. Customs declarations are submitted electronically via the Gibraltar Customs and Border Control system using the SAD/C88 format, but no continuous VAT-related digital tax clearance is applied.

No VAT clawback is possible because no VAT regime exists. Fraudulent export declarations or misuse of the outward export procedure for duty relief attract seizure of goods, collection of under-declared duties, a penalty of up to 300% of the duty evaded, and potential criminal prosecution in the Supreme Court of Gibraltar. If export profits are deliberately misreported in the corporation tax return, the Income Tax Office may impose a gross negligence penalty of up to 100% of the tax under-declared plus interest accruing at 12% per annum.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both the current account and capital account are fully liberalised in Gibraltar; no exchange control approvals, surrender requirements, or prior central bank permits exist for convertibility of GIP/GBP or for remittance of profits. The Cayman Exempted Company beneficiary must nevertheless evidence economic substance under Cayman Islands' International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act and must provide a clear shareholder dividend trail to satisfy Gibraltar banks' provenance-of-funds and tax-transparency rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rules or mandatory conversion ratios apply in Gibraltar. A plush toy OEM exporter may retain foreign currency receivables in hard currency accounts, but all proceeds must be matched to commercial invoices, customs declarations (SAD in the UK context, or equivalent), and bank settlement records under GFSC AML obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for the Sterling Zone) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) acting as the prudential, conduct, and AML/CFT supervisor; His Majesty's Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at a fixed 1:1 peg to GBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-Medium feasibility. UK-authorised (FCA) e-money institutions and EU EMIs may be technically accessible, but the Cayman tax-exempted status, non-EEA nature, and lack of a real economic presence in Gibraltar materially increase underwriting friction. Platforms such as Wise, Currencycloud, and Multitude are not licensed as Gibraltar banks and generally cannot provide account numbers capable of receiving GIP local clearing; offshore EMIs may be used solely as settlement conduits, not as primary treasury accounts.

Box 10: Corporation Tax Payable State the final Gibraltar tax liability after all credits and payments on account. Any withholding tax suffered on Gibraltar-source payments to non-residents must be reconciled here; non-residents with no actual liability should issue a nil return only if requested by ITO. Late filing triggers: initial penalty of GBP 100; if more than three months late, a further GBP 100 fixed penalty; continuing daily surcharge of GBP 30 per day up to a maximum of GBP 1,800; automatic interest at 2% above HMRC base rate on unpaid tax; and the court may impose additional penalties up to twice the amount of tax avoidable. Directors may receive separate fines for non-compliance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared and filed as part of the corporate income tax return when the Gibraltar company's aggregate annual related-party cross-border transactions exceed GBP 1,000,000; Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated annual group revenue exceeding GBP 10,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to companies carrying on relevant activities (distribution and service centres, intellectual property holding, financing, insurance, headquarters, and shipping). Plush-toy OEM manufacturing is treated as a core income-generating activity requiring adequate physical office premises, qualified employees, board meetings in Gibraltar, and substantive expenditure in the jurisdiction. Annual ESR notification and substance verification forms must be submitted through the GTS portal within 6 months of the year-end. Failure to satisfy ESR leads to the company being considered not tax resident in Gibraltar for treaty purposes, jeopardizing all tax reliefs, and exposes the entity to a penalty of up to GBP 100,000 and potential compulsory strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Deliberate Underreporting or Falsification)

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 100% of the evaded tax, plus interest at 2% above base rate; criminal conviction on indictment with a maximum fine of GBP 50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to seven years.

Director personal liability: Directors found to have directed or consented to evasion are jointly and severally liable for the restitution of evaded tax; they face travel bans, asset freezing orders, disqualification from acting as directors for up to 15 years, and imprisonment for fraud.
Artificial Shell Company Arrangement / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the structure as a sham; Gibraltar will deny tax residence status and treaty benefits, assessing all worldwide profits at 10% with a penalty of up to GBP 100,000 and automatic strike-off under the Companies Act.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for all underpaid tax and penalties if the corporate veil is pierced, and may be subject to civil proceedings for fraudulent trading, disqualification, and confiscation orders.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Inadequate TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: Tax authority re-adjusts profits to arm's length, increasing taxable income; penalty up to 10% of the adjusted transfer pricing adjustment and interest; additional penalty of GBP 5,000 for failure to maintain contemporaneous documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's length pricing are personally liable for the additional tax, and may be charged with negligently causing the company to breach its duty to self-assess; criminal liability may follow if fraudulent intent is shown.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Civil fines of up to GBP 50,000 per non-compliance event, daily penalties of GBP 500 for each day the beneficial ownership register remains unfiled, and suspension of the company's regulatory approvals; the company may be blacklisted by banking partners.

Director personal liability: Directors may be criminally prosecuted for money laundering facilitation under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act, carrying up to 14 years' imprisonment, unlimited fines, and personal asset confiscation; officers can also receive individual travel bans.
Repatriation Tax Fraud / Abuse of DTA Relief

Statutory sanction: Forfeiture of all treaty benefits, recovery of foreign withholding tax plus interest and penalties in the source jurisdiction; a Gibraltar penalty of up to GBP 100,000 is imposed for providing false residence statements.

Director personal liability: Directors may become personally liable for restitution of the foreign tax shortfall and may face criminal fraud charges in the source jurisdiction, including extradition risk, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 10 years.
