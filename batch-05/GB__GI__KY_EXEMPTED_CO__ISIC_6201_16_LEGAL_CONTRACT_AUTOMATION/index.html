Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. A Cayman exempted company is typically formed with an authorised share capital of US$50,000 but no minimum paid-up capital is required at incorporation; a single issued subscriber share of US$1 (or one no-par-value share) is standard. No escrow account or capital contribution is required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the Gibraltar Fire and Rescue Service with annual inspection of the registered office; building occupancy and structural clearance from the Gibraltar Building Control Office; compliance with Gibraltar health and safety at work legislation, including Display Screen Equipment assessments, first aid, emergency lighting, electrical fixed-wire testing on a five-year cycle, and a documented civil-defence evacuation plan.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (ITO); e-portal: Gibraltar Tax e-filing (GMTax); not HMRC because Gibraltar is a separate tax jurisdiction from the UK for domestic taxation purposes.

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company is not automatically tax-resident in Gibraltar. If the company carries on a trade through a permanent establishment in Gibraltar, the Gibraltar branch or agent must file Form GI-IT2 'Application for Registration of a Non-Resident Company' with the Income Tax Office, accompanied by a certified certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered office and agent details, and proof of Cayman tax-exempt status. Upon approval, the ITO issues a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (GTIN). If the company has no Gibraltar permanent establishment and derives no Gibraltar-source income, statutory registration is not compulsory; however, a voluntary registration may be sought for customs, banking, or counterparty verification purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone – non-resident Cayman Exempted Company outside the Gibraltar tax net unless a Gibraltar permanent establishment arises; Gibraltar-source income of a non-resident is generally not charged to income tax except where profits arise from a local permanent establishment or from specified property and investment income under the Income Tax Act. — A Cayman Exempted Company is not automatically tax-resident in Gibraltar. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone – non-resident Cayman Exempted Company outside the Gibraltar tax net unless a Gibraltar permanent establishment arises; Gibraltar-source income of a non-resident is generally not charged to income tax except where profits arise from a local permanent establishment or from specified property and investment income under the Income Tax Act.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box system. Elective mechanisms under the Income Tax Act include: (1) binding ruling request on permanent establishment treatment; (2) group relief joint election under s.39 ITA for Gibraltar group companies; (3) election to apply substance requirements equivalent to 'qualifying company' status for OECD and EU tax good-governance compliance; and (4) elections available under the anti-hybrid and controlled-foreign-company rules for non-resident Cayman entities, including de minimis profitability and excluded-territory elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST – Gibraltar applies import, excise, and consumer protection duties under the Customs Duties Ordinance; exports are duty-free.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

None – Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST and therefore has no registration threshold for services. Commercial importers of dutiable goods must register with Her Majesty's Customs (Gibraltar) as importers and obtain a customs registration number; no economic turnover threshold applies to imports of services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – Gibraltar does not operate export zero-rating because there is no VAT/GST. Goods physically exported from Gibraltar are carved out from the charge to Gibraltar import duty, and services supplied by a non-resident Cayman company to non-Gibraltar recipients are outside the scope of Gibraltar income tax absent a permanent establishment.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Evidence of physical departure from Gibraltar endorsed by Customs

No real-time VAT e-invoicing, fiscal stamping, or API-based continuous transaction control mandate exists in Gibraltar. Customs clearance is conducted electronically via the Gibraltar Customs Single Administrative Document (SAD) and the tariff/pre-arrival declaration interface; there is no digital clearance certificate for intra-jurisdictional service invoices.

Penalties under the Customs Duties Ordinance include administrative fines, seizure and forfeiture of goods, retrospective liability for unpaid duty, and criminal sanctions for fraudulent export declarations or false inward remittance statements. The Income Tax Office may disregard sham or artificial arrangements, reclassify the Cayman Exempted Company as resident by reference to central management and control, and impose back taxes, interest, and penalties of up to treble the tax underassessed in cases of deliberate misrepresentation.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The UK's residual Exchange Control Act restrictions were repealed in 1979 and have not been reinstated; Gibraltar imposes no exchange-control approvals, no prior permits for dividends or capital redemptions, and no mandatory conversion quotas. Profit remittance from Gibraltar to the Cayman Exempted Company is free of withholding and exchange-control clearance, subject only to CRS/DAC6 disclosure obligations, the Gibraltar Genuine Commercial Activity (GCA) substance attestation, and the bank's source-of-funds declaration. Capital inflows from Cayman may be injected, retained in any currency, or redeployed without central-bank consent; the receiving bank completes statistical and AML reporting only. The Gibraltar pound is pegged 1:1 to GBP; commercial accounts settle GIP at par and route foreign-currency positions through London correspondents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio applies to UK or Gibraltar export receipts. SaaS subscription revenues earned by the Exempted Company through the UK/Gibraltar treasury centre may be retained indefinitely in GBP, EUR, or USD and disbursed to any counterparty without central-bank permission. Banks code cross-border receipts for the Bank of England International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) statistics and for GFSC AML reporting, but those obligations do not compel conversion. The GIP/GBP 1:1 parity eliminates local-currency conversion risk.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (UK monetary authority and GBP policy anchor); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) (banking, AML/CFT and FX-market supervision); Government of Gibraltar (Gibraltar pound note issuance under the Currency Notes Act with 100% GBP backing). No separate exchange-control authority operates in Gibraltar because the jurisdiction sits in a de facto currency union with the United Kingdom.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High for the UK/Gibraltar treasury layer: FCA-authorised EMIs such as ClearBank, Modulr, and Currency Cloud can open accounts for the Gibraltar-incorporated operating company, provide virtual IBANs, and issue corporate card programmes; the UK-Gibraltar Financial Services Agreement preserves passporting between the two jurisdictions, while EU-currency rails require an EEA hub because EU passporting ended with Brexit. Low for the Cayman Exempted Company itself: most EMIs reject Cayman-incorporated entities as outside their supported-country matrices. Stripe and direct card acquiring require a separate UK-incorporated merchant of record because neither Stripe nor core acquirers natively support Gibraltar or Cayman entities; the Gibraltar treasury then sweeps settlement funds under an intercompany agreement.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% flat corporate income tax rate on assessable profits (Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, as amended; rate effective for Years of Assessment beginning on or after 1 July 2019)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar-resident company or branch to non-resident shareholders, irrespective of the shareholder's country of residence.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return and final self-assessment payment are due on or before 30 November immediately following the end of the Year of Assessment (which runs 1 July to 30 June). A company with a 31 December accounting year-end is assessed in the Year of Assessment ending the following 30 June; the statutory deadline is therefore 30 November of the subsequent calendar year, i.e. up to 11 months after the accounting period end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GBP 1,000,000 aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions (best-practice benchmark). Gibraltar statute imports the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and the arm's length standard without a published monetary safe-harbour threshold; contemporaneous Local File documentation should be maintained where the Gibraltar entity's annual related-party inflows or outflows exceed GBP 1,000,000, or where Gibraltar gross turnover exceeds GBP 10,000,000; a Master File should be prepared where the group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000. The LegalTech SaaS pricing model (subscription, per-seat, e-signature transaction fees) should be corroborated by a benchmarking study using TNMM/CUP.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Gibraltar's Economic Substance Regulations apply to any entity tax resident in Gibraltar that carries on a 'relevant activity'; a cloud document automation and e-signature business engaged in intra-group contract administration or management services is typically categorised as a 'distribution and service centre' or 'headquarters'. The company must: (a) file an annual ESR return; (b) demonstrate premises, employees, and core income-generating activities in Gibraltar proportionate to its income; (c) maintain board minutes evidencing direction in Gibraltar. The Cayman Exempted Co parent is itself simultaneously subject to Cayman's Economic Substance Act (ES Act, 2018) for its Cayman legal person, creating a dual ESR exposure that requires careful allocation of substance between Cayman and Gibraltar. Failure is punishable by financial penalties, exchange of information with HMRC and the Cayman Tax Information Authority, and strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False or Inaccurate Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax undercharged plus interest at the statutory rate from the due date; criminal conviction for knowingly making a false declaration or delivering an incorrect return: unlimited fine and imprisonment for up to 7 years under the Income Tax Act 2010; the Commissioner may also raise a best-of-judgment assessment and impose daily penalties for failure to produce records.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors may be pursued personally for the unpaid tax and penalties where they were knowingly concerned in the fraud, or where the company has been stripped of assets; the Gibraltar Court may issue travel bans, asset-freezing orders, disqualification orders of up to 15 years under the Companies (Disqualification of Directors) legislation, and confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act.
Shell Company / Failed Economic Substance (Dual ESR Risk)

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar ESR penalties: up to £10,000 per report for a first failure; up to £200,000 for serious, repeated and wilful non-compliance; exchange of information with HMRC and the Cayman Tax Information Authority; the entity may be re-characterised as Gibraltar tax resident and assessed at 12.5% on its worldwide profits with interest and penalties; in extreme cases, strike-off from the Companies Register.

Director personal liability: Directors who consented to or negligently permitted the substance failure may be personally ordered by the Commissioner to pay penalties and costs; they remain exposed to disqualification and to immigration/travel restrictions; a travel ban may be imposed pending full compliance; the financial institution may also freeze the corporate accounts, causing personal liability for wrongful trading if the entity continues transacting while insolvent.
AML and Failure to Prevent Facilitation of Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: AML penalties: unlimited fines and imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering offences; under the Criminal Finances Act 2017, a corporate body (including a Gibraltar-incorporated or branch entity acting for a UK-relevant body) is liable to an unlimited financial penalty where an associated person criminally facilitates tax evasion and the company did not have reasonable prevention procedures in place; the regulator may also revoke the firm's unauthorised status or block payment-rail access with Stripe and card schemes.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and the appointed Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) can be prosecuted personally if they knew of or were blind to the suspicious activity; penalties include imprisonment, confiscation orders, director disqualification, travel bans, and personal restitution orders for funds that passed through the company bank accounts.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Deficient Intercompany Documentation

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner of Income Tax may adjust the taxable profit to the arm's length price, disallow the excessive deduction, impose a civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax displaced, and charge statutory interest at 8% per annum; where the adjustment creates a constructive dividend or loan, secondary adjustments and any related GAAR anti-avoidance assessments may follow; the taxpayer bears the burden of proving commercial rationality.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or caused the filing of transfer pricing documentation known to be false, or who approved intercompany rates without benchmarking, may be held jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax as persons 'knowingly concerned' in the evasion; professional disqualification, personal fines, and, in cross-border cases, referral to HMRC and Cayman authorities for coordinated civil recovery actions are possible.
