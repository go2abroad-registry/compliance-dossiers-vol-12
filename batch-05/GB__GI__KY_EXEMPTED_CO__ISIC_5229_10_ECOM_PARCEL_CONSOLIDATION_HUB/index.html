Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (ITO), 177 Queen's Road, Gibraltar; online portal: https://etax.gibraltar.gov.gi. UK HMRC applies concurrently for any UK VAT or customs registration: https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/hm-revenue-customs.

E-government filing portal

The Cayman Exempted Company is not automatically registered in Gibraltar. The SOP is: (1) assess whether the entity has Gibraltar management and control, a permanent establishment, or Gibraltar-source income; if not, no Gibraltar income-tax file is needed solely for holding activities. (2) If registration is required, file the initial corporate income-tax registration with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office using the statutory form and submit certified copies of the Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, memorandum and articles, register of directors/members, and documentary evidence of the registered office. (3) The ITO will assign a Gibraltar Tax File Number/TIN; no Gibraltar VAT ID exists because Gibraltar has no VAT. (4) If the entity establishes a place of business, register the overseas company with Gibraltar Companies House within the prescribed period after commencement. (5) For parcel-linehaul customs operations, obtain the relevant Gibraltar importer/exporter customs identifier and, if UK-origin goods are streamed through a UK entity, an HMRC EORI number and UK VAT registration where required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Cayman exempted-company status; for Gibraltar direct-tax purposes the entity is classified as a non-resident body corporate unless management and control are exercised in Gibraltar, in which case it may become resident and subject to corporation tax on territorial-source profits).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or elective pass-through election is available in Gibraltar or the UK for this structure. The relevant statutory mechanisms are: (a) the Cayman Islands Tax Concessions Law s.6 exemption election/undertaking, which gives the exempted company a government-guaranteed tax exemption in the Cayman Islands for 20 years; (b) if a UK taxable presence exists, a UK VAT group election under Value Added Tax Act 1994 s.43 where conditions are met; and (c) alignment with Gibraltar's anti-hybrid mismatch rules, which may override inconsistent entity or payment characterisations to prevent double non-taxation, double deductions, or hybrid transfer mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax in Gibraltar; the relevant indirect regime for the hub is Gibraltar customs and import duties plus, where the Cayman entity or a UK affiliate makes UK taxable supplies, the UK VAT regime under the Value Added Tax Act 1994.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP: nil (no Gibraltar VAT/GST registration threshold). In the UK parallel regime, the VAT registration threshold is £90,000 taxable turnover for a UK-established business; a non-established business registering purely in the UK has no de minimis turnover threshold and must consider registration as soon as it makes taxable or import/acquisition supplies relevant to the parcel hub.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar, not having VAT, has no domestic export zero-rating or VAT-refund mechanism for the Cayman entity. UK zero-rating may be claimed on the UK side only if the supply is made by the UK VAT-registered taxable person, goods are physically exported from the UK to a destination outside the UK, the customer is outside the UK, and the prescribed export evidence is obtained within three months and retained. Offshore B2B freight and logistics services supplied to counterparties outside the UK are normally outside the scope of UK VAT under place-of-supply rules rather than 'zero-rated'; if invoiced from Gibraltar, they do not entitle the Gibraltar entity to UK input VAT refunds.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic CDS/ASYCUDA-style entry with export MRN or local declaration reference)
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, Airway Bill, or multimodal transport document issued in the hub's name or as direct consignee/exporter
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank credit advice confirming foreign-currency payment or consideration received for the exported parcel linehaul services
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Parcel item-level consolidation manifest substantiating sorting and direct-injection linehaul volumes
  • Proof of physical removal and delivery (trailer manifest, terminal receipts, courier proof of delivery, electronic geofencing audit trail)

Gibraltar and the UK have not introduced mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital tax stamping, or government-authorised continuous transaction controls for B2B e-invoicing. Parcel consolidation activities require electronic customs declarations: HMRC Customs Declaration Service (CDS) for UK export and import clearances, and electronic customs processing/ASYCUDA-type procedures for Gibraltar border formalities. The hub must maintain item-level parcel manifests, EORI/importer references, and PDF/e-invoice records for inspection; no API clearance stamping or digital stamping is required for VAT, but customs entries must be submitted electronically before goods are released.

A zero-rating claim is subject to mandatory audit evidence inspection. If HMRC or Gibraltar Customs establishes that goods were not exported, were diverted, or that documentary proof is false, the claimed VAT/export relief may be clawed back with statutory interest. UK penalties range from 30% to 100% depending on the category of inaccuracy; deliberate concealment or cross-border VAT evasion attracts criminal investigation, unlimited fines, and imprisonment of up to 7 years, plus loss of customs status and seizure of goods or conveyances. Gibraltar customs offences carry independent penalties and forfeiture, and participation in a false export or inward-remittance scheme may be prosecuted as cheating the public revenue.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The regime provides full capital and current account convertibility for GBP, GIP, EUR, and USD; there are no GFSC or HM Treasury conversion approvals required for trade settlement, capital injections, or profit repatriation. Gibraltar imposes no dividend withholding tax on profit remittance, and the Cayman Exempted Company is ordinarily granted a Tax Exemption Undertaking under the Cayman Tax Concessions Law (typically 20 years, with extension available) exempting it from all local income, capital, and withholding taxes, which permits unrestricted distribution of profits to members. The only practical remittance hurdles are documentary rather than statutory: counterparty banks require evidence of economic substance, full UBO disclosure, and CIMA or GFSC good standing certificates to avoid enhanced-scrutiny delays.

Export proceeds surrender rules

NONE: there is no statutory export-proceeds surrender, no mandatory conversion ratio, no minimum settlement window, and no requirement to route export receipts through a designated domestic account in the UK, Gibraltar, or the Cayman Islands. GIP is pegged at par to GBP, but e-commerce and parcel-consolidation receivables may be retained in the original invoicing currency and freely redeployed for linehaul and supplier settlement. Documentary discipline requires each receipt to be mapped to an underlying sales or transport invoice and kept within the bank's approved transaction envelope to pre-empt Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) review.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar maintains no independent central bank. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory financial services supervisor for all licensed banks in Gibraltar; the Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) under a fixed 1:1 parity with the British pound, and the Bank of England provides the underlying currency anchor alongside HM Treasury and the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) for sanctions enforcement. For the Cayman Exempted Company, the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) is the registry and supervisory authority, although all banking deposits and treasury operations are hosted through UK and Gibraltar licensed banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility as a designated clearing rail (Airwallex and UK FCA-authorized EMIs) for small-parcel marketplace payouts, but operationally restricted: an EMI is not a bank, provides no FSCS deposit protection, and cannot host the group's primary capital base. The Cayman Exempted Company must source its liquidity from regulated banks while using virtual IBANs and local clearing rails (UK Faster Payments, SEPA Instant, US ACH/Fedwire) for sub-threshold disbursements. Post-Brexit, EU passporting for Gibraltar-licensed firms is discontinued; the UK-Gibraltar Treaty preserves UK market access, but onboarding a Cayman Exempted Company still requires full UBO disclosure, a documented commercial substance narrative, and board-level approval by the EMI's compliance committee.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar transfer pricing rules require mandatory Local File documentation when aggregate related-party cross-border transactions of the Gibraltar PE exceed GIP 5,000,000 in an accounting period. A Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds GIP 50,000,000. The Cayman Exempted Company has no Cayman transfer pricing filing obligation but must preserve group-level documentation supporting the arm's-length allocation of profit to the Gibraltar parcel consolidation PE.

Economic substance scope: Cayman Islands Economic Substance Regulations apply to the Cayman Exempted Company in respect of relevant activities, including distribution and service centre activities; the parcel consolidation hub must demonstrate management, employees, premises, and core income-generating assets in the Cayman Islands unless it is treated as a pure holding company. Gibraltar applies its own territorial economic substance doctrine: a Shell company with no operational substance is disregarded for tax purposes and the profits are re-attributed to the place of actual management and control outside Gibraltar. The hub must therefore maintain real physical presence, payroll, and risk-control functions in line with the substance actually claimed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act and criminal fraud statutes; fines up to GIP 30,000 per offence, tax-geared penalties up to 200% of the understated tax, seizure of business records, and imprisonment for up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a false return are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. The Supreme Court of Gibraltar may impose travel bans and asset freezing orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act; imprisonment applies to directors who personally direct the fraudulent filing.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Cayman Islands Registrar penalties of CI$10,000 per failure, escalating by CI$10,000 per month up to CI$100,000, with possible strike-off and deregistration. Gibraltar can assess undeclared PE profits at 10%, add administrative fines of up to GIP 10,000, and issue a closure notice against the company.

Director personal liability: Cayman courts may make compliance orders against directors for ESR failure; ongoing breach is punishable as contempt of court with fines and imprisonment. Gibraltar law treats directors as jointly liable for the PE's unpaid tax where the shell is struck off and the company cannot pay.
AML / Terrorist Financing and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: The Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 criminalises failure to maintain CDD/EWRA records; fines up to GIP 500,000 or three times the laundered value; the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit can block accounts and suspend the e-money licence; criminal conviction carries imprisonment from 5 to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, MLROs, and compliance officers are personally liable with no liability cap; the Court can impose travel bans pending investigation and restraint orders over personal bank accounts; prison sentences are imposed against individuals who knowingly allow the rail to be used for opacity.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar may make a transfer pricing adjustment to the PE's chargeable profits, impose additional tax at 10%, and apply a tax-geared penalty of up to 75% of the adjustment. Failure to produce statutory documentation carries fixed penalties of up to GIP 50,000. Default interest runs from the due date of the original assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly adopt intercompany pricing without substantiation are jointly liable for the additional tax. The Gibraltar Court can pierce the corporate veil and order personal restitution where the TP adjustment would otherwise be uncollectable.
Foreign Permanent Establishment / UK Nexus Avoidance

Statutory sanction: HMRC may apply UK CTA09 controlled-company and permanent-establishment rules to attribute profits to a UK PE, charge UK corporation tax at the prevailing rate with daily interest, and impose penalties of up to 100% of the tax for deliberate behaviour. European VAT authorities may assess place-of-supply VAT at 20% to 27% on the unsorted parcel services, with additional penalties for deliberate evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors acting in the UK for the parcel business may be treated as shadow directors and held personally liable for penalties under UK Finance Act 2009 Schedule 49. UK criminal courts can issue travel bans and confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002; imprisonment of up to 6 years applies for cheating the public revenue.
