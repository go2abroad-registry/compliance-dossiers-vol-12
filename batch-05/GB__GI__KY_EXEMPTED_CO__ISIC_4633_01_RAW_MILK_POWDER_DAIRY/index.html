Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
In Gibraltar, the Commissioner of Income Tax (Income Tax Office) under the Income Tax Act, administered via the Gibraltar Income Tax Office portal (https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi). For UK customs and tax interface, HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) applies to cross-border UK movements; however, the primary operating registration for this jurisdiction is Gibraltar's Income Tax Office. The Cayman Exempted Company remains outside the Cayman tax net by virtue of its exemption certificate.

        
E-government filing portal
A Cayman Exempted Company engaging in Gibraltar trade, having a permanent establishment, or being managed and controlled from Gibraltar must register with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office on Form GIT-1, providing the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, and proof of registered office. Obtain a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) upon registration. The company must also file an annual tax return and, where applicable, apply for a Gibraltar tax residence certificate. If the entity merely maintains a non-taxable presence, it must still register as a foreign company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry. No VAT or GST registration is required because Gibraltar imposes no value-added tax; separate registration with the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Department is needed for the import/export of controlled dairy goods.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Cayman Exempted Company is fiscally exempt in its incorporation territory under the Companies Act and Tax Information Authority Law. In Gibraltar, the entity is subject to territorial corporate income tax at 12.5% only on Gibraltar-source profits, unless it obtains a tax-exempt status or election. The default classification is therefore Tax_Exempt_Zone with a territorial carve-out for Gibraltar-sourced trading income, which is not pass-through but entity-level territorial taxation.

        
Available election forms
Gibraltar does not provide check-the-box or disregarded entity elections. The Cayman Exempted Company may elect to apply for a Gibraltar tax residence certificate or rely on the territoriality principle to limit taxation to Gibraltar-source profits. Hybrid mismatch election rules under Gibraltar's Income Tax Act (aligned with OECD BEPS Action 2) may apply to the extent the Cayman entity is treated as transparent in another jurisdiction. There is no available election to treat the entity as pass-through for Gibraltar tax purposes; the entity is taxed at corporate level when taxable presence exists.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax — Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and imposes no value-added tax, goods and services tax, or general sales tax. The only relevant indirect taxation nexus is customs and excise duty (import duty on specific goods) and certain local duties such as duty on alcoholic beverages, tobacco, fuel, and betting and gaming. For milk powder trading, no VAT or GST is chargeable; however, customs formalities apply to cross-border goods movements.

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Not applicable — Gibraltar has no VAT/GST registration threshold because no VAT/GST system exists. For customs duty purposes, any importer of controlled goods, including whole and skimmed milk powder, must register with the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Department; there is no minimum economic turnover threshold triggering customs registration, but a fiscal representation may be required for non-resident importers.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Not applicable — because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST, there is no export zero-rating mechanism or input VAT refund regime. For customs duty purposes, exports from Gibraltar are generally free of export duty. To benefit from any relevant duty suspension or relief (e.g., customs warehousing or outward processing), the exporter must demonstrate that the goods are destined for export outside Gibraltar and not for internal consumption. The standard criteria include physical departure from Gibraltar, existence of a genuine export sales contract, and the retention of customs-approved documentation.

Gibraltar does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping scheme for VAT/GST. Commercial invoices are not required to be fiscally stamped. For customs purposes, electronic declarations must be filed through the Gibraltar Customs Single Administrative Document (SAD) system, using the UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS) and Integrated Tariff, with digital submission via the Customs Handling of Import and Export Freight (CHIEF) legacy system or CDS as applicable. No API-based real-time tax clearance is required for ordinary dairy commodity exports.

      
Customs authorities in Gibraltar may conduct post-export verification and audit of export declarations. If the export is found to be fictitious or the goods are re-imported or diverted into local consumption without duty payment, deemed export duty and excise are clawed back, plus interest and penalties up to 200% of the value of the goods. In cases of intentional fraud for obtaining undeserved customs or tax relief, criminal prosecution may follow under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance, including fines and imprisonment. Because no VAT refund exists, abuse of the no-VAT regime is usually framed as customs fraud or money laundering in connection with financial proceeds.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Gibraltar Pound operates under a currency-board system pegged at parity (1:00 GIP = 1:00 GBP) and is fully convertible on both current and capital account. There are no exchange controls, no capital-account licensing gates, and no restrictions on profit remittance, dividend distribution, loan repayment, or liquidation proceeds for non-resident beneficial owners. The Cayman Exempted Company may lawfully hold multicurrency operating accounts (GBP, GIP, USD, EUR, CHF, CNH) at GFSC-licensed banks and clear obligations in any strong currency without central-bank approval. The reframed UK-Gibraltar relationship post-Brexit preserves the currency-board parity and unrestricted one-for-one substitution of GIP for GBP at all licensed banks.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
None. Gibraltar imposes no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no mandated holding period on export proceeds from dairy commodity trade. The Cayman Exempted Company may retain all USD, EUR, or GBP export receipts in its treasury accounts indefinitely, may net them against incoming letters of credit for supply finance, or may freely convert them through any GFSC-licensed counterparty. Sole compliance obligations concern the verification of counterparties against the UK Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI), EU consolidated, United Nations, and Gibraltar Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2021 lists, plus standard anti-money-laundering travel-rule record retention for five years.

      

      
Central bank supervision: HM Government of Gibraltar (Currency Notes Act 1934) is the exclusive issuer of the Gibraltar Pound (GIP); the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the licensing and prudential conduct regulator for all banking and e-money institutions; the Bank of England provides the final sterling reserve backing for the GIP currency-board arrangement and governs the Sterling settlement network on which GIP-clearing ultimately settles.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: GBP 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions, which is the threshold requiring a Local File under Gibraltar TP rules aligned with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines; a Master File is additionally mandatory for an MNE group with consolidated group revenue of EUR 50,000,000 or more, and Country-by-Country reporting applies where group revenue reaches EUR 750,000,000.

      
Economic substance scope: A Cayman Exempted Company conducting whole and skimmed milk powder trading as a 'distribution and service centre' business is subject to the Cayman Islands Economic Substance Act (as revised). It must satisfy the following tests: (i) core income-generating activities such as contract negotiation, inventory management, invoicing, and risk assumption occur in the Cayman Islands; (ii) management and control is exercised in Cayman (board meetings, strategic decisions); and (iii) the entity maintains adequate physical premises, full-time employees, and operating expenditure in Cayman. In addition, if the entity is managed and controlled in Gibraltar or maintains a Gibraltar PE, Gibraltar's economic substance requirements for geographically mobile activities apply, including demonstrable real management, employees, and operational presence proportionate to the dairy trading activity. Failure on either front triggers automatic exchange of information with the UK and significant penalties.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion (Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, as amended)
        
Statutory sanction: On summary conviction: a fine not exceeding GBP 5,000 and/or imprisonment up to 6 months; on indictment: an unlimited fine and/or imprisonment up to 7 years. Civil tax-geared penalties of up to 100% of the evaded tax, plus statutory interest on late payments.

        
Director personal liability: Directors knowingly concerned in the fraud face personal prosecution, personal civil restitution orders for the evaded tax and interest, director disqualification for up to 15 years, and travel bans or travel restrictions where the court finds a flight risk.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / False Economic Substance (Cayman ES Act and Gibraltar ESR)
        
Statutory sanction: Cayman Islands: initial penalty of USD 10,000 plus USD 10,000 for each additional month of non-compliance; automatic exchange of information with UK and Gibraltar; possible restoration proceedings and strike-off. Gibraltar: financial penalties, strike-off action, and retroactive denial of any non-resident tax protection.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and officers of the non-compliant entity are personally liable for fines of up to USD 10,000 per offence in Cayman; in Gibraltar, directors may have the corporate veil pierced, becoming jointly and severally liable for the entity's tax liabilities, and may be subject to travel bans and asset freezes pending investigation.

      

      
      

        AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering (Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, Gibraltar)
        
Statutory sanction: Money laundering carries a maximum penalty of 14 years' imprisonment and/or an unlimited fine; failure to file Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) triggers civil penalties from the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit; the proceeds of laundering are subject to seizure, confiscation, and restraint orders.

        
Director personal liability: Directors face personal criminal liability for consenting to or facilitating laundering transactions, personal confiscation orders for benefits received, director disqualification, and international travel bans. Senior management must evidence AML-KYC controls, independent audits of dairy trade financing flows, and ongoing due diligence on counterparties.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing / BEPS Abuse (Pillar Two and Arm's Length Principle)
        
Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment re-characterizing the profit at arm's length, additional CIT at 10%, statutory interest, and penalties of up to GBP 10,000 for late or incomplete Master File/Local File documentation; for in-scope MNE groups, a GloBE top-up tax of 15% on excess profits under Pillar Two in Gibraltar or the Cayman Islands.

        
Director personal liability: Where the arrangement is legally characterized as artificial and lacking commercial rationale, the corporate veil may be pierced; directors become jointly and severally liable for underpaid tax and penalties, may be subject to personal civil restitution and director disqualification, and may be placed on immigration watchlists or travel bans during the investigation.
