Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office (Ministry of Finance, HM Government of Gibraltar), 6-8 Hargreaves Chambers, 311 Main Street, Gibraltar; online filing portal: GO Tax (https://taxoffice.gibraltar.gov.gi); customs administration: Gibraltar Customs and Border Agency (GCBA), Gibraltar Port.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Engage a Gibraltar-licensed corporate services provider (Companies Act 2014, Part 16; FSC licensing regime) to file the foreign-company registration particulars with the Gibraltar Companies Registry, including certified Cayman certificate of incorporation, memorandum/articles, and register of directors and members. Step 2: Within 30 days of commencing economic activity, file the corporate tax registration application with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office (GO Tax e-portal), submitting the certified Cayman incorporation documents, beneficial-ownership details (Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Act 2019), and particulars of the appointed Gibraltar resident agent/registered office. Step 3: Obtain the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN), normally issued in 5-10 business days, and the Customs Reference Number (GRN) from GCBA for import/export clearance. Step 4: If the entity establishes an office, staff, or dependent agent in Gibraltar (creating a permanent establishment under UK/Gibraltar arrangements), register the branch/PE with the Income Tax Office and file annual returns; if no PE exists and no central management-and-control is exercised from Gibraltar, file an annual non-residence declaration to preserve status outside the Gibraltar income tax net.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (the Cayman Exempted Company is tax-exempt in its home jurisdiction under the Cayman Tax Concessions Act; in Gibraltar it is treated as a non-resident foreign company unless managed-and-controlled from Gibraltar, in which case territorial corporate income tax applies at 12.5% on Gibraltar-source and remitted income only, per Income Tax Act 2010, s. 8-16, as amended)

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box system. Available statutory elections: (i) group-loss surrender election (Income Tax Act 2010, s. 54) for 75%-related companies; (ii) capital-allowances pooling election including the annual investment allowance (GIP 50,000); (iii) Notional Interest Deduction (NID) election on qualifying new equity under the Income Tax (Notional Interest Deduction) Regulations 2019; (iv) residence confirmation election / advance clearance with the Commissioner under the Income Tax (Residence of Companies) Rules 2018; (v) advance pricing arrangement for inter-company royalty and services transactions under the Income Tax Act 2010, s. 76. Hybrid mismatch elections are unavailable: OECD BEPS Action 2 rules are mandatory and automatically re-characterise deductible payments by the Cayman entity to related offshore recipients.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Import Duty and Excise Regime (Customs Duties Ordinance 1958) - Gibraltar has NO VAT, NO GST, and NO sales/use tax, being excluded from the EU VAT area (1972 Act of Accession, Art. 28) and outside the UK VAT regime post-Brexit

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A - no turnover-based indirect tax registration exists in Gibraltar; importers/exporters must obtain a GRN (Gibraltar Reference Number) from GCBA. For supplies routed through the UK or EU, the Cayman entity must register for UK VAT (currently GBP 90,000 taxable-supplies threshold) or destination EU Member State VAT where distance-selling thresholds (e.g., EUR 10,000 cross-border threshold) are exceeded; domestic medical-device sales are standard-rated (UK 20%) with zero-rating applying only to direct export.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT, so export zero-rating arises only where the Cayman entity routes DR flat-panel detectors through the UK/EU supply chain: (i) UK zero-rating under VATA 1994, s. 30(6) and Sch. 8, Group 14 applies to goods physically exported outside the UK within 3 months, conditional on prescribed export evidence; (ii) EU zero-rating under Directive 2006/112/EC, Art. 146 applies to intra-Community dispatches where the customer holds a valid EORI/VAT number; (iii) input VAT suffered in the UK/EU may be recovered by the non-resident Cayman entity under the 13th VAT Directive procedure (Directive 86/560/EEC) or via UK VAT 161A certificate procedures; (iv) exports of HS 9022.14 / 9018.14 X-ray detector panels from Gibraltar attract no Gibraltar export duty and no Gibraltar VAT refund mechanism is available because no input VAT is chargeable in Gibraltar. Zero-rating is denied if export evidence is not secured within the statutory time limits or if goods are subsequently returned to the UK/EU market.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (GECS/CDS single administrative declaration with unique movement reference)
  • Bill of Lading (master and house) or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Gibraltar/UK/EU territory
  • Commercial Invoice (HS 2017 code 9022.14 or 9018.14; DAP/CIF value; exporter GRN and EORI references)
  • Packing List with serial numbers for the amorphous-silicon / caesium-iodide flat-panel detectors
  • Certificate of Origin (UK-Gibraltar export certification, or EUR.1/ATR movement certificate for preferential EU trade)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank FX conversion confirmation from a Gibraltar-licensed bank evidencing receipt and crediting of export proceeds)
  • Medical Device Export / Free Sale Certificate issued by the Gibraltar Medicines Authority under the Medical Devices Regulations 2021 (CE/UKCA-marked device)
  • End-User Clinical Installation Certificate (radiology OEM attestation of final installation at a licensed healthcare facility)

No real-time fiscal e-invoicing or Continuous Transaction Controls (CTC) stamping regime applies in Gibraltar. Customs declarations are made electronically through the Gibraltar Electronic Customs System (GECS), interoperable with the UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS); EU-bound exports require an ICS2 ENS filing. E-invoicing (PEPPOL/UBL or UN/CEFACT) is commercially recommended but not statutorily mandated for digital X-ray flat-panel detector transactions.

Failure to substantiate export within the evidence deadlines triggers: (i) retrospective clawback of UK zero-rating (VATA 1994, s. 36 and s. 73) with statutory interest from the date of supply plus penalties of 30%-100% for deliberate inaccuracies; (ii) Gibraltar Customs Ordinance 1958, s. 178-179, penalties up to GIP 10,000 per false declaration and forfeiture of goods; (iii) Income Tax Act 2010, s. 89-91, a 100% negligence penalty (reducible to 50% on voluntary disclosure) where export proceeds unrepatriated beyond the 180-day deadline are reclassified as unremitted offshore income; (iv) criminal liability for fraudulent evasion of duty/tax, punishable by imprisonment of up to 7 years under the Criminal Procedure (Fraud) Act 2011; (v) asset-freezing and anti-money-laundering enforcement by the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015. The Gibraltar-GB-Cayman Tax Information Exchange Agreement and OECD Common Reporting Standard enable automatic retrospective data exchange among the Gibraltar Income Tax Office, HMRC, and the Cayman Islands Tax Information Authority in cases of suspected export zero-rating fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account. Gibraltar and the UK impose no exchange controls, no central bank approval for inbound capital injection into a Cayman Exempted Company, and no restriction on outbound dividends, intercompany loan repayments, or profit repatriation to the Cayman shareholder. GIP and GBP circulate at par with unlimited interchangeability; USD, EUR, and GBP multi-currency accounts are standard. Profit remittances are subject to zero Gibraltar withholding tax (dividends, interest, royalties, capital gains), and the Cayman entity itself is tax-exempt under Cayman law. The only material hurdles are bank-level source-of-funds due diligence and OECD international tax compliance (Cayman Economic Substance Notification and CRS reporting).

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio to GIP/GBP, and no repatriation deadline under Gibraltar or UK law. Export proceeds from the ISIC 3250 digital X-ray flat panel detector manufacturing business may be held indefinitely in USD, EUR, or GBP, applied toward supplier settlements, or used as collateral for LC and trade finance facilities.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England anchors the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) framework at a 1:1 parity with GBP; GIP banknotes are legally issued by the Government of Gibraltar through the Commissioner of Currency. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the exclusive statutory regulator licensing and supervising all banks, electronic money institutions, and payment service providers in Gibraltar. HM Treasury retains sovereignty oversight over Gibraltar's financial services sector.
