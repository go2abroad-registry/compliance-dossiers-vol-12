Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope (Cayman-side classification remains Tax_Exempt_Zone under Cayman law; Gibraltar recharacterises the entity as a non-resident foreign company taxed on a territorial basis: only profits accruing in or derived from Gibraltar, or attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment, are chargeable, while non-Gibraltar-sourced gateway fees fall outside the Gibraltar charge).

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Gibraltar. Available statutory elections: (i) corporate residence determination by central management and control – incorporation in Gibraltar creates residence, while a foreign company may be treated as non-resident where management and control is abroad; (ii) group relief election allowing surrender of losses between Gibraltar-resident group companies; (iii) employer election for payroll and Employees Tax centralisation; and (iv) hybrid mismatch and DAC6/ATAD-aligned disclosure elections under the Income Tax Act. All elections require prior written notice to the Commissioner.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales & Use Tax. Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area (Protocol 3 of the UK Act of Accession 1972); indirect levies are limited to Gibraltar Customs Import Duties on goods and Excise Duties; cross-border payment services under ISIC 6619 are outside the indirect tax charge entirely.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A – no VAT/GST registration threshold exists in Gibraltar; customs registration with the Gibraltar Customs Office is required only if the PSP imports physical goods (e.g., POS terminals/hardware); GFSC licensing thresholds apply under the Financial Services Act 2019 rather than any indirect tax law.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in VAT terms because Gibraltar has no VAT and no refund mechanism to claim. The functional equivalent is the territorial corporate exclusion: gateway commissions and settlement fees sourced to non-Gibraltar merchants fall outside the Gibraltar charge, producing a 0% effective Gibraltar rate where (i) the entity is not managed and controlled in Gibraltar, (ii) no Gibraltar permanent establishment exists, and (iii) core income-generating activities (contract negotiation, risk retention, pricing, treasury management) are performed outside Gibraltar.

  • Cayman exempted company documentation: certificate of incorporation, certificate of good standing, registered agent letter, and annual audited financial statements
  • Service export substantiation: signed merchant acquiring and processing agreements allocating contracting jurisdiction outside Gibraltar, scheme settlement statements (Visa/Mastercard), and PSP fee invoices identifying foreign merchant jurisdictions
  • Bank/FX evidence: SWIFT MT103 inbound remittance records and FX conversion statements evidencing settlement outside Gibraltar
  • Gibraltar compliance file: GFSC periodic licence returns, economic substance declaration, PAYE/Employees Tax records (if any), and the ITO non-resident or residence tax returns

No real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction control, no B2B e-invoicing mandate, and no digital fiscal stamping regime in Gibraltar. Record-keeping duties arise under GFSC AML/CTF rules and economic substance regulations (minimum 5-year retention); electronic customs declarations (single administrative document) apply only to goods movements, with no fiscal digital clearance for pure cross-border services.

The ITO may raise assessments within 6 years for incorrect returns (21 years for fraud or neglect) and may retrospectively claw back the territorial exclusion, with interest and penalties, where the entity is recharacterised as managed and controlled in Gibraltar. Civil penalties of up to 100% of the tax or counteracted advantage apply for negligent or fraudulent claims, plus daily default fines; criminal prosecution for tax evasion carries custodial exposure; GFSC licence sanctions apply for AML and substance breaches; and disclosure penalties apply under the GAAR/DAC6 framework.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates an open-forex, sterling-pegged monetary system with no exchange-control statute. Current-account and capital-account transactions are fully convertible; the Cayman Exempted Company may freely inject share capital, fund intercompany loans, and remit profits to shareholders without GFSC approval, waiting periods, or conversion surcharges. Gibraltar levies no withholding tax on dividends, interest, or royalties paid by a Gibraltar-resident company to non-residents, and distributions to the Cayman shareholder are tax-neutral. Merchant settlement balances in GBP, EUR, and USD may be held as multi-currency non-resident accounts with no statutory net-open-position limit, subject only to regulator-expected treasury risk-management governance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender regime exists in Gibraltar: there is no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio (effective 0%), and no obligation to repatriate or convert export or merchant-service receipts into GIP/GBP. All cross-border acquiring revenue, gateway fees, and commission income may be retained in the original currency of receipt (GBP, EUR, USD) or converted at the PSP's discretion. The sole duties are transaction record-keeping, reconstruction capacity, and safeguarding of client funds under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and Gibraltar AML/CFT Regulations 2020, plus periodic statistical/information returns to the GFSC carrying no surrender or conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), acting as the licensing and prudential supervisor for banks and payment institutions; the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934 as the sole issuer of Gibraltar pound (GIP) notes at a fixed 1:1 parity with pound sterling (GBP); the Bank of England as the de facto anchor monetary authority because GIP is fully sterling-backed and high-value sterling clearing operates through Bank of England CHAPS.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium (conditionally feasible). A Cayman-exempted PSP may lawfully use an offshore digital EMI (UK-, EU-, or US-licensed) as a funds gateway only if: (a) the EMI performs enhanced due diligence on the GFSC-licensed PSP; (b) the EMI maintains a segregated safeguarding client ledger for the PSP's merchant funds at a tier-1 Gibraltar bank in the PSP's own name; and (c) no EEA passport is relied upon — post-Brexit the GFSC affords no automatic recognition of UK or EU EMI authorisations, so the digital EMI must either obtain a Gibraltar top-up licence or operate through a licensed Gibraltar-appointed agent/introducer. USD routes via non-bank EMIs are constrained by indirect correspondent access rather than direct Fedwire membership. Practical viability is High with Gibraltar-licensed EMIs authorized under the Financial Services Act 2019 framework and Medium-Low with unlicensed EU/UK digital EMIs lacking a Gibraltar agent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: SWIFT MT103/MT202 COV wire from the Cayman shareholder account to the PSP's GIB operational account using ISO 20022 purpose codes — 'EQUI' for equity injection, 'CABI' for capital increase, or 'INTR' for intercompany loan. The receiving bank applies enhanced EDD: beneficial-owner verification, board-resolution review, and source-of-funds affirmation. Capital is layer-separated: statutory licence capital (GFSC threshold aligned to EU standards — EUR 125,000 for a payment institution and EUR 350,000 for an electronic-money institution, as applicable) must sit in a capital escrow ledger, while merchant settlement float is held in the safeguarded client-funds trust account. For USD inbound from a US investor or card-network settlement, the route must pass through GIB's New York correspondent, disclose an OFAC-screened remitter, and match exactly the PSP's legal-name identity on the beneficiary account.Primary clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for cross-border merchant payouts, acquirer buy-outs, and supplier/fee wires; (ii) commercial letters of credit cleared through the issuing Gibraltar bank's correspondent network for capital-intensive procurement such as tier-1 processor contracts, data-centre leases, and treasury collateral purchases; (iii) CHAPS for high-value GBP sterling settlement with UK acquiring banks, card-scheme clearing banks, and HMRC; (iv) Visa Base II / GCRS and Mastercard Global Clearing Management System for card-scheme settlement; (v) Fedwire for USD same-day finality settlement via a US correspondent bank; and (vi) SEPA Credit Transfers for EUR merchant disbursements, accessible only through a non-EEA correspondent or a UK/EU partner bank because Gibraltar is not a SEPA member.Spot FX (T+0/T+2) conversion between GIP, GBP, EUR, and USD for merchant settlement float managementDeliverable FX forwards (standard tenors up to 12 months; extendable to 24 months against a credit-approved bank counterparty)FX swaps and swap-purchase contracts to cover funding-date and settlement-date mismatchesVanilla European FX options (GBP/USD and EUR/USD puts and calls) to cap adverse rate movements while retaining upsideCross-currency interest rate swaps solely for funded trade-credit or treasury-collateral portfoliosNon-Deliverable Forwards are not ordinarily permissible or necessary in the fully convertible, deliverable GBP/USD market; admissible only as a documentary hedge-evolution overlay for currencies subject to convertibility restrictions in the PSP's acquiring markets

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium (conditionally feasible). A Cayman-exempted PSP may lawfully use an offshore digital EMI (UK-, EU-, or US-licensed) as a funds gateway only if: (a) the EMI performs enhanced due diligence on the GFSC-licensed PSP; (b) the EMI maintains a segregated safeguarding client ledger for the PSP's merchant funds at a tier-1 Gibraltar bank in the PSP's own name; and (c) no EEA passport is relied upon — post-Brexit the GFSC affords no automatic recognition of UK or EU EMI authorisations, so the digital EMI must either obtain a Gibraltar top-up licence or operate through a licensed Gibraltar-appointed agent/introducer. USD routes via non-bank EMIs are constrained by indirect correspondent access rather than direct Fedwire membership. Practical viability is High with Gibraltar-licensed EMIs authorized under the Financial Services Act 2019 framework and Medium-Low with unlicensed EU/UK digital EMIs lacking a Gibraltar agent.
