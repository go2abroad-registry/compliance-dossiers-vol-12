Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office (ITO), HM Government of Gibraltar — official e-portal: https://www.ito.gi, with self-service e-filing via MyITO and the AgentPortal. Customs and excise matters are administered separately by HM Customs Gibraltar, HM Government of Gibraltar (https://www.customs.gov.gi). HMRC (UK) has no jurisdiction over Gibraltar taxation, and the UK VAT system does not extend to Gibraltar, which remains outside the EU customs union and the EU VAT area.

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company establishing a branch, registered office, or place of business in Gibraltar must first register as a foreign company with the Registrar of Companies, Gibraltar, under the Companies Act 2014, obtaining a Gibraltar company registration number. For tax purposes, the entity must then register with the Income Tax Office within 30 days of commencing taxable activity by filing the application for company registration prescribed by the ITO, together with certified copies of the Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, memorandum and articles of association, register of directors and members, identity and due-diligence documentation for all ultimate beneficial owners, and a letter appointing a Gibraltar-resident tax agent or representative. Upon approval, the ITO issues a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN). If goods are imported into or exported from Gibraltar for the bulk polymer distribution operation, the entity must additionally register with HM Customs Gibraltar as an importer/exporter and economic operator in the Gibraltar Customs Management System (CMS), obtaining a customs EOI/consignee code. A Cayman exempted company managed and controlled outside Gibraltar and having no fixed place of business, dependent agent, or employees in Gibraltar remains non-resident and is not subject to Gibraltar Corporation Tax on its worldwide profits; however, a TIN is still required where any Gibraltar-source income arises, and a physical freeport/warehouse presence used only for transit may be maintained without creating a taxable permanent establishment if no local sales or contracts are concluded in Gibraltar.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box regime, pass-through election, or hybrid entity election; all companies, including foreign-incorporated entities carrying on trade in Gibraltar, are treated as corporation taxpayers at entity level under the Corporation Tax Act 2019 (standard rate 10%; special statutory rates for public utilities and banks; a global minimum top-up tax of 15% may apply to in-scope multinational groups under Pillar Two/QDMTT rules). Statutory elections available include: (a) election to be assessed only on Gibraltar-source income where central management and control remains outside Gibraltar; (b) election for Gibraltar tax residence by relocating board meetings and central management and control to Gibraltar, thereby subjecting the worldwide profits of the Cayman entity to Gibraltar Corporation Tax; (c) group relief election for the surrender of trading losses among Gibraltar-resident group companies; (d) tonnage tax election for qualifying shipping activities; and (e) entity classification elections for local-law purposes such as Gibraltar trust or foundation arrangements for the holding of shares. The Cayman Islands exempted-company status confers zero tax in the Cayman Islands but has no extraterritorial effect; Gibraltar treats the Cayman entity as an ordinary limited-liability corporate taxpayer and never as a disregarded or flow-through vehicle.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax; Gibraltar applies Import Duty and Excise Duty on the importation of goods under the Customs Ordinance and the Excise Duties Ordinance

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; Gibraltar has no VAT or GST and therefore no turnover-based indirect-tax registration threshold. Registration with HM Customs Gibraltar for import duty purposes is mandatory for every person importing goods into Gibraltar, regardless of annual value. Bulk commercial consignments of petrochemical polymers are never exempt by value; only accompanied personal luggage allowances benefit from de minimis relief.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a VAT concept because Gibraltar has no VAT to zero-rate. The equivalent relief is the exemption from Gibraltar Import Duty for: (i) goods exported from Gibraltar, (ii) goods in transit or transshipment through Gibraltar, and (iii) goods placed under bonded warehousing pending export. Bulk polyethylene and polypropylene granules routed via Gibraltar Freeport qualify for duty suspension without payment where the goods physically leave Gibraltar for a third-country destination within the permitted transshipment or warehousing period. The exporter must demonstrate that the goods were not released into Gibraltar local consumption and were sold or consigned to a non-Gibraltar buyer. Upon import into a European Union member state, EU import VAT and customs duty will be levied at the destination point, outside the Gibraltar indirect-tax net.

HM Customs Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office conduct post-clearance audits of transshipment and export documentation for bulk polymer traders. Failure to prove physical export within the 180-day documentary benchmark causes the Import Duty relief to be void and the duty is clawed back with interest from the date of import. Knowingly false export declarations render the goods liable to forfeiture and expose the declarant to civil penalties of up to three times the value of the goods, plus criminal prosecution and imprisonment of up to three years under the Customs Ordinance. In the corporate-tax sphere, fabricated export documents, circular letters of credit, or unsubstantiated inward remittances trigger reassessment of Gibraltar-source trading profits, denial of reliefs, penalties of up to 100% of the tax due, interest, and potential joint and several liability of directors and the Gibraltar tax agent.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The UK, Gibraltar, and the Cayman Islands each abolished exchange controls (UK Exchange Control Act repealed 1979; Gibraltar mirrored this; Cayman has never imposed controls on exempted-company accounts). Both current-account items (trade settlements, commissions, freight) and capital-account items (equity injections, shareholder loans, profit distributions) are fully convertible into USD, EUR, GBP, or GIP without central-bank approval, licensing, or deposit. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders of the Cayman Exempted Company is unrestricted, subject only to CRS/FATCA reporting and standard EDD documentary evidence; there is no withholding tax or transfer tax in Gibraltar and no Cayman tax on distributions. Practical hurdles are solely bank-driven: expected source-of-wealth evidence, board resolutions for each conversion, and sanctions screening of the FX counterparty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. There is no statutory requirement to convert, surrender, repatriate, or hold export proceeds onshore in Gibraltar, the UK, or the Cayman Islands. Receipts from polymer wholesale sales may remain indefinitely in USD or any chosen currency in an offshore account. No mandatory conversion ratio applies; no time limit regulates the bring-back of proceeds. The entity must nevertheless retain the full trade documentation trail (invoice, bill of lading, LC, SWIFT advice) for five years to meet the Gibraltar Criminal Procedure (Money Laundering and Terrorism Financing) Rules 2019 record-keeping obligation.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England is the monetary authority for the Gibraltar pound (GIP), which circulates at a 1:1 peg to GBP under the Gibraltar Currency Notes Act 1934; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory banking, EMI, and trade-licensing regulator. The Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) does not control FX flows for exempted companies. No separate Gibraltar central bank exists; the GFSC administers no reserve requirement on non-resident trading accounts.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Overall rating: MEDIUM-LOW for offshore digital EMIs; MEDIUM for a locally licensed Gibraltar EMI. Legal constraints: after Brexit, neither the UK nor EEA EMI passport applies to Gibraltar; any e-money issuer must hold a licence under the Gibraltar Financial Services Act 2019 and the Electronic Money Regulations, and most Gibraltar EMIs lack the USD nostro, correspondent banking, and LC issuance infrastructure required for bulk petrochemical clearing. UK fintech EMIs (for example Revolut, Wise) will generally decline a Cayman Exempted Company and prohibit bulk commodity trading; therefore EMIs may be used only as a fallback for GBP/EUR operating expenses and never as the main collection or disbursement rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: initial subscribed capital is remitted by the shareholder's bank by SWIFT MT103 to the entity's Tier-1 operational account using ISO 20022 purpose code 'CAPH' (capital payments) or 'EQUI' (equity-stake purchase), with the remittance information citing the Cayman registration number and the board resolution date. The remitter must be identical to the shareholder of record on the Certificate of Incumbency; any third-party contribution is treated as an intercompany loan and requires an executed loan agreement, board resolution, and purpose code 'LOAN'. The receiving bank will typically apply an enhanced-diligence hold of two to five business days pending verification of the UBO identity, source-of-funds evidence, and confirmation that no director or UBO is a PEP. Amounts above GBP 50,000 additionally require a sanctions-screening confirmation and, if conversion into USD is needed, a priced FX swap ticket documenting the GBP/USD rate. Routing through personal accounts, EMIs, crypto on-ramps, or third-country clearing banks is prohibited; such routing is a presumptive suspicious-activity indicator under the Gibraltar AML regime.Direct settlements with EU polymer vendors use SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) with purpose codes 'TRAD' or 'COMC' and full invoice/B/L references in the beneficiary information. For major bulk feedstock purchases, the target rail is an Irrevocable Confirmed Letter of Credit: the issuing bank transmits via MT700, the confirming bank adds its undertaking via MT710, and settlement at sight occurs via MT756/MT742; the company must post cash cover or a parent guarantee for the LC line. For repeated small-lot deliveries, documentary collections via MT410/MT412 are used as a cheaper secondary rail. Bank-to-bank liquidity repositioning is executed only by the Gibraltar Tier-1 bank through MT202/MT202 COV on its own UK/EU nosters; the company never uses MT202 for third-party payments.
