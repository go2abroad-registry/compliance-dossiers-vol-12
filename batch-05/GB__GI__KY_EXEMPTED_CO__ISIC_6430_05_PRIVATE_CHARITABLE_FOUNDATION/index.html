Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office (ITO), HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax; e-filing via the ITO portal); corporate and private-foundation registries administered by Gibraltar Companies House / Registrar of Foundations; GST and customs administration by HM Customs Gibraltar.

E-government filing portal

1) Register the Cayman Exempted Company as an overseas company at Gibraltar Companies House within 1 month of establishing a place of business (submit Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, memorandum/articles, particulars of directors, Gibraltar registered office address). 2) File the ITO company registration form and obtain the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number within 30 days of commencement. 3) Apply to the Charity Registry / Commissioner of Income Tax under the Charities Act 1961 for charitable status and the s.10 Income Tax Act 2010 exemption certificate. 4) Optionally register the vehicle as a Private Foundation under the Private Foundations Act 2017, with council members and charitable objects recorded. 5) If GST-registrable supplies are made, apply for a GST registration number with HM Customs Gibraltar. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - Cayman Exempted Company enjoys zero direct taxation in the Cayman Islands (exempted-company certificate), and the Gibraltar Private Charitable Foundation is fully exempt on income applied to charitable or scientific grant purposes under s.10 Income Tax Act 2010. Residual non-charitable income follows Gibraltar territorial-source rules at the 12.5% corporate rate; the vehicle is not pass-through.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in Gibraltar. Available statutory mechanisms: charitable-exemption election before the Commissioner of Income Tax; Private Foundations Act 2017 constitutional election (enforceability of objects and council composition); voluntary GST registration or non-profit exemption application; Gibraltar corporate tax grouping election (Group Payment Arrangement) for resident group members; OECD hybrid-mismatch rules apply mandatorily without elective carve-outs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

£100,000 (Gibraltar pounds, at par with GBP) annual taxable turnover; charitable endowment-management services and grant distributions are exempt or outside scope, so no GST registration is ordinarily triggered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% GST applies to: (a) exported goods proven to leave Gibraltar; (b) cross-border B2B services where the recipient is outside Gibraltar, including offshore grant-management and endowment services; and (c) charitable supplies where no consideration is received. Donations, grants and philanthropic distributions are not consideration for a supply and fall entirely outside GST. Financial services supplied to Gibraltar recipients are exempt (not zero-rated) and carry no input-tax recovery. Zero-rating requires contemporaneous export evidence and a compliant zero-rated tax invoice.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic declaration lodged with HM Customs Gibraltar)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or other transport document evidencing physical departure from Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank confirmation evidencing payment of exported goods or receipt of offshore grant funding
  • Zero-rated GST tax invoice with exporter details and zero-rate code
  • Grant agreement and charitable-purpose evidence substantiating the exempt or outside-scope supply treatment

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate; no mandatory government API e-invoice clearance. Suppliers must issue GST invoices within 30 days and retain electronic records for 10 years; goods crossings require electronic customs declarations via HM Customs Gibraltar, with economic-operator registration for importers and exporters.

Income Tax Office and HM Customs Gibraltar may conduct retrospective assessments within 6 years (extended to 20 years for fraud or negligent conduct). Unsupported zero-rating or forged export documents trigger clawback of the GST deemed payable, tax-geared penalties of up to 100%, and criminal liability (unlimited fine and/or up to 7 years' imprisonment). Misuse of the charitable exemption (income diverted from charitable purposes) causes withdrawal of exemption, assessment at 12.5% with penalties of up to double the tax, and compulsory deregistration. Gibraltar is subject to OECD/Global Forum transparency reviews and automatic exchange of information, increasing audit scrutiny of cross-border funding flows.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar imposes no exchange controls and grants full convertibility on both capital and current account for non-resident entities. The Cayman exempted company may hold multi-currency accounts (GBP, USD, EUR), receive endowment donations, and remit grant payments abroad without GFSC or government approval. The only practical hurdles are FATCA/CRS self-certification, bank-level source-of-funds verification, and confirmation that no profit-remittance or dividend withholding applies (Gibraltar does not levy dividend withholding on non-residents; Cayman levies no tax).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable: neither Gibraltar nor the United Kingdom imposes export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation deadlines. Charitable donation inflows and retained foreign-currency balances may be held indefinitely in the original currency, and conversion to GBP for grant-making is at the bank's commercial rate without central-bank authorisation.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory regulator of all banks, e-money institutions, and fiduciary/licensed trust and company service providers under the Gibraltar Financial Services Act 2019 and the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) operates on a currency-board regime at a fixed 1:1 parity with the pound sterling, making Bank of England monetary policy the de facto monetary anchor; no separate exchange-control authority exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with material constraints. UK FCA-authorised EMIs and Gibraltar-licensed e-money institutions can process GBP Faster Payments and SEPA euro flows for the foundation, but many reject Cayman-registered charities in automated AML scoring because the exempted-company register is opaque. Viability improves only if the foundation holds a Gibraltar or UK registered office, presents a Cayman beneficial-ownership register extract, and appoints a named UK/Gibraltar compliance liaison. Crypto-native EMIs and digital-asset rails are categorically unsuitable for Low_Risk_Standard endowment treasury operations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 aggregate annual connected-party revenue transactions triggers mandatory Local File; Master File required when group consolidated turnover exceeds GIP 5,000,000; financial-related intangible transactions with connected parties trigger Local File at GIP 300,000

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to entities carrying on relevant activities, including Fund Management and Holding Company activities. A charitable endowment manager domiciled in Gibraltar must demonstrate adequate physical offices, qualified staff, and core income-generating activities in Gibraltar. Failure to satisfy ESR triggers recharacterisation of tax exemption, financial penalties, and potential strike-off

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Under Gibraltar Criminal Offences (Taxation) Act, fine of up to 250% of tax underpaid, public reprimand, and term of imprisonment not exceeding seven years

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly consent or connive in evasion are jointly and severally liable for all unpaid tax and penal interest; director disqualification for up to five years; travel restrictions; civil restitution of all diverted charitable assets
Economic Substance / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: ESR failure penalty of GIP 25,000 fixed penalty plus GIP 5,000 per month of non-compliance, up to GIP 60,000; strike-off from the Gibraltar Companies Register; denial of charitable exemption leads to corporation tax at 12.5% plus 4% quarterly interest

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly submitting false ESR declarations face personal fines up to GIP 30,000, disqualification for up to five years, seizure of assets through debt recovery, and imprisonment not exceeding two years under the Gibraltar Companies Act
Money Laundering and Charitable Misuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act and AML Regulations, fines up to GIP 250,000, forfeiture of endowed assets, revocation of tax-exempt charitable status, and referral to the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit

Director personal liability: Directors face civil liability for breach of fiduciary duty, disgorgement of private benefits, travel bans under the Inland Police Act, and imprisonment up to ten years for money laundering
Unlawful Profit Repatriation / Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Recharacterisation of such payments as disallowed distributions; Gibraltar corporation tax at 12.5% on disallowed amounts; 4% quarterly interest; restitution of improperly distributed endowment funds

Director personal liability: Schemes that cause a fraudulent preference of connected shareholders trigger personal restitution from directors; court-imposed travel bans and director disqualification; criminal liability under the Companies (Powers) Act for wrongful distributions
