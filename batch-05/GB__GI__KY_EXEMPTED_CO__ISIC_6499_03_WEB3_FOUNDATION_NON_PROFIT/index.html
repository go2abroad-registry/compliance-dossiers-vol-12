Available election forms

No Gibraltar 'check-the-box' election exists. Available elections include: (i) Qualifying Company election under the Companies (Taxation of Qualifying Companies) Regulations 2022 granting 0%–2% reduced rates for eligible offshore e-commerce, royalty and holding-company income, conditional on economic substance in Gibraltar; (ii) charitable exemption under the Income Tax Act 2010 for public-benefit non-profit foundations; (iii) group-relief elections for 75% corporate groups; (iv) OECD-aligned CFC and hybrid-mismatch notification obligations; and (v) for US federal tax purposes only, the US Form 8832 check-the-box election remains available to the Cayman entity (default classification: taxable corporation).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — Gibraltar has no VAT, GST or sales-and-use tax; the only indirect levies are customs tariffs and excise duties

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no general consumption tax exists in Gibraltar; all companies with a Gibraltar presence must register for Income Tax regardless of turnover. Ancillary UK note: if the foundation establishes a separate UK VAT nexus, the UK registration threshold is GBP 90,000 (from 1 April 2024).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable within Gibraltar — with no VAT system, there is no 0% export rate and no export VAT refund mechanism. For a separately-held UK VAT registration, UK 0% rating is available only where genuine exports of goods or qualifying international services occur; native-token issuance and decentralised-governance services supplied by the non-profit foundation are generally outside UK VAT scope or treated as exempt financial services, thereby restricting input-VAT recovery.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar SAD) — required only for physical exports from Gibraltar
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank SWIFT/treasury record evidencing the completed offshore-sale cycle and repatriation of export proceeds

None — Gibraltar has no e-invoicing mandate, no real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction controls and no digital stamping; standard commercial invoicing and 6-year record retention under the Income Tax Act 2010 apply.

Gibraltar imposes no exchange controls, so the 180-day return benchmark is a conservative treasury/audit policy rather than a statutory deadline. Clawback risk concentrates in three areas: (i) UK VAT fraud or repeated misdeclaration where 0% export rating is unsupported — HMRC penalties of 30%–100% of tax lost plus criminal liability; (ii) Gibraltar customs misdeclaration — forfeiture, fines and prosecution; (iii) Gibraltar Income Tax false-statement penalties — up to 100% of tax lost plus interest and prosecution, amplified by GFIU/FSC AML scrutiny of High-Risk Special Licence token-foundation flows.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar imposes no static exchange control legislation. Capital and current account items are fully convertible; the Gibraltar pound is legally pegged to the British pound sterling at parity. Profit remittances to offshore shareholders require no regulatory licence or approval, provided the entity maintains adequate economic substance records, files any required Gibraltar tax/ESR declarations, and passes banking-source-of-funds checks. The Web3 foundation’s token holdings remain outside conventional convertibility assessments, but any fiat GBp/USD conversion must be executed via a GFSC-licensed bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion timeline exists for export proceeds or service revenue. A non-profit foundation receiving voluntary contributions, grants or grants-in-kind may retain foreign currency indefinitely. Only custodial banks apply internal Suspicious Transaction Reporting (STR) thresholds if cash or high-risk digital asset payments exceed €10,000 equivalent.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) acting as the principal monetary, banking and financial conduct supervisor, with the Government of Gibraltar Finance Centre Department responsible for currency issuance; the Bank of England exerts lender-of-last-resort influence on the Gibraltar pound’s parity through the sterling peg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility with legal constraints. Following UK withdrawal from the EU, no EEA EMI passport operates in Gibraltar; a Gibraltar-licensed electronic-money institution (such as GSI Bank or a GFSC 'Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2011' firm) may act as a primary clearing node. Offshore EMIs like Revolut or Wise can serve only as non-regulated secondary disbursement channels; they cannot issue a formal capital-escrow account and may freeze crypto-adjacent foundations due to their internal high-risk policies. The foundation should maintain at least one GFSC-licensed bank as the settlement endpoint and use EMIs strictly for low-value operational expenses.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 0 (zero) formal transfer pricing legislation threshold; however, the Gibraltar Transfer Pricing Rules, aligned with OECD BEPS Action 8-10, require contemporaneous documentation for intercompany arrangements involving related parties where the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds GIP 500,000 per annum; a Local File and a Gibraltar-relevant Master File are mandatory for transactions above GIP 5,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar has enacted Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) applying to 'relevant activities' including distribution and service centre businesses, financing, and holding company activities. A non-resident Cayman Exempted Company acting as a pure equity-holding or token-holding vehicle must satisfy core ESR tests: (i) managed and controlled in Gibraltar or the Cayman Islands (as applicable); (ii) conducting core income-generating activities locally; (iii) adequate physical presence, employees, and expenditure proportionate to activity. For a Web3 Foundation, the 'non-profit' and 'governance' status does not exempt it from ESR; failure to file the annual economic substance return results in penalties up to GIP 10,000, possible strike-off, and a 5-year compliance restriction on directors.

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Gibraltar's Income Tax Act, tax evasion carries civil penalties up to 100% of the tax undercharged, plus daily interest at 1.5% per month; criminal conviction can lead to an unlimited fine and imprisonment for up to 7 years; the Cayman Islands Exempted Company risks revocation of its exempt status and a fine under the Companies Act.

Shell Company / Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar ESR penalties: failure to satisfy the economic substance test triggers a GIP 10,000 initial fine, GIP 100,000 for continued non-compliance, and a potential strike-off of the company; the Cayman Islands Tax Information Authority can levy CI$ 10,000 fines and the Registrar may strike the company off the register, leading to asset escheatment.

AML / Counter-Terrorist Financing Breach
