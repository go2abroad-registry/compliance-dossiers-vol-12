Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Companies of Gibraltar / Companies House Gibraltar, Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar (online corporate e-Services portal; paper filings accepted at the Registry counter).

Governing statute

Companies Act 2014 (Gibraltar), as amended - ss. 5 (incorporation), 12-14 (registration and certificate of incorporation), 15-24 (name, registered office and statutory filings), 64 (solvency statement on share capital) and ss. 304-307 (exempted company designation); Income Tax Act 2010 ss. 46-50 and Income Tax (Qualifying Companies) Rules 2010 (10-year tax-exempt status for qualifying companies deriving profits from outside Gibraltar); Data Protection Act 2004 (GDPR) Part VI (data controller registration).

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement; a single subscriber ordinary share of GBP 1 (or USD equivalent) is the standard practice. Distributions are subject to the solvency statement regime under s. 64 of the Companies Act 2014; no Government escrow deposit is mandated for this non-regulated telematics SaaS business.

Funding & unlock path: For the leased office premises: fire safety certificate to be issued by the Gibraltar Fire and Rescue Service after annual inspection; electrical installation certificate and fire alarm testing certificate from a certified electrical contractor; structural load certification only required if the premises are materially altered; workplace risk assessment and first-aid arrangements under the Health and Safety at Work Act (Gibraltar) must be maintained for all staff.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement; a single subscriber ordinary share of GBP 1 (or USD equivalent) is the standard practice. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar - Income Tax Office (ITO), a directorate of the Financial Services and Taxation Ministry; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/tax (e-Tax). The ITO administers Gibraltar corporation tax, issues Tax Reference Numbers (TRNs), and is the competent authority for exchange of tax information and issuance of tax residence certificates.

E-government filing portal

For the Cayman Exempted Company, a Gibraltar TRN is required only if the entity commences trade in Gibraltar or establishes a branch/permanent establishment. SOP: (1) file Form IT-1A (Non-Resident/Branch) with the ITO prior to commencement of activity; (2) attach certified Cayman Islands Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, constitutional documents, audited financial statements or opening balance sheet, Gibraltar registered-office confirmation, Certificate of Incumbency, and passport copies of directors; (3) declare principal banking/EMI relationships and payment gateway (Stripe Billing) details in the registration annex; (4) complete the ITO economic substance questionnaire under the Gibraltar Economic Substance regime; (5) receive the TRN within 10-15 working days. The overseas branch must also file the registration under the Companies (Overseas Companies) Regulations unless a statutory exemption applies.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone - the Cayman Exempted Company is exempt from Cayman Islands income, corporate, and capital gains tax. Under Gibraltar's territorial tax system (post-2020 Income Tax Act), if the entity maintains no Gibraltar permanent establishment (no office, employees, or core fleet-dispatch management functions in Gibraltar), no Gibraltar-source profits arise and no Gibraltar corporation tax is due. If a Gibraltar PE is constituted, attributable profits are chargeable at 12.5%; capital gains and dividends are generally outside the charge. The structure is therefore Tax_Exempt_Zone at the Cayman level, with Territorial_Scope exposure in Gibraltar contingent on PE substance. — For the Cayman Exempted Company, a Gibraltar TRN is required only if the entity commences trade in Gibraltar or establishes a branch/permanent establishment. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - the Cayman Exempted Company is exempt from Cayman Islands income, corporate, and capital gains tax. Under Gibraltar's territorial tax system (post-2020 Income Tax Act), if the entity maintains no Gibraltar permanent establishment (no office, employees, or core fleet-dispatch management functions in Gibraltar), no Gibraltar-source profits arise and no Gibraltar corporation tax is due. If a Gibraltar PE is constituted, attributable profits are chargeable at 12.5%; capital gains and dividends are generally outside the charge. The structure is therefore Tax_Exempt_Zone at the Cayman level, with Territorial_Scope exposure in Gibraltar contingent on PE substance.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not employ US-style check-the-box elections. Available statutory mechanisms: (1) transitional election under the territorial regime to retain pre-2020 exemption-method treatment for accumulated reserves; (2) election/deeming of tax residency where central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, aligned with Cayman economic substance filings; (3) advance pricing arrangement (APA) application under the Gibraltar Transfer Pricing Act 2021 for intercompany SaaS licensing between the Gibraltar branch and the Cayman parent; (4) UK anti-hybrid elections under ITA 2010 Part 6A are not statutorily required for this structure, but the group must document any disregarded-payment treatment to prevent double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No indirect tax system in Gibraltar: Gibraltar levies neither VAT, GST, nor sales and use tax; the only indirect charge is customs/excise import duty on physical goods entering Gibraltar. UK VAT at 20% may attach upstream where the Cayman entity registers in the UK (Non-Union OSS) for digital-services supplies to UK consumers; B2B SaaS into the UK/EU remains under customer reverse charge and outside Gibraltar fiscal control.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for Gibraltar domestic indirect taxes (no VAT registration regime exists). UK VAT threshold: GBP 90,000 of UK taxable turnover for non-established suppliers supplying digital services to UK consumers; registration is mandatory above the threshold and optional below. EU VAT registration is not triggered for cross-border B2B SaaS because the reverse charge applies to the EU customer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable within Gibraltar because no VAT zero-rating mechanism exists. For income-tax territorial relief on the export of logistics-fleet SaaS: exports of services to non-Gibraltar counterparties fall outside Gibraltar source rules when no PE exists; where a PE exists, support contracts with foreign logistics operators are attributed to foreign source under territorial-allocation and transfer-pricing rules. Physical telematics hardware exported from Gibraltar benefits from customs duty suspension/exemption upon export outside the customs territory. For UK VAT, B2B supplies of the SaaS to UK VAT-registered customers are outside scope under reverse charge; B2C UK digital supplies are standard-rated at 20% with no zero-rating.

  • Customs/Commercial Export Declaration (SAD/Gibraltar customs documentation) for telematics hardware movements
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill and packing list evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or bank/Stripe settlement confirmations) evidencing FX repatriation within 180 days
  • Signed SaaS licensing agreements, deployment/telematics dashboards, and server delivery logs as service-export substantiation

Gibraltar imposes no electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping obligation; standard digital invoices generated via Stripe Billing or the ERP are legally sufficient. If the entity holds UK VAT registration, VAT returns must be filed through HMRC Making Tax Digital (MTD)-compatible APIs; e-invoices are accepted without government pre-clearance. The UK has announced a potential e-invoicing mandate for 2027-2030, which has no Gibraltar analogue.

Gibraltar: the ITO may reopen returns within extended limitation periods (up to 20 years for fraud or negligent conduct) and impose penalties of up to 100% of the additional tax with interest; false customs declarations trigger forfeiture and fines of up to three times the value of the goods; criminal liability may arise under the Criminal Finances Act 2017 for failure to prevent tax-evasion facilitation. UK VAT: unsupported zero-rating/outside-scope claims are retrospectively clawed back with penalties up to 100% of the VAT per Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 for deliberate-and-concealed errors, plus a 4-year lookback for careless behavior. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day window may recharacterize revenue as Gibraltar-source and cause corporation tax at 12.5% with interest, disregarding the Cayman offshore accumulation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at 1:1 to GBP and is freely convertible. Gibraltar imposes no general foreign exchange controls on offshore corporate accounts. Current account transactions, including SaaS subscription fees, telematics service revenues and logistics dispatch commissions, are fully convertible without restriction. Capital account transactions, including equity injections, intercompany loans and shareholder subordinated debt, are likewise permitted. Profit remittance to the Cayman Exempted Company is not subject to Gibraltar withholding tax. The practical hurdles are therefore bank-level rather than statutory: AML/KYC verification, sanctions screening, demonstration of beneficial ownership, and avoidance of inadvertent UK tax residence under the UK statutory residence test.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rule applies to Gibraltar, the United Kingdom or the Cayman Islands for logistics-telematics SaaS or software-enabled dispatch services. Export receipts may be retained in any freely convertible currency, including USD, EUR, GBP or GIP. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%. Surrender timeline: N/A. The only compliance requirement is accurate statistical/balance-of-payments reporting where applicable, plus retention of underlying trade documentation for AML/CTF record-keeping purposes for a minimum of eight years.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary anchor for the Gibraltar pound) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the local banking and payments supervisor; exchange control administration is exercised by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH conditionally. GFSC-licensed electronic money institutions, UK FCA-authorised EMIs and EEA-authorised payment institutions may provide cross-border virtual IBANs, card acquiring and SWIFT-equivalent settlement to a Cayman Exempted Company. However, EMIs are not licensed credit institutions, cannot grant overdraft or treasury credit lines, and generally impose lower transaction velocity limits. The Cayman exempted status will trigger enhanced due diligence and may require a personal guarantee of a director or UBO. The legal constraint is that a Gibraltar-regulated EMI has no token tender or capital-market role and is best used as a settlement overlay, not as the primary treasury clearing rail. For fleet dispatch volumes above USD 250,000/month, the company should maintain a full commercial banking account at a Tier-1 bank and use the EMI only for card acquirer settlement and rapid vendor payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital and shareholder loans must be routed by direct SWIFT wire from the shareholder's own bank account to the Gibraltar operational account. The payment should use SWIFT MT103 or MT202 with IMF BPM6 purpose code E511 for equity investment or F511 for intercompany lending, with remittance narrative stating plainly: 'Capital injection / shareholder loan to Cayman Exempted Company; no trade underlying'. The receiving bank must be pre-notified with a corporate resolution authorising the capital increase, share subscription agreement or loan agreement, source of funds and source of wealth documentation, and a certificate of incumbency. Any receipt above EUR 50,000 may trigger enhanced due diligence; the company must retain an investor board minute, bank confirmation and annual return. To avoid funds being held for manual review, the originator should use a well-established UK or EU correspondent bank, include the exact purpose code in the SWIFT remittance, and avoid cash-like descriptions or delayed third-party wiring instructions.The primary outward clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 with the SWIFT gpi tracker for low-value and mid-value settlement of fleet-management subscriptions, telematics service fees, driver payroll and software vendor payments in GBP, EUR and USD. For larger hardware infrastructure purchases, such as telematics devices installation or fleet communications equipment, a documentary Commercial Letter of Credit opened by SWIFT MT700 and amended via MT707 is the preferred rail, with invoices and transport documents checked by the presenting bank. UK domestic supplier payments may be cleared over Faster Payments or CHAPS where the beneficiary has a UK bank account; euro-denominated settlements inside the SEPA zone may use SEPA Credit Transfer through a Gibraltar or UK intermediary where authorised.
