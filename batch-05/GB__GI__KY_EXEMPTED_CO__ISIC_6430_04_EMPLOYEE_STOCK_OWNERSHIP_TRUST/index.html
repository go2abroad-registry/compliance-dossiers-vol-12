Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Cayman Exempted Company retaining Cayman tax-exempt status; non-resident for Gibraltar purposes where central management and control is exercised outside Gibraltar; ESOP administration fees earned from non-resident employers are non-Gibraltar-source income)

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election. A foreign-incorporated entity may elect non-Gibraltar tax residence under the Income Tax Act 2010 by filing an annual declaration of central management and control; an ESOP trust may elect fiscal transparency under the Trustees Act 2007 and International Trusts Act 2012 where all settlors and beneficiaries are non-resident.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT or GST; Gibraltar operates only customs import duty and excise duties (excluded from EU VAT territory; no domestic sales and use tax)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST registration threshold exists in Gibraltar (GIP 0). Import duty is triggered from the first value of physical goods imported into Gibraltar; percentage rates range from 0% to 12% depending on classification. Intangible services and ESOP administration are outside the scope of duty.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a VAT concept: Gibraltar has no VAT or GST to zero-rate. ESOP trust administration services supplied to non-resident corporate clients are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation and generate no duty liability or refund mechanism. Import duty applies solely to tangible goods entering Gibraltar; no duty is assessed on services, intangibles, or equity transfers.

  • Customs Export Declaration - required only for physical goods exported from Gibraltar; not applicable to ESOP administration services
  • Bill of Lading / Airwaybill - required only where physical asset certificates or securities are transported
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from a Gibraltar-licensed bank evidencing receipt of foreign-service consideration

No e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, real-time API or digital stamping mandate exists. Commercial invoices require no governmental authentication; records must be retained with supporting invoices and banking documentation for six years for Income Tax Office audit.

No VAT zero-rating fraud exposure exists. However, misdeclaration under the Customs Duties Ordinance attracts penalties of up to 100% of duty evaded and fines up to GIP 10,000; fraudulent misrepresentation of residence or exempt status enables retrospective Income Tax Office reassessment for six years (unlimited in cases of fraud) and may disqualify the ESOP trust administrator.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound is pegged at par to the pound sterling and is fully convertible. Gibraltar maintains no exchange control regime for capital or current account transactions. The Cayman Exempted Company, operating as a Gibraltar-registered ESOP trust administrator, may remit income, dividends, or trust distributions freely without exchange control clearance. Convertibility is unrestricted for all G10 currencies; non-sterling accounts may be held in GBP, EUR, or USD. The only practical hurdles are iterative AML/KYC verification of source of funds and compliance with EU/UK sanctions in relation to underlying beneficiaries.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Gibraltar has no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no pre-existing foreign exchange control reporting beyond ordinary balance-of-payments data collection. Export receipts or trust contributions received in any convertible currency may be retained, held, or remitted in the original currency without mandatory sale to the Gibraltar pound.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), functioning as the integrated financial services regulator for banking, currency custody, and foreign exchange activities; monetary and sanctions oversight remains with the Bank of England and HM Treasury in the United Kingdom.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to Low. Gibraltar is not a member of the European Union, so UK or EU licensed electronic money institutions (EMIs) do not enjoy a statutory passport into Gibraltar. A non-bank EMI may operate only if separately licensed by the GFSC under the Electronic Money Regulations 2023 or through an agent/white-label arrangement with a licensed Gibraltar bank. For a Cayman exempted company serving as an ESOP trust, digital EMIs are best used solely for client-facing gift or incentive card issuance and prohibited against acting as the primary custodian of capital escrow funds or equity settlements. The margins for operational flexibility are outweighed by the clearing risk of unlicensed EMI counterparties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing begins with a SWIFT MT103 from the Cayman settlement bank to the Gibraltar receiving bank, using the beneficiary account held in the name of the exempted company's Gibraltar branch. For contributions above an equivalent of EUR 100,000, an MT202 COV must be appended to allow transparent onward correspondent clearing. The payment should be tagged with an appropriate IMF/BOP purpose code such as 'INV' or 'PFI' (portfolio investment) for cross-border equity injection; the Gibraltar bank will require the board resolution authorising the sending bank, the capital escrow instruction, and the ESOP trust deed as the commercial backup. There is no mandatory Gibraltar exchange control formality or pre-clearance for such capital accounts, provided that the funding source is documented and free of sanctions exposure. Cash deposits are disallowed; only cleared wire transfers from the account-holder’s own external banking account are permitted.Standard clearing rails are SWIFT MT103/MT202 for cross-border employee distributions, UK CHAPS for sterling-denominated payroll wires to UK participants, TARGET2 via correspondent banks for euro-denominated settlements, and the Gibraltar-incorporated real-time gross settlement (RTGS) link to digibank for local sterling clearing. Commercial letters of credit for third-party service providers or share-purchase settlements are to be issued as a confirmed irrevocable documentary credit via a Gibraltar-based negotiating bank using the UCP600 rules under a SWIFT MT700. Employee incentive payments should be initiated as Direct Payroll Wire (ACH SDD or BACS) only when the offshore trust operates a UK payroll agent; otherwise SWIFT MT103 is the mandatory fallback. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% standard corporate income tax rate; a reduced 10% rate applies to qualifying regulated and fiduciary businesses, and a 20% rate applies to utilities, fuel distribution, tobacco and alcoholic beverages. An exempted ESOP administration company carrying on trust and fiduciary services in Gibraltar is assessable at the standard 12.5% trading rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. Gibraltar does not impose any withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar-resident company, including a Cayman exempted company managed and controlled in Gibraltar. Dividends paid out of taxed profits are exempt in the hands of shareholders of all jurisdictions, and no branch remittance tax is levied on distributions to non-resident shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Form CT-1 must be delivered within 12 months after the end of the relevant Year of Assessment. For an accounting period ending 31 December 2024 (falling within the Year of Assessment ending 30 June 2025), the statutory filing deadline is 30 June 2026. Provisional tax is payable in two instalments during the Year of Assessment based on the prior year's liability, with any balancing payment due with the return.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 (Identification) Gibraltar company registration number, registered name, registered office address, Gibraltar Income Tax Number and appointed tax agent details. For a Cayman exempted company registered as a foreign company in Gibraltar, insert the Gibraltar foreign company registration number (Part-L registration) and declare the local place of management, if any; leave the local incorporation number blank. Late filing attracts an automatic fixed penalty of £100 plus a daily penalty of £50 per day for continuing default (statutory cap £30,000). Inaccurate returns trigger penalties of up to 100% of the understated tax for negligent filing and up to 200% for deliberate concealment or fraud. Unpaid tax bears default interest at 8% per annum. The Commissioner may issue best-of-judgment estimated assessments, enforce recovery through debt proceedings, and the company may be struck off the Gibraltar register for persistent non-filing.
Box 2 (Year of Assessment and Basis Period) State the Year of Assessment and the precise accounting period start and end dates forming the basis period. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous Local File documentation applies where the aggregate annual value of cross-border controlled transactions exceeds GBP 1,000,000 or intra-group loans exceed GBP 2,000,000. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required where the multinational group has consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more. Below the monetary thresholds, a concise transfer pricing policy note must still be retained to evidence arm's length compliance during assessment.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations 2019 apply to every Gibraltar tax-resident company and foreign company registered in Gibraltar, including a Cayman exempted company administering an ESOP trust. The relevant activity is fund management and service-centre administration; the entity must demonstrate that all economically substantive activities are performed in Gibraltar through: (i) board meetings held and directed in Gibraltar; (ii) qualified local employees engaged on payroll; (iii) commensurate annual operating expenditure incurred in Gibraltar; and (iv) physical premises owned or leased in Gibraltar. An annual Economic Substance Return must be filed. The parallel Cayman economic substance filing cannot be relied on to satisfy the Gibraltar requirement. Non-compliance attracts penalties up to GBP 50,000, daily penalties of GBP 500, and possible striking-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporation Tax Evasion and Filing Fraud

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 200% of the tax understated plus default interest at 8% per annum. Criminal conviction under Gibraltar's Income Tax Act carries an unlimited fine and imprisonment for up to 7 years. The Commissioner may raise estimated assessments, freeze bank accounts, and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission may revoke the fiduciary licence.

Director personal liability: Directors, shadow directors and the ESOP trust's Gibraltar Senior Manager are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax where the company is used as a vehicle for evasion. Conviction exposes directors to imprisonment, disqualification from acting as a director for up to 15 years, and personal confiscation of assets under the Proceeds of Crime Act.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Regulations penalties up to GBP 50,000 plus GBP 500 for each day of continuing default. The Commissioner may deem the company to be managed and controlled in Gibraltar, assess the full worldwide income received, impose estimated assessments, and the registrar may strike off the Gibraltar registration with consequential loss of limited liability protection.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for penalty liabilities where they knowingly permitted the shell arrangement. Travel bans and exit controls may be imposed pending settlement of tax arrears. Directors of the Cayman registered company failing to file the Cayman economic substance return face Cayman sanctions, including imprisonment for up to 5 years, monetary penalties and restitution orders.
Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Failures

Statutory sanction: Offence under Gibraltar's Proceeds of Crime Act and Criminal Finances Act: unlimited fine and imprisonment for up to 14 years; confiscation of the entire trust asset pool. The Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit may impose regulatory fines up to 10% of group turnover and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission may suspend or revoke the fiduciary and trust administration licence.

Director personal liability: Each director and the Money Laundering Reporting Officer can be criminally prosecuted for failing to disclose suspicious activity. Personal civil liability to defrauded beneficiaries under the Gibraltar Trustee Ordinance includes restitution of trust property with interest. Personal asset freezes, travel bans and pre-charge restraint orders are standard enforcement measures.
Profit Repatriation and Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment substituting the arm's length consideration; additional assessments with penalties up to 100% of the adjustment; disallowance of excess interest deductions and royalty deductions; default interest at 8% per annum; and Country-by-Country reporting penalties for failure to file within the statutory deadline.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false transfer pricing documentation are criminally liable for false accounting, carrying imprisonment up to 10 years. On insolvency, Gibraltar corporation tax liabilities can be recovered personally from directors for wrongful trading and from shadow directors who engineered or knowingly participated in the artificial arrangements, with ancillary orders for civil restitution and travel bans.
