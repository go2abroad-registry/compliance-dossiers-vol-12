E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Cayman: maintain the Exempted Company at the Cayman Islands General Registry via annual return and government fees; no direct-tax registration exists in Cayman because it imposes no income or corporate tax; obtain the Cayman company number and, if a relevant activity is present, register for the TIA Economic Substance filing reference. Step 2 — Gibraltar: register the Cayman company as an external/oversea company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Gibraltar Companies Act, delivering the certificate of incorporation, memorandum/articles and registered-office evidence, and obtain the Gibraltar registration number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — non-resident Cayman Exempted Company is within the Gibraltar territorial income-tax net only for income accrued in or derived from Gibraltar (Income Tax Act 2010); all non-Gibraltar-source middleware royalties, licence fees and export software revenue are outside the charge to Gibraltar tax, while the Cayman domicile imposes zero direct tax. The entity is not a pass-through; it is an entity-level taxpayer solely for Gibraltar-source profits. For UK VAT the company is a non-established taxable person, chargeable only on UK business-to-consumer digital supplies.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no US-style check-the-box entity classification. Elections and mechanisms available: (1) claim/election of non-resident company status grounded on location of central management and control, confining chargeability to Gibraltar-source income; (2) application for Qualifying Company zero-rate certificates under the Income Tax Act — available only for designated activities such as headquarters, insurance, shipping and finance, and not for ISIC 6201 anti-cheat software middleware licensing, so a 0% certificate is unavailable here; (3) filings/elections under the Gibraltar Income Tax (International Tax Compliance) Directions implementing the OECD hybrid-mismatch framework, requiring notification of the Cayman vehicle's tax-transparency or non-transparency treatment to prevent double non-taxation; (4) under the Cayman Entities (Economic Substance) Law, annual election between demonstrating adequate Cayman substance or declaring tax residence in a qualifying foreign jurisdiction — a Gibraltar tax-residence declaration would forfeit territorial non-residence protection and is therefore not used where the exempted status is to be preserved; and (5) no UK VAT group or annual-accounting election is available to a non-established supplier.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

UK Value Added Tax (VAT) under the Value Added Tax Act 1994 for cross-border digital services; Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands have no VAT, GST or sales-and-use tax regime.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: no VAT registration regime exists and no threshold is applicable because it is a consumption-tax-free jurisdiction. Cayman: no VAT/GST and no registration threshold. United Kingdom: non-established taxable persons making any in-scope UK business-to-consumer supplies of digital services must VAT-register from the first supply with no de-minimis threshold; established UK businesses have a £90,000 rolling-12-month taxable-turnover registration threshold. Because the anti-cheat middleware is licensed primarily business-to-business to game studios, the customer's location fixes the place of supply and UK VAT registration is triggered only where UK-consumer digital supplies are made.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands levy no VAT/GST, so no domestic export zero-rating claim or refund mechanism exists for the anti-cheat engine; the benefit is structural in that exported digital middleware falls outside any consumption-tax net. If the company holds a UK VAT number: (i) business-to-business digital supplies to UK VAT-registered customers are subject to the domestic reverse charge, with the supplier issuing an invoice without UK VAT and the customer self-accounting; (ii) supplies to customers outside the UK are outside the scope of UK VAT because the place of supply is the customer's country; (iii) no UK zero-rating certificate is required for electronically supplied services; and (iv) UK input VAT may be recovered only through the UK VAT return if registered or through the HMRC non-established-business refund route otherwise. Supporting evidence proving customer location and export consideration should be retained to rebut reverse-charge and compliance challenges.

  • Customs Export Declaration (UK CDS export entry or Gibraltar trade-statistics declaration) — required only where tangible media or hardware incorporating the kernel module is exported; pure software licences are outside customs procedure
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / courier proof of export for any physical media shipments
  • Signed software licence, end-user licence agreement and anti-cheat middleware service agreement identifying the customer's tax jurisdiction
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 / bank statement evidencing receipt of export consideration
  • Place-of-supply and customer-location evidence (IP logs, customer VAT number, billing address, payment routing data)
  • IP royalty documentation and transfer-pricing benchmarking file for the Cayman/Gibraltar intercompany licence

Gibraltar and Cayman impose no electronic-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, API clearance or continuous-transaction-control mandate. The United Kingdom likewise operates no government e-invoicing clearance platform; however, a UK VAT-registered supplier must keep digital VAT records and file returns via HMRC-recognized Making Tax Digital (MTD) compatible software using HMRC application programming interfaces. No government fiscal seal or QR-code clearance is required for invoices issued from Gibraltar.

UK VAT: deliberate or fraudulent export/zero-rating and refund claims attract a penalty up to 100% of the tax (VATA 1994 s.60/s.61), a deliberate-inaccuracy penalty up to 100% under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007, interest, and criminal liability under VATA 1994 s.72 with unlimited fines and imprisonment; retrospective assessments may extend to 20 years for fraud or failure to notify. Gibraltar Income Tax Act: false statements or omission of Gibraltar-source income attract a penalty up to 100% of the under-assessed tax, interest and criminal sanctions for tax fraud, and the Commissioner may reopen assessments up to 20 years where fraud is established and 6 years otherwise. Cayman: false economic-substance declarations attract a penalty up to KYD 20,000 per failure, possible director disqualification and striking-off of the exempted company. Although no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline exists in Gibraltar or Cayman, the 180-day benchmark is applied by HMRC and payment-rail AML reviewers as a commercial-substance indicator; failure to substantiate export invoicing, customer location and inward remittance triggers the foregoing clawback and penalty regimes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Cayman Islands Dollar (KYD) is hard-pegged at 0.80 KYD = 1.00 USD and is freely convertible for external or non-resident entities; the Cayman Exempted Company must secure an Exchange Control non-resident designation at incorporation through its registered office, which releases the entity from all exchange-control restrictions and permits unrestricted foreign-currency accounts, foreign-currency borrowing, and free dividend or profit remittance without CIMA approval. The Gibraltar Pound circulates at par with GBP and is 100% backed by Bank of England notes; no Gibraltar exchange controls apply to current-account flows (software licence fees, intercompany royalties) or capital-account flows (equity subscriptions, loan repayments, dividends). The United Kingdom has maintained full current- and capital-account convertibility since the 1979 repeal of the Exchange Control Act 1947; no UK Treasury or Bank of England approval is required for any remittance. The practical residual constraint is reputational rather than regulatory: the onboarding bank will require proof of the CIMA non-resident designation and genuine Gibraltar economic substance to confirm the structure is not a dormant offshore shell.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timelines for export proceeds in the Cayman Islands (where the exempted company holds external non-resident status), Gibraltar, or the United Kingdom. Export receipts from anti-cheat middleware licence fees may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, GBP, or GIP; no mandatory conversion ratio applies and no central-bank approval is required for holding or repatriating such proceeds. Administrative filings are limited to the Cayman General Registry annual return and the Cayman economic-substance notification (where applicable), classifying the entity as tax-exempt and external for exchange-control purposes; a Gibraltar-registered branch or subsidiary would additionally file under the Gibraltar Companies Act. Recommended treasury policy: consolidate export receipts in a single USD settlement account (Stripe/Wise) and sweep to the Gibraltar operating account on a daily basis to preserve an audit trail, with intercompany re-invoicing to the Cayman IP-holder only where a documented transfer-pricing file exists.

Central bank supervision: Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA), acting as the designated Exchange Control Authority under the Currency and Exchange Controls Act (Act 14 of 1961) and the Exchange Control Regulations 1961; Bank of England (BoE) as the ultimate sovereign monetary authority for the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) under the statutory 1:1 GBP peg; Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as licensing and AML/CFT supervisor for Gibraltar-incorporated banks and e-money institutions; Prudential Regulation Authority and Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) of the United Kingdom as home-state regulators for UK clearing banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No general Gibraltar statutory revenue threshold has been identified that automatically triggers a standard Local File or Master File for every taxpayer. Gibraltar applies an arm's-length requirement to relevant related-party dealings and expects documentation proportionate to transaction complexity and risk. For country-by-country reporting, the usual multinational-group threshold is EUR 750 million consolidated revenue, or the applicable sterling equivalent and local implementation threshold, where the group is otherwise within the reporting rules.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's Economic Substance Act 2019 and related regulations apply to Gibraltar tax-resident entities and relevant activities, including geographically mobile activities such as intellectual-property holding, financing and leasing, headquarters, distribution and service-centre activities, and other prescribed activities. The entity must conduct core income-generating activities in Gibraltar, be adequately directed and managed in Gibraltar, maintain appropriate qualified employees and premises, and incur adequate operating expenditure. A gaming anti-cheat software business may fall within intellectual-property or relevant service categories depending on its legal rights and activities. Development, enhancement, maintenance, protection, and exploitation functions must be mapped under the DEMPE framework. High-risk intellectual-property arrangements face enhanced evidentiary scrutiny. A Cayman exempted company is not automatically subject to Gibraltar ESR merely because it uses Gibraltar service providers, banking, or directors, but Gibraltar tax residence, a Gibraltar permanent establishment, or a relevant Gibraltar entity can trigger analysis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Reporting

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax, statutory interest, civil penalties, enforcement costs, and potential criminal prosecution. Penalties depend on the applicable Gibraltar tax provision, intent, tax shortfall, and cooperation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers may face personal criminal prosecution, fines, imprisonment where prescribed, disqualification, and personal liability for deliberate misconduct or statutory breaches. Civil tax debt does not ordinarily transfer automatically to directors, and veil piercing requires a legally recognized basis such as fraud, agency, unlawful conduct, or statutory personal liability. Travel restrictions may arise from criminal or enforcement proceedings but are not an automatic tax penalty.
Artificial Permanent-Establishment Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar may impose tax on attributable profits, interest, penalties, assessments, and transfer-pricing adjustments. Deliberate concealment may result in criminal proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors may incur personal exposure for knowing participation in fraudulent filings, obstruction, or breach of statutory duties. Ordinary corporate tax liability remains primarily with the company unless a statutory or fraud-based exception applies.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustments, denial of deductions, secondary tax consequences, interest, penalties, information requests, and possible dispute or corresponding-adjustment procedures.

Director personal liability: Personal liability may arise for knowingly approving fraudulent records or unlawful distributions. Directors should maintain intercompany agreements, functional analyses, DEMPE evidence, benchmarking, invoices, and board approvals.
Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Annual information filings, exchange of information, monetary penalties, increased penalties for continued failure, and potential restriction or denial of the intended tax position under Gibraltar substance legislation.

Director personal liability: Responsible officers may face personal penalties for knowingly false submissions or failure to provide required information. A substance failure alone does not automatically pierce the corporate veil or create automatic imprisonment.
Shell Company, Beneficial Ownership, and Nominee Misuse

Statutory sanction: Corporate-service-provider escalation, beneficial-ownership enforcement, account suspension, tax reassessment, asset restraint, administrative fines, and criminal sanctions where fraud or laundering is established.

Director personal liability: Directors, beneficial owners, and nominees may face criminal fines, imprisonment, disqualification, confiscation, and civil restitution. Travel restrictions may be imposed by competent authorities in connection with criminal proceedings or enforcement orders.
Anti-Money-Laundering and Counter-Terrorist-Financing

Statutory sanction: Account freezing, suspicious-activity reporting, regulatory enforcement, substantial monetary penalties, confiscation, and criminal prosecution under applicable Gibraltar, Cayman, United Kingdom, or payment-service-provider rules.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may face personal fines, imprisonment, disqualification, and confiscation exposure for consent, connivance, neglect, or failure to maintain required controls. A company cannot lawfully rely on its low-risk banking classification to avoid enhanced review.
Intellectual-Property and Software Mischaracterization

Statutory sanction: Tax adjustments, denied deductions, withholding disputes, infringement damages, injunctions, contractual termination, and potential criminal sanctions for intentional infringement or fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for knowing infringement, fraudulent representations, or breach of fiduciary and statutory duties. Corporate limited liability generally remains effective for genuine, properly documented commercial claims.
Cybersecurity, Privacy, and Unauthorized System Access

Statutory sanction: Regulatory investigation, data-protection penalties, contractual damages, injunctions, service termination, and criminal prosecution under applicable Gibraltar, United Kingdom, European, or customer-jurisdiction law.

Director personal liability: Individuals may face criminal liability for unauthorized access, unlawful interception, fraud, or knowing privacy violations. Directors may also face regulatory sanctions for governance failures, while corporate liability depends on the applicable statute and conduct.
Unlawful Dividend or Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: Repayment or restoration of unlawful distributions, creditor claims, insolvency proceedings, director-duty claims, and potential penalties for false solvency statements.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to repay amounts, contribute to the insolvent estate, and face disqualification or criminal liability for reckless or fraudulent conduct. A distribution from a Cayman company must be tested under Cayman corporate and solvency rules, not only Gibraltar tax rules.
Unregistered Gibraltar Business Activity

Statutory sanction: Backdated registration, tax assessments, payroll and social-security liabilities, penalties, interest, licensing consequences, and enforcement action.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal liability for unpaid employee deductions, payroll taxes, deliberate non-registration, or statutory reporting failures. Imprisonment is fact-specific and generally requires an underlying criminal offense rather than mere late registration.
Sanctions, Export-Control, and Restricted-Customer Breach

Statutory sanction: Asset freezes, transaction prohibitions, payment termination, civil monetary penalties, criminal prosecution, confiscation, and loss of banking access under applicable United Kingdom, Gibraltar, Cayman, United States, or other relevant sanctions regimes.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible employees may face criminal fines, imprisonment, disqualification, and asset-freezing consequences for knowing or reckless violations. Reliance on payment-processor approval does not replace sanctions screening.
