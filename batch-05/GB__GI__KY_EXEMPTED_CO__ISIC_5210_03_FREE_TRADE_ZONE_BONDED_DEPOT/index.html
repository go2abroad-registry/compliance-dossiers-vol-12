Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a Gibraltar incorporated entity (including a branch or a re-domiciled foreign company), the Companies Act 2014, s. 89 requires a physical registered office address in Gibraltar. Virtual office (non-physical, mailbox-only) is NOT acceptable for this industry (Bonded Warehouse / Free Trade Zone) because the GFSC and HM Customs require an actual operational presence, a designated licensed officer, and an available physical filing location for customs declarations and inspection notifications. However, a shared physical office within a licensed business center or a professional trustee company's office in Gibraltar is permissible, provided it has a physical reception, staff presence, and secure document storage. The registered office must be operational during business hours to receive formal legal process and customs notices.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar - Income Tax Office (ITO) and HM Customs Gibraltar; official portal: www.gibraltar.gov.gi (ITO e-filing: tax.gov.gi).

E-government filing portal

Cayman Exempted Company (foreign entity) first registers as an oversea company with Gibraltar Companies House under the Companies Act 2014; then applies to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar TIN using Form T1-RF, attaching certified certificate of incorporation, register of directors, constitutional documents, and proof of business address in Gibraltar. Separate registration with HM Customs Gibraltar for an EORI/customs trader ID is required for bonded warehouse operations in the free trade zone. Processing time is circa 10-21 working days. No VAT registration threshold applies because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not provide a check-the-box regime. Available elections include: (i) application for Certified Company status (excluded from income tax on non-Gibraltar-source income); (ii) election under the Income Tax Act sections 26/29 for territorial source treatment; (iii) OECD/G20 BEPS-compliant hybrid mismatch election (Gibraltar is within UK's controlled list for double-tax relief; no US check-the-box applies to the Cayman Exempted Company).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None (No VAT/GST; Gibraltar customs duty and purchase tax regime only)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A - no VAT/GST regime in Gibraltar; bonded depot requires HM Customs approval and financial guarantee irrespective of turnover

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT export zero-rating mechanism because no VAT is chargeable. Removal of bonded goods from the Free Trade Zone to third-country ports is treated as an export free of duty and tax; supply must be evidenced by customs outward processing declarations (SAD Ex 6) and physical exit from Gibraltar customs territory within the prescribed three-month period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / NCAD Ex-6)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document
  • Bonded warehouse removal certificate (EDW-WR-7) duly endorsed by warehouse keeper
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT proof of receipt of export proceeds

Post-Brexit, goods moving through Gibraltar Free Trade Zone must be declared in the Gibraltar Customs Single Window (GCSW) using electronic SAD/ICS/AES formats; real-time frontier clearance and bond-status reporting via customs electronic interface (API). No national e-invoicing or fiscal stamping regime is in force for Gibraltar domestic supplies.

HM Customs Gibraltar may reclaim exempted duties and tax plus interest, with civil penalties up to 100% of the evaded amount under the Customs (Penalties and Associated Provisions) Regulations; fraudulent export declarations attract criminal liability with custodial sentences up to 7 years under the Taxation (Fraudulent Declarations) Act. Documentary proof must be retained for six years for retrospective audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar is a British Overseas Territory with no independent central bank. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is legal tender and is pegged to the pound sterling (GBP) at a fixed 1:1 parity, with sterling also accepted as legal tender. There are no exchange-control restrictions on current or capital account transactions. Since the territory operates under the UK's international financial sanctions and trade framework, both residents and non-resident corporate entities such as the Cayman Exempted Company may hold balances in GIP, GBP, EUR, or USD and may remit dividends, licence fees, trade settlements, and capital repatriation without prior exchange-control approval or repatriation taxes. Profit remittance is unfettered except for sanctions screening and AML compliance. No special hurdles apply to repatriation of proceeds from bonded-warehouse free-trade-zone storage activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export-proceeds surrender regime exists in Gibraltar or in the UK. The statutory timeline is N/A and the mandatory conversion ratio is 0%. Exporters receiving invoices denominated in GIP, GBP, EUR, or any freely convertible currency may retain 100% of the proceeds in any permitted currency without obligation to convert, repatriate within a prescribed timeframe, or deposit with any designated authorised dealer.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (de facto monetary authority for the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland) / Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the statutory banking and conduct regulator / HM Government of Gibraltar Commissioner of Currency (issuer of Gibraltar pound notes and coins)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection is executed via SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) or MT202 (bank-to-bank cover) from the Cayman exempted company's treasury or the ultimate group account to the Gibraltar bank's Capital Escrow Account. The wire instruction must reference a purpose code corresponding to equity/capital contribution, accompanied by (1) a Cayman board resolution approving the capital allocation to the Gibraltar bonded-depot project, (2) a share-allotment or subscription contract if the injection is equity, (3) certified source-of-funds statements from the remitting bank, and (4) a compliance novation letter confirming that the funds are not derived from proceeds of crime or sanctions-restricted activity. Use a Tier-1 correspondent chain through London (CHAPS/FPS for GBP) or New York/UK correspondents for USD; maintain per-cargo escrow release triggers that condition disbursement on receipt of the Gibraltar bonded-depot licence and customs approval. Direct transfers from unlicensed money-service businesses or non-bank fintech wallets should be rejected.Outbound trade settlements are cleared primarily via SWIFT MT103 transfers for bonded-warehouse inventory purchases, freight, customs duty top-ups, and third-party logistics payments. For large or documentary trade obligations, the Gibraltar bank's trade-finance desk issues Commercial Letters of Credit (documentary credits under UCP600) or standby letters of credit, with funds cleared through MT798 or local telex-authenticated LC advices. Local clearing in GBP is run through CHAPS/FPS (with participation via the UK correspondent), EUR clearing through TARGET2 via the bank's euro correspondent, and USD through the US clearing system (Fedwire/CHIPS) via New York correspondent banks. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GBP 1,000,000 (annual aggregate value of controlled transactions in the Gibraltar PE) triggers mandatory Local File documentation; Master File applies for MNE groups with consolidated turnover of at least GBP 50,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting applies at the OECD EUR 750,000,000 threshold. The bonded warehouse depot must in all cases prepare and retain a benchmarking or comparability file for storage and logistics service fees.

Economic substance scope: The Companies (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019 and associated legislation apply to the Cayman Exempted Company if it carries on a relevant activity through a Gibraltar branch. Bonded warehousing for related parties falls under 'distribution and service centre business'. The test requires: (a) the activity is undertaken by a locally managed workforce; (b) premises are adequate and exclusive; (c) the core income-generating activity occurs in Gibraltar; and (d) no gross margin leakage exists without transfer pricing justification. If the company is tax resident in Cayman, the Cayman ESR exclusion applies only where another jurisdiction (here Gibraltar) subjects the PE to tax at 12.5%.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Oblique Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 100% of the under-assessed tax plus statutory interest at 6% per annum; on conviction in the Supreme Court of Gibraltar, a fine of up to GBP 5,000 per charge and imprisonment for up to 7 years; forfeiture of goods or the bonded warehouse licence.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Cayman Exempted Company are jointly and severally liable for penalties and tax restitution; courts may issue travel bans, asset freezes, and 15-year disqualification orders against directors found to have signed false returns.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the taxpayer as a shell, removal of the free-zone licence, assessment of avoided tax on the whole group profit attributable to the depot, ES penalties up to GBP 10,000 plus daily GBP 500, and strike-off under the Companies Act.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for tax arrears under anti-avoidance legislation; travel bans and citizenship-request protocols apply to directors who fail to cooperate with the ESR investigator; nominee shareholders face similar liability.
Trade-Based Money Laundering / Customs and Sanctions Fraud

Statutory sanction: Offence under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and Customs legislation: fines up to GBP 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years; warrant, confiscation and forfeiture of goods and bank accounts; revocation of the Free Trade Zone and Bonded Warehousing licence by HM Customs Gibraltar.

Director personal liability: Directors can be prosecuted as principals where they directed the scheme; the corporate veil is pierced, exposing personal assets to confiscation; travel bans and extradition cooperation with the UK NCA are available.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Secondary adjustment increasing Gibraltar chargeable profits by the arm's-length differential, disallowance of the management fee deduction, penalty up to 20% of the adjusted tax plus interest from the filing deadline.

Director personal liability: Directors must certify the transfer pricing policy annually; failure renders them liable to personal assessments for the additional tax and penalty and to disqualification as officers of a Gibraltar-regulated company.
