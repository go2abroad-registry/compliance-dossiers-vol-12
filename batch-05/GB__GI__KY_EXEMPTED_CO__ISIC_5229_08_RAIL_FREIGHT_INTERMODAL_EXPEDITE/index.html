Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar, 8 Carlton House, Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax); official digital portal: Income Tax e-services module of www.gibraltar.gov.gi. UK VAT is administered separately by HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Cayman Exempted Company as an overseas company in Gibraltar under Part 11 of the Companies Act 2014, obtaining a Gibraltar Companies House registration number. Step 2: File Form 1 (Notice of Chargeability) with the Income Tax Office, accompanied by certified charter documents (Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles, register of directors and members, Gibraltar registered-office confirmation, and AML/KYC source-of-funds declarations). Step 3: Obtain the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN), generally formatted as 'G' followed by the Companies House number, required where the entity derives Gibraltar-source income, files the annual levy, or seeks tax-residency certification; a non-resident entity with no Gibraltar-source activity is not statutorily required to hold a TIN. Step 4: If UK VAT is triggered, register separately with HMRC under Form VAT1 (VATA 1994, Schedule 1), using either a UK establishment or the Non-Established Taxable Person route; the Gibraltar TIN is not interchangeable with a UK VAT registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not adopt the US check-the-box fiscal-transparency election. Available statutory elections and regimes include: (i) Qualifying Company election under the 2018 Gibraltar qualifying-company legislation, permitting a fixed-rate tax election at 5% of chargeable profits with a participation exemption on qualifying shareholdings; (ii) Annual Levy election (Form DE1), substituting fixed duty in the statutory range of GIP 1,000 to GIP 5,500 for income tax in respect of dormant or foreign-owned companies; (iii) the elective three-year fixed-rate rule under section 132A of the Income Tax Act 2010; (iv) treaty-tie-breaker and mutual-agreement elections under the UK-Gibraltar and Cayman Islands-Gibraltar arrangements; and (v) mandatory ATAD-aligned anti-hybrid rules with no elective opt-out, so hybrid-mismatch relief cannot be elected for a Cayman exempted entity. UK VAT elections, such as option to tax on land, may separately apply to the taxable business.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar administers no VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax; Gibraltar is outside the UK and EU VAT territories, and its indirect fiscal regime is limited to customs import duties, excise duties on hydrocarbons, alcohol, and tobacco, and stamp duties. For transcontinental rail-freight exports originating in or transiting the United Kingdom, the applicable indirect tax system is UK VAT under the Value Added Tax Act 1994.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable within Gibraltar (no VAT/GST registration threshold exists). If the Cayman Exempted Company supplies freight-forwarding or international transport services in the UK in the course of business, mandatory UK VAT registration arises when taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 (effective 1 April 2024) over a rolling 12-month period; voluntary UK VAT registration is permitted below that threshold. A Gibraltar-registered overseas company is also liable to the annual levy irrespective of turnover, capped at GIP 5,500 per annum.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

No local Gibraltar zero-rating applies. Under UK VAT, the Cayman Exempted Company may apply a 0% rate to: (i) export of goods physically removed from the UK to a destination outside the UK under VATA 1994, Schedule 8, Group 7, provided export occurs within three months of supply and valid commercial and customs evidence is retained; (ii) international transport of goods by rail (including the UK rail leg plus the non-UK leg) from the UK to a place outside the UK under Group 15; and (iii) freight-forwarding, customs handling, container stuffing, transshipment, and ancillary services supplied to another business customer in connection with such international transport, where the service is closely linked to the cross-border movement. An entirely domestic UK rail leg that is not part of a single international transport contract remains standard-rated at 20%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Export Declaration / SAD with Movement Reference Number and export inventory records)
  • Bill of Lading or CIM/SMGS Rail Consignment Note evidencing cross-border movement from the UK to a non-UK destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate confirming receipt of SWIFT wire transfer or letter-of-credit settlement against the export invoice

None in Gibraltar: no real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoice API platform, or digital stamping requirement is imposed. UK VAT-recorded persons must maintain digital records under Making Tax Digital, file quarterly electronic VAT returns (Form VAT100), and retain e-invoices for six years, but HMRC does not operate real-time invoice pre-clearance; customs declarations remain separate from invoice clearance.

Export zero-rating is an exception, not an entitlement. HMRC may retrospectively deny zero-rating and assess unpaid VAT, interest, and penalties for up to four years, extended to twenty years in cases of deliberate non-disclosure. The 0% rate is clawed back where the 180-day FX repatriation deadline is missed, the customs evidence bundle is incomplete, the goods are diverted for UK consumption, or the freight forwarder acts as principal rather than disclosed agent. Civil consequences include default surcharge, careless-error penalties of up to 30% of the VAT lost, deliberate-and-concealed penalties of up to 100%, and liability of responsible officers. Criminal exposure includes fraudulent evasion of VAT under section 72 VATA 1994 and Cheating the Public Revenue, while the Gibraltar Income Tax Office may impose late-filing penalties and revoke qualifying-company or exempt status upon material misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full de jure convertibility on both current and capital account: the Exchange Control Act 1947 was repealed in 1979, and neither the United Kingdom, Gibraltar, nor the Cayman Islands imposes approval-based exchange controls, mandatory surrender, or capital-repatriation restrictions. Profit remittance from the Gibraltar clearing operation to the Cayman shareholder is free of Gibraltar withholding tax and FX-authorisation requirements, subject only to audited financial statements, a valid board declaration, the Gibraltar Ultimate Beneficial Ownership register filing, and standard correspondent-bank due diligence. The only practical hurdles are bank de-risking for Cayman-OFC structures, OFAC/UK-Sanctions screening of USD and Eurasian rail counterparties, and the absence of direct CHAPS membership for most Gibraltar banks, which forces clearing through UK/EU correspondents.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil statutory requirement. There is no timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export, freight, or logistics receivables. Proceeds may be held in any invoice currency (EUR, USD, GBP, CNY/CNH, CHF) and converted at the Group's discretion. Banks may apply internal policies converting excess non-base balances within 30 to 90 days under Basel III liquidity-risk management, and any proceeds connected to a sanctioned Russian or Belarusian rail/freight counterparty must be frozen and reported to HM Treasury's Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI).

Central bank supervision: There is no separate Gibraltar central bank; monetary and FX authority rests with the Governor and Company of the Bank of England (Bank of England), while HM Treasury is the policy custodian for UK exchange-control remnants and financial sanctions. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory licensing and AML/CFT supervisor for Gibraltar banks and payment institutions. The Cayman Exempted Company's home regulator is the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA), which administers a currency-board regime with no exchange-control function.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for a Cayman Exempted Company under Gibraltar supervision. Since Brexit, UK-authorised EMIs do not passport into Gibraltar and Gibraltar-authorised payment firms do not benefit from EEA passporting. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) commonly reject direct onboarding of Cayman Exempted companies because of closed-loop bank counterparty risk, inability to evidence economic substance, and sanctions exposure in Eurasian rail corridors. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 of aggregate related-party transactions per accounting period triggers a mandatory Gibraltar Local File; Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance (Companies and Construction) Regulations 2019 apply because the company is managed and controlled in Gibraltar. Freight forwarding is treated as a distribution and service centre relevant activity; the company must show board meetings and management in Gibraltar, adequate physical premises, full-time qualified employees, and core income-generating activities performed in Gibraltar. An annual ESR return is mandatory. Because the shareholder is a Cayman exempted company, parallel economic substance monitoring by Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands applies; artificial routing of rail freight income through the Cayman entity is disregarded under the substance-over-form doctrine.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Prosecution for fraudulent evasion of income tax and cheating the public revenue: fine at the discretion of the court and/or imprisonment for up to 7 years; civil penalty up to 100% of the tax evaded plus statutory interest at 8% per annum; denial of treaty relief and automatic exchange of information with HMRC.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced; directors who signed false returns face personal civil restitution for the unpaid tax, disqualification as directors for up to 15 years, travel bans, and confiscation of personal assets under the Criminal Justice (Confiscation) framework.
Shell Company / Zero Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic substance penalties of up to GIP 10,000 plus daily GIP 500 default penalties; cancellation of Gibraltar tax residence; strike-off from the Companies Register; adverse information automatically shared with HMRC and the Cayman authorities; loss of banking relationships and SWIFT or letter-of-credit facilities.

Director personal liability: Directors knowingly managing an economic substance non-compliant entity are subject to personal fines, director disqualification orders, and personal liability for wrongful trading in insolvency; courts may impose travel restrictions pending compliance.
Trade-Based Money Laundering / AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Conviction on indictment under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar): imprisonment for up to 14 years and an unlimited fine; civil recovery and confiscation orders over proceeds; freezing orders on corporate bank accounts; revocation of banking and financial services permissions.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated AML compliance officer face personal criminal prosecution, passport surrender and travel bans, confiscation of personal accounts, and disqualification as corporate directors for up to 15 years.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / False Documentation

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment by the Comptroller of Income Tax; penalty of 30% to 100% of the underpaid tax; statutory interest at 8% per annum; late-payment surcharge up to 10%; joint referral to HMRC for a coordinated UK-Gibraltar investigation.

Director personal liability: The director who signed the return is jointly liable for the additional tax where the misstatement was made knowingly; the corporate veil is pierced if the arrangement had no economic substance and was designed solely to divert profits; civil restitution and prohibition orders follow.
