Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar, 6-8 Harkness Court, Watergardens, Gibraltar; online portal at https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation; customs and indirect tax administration by HM Customs Gibraltar.

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company seeking tax registration in Gibraltar must first notify the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies if it establishes a place of business or branch. For corporate income tax, it must register with the Income Tax Office using Form GI-CO01, attaching the Cayman certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, and evidence of the place of central management and control. The Commissioner of Income Tax assigns a Gibraltar TIN once the foreign entity is registered as an overseas company or has a permanent establishment in Gibraltar. Because Gibraltar does not levy VAT or GST, there is no VAT registration ID; importers and exporters must separately apply to HM Customs Gibraltar for a Customs Registration and Authorisation Number for customs declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not adopt a 'check-the-box' election regime. A foreign-incorporated entity may, however, elect under section 2(4) of the Income Tax Act 2018 to be treated as a Gibraltar resident company if central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar. Alternatively, a non-resident Cayman exempted company with no permanent establishment may file a 'transparency election' to have its Gibraltar-source income computed on a territorial basis. The Income Tax (Special Elections) Regulations also permit exemption from tax on passive income where the entity holds a trade exemption certificate approved by the Commissioner.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Customs and Excise Duties (No VAT; No GST; export duty suspensions)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; Gibraltar levies no VAT or GST. For customs and excise purposes, any business importing commercial goods into Gibraltar must register with HM Customs Gibraltar and obtain an authorisation to make declarations. Economic turnover thresholds do not trigger registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Since Gibraltar has no VAT, exports are not zero-rated. Instead, goods physically exported from Gibraltar are excluded from the scope of Gibraltar customs and excise duties, provided the exporter is authorised by HM Customs Gibraltar, the goods are exported by an approved carrier, and the export is accompanied by a valid electronic export declaration. Services supplied to non-residents from Gibraltar do not attract any Gibraltar consumption tax; there is no VAT refund mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic, submitted through the Gibraltar Customs Electronic Declaration System)
  • Commercial Invoice (two copies)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or CMR movement document
  • Certificate of Origin (UK/Gibraltar preferential source where applicable)
  • Sanitary and phytosanitary certificate for self-heating food product
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment evidence proving export proceeds remittance

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time electronic invoice clearance, digital stamping, or API-based fiscal control. Electronic declarations for imports and exports are submitted through the Gibraltar Customs Electronic Declaration System ('GCEDS') using the UK/Gibraltar Goods Movement Procedure manual and the Harmonised Tariff schedule. No third-party clearance repository is required.

Fraudulent or incorrect export relief claims are retrospectively clawed back. HM Customs Gibraltar may assess unpaid customs and excise duty on the full value of the goods, impose a penalty of 100% of the duty evaded, and charge statutory interest at 1% above bank base rate from the date of export. Criminal conviction for fraudulent export declaration may lead to a fine not exceeding £10,000 and/or imprisonment for up to seven years under the Customs Ordinance (Cap. 112).

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains a fully convertible currency with the Gibraltar pound pegged at 1:1 to sterling. Both the current account and the capital account are free from exchange-control restrictions. Non-resident Cayman shareholders may receive dividends and loan repayments in any major currency without prior exchange approval. The only practical hurdles are anti-money-laundering source-of-funds reviews and Gibraltar Profit Tax / UK administrative cooperation filings where the company is tax resident or has substance in Gibraltar.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Gibraltar does not impose a statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio under foreign exchange law. Export receipts may be held indefinitely in their original currency or converted at the commercial market rate. For banking compliance, each inward receipt must be backed by an export invoice, bill of lading / air waybill, and a licensed banker's record of the underlying transaction.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of England (United Kingdom) is the ultimate sovereign monetary authority for sterling; Gibraltar has no separate central bank. The Government of Gibraltar's Currency Commissioner issues Gibraltar pound banknotes under the Currency Notes Act, and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) regulates banking and investment activities, including FX intermediation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-Low. Gibraltar does not maintain a UK-style statutory e-money institution passport; e-money and payment activities are controlled under the Financial Services Act 2019 and require GFSC authorisation or a permitted, ring-fenced agency arrangement through a licensed bank. UK and EU EMIs do not automatically passport into Gibraltar after Brexit. A digital EMI such as Wise or Revolut can support ancillary global payment collection and retail conversions, but it cannot replace a licensed Gibraltar bank for clearing, escrow, trade settlements, or multi-currency liquidity. Consequently, the entity should use EMIs only as supplementary collection rails, not as the primary treasury infrastructure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity or shareholder loan capital from the Cayman parent should be remitted by SWIFT MT103 to the Gibraltar bank's designated account using the parent bank's BIC/IBAN or Gibraltar sort code with remittance information coded as 'SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION' or 'SUBSCRIBED CAPITAL' and the appropriate proprietary purpose code for intercompany equity injection. The remittance must be accompanied by a board resolution from the Cayman entity, certified copy of the share subscription or allotment, and source-of-funds confirmation. The Gibraltar treasury team must verify that the proceeds are not routed through a private digital-exchange wallet or an unregulated third-party aggregator; correspondent banks typically require the ordering bank to be an established licensed institution in the Cayman Islands or the United Kingdom.Trade settlements for procurement of packaging, chemicals, food ingredients, and finished self-heating product should be executed through licensed Gibraltar clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for supplier payments below threshold values requiring FX pre-approval, referencing the supplier master file and invoice number; (ii) SWIFT MT202 COV for USD and EUR correspondent interbank transfers where a nostro concentration count is used; (iii) documentary import/export letters of credit issued by the Gibraltar bank or a London correspondent, with confirming banks handling the add-on confirmations for beneficiary banks in Asia; and (iv) standby commercial LCs for advance-payment or performance-secured structures.
