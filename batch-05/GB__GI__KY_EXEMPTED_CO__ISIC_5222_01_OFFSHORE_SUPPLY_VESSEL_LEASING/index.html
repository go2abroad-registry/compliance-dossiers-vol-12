Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Commissioner of Income Tax, HM Government of Gibraltar (Income Tax Office) for direct taxation; Companies House Gibraltar (part of the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission group) for company registration; HM Customs of Gibraltar for import/export duties. Portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax and https://www.csig.gi.

        
E-government filing portal
A Cayman Exempted Company operating offshore supply vessel leasing via a Gibraltar service presence must: (1) register at Companies House Gibraltar as an external company (Part 18, Companies Act 2014) or obtain a trade licence under the Gibraltar Trade, Business, Licences and Services Act; (2) apply to the Income Tax Office on Form CT-1 for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN), supplying the Cayman certificate of incorporation, Cayman Tax Concessions Act exemption certificate, memorandum of association, registered-office particulars, and proof of central management and control location; (3) obtain an HM Customs of Gibraltar Reference Number (GRN) for vessel stores and export declarations; (4) where vessels fly the Gibraltar flag, file with the Gibraltar Maritime Administration (Gibraltar Shipping Registry) for a ship identification number and radio licence. A TIN is issued within 7-10 business days; no notarisation is required if documents are apostilled under the Hague Convention.

      

      
Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A Cayman Exempted Company operating offshore supply vessel leasing via a Gibraltar service presence must: (1) register at Companies House Gibraltar as an external company (Part 18, Companies Act 2014) or obtain a trade licence under the Gibraltar Trade, Business, Licences and Services Act; (2) apply to the Income Tax Office on Form CT-1 for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN), supplying the Cayman certificate of incorporation, Cayman Tax Concessions Act exemption certificate, memorandum of association, registered-office particulars, and proof of central management and control location; (3) obtain an HM Customs of Gibraltar Reference Number (GRN) for vessel stores and export declarations; (4) where vessels fly the Gibraltar flag, file with the Gibraltar Maritime Administration (Gibraltar Shipping Registry) for a ship identification number and radio licence. A TIN is issued within 7-10 business days; no notarisation is required if documents are apostilled under the Hague Convention.

    

    

      
Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Tax_Exempt_Zone

        
Available election forms
The Cayman Exempted Company may elect for a 20-year tax-exemption certificate under Section 6 of the Cayman Islands Tax Concessions Act (2021 revision), guaranteeing no direct or indirect taxes in the Cayman Islands. In Gibraltar, there is no check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch election under the Gibraltar Income Tax (Hybrids) Rules, which conform to the OECD/G20 BEPS Report. Where the entity is managed and controlled outside Gibraltar, it remains non-resident and outside Gibraltar corporation tax. If central management and control is found in Gibraltar, the entity may apply for the flat-rate 'qualifying company' deemed-profits arrangement or otherwise be charged at the Gibraltar standard corporate rate of 12.5% on Gibraltar-source income.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
Gibraltar does not impose VAT or GST. Indirect taxation comprises import and excise duties under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance, and a separate accommodation/services tax on hotel and restaurant supplies. Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and maintains its own tariff schedule post-Brexit.

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Not applicable for VAT (no VAT regime). For customs purposes, every importer or service provider moving dutiable goods into Gibraltar must hold a Customs GRN; there is no minimum turnover threshold. Vessel operators under the Offshore Marine Charter model must register each chartered vessel under the Gibraltar Shipping (Fees) Ordinance, file a temporary importation notification for non-Gibraltar-flagged units, and provide a port-clearance guarantee where duty on stores or spares exceeds GIP 1,000.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Since Gibraltar has no VAT, zero-rating is replaced by customs-duty relief and deemed-export treatment. A 0% duty/refund position applies to: (1) goods physically exported from Gibraltar; (2) stores, bunkers, spare parts and provisions supplied to a vessel engaged in offshore or international trade while within Gibraltar port; (3) the supply of an offshore platform support vessel under a bareboat or time charter where the vessel is operated exclusively beyond Gibraltar's territorial waters (3 nautical miles); and (4) services of crew and logistics consumed outside Gibraltar. Documentary proof must confirm each voyage's geographic coordinates and that the vessel does not undertake cabotage within Gibraltar. Leases of vessels used within Gibraltar port limits, for local passenger/freight transport or in civil engineering within territorial waters, are subject to duty and are not eligible for relief.

Gibraltar has no statutory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate. Invoices may be issued in paper or PDF. Mandatory electronic filing applies to (a) crew/cargo digital manifests and Advanced Departure Notifications submitted via the Gibraltar Maritime Administration e-portal, (b) AIS/GPS position-log downloads for voyage evidence, and (c) outward-processing and export declarations submitted through the HM Customs of Gibraltar single-window electronic system (GUIDES).

      
HM Customs of Gibraltar may conduct retrospective duty verification for up to 6 years. Fraudulent or negligent zero-rating/export claims attract penalties under Section 40(5) of the Customs Ordinance: a fine not exceeding GIP 5,000 or three times the amount of duty evaded or refunded, forfeiture of the vessel and cargo, and imprisonment up to 2 years on conviction on indictment. Under Section 172 of the Income Tax Act (Gibraltar), false or misleading declarations in support of a refund attract penalties up to GIP 7,500 plus treble the tax understated and interest at 8% per annum; the Commissioner may also issue a protective assessment against the Cayman beneficial owner and seek a ship arrest order in the Gibraltar Admiralty Court. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission may suspend or revoke the trade licence for compliance failures, and directors may be disqualified under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 2014.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Gibraltar operates without exchange controls and the Gibraltar pound is pegged at 1:1 to sterling; capital and current account items are fully convertible for commercial and treasury purposes, subject solely to AML, sanctions and source-of-funds diligence. The Cayman dollar is legally fixed at 0.8333 KYD per USD and profits, dividends and loan repayments from the exempted company may be remitted freely to non-residents without Cayman exchange-control permission. The practical remittance hurdles are not statutory convertibility restrictions but the enhanced documentary burden imposed by correspondent banks when an offshore tax-exempt entity routes vessel-charter revenue through Gibraltar.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline is imposed by Gibraltar, the United Kingdom, or the Cayman Islands. Offshore supply-vessel charter and leasing contract proceeds may be held in GBP, USD, EUR or other convertible currencies in a licensed Gibraltar bank account. In the absence of surrender rules, the bank will nonetheless require annual reconciliation between charter invoices, the vessel operating licence, cash movements and the claimed tax treatment so that the account is not perceived as an unregulated offshore treasury.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Bank of England (central bank for the sovereign United Kingdom); no separate Gibraltar central bank exists. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the integrated regulator of Gibraltar-licensed banks, and the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) is the home-jurisdiction currency and banking regulator for the Cayman Exempted Company.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Low-to-moderate as a primary banking rail and Low as a standalone treasury hub: Gibraltar has no broad standalone e-money institution (EMI) licensing corridor, and the Cayman Exempted Company's offshore-vessel-leasing profile triggers enhanced due diligence that most UK or Gibraltar fintech EMIs will not accept. A UK-authorised EMI may provide payment initiation or collection services if the underlying account is held with a GB/GI-licensed bank, but the main legal constraint is that the EMI cannot itself hold escrowed maritime-charter funds or issue commercial letters of credit.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Offshore Cayman exemption certificate and tax-neutral legal form
        
Root cause: Offshore Cayman exemption certificate and tax-neutral legal form

        
Defense evidence: Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, Certificate of Incumbency, Cayman economic-substance notification or exemption filing, register of members and directors, beneficial-ownership register and explanatory board minute describing the genuine commercial business in offshore chartering.

      

      
      

        Maritime counterparty and vessel-supply disbursement pattern
        
Root cause: Maritime counterparty and vessel-supply disbursement pattern

        
Defense evidence: Fully executed offshore supply vessel charter or leasing agreement, ISIC 5222 statutory licence or Gibraltar vessel registration certificate, P&I and hull insurance, supplier contracts, invoice ledger and expected annual trade volume.

      

      
      

        Non-resident shareholders and perceived nominee ownership
        
Root cause: Non-resident shareholders and perceived nominee ownership

        
Defense evidence: Identity documents for all ultimate beneficial owners, PEP/sanction screening results, an ownership chain chart signed by the administrator, a Cayman legal opinion on non-resident ownership, and written confirmation that no nominee arrangement exists.

      

      
      

        High volume and geography of inbound charter hires and outbound payments
        
Root cause: High volume and geography of inbound charter hires and outbound payments

        
Defense evidence: Projected cash-flow model, charter-party agreements, vessel utilisation schedule, due-diligence files on major charterers, third-party supplier KYC and an annual treasury audit mandate.
