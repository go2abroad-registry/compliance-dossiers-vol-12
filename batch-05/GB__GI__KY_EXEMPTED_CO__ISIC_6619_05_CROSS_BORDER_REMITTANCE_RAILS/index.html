Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax), operating the Gibraltar digital filing portal; indirect/customs jurisdiction vested in HM Customs Gibraltar; UK sovereign nexus administered by HM Revenue & Customs via GOV.UK for any UK taxable activities.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Cayman Exempted Company as a foreign company under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies to obtain a Gibraltar Company Registration Number. Step 2: File the prescribed registration return with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN); the Cayman parent itself holds only a Cayman Companies Register number because the Cayman Islands issue no TIN to tax-exempted companies. Step 3: No Gibraltar VAT ID exists because Gibraltar levies no VAT/GST; if UK taxable supplies were made, the entity would file HMRC form VAT1 to obtain a UK VAT registration number and an EORI number for any physical goods movements. Step 4: Obtain the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) payment-services/remittance licence separately; this statutory licence is not a tax registration but requires submission of the Gibraltar TIN in fit-and-proper due diligence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

VAT / GST / Sales and Use Tax — Gibraltar imposes no domestic VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax; indirect taxation arises only through Gibraltar customs/import duties, excise duties, and stamp duties, while UK VAT under VATA 1994 applies strictly where a UK taxable nexus exists.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: no VAT/GST registration threshold exists because no such tax is in force. UK VAT: statutory taxable turnover threshold GBP 90,000 (2024/25); cross-border remittance fees are exempt financial services under VATA 1994 Schedule 9, so mandatory registration is not triggered unless the entity makes separate non-exempt UK taxable supplies; voluntary registration would yield no input VAT recovery because exempt outputs block credit.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar lacks a VAT/GST framework, so no domestic zero-rating claim is available. Under UK VAT, zero-rating applies only to exported goods evidenced by physical removal from the UK (VATA 1994 section 30); B2B services to non-UK customers are outside the scope of UK VAT or reverse-charged abroad. Cross-border remittance and money transmission services are exempt financial services under VATA 1994 Schedule 9 rather than zero-rated, and that exemption carries no entitlement to input VAT recovery; consequently, the entity's export fee income is effectively untaxed at the indirect level but with blocked input VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration

Gibraltar imposes no real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping mandate. The UK operates a post-audit model requiring digital VAT return filing under HMRC Making Tax Digital; no continuous transaction control or fiscal API applies. For this entity, the operative reporting obligation is GFSC AML/CTF transaction monitoring under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act, which is a compliance requirement distinct from tax invoicing.

Authorities may reassess and claw back benefits with retrospective effect where export zero-rating or VAT exemption is abused: (i) HMRC or HM Customs Gibraltar may issue assessments for up to 4 years, extended to 20 years for fraud, under VATA 1994 section 73 and CEMA 1979; (ii) penalties up to 100% of evaded VAT/duty plus daily penalties apply under FA 2007 Schedule 24 and FA 2008 Schedule 41; (iii) goods connected to customs fraud are liable to seizure and forfeiture under CEMA 1979; (iv) the UK Criminal Finances Act 2017 corporate offence of failure to prevent facilitation of tax evasion carries unlimited fines; (v) Gibraltar participates in HMRC mutual assistance for customs investigations, enabling interest and duty clawback on cross-border flows; (vi) the GFSC may suspend or revoke the remittance/payment-services licence and disqualify controllers for unsubstantiated cross-border transactions; and (vii) a Cayman tax exemption certificate may be cancelled with retrospective effect where the company is found to have engaged in tax evasion or to have misrepresented its exempted activities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar has a broadly open foreign-exchange regime and no general capital-account exchange controls. GBP is the domestic currency, and major currencies are generally convertible through regulated banks and payment institutions. There is no general prohibition on non-resident ownership, capital injections, dividend remittances, loan repayments, or repatriation of sale proceeds. Remittances remain subject to bank-level AML, sanctions, source-of-funds, beneficial-ownership, tax-residency, and transaction-purpose controls. A Cayman exempted company must not assume that its exempted-company status authorizes regulated money transmission, payment services, deposit-taking, or local Gibraltar operations. Conducting consumer remittance activity from or into Gibraltar may require authorization or registration under Gibraltar financial-services legislation, including the payment-services and money-services framework, and may require separate authorization in each destination or source jurisdiction. A Gibraltar bank may require evidence of the entity's Cayman corporate good standing, tax status, licensing perimeter, safeguarding model, correspondent-bank arrangements, customer-risk controls, and a legal opinion addressing whether the proposed activity is regulated in Gibraltar.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory repatriation rule, or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. Export receipts may generally be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted at the customer's election, subject to contractual bank terms and AML, sanctions, tax, and reporting obligations. The stated business is a remittance and payment-services activity rather than a goods-export business; therefore, ordinary export-proceeds rules are not the primary compliance issue. Payment-firm safeguarding, client-money segregation, reconciliation, suspicious-transaction reporting, regulatory returns, and transaction-record retention are more relevant.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar does not have a separate central bank or a general exchange-control authority. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission is the principal financial-services regulator, while Gibraltar Finance and HM Government of Gibraltar administer financial-services policy and applicable local legislation. Gibraltar uses sterling under the Gibraltar Currency Notes Act 1934 and related currency arrangements. The Bank of England is not Gibraltar's domestic banking regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium to low for the proposed high-risk consumer-remittance model. An offshore digital EMI or payment institution cannot rely solely on its home-country authorization to provide regulated payment or money-transmission services in Gibraltar or to Gibraltar residents. The provider must establish the applicable Gibraltar authorization, registration, passporting, local-agent, outsourcing, safeguarding, and consumer-protection position before launch. A Cayman exempted company is not itself an EMI license. Offshore fintech accounts may be used for treasury or settlement only where the provider accepts the entity, permits remittance activity, supports safeguarded client funds, and can evidence a lawful regulatory perimeter. Providers commonly restrict cash-pickup corridors, high-risk countries, nested payment activity, virtual assets, third-party funding, and rapid pass-through flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route shareholder capital, intercompany loans, or paid-in equity through an account held in the legal name of the Cayman company at a regulated bank. Use SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 customer-credit-transfer messaging with the exact legal name, registered address, account or IBAN details, beneficial-owner information where requested, and a truthful payment narrative such as 'share capital contribution' or 'shareholder loan under agreement dated [date]'. Maintain the subscription agreement, board resolutions, loan agreement, source-of-funds evidence, investor ownership records, and bank advice. Do not route capital through customer safeguarded accounts, personal accounts, unrelated affiliates, cash agents, or unexplained third-party wallets. Gibraltar does not generally impose domestic FX purpose codes; however, the sending or receiving bank may require internal purpose classifications, LEI information, tax declarations, and regulatory-license evidence. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be maintained where the gross value of intercompany transactions of the Gibraltar entity exceeds GBP 1,000,000 in the accounting period, or where the entity is a member of a group subject to OECD Country-by-Country Reporting. Master File documentation is required where the multinational group has consolidated annual revenue above EUR 750,000,000 (or the equivalent in GBP). For the remittance rails, intercompany clearing fees, technology licensing and settlement float arrangements with the Cayman parent must be supported by a benchmarking study.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies economic substance requirements under its Income Tax Act, aligned with the EU Code of Conduct Group and the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting standards. Cross-border money transmission and payment intermediation are treated as financial-services relevant activities; the Gibraltar branch must demonstrate that: (i) the board manages and controls the business in Gibraltar, (ii) there are locally resident staff and physical premises, and (iii) core income-generating activities occur in Gibraltar. In parallel, the Cayman parent remains a tax-exempt zone entity but is subject to the Cayman Islands Economic Substance Regulations 2018 for geographically mobile financial activities; therefore the group cannot rely on minimal substance in either jurisdiction without creating a fatal compliance risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Upon conviction on indictment: an unlimited fine and/or imprisonment for a term up to seven years under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010. The Commissioner may raise a discovery assessment without time limits where fraud is established, and may impose a penalty of up to double the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors and the appointed Gibraltar tax representative are personally liable for fraudulent evasion. The court may order civil restitution, attachment of personal assets, accounts freezing orders, and a restriction order preventing departure from Gibraltar pending prosecution. Directors may also be disqualified under the Gibraltar Companies Act for a period of up to 15 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The Income Tax Office may deny the Gibraltar tax residence certificate, disallow the branch's deductions, strike the foreign company registration, and impose a civil penalty of up to GBP 100,000 plus a surcharge of 10% of unreported tax. The Cayman Registrar has a parallel power to strike off the Cayman exempted company for economic substance default.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed personally for up to three times the tax avoided where the company is struck off or becomes insolvent. A director who knowingly participates in a shell arrangement risks personal disqualification, a port-level travel restriction, and criminal liability for false declarations made in the course of the strike-off proceedings.
Unlicensed Payment Activity and AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed payment activity is a criminal offence punishable on indictment by an unlimited fine and/or imprisonment up to 14 years under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015. Each day of unlicensed operation constitutes a separate offence. The GFSC may impose administrative fines of up to GBP 5 million or 10% of annual turnover and may publicly censure the company.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are statutory 'relevant officers' for AML purposes and are personally prosecutable. Sanctions include imprisonment up to 14 years, disqualification from managing any regulated entity, seizure of personal assets through unexplained wealth orders, and court-imposed travel bans restricting departure from Gibraltar.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The Income Tax Office may recharacterize or adjust the intercompany pricing, increasing the Gibraltar assessable profit and issuing a tax demand. A tax-geared penalty of up to 100% of the under-assessed tax may be imposed, together with a fixed civil penalty of GBP 50,000 for non-production of contemporaneous documentation.

Director personal liability: The named Gibraltar tax representative, usually a resident director, is jointly and severally liable for the fixed documentation penalty. Where the documentation failure is deliberate or conceals a diversion of taxable profit, directors may be personally liable for the adjusted tax and may face criminal prosecution for dishonesty, resulting in imprisonment and restriction orders limiting travel.
