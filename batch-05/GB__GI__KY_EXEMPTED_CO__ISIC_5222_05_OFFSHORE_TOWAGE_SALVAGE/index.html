Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax); eTax portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax. Indirect/customs: HM Customs & Excise Department, Gibraltar; portal: https://www.gibcustoms.gi (UK-Gibraltar Customs Declaration Service).

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company must obtain a Gibraltar TIN from the Income Tax Office using Form EC (Entity Registration). Steps: (1) file certified Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum/Articles, and Certificate of Good Standing; (2) appoint a Gibraltar resident agent to act as representative; (3) declare a registered office or permanent establishment address in Gibraltar; (4) upon TIN issuance, register additionally with HM Customs & Excise as a Customs Trader/Operator (Economic Operator Code) before first vessel clearance or import of stores; (5) submit annual nil-return or Qualifying Company levy return, whichever applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone - The Cayman Exempted Company is exempt from Cayman direct taxation; in Gibraltar it is treated as a non-resident company unless it has a permanent establishment or elects Gibraltar residence. Gibraltar-source profits would otherwise be chargeable at 10% corporate tax (territorial scope), but QC status substitutes fixed annual levy for full taxation.

Available election forms

(1) Election for Qualifying Company status under the Qualifying Companies Act 2011 (fixed annual levy of GIP 3,100 to GIP 31,000 in lieu of 10% corporation tax, available to locally managed companies with no local income); (2) election under Foreign Income Tax Rules 2019 to exempt foreign-source income (not required for QC entities); (3) no full check-the-box partnership-transparency election exists in Gibraltar, but the Income Tax Act permits attribution of certain maritime income to participators where the vessel is operated internationally; (4) OECD BEPS Hybrid Mismatch Regulations 2019/2020 apply, restricting double-deduction and deduction-without-inclusion outcomes for hybrid/dual-resident arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No_VAT_No_GST (Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area; indirect taxation exists only as Customs Import Duties and Excise Duties on physical goods - alcohol, tobacco, fuel. Offshore towage and salvage services under ISIC 5222 rendered on the high seas or in international waters are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax.)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 0 - No value-added or sales tax registration threshold applies because no VAT/GST is levied. However, any commercial importer/exporter, including a tugboat operator bringing stores, spare parts, or bunkers ashore, must hold a Customs Trader Registration and obtain a Customs Economic Operator Code before the first import transaction; no de minimis threshold exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar levies no VAT, so 0% VAT zero-rating is inapplicable. Substitute reliefs: (a) goods exported from Gibraltar to third countries are relieved from Customs Import Duty; (b) tugboat stores, equipment, and bunkers loaded for use on 'foreign-going' vessels (voyages to non-Gibraltar ports) are duty-free under the foreign-going ship relief; (c) maritime salvage and towage remuneration under a Lloyds Open Form or similar contract, performed outside territorial internal waters, is treated as supplied in international waters and thus outside Gibraltar indirect tax. The entity must demonstrate the tug cleared out to a foreign port and that the salved or towed vessel is foreign-going; no refund mechanism exists since no tax is chargeable at issue.

  • Gibraltar Customs Outward Foreign-Going Clearance Declaration (CDS entry generating export reference), Gibraltar Ship Registry Certificate of Registration for the tug
  • Lloyds Open Form 2011 Salvage Contract / Towage Services Agreement, Salved Value and Bailment Survey Report
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill evidencing foreign destination of towed cargo/floating unit
  • Port Departure Certificate from the Captain of the Port, Gibraltar Port Authority (vessel sign-out)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT confirmation proving fee repatriation within 180 days
  • Store and Bunker Inventory Return plus proof of P&I Club insurance covering foreign-going voyage

No continuous-transaction-control (CTC) regime, real-time fiscal clearance, B2B e-invoicing mandate, or digital stamping requirement is in force in Gibraltar. Nevertheless, export and inward/outward vessel clearance must be pre-lodged electronically via the UK-Gibraltar Customs Declaration Service (API/EDIFACT-based) for UK and foreign trade statistics; stores and bunker inventory returns are filed through the Gibraltar e-Port system, and electronic discharge clearance is required to preserve duty-free stores privileges.

HM Customs & Excise may reopen duty-free stores/export declarations within 4 years, extended to 6 years on allegations of fraud. Misuse of foreign-going ship relief or false export declarations triggers forfeiture of the tug, stores, and bunkers, plus a monetary penalty of the greater of 100% of evaded duty or three times the value of the goods, and on conviction a fine up to GIP 500,000 and/or imprisonment for 2 years under the Customs Duties Act and Criminal Fraud Act. In parallel, the Gibraltar Income Tax Office may revoke Qualifying Company or nil-tax status retrospectively, clawing back 10% corporate tax with interest at 12% per annum and penalties up to 100% of the under-declared tax; cross-border information exchange with the Cayman Islands Tax Information Authority under the TIEA is automatic in such audit cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar uses the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) pegged at par to GBP, and the territory adopts UK exchange-control freedoms with no separate currency control regime. A Cayman Exempted Company operating through Gibraltar for offshore towage and salvage contracts enjoys full current-account convertibility for trade receipts, operational disbursements, and profit remittances. Capital-account movements are unrestricted between Gibraltar, the UK, and the EEA; however, remittances to non-EEA beneficiaries may be subject to enhanced due diligence under the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2023 and notification thresholds. The only practical hurdles are documentary: proof of beneficial ownership, board resolutions, tax residency certificates, and an economic substance filing with the GFSC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender of export proceeds or mandatory conversion ratios exist in Gibraltar or the UK. Companies may retain 100% of export earnings in any freely convertible currency. For deep-sea towage and marine salvage contracts, invoicing in USD or GBP is customary, and no timeline is imposed for conversion or repatriation. However, banks may impose internal policies requiring conversion within 90 days for operational liquidity forecasting, and companies should maintain contemporaneous contract and delivery records to satisfy GFSC AML obligations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England as the monetary authority for the United Kingdom and Gibraltar, with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) acting as the integrated financial regulator for banking, licensing, and AML/CFT supervision in Gibraltar. HM Treasury retains ultimate authority over UK-wide sanctions and financial crime policy applicable to Gibraltar as a British Overseas Territory.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for offshore digital EMIs holding a UK E-Money licence or an EEA passport, but legally constrained for high-value salvage and towage flows. Gibraltar-licensed EMIs regulated by the GFSC can support low-value operational payments such as port dues and crew wages. However, for Lloyds Standard Form of Salvage or escrow releases above USD 250,000, a tier-1 domestic bank account is mandatory because EMIs are not deposit-taking institutions, are not covered by any deposit guarantee scheme, and generally decline higher-risk maritime counterparties. Use EMIs exclusively as a supplementary clearing rail for sub-threshold disbursements.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% — Gibraltar standard corporate income tax rate under the Income Tax Act 2010 as amended; the Cayman Exempted Company itself incurs zero Cayman income tax, and towage/salvage profits sourced outside Gibraltar are generally outside the Gibraltar territorial charge.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on distributions to non-resident shareholders, and the Cayman Exempted Company framework likewise levies nil withholding tax on dividend payments.
