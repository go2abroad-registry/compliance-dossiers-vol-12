Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, 6 Europa Point, Gibraltar GX11 1AA; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax. For indirect tax and customs: HM Customs Gibraltar, New Mole House, Waterport, Gibraltar; e-services portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/customs.

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company not managed or controlled in Gibraltar is not automatically issued a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN). The entity must file Form GIB-NR-1 (Non-Resident Company Declaration) with the Commissioner of Income Tax to declare non-residence and obtain a Gibraltar corporate reference code. If the entity makes taxable supplies in Gibraltar, it must separately register under the Goods and Services Tax Act 2022 through HM Customs Gibraltar's GST e-Service, submitting: (1) Cayman Islands certificate of incorporation and certificate of good standing; (2) registered office address in Cayman or a service address in Gibraltar; (3) minutes of directors' meeting confirming no central management and control in Gibraltar; and (4) bank confirmation for inward remittance. The GST registration number is issued on Form GST-REG-01 within 15 business days and serves as the indirect tax identification number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Gibraltar law. A foreign company may, with prior written approval of the Commissioner, elect to be treated as a Gibraltar-resident company where central management and control is effectively exercised in Gibraltar (Income Tax Act 2010, section 8(3)). Hybrid mismatch elections are not available to non-transparent entities; the Cayman Exempted Company is treated as an opaque corporation for Gibraltar tax purposes, and no such election is applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

1%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 150,000 (approximately USD 190,000) of annual taxable supplies; mandatory registration is triggered when the twelve-month rolling taxable turnover exceeds this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to the supply of goods physically exported from Gibraltar to a destination outside Gibraltar, and to supplies for use on international seagoing vessels engaged in ocean freight. The exporter must be a GST registered person; the goods must leave Gibraltar within 90 days from the date of supply; and the foreign buyer must receive direct shipment. For raw cane sugar, the seller must hold a valid customs export declaration and produce evidence that the goods were not subject to local consumption in Gibraltar.

  • Customs Export Declaration (CUS/GEO-EXP-01) accepted by HM Customs Gibraltar
  • Bill of Lading (clean on-board bill issued by the shipping carrier showing the Cayman Exempted Company as shipper and the overseas buyer as consignee)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of the full sale consideration in foreign currency into the entity's bank account within 180 days

No real-time electronic invoice clearance mandate is currently in force. Tax invoices must be issued within 15 days of the supply, containing the GST registration number, description, value, and tax amount. Imports into Gibraltar are cleared through the ASYCUDA World customs computerized system; electronic cargo manifests and customs declarations are mandatory for all physical consignments. No digital stamping or real-time API submission is required for raw cane sugar commodity operations.

HM Customs Gibraltar may reject zero-rating and retrospectively claw back the unrecovered GST (1% of the transaction value) plus interest at 8% per annum if the required documentation bundle is not produced within 180 days. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the mandatory deadline triggers a presumption of over-recovery; penalties of up to £3,000 or 200% of the tax evaded apply for fraudulent claims under the Goods and Services Tax Act 2022, section 88, with criminal prosecution available under the Customs and Excise Duties Act 2002.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility without exchange control approval. The Gibraltar pound is freely convertible at parity to the pound sterling. The Cayman Exempted Company may hold and transact in GIP, GBP, USD, and EUR. No restrictions on profit remittance, dividend repatriation, or outward portfolio investments. Operational constraints arise solely through correspondent banking compliance, not local FX regulation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar maintains no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no definition of a state settlement account. The Cayman Exempted Company may retain all export revenues in USD or any permissible currency. Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and AML/CFTF rules, export proceeds must be reconciled with underlying trade invoices for record-keeping, but there is no regulatory requirement to convert, repo, or repatriate.

Central bank supervision: Government of Gibraltar (Financial Secretary) and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC); the Gibraltar pound is peg-issued to GBP under the Currency Notes Act, with monetary stability anchored to the Bank of England. No separate central bank exercises exchange control; HM Treasury retains ultimate currency issuing authority for sterling.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Low-Medium (2.5/5). A non-Gibraltar offshore EMI cannot be used as the primary clearing node for physical sugar trade. Only GFSC-licensed or UK/EEA passported Electronic Money Institutions are permitted to hold funds for a Cayman Exempted Company. These EMIs can support operational GIP/GBP/USD account balances and low-value wires, but they cannot issue documentary Letters of Credit, negotiate bills of lading, or provide trade finance facilities. For ISIC 4631 vessel-sized VHP raw cane sugar transactions, SWIFT-clearing through a tier-one Gibraltarian or British correspondent bank is mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

SWIFT MT103 wire from the Cayman parent or the ultimate shareholder with the payment narrative 'Share capital contribution — Cayman Exempted Company, non-resident'. The Gibraltar bank will require a certified board resolution and a completed lender's source declaration; funds above GBP 20,000 are subject to enhanced due diligence. Loan or quasi-equity funding must be supported by an intercompany facility agreement with an arm's-length coupon and must use the bank's standard capital purpose code for inward direct investment. Pre-approval by the bank's Compliance Monitoring Officer is mandatory before the funds can be credited to a trading book.Revolving Documentary Letter of Credit (MT700) with the Gibraltarian issuing bank is the preferred clearing rail for physical VHP raw cane sugar purchases. Direct open-account payments are permitted via SWIFT MT103 where the invoice presentation is accompanied by a copy of the shipping bill of lading and cargo insurance. USD and EUR settlements are cleared through the bank's correspondent network (normally New York for USD and London for EUR/GBP); all wires are screened against OFAC, HM Treasury, and EU sanctions.
