Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office (ITO), HM Government of Gibraltar. Corporate registration authority: Companies House Gibraltar. Primary taxpayer portal: ITO electronic filing portal and Companies House Gibraltar e-Registry (no separate centralised single-window tax portal; forms are lodged through ITO online services or paper filing).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign (Cayman Exempted) company operating in Gibraltar: (1) Register the overseas company with Companies House Gibraltar as an external company within one month of establishing a place of business, filing the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, register of directors, and Gibraltar registered office and local agent details. (2) Obtain the Gibraltar Company Number and, once registered, file a Statutory Declaration of Domestic Resolution and the company tax registration form with the Income Tax Office to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN); notice of chargeability must be given within the statutory period prescribed by the Income Tax Act. (3) Apply to HM Customs Gibraltar for an Excise Licence (Registered Consignor/Consignee authorisation) and, separately, to the Gibraltar Licensing Authority for a wholesale alcohol licence under the Licensing Act; both are required before any beer import, dispatch, or wholesale activity. (4) If the entity is treated as a branch of a Cayman Exempted Company, file the branch/agent appointment with the Registrar of Companies and provide the ITO with the parent company tax-residency evidence; no distinct VAT ID is needed because Gibraltar has no VAT.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone, per the composite key: the Cayman Exempted Company is statutorily exempt from all Cayman Islands direct taxation (income, capital gains, and withholding). Gibraltar, however, applies Entity_Level_Taxation with Territorial_Scope: a company is Gibraltarian tax-resident if incorporated in Gibraltar or if its central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, and is then chargeable at 12.5% corporate tax on profits accruing in or derived from Gibraltar. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone, per the composite key: the Cayman Exempted Company is statutorily exempt from all Cayman Islands direct taxation (income, capital gains, and withholding). Gibraltar, however, applies Entity_Level_Taxation with Territorial_Scope: a company is Gibraltarian tax-resident if incorporated in Gibraltar or if its central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, and is then chargeable at 12.5% corporate tax on profits accruing in or derived from Gibraltar. No Gibraltar 'exempt company' status exists (abolished 2012); hence, although the entity is tax-exempt in its home jurisdiction, a Gibraltar-managed and controlled business activity will create a taxable Gibraltar presence and the Cayman exemption does not extend to Gibraltar-source trading profits.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not offer a 'check-the-box' or entity-classification election; the tax classification of a company is determined solely by the Companies Act legal form and the Income Tax Act residence tests. Available statutory elections relevant to the craft-beer distribution structure: (a) group relief election to surrender losses between at least 75%-owned group companies; (b) election for small-company administrative filing and instalment arrangements where turnover thresholds are met; (c) hybrid mismatch elections/disapplications under the Taxation (International Compliance and Disputes Resolution) Rules 2017 implementing BEPS Action 2 (relevant where the Cayman parent is treated as tax-transparent or opaque inconsistently between jurisdictions); and (d) US federal tax classification of the Cayman Exempted Company, if required for US stakeholders, is effected solely through IRS Form 8832 with the US Internal Revenue Service, not through any Gibraltar filing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST. Gibraltar operates a Customs Duties Act regime composed of: Gibraltar Import Duty (GIBD) levied on goods imported into Gibraltar (ad valorem on most categories) and Excise Duty on beer and other alcoholic beverages, tobacco, and hydrocarbon oil, administered by HM Customs Gibraltar.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

NIL - zero turnover threshold. There is no VAT registration threshold because no VAT is imposed. Excise registration is mandatory from the first transaction: any person who produces, imports, dispatches, or wholesales beer in or from Gibraltar must obtain an Excise Licence and be registered with HM Customs Gibraltar as a Registered Consignor or Registered Consignee, irrespective of turnover; a separate wholesale alcohol licence under the Licensing Act is required for the ISIC 4632 distribution activity. The obligation arises immediately upon commencement of commercial activity in Gibraltar.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as VAT zero-rating because Gibraltar has no VAT. Export relief is instead obtained through the excise duty-suspension mechanism under the Customs Duties Act: a licensed exporter holding Registered Consignor status may dispatch craft beer to an authorised consignee outside Gibraltar without payment of GIBD or excise duty, provided the goods physically leave Gibraltar and the EMCS movement is closed with proof of receipt. General export eligibility conditions: (i) both consignor and consignee hold valid excise authorisations; (ii) the beer is exported in unchanged condition under an e-AD; (iii) transport documentation is consistent with the e-AD; and (iv) the exporter retains evidence of payment of the sales price duly received from abroad. No duty refund mechanism is needed for genuine exports because duty is suspended on despatch rather than refunded after payment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document or National Export System declaration released by HM Customs Gibraltar)
  • Bill of Lading (original, telex, or electronic bill showing Gibraltar as port of loading and the overseas destination port)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT credit advice or covered letter matching the invoice value and shipment date)
  • Excise e-AD (Electronic Administrative Document) and e-FAD (returned final receipt from the UK Excise Movement and Control System)
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list detailing keg count, volume in hectolitres, and alcoholic strength by beer brand
  • Certificate of Origin or preferential origin evidence where preferential tariff treatment is claimed at destination

No real-time fiscal clearance, government API e-invoicing, or digital stamping is mandated for B2B distribution in Gibraltar. For duty-suspended movements of craft beer between Gibraltar and the UK/EU, the UK Excise Movement and Control System (EMCS) is used: electronic Administrative Documents (e-AD) must be issued and closed with an electronic receipt (e-FAD) proving delivery to the authorised consignee. Standard wholesale invoices must identify supplier and consignee excise registration numbers, beer description, volume in hectolitres, alcoholic strength, and despatch date; EDI or PDF invoicing is acceptable and no fiscal stamp or digital signature is required.

HM Customs Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office may audit production, movement, and export records for up to six years (unlimited in the case of fraud or deliberate error). If export substantiation fails, the suspended duty and import duty are retrospectively clawed back, the supply is re-characterised as a domestic duty-paid supply, and the exporter is liable to civil penalties up to 100% of the duty evaded plus statutory interest. Fraudulent evasion of excise duty is a criminal offence punishable by an unlimited fine and up to seven years' imprisonment; additional administrative consequences include suspension or revocation of the Excise Licence, forfeiture of the goods or conveyances, and public naming. Although Gibraltar has no statutory exchange-control requirement for repatriation of export proceeds, the 180-day benchmark is applied by banking and tax authorities as documentary evidence of genuine trade substance; missing or late repatriation may trigger transfer-pricing adjustments, general anti-abuse assessments, and feeding into the Tax Justice Board for extended audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at parity with GBP and enjoys full convertibility on both current and capital account; neither the United Kingdom nor Gibraltar maintains exchange-control restrictions (the UK Exchange Control Act 1947 was repealed), and the Cayman Islands imposes no foreign-exchange controls on exempted companies. Profit remittances from the Gibraltar distribution book incur zero Gibraltar withholding tax and no repatriation hurdle; the Cayman Exempted Company is tax-exempt on passive and foreign-source income, and no Gibraltar permanent establishment is expected under the proposed commissionaire-plus-third-party-warehousing structure, so no Gibraltar corporation tax at 12.5% should attach.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation timeline applies under UK, Gibraltar, or Cayman law. Export receipts invoiced in EUR and USD may be retained freely in correspondent accounts, converted through the interbank market, or applied directly to trade settlement. The only mandatory obligations are transactional reporting under the Bank of England International Transaction Reporting System (ITRS) for UK-resident counterparties and GFSC/FATCA-CRS declarations, none of which requires currency surrender or conversion.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority, sterling convertibility, and payments-system oversight) acting in concert with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the integrated regulator for Gibraltar banks and payment institutions; the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) retains constituent-home regulatory oversight of the Cayman Exempted Company participant.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium feasibility with legal constraints: UK-authorised electronic money institutions may operate into Gibraltar under the UK-Gibraltar treaty, but most prohibit offshore Cayman corporate clients or cap annual throughput (commonly GBP 250,000); GFSC-licensed Gibraltar EMIs are few and require sponsorship by a licensed fiduciary. An EMI is viable only as a secondary disbursement rail for low-value deposits and never as the primary treasury conduit for the cold-chain keg wholesale receivables book.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% (flat rate on chargeable profits of Gibraltar-resident companies under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010; non-resident Cayman Exempted Company taxed only on Gibraltar-source income attributable to a permanent establishment; 0% in the Cayman Islands as no corporate income tax is imposed)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%: Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on cash, scrip or beneficial dividends paid by a Gibraltar-resident or Gibraltar-PE company to a non-resident shareholder; a Cayman Exempted Company may declare and pay dividends to its shareholders free of Gibraltar withholding tax, subject to the company being fully tax-compliant and economically substantive.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

On or before 30 November immediately following each Gibraltar fiscal year of assessment (which runs 1 July to 30 June); i.e. a five-month filing window from the 30 June year-end. Companies on self-assessment make two payments on account on 30 September and 30 December, with a final balancing payment due with the return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File: Gibraltar-resident companies and branches with cross-border related-party transactions exceeding GIP 1,000,000 (approx. GBP 1,000,000) per accounting period; Master File required where the group's consolidated turnover exceeds GIP 50,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting at the OECD EUR 750m consolidated group revenue threshold applies to the ultimate parent. Contemporaneous TP documentation must be in English and submitted within 30 days of a Commissioner's notice.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance (Companies and Partnerships) Act 2019 applies because cold-chain distribution of craft beer is a 'distribution and service centre business' relevant activity. The Cayman Exempted Company or its Gibraltar branch/entity must: (i) direct and manage the core income-generating activities from Gibraltar, including beer procurement, inventory risk-taking, freight contracting, invoicing and margin assumption; (ii) maintain a physical office in Gibraltar; (iii) employ full-time qualified staff proportionate to activity (minimum one licensed customs/freight operations manager); and (iv) incur adequate operating expenditure in Gibraltar. Annual ESR notification must be filed on the Gibraltar ESR portal. Sub-GIP 1m turnover may access simplified filing but not exemption. Dual ESR exposure arises because the Cayman Islands concurrently requires the same core activities to be performed in Cayman unless the entity declares Gibraltar as its place of economic substance; the intercompany agreements and board minutes must consistently evidence Gibraltar as the management seat to avoid dual non-compliance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / deliberate non-disclosure

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 100% of additional tax for careless inaccuracies and up to 200% for deliberate concealment under Income Tax Act 2010 s.55; daily interest at 1% per month compounded; criminal conviction on indictment with unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 7 years; publication in the Commissioner's serious-default list.

Director personal liability:
Sham company / façade abuse of tax-exempt status

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the entity as Gibraltar-resident under the central management and control test; assessment on worldwide profits for six years (unlimited in cases of fraud); penalty up to 200% of tax evaded; strike-off under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 for failure to file ESR declarations; disqualification of directors for up to 15 years.

Director personal liability:
Economic Substance Regulation non-compliance (Gibraltar ESR 2019)

Statutory sanction: Information Notice requiring remedy within 30 days; first-cycle financial penalty of GBP 100,000 plus daily penalty of GBP 1,000 per day of continued failure; second-cycle penalty of GBP 300,000 and automatic strike-off from the Gibraltar register; directors who knowingly cause false ESR declarations are liable to imprisonment up to 5 years.

Director personal liability:
Transfer pricing documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment re-attributing profit to the Gibraltar distribution entity; additional tax at 12.5%; tax-geared penalty of 100% of the under-declared profit for deliberate breach; daily interest at 1% per month; potential disclosure to HMRC under the UK-Gibraltar exchange of information arrangement and referral for joint tax inspection.

Director personal liability:
AML / sanctions / anti-money laundering breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 as amended: imprisonment up to 14 years and unlimited fines for money laundering; civil forfeiture of beer inventory and freight assets; revocation of the alcohol wholesale licence; referral to the Royal Gibraltar Police Economic Crime Unit; inclusion on the UK sanctions list would freeze all EU distribution bank accounts.

Director personal liability:
Personal liability of directors / piercing the corporate veil

Statutory sanction: Wrongful trading liability for the full deficit of the Gibraltar tax debt; restitution ordered by the Supreme Court of Gibraltar for preferential dividends; travel ban and passport surrender for directors classified as tax fugitives; director disqualification for up to 15 years; imprisonment up to 7 years for fraudulent trading under Companies Act 2014 s.327.
