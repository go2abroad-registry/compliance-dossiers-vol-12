Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation); indirect tax and customs administration: Gibraltar Customs Division (https://www.customs.gov.gi); company registration: Companies House Gibraltar, Gibraltar Registry (https://www.csra.gi). The United Kingdom HMRC is not the competent tax authority for Gibraltar, which maintains a distinct territorial tax system under the Income Tax Act 2010.

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company is not automatically registered for Gibraltar tax. Registration for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) is triggered only if the company becomes tax resident in Gibraltar (central management and control located in Gibraltar), derives Gibraltar-source income, or establishes a branch/permanent establishment. Procedure: (1) obtain good standing and certified constitutional documents from the Cayman Islands General Registry (Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, Registered Office Certificate); (2) file a Gibraltar branch registration with the Registrar of Companies and obtain a Gibraltar company number; (3) submit the completed tax registration application with certified board resolutions and a statement of anticipated Gibraltar-source activities to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office; (4) register as an employer with the Income Tax Office and Social Security if staff are engaged in Gibraltar; (5) obtain an importer registration code from Gibraltar Customs if goods (e.g., syrups and franchise consumables) are imported into Gibraltar. VAT registration is not applicable because Gibraltar does not levy value added tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The Cayman Exempted Company may file a Tax Exemption Certificate under the Cayman Islands Tax Concessions Act, securing exemption from Cayman income tax until at least 2051. In Gibraltar, the company may elect to be treated as a non-resident company where central management and control remains outside Gibraltar, or as a Gibraltar tax-resident company in respect of Gibraltar-source income only. A Gibraltar 'Qualifying Company' designation may be available for qualifying licence-holding activities, subject to economic substance obligations under the Income Tax Act 2010 and the Taxation (Companies - Economic Substance) Regulations. No US-style check-the-box election exists; hybrid mismatch rules aligned with BEPS Action 2 apply where an instrument or entity is treated as transparent in one jurisdiction and opaque in another (Cayman exemption versus Gibraltar 12.5% corporate rate).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar operates no VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax; indirect taxation is confined to import and excise duties on specified dutiable goods under the Customs Ordinance, together with Gibraltar-specific duties on alcohol, tobacco, and hydrocarbon oils.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - there is no VAT registration threshold in Gibraltar. Businesses importing dutiable goods must register with Gibraltar Customs for an importer code based on import activity rather than turnover. Employer registration for payroll tax and social security arises as soon as any staff are employed in Gibraltar, without a monetary turnover threshold. For reference, the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is at parity with GBP.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Statutory VAT zero-rating is unavailable in Gibraltar because the jurisdiction has no VAT system. Exporters may instead obtain relief from import customs duty for goods entered into duty-suspension regimes, transit, customs warehousing, or re-export; goods of zero-tariff classification require no duty. For franchise services, royalty and management fees supplied to non-resident franchisees fall outside the scope of any Gibraltar indirect tax. Such fees will be outside Gibraltar corporate tax only if the Cayman Exempted Company has no Gibraltar branch or permanent establishment and the income is not deemed to arise in Gibraltar under territorial sourcing rules; if the income is sourced in Gibraltar, it is chargeable at the 12.5% corporate rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed electronically through the Gibraltar Customs single-window system
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT credit evidence for export proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (where applicable)
  • Franchise Agreement and Royalty Schedule (for treated-as-export services)

Gibraltar imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC), e-invoicing API, or digital stamping mandate comparable to EU systems. Commercial invoices must still contain sufficient particulars for income tax record-keeping and customs valuation. Customs declarations must be filed electronically through the Gibraltar Customs single-window system, and all import/export records, invoices, bills of lading, and payment evidence must be retained for at least six years for audit by the Income Tax Office and Gibraltar Customs.

Fraudulent export or customs declarations in Gibraltar are prosecuted under the Customs Ordinance, with penalties up to three times the duty evaded, forfeiture of goods, and criminal sanctions for dishonest misdeclaration. Retrospective assessments in cases of fraudulent or negligent conduct may extend up to six years, with no time limit in proven fraud cases. Although UK VAT zero-rating is not statutorily available in Gibraltar, any improper assertion of UK VAT zero-rating or refund claims would expose the taxpayer to HMRC assessment under the Value Added Tax Act 1994 section 73, penalty up to 100% of the tax under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007, and joint and several liability under the Missing Trader Intra-Community fraud rules, together with potential director personal liability under the Finance Act 2003 and Customs and Excise Management Act 1979. The Gibraltar Finance Centre (Tax Evasion) legislation also provides for criminal fines and confiscation orders for tax-evasion facilitation.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The GIP is freely inter-convertible with GBP and, because it circulates at parity, all UK-facing treasury flows can be executed through UK Faster Payments, CHAPS, SWIFT and SEPA without central bank approval. The Cayman exempted company is not subject to exchange-control limits under Cayman law; current account items—franchise royalties, management fees, licence fees and syrup import payments—can be converted and repatriated freely. The operative restrictions are not FX controls but anti-money-laundering surveillance: banks require a lawful business narrative, CRS/FATCA classification, economic-substance evidence and a clear payment-purpose code. Major hurdles include the zero-tax and perceived tax-haven profile of a Cayman exempted company, which may cause correspondent banks outside the UK to reject SWIFT credits or delay USD clearing, and the Economic Substance (International Tax Compliance) Act under Cayman law, which requires the company to demonstrate core income-generating activities in an adequate location, usually Gibraltar or the UK, if it claims non-Gibraltar tax residence. Profit remittance from Gibraltar to Cayman is free of withholding tax under Gibraltar's zero-royalty regime, but a tax-residency certificate, beneficial-ownership declaration and substance file must be ready for production to GFSC, HM Revenue & Customs or any counterparty bank where relevant.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules apply. In both Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands, export proceeds and trade receivables may be retained in the currency of collection; no fixed timeline, mandatory conversion ratio or central bank surrender requirement is imposed by the GFSC, CIMA, HM Treasury or the Bank of England. The only regulatory expectations are: the retention of full underlying trade documentation under the Gibraltar Money Laundering Regulations and the Cayman Proceeds of Crime Law; the maintenance of conversion-rate evidence for transfer-pricing audits; and the requirement that cash or POS proceeds from franchisees be settled through licensed payment service providers rather than accumulated in physical cash above the applicable reporting threshold. A Cayman exempted company may therefore maintain USD syrup-payment accounts, GBP royalty accounts and EUR franchisee collection accounts without any requirement to repatriate or convert balances within a prescribed period.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar does not operate an independent central bank. The monetary and foreign-exchange reserve framework is anchored by HM Treasury and the Bank of England, with the Government of Gibraltar issuing Gibraltar pound (GIP) notes and coin under the Currency Notes Act at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory licensing, conduct-of-business, prudential and AML/CFT supervisor for banks and electronic money institutions. For the Cayman exempted company, the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) is the applicable banking supervisor and designated authority under the Tax Information Authority Act; the Cayman Islands has no central bank and no restrictive exchange-control mechanism.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: High for quick GBP/EUR/USD collections, conversions and small-value franchise royalty payments; Medium for third-party inbound franchise royalties because most electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not authorised to provide credit, deposit-taking or trade finance; Low for capital escrow, commercial letters of credit and substantial syrup-import settlement because unauthorised EMIs cannot issue or advise L/Cs. Legal constraints under the UK Electronic Money Regulations 2011 and the Gibraltar Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act: a licensed EMI must safeguard client funds, but such funds do not benefit from the UK Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS) or equivalent Gibraltar depositor protection. The Cayman exempted company may use Wise Business, CurrencyCloud or Modulr for low-value and sub-EUR 10,000 franchise invoices, but no EMI should be used as the sole treasury rail for operations in more than five regulated markets. A dedicated bank account in a tier-1 licensed bank must hold the capital base, L/C collateral and all balances above the EMI's own safeguard limit.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All capital injections from the Cayman exempted company to the Gibraltar operating treasury should be remitted by SWIFT MT103 using purpose code 'CAPB' for capital payments, or ISO 20022 category '4200'/'GDSB' for parent-company loans if the injection is structured as shareholder debt. The wire must be accompanied by: a board resolution and capital allocation letter signed by the Cayman directors; a source-of-wealth and source-of-funds statement; CRS/FATCA self-certification; certificate of incorporation and good standing; current registers of members and directors; and an AML clearance memorandum from the receiving bank. UK National Security and Investment Act screening must be checked where the capital injection grants control over a UK or Gibraltar entity operating in sensitive sectors, although a boba tea franchising entity is unlikely to be notifiable. No FX authorisation is required from the Bank of England, GFSC or CIMA; the credit should be marked 'consumption of capital in operating subsidiary' rather than 'trade settlement' to avoid misclassification. The injection amount should be precisely reconciled against the Cayman company's audited capital account, using no funnel structure through third-party jurisdictions, and should be routed through the cleared international bank in Gibraltar before any onward FX conversion.For syrup-supply imports, use traditional commercial letters of credit (L/C at sight) issued via a UK or Gibraltar bank and advised through the supplier's bank, supported by the full shipping and compliance file: commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, certificate of origin, sanitary/phytosanitary certificate, inspection certificate and draft. For smaller consignments, use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code '7010' or '1000' for trade settlement. The clearing rails should be tiered as follows: (1) UK Faster Payments for UK franchisee royalty payments under GBP 1 million per transaction; (2) SEPA Instant for Eurozone franchisees under EUR 100,000; (3) SWIFT MT103 for all cross-border wire payments above EUR 10,000; (4) Fedwire/CHIPS for USD settlement to US or Taiwanese syrup and equipment suppliers; (5) commercial L/Cs for container-sized commodity shipments under ICC UCP 600. All disbursements must be made only to bank accounts in the name of the actual supplier or franchisee; third-party disbursements are prohibited. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: High for quick GBP/EUR/USD collections, conversions and small-value franchise royalty payments; Medium for third-party inbound franchise royalties because most electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not authorised to provide credit, deposit-taking or trade finance; Low for capital escrow, commercial letters of credit and substantial syrup-import settlement because unauthorised EMIs cannot issue or advise L/Cs. Legal constraints under the UK Electronic Money Regulations 2011 and the Gibraltar Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act: a licensed EMI must safeguard client funds, but such funds do not benefit from the UK Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS) or equivalent Gibraltar depositor protection. The Cayman exempted company may use Wise Business, CurrencyCloud or Modulr for low-value and sub-EUR 10,000 franchise invoices, but no EMI should be used as the sole treasury rail for operations in more than five regulated markets. A dedicated bank account in a tier-1 licensed bank must hold the capital base, L/C collateral and all balances above the EMI's own safeguard limit.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 of aggregated annual related-party transactions (franchise royalties, syrup supply, and sub-licence payments) triggers mandatory Local File and Master File preparation under Gibraltar transfer pricing practice

Economic substance scope: The Cayman Exempted Company is subject to the Cayman Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) and must demonstrate core income-generating activities in Cayman for franchise licensing; alternatively, it may justify bona fide tax residence in Gibraltar by showing management and control, employees, premises, and commercial substance in Gibraltar; lack of either Cayman or Gibraltar substance exposes the structure to tax avoidance recharacterisation and anti-abuse rules

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty up to 100% of the understated tax, interest on unpaid tax, criminal fines up to GIP 500,000, and forfeiture of the commercial franchise licence

Director personal liability: Directors face civil restitution, automatic disqualification from holding office for up to 15 years, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to 7 years on conviction for deliberate tax evasion
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Denial of non-resident or treaty status, assessment on deemed Gibraltar-source income, imposition of anti-avoidance penalties up to 300% of the tax liability, and possible extreme dissolution by the Gibraltar Registrar

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil allows the tax authority and creditors to seize personal director assets, impose personal restitution orders, apply for travel restrictions, and pursue criminal convictions for fraudulent trading
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment, late payment interest, fixed penalty of GIP 20,000 for documentation non-compliance, and additional accuracy penalty up to 50% of any under-assessment

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve the non-compliant transfer pricing documentation are jointly and severally liable for the adjustment and face regulatory disqualification as company officers
AML and Suspicious Transaction Reporting

Statutory sanction: Asset freezing orders, fines up to 10% of global turnover under anti-money laundering legislation, forced derecognition of nominee directors, and referral to the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit

Director personal liability: Individual directors face criminal money-laundering charges under the Proceeds of Crime Act with imprisonment up to 14 years, confiscation orders, personal overseas asset freezes, and automatic prohibition from managing any Gibraltar-licensed entity
