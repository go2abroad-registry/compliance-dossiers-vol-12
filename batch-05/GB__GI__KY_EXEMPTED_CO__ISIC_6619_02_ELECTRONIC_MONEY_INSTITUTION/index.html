Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Phase 1 (statutory licence precondition): obtain GFSC authorisation as an Electronic Money Institution under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2012 before any fiscal registration; an authorised EMI requires initial capital of at least EUR 350,000, safeguarding of customer e-money reserves in separately held accounts, approved-person fit-and-proper clearance, and a compliant AML/CFT programme. Phase 2 (foreign company registration at Companies House Gibraltar): file the prescribed foreign-company application under Part 3 of the Companies Act 2014 within one month of establishing a place of business in Gibraltar; lodge certified copies of the Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing from the Cayman Registry, constitutional documents, registered agent consent, and register of directors and members; obtain the Gibraltar company registration number. Phase 3 (Income Tax Office registration): submit the prescribed company tax registration application, attaching the GFSC licence and Companies House registration evidence, and appoint a Gibraltar-resident fiscal representative; the Income Tax Office then allocates the Gibraltar TIN (Company Reference Number), which must be quoted on corporation tax returns, payroll returns, CRS/FATCA filings, and customs entries. Phase 4 (Social Security): register as an employer with the Gibraltar Social Security Office if any staff are engaged in Gibraltar. Phase 5 (Customs): register at H.M. Customs only where physical duty-liable goods are imported or exported; the issuance of virtual IBANs, e-money wallet management, and payment processing are outside the scope of import duty and do not trigger any customs registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation in Gibraltar. The Cayman Exempted Company is, in its home jurisdiction, a Tax_Exempt_Zone vehicle: the Cayman Islands impose no income or corporation tax, and a Tax Exemption Certificate obtained under the Tax Exemptions Law (2021 Revision) guarantees no Cayman tax for a 20-year term; the company is nevertheless a per-se corporation and is not tax-transparent. In Gibraltar, that status confers no immunity. Under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, a foreign-incorporated company is resident in Gibraltar if it is managed and controlled from Gibraltar; a resident company is chargeable to Gibraltar corporation tax on worldwide profits at the standard 12.5% rate, while a non-resident company is chargeable only on Gibraltar-source income. A licensed EMI that takes board decisions, treasury reserve management, client onboarding, and AML/CFT governance in Gibraltar will ordinarily be managed and controlled there and will therefore fall within entity-level Gibraltar taxation, with no foreign tax credit available from the Cayman exemption.

Available election forms

Gibraltar: (a) Qualifying Company election under the Income Tax (Qualifying Companies) Regulations allows an eligible EMI to elect a 0% rate on qualifying financial-services profits in exchange for a flat annual charge (approximately GBP 1,000 to GBP 10,000) and requires real economic substance and effective management in Gibraltar; loss of the election or substance failure triggers recapture at 12.5%. (b) The former exempt-company regime is closed to new companies and available only to grandfathered entities, so it cannot be elected by a newly established Cayman-owned EMI. (c) Non-residence election is unavailable in practice for an entity managed from Gibraltar. Cayman Islands: the Tax Exemption Certificate application is the only relevant statutory election and does not affect Gibraltar taxation. Hybrid mismatch: no check-the-box or partnership look-through election exists in Gibraltar or Cayman; the Cayman Exempted Company is a per-se taxable corporation in both jurisdictions, and Gibraltar applies OECD BEPS Action 2 domestic rules denying deductions for payments that produce double non-taxation. The UK-Gibraltar Double Taxation Arrangement (effective January 2021) uses place of effective management as the residence tie-breaker but does not exempt the EMI from Gibraltar tax absent genuine substance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Import Duty and Consumption Tax system. Gibraltar has no VAT, no GST, and no general sales and use tax; it sits outside both the UK and EU VAT areas. The only indirect taxation in force is import duty, levied principally on alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oils, and selected goods at specific or ad valorem rates, together with ancillary consumption charges.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Gibraltar imposes no VAT or general sales tax, so no registration threshold exists. Import-duty registration is triggered at the first importation of duty-liable goods into Gibraltar, with no monetary minimum threshold. For completeness, UK VAT group registration (if the wider group independently establishes a UK taxable presence) would only be triggered by UK taxable turnover above GBP 90,000 per annum (2024/25); however, supplies taking place in Gibraltar are outside the UK VAT area and do not count toward UK taxable turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Gibraltar as a VAT zero-rating concept, because Gibraltar does not charge VAT. Goods exported from Gibraltar are outside the charge to import duty: the duty point arises at importation into Gibraltar, and goods that leave Gibraltar under duty suspension, inward processing, or re-export do not attract duty. Gibraltar-sourced financial services provided to non-residents are entirely outside Gibraltar's indirect-tax system, and there is consequently no export VAT refund procedure to claim. If the wider group or an affiliate becomes UK VAT registered, exports of physical goods from the UK to destinations outside the UK VAT area (including Gibraltar as a third territory) are zero-rated under UK VAT law, subject to proof of export within three months and preservation of shipping and payment evidence; financial services remain VAT-exempt and cannot be zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration or re-export entry submitted via the Gibraltar Customs Automated Import/Export System (required only where physical goods move through Gibraltar)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing continuous shipment and final destination outside Gibraltar
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list (for duty-suspension, inward processing, or outward processing claims)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT MT103 evidence of receipt of proceeds (applicable to EMI service transactions and required by the GFSC for safeguarding reconciliations)

Gibraltar imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing validation mandate. H.M. Customs operates a mandatory digital import and export declarations platform (the Gibraltar Customs Automated Import/Export System) for physical goods movements, but this is a customs inventory-control system, not an invoice clearance mechanism. The Income Tax Office portal requires electronic filing of tax returns and CRS/FATCA reports, yet commercial invoices and e-money transaction documents are not subject to pre-clearance, fiscal seal, or statutory QR-code requirements. The future EU VAT in the Digital Age initiative does not extend to Gibraltar.

Gibraltar income tax: deliberate default or fraudulent evasion attracts civil penalties of up to 100% of the tax lost, daily default fines, and criminal sanction of fines and imprisonment; H.M. Commissioner of Income Tax can retrospectively assess omitted profits and claw back any qualifying-company benefits if economic substance conditions are not met. Import duty: evasion results in forfeiture of goods, a civil penalty equal to the duty evaded, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent customs conduct. GFSC enforcement: the Financial Services Commission may impose financial penalties, suspend or revoke the EMI licence, issue prohibition orders against approved persons, and compel restitution of the e-money reserve; AML/CFT breaches are prosecuted under the Gibraltar AML legislation with substantial fines and director disqualification. UK VAT context (for any UK-registered group affiliate): zero-rating is reviewed on a transaction basis; invalid export evidence causes retrospective output tax assessments with assessment periods extended to 20 years for deliberate error, plus accuracy penalties of up to 100% and potential criminal liability for fraudulent evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. There are no UK or Gibraltar exchange controls restricting the conversion of GBP into foreign currency, the holding of non-GBP balances, or the repatriation of dividend, interest, fee or liquidation proceeds to non-resident shareholders. The Cayman Exempted Company's tax-exempt status does not create a profit-remittance barrier; distributions and treasury flows may be paid freely through licensed Gibraltar banks, subject only to standard anti-money-laundering due diligence, sanctions screening and transaction-narrative requirements. Capital account flows, including capital injections, shareholder loans and internal treasury repositioning, do not require prior central bank approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export receipts may be retained in the original foreign currency, converted to GBP, or used for trade settlement at commercial bank rates with no deadline for conversion. For AML and accounting purposes, supporting documents (contracts, invoices, bills of lading, customs declarations) must be retained for at least five years, and any large or unusual transaction must be reported to the GFSC under the proceeds-of-crime framework if suspicious.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (ultimate monetary authority for Gibraltar as a British Overseas Territory); Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar pound at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the licensing, conduct and AML/CFT supervisory authority for banks and electronic money institutions operating in Gibraltar. There is no separate Gibraltar central bank with independent exchange-control powers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability Rating: Medium for a regulated Gibraltar/UK-licensed EMI structure; Low for an unregulated offshore digital EMI. After Brexit, there is no automatic EEA passport into Gibraltar. A Cayman Exempted Company cannot itself act as an EMI solely on the strength of its Cayman corporate status; it must hold a GFSC EMI authorisation or be attached to a GFSC-licensed Gibraltar electronic money institution. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., authorised in Lithuania, Cyprus, Ireland or the UK) may be used as banking-as-a-service and correspondent settlement partners if they are licensed in their home jurisdiction, but the Gibraltar bank will treat them as high-risk correspondent institutions requiring a legal opinion, licence verification and sanctioned-jurisdiction due diligence. A purely digital, substance-less EMI with no physical or regulatory presence in Gibraltar is not viable for local account opening or central-bank clearing access.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection from the Cayman shareholder or parent treasury to the Gibraltar EMI account must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) or MT202 COV for bank-to-bank cover payments. The wire should use ISO 20022 purpose code CAPC with the narrative 'CAPITAL INJECTION / PAID-IN SHARE CAPITAL' and, if using the UK Faster Payments/CHAPS infrastructure, the beneficiary account must be a sterling account with a valid UK sort code. A board resolution authorising the capital injection, a source-of-funds letter from the remitting bank, and a copy of the notarised share-register update must be provided to the Gibraltar receiving bank. Since Gibraltar maintains full convertibility, no central bank approval is required, but the receiving bank must complete the source-of-wealth verification before crediting the account.Trade disbursements from the Gibraltar treasury should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for direct remittances, MT700/MT710 for documentary letters of credit, and MT760 for standby guarantees. For GBP clearing, use CHAPS for same-day high-value payments and Faster Payments for lower-value batches through Bank of England settlement systems. For EUR treasury flows, use SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) with beneficiary IBAN; for USD, use Fedwire or CHIPS through a corresponding US bank. Each payment must include a purpose narrative, underlying commercial invoice/reference, and must pass sanctions-screening and AML transaction-monitoring before release. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability Rating: Medium for a regulated Gibraltar/UK-licensed EMI structure; Low for an unregulated offshore digital EMI. After Brexit, there is no automatic EEA passport into Gibraltar. A Cayman Exempted Company cannot itself act as an EMI solely on the strength of its Cayman corporate status; it must hold a GFSC EMI authorisation or be attached to a GFSC-licensed Gibraltar electronic money institution. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., authorised in Lithuania, Cyprus, Ireland or the UK) may be used as banking-as-a-service and correspondent settlement partners if they are licensed in their home jurisdiction, but the Gibraltar bank will treat them as high-risk correspondent institutions requiring a legal opinion, licence verification and sanctioned-jurisdiction due diligence. A purely digital, substance-less EMI with no physical or regulatory presence in Gibraltar is not viable for local account opening or central-bank clearing access.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% flat rate on assessable income under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended). The Cayman Exempted Company (ELF 549300KY_EX) is exempt from Cayman Islands domestic corporate tax and becomes chargeable in Gibraltar only where it is managed and controlled in Gibraltar or derives Gibraltar-source trading income through a permanent establishment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%: Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax, advance corporation tax, or dividend distribution tax on dividends paid by Gibraltar-resident companies or Gibraltar permanent establishments to shareholders, including the Cayman ultimate parent.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and transfer pricing declaration where the aggregate value of controlled transactions (virtual IBAN fees, reserve management, platform IP, management charges) exceeds GIP 2,000,000 per accounting period or where the entity holds a high-risk GFSC EMI licence. Master File and Country-by-Country Report alignment applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750,000,000. The Cayman Exempted Company must prepare contemporaneous economic substance and transfer pricing documentation within 30 days of the filing deadline.

Economic substance scope: Applicability of the Cayman Economic Substance Regulation (International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act 2018) and Gibraltar's management-and-control and economic substance expectations. The Cayman Exempted Company must prove that core income-generating activities relating to e-money and IBAN issuance occur in the Cayman Islands; otherwise it will be treated as a Gibraltar tax-resident company. A full-time senior management team, physical premises, principal decision-making meetings, and board approval of client-money segregation must be evidenced. Failure to demonstrate substance recharacterises the structure as an economic substance shell and the Gibraltar branch profits may be re-attributed to the United Kingdom.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 100% to 200% of tax understated, interest at 8% per annum, and criminal conviction under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010. Financial sanction up to GIP 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment for up to seven years.

Director personal liability: Directors with knowledge or neglect are assessed for civil restitution and penalty recovery. Knowing concern leads to personal income tax charges, travel bans, director disqualification for up to 15 years, and extradition to the United Kingdom under OECD enforcement protocols.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Cayman economic substance failure: financial penalty of US$10,000 for the first year and US$25,000 per subsequent year, plus daily fines for continuing breach, and the Cayman registrar may strike off the company. Gibraltar consequence: deemed United Kingdom or Gibraltar tax residence of the beneficial owner, revocation of the GFSC licence, and a GBP 2,000,000 economic value verification charge for the non-compliant EMI.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally responsible for unpaid penalties and comfort letter liability to the regulator. Travel restrictions apply, personal guarantees of up to GIP 5,000,000 may be required, and criminal proceedings may be brought under the Companies Act for omission to ensure adequate economic substance.
Unlicensed and Shadow Banking Activity

Statutory sanction: Offence under the Gibraltar Financial Services Act 2019. Summary conviction: fine not exceeding GIP 500,000 or imprisonment up to 12 months. Indictment: unlimited fine and/or imprisonment for up to 10 years. The court may order disgorgement of profits and winding-up of the client money fund.

Director personal liability: Every director, manager, and permitted individual who consented or connived is personally liable to the same penalties. The GFSC may issue prohibition orders, restrict travel to Gibraltar and the United Kingdom, and interdict new business; an international arrest warrant may be issued for continuing non-compliance.
AML, MLRO, and Sanctions Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the Terrorism Act 2018, unlawful money laundering carries up to 14 years' imprisonment and an unlimited fine. AML and CTF civil penalties up to GIP 5,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover. Sanctions breach fines up to GIP 1,000,000 plus asset freezing orders.

Director personal liability: The Money Laundering Reporting Officer and directors are personally criminally liable for consent, connivance, or neglect in preventing money laundering. Consequences include travel bans, worldwide asset freezes, disqualification from regulated activity in the United Kingdom for 10 years, personal liability for client money shortfalls, and extradition to the United Kingdom or Gibraltarian courts with immediate custodial sentence.
