Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Overseas registration: the Cayman Exempted Company is registered as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under Part 21 of the Companies Act 2014, filing the certified Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, constitutional documents, power of attorney, and particulars of a Gibraltar registered office and local process agent. Step 2 – Income-tax registration: immediately thereafter, file the corporate income-tax registration return with the ITO, attaching the overseas-company certificate, group structure chart, a description of the Chinese Malatang master-licensing activities, and the parent's financial statements or an audit waiver; the ITO issues the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (numeric TIN) within approximately 7–10 working days and records the entity as a non-resident branch/permanent establishment of the tax-exempt Cayman parent, taxable only on Gibraltar-source profits at the 12.5% flat corporate rate. Step 3 – Employer and social-insurance registration: if any staff are employed, register the branch with the Gibraltar Contributions Office for social insurance and PAYE withholding. Step 4 – Business licence and food-safety registration: obtain a business licence under the Business Licensing Act 2015 in the restaurant/fast-food category, plus a Food Business Registration and HACCP plan approval from the Environmental Agency, including premises inspection, allergen, hygiene, and waste-management plans, before any food service commences. Step 5 – Customs and trader identification: if branded packaging, equipment, or broth ingredients are imported into or exported from Gibraltar, register as a customs declarant with HM Customs Gibraltar and obtain the Gibraltar trader identification number (EORI-equivalent); the threshold is nil for any physical goods movement. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or entity-transparency election exists under Gibraltar or Cayman law; the Cayman entity's exempt status is conferred by statutory election under the Cayman Companies Act, while in Gibraltar the branch remains a non-resident permanent establishment. The relevant Gibraltar statutory elections and protective filings are: (a) a permanent-establishment profit-attribution election under the Income Tax Act 2010 adopting the OECD Authorised OECD Approach, so that profits of the Gibraltar branch are computed on arm's-length functions, assets, and risks rather than by formula; (b) a group-relief election for loss surrender between qualifying Gibraltar group companies; (c) a capital-allowances election for leasehold fit-out, self-serve broth stations, dispensers, refrigeration, and kitchen plant (100% first-year allowance on qualifying energy-efficient low-emission equipment, otherwise a 25% reducing-balance pool); (d) an anti-hybrid protective election/notice to the Commissioner under Gibraltar's OECD BEPS Action 2/EU ATAD-aligned anti-hybrid rules, confirming that royalties paid to the Cayman licensor do not create a deduction-without-inclusion mismatch given the licensor's zero-tax status; and (e) an accounting-period election aligning the Gibraltar branch to a 31 December year-end to match the franchisee royalty cycle and the Cayman parent's reporting.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility in both Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands, with no prior approval, surrender, or repatriation licensing requirements. GIP trades at par with GBP; EUR, USD, and CNH can be freely held and cleared through GFSC-licensed banks. The Cayman exempted company operates under a tax-neutral, exchange-control-free regime (KYD pegged to USD at 1 KYD = 1.20 USD); royalty, interest, and dividend remittances attract zero Cayman and zero Gibraltar withholding tax. The only practical restriction is screening against UK financial sanctions and the bank's internal enhanced due diligence limits, not statutory FX controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in this corridor: neither Gibraltar nor the Cayman Islands imposes a statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or central-bank repatriation approval on export or royalty proceeds. Franchise and licensing receipts may be retained in GBP, EUR, USD, or CNH at the discretion of the treasury. Where PRC franchisees remit royalties, PRC SAFE rules apply at the source: the franchisee's designated bank requires a registered service-trade contract (SAFE code 228020, royalties and license fees) and a withholding tax certificate, but the receiving Cayman/Gibraltar account falls entirely outside PRC exchange-control jurisdiction.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary anchor for the Gibraltar pound, which is issued by the Government of Gibraltar at a 1:1 parity with GBP under sterling currency-board reserve rules); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (the on-territory banking and e-money regulatory authority under the Financial Services Act 2019); Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (home-state regulator and AML/CFT supervisor of the Cayman exempted company).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High with structural conditions. Gibraltar operates a GFSC e-money and payments licensing regime under the Financial Services Act 2019, but the population of active locally authorized EMIs is small and orientated toward gaming and payments. UK-licensed EMIs (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Currenxie) offer superior multi-currency aggregation and local receiving rails; however, post-Brexit UK-Gibraltar passporting is no longer automatic, and most EMI compliance teams will apply enhanced due diligence to a Cayman exempted company due to offshore-entity reputational risk. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Gibraltar: 10% standard corporate income tax rate on chargeable profits; 12.5% rate applies only to licensed utility/telecommunications undertakings. Cayman Exempted Company: 0% Cayman corporate tax; however, Gibraltar tax at 10% applies to profits attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment (PE) or derived from Gibraltar-source franchise royalty/licence income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% statutory withholding tax rate in Gibraltar on all dividends paid by a Gibraltar-resident company or a Gibraltar PE of a foreign company to any shareholder, including a non-resident Cayman exempted company; dividends are paid gross, with no withholding tax, no branch remittance tax, and no capital duty. No Cayman withholding or tax on inbound repatriation.

The return must be delivered not later than 15 January following the end of the Year of Assessment, or 9 months from the accounting period end if a non-calendar-year end is adopted with the Commissioner's consent; tax is payable on the same deadline. Estimated tax instalments are due on 31 January and 31 July.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local currency is the Gibraltar pound (GIP), at par with GBP. Full OECD BEPS Action 13-compliant Local File / Master File documentation is mandatory where the annual value of controlled transactions with the Cayman parent or related franchise supply entities exceeds GIP 1,000,000, or where related-party turnover exceeds 10% of the Gibraltar entity's total turnover. A simplified contemporaneous benchmark of at least three comparable licence agreements is required above GIP 500,000. A complete Chapter V documentation file must be prepared for any intercompany royalty flows above GIP 5,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 2019 fully apply. The Cayman exempted company is in scope where it is tax-resident in Gibraltar or where it carries on relevant activity (franchise master licensing / intangible property business or headquarters business) through a Gibraltar PE. Core income-generating activities (CIGAs) for the licensing business — negotiation of franchise agreements, management of sublicences, protection and improvement of the Malatang know-how, and distribution of licence rights — must be performed in Gibraltar. Minimum substance requirements: at least two physically present employees responsible for CIGAs, premises that are more than a post-box, and annual operating expenditure proportionate to gross income. A company that merely holds the master licence and receives royalties outside Gibraltar while negotiating from China is non-compliant; penalties and reassessments at the Gibraltar 10% rate will follow.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Sham-PE Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2010, s.76(b): penalty up to 100% of the tax evaded; interest at 8% per annum on the underpayment; the Commissioner may reopen assessments for up to 6 years, or 20 years in cases of fraud or voluntary omission. Criminal conviction: fine of GIP 50,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where a director actively participated in the invoicing scheme; the director is jointly and severally liable for restitution of the diverted tax and profits. Directors named on the Gibraltar PE registration are subject to a 5-year appointment disqualification, travel bans in the course of criminal investigation, and worldwide asset-freezing orders against Malatang franchise cash flows.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Regulations 2019: penalty of GIP 10,000 for the first failure; GIP 10,000 for a second consecutive year; and up to GIP 100,000 for continued non-compliance. The Registrar may strike off the Gibraltar PE or foreign-company registration, and the Commissioner may disregard the declared absence of a PE, taxing the entire royalty income in Gibraltar at 10%.

Director personal liability: Any director who knowingly files a false ESR certificate is subject to a personal fine of GIP 5,000 and up to 5 years of disqualification from acting as a Gibraltar director. After strike-off, directors become personally liable for the company's unpaid Gibraltar tax to the extent they approved the false declaration or collected royalty funds during insolvency.
AML / Money Laundering through Self-Serve Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime Act 2015: an offence of acquiring, possessing, or transferring criminal property, punishable on indictment by up to 14 years' imprisonment and an unlimited fine. The Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit may freeze the PE bank account immediately and preserve assets pending investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise cash commingling or fail to file a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) are personally prosecutable; courts may order asset restitution, a 5-year travel ban, and disqualification from any Gibraltar financial-services role. Each director is deemed a 'nominated officer' for transactions above GIP 10,000 involving politically-exposed persons or unexplained wealth.
Transfer-Pricing Misstatement / Under-Pricing of Master Licence

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner applies the arm's-length principle through s.78A of the Income Tax Act 2010 (as inserted by the ESR Regulations 2019), reassesses the PE at the 25% ALP-compliant royalty, charges interest at 8%, and imposes a 100% penalty on the additional tax where the misstatement is deliberate. Failure to produce TP documentation triggers an automatic GIP 10,000 fine.

Director personal liability: The director who signs the self-assessment declaration 'connected-party prices are at arm's length' is personally assessable if the company becomes insolvent after strike-off; civil recovery orders may be made against the Cayman holding company, and directors who knowingly signed the false TP declaration face criminal liability for tax-evasion conspiracy.
Unlawful Dividend / Void Distribution from Gibraltar Subsidiary

Statutory sanction: Companies Act 2014 (Gibraltar) s.158: any dividend paid otherwise than out of available profits is void; the directors who authorised the payment are jointly liable to repay the full amount to the company; creditors may issue a winding-up petition to recover the unlawful distribution.

Director personal liability: The court may issue a Civil Recovery Order against the Cayman director personally; the corporate veil is pierced where the distribution was made in bad faith, and the director is personally liable to restore the dividend with interest, may be ordered to pay the company's debts, and may have a Gibraltar passport surrender and travel restriction imposed during the winding-up.
