E-government filing portal

For a Cayman Exempted Company establishing any taxable connection in Gibraltar: (1) appoint a Gibraltar-licensed taxation agent; (2) file an external company registration with the Registrar of Companies if a Gibraltar branch is created; (3) deliver to the Commissioner of Income Tax a Notice of Commencement of Business within 21 days of starting activity, together with the Cayman certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles, certificate of good standing, and confirmation of a Gibraltar address for service; (4) a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) is assigned within 2-3 working days; (5) open an Import Duty Deferment Account with HM Customs where goods are imported; (6) register as an employer for social insurance and payroll tax if local staff are engaged. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope - the Cayman Exempted Company is tax-exempt in its jurisdiction of incorporation; Gibraltar taxes only profits arising in Gibraltar or profits attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment, at the standard 12.5% corporate rate. In the absence of a PE and of locally sourced income, the entity remains outside the Gibraltar income tax net while retaining limited liability.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box regime; legal classification follows incorporation law. Statutory elections available include: (i) group relief election under the Income Tax Act for qualifying 75%+ group companies; (ii) election for exemption of foreign-source dividends satisfying participation conditions; (iii) accelerated first-year capital allowance election on qualifying energy-efficient equipment; (iv) a reconciliation election under the hybrid mismatch rules preserving a deduction in a UK or third-country payer while including income in the Cayman hybrid in Gibraltar; and (v) treaty-allocative elections under the UK-Gibraltar Double Taxation Arrangement (in force since 2021). For e-commerce operations targeting UK consumers, a voluntary UK VAT registration election is available where UK stock is held.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - Gibraltar levies no VAT, GST, or retail sales and use tax. Indirect taxation operates through Import Duty (Customs Tariff Ordinance 2013), Purchase Tax on specified consumables, and Excise Duty on alcohol, tobacco, and petroleum products.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT registration threshold exists in Gibraltar. Any importer of record (resident or non-resident) bringing automotive replacement parts into Gibraltar must hold an Import Duty Deferment Account with HM Customs; ad valorem duty rates under the tariff schedule range from 0% to 22%, with components of UK/EU origin eligible for duty-free entry under preferential arrangements.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT and therefore no domestic export zero-rating rate. Relief is provided through duty drawback under the Customs Duties Ordinance: import duty paid on goods exported within 12 months of import is reclaimable on proof of export. If the Cayman company voluntarily registers for UK VAT on UK-held stock, UK VAT zero-rating under VATA 1994 s. 30 and SI 1995/2518 applies to (i) direct exports to third countries and (ii) deemed exports via an approved export warehouse; the 0% rate is conditional on obtaining and retaining proof of export within 3 months of supply (and, in appropriate cases, within 6 months).

  • Gibraltar Customs export declaration (electronic entry via GATE / Single Administrative Document)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the cross-border transport movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 demonstrating non-refundable receipt of foreign currency proceeds for the exported goods
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin (EUR.1, Form A, or UK origin statement where preferential duty treatment is claimed)

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate applies in Gibraltar. Retail invoices may be issued via any EDI/PEPPOL-based system without pre-approval; customs declarations must be submitted electronically through GATE, Gibraltar's customs clearance system, aligned to UK CDS data-set standards. Original invoices, stock records, and customs entries must be retained for six years.

HM Customs may audit export and drawback entries for up to 4 years, seize goods, and impose civil penalties of up to three times the duty for material misuse; false declarations attract criminal prosecution. For UK VAT, incorrect zero-rating is retrospectively clawed back with interest where proof of export is absent; deliberate misuse extends the assessment window to 20 years and incurs penalties up to 100% of the tax (VATA 1994 ss. 60, 72). For Gibraltar income tax, under-reported export turnover triggers a deemed profit assessment for any permanent establishment, a 100% misdeclaration penalty, and imprisonment of up to seven years (Income Tax Act ss. 160-177).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar regulatory framework imposes no material exchange controls on current account or capital account transactions involving GBP, EUR, or USD. The Cayman Exempted Company, as a non-resident offshore entity, is freely permitted to convert and remit profits, dividends, and proceeds of liquidation without e parity surcharges or conversion taxes. Profit repatriation is not subject to withholding tax in Gibraltar owing to the tax-exempt status of the underlying entity structure, and there are no general post-settlement monitoring requirements beyond standard AML/CTF checks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar does not impose any statutory timeline for surrender of export proceeds, nor any mandatory conversion ratio for receipts denominated in foreign currency. For online retail sales of automotive parts, incoming export revenue may be retained in the original currency, converted at market rates, or used for onward trade disbursements without central bank consent, subject only to routine reporting thresholds under the Beneficial Ownership and Sanctions regimes.

Central bank supervision: Financial Services Commission of Gibraltar (the statutory regulatory and supervisory body for banking, foreign exchange-related licensing, and financial crime compliance; the Gibraltar pound is pegged to the British pound sterling and the Bank of England acts as the currency issuer)

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Gibraltar corporation tax headline rate: 10% flat on chargeable profits. Large companies with taxable profits above EUR 42,000,000 are taxed at 12.5% on the excess (YA 2022-23 onwards); banking profit excess above EUR 42,000,000 attracts an additional 18% rate (YA 2023-24 onwards). The Cayman Exempted Company tier itself is taxed at 0% in the Cayman Islands; Gibraltar tax applies only to profits derived through a Gibraltar trade or permanent establishment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Gibraltar: 0% withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar company to non-residents; no Gibraltar income tax on such dividends. Cayman Islands: no tax and no withholding on dividends at either entity or shareholder level. Effective statutory WHT on profit repatriation through the structure is therefore 0%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar TP rules follow the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines (Income Tax Act 2010 as amended by the Transfer Pricing (Companies) Regulations 2019). Mandatory Local File: any company with controlled transactions exceeding GIP 1,000,000 in aggregate in the chargeable period. Mandatory Master File: group consolidated turnover above GIP 50,000,000. Country-by-Country Report: MNE groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more. Transactions below these thresholds are still subject to arm's length pricing but with simplified contemporaneous documentation. The Cayman Exempted Company must maintain a TP file covering all intercompany purchases of automotive parts, warehousing services, brand royalties, and management fees.

Economic substance scope: Dual-regime applicability: (1) Cayman ESR - the International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Law, 2018 applies to the Cayman Exempted Company; online distribution and service-centre activities are 'relevant activities' requiring the company to be managed and directed in the Cayman Islands, with adequate employees, premises, and core-income-generating activities performed there, and an annual economic substance report filed with the DITC. (2) Gibraltar ESR - the Income Tax (Economic Substance) Act 2021 requires any company tax resident in Gibraltar carrying on a relevant activity to demonstrate economic substance in Gibraltar, with annual substance reporting. A Cayman entity managed and controlled from Gibraltar would become Gibraltar tax resident and subject to both regimes; the commercial activity must be carried on with real local substance in one jurisdiction, not attributed to a mailbox office. Failure to satisfy ESR in either jurisdiction leads to exchange of information with the UK/EU, penalties, and in serious cases compulsory strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Concealed Online Turnover

Statutory sanction: Behaviour-based penalties up to 100% of the tax understated for deliberate and concealed errors; interest on the underpayment; criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act and the Criminal Offences (Money Laundering and Financing of Terrorism) Act, with fines at the discretion of the court and imprisonment up to seven years; forfeiture of the proceeds of the undeclared sales.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where a director signs a false return or knowingly participates in concealment: the director is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties, risks a worldwide asset-freezing order, a travel ban, disqualification as a director for up to 15 years, and personal imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Cayman ESR: initial fine of KYD 10,000, KYD 10,000 per additional month of default, exchange of information responses, and up to five years' imprisonment for knowingly and recklessly filing false information. Gibraltar ESR: fines of up to GIP 100,000, cancellation of tax filings, exchange of information with the UK/EU, and compulsory strike-off of the company.

Director personal liability: Officers who approve false economic substance declarations face criminal liability, disqualification, and civil liability for taxes previously avoided; shareholders may be treated as receiving a deemed distribution, and directors may be ordered by the court to make restitution for losses suffered by creditors.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment reallocating profits to the Gibraltar PE; additional tax at 10% to 12.5%; compensatory interest; accuracy penalties up to 100% of the additional tax for deliberate manipulation; CbCR penalties up to GIP 10,000 per failure.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign TP computations without adequate supporting evidence are personally liable for penalty assessments; habitual mispricing is treated as tax evasion, exposing directors to confiscation orders and imprisonment for up to seven years.
AML / Proceeds of Crime - Payment-Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime Act: money laundering offence carrying imprisonment up to 14 years and unlimited fines; terrorist financing up to life imprisonment; civil confiscation orders of the entire benefit derived; fines against the company up to 5% of annual turnover; potential freezing of the PayPal and Stripe settlement accounts by the FSC.

Director personal liability: Directors, MLROs, and compliance officers face individual prosecutions for consenting to or failing to report suspicious transactions; the court may impose travel bans pending trial, make post-conviction confiscation orders, and order personal restitution to defrauded customers; professional disqualification follows automatically.
Late or Non-Filing / Non-Payment

Statutory sanction: Default penalties of GIP 300 plus GIP 60 daily surcharges up to GIP 30,000; best-of-assessment raised by the Commissioner with interest; enforcement by distraint against bank accounts and the Stripe/PayPal settlement funds; publication of defaulting taxpayer names.

Director personal liability: Persistent defaulting directors are treated as public officers and may be made personally liable for the company's tax debt where the failure results from wilful neglect; courts may issue director disqualification orders and, in cross-border cases, coordinate travel restrictions with UK authorities.
