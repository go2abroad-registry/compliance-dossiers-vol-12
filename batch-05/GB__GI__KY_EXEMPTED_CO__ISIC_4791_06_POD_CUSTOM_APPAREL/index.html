Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office (GITO), HM Government of Gibraltar; online e-Tax portal: https://etax.gibraltar.gov.gi; customs duties separately administered by Gibraltar Customs and Excise Department.

E-government filing portal

A foreign Cayman exempted company requiring a Gibraltar tax identity must apply to the Commissioner of Income Tax at GITO. Submit the company registration form, certified Cayman Islands Certificate of Incorporation, constitutional documents, Gibraltar registered office or establishment details, directors and ultimate beneficial owner declarations, and evidence of central management and control. GITO will issue a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) for corporation tax, employment, social insurance, or customs purposes. If central management and control is outside Gibraltar and no permanent establishment exists, the company remains non-resident and generally outside Gibraltar corporation tax; a TRN may still be obtained for banking, payment processing, customs, or compliance identity purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone (Cayman exempted company status; Gibraltar territorial taxation applies only if management and control is in Gibraltar or a permanent establishment exists in Gibraltar) — A foreign Cayman exempted company requiring a Gibraltar tax identity must apply to the Commissioner of Income Tax at GITO. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Cayman exempted company status; Gibraltar territorial taxation applies only if management and control is in Gibraltar or a permanent establishment exists in Gibraltar)

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or elective entity classification mechanism exists in Gibraltar. Gibraltar tax residence is determined by fact of incorporation and central management and control, not by election. Anti-hybrid rules apply on a statutory basis; no hybrid-mismatch election is available to alter the Cayman entity's separate taxable personality for Gibraltar income-tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/sales-and-use tax; Gibraltar levies customs and excise duties only on specified imported goods.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - there is no VAT/GST/sales-tax registration threshold. A customs registration is required only when the entity acts as a declarant or importer in Gibraltar; no economic turnover threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as Gibraltar imposes no VAT. Goods exported from Gibraltar are outside the charge to customs duty; duty-paid goods later exported may qualify for customs duty relief or repayment under returned-goods or outward-processing provisions if satisfactory export evidence exists. Cross-border services supplied by a non-resident POD retailer are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD entry lodged with Gibraltar Customs and Excise Department)
  • Commercial invoice / POD sales invoice with HS classification, description, quantity, value, and consignee details
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill / courier consignment note evidencing physical export
  • Packing list and shipping manifest
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer proof for foreign-exchange and anti-money-laundering compliance
  • Previous import entry and receipt of duty payment when claiming duty repayment on re-export

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, B2B e-invoicing clearance, digital stamping, or SAF-T submission mandate exists in Gibraltar. Electronic invoicing is allowed voluntarily, but all invoices and underlying trade records must be retained for at least six years for income-tax and customs audit purposes.

Gibraltar Customs and Excise Department may seize goods and raise assessments for unpaid duty where export declarations are false; penalties may reach GBP 10,000 plus three times the duty evaded for fraudulent evasion under the Customs and Excise Ordinance. Knowingly supplying forged bills of lading, fake remittance certificates, or fabricated POD transaction records to obtain export relief or banking/EMI clearance is a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment, fine, and confiscation. As there is no VAT, there is no VAT clawback; however, any customs duty repayment improperly obtained may be recovered with interest and penalties for up to six years after the event.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains a fully convertible currency regime for both capital and current accounts under the Financial Services Act 2019 and subordinate regulations. Capital account transactions face no restrictions for qualifying exempted companies, while current account remittances, including profit distributions, are unrestricted subject only to standard AML/KYC verification and absence of sanctions exposure

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export receipts denominated in foreign currency must be surrendered to an authorised bank within thirty calendar days for conversion to GBP at the prevailing spot rate. Mandatory statutory reporting via GFSC Form FX-1 is required, with conversion ratios fixed at market rates and no surrender premium applicable

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the primary foreign exchange and banking regulator, with full alignment to the Bank of England for GBP transactions and post-Brexit UK regulatory frameworks

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 (approx. GBP 1,000,000 or USD 1,250,000) in aggregate value of controlled transactions in a fiscal year; a Local File is required for any individual transaction category exceeding GIP 500,000; a Master File is required if group turnover exceeds GIP 50,000,000 and cross-border related-party turnover exceeds GIP 10,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) to companies that are tax resident in Gibraltar or that carry on relevant activities through a Gibraltar PE. A Cayman Exempted Company engaged in POD Custom Apparel Retailing is not automatically caught by ESR because 'retail and distribution' is a relevant activity only when performed through a taxable presence in Gibraltar; nevertheless, a company that claims Gibraltar tax residence, or that is managed and controlled from Gibraltar, must demonstrate adequate premises, employees, and local decision-making. Companies that are pure offshore Cayman vehicles, with no Gibraltar PE and no Gibraltar-source income, remain exempt but must maintain substance in the Cayman Islands under Cayman economic substance rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment for up to 7 years and/or an unlimited fine under the Income Tax Act 2010 as amended by the Criminal Justice (Tax Evasion) Act 2019; civil penalty up to 100% of the tax understated plus interest; court order for restitution of evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors, shadow directors and beneficial owners can be personally liable for the company's tax liabilities where they knowingly facilitated evasion; they may face asset freezing orders, seizure of assets, travel bans, director disqualification for up to 15 years, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar ESR Code and underlying Income Tax rules, failure to satisfy substance requirements for a relevant activity attracts a fine of up to GIP 10,000 for the first year and GIP 50,000 for subsequent years; continued non-compliance can result in strike-off by the Registrar and denial of tax residence certificates. For a Cayman entity, the Cayman Tax Information Authority may impose penalties up to US$10,000 plus withdrawals of exemptions.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners can be jointly and severally liable for company fines where the failure is attributable to their deliberate conduct. The Supreme Court may issue disqualification orders, travel bans, and civil restitution orders against directors who abuse the corporate form.
AML / KYC / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Money laundering offences under POCA: up to 14 years' imprisonment, an unlimited fine, and forfeiture of the laundered property. AML Regulation breaches: fines up to GIP 1,000,000 or 10% of global turnover. Failure to file a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) or to maintain beneficial ownership records under the Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Regulations 2017: fines up to GIP 5,000 per officer plus daily default fines.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability attaches to directors, money laundering reporting officers and beneficial owners. Directors may be disqualified for up to 10 years; travel bans and confiscation orders over UK, Gibraltar and foreign assets are commonly imposed, and the corporate veil can be pierced to recover proceeds.
Transfer Pricing / Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustments increasing assessable profits; interest on unpaid tax; penalties up to 100% of additional tax arising from careless or deliberate transfer pricing errors; documentation penalties under the Taxation (Transfer Pricing) Rules 2018 ranging from GIP 3,000 to GIP 250,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign incorrect transfer pricing declarations may be personally fined to the extent of the additional tax plus 25%. In cases of insolvency, the Commissioner may apply for a court order making directors personally liable for the company's unpaid tax attributable to their deliberate conduct.
Late Filing and Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Initial penalty £100; after 3 months £500; after 6 months a daily surcharge of £10 per day up to £1,500. Companies Registry penalties up to GIP 5,000 per director for non-filing of accounts. Strike-off results in dissolution and the Crown vesting of company assets.

Director personal liability: Directors of a struck-off company who continue trading incur personal liability for company debts. Personal fines up to GIP 5,000 per director; director disqualification for up to 5 years; joint and several liability for tax debts can be extended to non-resident directors where they were involved in management and control.
