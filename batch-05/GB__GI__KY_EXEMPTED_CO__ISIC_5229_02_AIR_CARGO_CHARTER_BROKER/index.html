Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), HM Government of Gibraltar — the sole revenue authority for Gibraltar direct taxation; official portal and e-filing services are accessed via the HMGoG taxation section (https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation). Indirect-tax/customs competence sits with HM Customs Gibraltar, which operates the electronic customs declaration system aligned with the UK CDS framework.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Foreign-company branch registration: within one month of establishing a place of business in Gibraltar, the Cayman Exempted Company files with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies (certified Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Good Standing, constitutional documents, Gibraltar registered office, locally appointed process agent). Step 2 — TRN application: submit the company tax-registration form to the Income Tax Office, disclosing ultimate beneficial owners, board composition, and management-and-control location; the ITO issues the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN), the sole taxpayer identifier for corporate income tax, PAYE, and annual returns. Step 3 — Employer/Social Insurance registration: register as an employer for PAYE and Gibraltar Social Insurance if personnel are engaged. Step 4 — Customs registration: obtain the customs/ECC identifier from HM Customs Gibraltar before lodging any import, transit, or re-export declarations. Step 5 — Cayman-side continuity: maintain the exempted-company status with the Cayman Islands General Registry and register with the Cayman Tax Information Authority for CRS/FATCA so the zero-tax classification remains effective.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Cayman home status) overlaid by Gibraltar Territorial_Scope: the Exempted Company pays zero tax in the Cayman Islands, but once managed and controlled from Gibraltar it becomes Gibraltar tax resident. Under Gibraltar's territorial system only profits accruing in or derived from Gibraltar, including branch/PE-attributable income, are taxed at the 12.5% corporate rate; foreign-source trading income remains outside the Gibraltar charge.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists: a Cayman Exempted Company is a non-transparent body corporate and cannot elect fiscal transparency in Gibraltar; tax status is determined by the statutory management-and-control residence test and permanent-establishment rules. Available mechanisms: (i) formal relocation of central management and control to Gibraltar, triggering Gibraltar residence; (ii) application for Qualifying Company (QC) status under the Income Tax Act, taxable at a reduced 2% rate on assessed income but excluded from tax-treaty benefits, where statutorily available; (iii) territorial-source election limiting a non-resident PE's charge to Gibraltar-source receipts; and (iv) OECD/EU-aligned anti-hybrid rules, under which hybrid mismatch relief is denied rather than elected.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax in force in Gibraltar; the only indirect taxation nexus is the Gibraltar Customs Import Duty (tariff and excise) system, which sits outside the UK and EU VAT frameworks. Cross-border air-cargo charter brokerage services are outside the scope of any Gibraltar indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT/GST registration threshold exists because no VAT regime is in force; the operative customs trigger is the first physical entry of goods into Gibraltar (effectively £0/GIP0 — any import declaration activates customs registration). A pure brokerage business that never handles goods in Gibraltar has no indirect-tax registration trigger.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating is inapplicable as Gibraltar levies no VAT. Customs import-duty relief or zero-rating is available for a licensed broker only where charter movements involve goods physically leaving Gibraltar under a transit, temporary-admission, or re-export procedure; eligibility requires proof through the customs system that the cargo was carried to a destination outside Gibraltar and that the brokerage service was not connected with local consumption. No Gibraltar input-VAT refund mechanism exists, and no UK/EU VAT incurred on costs can be reclaimed in Gibraltar.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (SAD/C89) or transit report
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing international carriage out of Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT MT103) evidencing foreign-currency settlement
  • Charter Party Agreement and broker commission invoice
  • Destination-country customs entry or delivery receipt

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based invoicing, or digital stamping mandate operates in Gibraltar for ordinary B2B services. Income Tax returns and TRN-linked notifications must be filed electronically via the HMGoG Income Tax Office portal; customs entries are lodged electronically through the Gibraltar customs declaration system aligned with the UK CDS framework. Business invoices and underlying trade records must be retained for at least six years.

The Income Tax Office and HM Customs audit paper-export and fake-transit schemes against AWB numbers, fuel and landing logs, and remittance evidence. Unsupported or fraudulent export/duty-relief claims trigger retrospective assessment, interest, and a penalty of up to 100% of the tax or duty evaded, plus criminal liability for false declarations under the Income Tax Act and Gibraltar customs legislation; a Cayman Exempted Company abusing Gibraltar territorial status also risks loss of Qualifying Company or exempt treatment, strike-off, and reporting under Gibraltar's mandatory-disclosure rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. The United Kingdom and Gibraltar have maintained no exchange-control regime since the repeal of the Exchange Control Act 1947. For a Cayman Exempted Company owning a Gibraltar branch or operational office, profit remittance, dividend distribution, and liquidation proceeds can be transferred freely without prior central-bank approval and without Cayman or Gibraltar withholding tax, provided the company is not managed and controlled in Gibraltar (in which case Gibraltarian income tax at 10% applies on locally attributable profits). The GIP is pegged at par to GBP; USD, EUR, and GBP are all freely circulating in the air-cargo charter brokerage settlement chain.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. There is no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation for export-related receipts in the United Kingdom, Gibraltar, or the Cayman Islands. Freight commissions, charter hire, and brokerage fees derived from ISIC 5229 may be retained in USD, EUR, or GBP indefinitely. The only compliance obligation is statistical: banks must report cross-border flows under the Bank of England's International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) and the GFSC's Financial Crime Returns, none of which impose repatriation or conversion deadlines.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (United Kingdom) as the monetary authority for sterling; Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the statutory banking and financial services regulator in Gibraltar; the Government of Gibraltar as the issuing authority for the Gibraltar pound (GIP); and the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) as the corporate and economic substance supervisor for the Cayman Exempted Company.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a primary banking rail. UK FCA-regulated electronic money institutions (e.g., Revolut Business, Tide, Wise Business) and BaaS platforms (e.g., Currencycloud, Modulr) generally auto-decline Cayman tax-exempt corporate structures, particularly following the February 2024 FATF grey-listing of the Cayman Islands; they are also prohibited from extending credit and cannot replicate escrow or client-money segregation for a freight brokerage. Such an EMI may be used only as an ancillary collection float where the provider accepts an enhanced due-diligence dossier and the company demonstrates a genuine UK or Gibraltar operational nexus.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder funds are remitted by SWIFT MT103 from a Cayman Islands banking relationship (e.g., Butterfield, HSBC Cayman, or CIBC) to the Gibraltar operational account at Trusted Novus Bank, with field 70 stating 'SHARE CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / NON-TRADE / ISIC 5229' and the Bank of England ITRS purpose classification for direct investment in equity capital. The supporting verification dossier comprises the board resolution approving the allotment, updated register of members, share certificates, source-of-wealth statement of the parent shareholder, and a parent bank reference; funds are initially parked in the Capital Escrow Account and released to the Operational Account only after activation conditions, including a compliance clearance, are satisfied.Charter hire, airline settlement, handling fees, and sub-agent commissions are released via SWIFT MT103/COVER (MT202) using the Gibraltar bank's correspondent network: CHAPS for GBP, Fedwire for USD via a US correspondent such as BNY Mellon or Citibank NA, and TARGET2 for EUR. For charter deposits exceeding USD 250,000, a Commercial Letter of Credit governed by UCP600 (MT700) confirmed by a UK or international bank is permissible, with settlement against presentation of the signed charter confirmation, fuel and handling invoices, and the air waybill number.
