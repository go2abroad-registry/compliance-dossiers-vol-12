Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Cayman domicile only: Tax Exemption Certificate under the Cayman Tax Concessions Act excludes Cayman income, corporate and withholding taxes for up to 20 years; such exemption is not recognized in Gibraltar) + Entity_Level_Taxation in Gibraltar (corporation tax at 12.5% on profits attributable to the Gibraltar permanent establishment or trade) + Territorial_Scope (Gibraltar charges tax on profits arising in or derived from Gibraltar; non-resident companies are taxed only on Gibraltar-source trade profits, but management-and-control exercised from Gibraltar would expand the territorial charge to worldwide profits).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Gibraltar law; entity classification is determined by statute and corporate form. Available elections and reliefs: (i) group loss surrender election for 75%-owned Gibraltar group companies under the Income Tax Act; (ii) participation exemption notification for qualifying dividends and chargeable gains on substantial shareholdings; (iii) election to apply arm's-length transfer pricing on cross-border related-party transactions; (iv) hybrid mismatch rules under BEPS Action 2 may disallow deductions to the extent of non-taxation in Cayman if a deduction/non-inclusion mismatch arises - no elective opt-out is available; (v) Cayman-side only: election to apply for or renew the Tax Exemption Certificate under the Cayman Tax Concessions Act, which has no Gibraltar effect.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 100,000 of annual taxable turnover (Gibraltar pound, at par with GBP) from taxable supplies made in Gibraltar, subject to annual Budget revision. Non-resident suppliers making any taxable supply in Gibraltar, and exporters making zero-rated export supplies, are registrable without threshold benefit; compulsory registration applies from the first supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods (industrial quick-frozen steamed dim sum and dumplings) are zero-rated under the Consumption Tax Act 2001 where: (i) the goods are physically removed from Gibraltar customs territory to a destination outside Gibraltar (UK, EU, or third country) within the prescribed period, generally 3 months from the time of supply; (ii) the supplier holds the customs export declaration (SAD) and transport documentation naming it as consignor; and (iii) the export proceeds are received through the banking system and traceable to the shipment. Qualifying offshore services supplied to non-residents (including refrigerated processing, storage, or logistics services in connection with export goods) may also be zero-rated on the consumption-outside-Gibraltar test. Food product exports to the UK/EU must additionally satisfy the destination jurisdiction's import health, sanitary, and origin requirements.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document/C88) with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill/CMR consignment note evidencing physical removal from Gibraltar customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate and SWIFT MT103 wire confirmation evidencing crediting of export proceeds to the company's bank account
  • Commercial invoice and packing list in export form
  • Certificate of Origin (British/Gibraltarian origin) and any preferential origin certificate required by the destination customs regime
  • Export Health Certificate / veterinary certificate for food products of animal origin (pork or poultry fillings) issued by the competent Gibraltar authority and accepted by the importing country
  • Cold-chain temperature logger records demonstrating continuous frozen storage at or below -18°C during transit

No real-time fiscal clearance, API-based clearance, or digital stamping mandate exists in Gibraltar as of the effective date. Consumption Tax invoices must satisfy statutory content requirements (sequential numbering, supplier and purchaser identity, Gibraltar VAT identification number, description, quantity, taxable amount, VAT rate and amount, and zero-rating reference). Standard electronic invoices (PDF or structured data) are valid provided integrity and legibility are preserved. Export declarations must be lodged electronically through the Gibraltar Customs declaration environment as a Single Administrative Document (SAD) with a Movement Reference Number (MRN); no additional national stamping or certification is required. Records supporting indirect tax positions must be retained for 6 years.

Gibraltar Customs & Excise and the Commissioner of Income Tax may audit export zero-rating claims for up to 4 years after the relevant accounting period, extended to 20 years where fraud or willful default is alleged. If the export documentation bundle is incomplete or export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day benchmark, the supply is recharacterized as a standard-rated domestic supply and assessed to Consumption Tax at 12.5%, with interest and late payment penalties. Fraudulent or negligent zero-rating claims attract civil penalties of up to 100% of the tax undercharged, criminal prosecution with fines and/or imprisonment of up to 7 years, personal liability of directors, publication in the serious defaulter list, and, for food exporters, suspension or revocation of the statutory food establishment license and customs authorized exporter status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The structure enjoys full current-account and capital-account convertibility in both Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands. The GIP is pegged at 1:1 to GBP with unrestricted convertibility into GBP, EUR, USD and other major currencies; no Gibraltar or Bank of England approval is required for any current-account remittance (trade payments, dividends, interest, royalties) or capital-account movement (equity injection, shareholder loans, capital return). The Cayman Exempted Company regime imposes no exchange controls, no withholding tax, and no profit-remittance licensing; the only formality is a pro-forma Cayman Currency Control filing for share-issuance records. The practical profit-remittance hurdle is documentary rather than regulatory: distributions must be supported by audited financial statements, a board resolution, and a Gibraltar remittance instruction evidencing no AML concern regarding the ultimate beneficial owner. Because GIP is pegged to GBP, the group should invoice exports in GBP for UK-retail counterparties, in EUR for continental EU buyers, and in USD or CNH for Asian trade, leveraging freedom of foreign-currency accounts without surrender or conversion mandate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rule or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Gibraltar or in the Cayman Islands. Export receipts (frozen dim sum and dumpling sales to UK supermarkets, EU distributors, and Asian importers) may be retained indefinitely in the currency of receipt in a segregated foreign-currency treasury account, or converted at the group's discretion under a documented FX policy. For IAS 21 and Cayman GAAP purposes, the Gibraltarian operating subsidiary is the functional-currency analysis unit; export proceeds converted into GIP at the 1:1 GBP peg do not create transactional FX exposure, whereas USD, EUR, and CNY proceeds must be revalued at each reporting date. No statutory timeline for repatriation exists; however, a treasury best-practice '30-day recycling rule' is recommended for sweeping export cash into the main Gibraltar operating account for working-capital pooling.

Central bank supervision: No dedicated Gibraltar central bank exists; HM Government of Gibraltar issues Gibraltar Pound (GIP) currency notes under a 100% GBP reserve backing, with monetary policy anchored by the Bank of England. Banking prudential supervision is administered by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Banks Act. For the Cayman Exempted Company domicile, the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) exercises oversight under the Banks and Trust Companies Act, and the Cayman Exchange Control is administered by CIMA as delegated authority under the Currency Control Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. FCA-authorised UK digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) and EEA-licensed EMI platforms can legally hold a multicurrency account for a Gibraltarian operating entity, but most will decline a Cayman Exempted Company as the direct account holder because the legal form is classified as an offshore corporate vehicle in their AML matrices. Where onboarding is accepted, the Cayman vehicle is usually accompanied by the Gibraltar-licensed factory as the operating subsidiary, a two-year audited financial history, and a genuine economic-substance statement. Gibraltar has no indigenous EMI licensing track for such vehicles; the GFSC does not passport EMI licences from the EEA post-Brexit, and the Gibraltar AML Guidance requires EMIs to treat Cayman Exempted Companies with enhanced due diligence. Recommended architecture: use the Gibraltar tier-1 bank for primary liquidity and an FCA-regulated EMI only as a collection console for sub-€50,000 cross-border B2B receivables, sweeping daily to the main Gibraltar account. EMI accounts must not be used for export-proceeds warehousing, customs-duty settlement, or payroll, as those functions require a licensed Gibraltar bank for GFSC regulatory comfort.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound route: Cayman Exempted Company's USD account at a Cayman licensed class-A bank sends a SWIFT MT103 via New York or UK correspondent clearers to the Barclays or Banco de Sabadell Gibraltar account in GBP or USD. The MT103 must carry: (a) field 50K with the exact legal name of the Cayman company; (b) field 57D with the beneficiary Gibraltar bank and account; (c) field 70 with remittance information 'Cayman Exempted Co — paid-up equity injection for Gibraltar dim sum factory capitalisation'; (d) field 23B set to CRED; and (e) where required by the correspondent, a UN/CEFACT purpose narrative 'Capital transfer — equity injection'. Pre-wire verification packet: Cayman board resolution authorising the capital movement; updated share register and members' ledger; pro-forma Cayman Currency Control filing; signed source-of-funds forms; and the Gibraltar bank's pre-credit compliance clearance memo. No Bank of England or Gibraltar Exchange Control consent is required, and no official deposit ratio or notification deadline applies; nevertheless, the wire should be booked with a 24-hour hold for correspondent-bank sanctions and posting confirmation. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-Low. FCA-authorised UK digital EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) and EEA-licensed EMI platforms can legally hold a multicurrency account for a Gibraltarian operating entity, but most will decline a Cayman Exempted Company as the direct account holder because the legal form is classified as an offshore corporate vehicle in their AML matrices. Where onboarding is accepted, the Cayman vehicle is usually accompanied by the Gibraltar-licensed factory as the operating subsidiary, a two-year audited financial history, and a genuine economic-substance statement. Gibraltar has no indigenous EMI licensing track for such vehicles; the GFSC does not passport EMI licences from the EEA post-Brexit, and the Gibraltar AML Guidance requires EMIs to treat Cayman Exempted Companies with enhanced due diligence. Recommended architecture: use the Gibraltar tier-1 bank for primary liquidity and an FCA-regulated EMI only as a collection console for sub-€50,000 cross-border B2B receivables, sweeping daily to the main Gibraltar account. EMI accounts must not be used for export-proceeds warehousing, customs-duty settlement, or payroll, as those functions require a licensed Gibraltar bank for GFSC regulatory comfort.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% in Cayman under the Exempted Company tax undertaking; 12.5% Gibraltar corporation tax on any profits attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment or to central management and control exercised in Gibraltar.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% in Gibraltar and 0% in the Cayman Islands. Gibraltar does not impose withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar company to its shareholders, and the Cayman Islands imposes no income tax or withholding tax on dividends paid by an exempted company.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared for a Gibraltar entity when the value of controlled transactions exceeds GIP 1,000,000 in any accounting period; a Master File is required when the multinational group has related-party revenue exceeding GIP 50,000,000. The Cayman parent must maintain transfer pricing records if it holds the factory assets, recipes, brand intangibles, or financing that generates related-party payments from the Gibraltar operation.

Economic substance scope: The Cayman Exempted Company itself is subject to Cayman Economic Substance Regulations for relevant activities, but an active frozen-food manufacturing factory is not an automatic ESR relevant activity unless structured as a distribution and service centre, financing or IP holding activity. In Gibraltar, the Income Tax Act requires every company resident in Gibraltar to demonstrate real economic substance; a factory with physical premises, permanent employees, utility consumption and production machinery establishes a permanent establishment and can be a valid economic substance centre only if the business is actually managed and directed from Gibraltar. A foreign Cayman exempted company cannot claim Cayman tax immunity for industrial profits while exercising de facto management, manufacturing, and sales decisions in Gibraltar; tax theory will either create a Gibraltar PE or trigger Gibraltar tax-resident status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Abusive Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 100% of the tax underassessed, daily default fines, interest compounded, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion; Gibraltar courts may additionally impose a fine of up to GBP 100,000 and imprisonment for six years. In the Cayman Islands, providing false declarations to the Registrar or Tax Information Authority is an offence punishable by a fine of CI$10,000 per offence and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly submit false returns, conceal the Gibraltar PE, or divert profits to the tax-exempt Cayman vehicle can be personally assessed for the unpaid corporate tax; they may face civil restitution, disqualification from acting as directors, travel bans preventing departure from Gibraltar, freezing of personal bank accounts, and custodial sentences in cases of deliberate concealment.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Loss of the Cayman exempted-company undertaking may be declared void; Gibraltar may treat the Cayman entity as a Gibraltar tax-resident company on all worldwide profits. Penalties for failing to meet economic substance requirements in Gibraltar range from financial penalties to compulsory strike-off; Cayman ESR non-compliance attracts CI$10,000 penalties per relevant activity and escalating to CI$100,000 plus daily penalties for deliberate non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors and corporate service providers may be jointly liable for penalties; personal liability extends to nominees who sign false declarations of non-tax residence. Courts may pierce the corporate veil and order disgorgement of all fees and dividends received; directors can be prohibited from leaving Gibraltar and from managing any Gibraltar-licensed company for up to 10 years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: Tax authority may compel the preparation of documentation, apply a transfer pricing correction, disallow expense deductions, and impose penalties of GBP 50,000 plus additional tax of up to 35% of any arm's length adjustment. Large groups may also face Country-by-Country reporting penalties and multilateral exchange-of-information sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to ensure contemporaneous documentation and who approve non-arm's-length prices may be held personally liable for the resultant corporate tax deficiency if the company has been left without assets through related-party distributions; imprisonment may result from false statements made during a transfer pricing audit.
Disguised Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The Gibraltar tax authority will recharacterise excess payments as constructive dividends, deny deductibility, apply withholding tax if any is chargeable, and assess interest and penalties up to 100% of the additional tax. Criminal investigation may follow if the recharacterisation reveals fraudulent intent.

Director personal liability: Directors who structure sham payment flows to return cash to Cayman may be personally liable for breach of fiduciary duty, repayment of the disguised dividends, and civil penalties. If the Gibraltar company becomes insolvent, directors may face wrongful trading claims and personal asset tracing through the Cayman corporate veil.
AML, Beneficial Ownership and Banking Integrity Failures

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act penalties include unlimited fines and up to 14 years' imprisonment for money laundering; Cayman beneficial ownership non-compliance attracts fines of CI$25,000 and up to 5 years' imprisonment for officers and money laundering reporting officers. The corporate bank/EMI relationship will be terminated, and the company may be wound up at public expense.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to AML sanctions, confiscation orders, travel bans and disqualification; directors who allow the company to trade under false ownership declarations can be made personally liable for unpaid invoices and creditor losses, and may be subject to extradition proceedings within the UK-Cayman-Gibraltar legal area.
