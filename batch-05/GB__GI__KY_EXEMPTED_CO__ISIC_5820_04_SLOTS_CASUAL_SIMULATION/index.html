Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Cayman Exempted Company is statutorily exempt from Cayman Islands direct taxes (income, capital, withholding) for up to 30 years under the Section 6 Tax Exemption Undertaking, and is not subject to any Cayman economic substance requirement for pure holding/IP activity. For Gibraltar purposes, classification is dual: (a) non-resident company subject to territorial sourcing rules limited to Gibraltar-PE trading profits, or (b) resident company subject to entity-level Corporation Tax at 12.5% on worldwide profits if central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar. The entity is not fiscally transparent in either the Cayman Islands or Gibraltar; it is a distinct legal person with limited liability.

Available election forms

Available elections: (1) Cayman Islands Tax Exemption Undertaking (Section 6, Tax Concessions Act Cap.152) — one-time statutory election providing binding immunity from all Cayman direct taxes for 20 years, extendable by a further 10 years (maximum 30 years). (2) Gibraltar hybrid-mismatch election under the Taxation (Hybrid Mismatch) Rules (implementing EU ATAD 2, effective 1 January 2020) — if the Cayman entity is treated as fiscally transparent under another jurisdiction's rules (e.g., a US Form 8832 check-the-box election), the ITO must be notified and a mirroring adjustment applied to prevent deduction/non-inclusion outcomes. (3) US Form 8832 'check-the-box' election may be used by US owners to classify the Cayman entity as disregarded or partnership for US federal tax, but such election has no Gibraltar tax effect because Gibraltar Corporation Tax applies at the entity level without regard to foreign classification. (4) No Gibraltar 'check-the-box' regime exists; no statutory election permits pass-through treatment of a company for Gibraltar tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar does not operate a VAT or GST system. Indirect taxation in Gibraltar consists of: (a) Import duties under the Gibraltar Customs Tariff (Customs Ordinance), (b) Excise duties on alcohol, tobacco and hydrocarbon oils, and (c) a remote gambling duty only for licensed real-money gambling operators under the Gambling Act 2005 — not applicable to simulated social casino with non-cashable virtual credits. For the app-store channel, UK VAT at 20% on UK consumer purchases is collected and remitted by Apple/Google as deemed marketplace suppliers under UK VATA 1994 Schedule 9A; EU consumer supplies are handled under the EU OSS/VAT regime by the platforms. The Cayman entity's developer receipts are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: nil threshold for customs registration — every importer of goods into Gibraltar must register with HM Customs Gibraltar; excise licensing applies only to alcohol/tobacco/fuel (not relevant). There is no VAT/GST registration threshold in Gibraltar because no VAT/GST exists. UK VAT: the Cayman entity is not required to register for UK VAT because Apple IAP and Google Play Billing are deemed suppliers of the digital services to consumers under UK VATA 1994 Schedule 9A; the entity's consideration attributable to the platforms is outside the scope. If the entity were ever to make direct B2C digital supplies in the UK outside a marketplace, the £85,000 UK VAT registration threshold (GBP) would apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar itself has no VAT, so no Gibraltar zero-rating mechanism exists. For UK VAT purposes (if any UK registration becomes relevant): (a) physical exports of goods from the UK are zero-rated at 0% provided the goods leave the UK within the applicable time limits and the exporter retains prescribed evidence of export; (b) B2B supplies of digital services (including social casino publishing services) are outside the scope of UK VAT with the customer self-assessing reverse charge; (c) B2C digital services supplied to non-UK/non-EU consumers are outside the scope of UK VAT; for UK consumers the marketplace operators (Apple/Google) are the deemed suppliers and charge 20% UK VAT. The simulated social casino business under ISIC 5820 with virtual credits and no real-money wagering qualifies as a supply of digital entertainment services, not gambling services, and therefore no Gibraltar remote gambling duty or UK gambling tax applies. For export proceeds substantiation, this structure adopts a 180-day inward remittance benchmark (see below) to evidence the collection chain; no Gibraltar exchange-control statute imposes a fixed statutory repatriation deadline.

  • Official Customs Export Declaration (UK SAD/C88 or HM Customs Gibraltar export entry) with Unique Consignment Reference (UCR) evidencing the export movement
  • Transport document proving physical export: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, CMR, or electronic export evidence issued by the carrier
  • Forex/inward remittance certificate or SWIFT MT103 bank statement evidencing collection of export sales proceeds within the 180-day repatriation benchmark

Gibraltar: no real-time fiscal clearance, no compulsory electronic invoicing, no digital stamping or SAF-T filing requirement; ordinary PDF/paper invoices satisfy Income Tax Act 2010 record-keeping obligations (minimum 6 years). UK indirect interface: if UK VAT registration were separately triggered (not applicable under the marketplace model), Making Tax Digital (MTD) would require functional compatible software for quarterly digital VAT returns — the UK has no PEPPOL e-invoicing mandate and no continuous transaction controls. Customs declarations into/out of Gibraltar and the UK use electronic SAD/C88 submissions via brokers; app-store transaction receipts (Apple/Google billing statements) serve as the principal evidential documents for platform fee reconciliation, AML/KYC and transfer-pricing audits.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating/export claims: UK — civil penalty of up to 100% of the VAT evaded (VATA 1994 Schedule 24), conduct-based penalties (30% careless, 70% deliberate, 100% deliberate and concealed), default interest, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion under VATA 1994 s.72 with imprisonment up to 7 years; customs export misdeclarations attract penalties under CEMA 1979, forfeiture and seizure of goods. Gibraltar — under the Income Tax Act 2010, knowingly false returns attract under-assessment penalties up to 100% of additional tax, statutory interest, and criminal sanctions; HM Customs Gibraltar may impose duty evasion penalties (up to treble the duty or £3,000 per offence) and confiscation. Retrospective clawback: the taxpayer must retain full export evidence and underlying app-store transaction records for at least 6 years (20 years where fraud or concealment is established); missing or fabricated evidence reopens the assessment period and reverses the zero-rating/outside-scope treatment with interest from the original due date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both the Cayman Islands and Gibraltar maintain fully convertible currencies with no exchange controls. Cayman: capital and current-account items are freely convertible; the KYD/USD statutory peg of 1 USD = 0.80 KYD (i.e., 1 KYD = 1.25 USD) has been in force since 1974; profit and dividend remittance from an Exempted Company is unrestricted and is not subject to Central Bank approval or any withholding, because Cayman imposes no direct taxation. Gibraltar: GIP is freely convertible at par with GBP; there are no restrictions on capital-account transactions, profit repatriation, or dividend distribution; a Cayman Exempted Company that does not manage and control its affairs in Gibraltar remains outside Gibraltar income tax, so profit remittance through GBP/EUR/USD is unhindered. The practical hurdle is bank-level AML/CDD, not statutory FX control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion obligation exists in the United Kingdom, Gibraltar, or the Cayman Islands. There is no prescribed timeline and no conversion ratio for export proceeds, including App-Store digital-content receipts; the company may lawfully retain USD, EUR, and GBP balances indefinitely. The former UK Exchange Control Act 1947 generally ceased to apply with effect from 1979, and neither the Cayman Islands Currency Law (2021 Revision) nor Gibraltar's Currency Notes Act imposes a surrender rule on software-publishing receivables. Banks apply only statistical reporting thresholds (e.g., UK International Transactions Reporting System/Balance of Payments reporting above GBP 50,000) and neither CIMA nor the GFSC requires repatriation or conversion of foreign-currency revenue.

Central bank supervision: Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) — central bank and currency authority of the Cayman Islands, domicile of the Exempted Company; for the Gibraltar operational jurisdiction, the relevant authorities are the Government of Gibraltar (issuer of Gibraltar pound notes at par with sterling under the Currency Notes Act 1934) and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), which licenses and supervises banks and e-money institutions; ultimate sterling monetary policy is set by the Bank of England because the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged 1:1 to GBP.
