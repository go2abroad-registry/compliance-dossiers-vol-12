Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar, Income Tax Office (Ministry of Finance); official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/tax-and-customs

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company entering Gibraltar must (1) file a foreign-company application with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Companies Act 2014, submitting its Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles, and registered-office details; (2) complete Form 1 at the Income Tax Office to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (GIN), disclosing the company's Cayman tax-exempt status, place of management, and anticipated profits; (3) if staff are engaged locally, register as an employer for PAYE and Gibraltar Social Insurance contributions; (4) no VAT/GST registration is required as Gibraltar imposes no general indirect tax, but importation of goods requires customs registration with the Gibraltar Customs Service, including an EORI number.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Sales and Use Tax

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general indirect tax registration threshold applies because Gibraltar does not operate VAT/GST or a comprehensive sales tax. Customs registration is triggered by any importation of goods for use in Gibraltar irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar imposes no VAT or GST, so export software development services supplied by the Cayman Exempted Company from Gibraltar are outside the indirect tax base; no zero-rate claim or VAT refund is required. Eligibility for any Gibraltar income-tax relief requires the services to be demonstrably performed for non-resident counterparties, supported by contracts, time and delivery records, and foreign consideration remitted from abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping for indirect tax purposes. For customs export formalities, electronic export declarations are lodged through the Gibraltar/UK customs systems; ordinary commercial invoices and service agreements are acceptable for software services.

Fraudulent or unsupported export and tax-relief claims expose the entity to full retrospective reassessment by the Gibraltar Income Tax Office, statutory interest of up to 1% per month, penalties up to 100% of tax understated, and criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act and Customs Ordinance. The normal look-back period is 6 years, extended without limit in cases of deliberate fraud or concealment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains a fully liberalised current and capital account. There are no exchange-control licences, no prior-approval mechanisms, and no restrictions on inbound or outbound capital movements. GIP, GBP, EUR, and USD may be freely transferred and settled. Profit remittance by a Cayman Exempted Company from its Gibraltar-held accounts to the Cayman parent is unrestricted, subject only to standard AML/sanctions documentation. Gibraltar does not impose withholding taxes on dividends, interest, royalties, or management fees paid to a non-resident Cayman entity; however, if the Cayman Exempted Company is managed and controlled from Gibraltar, it will be subject to Gibraltar corporation tax and must file under the Income Tax Act with economic substance documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Gibraltar has no mandatory export-proceeds surrender rules, no statutory conversion ratios, and no timeline within which export service receipts must be repatriated or converted. A dedicated offshore software development centre may retain all export earnings in the original invoiced currency. When settlement is made through a UK correspondent, the UK anti-money-laundering framework requires only appropriate purpose codes and sanctions screening; no conversion or surrender obligation exists.

Central bank supervision: Financial Services Commission (FSC) of Gibraltar; monetary policy, note issuance, and the Gibraltar pound (GIP) are administered by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act, with GIP pegged 1:1 to UK Pound Sterling. UK HM Treasury and the Bank of England are relevant for any cross-border transaction routed through UK clearing or correspondent banking systems.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-High viability for UK-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs), specifically Wise B2B and Payoneer, provided the Cayman Exempted Company is accompanied by a full substance and beneficial-ownership dossier. Legal constraint: the EMI must hold either an FCA e-money licence or an equivalent EEA authorization and must regard Gibraltar as a permitted territory under its regional coverage. Wise and Payoneer offer GBP, EUR, and USD virtual IBANs and support SWIFT/Faster Payments/SEPA/ACH clearing; however, a tax-exempt Cayman vehicle without demonstrable operating activity in Gibraltar may be rejected at onboarding or capped at lower transaction thresholds. A Gibraltar-local EMI alternative is scarce, so the primary recommendation is a UK-licensed EMI with a Gibraltar-registered office and a Gibraltar bank as the underlying settlement account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

A Cayman Islands Exempted Company of low operational substance applies for a Gibraltar corporate account, creating a tax-evasion or sham-substance red flag.

Root cause: A Cayman Islands Exempted Company of low operational substance applies for a Gibraltar corporate account, creating a tax-evasion or sham-substance red flag.

Defense evidence: Deliver (1) current Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum & Articles, and Cayman Certificate of Good Standing; (2) full beneficial ownership register naming all directors and ultimate individuals; (3) evidence of the Gibraltar nexus: registered-office agreement, lease or sub-lease for the development centre, five client invoices for software engineering, payroll and labour contracts; (4) board resolution approving the account opening and treasury signatories; (5) the Gibraltar Income Tax Act or FSC compliance memorandum confirming either non-residence or full PE compliance; (6) a 24-month cash-flow forecast and source-of-wealth statement for the injected capital; and (7) a letter of good conduct from the Cayman registered agent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from the ultimate parent or founders into the Cayman Exempted Company's Gibraltar treasury account must be routed through SWIFT MT103 for direct single remittance or MT202 COVER for interbank settlement. Recommended purpose codes: use 'EC' (direct investment / equity capital) for EEA/UK-origin transfers, or UK BOP code 'A3000' for investment flows; if routed through a US correspondent, use 'RE' (real property?) or 'W1' (deposits) only if the funds are clearly equity; otherwise use code 'CM' for capital payments. Verification required includes: Cayman board resolution, source-of-funds statement, UBO identification, copy of the capital contribution ledger entry, and, if the injection exceeds GBP 50,000, a source-of-wealth statement with prior bank statements or security valuation. Use only the Gibraltar bank's stated correspondent network and do not route equity through domestic faster-payment rails unless the provider explicitly supports corporate investment flows.For software subcontractors, payroll, licences, and infra costs, use SWIFT MT103 with a unique end-to-end reference across all clearing rails. For GBP-denominated vendor payments within the UK, use the Faster Payments Service (FPS) or CHAPS if the Gibraltar bank has direct membership; for EUR clearing, route via SEPA or SEPA Instant using a TARGET2-connected correspondent; for USD, use Fedwire or CHIPS through the bank's US correspondent. For large institutional settlements, the issuing bank may open a Standby or Commercial Letter of Credit (SBLC/LC) against the client work contract; however, because the underlying ODC business is service-based, documentary collections (DP/DA) are replaced by TT/SWIFT MT103 settlement. A Wise B2B or Payoneer currency account may be used for low-value disbursements to remote developers, but the disbursement instruction must reference the client invoice and the task order. All output payments must be screened and recorded in the treasury audit trail.Outright FX forwards between GIP/GBP, EUR, and USD for hedging invoiced software receivables and committed supplier payables; executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or a local master derivatives addendum.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for currencies of offshore developer locations where onshore settlement is restricted or illiquid, such as INR, PHP, or NGN; subject to the counterparty bank's credit-line and collateral margin rules.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) to align the Cayman Exempted Company's USD functional currency liquidity against GIP/GBP-denominated operating expenses and intercompany obligations; speculative trading is prohibited and all hedges must be formally documented and board-approved.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 (one million Gibraltar pounds) of gross controlled transactions requiring mandatory contemporaneous Local File; Group-wide Master File is mandatory where consolidated group revenue exceeds GIP 50,000,000 and the Gibraltar entity's controlled transactions exceed GIP 750,000.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2024 applies to all companies, including redomiciled Cayman Exempted Companies conducting an Offshore Development Centre business. The relevant activity is 'providing IT services and software development', classified as a 'core income-generating activity' under the Act. A resident entity must satisfy all of: (i) management and control of the ODC business is exercised in Gibraltar; (ii) an adequate number of qualified employees are physically present in Gibraltar (full-time software engineers and project managers, proportionate to the volume of contracted work); (iii) adequate physical premises and equipment in Gibraltar are held or leased exclusively for the ODC; (iv) core decision-making on pricing, client contracts, staffing, and risk assumption takes place in Gibraltar; and (v) the entity derives real economic value from the activities and does not merely invoice group services. If a Cayman Exempted Company is non-resident in Gibraltar, it must either demonstrate that it does not carry on a Gibraltar relevant activity or must appoint a Gibraltar representative and file an economic substance declaration. Failure to satisfy the economic substance test triggers penalties up to GIP 100,000 per year of default, a mandatory 5-year compliance audit, and possible strike-off or revocation of tax residence status where the entity is managed from Gibraltar but lacks presence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Non-Filing

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under the Income Tax Act 2010 and the Criminal Justice Act. Financial penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded plus interest; criminal fine at the discretion of the court of up to GIP 500,000. The Commissioner may issue a civil penalty of GIP 10,000 per instance of false statement and a further GIP 100 per day for continued non-disclosure.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors face personal civil restitution for tax lost plus joint and several liability. Criminal conviction can result in personal fines up to GIP 100,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years. Travel bans may be imposed under the Court of Appeal's asset-preservation jurisdiction, and directors of a struck-off Cayman Exempted Company may be banned from acting as directors for up to 15 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty under the Companies (Economic Substance) Act 2024: GIP 100,000 for the initial failure; GIP 20,000 per month for continuing failure. The Registrar may remove the company from the Gibraltar register, revoke tax residence, or require compulsory winding up. The company may also be denied treaty benefits and subject to a 10% punitive assessment on gross revenue where records are inadequate.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for penalties where the failure resulted from the director's intentional or reckless conduct. The court may order a director to pay all unpaid tax and penalties, impose a travel ban, and disqualify the director from serving as a director in any jurisdiction where mutual disqualification is recognised. Deliberate misdeclaration in the economic substance return gives rise to personal criminal liability and imprisonment for up to 5 years.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: The Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Act 2017 and the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 impose fines up to GIP 500,000 for non-registration; a further GIP 10,000 per day for continued non-compliance. Financial Intelligence Unit orders may freeze managed accounts for up to 2 years. Non-regulated payment service provider channels may be terminated, and funds subject to confiscation under the Proceeds of Crime Act if derived from undeclared commercial activity.

Director personal liability: Directors and company managers are criminally liable for AML failures with fines up to GIP 200,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years. Directors who knowingly permit the use of unreported beneficial ownership structures face civil confiscation orders of up to full value of the concealed assets, travel bans, and disqualification. Co-operation with foreign revenue authorities may be compelled, and extradition to the United Kingdom or Cayman Islands is possible for serious economic crime.
Transfer Pricing Violation / Cross-Border Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar transfer pricing rules, the Commissioner may adjust taxable profits to the arm's-length standard, impose a penalty of 50% of the tax adjustment (no tax will be assessed at 0% headline rate, but the adjustment may trigger UK or Gibraltar secondary adjustments), and levy documentation penalties of GIP 10,000 plus an additional GIP 1,000 per month for material non-compliance. Unjustified benefit from intra-group financing may result in disallowance of deduction through the controlled foreign company regime in the parent jurisdiction.

Director personal liability: Where a transfer pricing adjustment arises from a tax avoidance arrangement, directors who signed or approved the intercompany agreements may be assessed personally for the unpaid liabilities of the company if the company is struck off or insolvent. Courts may impose personal civil penalties equal to the profit understatement and issue conduct orders restricting cross-border travel. In cases involving sham service agreements, criminal liability for fraudulent tax evasion by company officers carries imprisonment up to 7 years.
Late Filing / Administrative Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Late filing penalty of GIP 250 escalating to GIP 3,000 plus daily surcharges GIP 30 per day. Striking-off after 3 consecutive default reminders results in the company's assets vesting in the Crown; restoration requires a court order and payment of all default penalties plus restoration fee GIP 500. Payments due but unpaid carry interest at 8% per annum compounded monthly.

Director personal liability: Directors of a struck-off company remain liable for all civil debts and penalties and may be personally pursued in the Gibraltar Supreme Court. The Registrar has power to issue a director disqualification order for up to 5 years, and directors may be barred from reincorporating any entity in Gibraltar or the Cayman Islands. A travel ban may be issued where unpaid penalties exceed GIP 50,000, and continued trading after a strike-off notice is a criminal offence with imprisonment up to 2 years.
