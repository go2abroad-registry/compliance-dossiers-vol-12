Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a Cayman exempted company; the standard authorised capital is USD 50,000 expressed as 50,000 ordinary shares of USD 1.00 each. No escrow account or paid-up deposit is mandated by the Companies Act. The exempted company must pay the annual Government fee and may not carry on domestic trading within the Cayman Islands without a trade licence.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax); official filing portal: Gibraltar Tax Office e-services (https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi); company registry: Gibraltar Companies House (https://www.companieshouse.gi); goods control: HM Customs and Excise Department, Gibraltar.

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company must first register as an overseas company at Gibraltar Companies House under the Companies Act 2014 to obtain a Gibraltar Company Number and Gibraltar Reference Number (GRN). The branch or permanent establishment is then registered with the Income Tax Office, which issues a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number; no VAT ID is obtainable because Gibraltar levies no VAT or GST. Finally, the branch must obtain the statutory customs brokerage licence from HM Customs and Excise and be registered as an Approved Importer/Exporter with a customs reference number, with a local Gibraltar agent appointed for service of process.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone in the Cayman Islands (statutory exemption for a Cayman Exempted Company); for Gibraltar purposes the same entity is a non-resident company whose branch or permanent establishment falls within Entity_Level_Taxation on a Territorial_Scope basis: only Gibraltar-source trading profits of the permanent establishment are charged at the 12.5% corporate rate, while foreign-source income and capital gains remain outside the Gibraltar tax charge.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no US-style check-the-box election mechanism. Relevant elections and classifications: (1) residence election – a foreign-incorporated company is treated as resident only if management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, with residence triggering worldwide-profit taxation; (2) branch accounting elections for permanent establishment attribution under the Income Tax Act; (3) mandatory anti-avoidance characterisation under the Taxation (Hybrid Mismatches) Act 2020 aligned with OECD BEPS Action 2; (4) the former Qualifying Company tax-exemption regime is closed to new applicants following EU state-aid decisions; (5) Approved Importer/Exporter status under the Purchase Tax Act 2006 is a customs authorisation rather than an elective tax status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis threshold stated in Gibraltar pounds (GIP, at parity with GBP): any person or entity importing goods into Gibraltar in the course of business must be an Approved Importer and must account for Purchase Tax on the customs value of the goods. Customs brokerage services have no registration threshold because services are not taxable supplies; if the brokerage also regularly imports or re-exports goods for its own account, Approved Importer/Exporter registration is triggered from the first commercial importation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Purchase Tax; consequently no zero-rating claim mechanism is needed for exported goods. Goods moving through Gibraltar bonded warehouses or under transit procedures have customs duty and Purchase Tax suspended, and the liability is extinguished upon export. Customs brokerage services supplied to non-resident clients or in connection with export movements are outside the Purchase Tax charge and attract a 0% effective rate. Where Purchase Tax has already been paid on goods subsequently exported, the Approved Importer may apply for a rebate under the Purchase Tax Act 2006, supported by export declarations and movement evidence.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration filed via the Gibraltar Trade System (GTS) or customs EDI
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exportation from Gibraltar
  • Commercial invoice and packing list itemising the exported goods
  • Approved Importer/Exporter registration reference covering the transaction
  • Inward remittance certificate or bank receipt evidencing collection of export proceeds

Gibraltar does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction-control, or digital invoice stamping system. Customs and Purchase Tax are cleared through electronic customs declarations filed via the Gibraltar Trade System (GTS) and customs EDI interfaces; Purchase Tax is discharged at the frontier on release of goods into free circulation. Approved importers file periodic Purchase Tax returns with HM Customs. For the brokerage activity, ordinary commercial invoicing with six-year record retention satisfies the mandate; no API clearance, certified invoicing software, or digital stamping is required.

HM Customs may conduct post-clearance audits of export records within the statutory limitation period, normally six years. False or inadequate export substantiation triggers forfeiture of the goods, monetary penalties, criminal liability for customs fraud (fine and/or imprisonment), and retrospective assessment with interest clawing back any Purchase Tax rebate or credit improperly granted. Zero-rating and rebate eligibility for the brokerage can be suspended or revoked on repeated non-compliance, and the customs broker licence itself is at risk of suspension.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full current and capital account convertibility with no exchange control legislation; GIP is freely convertible into GBP and other major currencies without Bank of England approval. A Cayman Exempted Company operating a licensed customs brokerage branch in Gibraltar may remit profits, dividends, and capital proceeds to its Cayman parent without Gibraltar withholding tax, foreign investment screening, or exchange-control permission. The only practical hurdles are (i) Gibraltar corporation tax at the standard rate of 12.5% on profits arising within Gibraltar for customs brokerage activities and (ii) the UK permanent establishment analysis if substantive operational decisions or UK customs filings are managed from the UK mainland, which could expose the non-resident Cayman entity to UK corporation tax under the OECD Model Article 5.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Gibraltar or the United Kingdom; a customs brokerage may retain 100% of foreign currency receipts in designated overseas or local currency accounts and may settle supplier invoices in any fully convertible currency without priority ordering. The only binding requirements are the AML/CFT record-keeping rules under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar) and the Sanctions Act 2019 (Gibraltar), which require originator/beneficiary information on all cross-border transfers and make no distinction between current and capital receipts.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (sovereign sterling monetary authority) and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the integrated licensing, conduct, and AML/CFT supervisor; Gibraltar has no independent central bank and the Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at a statutory 1:1 peg to GBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium feasibility as a secondary or pass-through clearing channel only; not appropriate as the primary treasury bank. UK FCA-authorised EMIs (including Revolut Business and Wise) lost EEA/Gibraltar passporting rights post-Brexit and can only serve the entity from a UK location on a reverse-solicitation basis. Gibraltar-licensed EMIs and DLT payment firms are few in number, require local substance, and generally cannot provide bank guarantees, customs comprehensive guarantees, documentary letters of credit, or ISDA-backed FX margining. An offshore digital EMI without a UK or Gibraltar licence would breach the Financial Services Act 2019 (Gibraltar) for deposit-taking in the course of customs brokerage and would expose the firm to HM Treasury licensing breach risk if any bonded duty float is held outside regulated banking perimeter. Recommended deployment: segregated low-balance disbursement rail for mass small-value duty/tax payments (under GIP 10,000 per transaction) via Automated_Broker_Interface_ACH, with zero role in client money safeguarding or customs bond custody.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial equity or shareholder loan injections must route from the Cayman Exempted Company's Cayman bank to the Gibraltar branch via SWIFT MT103 with the reference line stating 'CAPITAL INJECTION - EQUITY CONTRIBUTION - [CAYMAN ENTITY NAME]' or 'SHAREHOLDER LOAN - [DATED LOAN AGREEMENT]' and transaction purpose code LOAN (non-UK: XLO) or capital code (LED/GIB equivalent). Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

18% Gibraltar corporation tax on territorial profits; Cayman Exempted Entity tax rate 0% in the Cayman Islands. A Cayman Exempted Company that is centrally managed and controlled from Gibraltar is taxed under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 on profits accruing in or derived from Gibraltar.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividend distributions by a Gibraltar-resident company to non-resident shareholders; the Cayman Exempted Company also pays no Cayman withholding tax on distributions, producing zero leakage at the point of dividend payment.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed within nine months of the end of the accounting period (e.g., by 30 September for a 31 December year-end). Interim tax payments are due on 31 January and 30 June; the final balancing payment falls due on the filing deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 — mandatory Local File preparation where the aggregate annual value of cross-border transactions with related parties exceeds GIP 1,000,000; Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds EUR 750,000,000 (BEPS Action 13 threshold).

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance (Requirements and Compliance) Regulations 2019 apply. A Cayman Exempted Company tax resident in Gibraltar carrying on customs brokerage for connected persons falls within the 'distribution and service centre' relevant activity, requiring: (i) core income-generating activities performed in Gibraltar; (ii) adequate physical premises; (iii) full-time employees proportionate to revenue; and (iv) board meetings at appropriate frequency and quorum in Gibraltar. A notification and annual ESR return must be delivered to the GTCA Commissioner. Non-resident companies with a Gibraltar permanent establishment are inside the ESR regime where the permanent establishment constitutes a relevant activity; the Commissioner may treat brokerage services to connected persons as a relevant activity regardless of the PE classification. Penalties for non-compliance reach GIP 100,000 per assessment year, plus director disqualification of up to five years and potential strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 100% of the tax undercharged; tax-geared penalty up to GIP 25,000; criminal prosecution for evading income tax with fines up to GIP 100,000 and imprisonment up to seven years; interest at 7.5% per annum accrues from the due date; publication on the GTCA defaulters list.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or knowingly cause a false return are personally prosecutable; the corporate veil is pierced on proof of sham or impropriety, exposing directors to joint and several liability for unpaid tax, penalties and interest; Gibraltar courts may impose travel bans, asset freezing orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, and custodial sentences of up to seven years (ten years where money laundering is aggregated).
Shell Company / Sham Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Retrospective resident assessment from the start of the accounting period; disallowance of deductions for lack of substance; ESR penalties up to GIP 100,000 per assessment year; CRS and CbCR non-compliant status; strike-off by the Registrar of Companies; denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test.

Director personal liability: Directors of non-compliant companies are personally liable for ESR penalties and may be disqualified for up to five years; travel bans may issue while tax liabilities remain outstanding; deliberate concealment of beneficial ownership triggers money-laundering prosecution, civil restitution orders, and confiscation of personal estates.
