Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Companies, Companies House Gibraltar, HM Government of Gibraltar - statutory corporate registry and online e-Registry filing window; GFSC-licensed corporate services providers act as sponsored filers and registered-office agents.

Governing statute

Gibraltar Companies Act 2014 (as amended) - statutory formation of a private company limited by shares, covering memorandum and articles of association, registered office, share capital, directors, and company secretary provisions; Gibraltar Data Protection Act 2019 (GDPR-aligned registration for personal wealth data processing); Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended) - non-resident company status ensuring foreign-source revenue is outside the charge to Gibraltar tax; applicable Companies (Fees) Regulations. The vehicle is structured as a Cayman Exempted Company equivalent using Gibraltar exempted-company features: non-resident management, confidentiality, and zero Gibraltar tax on foreign income.

Formation / constitutional filings

Memorandum of Association and Articles of Association lodged with the Application for Registration (Companies House Form CO-01, filed electronically through the Gibraltarian e-Registry), followed by issuance of the sealed Certificate of Incorporation under the Companies Act 2014.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar — HM Government of Gibraltar Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), portal https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation; Cayman Islands — Department for International Tax Cooperation (DITC) and Registrar of Companies, portal https://www.ditc.ky; United Kingdom — HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC), portal https://www.gov.uk/hmrc.

E-government filing portal

Cayman Exempted Company: (1) incorporate with the Cayman Registrar of Companies and obtain the company registration number; (2) file the annual Economic Substance Notification/Return with DITC if in scope; (3) apply to DITC for a Tax Exemption Certificate under the Tax Exemption Law (2021 Revision), which grants exemption from all Cayman direct taxes for 20 years, renewable to 50 years; no Cayman TIN is issued for direct-tax purposes (the registration number is used for CRS/FATCA self-certification). Because the fintech application activity may be carried on from Gibraltar, the company must also register with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office via the foreign-company registration procedure and obtain a Gibraltar TIN/reference number; Gibraltar tax returns are required if the company becomes Gibraltar tax resident or holds a Gibraltar permanent establishment. For UK VAT on B2C digital supplies consumed in the UK, the non-established company must register with HMRC using Form VAT1A; there is no turnover threshold for non-established suppliers, and the effective date of registration is the date of the first UK supply.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone. The Cayman Exempted Company is exempt from all Cayman direct taxation (income, capital gains, estate, gift) under a valid Tax Exemption Certificate; Cayman imposes no corporate income tax, no withholding tax, and no VAT/GST. The Tax_Exempt_Zone status is preserved by not acquiring Gibraltar tax residence or a Gibraltar permanent establishment beyond the notified substance activity; if such nexus arises, Gibraltar corporation tax applies at marginal rates up to a 12.5% headline ceiling. For UK VAT, the entity is treated as a non-established taxable person outside UK corporation tax unless a UK permanent establishment exists.

Available election forms

Available: (i) Cayman — statutory election to obtain a Tax Exemption Certificate and to file the Economic Substance Notification/Return in the relevant form; (ii) Gibraltar — no 'check-the-box' mechanism; statutory elections include group-loss surrender between Gibraltar-resident group companies, capital-allowance pooling elections, and election for the remittance basis (companies cannot elect this); hybrid mismatch rules under Gibraltar's OECD BEPS Action 2 legislation may recharacterise dual-deduction or deductible-payment arrangements; (iii) for the US tax layer only, the Cayman company is an eligible entity that may file US Form 8832 to elect classification as a disregarded entity, partnership, or corporation and must file Form 5471 where any US person is an officer, director, or shareholder under the IRS filing categories.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar: No VAT/GST (Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area); indirect taxation is limited to customs/excise duties on imported goods (hydrocarbons, tobacco, alcohol) and stamp duty on certain instruments/property. Cayman Islands: No VAT/GST; only import duty on goods. United Kingdom: UK VAT (VATA 1994) at 20% standard rate applies solely to digital services consumed in the UK.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: no VAT/GST registration threshold (no regime). Cayman: no VAT/GST registration threshold (no regime). United Kingdom: £85,000 annual taxable turnover threshold for UK-established businesses, but the threshold does not apply to non-established suppliers; UK VAT registration is mandatory from the first UK B2C supply of digital services. For EU B2C digital supplies after Brexit, the non-EU-established supplier registers under the EU non-Union VAT OSS (no threshold) in one chosen member state.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Gibraltar or Cayman are outside any domestic VAT/GST charge because neither territory levies VAT/GST; no zero-rating claim is required for exports from the entity's home jurisdictions. For UK VAT, digital services supplied to recipients outside the UK are 'outside the scope' under the place-of-supply rules (VATA 1994, Sch 4A); the supplier charges no UK output VAT and may recover related input VAT subject to partial-exemption rules. To substantiate non-UK supply, at least two consistent, non-contradictory evidence items are required (billing country, bank country, SIM card country, IP address, permanent home address). For EU B2C consumers the supply becomes subject to EU VAT in the member state of consumption (non-Union OSS); for EU B2B, reverse charge applies where the customer's valid EU VAT number is held.

  • Customs Export Declaration (UK CDS/SAD export entry or Gibraltarian export declaration for physical goods)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or other transport evidence proving exportation from the territory
  • Commercial invoice and, where applicable, Inward Remittance Certificate or bank FX credit confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • For digital services: geolocation/IP evidence, cloud analytics records, customer contracts, payment gateway settlement statements, and OSS records

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous API transaction clearance exists in Gibraltar, Cayman, or the UK. The UK operates Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT: VAT-registered businesses must keep digital records and submit VAT returns through MTD-compatible software via HMRC's Application Programming Interface. Electronic invoicing is not mandatory in the UK, Gibraltar, or Cayman; B2B supplies require a valid UK VAT invoice, while simplified e-receipts are acceptable for B2C digital supplies. Gibraltar's eTax portal handles corporate income-tax filings without per-invoice clearance.

UK VAT: HMRC may reassess under-declared VAT and claw back zero-rating or input VAT relief; assessment time limits are 2 years (ordinary), 4 years (careless), and 20 years (deliberate evasion). Penalties include error-in-return penalties up to 30% of the potential lost revenue, default surcharge and interest, and civil evasion penalties up to 100% of VAT, with criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion (VATA 1994, ss. 60-72; FA 2007, Sch 24). False export substantiation triggers repayment of all recovered input VAT and prospective denial of zero-rating until satisfactory evidence is produced. Cayman: false declarations in the Tax Exemption application may void the exemption certificate and reinstate tax liability. Gibraltar: incorrect returns are subject to penalties under the Income Tax Act (up to 100% of tax avoided plus daily default penalties) and breaches of the Economic Substance Act attract civil penalties and potential striking-off.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP and GBP are fully convertible for both current and capital account purposes. Gibraltar maintains a free-capital regime with no exchange control legislation, no central bank approval requirement for foreign direct investment, no limits on dividend distribution, royalty remittance or loan interest payments to non-residents, and no surrender, repatriation-timeline or conversion-ratio obligations on export proceeds. The only practical hurdles to profit remittance are standard AML/KYC documentary requirements, FATCA/CRS reporting of the Cayman Exempted Company as an Active Non-Financial Entity, and sanctions screening of the ultimate beneficial owners. Offshore market liquidity is limited: the GIP is not traded in global FX markets, so treasury operations should maintain multi-currency accounts in GBP, EUR and USD with full cross-currency convertibility executed by the Gibraltar banking counterparty at negotiated interbank margins.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None apply. Gibraltar imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio; software licence and SaaS export receipts (ISIC 6201 / NAICS 511210) may be retained in any currency, in any jurisdiction, indefinitely. Banking counterparties nonetheless apply their own AML monitoring: inbound receipts above EUR 10,000 require supporting invoices and executed subscription agreements to evidence the underlying economic activity and to distinguish export proceeds from capital contributions, and the treasury surveillance system must differentiate corporate wire movements from merchant settlements originating from Stripe and Apple IAP.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of England (Monetary Policy Committee, London) is the ultimate monetary authority for the Gibraltar pound (GIP), which circulates at a fixed 1:1 parity with the pound sterling under the Gibraltar Currency Note Act 1934; there is no independent central bank in Gibraltar. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the integrated prudential, conduct and AML/CFT regulator of banks, e-money institutions and payment service providers. HM Treasury (United Kingdom) retains sanctions, anti-money laundering and financial restrictions authority that extends to Gibraltar as a British Overseas Territory, supplemented by the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the Sanctions Act 2019.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-to-High for UK-licensed EMIs (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Currenxie, Airwallex) when the Cayman Exempted Company can evidence an operational nexus in Gibraltar; Low for offshore or EEA EMIs that programmatically reject Cayman tax-exempt incorporations as a restricted jurisdiction category. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File where related-party transactions exceed GIP £1,000,000 or net financing costs exceed £500,000 in the chargeable period; Master File required when group consolidated revenue exceeds GIP £100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Company and Transparent) Regulations 2019 apply to every Gibraltar company, including Cayman Exempted Companies registered or managed from Gibraltar. The relevant activity 'headquarter, financing, distribution and services' includes financial portfolio-tracking software. Core income-generating activities must be performed by staff physically in Gibraltar; just-in-time outsourcing does not satisfy the test. Annual economic substance filings are due six months after year-end.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Banking Representations

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 100% of the tax unpaid, strict daily surcharges, criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act 2010 with imprisonment not exceeding two years, and a financial fine at the discretion of the court.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly procure false returns are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution; courts may issue travel bans, worldwide asset freezing orders, and custodial sentences.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Failures under the Economic Substance Regulations trigger a financial penalty between £5,000 and £50,000, possible striking-off, and exchange of information to tax authorities in the UK and Cayman.

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified for up to 15 years; personal civil liability for fines if they knowingly caused the company to make a false substance declaration.
AML / Concealment of Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Offense under the Criminal Finances Act 2017 (Gibraltar): up to 14 years' imprisonment, unlimited fines, confiscation of the portfolio assets, and revocation of financial intermediary authorization.

Director personal liability: Individual directors face personal criminal conviction, disqualification, and forfeiture of personal assets to the extent of unjust benefit obtained through the scheme.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Commissioner may assess adjustments, apply interest at reference rate plus 3%, and impose a transfer-pricing penalty of 100% of the under-declared tax if no contemporaneous Local File is maintained.

Director personal liability: A director who approves non-arm's-length invoices with knowledge of the tax benefit is personally liable for restitution and may be deemed personally liable for the adjusted tax in case of insufficiency of corporate assets.
