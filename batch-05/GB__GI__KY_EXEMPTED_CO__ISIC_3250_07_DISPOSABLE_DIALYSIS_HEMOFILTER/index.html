Virtual office is generally permissible for a Gibraltar incorporated company as long as the company maintains a registered office at a physical address in Gibraltar where service of process can be effected, and where statutory records and registers (including UBO register) are physically accessible. However, for an exempt company applying for non-resident tax status and for a manufacturing entity eventually carrying on commercial activity, substance requirements apply under the Income Tax (Substance Requirements) Regulations 2019 – the company must demonstrate economic activity, core income-generating activities conducted in Gibraltar, and adequate physical presence in terms of office, staff, or equipment. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone (Cayman Exempted Company is statutorily exempt from Cayman Islands income tax under the Cayman Companies Act (as revised) and enjoys a tax-exempt status with no direct tax until at least 2045. For Gibraltar purposes, the entity is nonetheless within the territorial classification: Gibraltar charges corporation tax only on income accruing in or derived from Gibraltar; foreign-source income is outside the charge unless routed through a Gibraltar permanent establishment, and full Gibraltar tax residence arises only if central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar).

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US check-the-box election. Available statutory mechanisms: (a) residence election — a company incorporated abroad may elect to be assessed on a territorial/source-only basis, while a company with central management and control in Gibraltar is automatically Gibraltar-resident; (b) transitional Exempt Company status — closed to new applicants since 2011 and therefore unavailable to this newly formed or newly registered Cayman entity; (c) hybrid mismatch screening — the Cayman Exempted Company's tax-transparent or opaque treatment, combined with Gibraltar's territorial charge, must be tested under the Gibraltar hybrid mismatch regulations (OECD BEPS Action 2, implemented via the Income Tax (Amendment) Regulations); no elective safe harbour exists, so payments between the Cayman entity and UK/EU group companies must be reviewed for deduction/non-inclusion mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar does not operate VAT or GST. The applicable indirect tax system comprises: Customs Import Duties under the Customs Tariff Ordinance (levied on goods entering Gibraltar), Excise Duties on alcohol, tobacco and hydrocarbon oils, and a 5% Hotel Accommodation Tax on tourist accommodation. The export of disposable dialysis hemofilters (ISIC 3250 / NAICS 339112) from Gibraltar is outside the scope of all Gibraltar indirect taxes.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Nil — Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST statute and therefore no turnover-based registration threshold. Import duty is exigible per customs importation with no economic-turnover test; administrative de minimis relief applies only to consignments of negligible value. The operative threshold analysis is in the destination jurisdiction (e.g., UK VAT registration threshold or EU member state distance-sales thresholds) when the goods are imported into the UK or EU.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT, so no domestic zero-rating claim is available or required. For cross-border supplies: (i) removal of goods from Gibraltar is an export for Gibraltar customs purposes and is outside the scope of Gibraltar import duty/excise upon departure; (ii) where the Cayman Exempted Company acts as principal and sells into the UK or EU, export zero-rating or a similar derogation is claimed in the destination jurisdiction (e.g., UK VAT Act 1994 s.30 or EU VAT Directive Art. 146), requiring the normal proof of removal — customs export declaration, transport contract and proof of departure, generally within three months of supply; (iii) for medical devices such as disposable dialysis hemofilters, destination exemptions may be narrower than general export zero-rating (UK VATA Sch. 8 Gp. 12 or EU Art. 98 reduced-rate concessions), so the product's qualifying status must be verified against the import-country's medical-device tariff classification before claiming any preferential rate.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (SAD / EDIFACT XML export entry on departure from Gibraltar)
  • Commercial Invoice in English, stating exporter, consignee, Incoterms and the HS 9018 (medical/surgical instrument) description of the disposable dialysis hemofilter
  • Packing List with lot numbers and sterilisation certificate (ISO 11135 / EN 556)
  • Original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the export transport contract

Gibraltar imposes no domestic real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing clearance, or digital stamping for invoices. Customs export declarations must, however, be filed electronically through the Gibraltar Customs XML/EDIFACT gateway using the Single Administrative Document (SAD) dataset. Once the goods enter the UK or EU customs territory, destination-country e-invoicing mandates become binding (e.g., Italy's Sistema di Interscambio (SDI), Poland's KSeF, France's Factur-X, Spain's Verifactu); the exporter should therefore issue structured e-invoices (PEPPOL BIS 3.0 / UBL 2.1) to all EU/UK buyer counterparts to avoid clearance rejection.

Gibraltar applies strict anti-fraud scrutiny to customs and export claims. False export declarations, forged bills of lading, or fabricated inward remittance evidence attract: (a) forfeiture of the goods and summons of their value under the Customs Ordinance (Gibraltar); (b) a civil penalty of up to the greater of GBP 5,000 or three times the value of the goods for untrue declarations; (c) criminal prosecution with fines up to GBP 50,000 and/or imprisonment up to 7 years for fraudulent export claims; (d) retrospective clawback of any duty relief, drawback, or zero-rating previously granted in the destination jurisdiction, with interest from the original claim date; (e) in UK/EU destination states, joint-and-several 'missing trader' assessments and penalties of up to 100% of the tax lost for deliberate inaccuracy; and (f) automated review of the SWIFT MT103 remittance trail by the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit and Gibraltar Customs where export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar has no exchange-control legislation and no separate monetary policy. The Gibraltar pound is pegged at one-to-one parity to sterling and is fully backed by Bank of England notes. Capital and current account convertibility are unrestricted for non-resident Cayman exempted companies, provided the underlying flows are supported by audited financial statements, board resolutions, and beneficial ownership disclosures. Profit remittances and dividend distributions are not subject to Gibraltar withholding taxes; however, banks will apply enhanced due diligence where funds flow to tax-exempt offshore holding structures. UK financial sanctions and counter-terrorist financing screening apply to all sterling clearing and are incorporated by reference into Gibraltar practice.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement exists in Gibraltar or the United Kingdom. Export receipts from the sale of disposable dialysis hemofilters may be retained in GBP, USD, EUR, or any freely convertible currency. Settlement proceeds may be used directly for supplier payments, treasury hedging, or onward remittance to the Cayman parent without any forced conversion into Gibraltar pounds.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary anchor for the Gibraltar pound) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC, as the integrated financial supervisor and AML/CFT authority for banking, e-money, and investment firms)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary operational banking vehicle. EEA- or UK-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) have lost automatic EU/EEA passporting rights into Gibraltar following the post-Brexit arrangement, and any EMI wishing to serve a Gibraltar-connected corporate must obtain a separate GFSC authorisation under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Regulations. Cayman- or BVI-incorporated digital EMIs are not recognised as credit institutions and cannot lawfully accept deposits from this entity in Gibraltar. A digital EMI may be used only as a secondary payment-orchestration wrapper, with the Gibraltar-licensed commercial bank remaining the authorised execution venue for all material flows.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are mandatory where the Gibraltar entity is part of a multinational group with consolidated turnover above GIP 750,000,000. A simplified transfer pricing documentation package is required for any cross-border related-party transaction exceeding GIP 1,000,000 in a reporting period. For intercompany sales of finished dialyzers, the taxpayer must maintain a benchmarking analysis showing gross margins within the 12%–18% range.

Economic substance scope: The Income Tax (Economic Substance) Regulations 2021 apply to resident and non-resident exempted companies conducting relevant activities. Manufacturing of disposable dialysis hemofilters is a relevant activity where production occurs in Gibraltar. The company must demonstrate that core income generating activities, including quality control, supply chain management, and management of manufacturing risks, are conducted in Gibraltar through locally-based qualified employees, a permanent local physical office, and adequate IT systems. An annual Economic Substance Return must be filed within 12 months of the accounting period end. Failure to satisfy the economic substance test permits the Commissioner to exchange information with the Cayman Islands tax authority and to treat the company as tax-resident in Gibraltar.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction with a fine of up to GIP 1,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years; civil penalty of 100% of the understated tax plus default interest at 9% per annum.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or authorize a false return are jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency; they may be subjected to travel bans prohibiting departure from Gibraltar, seizure of personal assets, and disqualification from board service for 12 years.
Abusive Shell Company / ESR Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Revocation of the exempted company certificate, retroactive re-qualification as a resident company, immediate taxation on worldwide income at 10%, back-assessment for six years, plus a penalty of GIP 10,000 per relevant activity and GIP 5,000 per day for failure to file the ESR return.

Director personal liability: Directors who make a false annual substance certification face civil restitution of all back taxes, a Gibraltar-wide travel ban, and imprisonment for up to 4 years under the Income Tax (Fraud Penalties) Rules.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's-length result, penalty of 5% of the total value of the related-party transactions (minimum GIP 25,000), and interest on underpaid tax at 10% per annum for the period of underpayment.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors are subject to personal penalties of GIP 20,000 where the failure is attributable to their gross negligence; they may also be disqualified from holding office in any Gibraltar-registered company for five years.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Disclosure Failure

Statutory sanction: Corporate financial penalties up to GIP 150,000 and an additional GIP 20,000 for each month the beneficial ownership register remains incomplete; suspension of voting rights; freezing of corporate bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for unpaid penalties, may be imprisoned for up to 2 years for knowingly permitting non-disclosure, and habitual offenders face a lifelong travel ban from Gibraltar.
