Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax) and HM Customs of Gibraltar (Gibraltar Customs & Border Agency). Portals: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation and https://www.customs.gov.gi.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity, including a Cayman Islands Exempted Company, is not automatically registered for Gibraltar tax. It must first assess nexus: if it has no permanent establishment, no office or business presence in Gibraltar, and no central management and control exercised there, it has no statutory obligation to register. Where registration becomes necessary (e.g., opening a bank account through a Gibraltar branch, employing local staff, owning Gibraltar taxable property, or receiving Gibraltar-source income), the entity files Form IO-100 with the Commissioner of Income Tax, attaching notarised incorporation documents, certificate of registered office, register of directors and members, a board resolution appointing a Gibraltar tax representative, and a non-residence declaration. The Commissioner issues a 10-digit Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) under the Income Tax Act 2010. For customs operations, an importer/exporter must apply to HM Customs of Gibraltar for a Gibraltar Customs Registration Reference; no VAT registration number can be issued because Gibraltar does not impose VAT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar law does not provide a check-the-box election; corporate residence is determined solely by central management and control under the Income Tax Act 2010. A non-resident Cayman exempted company may file a nil annual return (Form GI-70) to evidence non-residence and protect its Tax_Exempt_Zone status. A board resolution may elect to become Gibraltar tax resident for treaty access if substance is created, but for a Cayman exempted company this is generally adverse. Under Cayman Islands law, the Exempted Company elects exempted status under Section 6 of the Companies Act (Cayman); it may also elect to be treated as tax-transparent at the entity level if the ultimate foreign parent's regime requires it. Gibraltar and the UK apply ATAD-aligned hybrid mismatch rules, which override elective transparency and deny deductions for payments that a hybrid entity's counterparty does not or cannot include in taxable income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/sales tax in Gibraltar; indirect taxation is limited to customs import duty, excise duties on alcohol, tobacco and hydrocarbons, environmental taxes, and stamp duty.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no general indirect tax registration threshold. A business moving goods through the Gibraltar customs frontier must possess a Gibraltar Customs Registration Reference and, for excise goods, an excise trader licence; no de minimis customs threshold applies to controlled goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT, so no zero-rate exists to claim. Exports of goods from Gibraltar are outside the charge to any Gibraltar indirect tax; the supplier issues a non-VAT invoice. If the goods are subsequently exported from the UK or EU, the UK/EU supplier may apply a 0% export VAT rate only with conclusive proof of removal from the UK/EU, including Gibraltar customs exit data and transport documents. No VAT refund mechanism applies in Gibraltar for a Cayman exempted company, and attempting to reclaim non-existent VAT is a legal nullity.

None. Gibraltar does not mandate e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping. Import and export entries are submitted through HM Customs of Gibraltar's electronic clearance system (AISXML / Single Administrative Document); electronic submission is encouraged but is not a statutory real-time fiscal pre-clearance regime.

Because no VAT/GST system exists, the enforcement focus is customs misdeclaration and fraudulent export-relief claims. Under the Customs and Excise Duties Act 2010 and Tax Administration Act 2011, submitting false documents or a false goods movement declaration can lead to seizure and forfeiture, a civil penalty up to 175% of the evaded duty, retrospective assessment for up to four years, and criminal penalties of up to GBP 5,000 plus three times value of the duty evaded and/or imprisonment up to seven years. If an exporter fraudulently obtains customs clearance references, HM Customs of Gibraltar may claw back all duty relief or suspension, cancel the trader's customs authorisation, and refer directors for aggravated penalties under the UK-Gibraltar administrative assistance framework.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP is pegged at 1:1 parity with GBP and is fully convertible. Gibraltar maintains no exchange-control legislation; current-account operations (trade settlements, service fees, royalties) and capital-account operations (equity injections, inter-company loans, branch profit repatriation) are equally unrestricted. A Cayman Exempted Company operating through a Gibraltar branch may transfer, retain, and repatriate profits in any freely convertible currency without obtaining exchange-control permissions; no Gibraltar withholding tax applies to dividends, interest, or branch remittances to the Cayman parent, and Cayman law imposes no tax or exchange controls, making profit remittance fully hurdle-free.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Gibraltar abolished exchange controls in 1979 and mandates no surrender, conversion, or repatriation deadline for export proceeds. Receipts from the manufacture and canning of retort river snail noodles may be held indefinitely in GBP, USD, EUR, CNY, or other convertible currencies without central-bank consent; only standard FATF record-keeping and suspicious-transaction reporting thresholds apply.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the statutory supervisor of banks, EMIs and foreign-exchange dealing; Government of Gibraltar (Currency Notes Issuing Authority) as issuer of the Gibraltar pound (GIP); Bank of England holds no direct regulatory authority over Gibraltar but the GIP circulation is 100% sterling-backed and UK monetary conditions anchor the fixed parity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium/Low. Gibraltar's Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2019 permits GFSC-licensed EMIs to onboard non-resident Cayman exempted companies after enhanced due diligence. However, UK and EEA EMIs (e.g., Revolut, Wise, Ebury) lost passporting rights into Gibraltar post-Brexit, and most online-only platforms decline Cayman tax-exempt vehicles and flag China-linked food-supply corridors as high-risk. A licensed Gibraltar EMI is defensible solely as a supplementary multi-currency collections rail (EUR/GBP/USD) for e-commerce receipts; core treasury liquidity, trade finance, and hedging must remain with a Tier-1 commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File is mandatory where annual Gibraltar-source gross turnover exceeds GIP 10,000,000; a full Master File is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue above GIP 750,000,000. Below these thresholds, a contingency transfer pricing file must be maintained wherever related-party cross-border flows exceed GIP 1,000,000 annually.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies OECD/EU-aligned economic substance and commercial-presence rules under the Income Tax Act. A non-resident Cayman Exempted Company operating a PE must be managed and directed from Gibraltar, must maintain local premises, full-time staff, and strategic decision-taking functions proportionate to the retort noodle canning activity, and may not book Gibraltar manufacturing profits into the Cayman head office without operational substance supporting the transfer.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax undercharged; on conviction, a fine of up to GBP 5,000 for summary offences; interest at 8% per annum on all unpaid amounts; asset restraint and confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly procure evasion are jointly and severally liable for the uncollected corporation tax; corporate veil may be pierced to reach personal assets; travel bans and imprisonment for up to 7 years on conviction on indictment.
Shell Company and Artificial Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the Cayman entity as carrying on a permanent establishment in Gibraltar, retrospective reassessment for up to 6 years, targeted anti-avoidance unwinding of the artificial arrangements, and a penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors face disqualification orders under the Gibraltar Companies Act, personal civil liability for creditor losses, and disclosure to UK HMRC under the exchange-of-information protocol, which may produce joint assessments and travel restriction notices.
Money Laundering and AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Confiscation order for the full value of tainted funds; court-discretionary fine; suspension or revocation of the Gibraltar food production and commercial trading licence; freezing of company bank accounts by the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers may be personally prosecuted for money laundering with a maximum imprisonment term of 14 years; travel bans and asset freezing orders are issued during investigation; directors are barred from holding office for up to 15 years.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment by the Gibraltar Commissioner, additional tax at 10%, interest at 8% per annum, and a penalty of up to 100% of the price-adjustment tax differential where manipulation is deliberate.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign off false transfer pricing documentation are treated as responsible officers; personal fines, disqualification, and extradition exposure arise where cross-border tax loss is reported to UK HMRC under the treaty exchange article.
Unlicensed Manufacturing and Trading

Statutory sanction: Cease-and-desist order shutting the production line; daily fine of up to GBP 10,000 per day of continuing unlicensed operation; customs seizure of non-compliant product; deregistration of the company's Gibraltar branch.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines of up to GBP 10,000 and imprisonment not exceeding 6 months for continued production after notice; civil restitution of commercial losses is enforceable against personal assets where corporate assets are stripped.
