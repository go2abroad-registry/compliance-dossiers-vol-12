Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone: A Cayman Exempted Company is not subject to Cayman income tax and, for Gibraltar purposes, is normally outside the Gibraltar charge to corporation tax unless managed and controlled in Gibraltar. If central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, the company becomes Gibraltar tax resident and is subject to entity-level corporation tax at 12.5% on worldwide income. As a non-resident, only Gibraltar-source income is chargeable; a pure offshore e-commerce exporter with sales to non-Gibraltar customers and no physical presence or employees in Gibraltar will typically have no Gibraltar-source taxable income.

Available election forms

Gibraltar law does not provide U.S.-style check-the-box elections, partnership-style transparency elections, or hybrid-mismatch elective classifications available to a Cayman Exempted Company. The relevant statutory mechanisms are: (a) an election under the Income Tax Act to be treated as non-resident where central management and control is exercised outside Gibraltar; (b) an application for an advance tax ruling to obtain certainty on residence and permanent establishment; and (c) elections under double-taxation agreements to determine treaty residency and attributable profits.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar General Consumption Tax (GCT) and Import Duty System (outside the UK VAT regime; no conventional VAT/GST applies in Gibraltar)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP nil—no turnover threshold applies for GCT registration; a supplier making shortlisted taxable supplies in Gibraltar must register regardless of turnover, and any importer of physical outdoor equipment must register for customs duty and import declaration. Exports outside Gibraltar are not chargeable GCT supplies and do not trigger registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Although Gibraltar does not operate a standard VAT regime, exported goods—being physically removed from Gibraltar and sold to a non-Gibraltar customer—are outside the scope of GCT and the supplier incurs no output GCT. Zero-rating eligibility requires that title passes outside Gibraltar, that goods leave Gibraltar within three months of supply, and that the exporter retains delivery and payment evidence. Services directly ancillary to exported goods may be treated as outside the scope of GCT where provided to a non-resident recipient and performed wholly in connection with the export.

Gibraltar does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping mandate. Commercial electronic invoices are accepted. Import and export clearances must be filed electronically through Gibraltar Customs electronic entry processing, and where movement occurs through the EU/UK customs procedures, a valid UK or Gibraltar EORI-linked reference may be required.

HM Customs and the Income Tax Office may reopen export claims within a six-year audit window. If supporting documentation is incomplete or misleading, the zero-rated treatment is clawed back and the tax element is reassessed at the applicable local GCT or import duty rate, with late payment interest. Civil penalties of up to 100% of the tax due can be imposed. Criminal penalties include fines of up to GIP 10,000 per invoice and imprisonment for officers where fraudulent export claims are established. The exporter must retain customs export declarations, bills of lading, freight contracts, payment receipts, and evidence of foreign-currency repatriation within 180 days; failure to repatriate export proceeds in time can trigger an economic substance inquiry and loss of the beneficial tax-exempt status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The KYD is fully convertible at the statutory fixed rate of KYD 0.80 = USD 1.00, a peg maintained since 2 April 1972. The Cayman Islands imposes no exchange-control restrictions on current or capital account transactions; for a non-resident-owned exempted company, inward capital contributions, outward trade disbursements, dividend distributions and repatriation of liquidation proceeds may proceed freely without CIMA authorisation, and no Cayman withholding tax applies to outbound dividends or interest. The capital account is therefore as freely convertible as the current account. The only material convertibility hurdle is bank-level: USD flows must clear through the licensed local bank's US correspondent account (e.g., Citibank N.A., New York, or Comerica Bank), and the receiving bank will enforce its own source-of-funds and beneficial-ownership controls before allowing onward remittances. For the target e-commerce model, Stripe and PayPal settlement proceeds received in USD, GBP and EUR may be retained indefinitely in matching-currency operational accounts without any obligation to convert into KYD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, repatriation timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists for export or retail-sale proceeds in the Cayman Islands. There is no requirement that hard-currency receipts be converted into KYD within a prescribed period, nor any rule requiring export proceeds to be deposited with a designated domestic bank; the company may hold USD, GBP and EUR proceeds in foreign-currency accounts indefinitely. For the specific internet-retailing scope (ISIC 4791), card-acquirer settlements from Stripe and PayPal may remain in USD until a trade payment to a supplier is due, and FX conversion may be deferred or executed only as operationally required, at the prevailing commercial rate quoted by the licensed bank, with no exchange-control certificate required either for import of capital or for export of trade receipts.

Central bank supervision: Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) - the statutory monetary, financial-services and currency regulatory authority of the Cayman Islands, operating the Cayman Islands Dollar (KYD) at a fixed USD peg; Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the secondary supervisory authority for the Gibraltar settlement layer, with the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) issued at 1:1 parity with GBP and cleared under the framework of the Bank of England and UK-Gibraltar treaty arrangements.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder capital is contributed in USD via SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's home bank (UK high-street bank or regulated neobank) to the Cayman licensed bank's US-dollar correspondent account, typically Citibank N.A., New York for Butterfield Bank (Cayman) Limited, with field 50K naming the contributing shareholder and field 70 narrative: 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - [EXEMPTED COMPANY NAME] - NON-RETURNABLE'. No purpose code is imposed by CIMA because the Cayman Islands operate a general-permission regime, but the receiving bank will request a board resolution and a source-of-funds certificate for contributions above USD 100,000. For GBP-denominated capital, use CHAPS from a UK bank or a SWIFT MT103 routed through a UK clearer into the company's GIP/GBP account at a Gibraltar-licensed bank, given the fixed 1:1 GIP/GBP parity. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Direct onboarding of a Cayman-exempted company at mainstream UK FCA-authorised EMIs (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Payoneer) is frequently declined because offshore-corporate profiles trigger restrictive underwriting policies; where accepted, account limits and geopolitical routing restrictions materially constrain treasury flexibility. The highest-probability fintech route is a Gibraltar-licensed e-money or payment institution supervised by the GFSC under the Financial Services (E-Money) Act 2019, which recognises the Cayman exempted-company form and can issue GBP, EUR and USD IBANs under the UK-Gibraltar passporting and treaty framework. A GFSC-licensed EMI may be used for secondary settlement pooling, client refunds and FX conversion while the primary operating account remains at a Tier-1 Cayman bank. Stripe and PayPal are the target acquiring rails and will settle only to a verified bank account or licensed EMI account held in the company's name; Stripe supports Cayman-incorporated payees with USD settlement, while PayPal requires a KYD or USD-denominated bank account and imposes periodic withdrawal verification. The entity should maintain at least one Cayman bank account as the anchor and use the Gibraltar EMI only as a supplementary liquidity node.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where the aggregate value of controlled transactions with related parties exceeds GIP 1,000,000 in a fiscal year. Master File is required for members of multinational groups with consolidated group revenue above GIP 70,000,000. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) applies at the GIP 750,000,000 consolidated group threshold. Companies below these thresholds must nevertheless maintain reasonable contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for inspection by the Commissioner.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies a management and control economic substance test under the Income Tax Act 2010. A Cayman Exempted Company that is centrally managed and controlled from Gibraltar is taxable as a Gibraltar resident. If the entity is a pure Cayman exempt company, it must independently satisfy the Cayman Islands Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for any applicable relevant activity; internet retailing is not automatically a relevant activity, but the company must demonstrate real presence, core income-generating activities, and adequate physical resources where a relevant activity is carried on. A box structure without genuine decision-making and operations in either Gibraltar or Cayman will be treated as a sham and exposed to re-characterisation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax understated; criminal prosecution may result in a fine of up to GIP 250,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise or collude in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, face travel restrictions under Gibraltar tax collection rules, and may be subject to personal bankruptcy proceedings and extradition where criminal fines remain unpaid.
Shell Company / Artificial Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation of transactions, denial of capital allowances and loss relief, deemed assessment of 10% of gross revenues where actual profits cannot be established, and penalties up to GIP 10,000 for failure to maintain local accounting records.

Director personal liability: Under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014, a director who signs an incorrect solvency or compliance statement can be fined GIP 50,000 and disqualified. A court may pierce the corporate veil and order restitution from the director's personal assets where profits were extracted as loans or dividends without economic substance.
Anti-Money Laundering / AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fines up to GIP 500,000 or 10% of annual turnover under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; freezing of merchant accounts; money laundering conviction with imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Senior officers are personally exposed to criminal liability under the Proceeds of Crime Act; directors may be prohibited from acting as directors for up to 15 years and any unauthorised funds transfers are subject to civil recovery and global asset confiscation orders.
