E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Incorporate or register the Cayman Exempted Company as an overseas company under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014: obtain a legalized certificate of incorporation from the Cayman Islands General Registry, appoint a Gibraltar-resident service provider and company secretary, file Form F5 (Registration of Foreign Company) with the Registrar of Companies, and maintain a registered office in Gibraltar. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone — The Cayman Exempted Company carries the legal character of a tax-exempted vehicle under the Cayman Islands Exempted Companies Law (s.4(3)(d)) and is exempt from all Cayman direct taxation on income or capital gains; Gibraltar does not tax worldwide income, so the vehicle faces no Gibraltar corporation tax except on income accrued in or derived from Gibraltar (territorial scope). Where the company has neither management-and-control in Gibraltar nor a Gibraltar permanent establishment, its trading profit from cross-border lithium carbonate arbitrage remains completely outside the Gibraltar charge. The entity is therefore in an effective Tax_Exempt_Zone classification, with entity-level taxation arising only in a narrow territorial scenario and pass-through eligibility playing no role in Gibraltar or UK common-law corporate analysis.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or full attribution election exists in Gibraltar or Cayman. Available statutory instruments include: (1) an irrevocable election under the Cayman Exempted Companies Law to continue as a non-exempted ordinary resident company; (2) an election under Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 s.22A to adopt the preceding-year basis of assessment for a trade; (3) the qualifying company election (category 2) historically available under the Income Tax (Qualifying Company) Regulations — closed to new applicants but grandfathering rules may still apply; (4) an economic substance election under the Gibraltar Economic Substance Act 2019 to classify the entity as a 'pure equity holding entity' where it only holds equity participations and earns passive dividends/capital gains; and (5) hybrid mismatch arrangements with UK investors can be neutralized under UK CTA 2010 Pt 6A (s.259BR election for PE-provisional counteraction or investor-level transparency adjustments) if the Cayman/Gibraltar vehicle is disregarded for UK tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No_General_Indirect_Tax — Gibraltar does not impose VAT, GST, or general sales/use tax. The only indirect taxes are customs and excise duties levied at importation into Gibraltar under the Gibraltar Customs & Excise Ordinance and the Integrated Tariff alignment applied jointly with the UK, plus selected stamp duties and a hotel accommodation tax. The lithium carbonate trading activity, if conducted as offshore procurement with goods moving directly from third-country producers (e.g., Chile, Argentina, Australia, China) to end buyers outside Gibraltar, is entirely outside the scope of any Gibraltar indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST registration threshold because it operates no VAT system. A business importing dutiable goods physically into Gibraltar (whether from the EU, UK, or third countries) must register with the Gibraltar Customs & Excise Division, usually through a licensed customs agent or inland clearance depot, and may obtain a bonded warehouse deferment facility. Foreign entities that arrange only drop-shipments of battery-grade lithium carbonate (never landing in Gibraltar) owe no Gibraltar registration. Separate thresholds arise only if the vehicle later triggers UK or EU VAT: the UK standard VAT registration threshold is £90,000 of UK taxable turnover (for non-established persons the threshold is £0 when goods are physically located in the UK at the time of supply), and the EU-UK distance-selling threshold is £8,818/€10,000 depending on destination member state rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because Gibraltar has no VAT, traditional export zero-rating relief is inapplicable; exports from Gibraltar are simply outside the scope of Gibraltar customs duty. Eligible zero-rating arises only in respect of the UK VAT regime if the Cayman vehicle or its Gibraltar branch becomes UK-VAT-registered (e.g., from a UK PE, a UK warehouse, or UK-taxable distance sales). To claim the 0% rate on export of goods from the UK or Northern Ireland under VATA 1994 s.30 and Schedule 8 Item 1, the supplier must hold valid commercial and transport evidence that the goods were physically removed from the UK within 3 months of the sale, with the buyer being outside the UK, and must retain evidence for 6 years. Where the goods are shipped from a third country direct to a non-UK customer (the normal lithium carbonate arbitrage pattern), no UK zero-rating claim is needed, and refund submission is unnecessary because no output VAT arises.

  • Customs Export Declaration issued by the exporting customs authority (Gibraltar Automated Customs declaration or UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS) export entry) showing ISIC/HS code 2836.91 (lithium carbonate) or applicable HS 2841.90 classification, gross weight, and destination
  • Bill of Lading (original or electronic negotiable sea waybill), House Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical removal of the goods from the export territory within the statutory 3-month window
  • Commercial invoice and final export packing list detailing lithium carbonate grade, purity (e.g., 99.5% battery-grade), quantity, unit value, and Incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) / SWIFT MT103 or MT202 ledger evidence demonstrating that sale proceeds were received in the Cayman Exempted Company's Gibraltarian or other bank account from the foreign customer before or within 180 days of export
  • Certificate of Origin (non-preferential or EUR.1 / Form A) where an applicable trade agreement or preferential rate is invoked by the consignee
  • Signed sales contract, purchase order, and insurance certificate evidencing the transfer of risk and the foreign destination of the goods

Gibraltar has neither a real-time fiscal clearance regime nor an electronic invoicing mandate; paper invoices, PDF invoices, and standard EDI formats are legally valid, and no digital stamping or QR-code clearance is required. UK Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT exists only for UK VAT-registered taxpayers: a Cayman Exempted Company is not obliged to use MTD unless it independently registers for UK VAT after acquiring a UK fixed establishment. If and when the vehicle expands into EU member states, the applicable EU rules on real-time clearance reporting (e.g., Spain's SII, Italy's SDI stamping, France's Factur-X) could be triggered only by a fixed establishment in the respective member state — not by mere cross-border B2B invoicing from Gibraltar.

Abusive zero-rating or fraudulent export claims are treated aggressively by HMRC and Gibraltar courts. Penalties include: civil evasion penalties under VATA 1994 s.65 equal to 100% of the VAT evaded, mitigated down only where the taxpayer discloses voluntarily; the careless/error penalty regime under FA 2007 Sch 24 (15% for careless, 30% for deliberate but unprompted, 70% for intentional evasion); criminal prosecution under CEMA 1979 s.170 and Gibraltar's Criminal Justice (VAT and Financial Fraud) Rules carrying up to 7 years' imprisonment; and confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 (UK) / Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar). Retrospective clawback is permitted for a 6-year assessment window (extended to 20 years for intentional fraud-covered conduct), and HMRC may in addition deregister the trader, deny all outstanding repayment claims, levy interest, and impose a temporary or permanent customs compliance penalty under UCC/Gibraltar joint customs rules. The supplier bears the evidential burden in any subsequent audit; incomplete export documentation results in immediate repayment refusal regardless of economic substance.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands maintain fully convertible currencies with no exchange-control restrictions. The Gibraltar pound is fixed at parity to the British pound, and the Cayman Islands dollar is pegged to the US dollar. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, branch repatriations, and intercompany loan repayments may flow freely without central bank approval, subject only to standard AML/KYC documentation, beneficial-ownership disclosure, economic-substance filings, and tax-reporting formalities in the respective jurisdictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation applies. The Cayman Exempted Company may retain 100% of its export and trade proceeds in any freely convertible currency without a mandatory conversion ratio or statutory timeline. Gibraltar likewise imposes no surrender requirements. The only compliance conditions are proper book-keeping, audited accounts where statutory thresholds are met, and the filing of a Cayman Economic Substance Notice indicating that the entity is tax resident elsewhere or is otherwise out of scope.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England and HM Treasury (United Kingdom), with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the delegated territorial banking and e-money regulator for Gibraltar, and the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) as the home-country supervisor of the Cayman Exempted Company

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Gibraltar-licensed electronic money institutions may accept the Cayman Exempted Company for ancillary account operations, but they are not permitted to provide commercial credit, documentary letters of credit, or investment-grade trade settlement. Post-Brexit, Gibraltar EMIs cannot passport into the EEA, and UK EMIs generally reject offshore Cayman entities without a demonstrable UK/Gibraltar nexus. Offshore digital EMIs may impose low transaction thresholds, limited currency corridors, and stricter counterparty screening for battery-metals commodity flows. Feasibility rating for primary clearing rails: 3/10; for low-value supplier test payments and expense disbursements: 6/10.
