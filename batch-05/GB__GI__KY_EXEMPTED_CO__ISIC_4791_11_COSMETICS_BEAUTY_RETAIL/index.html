Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation-and-duties

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity such as a Cayman Exempted Company first registers as a foreign company with the Gibraltar Companies Registry to obtain a legal presence and registration number. If the entity becomes resident in Gibraltar or derives Gibraltar-source income, it must apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TIN) using Form GITIS and supporting constitutional documents. Gibraltar does not operate a VAT regime, so no VAT ID is required for pure Gibraltar activity; if the entity makes taxable supplies in the United Kingdom, it must separately apply for a UK VAT number under the UK VAT Act 1994.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No direct check-the-box equivalent exists under Gibraltar tax law. Entity classification is determined by the place of central management and control; a Cayman Exempted Company that is not managed or controlled in Gibraltar and has no Gibraltar-source income is outside the scope of Gibraltar income tax. For tax transparency purposes, hybrid mismatch rules under UK/Gibraltar tax treaties and the OECD BEPS Action 2 framework may apply, but no elective transparency mechanism is available in Gibraltar.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None – Gibraltar has no VAT or GST; import duties and excise taxes apply under the Customs (Gibraltar) Act. UK VAT may apply to supplies made into the United Kingdom mainland.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for Gibraltar indirect tax as no VAT/GST is levied. For UK VAT, the registration threshold is GBP 90,000 of taxable turnover in a 12-month rolling period if the entity is established in the UK or makes relevant supplies to UK consumers above the distance selling threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Gibraltar to a destination outside Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar tax and do not attract any indirect tax. Where the entity is registered for UK VAT, zero-rating for export of goods may be claimed only where the goods are physically exported from the UK to an address outside the UK within the statutory time limits, with evidence of export retained in accordance with HMRC Public Notice 200 and the VAT (Exports) Regulations. Offshore services are zero-rated only if they fall within the qualifying categories in VATA 1994 Schedule 8 or Schedule 9, such as services directly related to exported goods or supplied to a customer outside the UK.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Proof of export / delivery confirmation

Gibraltar imposes no real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate. Businesses may issue electronic invoices under normal commercial practice. If UK VAT registration is separately obtained, HMRC Making Tax Digital for VAT requires software-to-HMRC digital submission, but there is no government-authorised clearance or real-time stamping requirement for Gibraltar-based issuers.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating claims attract retrospective UK VAT assessments for up to 6 years in ordinary cases and 20 years in cases involving deliberate fraud, together with interest and penalties. Penalties may reach 100% of the tax evaded under Schedule 24 FA 2007 for inaccuracies and up to 100% under Schedule 24 FA 2007 for deliberate withholding of information; criminal prosecution for VAT evasion can result in fines and imprisonment. Documents must be retained for at least 6 years and made available to HMRC on demand; failure to evidence physical export results in immediate clawback of the zero-rated output VAT and additional default surcharges or penalty assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates a fully convertible currency regime pegged to GBP. Both capital and current account transactions are unrestricted under the Financial Services Act 2019 and Companies Act. Profit remittance and dividend distribution are permitted once solvency and statutory reporting obligations are met. Hurdles are limited to documentary compliance (negative assurance from auditor, proof of source of funds, CRS/FATCA registration) and the absence of a double tax treaty between Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands, which may trigger enhanced substance and beneficial ownership review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Gibraltar imposes no requirement to surrender export proceeds, nor any mandatory conversion ratio or timeline; trade receipts may be held in any freely convertible currency.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the principal financial services regulator; HM Treasury for sanctions and external affairs; Bank of England for pound sterling monetary stability (limited role).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from Cayman proceeds is routed via SWIFT MT103 to the Gibraltar operational bank. The payment must be labelled with ISO 20022 purpose code INTC (intra-company payment) or a supplementary narrative 'Equity Capital Contribution / Capital Injection' in field 72, together with the CRS/FATCA self-certification form, board resolution, and Funds Origin Statement. For amounts above GBP 10,000, the AML regime mandates a source-of-funds declaration, and the payment must be matched to the company's capitalisation statement under the Gibraltar Companies Act.Outbound supplier payments and operational disbursements are cleared via (a) SWIFT MT103 for direct bank wire transfers with purpose code SUPP (payment of trade goods) or GOOD (purchase of goods); (b) commercial letters of credit for large supplier balances above USD 100,000, with ICC UCP 600/URC 522 terms; (c) e-commerce settlement rails integrated with the online storefront (Shopify Payments / PayPal) for consumer refunds and payout to the operating account; and (d) an FX sweep mechanism for any residual balances in foreign settlement currencies.
