Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax) — online portal MyTax (https://mytax.gibraltar.gov.gi); corporate registry: Gibraltar Companies House, Registrar of Companies; indirect/customs authority: HM Customs Gibraltar.

E-government filing portal

Step 2: Submit the company tax registration file to the Income Tax Office (Form IT-COP with certified Cayman certificate of incorporation, memorandum/articles, directors register, Gibraltar registered office certificate and evidence of central management and control) within 30 days of commencing activity; the Commissioner issues a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number/TIN. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope — as a foreign-incorporated Cayman Exempted Company not centrally managed and controlled in Gibraltar, the default Gibraltar position is taxation only on Gibraltar-source profits; if management and control are actually exercised in Gibraltar (and no Qualifying Company certificate is held), the default becomes Entity_Level_Taxation on worldwide profits at 12.5%.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Available Gibraltar elections: (i) Qualifying Company certification under s.40 Income Tax Act 2010 / Qualifying Company Rules 2013 (territorial taxation on Gibraltar-source income only; requires unwinding of disposals, trustee certification and substance confirmation); (ii) group relief / consortium loss-surrender election between group companies; (iii) UK–Gibraltar Double Taxation Agreement residency tie-breaker election (gains applicable treaty benefits for the UK parent flow); (iv) Pillar Two GloBE safe-harbour and income inclusion election notifications for multinational groups exceeding EUR 750m turnover. Gibraltar has not substantively enacted hybrid mismatch rules, so no hybrid-mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST — Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and imposes no value added tax, goods and services tax or general sales tax; only customs import duty, excise duty and property transfer/consent fees may apply.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT registration threshold exists because no VAT is levied in Gibraltar. Any importer of goods subject to the Gibraltar Tariff must be registered as an importer with HM Customs Gibraltar (no minimum monetary threshold); the virtual-card issuer importing no physical goods has no indirect-tax registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — Gibraltar levies no VAT/GST, so there is no 0% export VAT rate, no export VAT refund mechanism and no VAT grouping claim for the card-issuing business. Cross-border supplies of virtual-card services (BIN sponsorship, scheme fees, interchange, processing and spend-management fees) are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax; under the Qualifying Company territorial regime, fees from non-Gibraltar card clients may also fall outside Gibraltar-source income tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at par to pound sterling, which is also legal tender; both currencies circulate interchangeably. Gibraltar operates under a fully liberalised exchange-control regime: there are no restrictions on current or capital account transactions, no obligatory conversion of foreign-currency receipts, and no prior approval requirement for cross-border fund movements. For the Cayman Exempted Company, profit remittance, dividend distribution, and repatriation of capital to non-resident shareholders are unrestricted, subject solely to standard bank due-diligence, CRS/FATCA reporting, and OFSI sanctions screening. Multi-currency operating balances in GBP, EUR, and USD may be retained offshore without a mandatory conversion obligation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Gibraltar has no statutory export-proceeds surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion rules for services deemed exports, including card-processing fees, network sponsorship income, and inter-affiliate service charges. Foreign-currency receipts generated by the virtual-card issuer may be retained, converted, or hedged at the company's discretion, and no percentage of export earnings is required to be liquidated into GIP/GBP.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) acts as the integrated financial services regulator and de facto foreign-exchange administration; the Commissioner of Currency (HM Government of Gibraltar) issues Gibraltar pound notes and coins under a statutory 1:1 sterling peg, and the Bank of England, together with HM Treasury, retains ultimate monetary sovereignty over the British Overseas Territory.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Overall feasibility: Medium. Offshore digital EMIs and banking-as-a-service platforms (e.g., ClearBank, Modulr, DECTA, Railsr, Airwallex, and similar UK/EU-authorized EMIs) are viable for sponsorship, BIN sponsorship, virtual-card programme management, and multi-currency settlement. Legal constraints: (1) A Cayman Exempted Company cannot obtain a GFSC payment-services or e-money licence directly; a Gibraltar-incorporated licensed subsidiary with substantive local presence is mandatory. (2) Post-Brexit, EEA EMI passports no longer extend to Gibraltar, requiring either GFSC fallback licensing or a UK FCA-authorized EMI operating via a Gibraltar registered agent. (3) Mastercard and Visa scheme rules require a Principal Member or approved Sponsor holding segregated settlement and safeguarding accounts at a regulated bank; therefore, pure EMI rails are insufficient for statutory licensing but fully permissible for back-office disbursements and intercompany treasury movement.

Only income economically connected with the Gibraltar PE is taxable; revenue apportionment methodology must be stated and consistent with the transfer pricing documentation. Late filing attracts an initial fixed penalty, a daily surcharge, and a tax-geared penalty up to 100% of the defaulted tax; deliberate understatements can lead to criminal fines. Interest is charged on late payments at the statutory rate.
Line 6 (Deductible expenditure) Operating expenses, GFSC licensing fees, card programme management costs, compliance and AML functions, staff, premises, and network licence fees. Costs must be attributable to the PE under the arm's length principle; payments to the Cayman parent for services must be supported by transfer pricing documentation and show actual service rendered. Late filing attracts an initial fixed penalty, a daily surcharge, and a tax-geared penalty up to 100% of the defaulted tax; deliberate understatements can lead to criminal fines. Interest is charged on late payments at the statutory rate.
Box 7 (Capital allowances and deductions) Capital allowances, depreciation, R&D uplift, and loss claims. Non-resident PE may claim capital allowances only for assets used in the Gibraltar PE; brought-forward losses are restricted if ownership or activity changes. Late filing attracts an initial fixed penalty, a daily surcharge, and a tax-geared penalty up to 100% of the defaulted tax; deliberate understatements can lead to criminal fines. Interest is charged on late payments at the statutory rate.
Box 8 (Chargeable profits and tax) Taxable profit after deductions and relief, computed at 12.5%, and residual tax payable after double taxation relief. Include DTA claim calculations and a certificate of tax residence where treaty relief is asserted; for the Cayman parent, submit the relevant form under the Gibraltar-Cayman exchange framework. Late filing attracts an initial fixed penalty, a daily surcharge, and a tax-geared penalty up to 100% of the defaulted tax; deliberate understatements can lead to criminal fines. Interest is charged on late payments at the statutory rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions for a Local File; Master File required for groups with consolidated revenue exceeding approximately EUR 50 million; arm's length evidence required for intra-group card processing fees, interchange commissions, API licensing, and software royalties.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to the licensed virtual card business as a relevant financial services activity. The Cayman Exempted Company must demonstrate in Gibraltar: a physically present office, full-time local employees, regular board or management meetings in Gibraltar, and operating expenses proportionate to card issuance volume. Cayman Economic Substance Regulations: if the company is tax resident in Gibraltar, it falls outside the Cayman substance test and must file the Cayman annual return confirming tax residence and revenue location; if no proof of Gibraltar place of effective management is maintained, Cayman will treat the entity as relevant and demand Cayman substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Returns

Statutory sanction: Tax-geared penalty up to 100% of underpaid tax, daily surcharges, interest, and criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act for wilful and deliberate false statements.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers face personal restitution for tax lost where they caused false filings, travel restrictions, disqualification, and custodial sentence of up to seven years.
Artificial Substance / Shell Company Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar ESR penalties up to GIP 300,000 or greater for deliberate defaults, possible winding-up and removal from the Register of Companies; GFSC may suspend or revoke the electronic money/card licence.

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified for up to 15 years and made personally liable for the company's unpaid taxes and third-party cardholder losses where the shell arrangement is a façade.
Unlicensed Payment and Virtual Card Issuing

Statutory sanction: Unlimited fine upon criminal conviction; restitution of client funds; regulatory seizure of revenue; revocation of any ancillary permissions; Mastercard/Visa may terminate association.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for illegal business debts, disgorgement of profits, prohibition orders, and imprisonment of up to ten years for carrying on regulated activities without authorisation.
AML / CTF Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: AML fines up to GIP 250,000 or 10% of relevant turnover under the Proceeds of Crime Act; GFSC enforcement action, licence revocation, freezing of client accounts, and public censure.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate money laundering face criminal money-laundering offences, confiscation orders, travel bans, and imprisonment of up to fourteen years; senior managers may also be subject to civil disqualification.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment, interest, shortfall penalty up to 100% of underpaid tax, documentation penalty for failure to maintain the Local File, and secondary adjustment where shifted profit is treated as a distribution.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation are personally liable for penalties if they acted negligently or intentionally; professional disqualification and clawback of bonuses linked to tax avoidance apply.
