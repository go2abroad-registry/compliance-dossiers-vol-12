Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office and HM Customs & Excise (GST Unit), located at The Treasury, Duke of Kent House, Cathedral Square, Gibraltar; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation and https://www.gibraltar.customs.gi

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity (including a Cayman Exempted Company) with a liability to Gibraltar tax must register with the Commissioner of Income Tax within 30 days of commencing taxable activities or of establishing a branch/place of business in Gibraltar. The applicant submits Form FT-1 (Foreign Taxpayer Registration) plus certified copies of the Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, the Gibraltar registered office or branch address, and a notice of appointment of a local representative or agent if applicable. Upon verification and issuance, the Tax Office grants a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) formatted as the Company Number suffixed by 'GI', and, where GST is applicable, a separate GST Registration Number. The registration is effective from the date specified in the certificate, and the applicant must file an annual return and, if GST-registered, periodic GST returns.

Tax ID type & application path: Tax_Exempt_Zone — A foreign entity (including a Cayman Exempted Company) with a liability to Gibraltar tax must register with the Commissioner of Income Tax within 30 days of commencing taxable activities or of establishing a branch/place of business in Gibraltar. The applicant submits Form FT-1 (Foreign Taxpayer Registration) plus certified copies of the Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, Memorandum and Articles of Association, the Gibraltar registered office or branch address, and a notice of appointment of a local representative or agent if applicable. Upon verification and issuance, the Tax Office grants a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) formatted as the Company Number suffixed by 'GI', and, where GST is applicable, a separate GST Registration Number. The registration is effective from the date specified in the certificate, and the applicant must file an annual return and, if GST-registered, periodic GST returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The Cayman Exempted Company may apply to be treated as an exempt/non-resident company for Gibraltar income tax purposes; no check-the-box election is recognised. Under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, the entity may elect to be taxed on a territorial basis, while the OECD hybrid mismatch rules disqualify double-deduction elections where the same loss is also deductible in the Cayman Islands or UK; a 'Qualifying Company' election is available for eligible activities under the Companies (Qualifying Companies) Regulations.

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for a supply of goods (including eco-friendly beechwood Montessori toys) that are exported from Gibraltar to a destination outside Gibraltar, provided the exporter is a GST-registered person and retains evidence of exportation within one month of supply. Services performed for a person who belongs in a country outside Gibraltar are zero-rated if the services are not physically performed in Gibraltar and the direct benefit is received outside Gibraltar. The supply must be recorded in a valid GST return with supporting digital customs declarations; failure to retain proof disentities the claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single European Administrative Document or Gibraltar electronic export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Airwaybill evidencing physical export from Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or foreign exchange bank credit statement proving repatriation of sales proceeds
  • Commercial Invoice describing the goods, quantities, and sale price
  • Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin or statement confirming EU/UK tariff treatment if requested by the destination jurisdiction

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time electronic clearance or tax authority API integration for general B2B supplies. A taxable person must issue a GST invoice containing specified particulars; electronic invoicing is permitted and accepted. Digital stamping is not required for ordinary export or commercial invoices. Mandatory fiscal marking applies only to excise goods (e.g., alcohol, tobacco and hydrocarbons) through the GB/UK CDS procedure, not to the export of educational wooden toys.

The Gibraltar Tax Office and HM Customs & Excise may audit zero-rated export claims within six years of the filing year. Where a claim is found to be fraudulent or unsupported, the full GST at 10% is clawed back from the date of supply together with interest at the official Gibraltar rate. Civil penalties start at 30% of the tax understated and rise to 150% where the return was deliberately submitted. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion in Gibraltar carries fines of up to GIP 10,000 plus three times the tax evaded, and imprisonment of up to two years. Export suspension and loss of good standing in the Registry of Companies may also follow, and the entity may be delisted from the Gibraltar trade register.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands operate fully convertible current and capital accounts, with no statutory approval required to convert GIP/GBP into EUR/USD or to repatriate dividends, capital, or intercompany balances. The GIP is legal tender only within Gibraltar and is not traded offshore; practical convertibility is achieved by clearing in Sterling at GFSC-licensed banks, with GIP book-converted at the fixed 1:1 parity. Profit remittance by the Cayman Exempted Company is unobstructed by Cayman or Gibraltar exchange controls; friction arises solely from AML/KYC-evidence requirements, not from convertibility or profit-remittance permissioning.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Neither Gibraltar nor the Cayman Islands imposes statutory export-proceeds surrender, repatriation timelines, or mandatory conversion ratios. The exporter may lawfully retain 100% of EUR/USD or other foreign-currency earnings in any account currency; any automatic conversion upon credit is a bank-level convenience policy, not a legal requirement.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no indigenous central bank; the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is statutorily fixed at 1:1 with Pound Sterling, making the Bank of England the monetary anchor. The note-issuing authority is HM Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act; banking and payment-system licensing and conduct supervision rest with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC). For the Cayman Exempted Company's home register, the Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) supervises the corporate-services sector; neither CIMA nor GFSC maintains exchange-control administration.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

10% for all corporate profits chargeable to Gibraltar income tax; a Cayman Exempted Company (KY_EXEMPTED_CO) is not subject to Cayman Islands corporate income tax but becomes taxable in Gibraltar at 10% if resident or carrying on trade through a Gibraltar permanent establishment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Gibraltar does not impose dividend withholding tax on distributions made by Gibraltar tax resident companies or by foreign companies in respect of a Gibraltar PE. Dividends paid to the Cayman exempt parent company are paid without Gibraltar WHT.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For an accounting period ending on 31 December, the return must be filed by the following 30 April; for a non-calendar year end, by the last day of the fourth month after the accounting period end. Administrative extensions do not extend the statutory date unless granted by the Commissioner of Income Tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Falsified transfer pricing through tax-exempt Cypher invoices

Statutory sanction: The Gibraltar Income Tax Act levies a penalty of 200% of the tax undercharged for deliberate fraudulent conduct, together with interest from the date the tax would have been payable. On conviction, the court may impose a fine of up to GIP 1,000,000 and order the publication of the taxpayer's name in the Official Gazette as a tax defaulter. Criminal imprisonment of up to seven years may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign intercompany purchase orders or year-end accounts reflecting fabricated intercompany mark-ups are jointly and severally liable for the restitution of the tax evaded. The Gibraltar Supreme Court can issue a travel ban preventing the directors from leaving the jurisdiction, asset freezing orders on their personal bank accounts, and a custodial sentence of between two and seven years for conspiracy to evade tax.
Shell Company - Violation of Economic Substance Regulations

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance (Implementation) Regulations, the Commissioner will issue a penalty of GIP 50,000 for initial non-compliance, a daily penalty of GIP 1,000 for each day the non-compliance continues beyond a 30-day correction window, and may strike the company off the Gibraltar register. Additionally, the tax residence of the company is re-determined, leading to an assessment of 10% tax on all worldwide profits attributable to the actual management place.

Director personal liability: Each director is personally liable to a fine not exceeding GIP 10,000 for a false certificate of economic substance. The directors may be ordered to disclose personal bank records and to surrender shares as security for the tax and penalties. Travel bans are routinely imposed during the criminal investigation and for enforcement of civil restitution orders, and the directors may be disqualified as directors of any Gibraltar company for up to ten years.
Shell Company - False Non-Resident Declaration

Statutory sanction: Making a false statutory declaration constitutes an offence under the Gibraltar Companies Act and the Income Tax Act. The Commissioner may impose a civil penalty not less than 100% of the tax that would have been chargeable, restitute the full shortfall, and refer the case to the Attorney General. The court may also order a fine of up to GIP 250,000 and imprisonment for up to four years.

Director personal liability: The director who signs the false return is liable to civil restitution for all tax, penalties and interest. A criminal conviction results in a mandatory director disqualification order and the court may impose a travel ban as a condition of bail so that the director cannot flee before sentence. Imprisonment of up to seven years may be awarded on the basis of fraudulent evasion of tax.
AML - Proceeds of E-commerce Concealment

Statutory sanction: The Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act grants the Supreme Court wide civil recovery powers, including confiscation of assets derived from criminal conduct equal to the full value of the laundered proceeds. The statutory criminal maximum is an unlimited fine and fourteen years' imprisonment. The Financial Services Commission may also revoke the payment institution licence and prohibit the use of Stripe and Amazon Pay for a period of five years.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal liability to repay the laundered sums under the tort of unlawful concealment. The court may issue a travel ban on each director, freeze their personal accounts related to the Cayman bank, and commit them to prison for non-payment of a confiscation order. Conviction for conspiracy to convert criminal property attracts up to fourteen years and the corporate veil is pierced to render the Cayman holding company directly liable for the Gibraltar debts.
