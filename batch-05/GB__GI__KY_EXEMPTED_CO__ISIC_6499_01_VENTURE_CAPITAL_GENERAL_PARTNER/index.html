Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar - Income Tax Office, 221 Main Street, Gibraltar; Companies House Gibraltar (Registrar of Companies) and Gibraltar Customs under HM Government of Gibraltar. Primary digital portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax and https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/companies-house.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Cayman Exempted Company that intends to operate as a venture capital general partner from/through Gibraltar must first assess whether it creates a permanent establishment in Gibraltar. If it establishes a branch or place of business, it must register as an overseas company with Companies House Gibraltar by filing Form F (Overseas Company), paying the registry fee and appointing a Gibraltar resident process agent. Thereafter, the entity must apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (GTIN) using the standard registration submission, providing certified documents of incorporation, foreign TIN (Cayman tax reference number or TIA number), constitutional documents, and contact details of responsible officers. If the company has no Gibraltar-source income, employees or physical establishment, it may still be required to file an annual nil income tax return or a notice of non-chargeability, depending on the Commissioner's directions. A Gibraltar VAT ID is not applicable because Gibraltar is not part of the EU VAT system and does not impose VAT. If the entity becomes an employer in Gibraltar, it must separately register as an Employer for Income Tax withholding and social insurance purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

There is no 'check-the-box' election in Gibraltar. An overseas company can apply for a formal 'non-residence' confirmation from the Commissioner of Income Tax if central management and control and all economic activity remain outside Gibraltar. Gibraltar has implemented hybrid mismatch rules under ATAD (Cap. 222), and a foreign entity is not automatically treated as tax-transparent; a Cayman Exempted Company may by its own constitution and Cayman law hold an exemption certificate, but such exemption has no automatic Gibraltar effect. For venture capital fund structures, no election is available under Gibraltar law to pass income through a corporate GP to ultimate investors; however, the entity may rely on territorial-source rules to ensure that foreign-sourced carried interest and capital gains remain outside the Gibraltar tax net unless a permanent establishment or Gibraltar-source income arises.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax; Gibraltar levies customs/excise duties on importation of goods and stamp duties on certain instruments. Services provided by a venture capital GP are therefore outside any Gibraltar indirect tax net.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable (GIP). Gibraltar does not operate a VAT turnover threshold. For customs purposes, any importer must register with Gibraltar Customs from the first importation; there is no de minimis economic turnover threshold for indirect tax registration. If the Cayman GP were to make taxable supplies in the United Kingdom mainland, the UK VAT registration threshold (currently GBP 90,000) would apply separately, but Gibraltar itself is outside the UK VAT area.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Gibraltar because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST and therefore no zero-rating mechanism for exported goods or offshore services. Any export of goods is simply outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation. For customs purposes, goods imported into Gibraltar and subsequently re-exported or placed in transit may qualify for relief from customs duty under the Customs Re-export/Transit regime, but there is no VAT refund to substantiate. A venture capital GP's international management services are not subject to Gibraltar output tax and no refund claim is required.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / electronic customs manifest) - required only for physical export movements from Gibraltar
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export and destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT payment evidence - not statutorily required for customs purposes, but retained for evidence of service proceeds in the offshore/V(C) fund context

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping or API-driven e-invoicing applies to ordinary B2B invoices issued by a Gibraltar-based venture capital GP. Invoices are subject to general commercial law. Physical imports and exports require electronic customs declarations submitted through the Gibraltar Customs digital declaration system; customs duty is payable at importation unless relief or suspension applies. Fund management fee invoices to offshore funds may be issued without tax clearance or governmental approval.

Because no VAT zero-rating claim can be made in Gibraltar, there is no special export refund clawback mechanism. Any false claim for a non-existent duty exemption, re-export drawback or other indirect tax relief would be prosecuted under Gibraltar customs legislation, the Criminal Justice (Offences) Act and the Income Tax Act. Civil penalties can reach 100% of the duty evaded plus interest, and criminal penalties for fraudulent evasion can include fines up to GBP 10,000 or twice the amount evaded and imprisonment for up to seven years. Taxpayers misreporting export-related income risk income tax penalties, including assessments on undeclared Gibraltar-source income, CbC filing penalties and transfer pricing adjustment penalties under the Taxation (International Compliance) Regulations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar Pound is pegged at par (1:1) to the British Pound Sterling and is fully convertible for both current account and capital account transactions. Gibraltar maintains no exchange control legislation, no prior approval requirement for inward or outward remittances, and no restriction on the denomination of corporate treasury balances. A Cayman Exempted Company acting as Venture Capital General Partner can freely hold, convert, and repatriate USD, EUR, GBP, and GIP balances. Profit remittance, management fee distributions, carried interest payments, and capital repatriation to non-resident LPs are not subject to withholding tax, remittance quotas, or balance-of-payments approval under Gibraltar law. The practical convertibility constraint is solely bank-level KYC/AML confirmation of source of funds and underlying economic substance, not statutory exchange control. Cross-border capital flows are reportable for statistical purposes under the GFSC balance-of-payments reporting framework and CRS/FATCA obligations, but are not approvable or restrainable by a central bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Gibraltar. There is no statutory timeline for the surrender of export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no designation of export receipts as a separate regulated category. A Gibraltar-resident entity, including the Cayman Exempted Company GP structure, may retain export or other trade receipts in any freely convertible currency. Any reference to surrender rules in group treasury policy is therefore a purely internal cash-management choice, not a regulatory obligation. This position is consistent with the UK's absence of exchange control and with Gibraltar's status as a British Overseas Territory operating outside the EU capital controls framework.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the integrated financial services regulator and de facto foreign exchange administration; monetary authority functions are exercised by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934, with the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) issued at par and fully backed by Bank of England sterling reserves. There is no independent central bank in Gibraltar; the Bank of England acts as the anchor monetary authority for GIP convertibility and systemic sterling liquidity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital Electronic Money Institution accounts are feasible only as an ancillary disbursement channel, not as a primary treasury or capital-call rail. Legal constraint: Gibraltar is outside the EEA, so an EEA EMI passport (e.g., a UK or Maltese EMI) does not automatically extend to Gibraltar; the GFSC does not operate a domestic EMI authorisation regime comparable to the UK EMR 2011. A UK-authorised EMI may lawfully provide e-money and payment services to a Gibraltar corporate client on a reverse-solicitation or cross-border services basis, but such services exclude deposit-taking, interest-bearing balances, and UK Faster Payments membership where settlement must ultimately route through a UK sponsor bank. Feasibility rating: Medium for low-value disbursements to portfolio companies and contractor payments; Low, and legally unsuitable, for the collection of LP capital calls or for segregated fund escrow, because EMI safeguarding accounts lack the credit institution protections required by LP agreements and by the GFSC's client-money expectations. Recommended posture: maintain the EMI as a non-core pre-paid disbursement tool with a £/EUR low balance cap, while all capital-call inflows and investment outflows clear through a Gibraltar authorised deposit-taker.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All LP capital calls are executed as commercial wire transfers via SWIFT MT103, or MT202 where a corporate treasury intermediary is used. The compliant protocol requires: (i) the LP remits directly to the GP's Capital Escrow Account held at a Gibraltar authorised deposit-taker, using a funding instruction that memorialises the capital-call reference number, fund name, and the LPA article authorising the drawdown; (ii) the wire purpose must be stated explicitly as 'Capital contribution under limited partnership agreement' or 'Fund subscription proceeds - early-stage technology fund investment', with the underlying source of funds identifying the LP's own bank and jurisdiction; (iii) no Gibraltar or UK statutory purpose-code approval applies, but the receiving bank completes a balance-of-payments statistical return and internal EDD check, so the GP should furnish the capital-call notice and an authorised signatory list to the bank before the first drawdown; (iv) for USD inbound wires, the correspondent routing must be pre-validated to avoid intermediary bank freezes, using the bank's prescribed US correspondent or a GBPS-aligned clearing path; and (v) where funds arrive from high-risk jurisdictions, the GP shall instruct the bank that the LP is already subject to equivalent KYC, supported by the LP's own regulated status certificate and the executed subscription agreement.Outbound disbursements comprise (i) equity and convertible-note investments into portfolio companies, (ii) management fees, carried interest, and operating costs, and (iii) refunds of excess capital calls. The principal clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for international wires, supported by MT202 for the bank's internal cover payment. For UK-domiciled portfolio companies, GBP transfers are expedited through the Faster Payments Service or CHAPS where the receiving account permits; intra-Gibraltar settlements clear through the local automated clearing house or real-time inter-bank settlement systems. A Commercial Letter of Credit is used only where a portfolio company's operating supplier requires documentary trade security (e.g., equipment purchases), and the GP's issuing bank will require the underlying commercial invoice and transport documents. For every outbound wire, the bank's four-eye authorisation rule applies, and the GP shall provide a board disbursement resolution plus the executed investment agreement or drawdown memo that names the payee, purpose, and final beneficiary. Legal constraint: Gibraltar is outside the EEA, so an EEA EMI passport (e.g., a UK or Maltese EMI) does not automatically extend to Gibraltar; the GFSC does not operate a domestic EMI authorisation regime comparable to the UK EMR 2011. A UK-authorised EMI may lawfully provide e-money and payment services to a Gibraltar corporate client on a reverse-solicitation or cross-border services basis, but such services exclude deposit-taking, interest-bearing balances, and UK Faster Payments membership where settlement must ultimately route through a UK sponsor bank. Feasibility rating: Medium for low-value disbursements to portfolio companies and contractor payments; Low, and legally unsuitable, for the collection of LP capital calls or for segregated fund escrow, because EMI safeguarding accounts lack the credit institution protections required by LP agreements and by the GFSC's client-money expectations. Recommended posture: maintain the EMI as a non-core pre-paid disbursement tool with a £/EUR low balance cap, while all capital-call inflows and investment outflows clear through a Gibraltar authorised deposit-taker.
