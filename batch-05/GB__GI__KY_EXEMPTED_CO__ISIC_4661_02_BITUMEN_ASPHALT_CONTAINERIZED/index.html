Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), 223 Main Street, Gibraltar; online portal at https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company first registers as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under Part 18 of the Companies Act (delivery of certified incorporation documents, memorandum/articles, registered office and local representative details). Upon issuance of a Gibraltar foreign company certificate, the entity must complete the Income Tax Office Corporate Registration Form (IR-1) to obtain a Gibraltar TIN (tax reference number). Non-resident companies are generally assessable only on Gibraltar-source income; registration remains mandatory where a permanent establishment, property, or employment is present. The SOP requires submission of the tax registration form, certified English translations, and evidence of beneficial ownership within the statutory filing periods.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box election. Elections are limited to: (1) designation as a non-resident company for income tax purposes; (2) status election as a 'Category 2' high-net-worth individual regime does not apply to corporate bitumen traders; (3) tonnage tax elections are restricted to qualifying shipping operators; (4) hybrid mismatch elections under the Income Tax Act are ineffective where they conflict with OECD BEPS Action 2 principles.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - no VAT/GST/sales tax is levied in Gibraltar; only narrow customs and excise duties (fuel, alcohol, tobacco) apply

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST registration regime. Excise trading registration is required only for goods falling under the Customs & Excise (Combined Regulations) Act; general commodity wholesalers have no indirect tax registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - because Gibraltar imposes no VAT, there is no zero-rated export status and no VAT refund entitlement. For customs duty purposes, the export of containerized bitumen/asphalt is duty-exempt as an outward and non-Gibraltar consumption movement; the exporter must nevertheless evidence physical removal from Gibraltar with transport documents and consignee destination records to maintain the exemption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (GOLO electronic declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or port/terminal movement receipt
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT confirmation (for trade audit purposes, not FX control)

Not applicable - Gibraltar does not operate continuous transaction controls, fiscal electronic invoicing, digital tax stamps, or real-time invoice clearance through an API gateway. Traders may use any commercial EDI/documentary system; customs declarations are lodged through the Gibraltar Customs On-Line System (GOLO).

In the absence of VAT zero-rating, no VAT clawback can arise. However, false customs declarations, forged transport documents, or misdescribed goods are punishable under the Customs & Excise Ordinance with fines up to 150% of duty avoided, seizure of goods, and imprisonment on indictment. Directors of the Cayman Exempted Company remain subject to Gibraltar criminal liability and disqualification orders for knowingly submitting fraudulent export documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains a fully liberalised capital and current account regime with no exchange controls, no restrictions on profit repatriation, and a hard currency-board peg to GBP. The Cayman Exempted Company is treated as a non-resident corporate investor for FX purposes; incoming equity injections, trade settlements, and dividend or capital distributions to non-resident shareholders are freely convertible into GBP, USD, EUR, and any other major currency without central bank pre-approval. Practical hurdles are limited to AML/KYC verification of the ultimate beneficial owners, source-of-funds documentation, and compliance with UK and international sanctions screening, rather than convertibility limits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement in Gibraltar. Export receipts in any convertible currency may be retained in full (100% retention) and used for operating expenses, intercompany payments, or repatriation without mandatory conversion to GBP. No obligatory timeline, conversion ratio, or central bank accounting exists. This applies equally to goods traded by offshore-incorporated entities using Gibraltar banking infrastructure; bitumen wholesale proceeds may therefore be held in USD or EUR settlement accounts indefinitely.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), acting as the integrated financial regulator and de facto exchange control authority for Gibraltar; the Bank of England retains ultimate monetary issuance for the British pound, to which the Gibraltar pound is pegged on a 1:1 currency-board basis.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-to-Low for primary trade settlement, Low for escrow capital holding. GFSC-licensed e-money institutions (EMIs) and cross-border digital payment platforms such as Airwallex or Ebury may be used for lower-value pre-shipment disbursements and FX conversions, but they are not permitted to take deposits from the public and offer no access to central bank money or deposit insurance. For a Cayman Exempted Company engaging in containerized bitumen wholesale, most leading EMIs impose enhanced restrictions on offshore-incorporated clients and capping daily transaction volumes; robust legal corporate opening is possible but treasury-critical funds should remain in tier-1 licensed banks with SWIFT membership. The predominant legal constraint is the Gibraltar E-Money Regulations 2011 (as amended), requiring safeguarding of client funds and prohibiting use of EMI accounts as settlement accounts for commercial letters of credit.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection from the Cayman Exempted Company or its non-resident shareholders to a Gibraltar operational account must be executed through SWIFT MT103 (customer credit transfer) or MT202 (bank-to-bank cover) with strict OFAC/UK Sanctions pre-screening. Recommended purpose codes for the wire transfer are '101' (Business to Business – Trade in Goods) for trade-related working capital, or '200' (Corporate Equity and Capital) for equity injection; for intercompany funding the code 'GIC' (Global Intercompany) or 'CF' (Capital Transfer) is accepted by Barclays and other GFSC-licensed banks. The routing chain must identify the ultimate beneficial owner as the first beneficiary, and the Gibraltar bank will request a copy of the underlying board resolution, signed KYC pack, and evidence of source of funds prior to crediting. Any inbound wire from a Cayman exempted entity must not mention only a generic 'corporate credit' without an economic purpose; a detailed remittance instruction referencing the bitumen purchase programme or general working capital allocation materially accelerates compliance approval.Disbursements to bitumen suppliers, freight forwarders, terminals, and customs agents are executed via (a) SWIFT MT103 single TT wires for high-velocity settlements not requiring documentary credits, and (b) commercial Letters of Credit issued through the Gibraltar account bank's correspondent network using SWIFT MT700 (issue), MT705 (pre-advice), MT707 (amendment), and MT734 (refusal advice). For containerized drummed and bagged penetration-grade bitumen (ISIC 4661), the standard clearing rail is a confirmed irrevocable documentary credit payable at sight against presentation of a clean on-board bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, and independent quality certificate. For open-account settlements with trusted counterparties, SWIFT MT103 TT wires with purpose code '101' are advised, accompanied by pre-shipment copies of the sales contract and invoice to maintain a fully auditable transaction ledger. Settlement currencies should remain in USD, EUR, or GBP; the trade contract must explicitly denominate a single settlement currency to avoid foreign exchange mismatch at the instruction level.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX forward contracts with a GFSC-licensed counterparty bank (up to 12 months tenor) on GBP/USD, GBP/EUR, and USD/EURNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on restricted or higher-volatility currencies used by the bitumen supply chain, where principal settlement is netted in USD or GBPCross-currency interest rate and principal swaps, negotiated under an ISDA agreement, with licensed counterparties for converting trade debt or capital balances into the desired operational currency

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

10%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar does not impose withholding tax on dividend distributions made by a Gibraltar company or by a non-resident company's Gibraltar PE. Cayman exempted company dividends are also exempt in the Cayman Islands by domestic law.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The CT-1 and payment must be delivered by 30 November following the end of the company's year of assessment (i.e., for an accounting period ending 31 December, filing is due 30 November of the next calendar year). Companies in activities requiring more regular reporting must remit provisional tax in four quarterly instalments (30 April, 31 July, 31 October and 31 January), with final balancing charge or refund due at the filing deadline.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar's Income Tax (Transfer Pricing) Regulations require a Local File covering controlled transactions where the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions (goods and services) attributable to Gibraltar exceeds GBP 5,000,000 per accounting period; a Master File must be prepared when the worldwide group revenue exceeds GBP 50,000,000. Lower thresholds (GBP 1,000,000 for intercompany services and GBP 2,000,000 for financing and interest) trigger transactional documentation. These apply to Cayman exempted companies treated as having a Gibraltar PE and to resident associated enterprises.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations 2019 (as revised) apply to every tax-resident entity carrying out a 'relevant activity'. The wholesale supply of drummed bitumen and asphalt is a 'distribution and service business'. A Cayman exempted company that is managed and administered in Gibraltar or that has a PE carrying on this relevant activity must demonstrate economic substance by employing adequate Gibraltar-resident staff with the required qualifications, incurring sufficient Gibraltar operating expenditure, being directed and managed from premises in Gibraltar, and maintaining the requisite evidence in Gibraltar. An annual Economic Substance Return must be supplied using the Gibraltar ESR portal. If the company is only a non-resident with no relevant activity in Gibraltar, ESR does not apply in Gibraltar, though the Cayman ESR obligations may still apply if the company is a Cayman tax-resident entity (which an exempted company normally is not).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fraudulent filing

Statutory sanction: Civil fraud: penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded plus daily default penalties and interest at 8% per annum. Criminal conviction: a fine not exceeding GBP 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 7 years under Gibraltar's Income Tax Act and customs/anti-fraud legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who knowingly take part in or consent to the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, interest and penalties; the court may issue a travel ban, seize passports, prohibit leaving Gibraltar, disqualify the individual as a director for 10 years, impose civil restitution of diverted corporate assets, and, in serious cases, impose custodial sentences of 2 to 5 years together with confiscation orders.
Shell company / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Failure to comply with an Economic Substance Notice attracts a financial penalty starting at GBP 10,000 and up to GBP 100,000 (or higher where the relevant activity is high-risk); the Commissioner may require a remedial plan, impose daily default penalties of GBP 2,500, strike the company off the Gibraltar Register of Companies, and deny tax residence certificates.

Director personal liability: Directors who allow the company to continue without substance are personally liable for the ESR penalty; on strike-off, the directors become jointly and severally liable for debts and tax liabilities; the Registrar may apply for disqualification and travel restrictions pending liquidation and tax collection.
Transfer pricing documentation and arm's-length violation

Statutory sanction: An adjustment will be made by the Commissioner using the approved OECD methods; additional tax will be charged at 10%, plus late-payment interest. The penalty for a TP adjustment is 35% of the additional tax, increased to 100% where the pricing error is due to deliberate falsification or concealment. The absence of a TP file may raise the penalty and shift the burden of proof.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the CT-1 without confirming arm's-length TP are exposed to TP penalty recovery to the extent the company is unable to pay; a negative TP opinion triggers anti-avoidance reporting in the Gibraltar tax office and can result in subsidiary-company director disqualification, personal restitution of tax deficits, and interdiction from receiving foreign exchange clearance in the port district.
AML / money laundering / sanctions evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar) and the Criminal Finances Act, money laundering is punishable by imprisonment up to 14 years, an unlimited fine, and a confiscation order; civil recovery proceedings allow the court to seize the bitumen cargo, containers, and bank balances; AML licensing sanctions include suspension of the payment rail (Swift LC/TT) and prohibition from trading in the Gibraltar port.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners with even gross negligence face personal criminal exposure; the prosecutor may issue a travel ban and passport surrender; a restraint order under the Proceeds of Crime Act immobilizes personal and corporate assets in Gibraltar; convicted directors are disqualified for life and subject to extradition agreements.
Non-filing and non-payment of corporation tax

Statutory sanction: Late filing: GBP 100 plus daily penalties up to GBP 60 per day, and an automatic tax-geared penalty of 5% of the tax where the return is up to 12 months late, rising to 10% after 12 months; if the company continues to evade, the penalty reaches 100%. The Commissioner may issue a 'Notice to Show Cause', enforce payment by seizure and sale of the containerized bitumen, issue garnishee proceedings on bank accounts, or petition the Gibraltar Supreme Court to wind up the company.

Director personal liability: Where the company is a close investment-holding company or a director-controlled trading company and tax remains unpaid for more than 6 months, the unpaid tax becomes a personal liability of the directors who knew or had reason to believe the tax would not be paid, and they must reimburse the company; personal enforcement includes bank account freezes, travel bans, and restriction on leaving Gibraltar until a judgment for corporation tax is secured.
