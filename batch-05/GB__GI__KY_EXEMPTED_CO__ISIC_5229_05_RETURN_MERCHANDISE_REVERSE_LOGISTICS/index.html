Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow deposit requirement apply to a Cayman Exempted Company establishing a Gibraltar branch; the Cayman vehicle may operate with nominal issued capital of USD 1.00 per share (authorised capital USD 50,000), and Gibraltar does not impose any local capitalisation or escrow condition for external companies.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate required under the Fire Safety (Premises) Regulations 2018, issued by the Gibraltar Fire and Rescue Service; structural load inspection of warehouse racking in accordance with BS EN 15512, certified by an approved structural engineer; workplace clearance and occupational health-and-safety registration under the Safety at Work Act 1989 (Gibraltar), enforced by the Safety at Work Directorate, covering staff induction, manual handling protocols, and goods-grading bench safety.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Ministry of Finance); official e-portal: Gibraltar Government e-Services portal at https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi. Ancillary authorities for this dossier are the Gibraltar Companies House (overseas company and branch registration) and the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Department (customs declarations, import/export duties, and excise administration).

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Company that establishes a taxable presence in Gibraltar must comply with the following procedure: (1) Determine whether a Gibraltar permanent establishment or branch exists; a branch is presumed where the reverse logistics activity is carried on from a fixed place in Gibraltar. (2) Register the Cayman Exempted Company as an overseas company with the Gibraltar Companies House under the Gibraltar Companies Act, submitting a certified copy of the Cayman Certificate of Incorporation, the Memorandum and Articles of Association, the register of directors and members, and a Gibraltar registered office address for service. (3) Obtain the Gibraltar company registration number issued by the Companies House. (4) Apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (GTIN) using the appropriate company registration form; the GTIN is the sole statutory tax reference. No separate VAT registration is required because Gibraltar does not operate a VAT/GST regime. (5) If the entity engages in physical import or export of returned merchandise, register as a trader with HM Customs and Excise to obtain customs handling codes and a trader's reference for the filing of customs declarations. (6) Maintain the Cayman tax-exemption undertaking and pay the annual Cayman Islands Government fee to preserve the exempted company status; that Cayman status does not displace Gibraltar registration or taxation obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope. For Gibraltar income tax purposes, the Cayman Exempted Company is a separate juridical person and is not automatically a Gibraltar tax resident. Where central management and control is outside Gibraltar and the company operates only through a Gibraltar permanent establishment, Gibraltar taxes only profits attributable to that Gibraltar-source activity. Where central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar, the company becomes Gibraltar tax resident and is subject to entity-level taxation on worldwide income and gains, subject to credit for foreign tax. The Cayman exemption certificate applies only to Cayman income and gains and is not a Gibraltar tax exemption.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not provide a check-the-box or fiscal-transparency entity classification election comparable to the U.S. system. A Cayman Exempted Company cannot elect to be treated as a disregarded entity or partnership for Gibraltar tax purposes. There is no hybrid-mismatch election available in Gibraltar domestic law; hybrid mismatch outcomes are addressed by reference to international tax principles and BEPS Action 2 recommendations. The only relevant statutory elections are ordinary tax-administration elections, such as the choice of accounting period and group relief elections where Gibraltar-resident group conditions are satisfied. The Cayman Islands statutory election to obtain a 20-year tax exemption undertaking is a Cayman law mechanism and is not recognized as a Gibraltar tax exemption.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales and use tax is imposed in Gibraltar. Gibraltar applies only customs and excise duties and similar import/export duties on specific goods.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no VAT/GST registration threshold in Gibraltar because no VAT/GST regime exists. For customs purposes, any economic operator importing or exporting commercial goods must be registered with the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Department for customs handling and must lodge a customs declaration for each consignment; there is no de minimis turnover threshold for commercial customs registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a Gibraltar VAT zero-rating claim because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST system. Goods exported from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation; no zero-rating claim is required for such exports. Offshore services supplied from Gibraltar to foreign customers are likewise outside Gibraltar indirect taxation. If the Cayman Exempted Company also moves goods through, or makes supplies from, the United Kingdom, UK VAT zero-rating may apply under the Value Added Tax Act 1994, section 30, and HMRC Notice 700/6, conditioned on qualifying status, export evidence, physical export, and receipt of payment within the statutory evidence period.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / C88 or electronic equivalent)
  • Commercial Invoice and detailed Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (original or electronic negotiable/non-negotiable evidence of physical movement)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment evidence evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Certificate of Origin, where required by the destination jurisdiction
  • Return Merchandise Authorization record and quality-grading report for reverse logistics traceability

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time electronic invoicing, continuous transaction controls, fiscal clearance, API-based reporting, or digital stamping for VAT/GST purposes. Electronic commerce is permitted but not legally mandated for indirect-tax clearance. Customs clearance is performed electronically through the Gibraltar customs electronic declaration system using the standard Single Administrative Document (SAD), with no separate national VAT e-invoice clearance requirement.

Because Gibraltar does not operate a VAT zero-rating system, there is no Gibraltar VAT clawback mechanism. However, false declarations made in connection with customs exports, temporary importation, re-export, or outward processing relief are subject to audit scrutiny by Gibraltar Customs and Excise and are prosecuted under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance; penalties include forfeiture of goods, monetary penalties up to three times the value of the goods or duty evaded, and imprisonment. If the entity invokes UK VAT zero-rating on goods exported through the UK, HMRC may retrospectively assess unpaid VAT with statutory interest under VAT Act 1994, Schedule 24 inaccuracies penalties, and business-promotion penalties for deliberate evasion; serious fraud cases may result in criminal prosecution and clawback of any previously granted refund or VAT credit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar has a free capital and current account regime with no general exchange control restrictions. The Gibraltar pound is pegged at parity to the British pound, and the euro, US dollar, and other freely convertible currencies are routinely accepted for banking purposes. A Cayman exempted company operating through Gibraltar can remit dividends, profits, and intercompany balances in any major currency without requiring a conversion permit, and there is no local withholding tax on outbound distributions to non-residents. Restrictions arise only where remittance touches UK/Gibraltar sanctions or where the financial institution is unable to satisfy source-of-funds requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Neither Gibraltar nor the Cayman Islands imposes any statutory export proceeds surrender obligation. There is no timeline within which export receipts must be repatriated, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies to foreign currency earnings. Export proceeds may remain in one of the clearing currencies (GBP, USD, EUR) at the treasury manager's discretion, subject only to ordinary AML reporting thresholds and sanctions screening.

Central bank supervision: No independent central bank exists in Gibraltar. Monetary and sterling issuance authority derives from the Bank of England and the UK Treasury framework. Legal tender notes are issued by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act, while banking and financial services supervision are administered by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC). UK HM Treasury remains the sanctions authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High relative feasibility, with clear legal constraints. Airwallex, as the designated fintech rail, is feasible for this reverse-logistics treasury structure because it offers local ACH, SEPA, and SWIFT MT103 clearing without requiring a traditional full-service banking relationship. Critical constraints: Airwallex is an electronic money institution, not a licensed bank, and its safeguarding arrangements do not constitute deposit insurance. The Gibraltar entity must ensure that the EMI’s services are not marketed into Gibraltar without proper GFSC notification or passporting approval, and the Cayman exempted company must be able to evidence economic substance and beneficial ownership to Airwallex’s UK FCA regulated compliance function.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from the Cayman shareholder must be routed through a compliant SWIFT MT103 wire from a recognized bank, using purpose code INVC-capital subscription and the remittance field 'RETURN LOGISTICS EQUITY CONTRIBUTION'. The beneficiary account is the Gibraltar operational account, and the charge code should be SHA to avoid excessive correspondent deductions. The complete verification pack must include the board capital allocation resolution, source-of-funds statement covering the prior six months, Cayman incorporation documents, Gibraltar GFSC registrability indication, and beneficial ownership declaration. No prior exchange-control permission is required, but the receiving bank will confirm the purpose code against its regulating reporting threshold.Trade disbursements for returned merchandise and reverse logistics settlements will use a multi-rail structure: domestic UK refunds via BACS or Faster Payments, EU refunds via SEPA Credit Transfer or SEPA Instant, and cross-border refunds or supplier payments via SWIFT MT103 with full remittance reference. Commercial letters of credit are reserved for high-value re-import or restocking contracts that require documentary compliance, using delayed payment or deferred payment LC forms consistent with ISBP 745.
