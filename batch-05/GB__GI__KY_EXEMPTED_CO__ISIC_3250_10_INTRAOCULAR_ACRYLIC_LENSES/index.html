Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar Income Tax Office, 6-10 Parliament Lane, Gibraltar; online portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Cayman Exempted Company that commences trade in Gibraltar, establishes a branch, or derives Gibraltar-source income must file a Company Application for Tax Identification Number (TIN) with the Commissioner of Income Tax. Supporting documents include notarised certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents from Cayman, certificate of good standing, certificate of tax exemption under the Cayman Tax Transparency Act, resolutions appointing Gibraltar local agent/representative, director and shareholder register extracts, and details of intended Sierra activity. Registration is due within one month of commencement of business in Gibraltar. The Income Tax Office issues a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) used for income tax, employer obligations, and customs/statistical filing. If the Cayman entity has no physical presence or local income and does not carry on a trade in Gibraltar, no statutory registration is compulsory, but banking and licensing counterparties normally require a letter of non-residence from the Cayman tax authority.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Territorial_Scope): The Cayman Exempted Company is treated as a separate corporate person subject to Gibraltar income tax at 12.5% only on income arising in or derived from Gibraltar. No pass-through or fiscal transparency is available. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Territorial_Scope): The Cayman Exempted Company is treated as a separate corporate person subject to Gibraltar income tax at 12.5% only on income arising in or derived from Gibraltar. No pass-through or fiscal transparency is available. The Cayman exempt status does not create a Gibraltar tax exemption; it merely confirms absence of Cayman corporate income tax.

Available election forms

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT or GST; Gibraltar operates a customs and excise duty regime (Gibraltar Customs Duty and environmental taxes) outside the UK VAT territory.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A - no VAT/GST registration regime and no turnover threshold in Gibraltar. Importers and excise-licensed businesses must obtain prior registration with HM Customs Gibraltar regardless of turnover; a valid TRN or Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) equivalent is required for customs declarations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar does not operate a VAT system, so no Gibraltar VAT zero-rating claim exists. Physical exports of goods from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar Cairns duties and qualify for duty relief where the goods leave the jurisdiction under a valid customs export declaration. For goods sold to UK/EU customers, the exporter may be required to register for UK VAT or an EU member state VAT; such cross-border supplies may be zero-rated under UK VAT section 30 VATA 1994 subject to evidence of physical export within 90 days. Offshore services supplied to non-residents are treated as outside the scope of Gibraltar income tax under territorial rules; no refund mechanism arises because no local indirect tax has been levied.

  • Gibraltar customs export declaration (CDS-G Export Summary Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical dispatch to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT / MT103 transfer evidence confirming export proceeds realisation
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration
  • Certificate of Origin (where preferential tariff treatment or origin rules are claimed)
  • UK/EU VAT registration number and export evidence where zero-rating is claimed in a VAT jurisdiction

No real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping requirement for Gibraltar domestic transactions. Goods entering or leaving Gibraltar must be declared electronically through the Reciprocal Customs Declaration Service (CDS-G). Invoices issued by the Cayman/Gibraltar entity must retain the TRN, sufficient consideration detail, and must be retained for six years under the Companies Act; no government clearance stamp is required before issue.

Gibraltar Income Tax Act allows assessments and reassessments within six years of the chargeable period, extending to twenty years where neglect or fraud is involved. Fraudulent export zero-rating or customs claims may trigger full clawback of relief, interest at statutory rate, and penalties up to 100% of the tax or duty evaded. Knowingly submitting false export documents, forged shipping evidence, or circular remittance documentation constitutes tax fraud punishable by civil penalties, forfeiture of imported/exported goods, and criminal sanctions (imprisonment and unlimited fines). UK/EU VAT zero-rating abuses additionally expose the taxpayer to VAT reassessment, penalty assessments, and disqualification from exporter schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility exists in all three layers (Cayman Islands, Gibraltar, and United Kingdom). The Cayman Islands dollar (KYD) maintains a statutory peg of USD 1.00 = KYD 0.80 with no profit-remittance restrictions and zero withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties, or branch remittances for a Cayman Exempted Company. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) circulates at par with GBP within the UK monetary system, and UK exchange controls have been abolished since 1979. Residual remittance hurdles are confined to (i) OFAC and UK HMT sanctions screening applied by correspondent banks, (ii) enhanced due diligence arising from the Cayman tax-exempt structure and historical FATF grey-list status, and (iii) compliance with the International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act requiring genuine substance in Gibraltar for the ISIC 3250 intraocular acrylic lens manufacturing activity. Profits may be freely remitted in USD, EUR, GBP, or CHF via SWIFT without central-bank approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules in the Cayman Islands, Gibraltar, or the United Kingdom. Export receipts from the sale of foldable hydrophobic intraocular acrylic lenses may be retained indefinitely in USD, EUR, GBP, or any other freely convertible currency in approved banking accounts. No mandatory conversion into KYD, GIP, or GBP is imposed. The only binding obligations are (i) banking documentation rules requiring clean underlying trade records (commercial invoices, airway bills/bills of lading, packing lists, customs declarations) for any export receipt exceeding USD 50,000 equivalent, and (ii) UK sovereign-level cross-border trade statistical declarations (Intrastat/GB import-export filings) where invoicing flows through the Gibraltar operating branch. Neither obligation constitutes exchange control or surrender of export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Cayman Islands Monetary Authority (CIMA) as the home-state regulator of the Cayman Exempted Company; Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the administering regulator of the Gibraltar banking jurisdiction; Bank of England (United Kingdom) as the ultimate sovereign monetary, lender-of-last-resort, and UK HMT sanctions/foreign-exchange policy authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-Low for primary banking and Medium as an ancillary disbursement rail. Legal and compliance constraints: leading UK/Gibraltar-regulated EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business, Currencycloud) generally prohibit or strictly de-risk corporate clients registered in the Cayman Islands or holding an offshore tax-exempt status; a Cayman Exempted Company in the high-risk medical device sector is frequently rejected at onboarding. Additional blockers: (i) ISIC 3250 intraocular lens trade invoices frequently exceed the per-transaction caps of EMIs; (ii) EMIs do not offer documentary Letter of Credit, trade finance, or escrow facilities required by the supply chain; (iii) transaction monitoring profiles flag tax-exempt offshore entities combined with high-value B2B movements. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Step 1: The shareholder's private bank executes a SWIFT MT103 wire to the Cayman escrow account (Cayman National Bank or Butterfield Bank) in USD with remittance field 70 referencing 'Foreign Direct Investment – Share Subscription in Cayman Exempted Company GB__GI__KY_EXEMPTED_CO__ISIC_3250_10'; the ISO 20022/BPM6 purpose code used is BDIR (foreign direct investment, equity) or INVE where structured as intercompany equity injection. Step 2: The paying and receiving banks screen the transaction against OFAC, UK HMT, and EU consolidated sanctions lists; the wire is cleared only if the Cayman escrow bank has a completed source-of-funds dossier on file. Step 3: After registration of the capital increase with the Cayman Registrar, funds are transferred intra-group via MT103 from the Cayman escrow account to the Gibraltar International Bank operational account using purpose code INCA (intra-company capital transfer) or GDSV (goods/services purchase). Step 4: The Unique End-to-End Transaction Reference (UETR) is logged for every leg to ensure auditability under GFSC transaction-monitoring rules. Step 5: Foreign-exchange conversion is immaterial for the KYD/USD peg but is executed at mid-market plus no more than 5 pips where USD is converted to GBP/EUR for Gibraltar working capital. Compliance restriction: no third-party deposits are accepted within the first 180 days after opening without completion of a supplementary KYC review.Primary rail: SWIFT MT103 free-format wires for routine procurement of hydrophobic acrylic copolymer raw material, lathe tooling, and sterilization consumables where the individual invoice is below USD 250,000; reference each transfer with the supplier invoice number (e.g., 'PUR-MED-AMPLEX-001') and settlement occurs via HSBC London correspondent clearing (CHAPS for GBP, TARGET2 for EUR, Fedwire/CHIPS for USD). Secondary rail (mandatory for capital equipment): irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit issued under UCP 600 in favor of Swiss or German CNC lathe and precision-tooling suppliers, payable at sight with a 10% amount tolerance and a certificate-of-origin document set; issuance bank is Butterfield Bank (Cayman) with reimbursement through its London correspondent. Customer collection rail: incoming MT103 documentary collections accompanied by airway bills and certificates of conformity for EU/US distributors, or open-account settlements on a monthly netting basis where a standby Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT760) supports the receivable. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-Low for primary banking and Medium as an ancillary disbursement rail. Legal and compliance constraints: leading UK/Gibraltar-regulated EMIs (Wise, Revolut Business, Currencycloud) generally prohibit or strictly de-risk corporate clients registered in the Cayman Islands or holding an offshore tax-exempt status; a Cayman Exempted Company in the high-risk medical device sector is frequently rejected at onboarding. Additional blockers: (i) ISIC 3250 intraocular lens trade invoices frequently exceed the per-transaction caps of EMIs; (ii) EMIs do not offer documentary Letter of Credit, trade finance, or escrow facilities required by the supply chain; (iii) transaction monitoring profiles flag tax-exempt offshore entities combined with high-value B2B movements. Viable structuring alternative: establish a Gibraltar-incorporated operating subsidiary or a UK-registered branch of the manufacturing business, open the primary treasury relationship with Gibraltar International Bank, and deploy an EMI exclusively for sub-GBP 50,000 monthly recurring payments (commissions, refunds, small supplier disbursements).
