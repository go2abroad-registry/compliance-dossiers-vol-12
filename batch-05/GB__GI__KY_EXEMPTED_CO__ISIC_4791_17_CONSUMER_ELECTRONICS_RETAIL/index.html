Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar — Income Tax Office (https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation); HM Revenue & Customs for UK VAT registration (https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration)

E-government filing portal

Foreign companies whose central management and control is in Gibraltar must apply to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office for a Company Tax Reference Number (TRN) using the prescribed registration form, attaching the certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, evidence of registered office or managed control in Gibraltar, and a completed information schedule; the TRN is issued within 14 business days. Separately, to make UK VAT-taxable supplies, the non-UK-established entity must file Form VAT1 with HMRC to obtain a UK VAT registration number; there is no Gibraltar VAT ID because Gibraltar does not operate a VAT regime.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available under Gibraltar or UK law. The Cayman Exempted Company’s status as tax-exempt in the Cayman Islands is an irrevocable election made under section 6 of the Cayman Companies Act, but it does not confer tax exemption in Gibraltar or the United Kingdom. Under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, there are no pass-through elections for companies; only limited elections for group relief and double-taxation relief are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 85,000 for UK-established persons; GBP 0 for non-UK-established persons making any taxable supply in the UK; Gibraltar-specific VAT registration threshold not applicable

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A supply of goods is zero-rated for UK VAT where the goods are exported from the United Kingdom to a destination outside the UK, including Gibraltar, within three months of the time of supply, provided the supplier holds and preserves valid evidence of export. Services are zero-rated when supplied to a recipient who is outside the UK and the relevant conditions in Schedule 8, Group 7 of the VAT Act 1994 are met. For goods sold and shipped directly from Gibraltar to a foreign buyer, no UK VAT zero-rating arises because the supply is not made in the UK.

  • Customs Export Declaration (UK ECSL/SAD electronic exit summary)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing export payment

No obligatory real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or API-based e-invoicing in Gibraltar. In the UK, VAT-registered businesses must comply with Making Tax Digital for VAT, maintaining digital records and submitting returns via functional compatible software; electronic invoices are permitted but not mandated.

HMRC will deny zero-rating and assess unpaid VAT at 20% plus interest if export evidence is incomplete or inaccurate. Deliberate inaccuracies attract penalties from 30% to 200% under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007, with potential criminal prosecution under section 72 of the VAT Act 1994; any input VAT recovered on goods subsequently found not to have been exported is clawed back with interest and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar Pound (GIP) is pegged at 1:1 to GBP with unlimited legal-tender convertibility within the territory; GBP circulates freely alongside GIP. Both current and capital account transactions are fully convertible with no exchange control legislation applicable, and there are no statutory restrictions on capital movement, profit repatriation, dividend payment, or loan servicing. Practical hurdles are limited to bank AML/KYC evidentiary requirements; UK CFC or UK income-tax risks may arise only if the Cayman entity's central management and control is exercised from the United Kingdom.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory time limit applies to export or e-commerce sales proceeds in Gibraltar. The entity may freely retain, convert, or disburse receipts in GIP, GBP, EUR, USD, or any other convertible currency through a GFSC-licensed bank without reporting to monetary authorities. Compliance outside the FX sphere is limited to standard AML/CFT and FATF/OECD expected standards.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (GBP monetary anchor) in conjunction with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), the statutory licensing and supervisory authority for banks and financial services in Gibraltar. Gibraltar has no own central bank; GIP currency issuance is conducted by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934, fully backed by sterling reserves.

The return must be filed within twelve months after the fiscal year-end (accounting period); companies with a 31 December year-end must file no later than 30 November of the following year. Quarterly provisional payments may apply to large taxpayers, but the annual return remains the sole annual filing event.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GBP 1,000,000

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar has adopted the OECD/Alberta-style Economic Substance Regulations applicable to entities carrying on relevant activities. The online retail of electronics constitutes distribution and e-commerce, which is a 'relevant activity' for this purpose. The Gibraltar PE must demonstrate compliance through: adequate physical premises in Gibraltar, full-time employees proportionate to the level of activity (minimally 2–5 for a low-risk distribution hub), annual compliance report, sufficient annual operating expenditure in Gibraltar, and a registered office actually used and supervised. Failure to satisfy economic substance requirements triggers a review by the Gibraltar Office of the Commissioner of Income Tax and may lead to a recharacterization of Cayman profits as Gibraltar-source, penalties up to GBP 100,000, and compulsory strike-off of the local registration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Additional assessment up to 200% of the omitted tax, GBP 100,000 fixed penalty, and daily surcharges of up to GBP 250. Criminal prosecution on indictment can result in imprisonment for up to 7 years. Tax records and banking records are subject to subpoena, and asset freezing orders may be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in evasion are personally jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid taxes and penalties. Criminal liability extends to imprisonment, prohibition from acting as a director for up to 10 years, travel bans, and seizure of personal assets where the corporate veil is pierced.
Shell Company / Sham PE

Statutory sanction: The Comptroller may recharacterize the arrangement as sham, attribute all profits derived from Gibraltar consumers to the Gibraltar PE, disallow expenses charged by the Cayman head office as non-allowable, and levy a protective penalty of GBP 50,000. Repeated non-compliance results in compulsory strike-off and cancellation of the Gibraltar trade license.

Director personal liability: Directors and anyone acting in a management capacity risk civil restitution for amounts re-assessed; courts may issue disqualification orders under the Gibraltar Companies Act, travel bans for questioning, and prison terms up to 5 years for fraudulent trading where the sham was knowingly used to mislead tax authorities.
AML / Banking and Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act (Gibraltar) and the AML Regulations, penalties may reach GBP 250,000 per offence plus confiscation of criminally derived funds. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission can revoke any pending EMI or e-money licence developed under the ATT. Criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment for up to 14 years for money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners face personal criminal liability, asset confiscation orders, travel bans, and serious crime prevention orders. The corporate veil may be pierced to recover laundered assets from directors' bank accounts; in high-value cases, extradition proceedings with the UK may be instituted.
Transfer Pricing / Substance Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar TP adjustment rules permit re-computation of profits using arm's length prices, plus a 50% penalty on any underpaid tax attributable to non-arm's length pricing. Annual non-substantiation after substance review triggers a GBP 25,000 administrative fine and may cause the return to be rejected as invalid.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the submission of a false substance declaration are personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall, including interest and penalties. Courts may impose personal financial liability under the principle of fraudulent misrepresentation, order disqualification from serving as officers, and issue travel bans pending investigation. In cases of cross-border conspiracy, imprisonment of up to 7 years applies.
