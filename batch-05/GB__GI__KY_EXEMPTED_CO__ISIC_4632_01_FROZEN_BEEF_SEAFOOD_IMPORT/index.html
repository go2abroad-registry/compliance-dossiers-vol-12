Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar ('Commissioner of Income Tax'), 8 Bayfront / Main Street, Gibraltar; registered through the Gibraltar iTax e-filing portal and central government website (https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi). HM Customs (Gibraltar) administers import/export duties and pre-clearance; the Gibraltar Environmental Health Department issues veterinary/health permits for frozen beef and pelagic fish; the Registrar of Companies (Companies House Gibraltar) handles branch registration of overseas companies.

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Islands Exempted Company is not required to re-incorporate in Gibraltar. Steps: (1) Appoint a licensed Gibraltar corporate service provider (a 'Registered Agent') and maintain a local registered office at an approved address. (2) Register the foreign company as an 'Overseas Company' with the Registrar of Companies under the Gibraltar Companies Act 2014, filing certified constitutional documents, a list of directors/secretary, and a Gibraltar registered office address; obtain a Gibraltar company registration number ('G-number'). (3) Within 30 days of commencing business registration, submit the 'Business Tax Registration' form (Form 1) to the Income Tax Office, attaching Cayman certificate of incorporation and certified translations, to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN). (4) Register with HM Customs (Gibraltar) as a customs trader for import of controlled frozen-meat/fish consignments and with the Gibraltar Social Security authority if employing staff. (5) Obtain the statutory Import Licence for food of animal origin from the Environmental Health Department (Gibraltar Health Authority) prior to first importation. Estimated timeline: 5–10 working days for standard registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Tax_Exempt_Zone

Available election forms

The Cayman Exempted Company benefits from zero corporate income tax in the Cayman Islands and, upon application under the Cayman Tax Concessions Act (2019 Revision), may obtain a Tax Exemption Certificate guaranteeing no Cayman tax on profits for up to 20 years (renewable). For Gibraltar direct tax, the operator may elect to be treated as a 'non-resident company' where central management and control is exercised outside Gibraltar, leaving only Gibraltar-source branch profits within the Gibraltar corporation tax net (standard rate 12.5%). Conversely, where full management and control is in Gibraltar, the company becomes Gibraltar-tax-resident and is taxable on worldwide profits. Gibraltar no longer grants 'exempt company' status (abolished 2010); no check-the-box election equivalent exists under UK or Gibraltar law. Groups must document transfer pricing and review UK hybrid mismatch rules (TIOPA 2010, Part 6A) and Gibraltar's general anti-avoidance 'hallmark' provisions if the Cayman entity provides intragroup funding to the Gibraltar branch.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / No GST / No Sales and Use Tax in Gibraltar; indirect taxation comprises Gibraltar customs and excise duties on physical importation of goods.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A – Gibraltar has no VAT/GST, so no monetary registration threshold exists; customs trader registration and a statutory import licence for frozen beef/seafood are mandatory from the first commercial import (GIP 0 threshold). Note: for onward supply into the UK, HMRC VAT registration is required in the UK once taxable turnover exceeds GBP 90,000; for supply into Spain/EU, Spanish import VAT applies at the border (e.g., 21%/10%/4% depending on product) with no distance-selling threshold for imports dispatched from a third territory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating does not apply because Gibraltar is not a VAT jurisdiction. Export relief is instead available as a customs duty drawback: under the Gibraltar Customs Duties Ordinance, import duty paid on frozen beef/seafood may be refunded (drawback) where the goods are exported within a prescribed period (typically 12 months), remain identifiable, are exported in original or processed state, and the exporter proves export through a Gibbs of Customs certificate of shipment. Alternatively, goods placed in temporary storage or a transit/transshipment regime at Gibraltar wharves are suspended from import duty. Sales into the UK may be zero-rated for UK VAT only where the goods physically leave the UK and are exported under HMRC export procedures (Customs (Export) (EU Exit) Regulations), with evidence of export retained within 3 months.

  • Gibraltar Customs Import Entry / Single Administrative Document (SAD - GENDEC)
  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD export copy) showing exit from Gibraltar/UK
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing export movement
  • Commercial Invoice (conforming to Incoterms 2020 and tariff description)
  • Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (for preferential duty claims)
  • Veterinary Health Certificate and export-country food safety certification (frozen beef)
  • Fishery Certificate / IUU catch documentation (pelagic fish)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 (or revolving LC draw certificate) proving receipt of foreign sales proceeds

Gibraltar does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping regime for B2B/B2C invoices. Customs entries, however, must be lodged electronically with HM Customs (Gibraltar) through its Customs Electronic Clearance System (EDI). For frozen beef and pelagic fish of animal origin, digital veterinary health certificates and, where applicable, EU TRACES documentation accompany the customs entry; shipments transiting through the EU/UK are subject to the Common Transit Convention safeguards. No mandatory national e-invoicing platform (such as Spain's SII or Italy's SDI) applies in Gibraltar.

Gibraltar's customs and tax authorities apply retrospective clawback and penalties for fraudulent duty relief claims: under the Gibraltar Customs Duties Ordinance, misdeclaration or forged certificates trigger forfeiture of goods, penalties up to three times the relevant duty, and criminal liability (fine/imprisonment) for smuggling. Under the Income Tax Ordinance, fraudulent or negligent tax filings enable the Commissioner to issue assessments outside normal time limits with penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded plus interest (statutory interest commonly 8% p.a.); false statements are indictable. For UK VAT zero-rating, HMRC may require repayment under Regulation 23A of the VAT Regulations 1995 if export is not proven within 3 months, with penalties up to 30% for careless/inaccurate submissions (Finance Act 2007, Sch. 24) and up to 100% for deliberate concealment or fraud. The Gibraltar anti-avoidance 'hallmark' rules may recharacterise artificial structures lacking commercial substance (e.g., routing cold-chain profits through the Cayman entity without local substance), subjecting the reallocated profit to 12.5% Gibraltar tax with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both current and capital account convertibility are unrestricted. GIP is pegged 1:1 to GBP and no exchange-control legislation is in force, so profit remittance, equity injections, loan repayments, and trade settlements flow freely with no central bank prior approval. The only frictions are FATCA/CRS reporting, AML/KYC screening, and submission of tax status certificates for the Cayman Exempted Company.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion rules apply. Exporters and foreign-currency account holders may retain 100% of proceeds in any denomination for any period. Reporting obligations are limited to the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and GFSC AML/CFT Rules, requiring cash transaction reports above EUR 10,000 and enhanced scrutiny of payments touching FATF grey-listed jurisdictions. The covered entity is an importer, but the same free-remittance standard governs any incidental receivables from EU/UK distribution.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no independent central bank. The Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) at par with GBP; the Bank of England acts as the residual monetary anchor for the Sterling currency union, and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory banking supervisor and licensing authority under the Financial Services Act 2019.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. UK FCA-regulated EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currencycloud, Wise Business) and EU EMIs passporting into Gibraltar generally decline accounts for a Cayman Exempted Company that lacks substantive UK/EEA economic nexus. GFSC-licensed e-money firms are similarly constrained by FATF Recommendation 25 on transparency and beneficial ownership. Viable axis: use a regulated Gibraltar bank as the primary treasury hub and EMIs strictly as disbursement sub-accounts, introduced by a GFSC-licensed fiduciary.
