The ELP must maintain a physical registered office in Gibraltar under the Limited Partnerships Act 2019 and must additionally demonstrate a verifiable operational nexus for customs purposes, being either its own licensed bonded-warehouse premises in Gibraltar or a formal contractual arrangement with a Gibraltar-licensed warehousekeeper and excise supervision contract. Virtual-office arrangements will not satisfy H.M. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

A foreign-incorporated Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) must obtain a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) by filing the partnership’s registration particulars with the Gibraltar Companies House, followed by an Income Tax Office application using the ERO (External Relations Office) correspondence protocol; the ELP is treated as tax-transparent and non-resident-derived income is outside the scope of Gibraltar income tax, but a TIN remains mandatory for customs, excise, and financial account reporting obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box election. The ELP is inherently transparent; no entity-level taxation applies unless the partnership carries on business in Gibraltar and derives local-source income. No hybrid mismatch election is statutorily available; any tax-status election must be evidenced via the partnership agreement and filed notices to the Commissioner of Income Tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Excise Duty and Customs Duty regime (no VAT/GST)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — excise and bonded warehouse registration is mandatory from the first import, intra-Community transfer, or commercial removal of wine/spirits; no monetary turnover threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar imposes no VAT, therefore the concept of zero-rated export VAT does not apply. Instead, excise duty is fully suspended and relieved on wine/spirits exported from a bonded warehouse directly out of Gibraltar customs territory or supplied to another customs-approved warehouse, provided the movement is covered by a valid electronic accompanying document and proof of arrival is returned within the required timeline.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document SAD)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or corresponding bank payment/credit advice
  • Bonded Warehouse Removal and Inventory Control Log
  • Excise Movement and Control System (EMCS) movement document where applicable

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping for commercial invoices. Bonded warehouse operations must instead comply with an electronic inventory and movement reporting system to HM Customs of Gibraltar, including API-compatible submission of warehouse receipts, removal records, and duty suspension entry/exit declarations.

Fraudulent export zero-rating or duty suspension claims attract immediate clawback of the suspended excise duty plus interest and fixed penalties up to 100% of the duty evaded. HM Customs may impose monetary penalty notices, revoke or suspend the warehousekeeper licence and the ELP’s bonded trading approval, and refer cases for criminal prosecution; personal liability may extend to partners under Gibraltar’s customs and excise enforcement provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar is an offshore special territory operating a sterling currency-board system under which GIP is pegged at 1:1 to the British pound sterling (GBP). There is no statutory exchange control regime in force: capital account and current account transactions are fully convertible, and foreign‑currency accounts in GBP, EUR, and USD are freely permissible. Profit remittance, capital repatriation, and upstream distributions from the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) to its Cayman parent (KY_ELP) and to ultimate UK‑resident partners are not subject to central-bank pre-approval or convertibility licensing. The only operative frictions are GFSC AML/CTF sub-sector guidance, OFSI sanctions screening, the FATF-aligned Gibraltar beneficial-ownership register, and bank-level policy requiring substantiation of a genuine bonded-warehouse commercial purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar law imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no central-bank ex-ante verification for export receipts generated from bonded wine and spirits wholesale trade. The ELP may lawfully retain 100% of export proceeds in the original invoiced currency (typically GBP, EUR, or USD). Bank-level credit policy, however, requires documentary evidence of the underlying Customs-bonded warehouse withdrawal within 180 days of receipt, and any retention beyond that period triggers an automatic enhanced due-diligence review by the relationship manager.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the prudential banking supervisor; HM Government of Gibraltar (Financial Secretary) as the Gibraltar pound (GIP) currency-issuing authority under the Currency Notes Act 1934; UK HM Treasury and the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) as the operative authority for the UK consolidated financial sanctions and exchange-control perimeter applicable to the British Overseas Territory of Gibraltar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rated 'Medium-Constrained'. Gibraltar-regulated e-money institutions and EEA/UK EMIs enjoying the Temporary Permissions Regime may be used for auxiliary payment flows, but the wine-and-spirits bonded trade is treated as a Medium-Risk-Enhanced sector, and most EMI rails impose a GBP 250,000 monthly aggregate throughput limit. EMIs cannot issue documentary letters of credit and are, therefore, viable only for supplier prepayments below EUR 25,000 per wire and for duty/excise settlement to HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC). The primary clearing of principal trade flows must occur through a Tier-1 licensed commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed from the Cayman parent (KY_ELP) via a UK-domiciled high-grade treasury corridor (Barclays UK plc, London) to the Gibraltar ELP's Capital Escrow Account at Santander Gibraltar using a SWIFT MT103 message carrying ISO 20022 purpose code EQUI (equity capital) or TOLL (costs/tolls) as determinable by the receiving bank, with explicit remittance narrative 'ELP capital contribution for bonded wine and spirits wholesale under ISIC 4632'. Mandatory verification steps: (i) pre-transaction GFSC AML clearance and OFSI sanctions screening; (ii) confirmation that the Gibraltar ELP has zero minimum capital requirement under the Limited Partnerships Act 2011, hence no compulsory capital threshold or stamp duty is triggered; (iii) documentary alignment of the wire amount with the executed capital-contribution deed; (iv) post-settlement filing of the GFSC quarterly return if the account balance exceeds EUR 100,000; and (v) retention of SWIFT gpi tracker evidence for audit trail completeness.Outbound settlements for bonded trade are executed through dual clearing tracks. (A) Corporate-to-bank SWIFT MT103/COVE wires for uncomplicated supplier installments to Bordeaux maisons (EUR BIC) and Scottish distilleries (GBP BIC), using ISO 20022 purpose code GOOD (trade in goods) or TRDP (trade payment) and relying on CHAPS for GBP-leg settlement and TARGET2 for EUR-leg settlement. (B) Irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit governed by ICC UCP600/eUCP, advised through a UK confirming bank, for bulk matured-stock transfers; payment is released only against presentation of the certificate of analysis, bonded-warehouse warrants, insurance cover notes, and the warehouse-exit certificate evidencing duty-suspension transfer. For intra-Gibraltar bonded transfers, the 'Bonded_Transfer' rail uses the Gibraltar Customs Bill of Discharge as the documentary anchor, with the settlement value date fixed at the point of warrant endorsement.Deliverable Currency Forwards over GBP/EUR and GBP/USD, bank-executed under an ISDA 2002 master agreement, capped at 12-month tenor and strictly limited to forecast bonded-trade receivables and payables with an underlying economic exposure. These instruments require no GFSC licence for the ELP when transacted through a Gibraltar-regulated bank for hedging-only purposes.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) for the residual uncovered receivable exposure of up to 20% of the notional trade portfolio, accessed via the Santander Gibraltar branch's London interbank corridor, permissible only where a documented bona-fide commercial invoice supports the position and where the forward matures before the contracted warehouse exit date.Cross-Currency Swaps (GBP/EUR and GBP/USD) entered into solely to align the Cayman parent's capital-call and liquidation obligations against expected Scotch and Bordeaux sale proceeds; each swap must be mapped to a specific commercial contract ledger line and may not exceed the net invoice value of the corresponding goods.FX Spot contracts with immediate settlement through the ELP's multi-currency trade settlement account, limited by the bank's daily liquidity trap ceiling (customarily GBP 5,000,000 equivalent per trading day) for wine-and-spirits clearable exposures; spot trades are documented using the bank's standard Treasury Confirmation Terms and reported quarter-end to the GFSC as part of the ELP's financial returns.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

10% (Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, as amended); the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) is fiscally transparent, so no entity-level corporation tax is charged on partnership profits; each partner is assessed on their allocated share (individuals: progressive rates up to 29%; corporate partners: 10%).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a Gibraltar company; for the ELP, distributions to partners are not subject to Gibraltar withholding tax where they are made from the partners' already-assessed share of profits or from capital.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Year of Assessment (YA) runs 1 July to 30 June. The partnership return and the partners' personal/company returns must be filed by 30 November following the end of the YA in which the ELP's accounting period ends (e.g., for a period ending 31 December 2025, the return is due 30 November 2026); tax is payable on each YA assessment by the filing date or as set out in instalment directions for large partnerships.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation is required where aggregate controlled transactions (intercompany purchases, bonded storage fees, cross-border sales to affiliates) exceed GBP 1,000,000 per annum; a Master File is required where the MNE group's consolidated revenue reaches EUR 750,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750,000,000, with the notification filed through the Gibraltar ITO.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Income Tax (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019 apply in full to the ELP. The bonded wholesale trade of wine and spirits (distribution and service centre activity) is a 'relevant activity' requiring: (i) direction and management at the Gibraltar level; (ii) a sufficient number of full-time employees in Gibraltar measured against the income generated; (iii) adequate physical premises and equipment in Gibraltar; and (iv) an appropriate level of core income-generating expenditure. The ELP must annually file an economic substance return and information notification; the Commissioner exchanges information on non-compliant entities with the UK HMRC under the Exchange of Information framework, and the ELP cannot rely on the substance of any foreign affiliated group entity to satisfy the Gibraltar test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Cheating the Public Revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal tax evasion under the Income Tax Act 2010 (Gibraltar): on conviction on indictment, an unlimited fine plus restitution of evaded tax and interest at 8% per annum; on summary conviction, a fine of up to GBP 5,000 per offence; civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax undercharged; seizure of the ELP's inventories, bank accounts, and bonded goods.

Director personal liability: The general partner (and any limited partner who participates in management) is jointly and severally liable for the partnership's tax debts and penalties; the court may issue a restraint or freezing order (Mareva injunction), impose a travel ban or order surrender of the passport pending trial, and impose a custodial sentence of up to 7 years; deliberate concealment also triggers disqualification from holding a bonded trade licence.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance (Amendment) Regulations 2019: financial penalty of up to GBP 100,000 per year of non-compliance; removal of the exemption benefit and cancellation of the ELP registration; automatic exchange of information with the UK HMRC enabling the UK to re-characterise the place of effective management and re-tax the entire non-distributed profit at UK corporation tax rates; additional penalties for filing a false economic substance return.

Director personal liability: The general partner and the beneficial owner are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalty amounts; the Supreme Court of Gibraltar may order civil restitution, grant freezing orders over foreign assets, and impose travel bans; individuals providing false economic substance declarations may be prosecuted for perjury, carrying a term of imprisonment of up to 5 years.
AML / MLRO Failure in Bonded Trade Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar): money laundering (including facilitating the laundering of profits of another person) is punishable on indictment by imprisonment of up to 14 years and/or an unlimited fine; failure to disclose suspicious activity and prejudicing the MLRO and GFSC investigation carry separate custodial penalties of up to 5 years; the GFSC may additionally impose an administrative penalty of up to GBP 2,000,000 or 5% of annual global turnover and revoke the entity's permission or licence.

Director personal liability: Every person designated as a nominated officer, general partner, or compliance officer is individually criminally liable; the GFSC may ban the individual from exercising any management function in Gibraltar for up to 10 years and the court may order confiscation of the entire proceeds derived in the 24-month period preceding the violation; travel bans and extradition to another jurisdiction can be triggered through the international mutual legal assistance framework.
Customs and Excise Fraud in Bonded Warehousing

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar customs and excise legislation: forfeiture and seizure of the spirits, the ELP's material stock, and any transport used in the fraudulent removal; the evader is liable to a penalty of treble the evaded duty or the full value of the goods, whichever is greater; the bonded trade licence of the ELP is revoked, ending its capacity to receive excise goods; criminal prosecution may add a fine of up to GBP 20,000 per offence and imprisonment of up to 7 years on indictment.

Director personal liability: The general partner holding the bonded warehouse licence is directly liable for the duty debt as principal; civil recovery proceedings may pierce the limited partnership shield and attach the personal assets, bank accounts, and directorships of the individuals concerned; the court may issue a restriction order preventing re-entry into the excise trade, a travel ban pending extradition, and a custodial sentence of up to 7 years for fraudulent evasion of excise duty.
Fraudulent Misreporting of Pass-Through Status

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2010 (Gibraltar): disregard of the allocation; the ELP may be deemed a taxable person and assessed at 10% on all profits; penalties for failure to register of up to GBP 3,000; interest on underpaid tax at 8% per annum; the Gibraltar tax authorities may additionally apply general anti-avoidance provisions to cancel any tax advantage obtained through the artificial structure, with a look-back period of up to 6 years.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who sign incorrect partnership returns expose the corporate general partner, themselves, and the underlying shareholders to civil restitution claims by the Commissioner; the court may impose a travel ban or disqualification order preventing the director from managing any Gibraltar company for a period of not less than 5 years and, in deliberate fraud cases, a custodial sentence of up to 7 years.
