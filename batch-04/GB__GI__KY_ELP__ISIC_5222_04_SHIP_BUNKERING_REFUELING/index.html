Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, HM Government of Gibraltar — Income Tax Office, 8 Secretary's Lane, Gibraltar GX11 1AA; operational portal: https://www.incometax.gov.gi (e-services for corporate/partnership returns, employer returns, PAYE, and status certificates). Customs and indirect tax administration is separately vested in HM Customs Gibraltar (https://www.customs.gi), operating under the Customs Duties Act 2015.

E-government filing portal

An Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) is first registered with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Limited Partnerships Act 2019, obtaining a GI Company/Partnership Number. The ELP must then register with the Income Tax Office by filing the prescribed Registration of Business/Corporation Form and the particulars of partners within 30 days of formation; the Income Tax Office issues a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number and PAYE/Employer Number (if applicable). Non-resident foreign partners are not required to appear physically; the ELP's registered general partner (or its appointed authorised tax representative — a Gibraltar-resident individual or regulated firm) procures the TIN and accepts service of process. A foreign entity converting to or migrating into an ELP must file a certified copy of the certificate of registration, constitutional instruments, and a declaration of the registered address in Gibraltar. If the ELP will engage in marine fuel supply, it must hold a bunkering licence from the Gibraltar Port Authority (an operating licence prerequisite for the tax file) and register with HM Customs for a Customs Unique Reference Number if importing dutiable fuel. Estimated processing: 2–5 business days for the tax reference number, subject to AML/KYC clearance by the licensed general partner.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Under the Income Tax Act 2010, an ELP is fiscally transparent: Gibraltar-source profits of the ELP are attributed directly to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement, and no ELP-level income tax is levied unless the ELP elects Qualifying Company treatment. Non-resident partners are chargeable to Gibraltar tax only on income/profits derived from trade carried on in Gibraltar; offshore bunkering activities (negotiation, invoicing, and delivery to international vessels within port limits) are treated as Gibraltar-source trading income but enjoy the Qualifying Company concession described below. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under the Income Tax Act 2010, an ELP is fiscally transparent: Gibraltar-source profits of the ELP are attributed directly to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement, and no ELP-level income tax is levied unless the ELP elects Qualifying Company treatment. Non-resident partners are chargeable to Gibraltar tax only on income/profits derived from trade carried on in Gibraltar; offshore bunkering activities (negotiation, invoicing, and delivery to international vessels within port limits) are treated as Gibraltar-source trading income but enjoy the Qualifying Company concession described below. The ELP is outside the scope of UK corporation tax (Gibraltar is a self-governing tax territory); no UK TIN is required except where a UK permanent establishment is created.

Available election forms

(1) Qualifying Company (QC) election — Income Tax (Qualifying Companies) Regulations 2019: an ELP may irrevocably elect to be treated as a Qualifying Company, paying a flat-rate charge of GBP 350 per annum (or the prevailing statutory amount) on qualifying income (broadly, income from international trade not derived from a trade carried on within Gibraltar), conditional upon no commercial activity in Gibraltar, no Gibraltar-resident employees or directors beyond statutory maxima, and no property/asset in Gibraltar. (2) Pass-through default election / partner attribution — the ELP may file annual partnership returns electing that profits be taxed wholly at partner level (applicable to Gibraltar-resident or Gibraltar-sourced partners at the standard 12.5% corporate/individual rate). (3) Hybrid mismatch rules — Part V Income Tax Act 2010 (Tax Treatment of Financing Costs and Arrangements) requires notification of intra-group hybrid arrangements and disallows interest deductions where a counterparty jurisdiction treats a payment inconsistently; an ELP with hybrid elements (e.g., treated as a company in the ultimate parent jurisdiction) must file Form HM-1 with the Commissioner. (4) Group relief/consortium elections — available under Schedule 3 Income Tax Act 2010. No 'check-the-box' classification regime equivalent to the US; classification is statutory by entity type and election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar has NO VAT, NO GST, and NO general sales/use tax. The applicable indirect regime is the Customs Duties Act 2015 (customs/excise duty on importation and local excise of fuel, alcohol, tobacco, and vehicles) together with Purchase Tax (a narrow single-stage tax at 15% on specified luxury goods, principally motor vehicles and yuletide goods), and the Duties on the Supply of Fuel (Bunkers) collection framework administered by HM Customs Gibraltar. Supplies of marine fuel oil/lubricants to ships for international voyages are 'international trade supplies' and are outside the scope of these duties (free of customs duty and excise).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. For customs purposes, every ELP importing, storing, or supplying marine fuel within Gibraltar must register with HM Customs (Customs Unique Reference Number) and obtain a Bunkering Operator Licence; there is no de minimis turnover threshold. Purchase Tax registration is required only if the ELP locally supplies vehicles/listed luxury goods. A GBP 350 annual QC flat tax applies irrespective of turnover; no indirect tax turnover threshold is prescribed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar does not apply VAT; consequently, the '0% export VAT rate' is structurally inapplicable. Instead, marine bunker fuel and lubricants supplied to ships engaged in international trade are treated as international supplies: no customs duty, no excise duty, and no purchase tax is chargeable, provided the operator holds a valid Bunkering Licence, the supply is delivered to a vessel undertaking an international voyage, the fuel is entered in the vessel's log as ship's stores, and the quantity does not exceed the vessel's genuine operational requirements. Supplies to fishing vessels, coastal/domestic trade, or pleasure craft operating solely in Gibraltar waters may attract excise duty at prevailing rates. The QC flat-rate regime achieves an effective zero rate on otherwise taxable profit. Duty paid on imported fuel subsequently exported or supplied as international bunkers is eligible for a customs duty drawback claim under s.35 Customs Duties Act 2015.

  • Gibraltar Customs export/out-of-charge declaration (electronic C88 / Single Administrative Document) for any fuel removed from customs territory
  • Bill of Lading or, for bunkering, a Port of Gibraltar Bunker Delivery Receipt (BDR) endorsed by the vessel master and agent
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT confirmation evidencing receipt of proceeds from the non-resident shipping counter-party
  • Customs fuel import entry and duty payment receipt (where duty-paid stock is subsequently drawback-claimed)
  • Bunkering licence and Gibraltar Port Authority operating permit
  • Vessel stores declaration and ship's log extract confirming international voyage status
  • Charter party or voyage order confirming the delivery of fuel to a foreign-going vessel

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or government API e-invoicing system operates in Gibraltar for VAT-type invoices; the ELP may issue non-certified commercial invoices under the Customs Duties Act. Mandatory electronic customs clearance applies to marine fuel imports, movements, and exports through HM Customs Gibraltar's electronic system (the Gibraltar Customs Electronic Data Interchange and the UK CDS integration for goods moving to/from the EU); bunker delivery notes (BDNs) must be lodged via the Gibraltar Port Authority's electronic bunkering reporting interface and reconciled with the annual Fuel Monitoring Report. Invoices must display: supplier name/TIN, purchaser name and IMO/vessel identifier, invoice date, product specification (ISO 8217 grade), quantity in metric tonnes, bunker delivery note reference, port location, and a statement that the supply is 'zero-rated international-trade fuel'. e-Invoicing in Peppol/BIS format is accepted but not obligatory.

HM Customs Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office conduct joint audits on bunkering operators. Where international-trade zero-ratement (duty exemption) is claimed fraudulently, or where fuel is diverted to local fishing/leisure/domestic use, the following apply: (i) retrospective withdrawal of duty exemption and assessment of the full excise/customs duty payable (rate as at date of supply); (ii) a penalty of up to GBP 3,000 per misdeclaration under ss.52–53 Customs Duties Act 2015, plus forfeiture of goods/means of transport; (iii) where intent to evade is established, criminal prosecution with fines of up to triple the duty evaded and imprisonment up to 7 years under s.68 Customs Duties Act 2015; (iv) for income-tax clawback, the QC flat-rate concession is retrospectively withdrawn for periods of non-qualifying activity, exposing profits to a 12.5% assessment plus interest at a statutory rate and a tax-geared penalty up to 100% for deliberate inaccuracy under the Tax Administration Act 2005; (v) AML/AML-for-tax aggravators under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 permit asset forfeiture and debarment from the bunkering licence. Compliance with the 180-day repatriation documentation condition is a prerequisite for retaining the duty-free status; late or non-repatriation compels a rebuttable presumption that the transaction was domestic, triggering full duty and penalty.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains no exchange control legislation and no capital account restrictions. The Gibraltar pound is pegged at par to GBP, and both current and capital account transactions are freely convertible. Profit remittance from an Exempted Limited Partnership to non-resident partners is unrestricted, although bank-level AML and tax transparency checks apply to outward distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rules and no mandatory conversion ratios. Export receipts, including bunkering trade proceeds, may be retained in any permitted currency in a Gibraltar account; however, banks may require underlying trade documentation for large foreign-currency credits.

Central bank supervision: HM Government of Gibraltar (Currency Notes Act) and Financial Services Commission; no separate Gibraltar central bank exists. Bank of England does not exercise supervisory jurisdiction over Gibraltar regulated firms.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rated Medium. Gibraltar-licensed electronic money institutions may on-board an ELP under the Financial Services Act, but most will decline or restrict pure marine bunkering because the sector is treated as medium-risk enhanced trade. Offshore digital EMIs without a Gibraltar licence cannot provide an effective domestic clearing route and are likely to be refused by correspondent banks for high-value oil-product payments. A regulated Gibraltar bank relationship remains the primary clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 for partner capital contributions, with remittance information including the ELP short reference, narrative 'Capital Contribution - Exempted Limited Partnership', and a clear purpose string. The beneficiary bank in Gibraltar will match the wire to a pre-opened capital escrow account. No exchange control approval is required, but CRS/FATCA tax residency declarations and ultimate beneficial ownership data must be captured before credit is released.Primary clearance is via SWIFT MT103 for bunker fuel and lubricant invoices, supported by underlying trade documents maintained in the bank's compliance file. Commercial Letters of Credit issued under UCP 600 and Standby Letters of Credit are used for larger supply contracts and performance security. The clearing rail is invalid if the counterparty bank is outside the correspondent network or the underlying port is subject to sanctions or enhanced due diligence.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 (covers GBP 1,000,000) of aggregate related-party transactions triggers mandatory Local File preparation; group Master File is required where worldwide consolidated revenue exceeds GIP 50,000,000; if the ELP is merely exempt, documentation is still required to demonstrate arm's length conduct.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations apply: marine bunkering and replenishment characterised as 'Distribution and Service Centre Business' requires (a) locally managed board meetings, (b) qualified resident employees, (c) physical premises in Gibraltar, and (d) core income-generating expenditure incurred in Gibraltar; failure to satisfy the substance test results in a GIP 20,000 penalty per accounting period plus potential striking-off and information exchange on shell-company indicators.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Unlimited fine and imprisonment of up to 7 years for tax evasion; civil penalty up to 100% of underpaid tax; daily compound interest on the deficiency.

Director personal liability: General partner and directors jointly and severally liable for company tax debts; asset freezing orders, travel bans and disqualification orders available to the Commissioner; fraudulent trading claims expose personal civil restitution.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty of GIP 20,000 per accounting period for non-compliance; penalty of GIP 100,000 for false/forged substance evidence; directed to rectify within 30 days; repeated failure leads to striking-off from the register.

Director personal liability: Directors disqualified for up to 5 years; personal responsibility for the ELP's tax arrears under wrongful trading provisions; international information exchange exposes partners to prosecution in their residence country.
AML / Financial Sanctions Breach (Medium-Risk Enhanced)

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act and the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act; unlimited fines, asset confiscation orders, revocation of the marine fuel supply licence under ISIC 5222.

Director personal liability: Directors personally fined and imprisoned for up to 14 years where the nominated officer fails to report a suspicious transaction; travel bans and extradition treaties apply.
Abusive Profit Stripping / Withholding Mismatch

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under the Gibraltar Income Tax (Transfer Pricing) Rules; denial of exempt status with full 12.5% tax recapture; penalties up to 100% of the adjusted tax and interest.

Director personal liability: General partners liable for restitution to affected tax authorities; criminal sanctions for cheating the public revenue; cross-border enforcement through tax information exchange and mutual assistance treaties.
