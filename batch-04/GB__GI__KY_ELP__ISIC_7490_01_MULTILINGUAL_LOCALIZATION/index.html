Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office (ITO), H.M. Government of Gibraltar, 3 Bayside Road, Gibraltar GX11 1AA. Official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation; secure e-filing via the ITO portal (https://www.ito.gov.gi). Customs and import-duty competence: HM Customs Gibraltar, Waterport House, Gibraltar.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Register the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) with the Registrar of Companies under the Limited Partnerships Act 1991 and obtain the statutory LP registration number. Step 2 - Submit the IT-ELP partnership tax registration form and the exempt-status election to the Income Tax Office to obtain the partnership Gibraltar TIN; resident general partners obtain individual Gibraltar TINs, while non-resident partners are identified on the partnership return by name and foreign TIN. Step 3 - Register as an employer for PAYE with the ITO upon hiring the first employee. Step 4 - Register with HM Customs as an operator only if physical goods or master media are imported or exported; VAT registration is structurally unavailable because Gibraltar levies no VAT. Step 5 - File an annual economic substance nil or positive return through the ITO portal where the ELP carries on a relevant activity under the Commercial (Economic Substance) (Amendment) Regulations 2019.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The Gibraltar ELP is tax-transparent by default under the Income Tax Act 2010: no entity-level tax is charged on partnership profits; each partner is assessed personally on their allocated profit share. Non-resident partners benefit from territorial protection, so localization, translation and dubbing income derived from non-Gibraltar clients falls outside the charge to Gibraltar income tax where services are performed and income is sourced abroad.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Available statutory devices: (i) the exempt-status election filed with the Registrar of Companies declaring all partners non-resident and barring the ELP from carrying on a taxable activity in Gibraltar; (ii) the joint partnership-return election under section 105 of the Income Tax Act 2010; (iii) a statement of tax-transparency may be filed with the ITO for OECD/BEPS purposes; (iv) hybrid-mismatch outcomes are managed through factual pass-through substantiation under the Income Tax (International Tax Compliance) Regulations 2018; no affirmative hybrid-mismatch matching election exists under Gibraltar law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Nil-VAT regime: Gibraltar levies no VAT, GST or general sales tax. The only indirect tax is Import Duty under the Customs Ordinance 2015, chargeable on physical imports of goods into Gibraltar. Cross-border services - including multilingual translation, localization and media dubbing supplied by the ELP - are outside the scope of any Gibraltar indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable: no VAT registration threshold exists because no VAT regime operates. Mandatory fiscal registrations are: (a) HM Customs operator registration from the first physical import or export movement (no trade threshold); (b) ITO employer or PAYE registration from the first payroll wage paid in Gibraltar; and (c) economic substance return filing where relevant income is earned, with a nil return mandatory from the first accounting period of a trading ELP.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is structurally inapplicable because Gibraltar has no VAT; the equivalent relief is full exclusion of exported services from indirect tax and, under territorial rules, from Gibraltar income tax. The ELP substantiates exclusion by evidencing: (i) supply to a non-resident client; (ii) performance of translation, localization or dubbing without a Gibraltar permanent establishment of the taxable partner; (iii) foreign-currency invoicing with settlement through Wise B2B, Payoneer or bank transfer; and (iv) repatriation of consideration within the 180-day administrative benchmark. Gibraltar imposes no statutory FX-repatriation deadline because there are no exchange controls, but ITO audit practice applies the 180-day inward-remittance norm as the substantiation standard.

  • HM Customs Gibraltar export declaration (nil-duty SAD or CD1) for physical master-media or ancillary goods movements
  • Commercial invoice and signed localization services agreement evidencing the non-resident client and foreign place of supply
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (physical shipments) or digital delivery-acceptance log (for electronic translation and dubbing deliverables)
  • Inward remittance credit confirmation from payment rails (Wise B2B or Payoneer) or the collecting bank evidencing receipts from abroad

No real-time fiscal clearance, API integration or digital stamping mandate exists in Gibraltar. The Income Tax (Records) Rules require the ELP to keep complete English-language invoices, receipts and contracts for six years and to issue invoices within 30 days of supply. Voluntary PEPPOL or BIS Billing 3.0 e-invoicing may be used for UK and EU B2B clients, but the ITO imposes no electronic clearance obligation.

Abusive reliance on exempt or offshore status triggers: (i) retrospective clawback of exempt status with reassessment of undeclared Gibraltar-source profits for up to 20 years in fraud cases and 6 years for negligence under the Income Tax Act 2010; (ii) tax-geared penalties of up to 100% of the undercharged tax plus statutory interest; (iii) criminal prosecution for false declarations with fines and imprisonment of up to 7 years; (iv) under the Customs Ordinance, false export declarations attract forfeiture of goods, penalties of up to three times the duty evaded and suspension of customs operator status; and (v) failure to file the economic substance return incurs fixed penalties of up to GBP 1,200, daily default penalties and potential strike-off of the ELP.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains a fully liberalised currency regime with no exchange control legislation. Current account items (cross-border translation/dubbing fees and intercompany royalties) and capital account items (equity contributions, partners' capital withdrawals, loan repayments, distribution of profits) are freely convertible and transferable without GFSC or HM Government of Gibraltar consent. Profit remittance to non-resident partners faces no statutory hurdles; the only practical gateways are bank-level AML/KYC, CRS/FATCA self-certification, the Economic Substance Act 2020, and the requirement that the ELP maintain its register of beneficial ownership under the Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Regulations. Settlement is executed in GBP, EUR, or USD via the commercial banks' UK/EU correspondent networks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar applies a zero-surrender, zero-conversion regime for export proceeds. There is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for receipts from professional services, translation, or media dubbing exports; all foreign currency receipts may be retained in GIP, GBP, EUR, or other currencies, netted against payables, or converted at market rates without approval.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) and the Government of Gibraltar's Financial Secretary under the Currency Notes Act 1934; there is no separate central bank. The Gibraltar Pound (GIP) circulates at a 1:1 peg to the Bank of England pound sterling, which is the monetary anchor and the effective settlement currency.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High viability for operational flows, subject to distinct constraints. Wise B2B (Wise Europe/UK Ltd, FCA regulated) and Payoneer (Payoneer Europe Ltd, Belgian NBB e-money licence) will onboard a Gibraltar ELP with a verified registered office, BO register filing, and banking rationale; the ISIC 7490 localization business is Low_Risk_Standard and requires no statutory licence. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not banks: no FSCS deposit protection, funds held under safeguarded client-money arrangements, and not eligible for capital escrow or documentary credit; (ii) the EMI may reject or restrict the account if partners' residency jurisdictions are in their higher-risk policy, necessitating a master operational bank in Gibraltar; (iii) EMI rails must be reserved for low-value trade disbursements and client collections, not for partner distributions or capital aggregation; (iv) volume and velocity limits apply and warrant a documented payment-routing policy.
