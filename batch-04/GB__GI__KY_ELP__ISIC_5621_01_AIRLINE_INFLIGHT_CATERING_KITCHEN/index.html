Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar; official guidance and digital services portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation. Customs administration is separately handled by HM Customs Gibraltar (Border and Coastguard Agency).

E-government filing portal

A foreign Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) must appoint a Gibraltar-resident tax representative, file a tax registration application with the Income Tax Office, and attach the ELP registration certificate under the Registration of Limited Partnerships Act, the constitutive deed, beneficial ownership declarations, and identification details of all general and limited partners. The Income Tax Office issues the ELP's Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN). No minimum capital is required, and non-resident ownership is permitted without exchange control consent.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) must appoint a Gibraltar-resident tax representative, file a tax registration application with the Income Tax Office, and attach the ELP registration certificate under the Registration of Limited Partnerships Act, the constitutive deed, beneficial ownership declarations, and identification details of all general and limited partners. The Income Tax Office issues the ELP's Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN). No minimum capital is required, and non-resident ownership is permitted without exchange control consent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box or UK corporate classification election. The ELP is fiscally transparent; the partnership deed allocates taxable profit among partners, and each partner is assessed on Gibraltar-source income under the territorial tax system. Partners may elect the remittance basis or make other elections available under the Income Tax Act 2010 by formal notice to the Commissioner of Income Tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST in Gibraltar. Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and has no separate VAT/GST legislation. Indirect taxation is limited to customs import duty, excise duty, and certain consumption-based levies administered by HM Customs Gibraltar. UK VAT does not apply to supplies made in Gibraltar, although UK VAT may apply where the place of supply of goods or services is the United Kingdom.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — there is no VAT/GST registration threshold in Gibraltar because no such tax is imposed. If the ELP is separately registered for UK VAT, the UK compulsory registration threshold is £90,000 of UK taxable turnover in a rolling 12-month period, and supplies originating in Gibraltar are outside the UK VAT charge unless the relevant place of supply is the UK.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar does not impose VAT, so no Gibraltar VAT zero-rating claim is available. For the ELP's airline inflight catering operation, supplies loaded in Gibraltar for international flights are not standard-rated Gibraltar supplies. If the ELP is also registered for UK VAT, zero-rating may be claimed under VATA 1994 only where a separate UK taxable supply has been made, such as exported goods or qualifying stores for a foreign-going aircraft; otherwise services supplied to a non-UK airline are outside the scope of UK VAT.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD form or equivalent)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill evidencing removal from Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank FX settlement advice showing foreign-currency payment
  • Commercial invoice and scale-load manifest with flight number
  • Evidence of ownership transfer and supply contract with the airline
  • Health and sanitation certificates for inflight meal products

No mandatory e-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime applies in Gibraltar. For customs purposes, export and import declarations are filed electronically through HM Customs Gibraltar's customs declaration system, including the use of the Single Administrative Document where applicable.

No Gibraltar VAT clawback applies because Gibraltar does not have VAT. However, false customs or tax declarations may trigger seizures, penalties up to 100% of evaded duty or tax, and loss of relief under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance and Income Tax Act 2010. If the ELP fraudulently claims UK VAT zero-rating on the basis of Gibraltar export documentation, HMRC may issue retrospective VAT assessments under VATA 1994 s.73, impose penalties up to 100% of tax under Schedule 24 to FA 2007, and pursue criminal proceedings under VATA 1994 s.72. The 180-day benchmark is the aviation-sector default contractual proof-of-payment window; failure to substantiate export receipts within that period increases audit challenge and exposes any UK zero-rating claim to clawback with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar is a fully convertible, zero-exchange-control jurisdiction. Both current account transactions (trade settlements, operational expenses, wages, royalties, catering supply payments) and capital account transactions (partner capital injections, loan drawdowns, equity redemptions, partnership profit distributions) are freely transferable in any currency without regulatory approval, surrender obligations, or quantitative limits. The fixed GIP/GBP parity at 1:1 eliminates intra-sterling conversion risk and guarantees economic equivalence for UK-based counterparties. Profit remittance to non-resident partners of a pass-through Exempted Limited Partnership is unrestricted and carries zero withholding tax under Gibraltar's Income Tax Act 2010 Part VIII conduct rules. The only practical hurdles are standard anti-money-laundering provenance checks, FATF-recommended wire-transfer edge verification, and the mandatory reporting of cross-border transfers above EUR 10,000 to the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit for an ELP operating a bonded airport catering kitchen.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar imposes no statutory timeline for the surrender or repatriation of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio for foreign currency receipts. The historical Exchange Control Act 1947 restrictions, which once applied to UK overseas territories, were formally abolished and are functionally obsolete. An ELP engaged in airport bonded in-flight meal production and direct ramp delivery may retain and deploy export receivables in GBP, EUR, USD, or any freely chosen settlement currency without any obligation to convert or remit within a specified period. The only administrative condition is that all cross-border receipts must pass through an account held with a GFSC-licensed bank or recognized e-money institution so that transaction monitoring and bespoke AML/CFT surveillance apply under the UK Sanctions Regulations 2019 and the Gibraltar Sanctions Regulations 2020.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar does not operate an independent central bank. The Bank of England serves as the overarching UK monetary authority and issues GBP which circulates freely in Gibraltar as legal tender alongside local currency. The Government of Gibraltar, under the Currency Notes Act 1934 (as amended), issues the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) at a fixed 1:1 peg to GBP with full interchangeability. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the prudential regulator and AML/CFT supervisor for all licensed banks, investment firms, and e-money institutions in or from Gibraltar. For cross-border FX policy, the UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) and HM Treasury exercise extraterritorial conduct and sanctions oversight, while the GFSC enforces the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, the Criminal Finances Act 2017, and the SFDR-adjacent AML/CFT rulebook.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: CONDITIONAL / MODERATE. Gibraltar-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (licensed under the GFSC Electronic Money Regulations 2011, as amended by the 2021 transposition) and UK FCA-authorised e-money institutions (including Wise Business, Revolut Business, Payoneer, and Airwallex) offer rapid remote onboarding, UK/euro IBANs, competitive cross-border FX conversion at mid-market rates, and API-driven transaction monitoring. They are viable for low-domiciliary operating disbursements, monthly partner distributions under the Monthly_ACH target rail, and EUR supplier sweeps. However, they are NOT viable as the primary treasury anchor because (i) EMIs cannot legally hold escrowed capital contributions with partner-segregated bankruptcy protection under the ELP's constitutional documents, (ii) they cannot issue commercial letters of credit or bank guarantees required for airport-license bonding at the catering facility, (iii) they generally prohibit high-velocity aviation contract settlement wires without case-by-case approval, and (iv) fine-grained counterparty due diligence on airline payors exceeds typical EMI transaction-monitoring logic. A GFSC-regulated tier-1 bank must remain the anchor liquidity relationship, with the EMI used exclusively as a treasury-optimization layer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing for limited-partner capital contributions from non-resident vehicles (including a Cayman-KY parent or UK holding entity) is executed via SWIFT MT103 with the purpose code 'CAPC' and narrative 'Limited Partner Capital Contribution — ISIC 5621 In-Flight Catering Kitchen, Gibraltar'. A supplementary MT199/MT103 reference confirming the underlying Partnership Agreement article must be attached where the bank requires. For convertible shareholder loans or working-capital subventions, use 'CRED' or 'TRAN' with a signed loan note. All inbound flows require a General Partner drawdown resolution, a source-of-wealth certificate signed by each contributor, sanctions screening against UK Sanctions Regulations 2019 and Gibraltar Sanctions Regulations 2020, and reconciliation against the ELP capital account ledger. Because Gibraltar has no exchange control approval, the credits can be credited immediately on confirmation after the transaction monitoring flag is cleared. Recommended staging: (i) escrow account at Gibraltar International Bank on day one, (ii) subsequent transfer to the multi-currency operational account upon the bank's confirmation of the beneficial-ownership register, and (iii) first disbursement only after the airport catering license is evidenced.Outbound trade settlement uses tiered clearing rails based on counter-party and currency. (1) High-value aviation contract wires to raw-food suppliers, logistics depots, and overseas airline procurement offices: SWIFT MT103 across the Gibraltar bank's correspondent network to London, Frankfurt, or New York. (2) Capital equipment imports (industrial ovens, blast chillers, tray packing lines) settle by Commercial Letter of Credit (SWIFT MT700) with a 90-180 day tenor, issued by Trusted Novus Bank or Euro Gibraltar Bank, under Incoterms advances against a bill of lading and proforma invoice; standby LCs (MT760) are used to support the airport license bond. (3) Monthly UK supplier and payroll disbursements use the UK Faster Payments Service (FPS) or CHAPS for same-day GBP clearing, and SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT) for EUR payments to European caterers and HACCP laboratories. (4) Monthly partner distributions and administrative fees under the target Monthly_ACH rail are executed through Faster Payments Out, SEPA, or a UK BACS batch, flagged as pass-through distributions supported by the Income Tax Office transparency letter. All outbound wires require the bank's counterparty screening and, for USD-denominated payments, an MT202 cover instruction naming the Gibraltar bank; the ELP must also compute final in-flight meal service under the bonded facility rules, with Customs Excise & VAT Authority documentation attached where wines/spirits or tobacco components are involved.GBP/EUR deliverable outright forwards (non-speculative, tenor up to 12 months, executed with UK FCA-authorized dealing counterparties or GFSC-licensed investment firms, hedged against expected monthly catering settlement flows from EU-based airlines)GBP/USD deliverable forwards and cross-currency swaps (XCCY) for matching USD-denominated revenue from transatlantic airline contracts against USD-denominated import equipment and US-sourced commodity inputsEUR/USD cross-currency swaps for euro-settlement legacy receivables, where the fixed aviation tariff is set in USD by the airline's treasury deskFX options and zero-premium collars (GBP/USD, GBP/EUR, EUR/USD) for locked catering tariffs and seasonality riders, permitted only when the underlying exposure is evidenced by a signed airline purchase order or in-flight supply agreementNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are structurally permitted from a regulatory standpoint but are commercially unnecessary and operationally disfavoured; GIP/GBP is pegged at par with full convertibility, so the NDF mechanism reserved for restricted or illiquid currencies is not required, and banks will treat NDF requests as low-priority unless tied to a structured Third-Country receivable

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: CONDITIONAL / MODERATE. Gibraltar-regulated Electronic Money Institutions (licensed under the GFSC Electronic Money Regulations 2011, as amended by the 2021 transposition) and UK FCA-authorised e-money institutions (including Wise Business, Revolut Business, Payoneer, and Airwallex) offer rapid remote onboarding, UK/euro IBANs, competitive cross-border FX conversion at mid-market rates, and API-driven transaction monitoring. They are viable for low-domiciliary operating disbursements, monthly partner distributions under the Monthly_ACH target rail, and EUR supplier sweeps. However, they are NOT viable as the primary treasury anchor because (i) EMIs cannot legally hold escrowed capital contributions with partner-segregated bankruptcy protection under the ELP's constitutional documents, (ii) they cannot issue commercial letters of credit or bank guarantees required for airport-license bonding at the catering facility, (iii) they generally prohibit high-velocity aviation contract settlement wires without case-by-case approval, and (iv) fine-grained counterparty due diligence on airline payors exceeds typical EMI transaction-monitoring logic. A GFSC-regulated tier-1 bank must remain the anchor liquidity relationship, with the EMI used exclusively as a treasury-optimization layer.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

10% standard Gibraltar Corporation Tax on chargeable profits of Gibraltar-resident companies and partnerships within the charge; 12.5% statutory rate applies to specified non-trading passive income categories (gross rental income, royalty income, interest income and dividend income) not derived from an active inflight catering trade

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% statutory dividend withholding tax. Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax when an ELP distributes profits to its general or limited partners, and no Gibraltar withholding arises when a corporate general partner pays dividends to non-resident shareholders; distributions are made gross provided the distributing entity has been fully assessed on its chargeable profits and the distribution is not recharacterised as a deemed taxable remission under the Income Tax Act anti-excessive-distribution provisions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Gibraltar Local File and Master File documentation is triggered where the ELP's aggregate related-party transactions exceed GIP 2,000,000 in a chargeable accounting period, or where annual gross inflight catering revenue exceeds GIP 10,000,000. Below the threshold a streamlined transfer pricing screening memorandum is recommended to evidence the arm's length pricing of inflight meal tray rates, direct ramp delivery charges, central kitchen management fees, and aircraft galley loading service margins.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance (Entities) Act 2019, aligned with the EU Code of Conduct Group and OECD BEPS minimum standards, classifies an inflight catering kitchen as a relevant 'distribution and service centre' activity. The ELP must demonstrate: a physical catering kitchen or bonded food-preparation premises in Gibraltar; regular board or general-partner meetings with substantive minutes; adequate number of employees engaged in actual meal production and ramp logistics; monthly operating expenditure proportionate to revenue; and the performance of core income-generating activities within Gibraltar. Pass-through entities must file Form ESR-01 annually, pay the GIP 2,500 substance filing fee, and maintain the substance evidence file for six years. Non-compliance creates a rebuttable presumption that the ELP is a shell entity and may trigger the recharacterisation of all distributions as Gibraltar-source taxable profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act and the Criminal Code, conviction for tax evasion is punishable by a fine of up to three times the tax evaded, an additional fraud penalty of GIP 50,000, and imprisonment for up to seven years; the Commissioner may issue a composite estimated assessment and an immediate asset-freezing notice upon discovery.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any shadow director who signed a false CT100 are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax under the doctrine of lifting the corporate veil; Gibraltar courts have granted travel-banning orders against directors, passport surrender mandates, and personal bank-account freeze orders until full civil restitution of the evaded tax plus 8% compound interest is made.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance (Entities) Act 2019, non-compliant ELPs face a substance penalty of GIP 10,000 in the first year, rising to GIP 100,000 for repeated failures, together with strike-off from the Gibraltar Limited Partnerships Register; distributions made during the non-compliant period may be recharacterised as assessable profits attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment and taxed at 12.5%.

Director personal liability: Where the ELP is determined to be a sham, the general partner is subject to disqualification as a director of any Gibraltar company for up to 15 years, a UK-wide or EEA travel ban, restitution of all distribution proceeds with 8% compound interest, and custodial liability of up to five years for fraud by false representation.
Anti-Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing, and Sanctions Evasion

Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime Act (Gibraltar) permits confiscation orders against the entire ELP catering asset base, cash seizure at the payment-rail level, and imprisonment for up to 14 years; corporate fines are uncapped and a minimum GIP 250,000 money-laundering penalty applies to the offending general-partner entity; the Financial Intelligence Unit may obtain a restraint order pending trial.

Director personal liability: A director or general partner who wilfully blinds themselves to a sanctions flag or PEP status of a counterparty faces personal criminal liability; courts routinely grant travel bans, extradition consents, and freezing orders over personal residential property, and the corporate veil is pierced on the 'facade of fraud' doctrine to expose the director's personal assets to confiscation.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may adjust the ELP's chargeable profits to the arm's length amount and apply a 30% adjusted-pricing penalty on the shifted margin, with the reallocated profits taxable at 12.5% if recharacterised as passive income; a daily penalty of GIP 250 applies for each day the transfer pricing documentation remains outstanding beyond the 30-day statutory notice period.

Director personal liability: The general partner or responsible finance officer causing the pricing manipulation is liable to a civil restitution order equal to the underpaid tax and may be disqualified from acting as a director or manager of any Gibraltar company for ten years; deliberate fabrication of comparable benchmarks is treated as falsification of accounts and gives rise to criminal tax fraud liability with imprisonment of up to seven years.
Repatriation WHT Evasion and Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may recharacterise the entire outflow as a royalty subject to 10% Gibraltar withholding, add a 20% late-payment surcharge and interest at 8% per annum; repeated dividend-stripping activity constitutes a statutory trigger for the 'excessive distribution arrangement' provisions, allowing denial of the pass-through election and taxation of the ELP at 12.5% on accumulated undistributed retained earnings.

Director personal liability: Distributions made with the intention of defeating Gibraltar tax liabilities render the general partner personally liable to account for the withholding tax not deducted; the Court of First Instance may issue a travel-ban order and a charging order over the director's personal residential property where the ELP assets have been stripped before assessment, and imprisonment follows upon a finding of fraudulent intent.
