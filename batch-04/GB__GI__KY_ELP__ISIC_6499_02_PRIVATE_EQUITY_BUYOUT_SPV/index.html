Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar, Income Tax Office (ITO), official portal: https://www.tax.gi (Income Tax Act 2010, as amended)

E-government filing portal

A foreign or non-resident entity, including an Exempted Limited Partnership, must register with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office by filing the prescribed registration form (Form 1) through a local authorised agent or tax representative. The application requires the partnership deed, certificate of registration, registered office address, details of all partners (general and limited), and identification documentation. The ITO issues a unique Gibraltar tax file reference number (Income Tax Identification Number) which is used for all filings, assessments, and correspondence.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign or non-resident entity, including an Exempted Limited Partnership, must register with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office by filing the prescribed registration form (Form 1) through a local authorised agent or tax representative. The application requires the partnership deed, certificate of registration, registered office address, details of all partners (general and limited), and identification documentation. The ITO issues a unique Gibraltar tax file reference number (Income Tax Identification Number) which is used for all filings, assessments, and correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No voluntary check-the-box election exists under Gibraltar law. Tax transparency is statutorily determined by the legal form: an Exempted Limited Partnership is treated as transparent unless the Income Tax Act or applicable anti-hybrid legislation overrides this characterisation. Hybrid mismatch rules arising from UK/Gibraltar and OECD Pillar Two implementation may recharacterise the entity for specific cross-border payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST; customs and excise duties only (Gibraltar Customs and Excise Act)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – no VAT registration threshold exists. For customs purposes, every importer of dutiable or controlled goods must register with HM Customs of Gibraltar, and no de minimis economic threshold applies to the initial import transaction.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar does not levy VAT, and therefore there is no 0% VAT export rating for goods or services. Exports leaving the territory and customs jurisdiction of Gibraltar are, however, outside the scope of import duties and excise duties; where duty has been paid on inputs that are subsequently physically exported, a drawback or conditional relief claim may be available under the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD / Single Administrative Document) including Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR transport evidence
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank settlement order (SWIFT MT103) proving the export proceeds are repatriated to Gibraltar

No real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital VAT reporting mandate is in force in Gibraltar. Import and export declarations are processed electronically through the Gibraltar Electronic Customs System (eCustoms), with risk-based selectivity, but no continuous transaction control is imposed on businesses.

No VAT export zero-rating clawback exists because no VAT is chargeable. Fraudulent claims for customs duty drawback or export relief attract penalties of up to 100% of the duty amount, forfeiture of the goods, monetary penalties under the Customs and Excise Act, and retrospective clawback of all relieved duties, with criminal prosecution including imprisonment where intent to evade is proved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP is fully convertible at par with GBP, and Gibraltar has no exchange-control law or capital-account restriction: current-account flows (dividends, interest, management fees, trade invoices) and capital-account flows (capital contributions, loan drawdowns, acquisition consideration, exit distributions) may be settled freely in GIP, GBP, EUR or USD without prior approval, licensing, or central-bank reporting. Profit and capital remittance by a non-resident partner of an Exempt Limited Partnership (ELP) is unrestricted and not subject to withholding tax. The only practical convertibility constraint is financial-crime and sanctions screening under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (POCA 2015), UK OFSI consolidated lists, EU/UN sanctions, and FATF-driven enhanced due diligence; funds connected to designated persons or embargoed states become non-convertible.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable to a financial holding and buyout SPV. Gibraltar imposes no mandatory surrender, no minimum conversion ratio, no repatriation deadline, and no centralised FX dealing requirement on export or any other receipts. The ELP may retain foreign currency balances in any convertible currency, convert between GIP/GBP/EUR/USD at will, and deploy proceeds for acquisition, escrow, security, or operating purposes without GFSC or treasury approval.

Central bank supervision: The jurisdiction does not maintain a resident central bank or a standalone foreign-exchange administration. The monetary authority is the Government of Gibraltar acting through the Finance Centre and the Currency Commission (issuer of the Gibraltar pound under the Currency Notes Act 1934, pegged at par with GBP). Banking, money-laundering and FX-conduct supervision is exercised by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019 (FSA 2019); sanctions and financial-crime policy align with HM Treasury OFSI and the Bank of England’s monetary framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High with legal constraints. Gibraltar does not issue a distinct domestic EMI licence; e-money and payment services are regulated under FSA 2019, and no broad EMI passport has been implemented by GFSC. UK-licensed EMIs and Payment Institutions may access Gibraltar through the UK–Gibraltar market-access provisions preserved by the Financial Services Act 2021, but they must not provide regulated payment services to Gibraltar resident persons without GFSC registration, and most Gibraltar clearing banks treat an EMI-held sub-account as requiring sponsor approval and enhanced due diligence. EU EMIs (e.g., Lithuanian or Polish licence holders) are viable as forward rails for EUR/GBP collections but do not substitute for a Gibraltar operational account; the ELP may use an EMI for non-core payment routing only if an inter-entity agreement is disclosed and the receiving bank approves.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

UBO opacity from a multi-tier LP/GP chain with Cayman or BVI holding layers; GFSC views this as Medium-Risk Enhanced.

Root cause: UBO opacity from a multi-tier LP/GP chain with Cayman or BVI holding layers; GFSC views this as Medium-Risk Enhanced.

Defense evidence: Complete organisational chart with ownership percentages; certificates of good standing for every vehicle; members and PSC registers; beneficial-owner declaration forms for each entity; trust deed and letter of wishes for any trust layer; passport and proof of address of every natural person holding 25% or more or exercising control; audited or reviewed financial statements of each feeder; a written commercial explanation for each offshore layer.
Nominee director/shareholder or 'managed office' lack of substance in Gibraltar.

Root cause: Nominee director/shareholder or 'managed office' lack of substance in Gibraltar.

Defense evidence: Executed director service agreements; professional CVs and licences; board minutes and written resolutions evidencing genuine decision-making; Gibraltar office lease and utility bills; payroll and bank statements showing local substance; a certificate from the registered agent; explicit nominee disclosure declarations or evidence of non-nominee status; correspondence with the GFSC-registered legal adviser.
Syndicated loan or deferred purchase consideration as source of initial and recurrent funding.

Root cause: Syndicated loan or deferred purchase consideration as source of initial and recurrent funding.

Defense evidence: LMA-form facility agreement and commitment letters; schedule of lenders with regulatory status (FCA, ECB, GFSC or equivalent); drawdown notices and repayment schedule; intercreditor and security trust deed; independent third-party valuation report; management-prepared cash-flow/LBO model; POCA-compliant source-of-funds statement signed by the sponsor and general partner.
Rapid capital concentration and acquisition-driven flows inconsistent with declared activity.

Root cause: Rapid capital concentration and acquisition-driven flows inconsistent with declared activity.

Defense evidence: Fund constitution and LPA; subscription agreements and closing statement; capital contribution ledger matched to bank credits; share/asset purchase agreement, equity commitment letter and conditions precedent; sanctions and PEP screening reports for every counterparty; legal opinion on the transaction; annual forecast supported by management accounts and the acquisition financing structure.
Sanctioned or high-risk limited-partner participation, including FATF grey-list jurisdictions.

Root cause: Sanctioned or high-risk limited-partner participation, including FATF grey-list jurisdictions.

Defense evidence: Per-LP KYC file satisfying GFSC AML Rules 2011 and POCA 2015; beneficial-ownership certificates for indirect holders; evidence of tax residence and economic activity of each LP; FATF/CFATF status and internal risk rating; negative media and sanctions screening reports; introducer's attestation; strict two-party payment rule documented in the subscription agreement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound routing must follow a pre-agreed wiring protocol with the receiving Gibraltar bank: (1) send cross-border injections by SWIFT MT103 with Field 50K remitter and Field 59 beneficiary exactly matching bank-held names; (2) include in Field 70 a remittance reference such as 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - LPA [date] - CLASS A' and in Field 72 the ISO 20022 category purpose code EQUI (equity), ACQP (acquisition), LOAN or FINA (loan financing); (3) for syndicated facility drawdowns attach the executed drawdown notice, facility agreement and lender commitment schedule; (4) pre-deliver a source-of-funds pack comprising the subscription agreement, equity commitment letter, valuation report and sanctions/PEP clearance to the Relationship Manager; (5) confirm all remitters are not on the UK OFSI Consolidated List, EU, UN, or Gibraltar POCA 2015 schedules; (6) use CHAPS or Faster Payments for GBP funding, SEPA Instant or TARGET2 for EUR funding where the bank is a SEPA participant, and Fedwire/CHIPS through a US correspondent for USD; (7) retain MT103 confirmations and, where a non-resident party gives payment instructions, an authorised FOA or W-8IMY-equivalent form.Outbound rails are structured by payment type: (i) closing consideration is settled by SWIFT MT103 with Field 70 referencing the deal name and Field 72 purpose code ACQP, supported by the executed SPA and security trustee instructions; (ii) syndicated loan capex and working capital draws use MT103 or MT103 STP with facility utilisation notices and LC schedules; (iii) intercompany and escrow releases use MT103, CHAPS or FPS with the intercompany loan agreement and escrow release conditions; (iv) trade-related acquisitions use Commercial Letters of Credit or documentary collections (MT700/MT710/MT720) for goods or equipment capex; (v) euro-denominated settlements use SEPA SCT with the creditor's structured identifier where the counterparty is in SEPA, otherwise TARGET2 via correspondent; (vi) dividend and exit distributions use MT103 with a distribution resolution and audited partnership accounts.
