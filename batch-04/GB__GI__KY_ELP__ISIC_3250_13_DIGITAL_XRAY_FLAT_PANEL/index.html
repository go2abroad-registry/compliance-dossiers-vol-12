Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar - Commissioner of Income Tax (Income Tax Office, Gibraltar) for direct taxation; H.M. Customs of Gibraltar for import duty, excise and customs registration. Official portals: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi and the Gibraltar Tax Referencing e-services available through the Government of Gibraltar digital platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Apply to the Registrar of Companies (Gibraltar Companies House) to register the Cayman Islands Exempted Limited Partnership (KY_ELP) as an overseas/foreign limited partnership under Gibraltar limited partnership legislation including the 2019 amendments, within one month of establishing a place of business or appointing a Gibraltar agent; file the Cayman certificate of formation, partnership deed and registered office confirmation. Step 2 - If no Gibraltar-source activity is anticipated, file a one-off non-resident status notification with the Commissioner of Income Tax; if Gibraltar-source trade is expected, apply for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) on the prescribed statutory form, annexing the partnership agreement, general partner identification, audited opening balance sheet and a letter of authorization for a Gibraltar fiscal representative. Step 3 - Register with H.M. Customs as importer/exporter for a Customs Identification Code; registration is compulsory regardless of turnover where goods cross the Gibraltar frontier. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope - the KY_ELP is fiscally transparent in Gibraltar, with each partner assessable personally on their distributable share of partnership profits; under the territorial principle of the Income Tax Act 2010 only Gibraltar-source income falls within the charge, so non-resident partners with no Gibraltar-source profits have no Gibraltar tax liability or filing obligation.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available in Gibraltar; partnership transparency is mandatory under the Income Tax Act 2010 and Gibraltar limited partnership legislation, including 2019 amendments. Elective mechanisms available: (i) formal registration of the ELP as an Exempted Limited Partnership used for investment-fund activity, confirming statutory pass-through treatment; (ii) an election for tax-exempt treatment of a qualifying investment fund, subject to economic substance requirements; (iii) contractual election in the partnership agreement governing profit allocation, which remains subject to OECD BEPS Action 2 hybrid mismatch rules that may deny deductions or impose withholding if the interaction of Cayman transparency and Gibraltar territoriality creates double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST - Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and the UK VAT area; indirect taxation comprises the Gibraltar Customs Tariff (import duty), excise duties on alcohol, tobacco and hydrocarbon oils, purchase tax on a narrow range of luxury goods, and insurance premium tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT/GST: not applicable, no registration threshold. Import duty: compulsory registration with H.M. Customs for every importer/exporter in Gibraltar with no de minimis turnover threshold. For the ELP's parallel UK-facing activity, the UK VAT registration threshold is GBP 90,000 (2024/25 rate, rolling 12-month basis), but fully exported goods are outside the scope of this threshold and qualify for zero-rating if export conditions are met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar side: exports of amorphous silicon cesium iodide DR flat-panel detectors physically leaving Gibraltar are outside the charge to import duty and purchase tax when declared on a valid export entry. UK side: supplies by the ELP of goods exported from the UK to non-UK customers qualify for 0% VAT under VATA 1994 s.30 and the VAT Regulations 1995 provided (i) the goods are exported by the ELP or its approved agent, (ii) the time of supply occurs before export, (iii) the goods are exported within three months of the time of supply, and (iv) specified export evidence is held before the VAT return is submitted.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD filed through Gibraltar ASYCUDA World or UK CDS pre-departure entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Gibraltar/UK to the overseas destination
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange receipt evidencing payment within 180 days of export

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or B2B e-invoicing mandate exists in Gibraltar. Customs clearance is mandatory through the ASYCUDA World electronic customs system using the Single Administrative Document (SAD); the UK equivalent for import/export declarations is the Customs Declaration Service (CDS). Making Tax Digital (MTD) e-filing applies only when the ELP is separately registered for UK VAT, and Peppol-based clearance is not mandated in either Gibraltar or the UK.

Where export evidence is absent or false, HMRC re-characterizes the supply as standard-rated and claws back the VAT due plus interest under ss.73-74 VATA 1994; inaccuracy penalties under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 range from 30% to 100% of the tax, reduced only for unprompted disclosure; deliberate evasion attracts criminal prosecution under CEMA 1979 ss.167-168 and extended assessment windows of up to 20 years. In Gibraltar, false or incomplete export declarations subject the ELP to forfeiture of goods, assessment of unpaid duty, monetary penalties up to three times the value of the goods, retrospective clawback of any export concessions and suspension of customs facilitation status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Capital and current account convertibility are unrestricted. GIP is pegged at par to GBP with free interchangeability inside the UK monetary area, so no exchange control approval is required for inward foreign investment, capital contributions, or repatriation. The ELP is tax transparent under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act (Chapter 100); non-resident partners are not subject to Gibraltar tax on foreign-source profits, and profit remittances or distributions are free of withholding tax, dividend tax, and capital gains tax. Residual constraints are limited to FATF-aligned KYC scrutiny, sanctions screening, and economic substance reporting under the Economic Substance Act 2020, none of which constitutes a material legal hurdle to remitting export proceeds from the DR-detector medical hardware trade.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no mandatory export proceeds surrender, no minimum conversion ratio, and no statutory repatriation timeline. Export receipts denominated in USD or EUR may be retained indefinitely in foreign-currency accounts at Gibraltar-licensed banks. The sole compliance duties are: (i) accurate trade-documentation matching on SWIFT MT103 or MT202 payment messages; (ii) record retention under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the Money Laundering Regulations 2019; and (iii) inclusion of the proceeds in the ELP's annual tax return and economic substance file evidencing management and control in Gibraltar. Neither the BoE nor the GFSC prescribes any conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: The monetary regime is jointly anchored by the Bank of England (BoE), which sets sterling monetary policy, and by the Government of Gibraltar, which issues Gibraltar pound (GIP) banknotes under the Currency Notes Act 1934 at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP. Prudential supervision of all Gibraltar-licensed banks and electronic money institutions is vested in the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC). There is no separate Gibraltar central bank; GIP is legal tender only within Gibraltar and is fully interchangeable with GBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for operational disbursements, Low for capital escrow. Gibraltar has a domestic electronic money institution licensing regime under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Regulations 2019 supervised by the GFSC, but the market is nascent and Gibraltar EMIs generally lack direct access to EU Target2 or SEPA rails post-Brexit. A more practical route is onboarding with a UK FCA-authorised EMI such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, or a B2B treasury platform accepting Gibraltar-incorporated ELPs; however, such EMIs will treat the GI ELP as a cross-border corporate, cap aggregate ledger balances, disallow escrow-like features, and require full ultimate beneficial owner disclosure. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled in Singapore, the UAE, or Hong Kong are legally permitted to service the ELP only if they hold a GFSC-recognised licence or an applicable UK/EU passporting arrangement; unlicensed pure offshore schemes are blocked under Gibraltar AML law.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Non-resident partners remit capital contributions via SWIFT MT103, or MT202 for interbank transfers, to the ELP's Gibraltar IBAN, which uses the 23-character GI country-code format. The remittance reference field must clearly state Capital Contribution, the ELP legal name, and the partnership agreement date. Because GIP has no exchange controls, no conversion approval is required; USD or EUR partner funds may be credited to a multicurrency ledger and converted at the prevailing market rate. The correspondent chain typically routes through a UK or US clearer such as Barclays or JPMorgan into the receiving Gibraltar bank. The evidence pack comprises the signed capital call notice, updated UBO and source-of-funds statement, partnership register extract dated within 30 days, CRS and FATCA passporting forms identifying the ELP as a pass-through entity, and a three to five working day compliance hold before funds are deployed.Outbound supplier settlements follow a two-rail matrix. Supplier disbursements below USD 100,000 equivalent use SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer from the ELP's Gibraltar operating account, with the remittance information field carrying the commercial invoice number and country of destination to facilitate bank screening. Principal supplier purchases above USD 100,000 use a documentary sight or usance Letter of Credit issued under UCP 600 by Gibraltar International Bank or Trusted Novus Bank, with the beneficiary being the GIS or TFT panel manufacturer in Korea, Japan, Taiwan, or China; the LC is settled through the USD SWIFT line and the presentation set comprises the commercial invoice, clean-on-board bill of lading, packing list, CE certificate, and certificate of origin. GBP or USD cover for the LC is booked at the concurrent spot rate. This fulfils the required Swift_LC and TT_Wire payment rails, with SWIFT gpi escrow-backed transfers as a secondary rail for large R&D deposits.Deliverable FX forwards in GBP/USD, GBP/EUR, and EUR/USD with Gibraltar or UK licensed banks, tenors up to 24 months, typically 1 to 6 months to cover DR-panel order cycles.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for CNY, KRW, TWD, and INR against USD where onshore deliverability is constrained, settled in GIP or GBP against a standard fixing such as CNH/USD or USD/KRW via London counterparties.FX swaps and rollover structures for short-term liquidity alignment between USD export receivables and GBP or EUR operating expenses.Vanilla EUR/USD call and put options and zero-premium collars as contingent hedges for tender-based export transactions.Cross-currency swaps for material multi-year supplier contracts, matching USD liabilities with USD receivables and eliminating long-term GIP to GBP revaluation risk; transacted as non-cleared bilateral derivatives with a licensed bank under EMIR-type margin rules, with the ELP likely qualifying as a non-financial counterparty below the clearing threshold.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High for operational disbursements, Low for capital escrow. Gibraltar has a domestic electronic money institution licensing regime under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Regulations 2019 supervised by the GFSC, but the market is nascent and Gibraltar EMIs generally lack direct access to EU Target2 or SEPA rails post-Brexit. A more practical route is onboarding with a UK FCA-authorised EMI such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, or a B2B treasury platform accepting Gibraltar-incorporated ELPs; however, such EMIs will treat the GI ELP as a cross-border corporate, cap aggregate ledger balances, disallow escrow-like features, and require full ultimate beneficial owner disclosure. Offshore digital EMIs domiciled in Singapore, the UAE, or Hong Kong are legally permitted to service the ELP only if they hold a GFSC-recognised licence or an applicable UK/EU passporting arrangement; unlicensed pure offshore schemes are blocked under Gibraltar AML law.
