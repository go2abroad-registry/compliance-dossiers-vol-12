Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Commissioner of Income Tax (Income Tax Office, Edinburgh House, Gibraltar; electronic portal: GM-Tax / Gibraltar e-Government Services at portal.egov.gi) for direct taxation; HM Customs Gibraltar Division (Customs House, Gibraltar; e-services via gov.gi customs platform) for indirect and customs taxation.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Limited Partnerships Act 2011, filing the ELP application and statutory declaration of exempted status. Step 2: Register the ELP with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office for a Partnership Tax Reference Number (TIN) by submitting the partnership registration particulars and details of each general and limited partner. Step 3: Because the ELP is fiscally transparent, the TIN is an administrative filing reference only; no Gibraltar tax is chargeable at ELP level and non-resident limited partners deriving income from outside Gibraltar are not within the charge to Gibraltar income tax. Step 4: If the ELP will supply digital services to UK consumers, register separately with HMRC for UK VAT under the non-established taxable person procedure (form VAT 1 through HMRC online services) and obtain a GB VAT registration number. Step 5: Register with HM Customs Gibraltar as an economic operator when undertaking physical import or export activity outside Gibraltar.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box election; pass-through taxation of the ELP is mandatory under the Income Tax Act and the Limited Partnerships Act 2011. Available statutory elections include: (i) election under the Income Tax Act for a partnership to have assessable income computed at partnership level with each partner charged on their distributive share; (ii) election for the ELP to be registered as a Private Fund Limited Partnership (PFLP) under the Limited Partnerships (Private Fund) Regulations, which preserves tax transparency and adds regulatory fund treatment; (iii) election for a UK partner to apply UK hybrid mismatch rules under TIOPA 2010 Part 6 where the Gibraltar ELP is treated as transparent in the UK but potentially opaque in a third jurisdiction; and (iv) election by a Gibraltar-resident partner for separate assessment. No non-resident partner is required to file a Gibraltar tax return unless they carry on a trade in Gibraltar through a permanent establishment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No Gibraltar VAT or GST is currently in force; Gibraltar applies import duties and excise duties under the Gibraltar Customs Tariff, and is outside the UK VAT area and outside the EU VAT regime. For digital and e-services supplied by a Gibraltar supplier to UK consumers, the UK VAT place-of-supply rules apply and UK VAT is chargeable under the UK VAT Act 1994; for cross-border B2C digital supplies, the Overseas Person UK VAT registration and UK OSS (One Stop Shop) non-union scheme apply. A Gibraltar Goods and Services Tax at 10% was proposed in 2021 but has not been enacted and is not in force.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 0 (no Gibraltar VAT/GST registration threshold exists because no Gibraltar indirect tax on services is in force; customs registration applies from the first import/export transaction). For UK VAT on B2C digital services supplied by a Gibraltar non-established supplier, the statutory threshold is GBP 0 — the GBP 85,000 normal UK VAT threshold does not apply to non-established suppliers, so UK VAT registration and OSS filing are mandatory from the first pound of UK consumer digital turnover. For UK B2B digital supplies, registration is also mandatory because the place of supply is the UK business customer with reverse charge only where the UK customer is VAT-registered and the supplier remains outside the UK VAT net.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar domestic law does not provide an export VAT zero-rating regime because Gibraltar levies no VAT or GST; the concept is inapplicable to physical exports from Gibraltar. Export shipments from Gibraltar may, where applicable, benefit from customs outward-processing relief, temporary export relief, or duty drawback under the Gibraltar Customs Tariff, provided the goods leave Gibraltar for a third destination and the export declaration is validated by HM Customs Gibraltar. In respect of UK VAT, a Gibraltar supplier of B2C EdTech digital services cannot apply zero-rating: the supply is treated as made in the UK where the consumer belongs, UK VAT at 20% is due, and the UK OSS mechanism is used to declare the tax; pure e-services do not qualify for dispatch/export zero-rating. For B2B digital services to a UK VAT-registered customer, the supply is outside the scope of Gibraltar taxation and subject to UK reverse charge, and the Gibraltar supplier must obtain and substantiate the customer's UK VAT number plus evidence that the customer is a business (validity certificate, VAT registration certificate, or comparable evidence).

  • Gibraltar Single Administrative Document (SAD) export declaration / C88 export declaration validated by HM Customs Gibraltar with accepted Export Accompaniment Document (EAD)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading, House/Sea Waybill, or Air Waybill evidencing export from Gibraltar to the destination country
  • Proof of arrival at the destination customs territory (delivery receipt, customs clearance certificate, or equivalent foreign customs stamp)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange repatriation receipt evidencing full settlement of the export sale proceeds to the ELP's Gibraltar or foreign bank account
  • Evidence of the underlying digital subscription or physical goods transaction, including IP logs, end-customer location evidence, and payment processor settlement reports where digital services are supplied

Gibraltar imposes no real-time e-invoice fiscal clearance mandate and no digital stamping/crypto-stamping requirement for e-invoices. Customs declarations for physical movements must be submitted electronically to HM Customs Gibraltar using the Gibraltar Electronic Customs Declarations system (SAD / C88 format) with automated risk assessment. Supplies of digital services to UK consumers under the UK VAT regime must be accounted for and reported through the OSS quarterly return, and all UK VAT returns and OSS filings must be made digitally either via HMRC portals or through Making Tax Digital (MTD) compatible software APIs. No government pre-clearance e-invoice validation exists for EdTech subscription transactions; however, records supporting place-of-supply and customer location must be retained and made available digitally on HMRC or HM Customs Gibraltar request.

Incorrect or fraudulent claims to UK zero-rating or incorrect OSS/RVC treatment attract penalty under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 of up to 100% of the UK VAT at risk, reduced only for unprompted disclosure and reasonable care; deliberate evasion carries a minimum 50% penalty and may rise to 100%, with interest charged from the due date. Under UK Value Added Tax Act 1994, fraudulent evasion attracts civil penalties of up to 100% of the tax plus criminal liability in the UK. In Gibraltar, false declarations to HM Customs Gibraltar constitute a customs offence under the Customs and Excise (Gibraltar) Ordinance, with penalties ranging from forfeiture of goods, monetary penalties equal to the aggregate of the duty evaded plus 20% thereof up to 100%, and on conviction to imprisonment up to 7 years and a substantial fine. Retrospective clawback applies: any zero-rating/customs relief or UK VAT deduction previously allowed is withdrawn for the full open review period (generally 4 years for HMRC assessments, 5 years for Gibraltar customs, unlimited in cases of fraud or dishonesty), and company officers and general partners of the ELP may be jointly and severally liable for the underlying tax and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at 1:1 to GBP and is fully convertible. Gibraltar has been free of exchange-control restrictions since the repeal of UK Exchange Control Act-era measures in 1979/1980; both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted. Profit remittances and capital distributions to non-resident partners of the Exempted Limited Partnership are not subject to exchange-control approval or withholding, provided the ELP's tax filings and economic-substance records are current. The pass-through tax model of the ELP means subscription profits flow transparently to partners, with no Gibraltar tax at the partnership level, and distributions in USD, EUR, or GBP may be made through the cleared banking system without conversion mandate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation obligation for export receipts or cross-border subscription revenues. EdTech revenues denominated in USD and EUR (e.g., settlements from Apple and Google payouts) may be retained in foreign currency accounts indefinitely; however, prudent treasury policy recommends a structured GBP liquidity buffer and permitted hedging instruments to manage sterling-functional-currency exposure.

Central bank supervision: The Financial Services Commission (GFSC) — the integrated regulatory authority discharging banking supervision and residual foreign-exchange control functions under the Banking Act 2015 (as amended) and the Financial Services Act 2019. There is no standalone Gibraltar central bank; note issuance rests with the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934, and monetary policy is anchored to the Bank of England via the Gibraltar pound's statutory parity to sterling.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High feasibility for payment collection but legally constrained for full treasury substitution. UK FCA-authorised e-money institutions (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, PPRO, Payoneer) and certain EU-licensed EMIs under the Payment Services Directive 2 may open multicurrency IBAN-denominated settlement accounts for the ELP with remote onboarding and lower minimum balances. Post-Brexit, Gibraltar-passported EMI authorisations lapsed, so reliance is on the EMI's own FCA or home-state licence. Most EMIs will classify the offshore ELP with a Cayman-linked general partner as an elevated-risk structure, often triggering enhanced due diligence, documentary UBO proof, and restricted product access. EMIs do not provide credit, documentary letters of credit, or meaningful FX hedging capacities; therefore they should be used only as settlement/collection rails, with the domestic Gibraltar bank position held as the primary reserve and liquidity buffer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant partner capital contributions must be sent via SWIFT MT103 wire with a clear purpose code: for GBP use 'Investment by non-resident partner in Gibraltar ELP capital'; for USD use SWIFT purpose code 'DEF - Direct Investment Equities' (or bank-specific local code for capital subscription); for EUR use ISO 20022 Remittance Information 'CAPITAL-INJECTION'. Each wire must be matched to the ELP capital account ledger, supported by the executed partnership deed, subscription documents, and a formation-agent closing certificate. USD transfers should route through a prime US correspondent (JP Morgan, Citi, Bank of America) to the beneficiary Gibraltar bank; GBP transfers should clear via CHAPS or Faster Payments; EUR via SEPA or SWIFT MT103 with an EU correspondent. any GIP-denominated residual is automatically convertible at par with no forward discount

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High feasibility for payment collection but legally constrained for full treasury substitution. UK FCA-authorised e-money institutions (Wise, Airwallex, Currencycloud, PPRO, Payoneer) and certain EU-licensed EMIs under the Payment Services Directive 2 may open multicurrency IBAN-denominated settlement accounts for the ELP with remote onboarding and lower minimum balances. Post-Brexit, Gibraltar-passported EMI authorisations lapsed, so reliance is on the EMI's own FCA or home-state licence. Most EMIs will classify the offshore ELP with a Cayman-linked general partner as an elevated-risk structure, often triggering enhanced due diligence, documentary UBO proof, and restricted product access. EMIs do not provide credit, documentary letters of credit, or meaningful FX hedging capacities; therefore they should be used only as settlement/collection rails, with the domestic Gibraltar bank position held as the primary reserve and liquidity buffer.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% flat Gibraltar corporation tax rate, chargeable on the ELP's Gibraltar-source trading profits; the ELP itself is fiscally transparent and profits are assessed on partners in accordance with their profit shares, with the ELP taxed as if it were a company only where it carries on a trade in Gibraltar.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Gibraltar does not impose any withholding tax on dividends or on partnership profit distributions; distributions from the ELP to limited and general partners are paid gross with no Gibraltar source taxation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where the ELP's Gibraltar turnover exceeds GBP 20 million and cross-border related-party transactions exceed GBP 1 million; Master File: mandatory where the multinational group consolidated global turnover exceeds EUR 50 million; simplified documentation applies to micro-entities with Gibraltar gross income below GBP 2 million.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations, embedded in the Income Tax Act 2010 and aligned with OECD/Code of Conduct standards, apply to ELPs carrying on relevant activities, including IP holding, distribution and service centers, and financing. An ELP deriving EdTech subscription revenue through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing must demonstrate that core income-generating activities, strategic decision-making, qualified staff, and physical premises are located in Gibraltar if the income is to be treated as Gibraltar-source or if treaty benefits are claimed. The offshore exemption for non-Gibraltar-source income requires affirmative proof that the ELP is not carrying on business in Gibraltar; passive equity holding and pure offshore administration will not by themselves satisfy the economic substance test once active platform business exceeds de minimis levels.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 100% of the evaded tax, daily default surcharges, interest charges, and criminal prosecution on indictment; on conviction, fines and imprisonment of up to 7 years under sections 128-129 of the Income Tax Act 2010.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any responsible officer face unlimited civil restitution for the unpaid tax plus interest; imposition of director disqualification orders; High Court travel bans and seizure of travel documents where the individual represents a flight risk.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Revocation of ELP exemption status; retroactive reassessment of the entire worldwide income at the 12.5% Gibraltar corporate rate; denial of UK-Gibraltar DTA benefits; referral to HMRC under the exchange-of-information framework, with additional penalties of up to 200% of the tax advantage.

Director personal liability: The general partner is personally liable for civil restitution of the unwarranted tax advantage; public naming as a promoter or enabler of tax avoidance; travel bans and curtailment of passport privileges pending full compliance and payment.
Anti-Money Laundering Failures

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2011 (Gibraltar), failure to report a suspicious transaction is punishable by a fine and/or imprisonment up to 2 years; substantive money laundering on indictment carries imprisonment of up to 14 years and unlimited fines; asset freezing orders may be imposed by the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: AML compliance officers and general partners face personal civil penalties of up to GBP 1 million, individual criminal prosecution, director disqualification, and travel restrictions routinely imposed during active GFIU investigations.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment by the Commissioner with tax charged at 12.5% on the uplifted profit; penalty of up to 100% of the net understatement; additional penalty of 25% of the adjustment for failure to comply with a transfer pricing information notice; interest accrues from the original due date.

Director personal liability: General partners who knowingly sign inaccurate TP schedules or approve contrived benchmark studies are personally liable for the penalty amounts and civil damages; courts may impose director disqualification orders of up to 15 years and travel bans pending settlement of the tax liability.
