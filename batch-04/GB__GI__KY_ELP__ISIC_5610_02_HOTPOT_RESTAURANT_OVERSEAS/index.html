Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), operating jointly with Companies House Gibraltar for entity registration and HM Customs Gibraltar for trade identification. Official portal: the Gibraltar Government Taxation portal (www.gibraltar.gov.gi) and the Income Tax Office's MyTax Gibraltar electronic filing services; company/partnership registry at Companies House Gibraltar (www.companieshouse.gi).

E-government filing portal

Procurement sequence for a non-resident ELP: (i) appoint a Gibraltar-licensed registered agent and file the Exempted Limited Partnership registration particulars with Companies House Gibraltar, obtaining the statutory registration number under Gibraltar partnership/company legislation; (ii) within 30 days of formation, file the income tax registration application with the Income Tax Office, attaching the certificate of registration, registered office address, full particulars of general and limited partners, registered agent confirmation and AML/KYC records; (iii) the Income Tax Office issues a Gibraltar Reference Number (GRN), which is the statutory TIN/taxpayer ID for all filings, together with a partnership assessment number, while each partner must evidence their own foreign TIN; (iv) separately apply to HM Customs Gibraltar for a GB/GI EORI number when the ELP or its agent will lodge import/export declarations. Issuance turn-around is normally 10–15 working days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – The ELP is tax transparent: Gibraltar income tax is charged on each partner's allocable share of partnership income rather than on the ELP as a taxable unit. Non-resident partners are taxed only on Gibraltar-source income; profits derived from overseas direct-operated hotpot dining, condiment procurement, offshore royalties and management services performed entirely outside Gibraltar are not subject to Gibraltar income tax unless attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment. A corporate partner resident in Gibraltar or with a Gibraltar establishment is subject to the standard Gibraltar company tax rate of 12.5% on its share of Gibraltar-source profits.

Available election forms

Available elections include: (a) an election for consolidated/opaque taxation of the ELP as a single taxpayer at entity level; (b) the default transparency (pass-through) election whereby non-resident partners remain outside Gibraltar tax on foreign-source shares; (c) a Qualifying Company (Category 2) fixed-tax election for a corporate partner, replacing percentage taxation with an annual fixed tax of GBP 30,000–38,000; (d) joint assessment versus separate assessment of partners on partnership profits; and (e) characterisation elections under the UK–Gibraltar Double Taxation Arrangement 2019 and the UK hybrid-mismatch rules, ensuring the ELP is respected as transparent in both jurisdictions so as to prevent double taxation or double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar does not impose VAT, GST, sales tax or any general consumption tax; indirect taxation is limited to customs and excise duties on the importation of specified goods (alcohol, tobacco, fuel and certain finished goods) under Gibraltar customs legislation. Goods exported from Gibraltar are free of export duty, and the notional general indirect tax headline rate is effectively zero.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – no VAT/GST registration threshold exists because no VAT/GST is charged in Gibraltar. Any business making imports or exports is required to obtain a Gibraltar EORI (Economic Operator Registration and Identification) number regardless of turnover; import customs duty arises only when the declared value of an inbound consignment exceeds the low-value exemption threshold (approximately GBP 135) or when the goods are excise goods; no registration threshold applies to exporters.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST, no domestic export zero-rating claim exists; exports from Gibraltar are duty-free by statute. Where the physical condiment and equipment supplies are shipped from the United Kingdom gateway plant, zero-rating applies under UK VAT Act 1994 s.30 provided: (i) the goods leave the UK within the statutory export time limits; (ii) the ELP or related UK supplier holds a Customs Single Administrative Document (C88), export MRN and authenticated transport documents; (iii) the foreign restaurant entity is the named consignee; and (iv) proof of export is obtained within 3 months of supply. Offshore services (franchise royalties, management, procurement and central kitchen support supplied to overseas restaurants) are outside the scope of Gibraltar income tax on the territorial basis and are generally not subject to any Gibraltar consumption tax; UK B2B place-of-supply rules may nonetheless treat such services as supplied where the UK recipient or the UK establishment is located.

  • Customs Export Declaration – SAD Form C88 / CDS export declaration with Movement Reference Number (MRN)
  • Bill of Lading (port-to-port) or Air Waybill evidencing export movement and consignee status
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank SWIFT MT103 evidencing credit of export proceeds to the ELP's designated bank account
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List with terms of sale (FOB/CIF/DDP) and HS commodity codes
  • Certificate of Origin where preferential origin relief or tariff preference is claimed

Gibraltar has not enacted real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls for invoices. E-invoices must comply with general record-keeping rules and be retained for at least 6 years; customs declarations are fully electronic and are lodged via the UK/Gibraltar CHIEF or Customs Declaration Service (CDS) through approved customs software, with each export consignment obtaining a Movement Reference Number (MRN) before physical departure. There is no API-based fiscal seal, no tax-authority pre-clearance of invoices, and no national digital clearance of POS receipts.

Where export zero-rating, customs duty relief or territorial tax treatment is claimed, revenue authorities may conduct audit scrutiny and retrospective clawback. Customs penalties: forfeiture of goods and financial penalties of up to three times the value of the goods or GBP 10,000 (whichever is greater) for false or fraudulent export declarations. Tax penalties: up to 200% of the under-declared tax for negligent or fraudulent returns, plus interest, with reassessment periods of up to 6 years (unlimited for fraud). An incorrect attestation that the ELP's substantive operations occur outside Gibraltar can result in loss of pass-through status, revocation or suspension of the GRN/EORI, disqualification of partners or directors, and criminal prosecution under Gibraltar's proceeds-of-crime and anti-money-laundering legislation, carrying imprisonment of up to 10 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for both current and capital account proceeds in the United Kingdom and Gibraltar. No foreign exchange controls are applied to inbound investment, dividend, or profit remittances. Non-resident partners may freely repatriate distributable profits and capital without quantitative ceilings, subject to standard AML/KYC, economic substance documentation, and ongoing tax filing requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. UK and Gibraltar law impose no statutory obligation to surrender, sell, or convert export receipts into domestic currency. A Gibraltar ELP may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency accounts and use them directly for operational disbursements without prior Central Bank approval.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England / HM Treasury (for monetary and exchange control policy); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the Gibraltar-licensed financial services and banking regulator

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

High feasibility for GFSC-licensed electronic money institutions, which offer fast onboarding for low-value operational flows and full AML/CFT compliance. Medium feasibility for UK FCA-authorised EMIs providing cross-border services, only if they obtain a Gibraltar e-money registration or equivalent local approval. Nil viability for unregulated offshore digital EMIs; their use is prohibited for treasury operations in Gibraltar.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% standard Gibraltar corporate rate under s.5 Income Tax Act 2010, charged only on profits arising in Gibraltar; the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) is fiscally transparent so the charge is levied on each partner individually - corporate partners at 12.5%, individual partners under progressive personal bands with a top marginal rate of 35%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Gibraltar imposes no statutory withholding tax on dividends or partnership profit distributions; an ELP distribution to non-resident partners is paid gross, and a Gibraltar company distributing dividend income to its shareholders likewise incurs 0% WHT under the Income Tax Act 2010.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous Local File documentation applies where aggregate controlled transactions with connected non-resident persons exceed GBP 500,000 (approximately EUR 580,000) in a year of assessment; Master File and Country-by-Country Reporting obligations apply only to multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least EUR 750 million (approximately GBP 645 million), adopting the OECD BEPS Action 13 threshold via the Income Tax (Transfer Pricing) Regulations 2019.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar does not maintain a separate Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations code; however, substance obligations bind the ELP through four channels: (i) the territorial chargeability rules of the Income Tax Act 2010 which require that management and control driving Gibraltar-source income remain in Gibraltar; (ii) the UK-Gibraltar DTA's Principal Purpose Test requiring genuine business purpose for treaty access; (iii) OECD BEPS Action 5 and the EU Code of Conduct Group commitments requiring real economic activity for preferential or zero-tax outcomes; and (iv) the Limited Partnerships Act 2014 requiring a general partner with a real registered office in Gibraltar. For the overseas hotpot chain, the general partner must maintain credible minutes, staffing and premises capabilities in Gibraltar for ownership functions, while the restaurant's daily management, hiring, purchasing and point-of-sale treasury must be documented in the foreign operating country to avoid a deemed Gibraltar permanent establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Deliberate Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 100% of the under-assessed tax, statutory interest at the prescribed rate, restitution of all tax evaded, and criminal prosecution under the Crimes Act 2011 with imprisonment up to 7 years and an unlimited fine; confiscation orders may extend to the restaurant chain's overseas assets.

Director personal liability: The general partner is jointly and severally liable for the entire tax, penalty and interest; a limited partner who takes part in management loses the limited liability shield and becomes personally liable for partnership debts; Gibraltar courts will pierce the ELP where it is a mere façade, and travel bans and director disqualification orders lasting up to 15 years may be imposed.
Shell Company - Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may disregard the ELP structure, issue a default assessment on the general partner for deemed Gibraltar-source profits, deny UK-Gibraltar DTA relief, and apply penalties up to 100% of the assessed amount; the Registrar may strike the ELP off under the Companies Act 2014 for non-compliance with registered office or filing obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner face joint and several liability for unpaid corporate assessments, civil restitution of treaty benefits wrongfully obtained, and disqualification from acting as a director or general partner for up to 15 years; persistent abuse can trigger criminal charges for fraudulent trading with imprisonment up to 7 years.
AML-CFT Deficiencies (Proceeds of Crime Act 2015)

Statutory sanction: Failure to disclose a suspicious transaction is punishable by imprisonment up to 5 years and an unlimited fine; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission may impose regulatory penalties up to GBP 1 million and revoke the ELP's good-standing status; confiscation orders may attach to the restaurant's overseas revenues tracing into Gibraltar.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors are criminally liable for tipping-off and for consent offences; the court may issue travel bans, worldwide asset freezing orders and confiscation orders against the individuals; deliberate AML facilitation can sustain prolonged custodial sentences and permanent exclusion from regulated activity.
Transfer Pricing and Disclosure Failure

Statutory sanction: A primary transfer pricing adjustment restoring the arm's length profit, a secondary adjustment recharacterising the excess as a deemed contribution or distribution, penalties up to 50% of the adjustment, and statutory interest on the deficiency; the host jurisdiction may also impose its own penalties, creating double liability where no mutual agreement procedure is invoked.

Director personal liability: The general partner is personally liable for penalties arising from the partnership's TP default; directors who approved the pricing policy may be subject to misfeasance claims, disqualification, and civil restitution; tax treaty relief and the UK-Gibraltar MAP may be denied where the arrangement breaches the Principal Purpose Test.
FATCA-CRS and Reporting Default

Statutory sanction: Penalties aligned with the international standards framework, including daily fines for non-filing, monetary penalties per unreported account, and suspension of the ELP's tax clearance certificate; the foreign partner's home tax authority receives exchanged data and may launch independent evasion proceedings.

Director personal liability: The general partner and the appointed reporting officer face personal fines for each failure to report; continued default can trigger a criminal penalty of imprisonment up to 2 years and travel restrictions while tax enforcement investigations are pending.
