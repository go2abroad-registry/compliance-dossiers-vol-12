Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar - Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax) under the Income Tax Act 2010 and Taxes (Administration) Act; Registrar of Companies (Gibraltar Companies Registry) for ELP formation; HM Customs & Excise Department for customs and excise matters. Primary portals: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi and the Income Tax Office e-services portal; the Companies Registry online filing system for annual statements and statutory filings.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Limited Partnerships Act 2020, filing the Statement of Particulars (name, registered office in Gibraltar, general partner details, exempt status election) and receiving a Companies Registry registration number. Step 2: File the ELP's particulars with the Income Tax Office and appoint a resident principal/finance officer; the Income Tax Office issues an Income Tax Reference Number covering all partnership and partner filings. Step 3: If the ELP imports or exports dutiable goods, register separately with HM Customs & Excise as a declarant to obtain a customs account/declaration ID. Step 4: No VAT identification number is required because Gibraltar levies no general VAT/GST. Step 5: For banking onboarding with underwriter wire or Wise payment rails, the Gibraltar-licensed bank will request the Companies Registry certificate, the ELP registration confirmation, and a CRS/FATCA self-certification for each partner in lieu of a domestic TIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope; Tax_Exempt_Zone. Under Part XXII Income Tax Act 2010, an ELP is fiscally transparent: each partner is assessed on their proportionate share of Gibraltar-source income. Non-resident partners deriving no Gibraltar-source income have no Gibraltar tax liability. A non-resident ELP may obtain 'exempt' status, placing it in the Tax_Exempt_Zone category with respect to income arising outside Gibraltar.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a U.S.-style check-the-box regime. Statutory elections include: (i) an election under s.15 Income Tax Act 2010 for the ELP to be assessed as a company where all partners are non-resident, providing corporate-rate certainty; (ii) an exempt-ELP status election filed with the Registrar, requiring an annual declaration of non-residence and absence of Gibraltar-source income; (iii) a transparent-treatment election obliging partners to file Form T1 schedules including their distributive share; (iv) hybrid mismatch declarations under Gibraltar's OECD BEPS Action 2 implementation, notifying the Income Tax Office of any deduction/non-inclusion mismatches and any UK/HMRC coordination; and (v), for multinational groups, elections regarding the Pillar Two Income Inclusion Rule and Undertaxed Profits Rule as adopted in Gibraltar's Taxation (International Tax Compliance) framework.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST or sales and use tax in Gibraltar, which is outside the EU VAT area; indirect taxation is limited to customs and excise duties under the Import Duties Act 2020 and Customs and Excise Acts (e.g., alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oils). Marine insurance surveying services exported to foreign underwriters are outside the Gibraltar indirect tax base.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST is in force and no economic turnover threshold triggers indirect tax registration in Gibraltar. For customs purposes, any importer/exporter of controlled or excisable goods must register with HM Customs & Excise as an approved declarant; there is no minimum turnover threshold. The Gibraltar pound (GI£) is at parity with GBP.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT/GST, so no 0% export VAT rating applies; marine hull and cargo damage survey services rendered to non-resident underwriters fall outside Gibraltar indirect taxation entirely, and the income is not subject to Gibraltar income tax unless attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment or source. For goods physically exported from Gibraltar, zero customs duty applies under the Import Duties Act 2020, provided the export declaration is filed and the goods exit the jurisdiction within statutory limits. Substantiation requires documentary proof that the principal is non-resident, the engagement relates to an insured vessel/cargo with a foreign insured risk, and the consideration is not borne by a Gibraltar-resident establishment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD/C88 filing accepted by HM Customs & Excise) for goods movements
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent ocean transport document evidencing the voyage, cargo, and final discharge port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/Wise transfer advice showing the foreign underwriter's remittance in a foreign currency

No real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance API, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping regime applies in Gibraltar for services. Electronic customs declarations (SAD/C88) must be filed through HM Customs & Excise's computerised clearance system before goods are released; no operator/API e-invoicing mandate applies to B2B marine surveying supplies.

Under the Income Tax Act 2010, incorrect or fraudulent partnership returns attract interest at the statutory rate plus a penalty of up to 100% of the under-assessed tax; the extended assessment window is six years for neglect and unlimited for fraud. False claims of exempt-ELP or non-residence status permit the Commissioner to withdraw exempt status, claw back Gibraltar-source income for the full extended period, and impose daily penalties for non-filing. Abusive zero-rating or customs export misdeclaration is prosecutable under the Customs and Excise Acts with seizure of goods, a civil penalty of up to three times the goods' value or GI£3,000, and imprisonment up to two years. In addition, the UK-Gibraltar Exchange of Notes and automatic exchange of information protocol allows HMRC to receive details of the ELP and partners, exposing parallel UK anti-avoidance reviews and Potential Pillar Two adjustable measures for groups in scope.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account. Gibraltar maintains no exchange-control legislation, no prior-approval requirement for capital inflows or outflows, and no restriction on profit remittance or capital repatriation for an Exempted Limited Partnership. The GIP is freely exchangeable with GBP at parity, and multi-currency USD/EUR balances are routinely available. Practical friction is limited to correspondent-bank clearing for USD and enhanced due diligence on the ultimate beneficial owners; there is no regulatory FX rationing, no repatriation tax, and no withholding tax on ELP distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Gibraltar law imposes no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender obligation with respect to export receipts. Marine surveying fees may be retained in any freely convertible currency. The only binding disciplines are the record-keeping and suspicious-transaction reporting requirements of the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, and the obligation to ensure that receipts reconcile to the underlying survey contract and commercial invoice.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) — the prudential and conduct regulator for all licensed banks and financial services in Gibraltar. There is no separate Gibraltar central bank; monetary issuance of the Gibraltar pound (GIP) rests with HM Government of Gibraltar through the Commissioner of Currency, while monetary stability is anchored by the Bank of England through the sterling parity arrangement (GIP pegged 1:1 to GBP). UK HM Treasury sanctions and AML frameworks apply to Gibraltar as a British Overseas Territory, with local implementation by the GFSC and the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit (GFIU).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High feasibility — Wise (the Target payment rail; an FCA-authorised Electronic Money Institution, FRN 900510) onboards Gibraltar-registered ELPs fully remotely. Legal constraints: Wise is not a bank, does not lend, and client funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011 without deposit-insurance cover. The ELP must pass beneficial-ownership verification and Wise prohibits sanctioned marine counterparties. Wise Business supplies a UK sort code, EU IBAN, and multi-currency balances (GBP/EUR/USD) with local ACH, FPS, and SEPA clearing, which is operationally optimal for a Low_Risk_Standard marine surveying fee-collection profile.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% (Gibraltar companies and partnerships are taxed at a standard corporate income tax rate of 0% under Gibraltar's Income Tax Act 2010, as amended; no branch profits tax is levied on non-resident entities carrying on business through a Gibraltar permanent establishment unless distributed as dividends to a Gibraltar corporate shareholder).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (Gibraltar does not impose a dividend withholding tax on distributions made by a Gibraltar company or partnership to residents or non-residents, and there is no Gibraltar withholding tax on partnership profit distributions or allocations to partners).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No specific Gibraltar statutory transfer pricing threshold in local currency is prescribed under the Income Tax Act 2010; however, the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines apply to connected party transactions where the transaction exceeds GIP 250,000 in aggregate annually for local file preparation. A formal Local File and Master File are required where the Gibraltar entity or ELP has related party transactions above GIP 250,000 (approx. USD 335,000) and either (a) the Gibraltar operations carry on a taxable activity, or (b) the transactions involve a jurisdiction with a preferential tax regime. For an Exempted Limited Partnership carrying on marine insurance survey services with no Gibraltar tax liability, the ITO expects contemporaneous documentation in accordance with OECD BEPS Action 13 principles when cross-border transactions exceed GIP 1,000,000, even though penalties are only enforceable where a taxable profit is understated.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's Economic Substance regulations are found in the Taxes (Economic Substance) Regulations 2020 and apply to entities that carry on relevant activities, which include distribution and service centre businesses but not expressly marine survey services; nevertheless, a marine insurance surveying ELP that performs claims inspection and risk advisory services for group companies may be characterized as providing 'headquarters services' or 'service centre' activities, requiring economic substance in Gibraltar. The test requires that (a) the entity is directed and managed in Gibraltar, (b) core income-generating activities are performed in Gibraltar by adequate employees, (c) there is adequate physical presence and expenditure proportionate to the level of activity, and (d) records are kept of the substance analysis. For an Exempted Limited Partnership that is tax transparent with all partners outside Gibraltar, the substance assessment follows the partners' substance; however, if the ELP has a Gibraltar permanent establishment or files a return claiming full exemption for non-residents, it must nonetheless file an Economic Substance return annually to the Commissioner of Income Tax in Gibraltar. Failure to meet substance requirements triggers a £50,000 financial penalty, possible annual penalties of £50,000 for continued failure, and the Commissioner may make a notification to the Registrar to strike the entity off the register.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and deliberate suppression of taxable income

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 and the Criminal Procedure Act, tax evasion is punishable upon conviction by a fine of up to £10,000 per offence and imprisonment for up to 7 years; the ITO may also determine tax on a best-of-judgment basis and add fines of up to 200% of the evaded tax, plus interest at 8% per annum. In addition, any false statement in a return can lead to a penalty not exceeding £5,000.

Director personal liability: Courts in Gibraltar will pierce the corporate veil of an Exempted Limited Partnership where the general partner has acted fraudulently or used the partnership as a sham; the general partner and any participating limited partner who knowingly made false returns are jointly and severally liable for the full tax underpaid, interest, and penalties. The Comptroller may issue a certificate of unpaid tax which is enforceable personally, and the Supreme Court of Gibraltar may impose a travel prevention order against partners suspected of departing Gibraltar without settling liabilities, and imprisonment may be ordered for contempt or for directors of the general partner who fail to comply with a restoration or winding-up order.
Shell company and economic substance violation

Statutory sanction: Under the Taxes (Economic Substance) Regulations 2020, the Commissioner may impose a financial penalty of £50,000 for failure to meet the economic substance test, plus £50,000 for each successive period of non-compliance, and may initiate a strike-off under the Limited Partnerships Act 2010 or Companies Act regulations. If the ELP is considered to have misleadingly claimed exempt status, the ITO may assess profits on the taxable basis of a Gibraltar-resident partnership and impose a penalty of up to 100% of the tax that would have been due. The Registrar may also strike the partnership from the register, freezing its ability to issue legally effective survey reports or to hold insurance contracts.

Director personal liability: The general partner and its directors are personally liable for economic substance penalties if they signed the annual substance declaration knowing it to be false; the Supreme Court may order disqualification of directors for up to 15 years, require personal restitution of any fees or distributions received, and issue a certificate to the Financial Intelligence Unit for money laundering proceedings where the shell structure was used to disguise the location of value.
Anti-money laundering and licensing failures

Statutory sanction: Under the Financial Services Act 2019, carrying on a regulated activity without a licence is a criminal offence punishable by a fine not exceeding £200,000 and/or imprisonment up to 2 years. The GFSC may impose a public statement, financial penalty of up to £500,000 for serious AML failings, and require an independent audit under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015. Each transaction processed without AML clearance may be treated as a separate offence, and the court may order confiscation of the proceeds under the Proceeds of Crime Act, including the full fees collected from marine insurers.

Director personal liability: Senior management and general partners who consent to, or connive in, unlicensed activity or AML contraventions are personally criminally liable and may be imprisoned for up to 2 years; the GFSC may issue prohibition orders banning them from any financial services role for up to 10 years; civil liability may extend to restitution to insurers for losses caused by negligent survey reports issued without regulatory oversight.
Inaccurate filing and failure to maintain records

Statutory sanction: The ITO may assess the ELP on a deemed profit basis at the standard company rate where the ELP would otherwise be taxable, impose a late filing penalty of £100 plus daily penalties up to £300, and where records are inadequate the penalty is up to £3,000 plus an additional £50 per day for continuing failure. Interest at 8% per annum is imposed on any resulting tax from the date the tax should have been paid.

Director personal liability: The general partner is personally liable for penalties to the extent the failure results from the general partner's default; if the record-keeping failure materially impedes a tax investigation, the court may order the disqualification of the general partner and grant a freezing injunction over personal assets; in aggravated cases of deliberate concealment, directors may face a term of imprisonment up to 2 years.
