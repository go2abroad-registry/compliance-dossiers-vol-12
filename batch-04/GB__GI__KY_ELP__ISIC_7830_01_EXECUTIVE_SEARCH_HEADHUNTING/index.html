Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership; capital contributions are fixed contractually in the Limited Partnership Agreement, with no escrow account requirement at the Companies Registry; however, the general partner must maintain a capital contribution schedule and provide verified source-of-funds documentation to the licensed registered agent for AML/CFT purposes.

Funding & unlock path: Fire and Building Safety Certificate issued by the Gibraltar Fire & Rescue Service and Building Authority certifying means of escape, emergency lighting and structural load compliance; plus an Occupational Health and Workplace Clearance under the Occupational Safety & Health Act (Cap. 155) covering first aid, sanitation, fire drills and employer's liability insurance.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar; official taxation portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation (Income Tax e-Services and e-Return filing); corporate registry: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/companies-house.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Before commencing regulated executive-search activities the ELP must obtain a statutory Employment Agency Licence from the Gibraltar Department of Employment, and any payroll-related activity requires registration with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office as an employer. Step 2: Appoint a Gibraltar-resident tax representative (a licensed accountant, lawyer, or tax agent) and register the foreign ELP's branch/representative office with the Companies House Gibraltar or the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission where fund or regulated activity is involved. Step 3: Submit the 'Application for Registration of a Person Chargeable to Tax' (Income Tax Act 2010, as amended) together with the ELP's Cayman certificate of registration as an exempted limited partnership, the consolidated partnership agreement, register of limited partners, certificates of incorporation and good standing, and customer due diligence/passport and tax-residence evidence for all general and limited partners. Step 4: The Income Tax Office issues the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) for the ELP and separate TINs are required for each individual/corporate partner only when they become separately assessable; the ELP must then file an annual partnership return, and each partner must file an individual/corporate return for the Gibraltar-source share of profits. Step 5: Where the ELP is not managed or controlled in Gibraltar and derives no Gibraltar-source income, it may instead file a non-resident nil-return, but registration is still compulsory once a dependent agent, office, or branch is maintained.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under Gibraltar law; partnership transparency is mandatory. The Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, as amended by ATAD-implementation instruments (Income Tax (Hybrid and Mismatch) Regulations), automatically recharacterises a transparent ELP where the partner's residence jurisdiction treats it as opaque, thereby neutralising hybrid mismatches. The only elective mechanics are the standard 'aggregation basis' election for computing partnership income at the ELP level and the option of the partners to be separately assessed on their allotted shares; no entity-level taxation election, tax-group election, or 12.5% withholding-collection election is available to an ELP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/general sales tax in Gibraltar; Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area. The only indirect taxes are customs duties and excise duties on controlled goods, administered under the Customs Ordinance and Excise Ordinances by HM Customs Gibraltar.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no turnover threshold exists because no VAT/GST regime is in force. For goods movements, every importer/exporter must obtain Gibraltar Customs registration and operate through a licensed customs agent; excise duty registration is required only for activities involving alcohol, tobacco, manufactured products, and fuel, with no monetary de minimis threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - there is no VAT/GST zero-rating mechanism in Gibraltar because there is no VAT/GST to charge. Cross-border executive-search and headhunting services (ISIC 7830) supplied by the ELP to non-Gibraltar clients are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation and attract zero indirect tax; goods exported from Gibraltar are relieved from customs and excise duty through export declarations and warehousing/re-export relief, not through an input-tax refund system. If the client is in the United Kingdom, the ELP must separately consider UK VAT place-of-supply rules, but no Gibraltar VAT registration or UK VAT registration is triggered by the Gibraltar passport structure alone.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar electronic export declaration filed by the licensed customs agent)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Gibraltar
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and the underlying executive-search retainer/service agreement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (banking evidence of collection of consideration from the non-Gibraltar client)

No real-time electronic invoicing clearance, API-based fiscal reporting, or digital stamping of invoices is mandated; standard commercial e-invoices are accepted. Customs clearance is digital and mandatory: pre-lodgment of electronic export/import declarations via licensed agents in the Gibraltar Customs automated environment, with Common Transit Convention/NCTS integration for goods transiting the EU/UK, and electronically tracked duty-stamps for excise goods.

No VAT clawback regime exists; however, false export declarations or misuse of customs/excise reliefs trigger penalties under the Customs Ordinance, including forfeiture of goods, fines of up to three times the duty, and retrospective reassessment of reliefs for up to six years. In the income-tax sphere, a fraudulent claim of non-residence or deliberate understatement of Gibraltar-source profits attracts penalty tax of up to 100% of the under-assessed tax, statutory interest, and criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act; forged remittance or customs documentation may further expose partners to the proceeds of crime and anti-money laundering enforcement, asset freezing, and confiscation orders in the Gibraltar courts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full and unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. GIP is pegged at parity (1:1) to GBP, and sterling is legal tender in Gibraltar. There are no exchange controls, no requirement for central bank approval on capital movements, no profit remittance authorisation hurdle, and no differentiation between current account and capital account treatment. Non-resident partners of an Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) may receive profit distributions free of Gibraltar withholding tax; the ELP is tax-transparent and the partners' residence jurisdictions apply their own rules. Because GIP is fully backed by GBP and freely convertible, there is no convertibility risk or surrender obligation. The only practical constraint is that GIP banknotes are not accepted by UK clearing systems, so cross-border payments are normally executed in GBP, EUR, or USD from a Gibraltar bank account via SWIFT.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Gibraltar imposes no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts or income derived from cross-border services, including executive search and headhunting retainer fees. Proceeds may be retained in the currency of receipt, held in foreign currency accounts, or converted freely at commercial bank rates. No surrender to the monetary authority is required.

Central bank supervision: Government of Gibraltar acting through its Currency Board mechanism, with day-to-day financial services supervision exercised by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC). The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued against 100% backing by Bank of England (BoE) pound sterling notes; consequently the Bank of England operates as the de facto monetary anchor, although direct prudential regulation of Gibraltar-licensed banks is administered by the GFSC under the Financial Services Act 2019 (Gibraltar).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High with legal constraints. Wise (formerly TransferWise) B2B and similar UK-authorized EMIs (e.g., ClearPay Finance, Modulr, or Airwallex) provide efficient GBP/EUR/USD collection and international disbursement rails at materially lower cost than SWIFT, suitable for receiving retainer wires from UK, EU, and US corporate clients. Legal constraints: (i) Wise is authorized by the UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) as an Electronic Money Institution, not a bank, so funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts rather than covered by the Gibraltar Deposit Guarantee Scheme; (ii) the ELP must be onboarded through the EMI's UK-based KYC process, which generally accepts Gibraltar-licensed entities if the Gibraltar registered agent and beneficial owner documentation satisfy the FCA's source-of-funds rules; (iii) an EMI cannot issue commercial letters of credit or provide overdraft facilities, so all committed capital balances and emergency liquidity must be held with a GFSC-licensed bank; (iv) Wise B2B does not accept GIP-denominated balances, thus all flows must be designated in GBP, EUR, or USD. Recommended structure: bank account at First Bank Gibraltar Limited serving as the treasury hub, with Wise Business (or Wise B2B) as a secondary collection/disbursement rail to optimise FX conversion and reduce correspondent fees.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for partner capital contributions into the ELP's Capital Escrow Account at First Bank Gibraltar Limited: wire must be sent via SWIFT MT103 (cover MT202 for back-value) from the partner's personal or corporate bank account using the full ELP legal name and the dedicated escrow IBAN (Gibraltar uses GB-prefixed IBANs under UK ISO country code conventions; the bank BIC is specific to First Bank Gibraltar). Purpose/reference field must state 'ELP Capital Contribution - [Partner Name] - no commercial invoice' to align with GFSC AML guidance on source-of-funds verification. The beneficial owner must provide an underlying source-of-funds statement, including recent bank statements, dividend vouchers, sale agreement, or inheritance documentation. The bank will apply the Beneficial Ownership Register (PSC) check and, once cleared, credit the funds to the escrow ledger. From the escrow account, funds are swept to the Operational Account only upon execution of an authorised partner resolution. Recommended routing corridors: (i) from UK: CHAPS or Faster Payments to the bank's UK settlement account; (ii) from EU: SEPA direct credit or SWIFT MT103 in EUR; (iii) from US/HK/SG: SWIFT MT103 in USD/USD via corresponding banks (e.g., Bank of New York Mellon or Citi) -- all inbound wires must include the ELP tax identification reference (Gibraltar GRN) to avoid rejection under the Gibraltar Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 reporting rules.Clearing rails for distributions, supplier payments, and client refunds: (i) standard retainer-fund disbursements to UK/EU suppliers and freelancers via SWIFT MT103 (or SEPA direct debit/credit for EU beneficiaries) with reference 'Headhunting service invoice [Invoice Number]'; (ii) commercial settlement of search fees to partner firms or sub-agents may be executed through the same MT103 rails or, where the beneficiary requires assurance of payment prior to candidate introduction, by issuing an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (confirmed by a UK/EU correspondent bank) from First Bank Gibraltar Limited's trade finance desk, although standby LC use is rare for executive search retainers and is typically restricted to large contingency-fee mandates; (iii) profit distributions to partners are executed via MT103 using the purpose 'ELP profit distribution - [Partner Name] - tax-transparent pass-through' with the bank generating an automatic CRS/FATCA reporting event; (iv) all FX conversions are executed at the bank's prevailing mid-market rate plus a transparent spread; (v) Wise B2B rail is used for sub-USD 50,000 payments to beneficiaries in low-risk jurisdictions to reduce cost; E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High with legal constraints. Wise (formerly TransferWise) B2B and similar UK-authorized EMIs (e.g., ClearPay Finance, Modulr, or Airwallex) provide efficient GBP/EUR/USD collection and international disbursement rails at materially lower cost than SWIFT, suitable for receiving retainer wires from UK, EU, and US corporate clients. Legal constraints: (i) Wise is authorized by the UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) as an Electronic Money Institution, not a bank, so funds are safeguarded in segregated accounts rather than covered by the Gibraltar Deposit Guarantee Scheme; (ii) the ELP must be onboarded through the EMI's UK-based KYC process, which generally accepts Gibraltar-licensed entities if the Gibraltar registered agent and beneficial owner documentation satisfy the FCA's source-of-funds rules; (iii) an EMI cannot issue commercial letters of credit or provide overdraft facilities, so all committed capital balances and emergency liquidity must be held with a GFSC-licensed bank; (iv) Wise B2B does not accept GIP-denominated balances, thus all flows must be designated in GBP, EUR, or USD. Recommended structure: bank account at First Bank Gibraltar Limited serving as the treasury hub, with Wise Business (or Wise B2B) as a secondary collection/disbursement rail to optimise FX conversion and reduce correspondent fees.
