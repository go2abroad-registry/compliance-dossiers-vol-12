Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax) under the Ministry of Finance; official portal is the Gibraltar Government eServices portal at www.gibraltar.gov.gi. HM Customs (Gibraltar) administers customs and excise matters.

E-government filing portal

For the Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership ('ELP'): (1) incorporate or register at the Gibraltar Companies Registry to obtain the company/partnership registration number; (2) submit an entity tax registration application to the Income Tax Office on the prescribed form, together with the partnership deed/LPA and identification of all general and limited partners; (3) obtain the Gibraltar tax reference number issued by the Commissioner. Since Gibraltar has no VAT/GST, no separate VAT identification is acquired. Non-resident partners are not required to register with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office unless Gibraltar-source income arises; if registration is triggered, Form IT registration/TIN issuance follows. An AI API platform operated wholly offshore can remain unregistered for Gibraltar income tax until a filing obligation is triggered.

Each partner's share of income will be classified under a strict territorial scope, and a non-resident partner is taxable only on income sourced in Gibraltar. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The ELP is fiscally transparent; it is not an entity-level tax taxpayer and no Gibraltar income tax is assessed on the partnership itself. Each partner's share of income will be classified under a strict territorial scope, and a non-resident partner is taxable only on income sourced in Gibraltar. For offshore AI/LLM API revenues with no Gibraltar permanent establishment, no Gibraltar income tax applies.

Available election forms

None at domestic level. Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box election and the ELP's pass-through status is mandatory. Foreign partners may make US federal check-the-box elections (Form 8832) or equivalent in their residence jurisdictions for foreign reporting, but such elections do not alter Gibraltar domestic tax outcomes. No Gibraltar hybrid mismatch election is available; OECD/UK anti-hybrid rules may apply where the transparent/non-transparent classification mismatch creates double non-taxation or excess deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST or general Sales and Use Tax regime; Gibraltar applies limited indirect taxation through customs import duties and excise duties on specified goods only.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable on an economy-wide basis. No VAT/GST turnover threshold exists in Gibraltar. Persons importing or dealing in excise-controlled goods, such as tobacco, alcohol, or fuel, may require a customs/excise registration in Gibraltar pounds (GIP) irrespective of turnover. Pure AI API service revenue creates no such obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST, there is no zero-rated supply mechanism, no export VAT refund, and no requirement to substantiate zero-rating for cross-border AI API services. Offshore service receipts are outside the Gibraltar indirect tax net and, under the ELP territorial tax structure, outside Gibraltar income tax absent Gibraltar-source nexus. A customer in a VAT jurisdiction may owe reverse-charge VAT locally.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Gibraltar Single Administrative Document (SAD), required only for physical export of goods from Gibraltar; not required for AI API service supply.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, as transport evidence where physical goods are exported; not required for pure digital services.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank payment confirmation, supporting export proceeds repatriation and financial audit trail if goods are exported.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, digital stamping, or PEPPOL-style e-invoicing mandate applies to Gibraltar domestic invoicing. Standard electronic invoices are legally acceptable for record-keeping. If the ELP or a connected UK establishment registers for UK VAT separately, UK Making Tax Digital and HMRC electronic filing/API rules will apply to that registration.

No VAT clawback risk exists because no VAT zero-rating refund is obtainable. However, false customs declarations to obtain duty relief or export drawback are subject to HM Customs (Gibraltar) penalties, including forfeiture of goods, a penal duty not exceeding three times the value of the goods, and criminal liability for fraudulent evasion. For income tax, deliberate failure to disclose taxable Gibraltar-source profits risks penalties up to 100% of the tax and retrospective assessments under the Income Tax Act. Offshore AI API operators must maintain contemporaneous substance and non-Gibraltar source evidence to rebut permanent establishment or source claims.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange control restrictions on the repatriation of profits, dividends, or capital. The GIP is freely exchangeable with GBP at a 1:1 ratio, and foreign currency accounts are permitted. Profit remittance hurdles are minimal, subject only to standard corporate documentation and tax clearance from the Gibraltar Income Tax Office.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirements exist. Exporters are not obligated to convert foreign currency receipts into GIP or GBP within any timeline. All export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts or freely converted at market rates.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) in coordination with the Government of Gibraltar's Ministry of Finance; the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued by the Government of Gibraltar and pegged at par to the British pound sterling (GBP).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High. Gibraltar has a progressive fintech regime under the Financial Services Act 2019, allowing Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) to be licensed by the GFSC. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., those based in the UK or EU) may operate in Gibraltar under passporting or local licensing, but must comply with GFSC AML/CFT regulations. For an AI API platform with low-risk profile, a licensed EMI can provide efficient cross-border payment rails, but full banking services (e.g., lending) are not available. Legal constraints include mandatory licensing, capital requirements (€350,000 for EMIs), and robust AML/KYC procedures.

Late filing: fixed penalty of GIP 100, increased to GIP 200 after six months, plus GIP 10 per day after twelve months. Tax-geared penalty up to 100% of the underpaid tax for deliberate understatement; interest charged at 5% above the Bank of England base rate.
Box 15 – Loss relief brought forward Enter qualifying trading losses carried forward from prior years; capped at the 5-year statutory limitation period. A non-resident corporate partner may utilise carried-forward losses only to the extent that the losses are attributable to Gibraltar-source activity. Late filing: fixed penalty of GIP 100, increased to GIP 200 after six months, plus GIP 10 per day after twelve months. Tax-geared penalty up to 100% of the underpaid tax for deliberate understatement; interest charged at 5% above the Bank of England base rate.
Box 20 – Allocation of taxable income to partners Allocate the adjusted taxable profit among general and limited partners in accordance with the partnership agreement. Tax is charged on each non-resident partner's share only if the profit is derived through a Gibraltar permanent establishment or from a Gibraltar source; passive portfolio income is generally not Gibraltar-source. Late filing: fixed penalty of GIP 100, increased to GIP 200 after six months, plus GIP 10 per day after twelve months. Tax-geared penalty up to 100% of the underpaid tax for deliberate understatement; interest charged at 5% above the Bank of England base rate.
Box 24 – Foreign tax credits and treaty relief Claim unilateral foreign tax credits or credits under an applicable double taxation agreement. Attach a duly completed DTA claim schedule and a valid Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) from the foreign tax authority. Late filing: fixed penalty of GIP 100, increased to GIP 200 after six months, plus GIP 10 per day after twelve months. Tax-geared penalty up to 100% of the underpaid tax for deliberate understatement; interest charged at 5% above the Bank of England base rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required where aggregate related-party controlled transactions exceed GIP 1,000,000 in a fiscal year. Master File and Country-by-Country Report required where the consolidated group revenue is at least GIP 750,000,000. Simplified documentation available under the OECD Low-Value Intra-Group Services approach.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Regulations 2019 apply to Gibraltarian tax-resident ELPs carrying on relevant activities, including IP holding, distribution and service centres, and financing. A pure equity-holding ELP is subject to a reduced substance test. The ELP must be directed and managed in Gibraltar, have physical premises and adequate employees in Gibraltar, and perform core income-generating activities in Gibraltar. A non-resident ELP that is not tax resident in Gibraltar is outside the ESR but must not misrepresent its tax residence to avoid the substance requirements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2010, a civil penalty of up to 150% of the tax evaded plus interest; criminal liability under the Crimes Act 2011 includes fines at the statutory maximum and imprisonment for up to seven years on conviction on indictment.

Director personal liability: The ELP general partner and shadow directors are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of evaded tax; courts may impose travel bans, asset-freezing orders, director disqualification for up to fifteen years, and custodial sentences.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Regulations 2019: initial penalty of GIP 50,000; daily penalty of GIP 1,000 for continued default; annual default penalty up to GIP 200,000; strike-off and winding-up for non-compliance over two consecutive years.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any director knowingly participating in the shell arrangement may be ordered to pay restitution, disqualified as a director, prohibited from leaving Gibraltar via a travel ban, and liable to imprisonment for up to two years for false declarations.
Anti-Money Laundering / Financial Sanctions

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018: unlimited fines, full-value confiscation orders, and imprisonment for up to fourteen years.

Director personal liability: Directors, managers, and MLROs face personal criminal liability, civil damages, withdrawal of GFSC authorisation, travel bans, and compensation orders in favour of the Crown.
Transfer Pricing / Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment plus interest, a tax-geared penalty of up to 100% of the underpaid tax, and mandatory filing of corrected returns; deliberate misreporting attracts criminal prosecution under the Crimal Justice (Fraud) Act.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a materially misleading return are personally liable for the additional tax and penalties; courts may also issue disqualification orders, travel bans, and imprisonment if the misreporting is part of a fraudulent evasion scheme.
