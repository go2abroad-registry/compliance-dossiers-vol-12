Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax), 140 Giro's Passage, Gibraltar GX11 1AA; electronic filing via the Gibraltar Tax System (GTS) portal accessible from https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/. For UK VAT and customs matters touching the UK VAT territory, the competent authority is HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC), https://www.gov.uk/hmrc.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies / Financial Services Commission under the Gibraltar Limited Partnerships Act (as amended), filing the statement containing the registered office, the General Partner's identity, and the names of limited partners, and paying the registration fee. Step 2: Within 30 days of registration, notify the Income Tax Office and obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) for the partnership and for the General Partner; the General Partner (a Gibraltar-resident individual or Gibraltar-incorporated company) acts as the tax filing contact. Step 3: If the supermarket employs staff in Gibraltar, register as an employer for pay-as-you-earn income tax withholding and Gibraltar social insurance, obtaining the relevant employer TRN and monthly filing obligations. Step 4: Submit annual partnership income tax returns setting out each partner's distributable share; the ELP itself is not taxed on non-Gibraltar-source income, and non-resident partners without a Gibraltar permanent establishment are outside Gibraltar income tax. Step 5: Where goods are imported into or supplied within the UK VAT territory, apply separately to HMRC for UK VAT registration and obtain a UK/XI EORI number for customs clearance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Gibraltar law does not provide a domestic check-the-box election; the ELP is statutorily fiscally transparent, so no local election is required. Partners may rely on their home-jurisdiction passthrough rules (e.g., US IRS Form 8832 check-the-box treatment for a foreign eligible entity, or UK rules treating offshore limited partnerships as transparent), subject to Gibraltar's OECD BEPS Action 2 and ATAD-aligned hybrid mismatch provisions, which may disallow deductions arising from dual classification or deduction/non-inclusion outcomes. No election is available to convert the ELP into a taxable entity for Gibraltar purposes without losing transparency.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar operates no VAT, GST, or sales/use tax. The indirect tax system comprises customs import duty and excise duty (on alcohol, tobacco, and hydrocarbon oils) under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance. UK VAT under the Value Added Tax Act 1994 is applicable only to supplies, imports, and exports involving the UK VAT territory, as Gibraltar is outside both the EU VAT area and the UK VAT area.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Nil local threshold: Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST registration. For UK VAT, the mandatory registration threshold is GBP 90,000 annual taxable turnover (from 1 April 2024) for UK-established businesses; a non-UK-established ELP has zero threshold and must register before making taxable supplies in the UK, and must obtain a UK EORI number before first customs import or export.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because Gibraltar has no VAT, the ELP cannot claim any Gibraltar VAT zero-rating or refund. For UK VAT purposes, zero-rating under VATA 1994 s.30 and the VAT (Exports) Order applies to (i) direct exports of goods from the UK VAT territory to Gibraltar or other non-UK destinations, provided the seller retains valid commercial evidence of export within the prescribed time limits; (ii) supplies of goods to UK VAT-registered customers where the goods are removed to a non-UK country; and (iii) certain international and offshore services that are outside the scope of UK VAT under the general B2B place-of-supply rule. A claim is sustainable only where title and risk pass to the overseas buyer, the goods are physically dispatched from the UK, and the exporter holds contemporaneous and complete zero-rating evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate (core Gibraltar/UK export evidence pack: Electronic Export Declaration (DUCR/MRN), Bill of Lading or Sea/Air Waybill, and bank SWIFT/MT103 inward remittance certificate evidencing proceeds repatriation)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list signed by the ELP's General Partner
  • Zero-rating certificate or HMRC export evidence worksheet as prescribed by VAT Notice 703
  • Certificate of Origin and, for imported Asian condiments and vegetables, phytosanitary and food-safety certificates where agricultural checks apply
  • Customs broker power of attorney, entry/exit summary declarations, and transit movement reference where goods pass through the UK or EU

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping; the Income Tax Office accepts commercial invoices and accounting records in electronic form. Where UK VAT applies, Making Tax Digital (MTD) for VAT requires MTD-compatible software and digital links but imposes no e-invoicing or real-time clearance requirement. Goods moving into or out of Gibraltar through the UK customs system must be declared electronically via the UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS), with API integration, a Declaration Unique Consignment Reference (DUCR), and, for goods moving under transit or accompanied by ro-ro freight, the Goods Vehicle Movement Service (GVMS); exit confirmation requires a valid Movement Reference Number (MRN).

HMRC may claw back input VAT and impose a penalty up to 100% of the VAT evaded or misdeclared under VATA 1994 ss.60, 66 and 67, reduced only by unprompted disclosure and co-operation, with statutory interest running from the original recovery date. Fraudulent evasion of export VAT is a criminal offence punishable by an unlimited fine and/or up to 7 years' imprisonment under VATA 1994 s.72 and CEMA 1979 s.170, with potential liability extending to the General Partner and corporate officers. In Gibraltar, the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance provides for forfeiture of goods, monetary penalties, and criminal prosecution, and the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar) treats export-refund fraud or associated money laundering as a predicate offence subject to confiscation and civil recovery; retrospective clawback is calculated on the original supply value plus interest and may disqualify the ELP from future simplified compliance arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains a fully convertible current and capital account regime with no blanket exchange controls. The GIP is pegged at par to GBP, and all forms of trade and financial transactions settle freely. Profit remittance is unrestricted, subject only to compliance with anti-money laundering (AML), corporate registry filings and, where applicable, the UK's economic substance requirements. Non-resident partnerships are allowed to repatriate capital and profits without prior Exchange Control approval. The only practical hurdle is documentary proof of source of funds and tax residence clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar does not impose any statutory surrender requirement, mandatory conversion horizon, or minimum settlement ratio for export proceeds. Export receipts, including the proceeds of wholesale or retail sales of imported goods, may be fully retained in any freely convertible currency (USD, EUR, GBP, GIP) without obligation to repatriate or convert within a fixed period. The sole administrative requirement is accurate invoicing and record-keeping for AML/tax reporting.

Central bank supervision: Financial Services Commission of Gibraltar (GFSC) under the Currency of the Government of Gibraltar; monetary policy and currency issuance are structurally aligned with the Bank of England through the 1:1 fixed peg between the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) and British Pound Sterling (GBP).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium. Digital EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise, or GFSC-licensed e-money firms) may be used for ancillary cross-border payments, FX hedging and virtual account receiving, but are not a full substitute for a regulated commercial bank account for a physical retail grocery operation. Legal constraints: (1) EMIs are not licensed for deposit-taking and do not offer sovereign deposit protection; (2) card acquiring for point-of-sale (POS) transactions typically requires an acquirer relationship with a bank or licensed merchant bank; (3) Gibraltar-licensed EMIs must operate under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act and cannot provide credit or an overdraft facility. Relay of funds between the EMI and a prime bank account is permissible under GFSC’s technology-neutral approach, provided the EMI performs KYC on the ultimate beneficial owners.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GBP 1,000,000 aggregate annual cross-border controlled transactions (administrative compliance benchmark; the statutory arm's length rule in Income Tax Act 2010 s.43 applies to all associated-party transactions without any de minimis)

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's Economic Substance Regulations require genuine management, employees, premises, and core income-generating activities in Gibraltar for entities carrying on relevant activities (banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing/leasing, headquarters, holding company, IP, distribution and service centres). Grocery-store retailing and distribution (ISIC 4721) is not a listed relevant activity; nevertheless, the ELP must maintain its physical retail premises, inventory, and operational staff in Gibraltar to protect its tax-transparent character and to counter substance challenges in the partners' home jurisdictions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Assessment of unpaid tax with statutory interest; penalties up to 100% of the under-assessed tax; criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion in the Gibraltar Magistrates' Court and Supreme Court, with unlimited fines and/or imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner may be jointly and severally liable for fraudulent trading and for knowingly assisting tax evasion; personal civil restitution, director disqualification orders, and imprisonment are available.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the arrangement, disallowance of deductions, full assessment on the ELP and its partners, and penalties for submitting misleading nil returns; enhanced audits under the Income Tax Act 2010 and Economic Substance Regulations.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the general partner is a bare nominee; directors face personal civil liability for partnership debts and mandatory compensation orders.
Limited Partner Management Participation

Statutory sanction: Loss of limited liability: the participating limited partner becomes personally liable without limit for all debts and obligations of the ELP incurred during the period of such participation.

Director personal liability: The management-participating limited partner, and any director of the general partner acting on its instructions, is exposed to direct creditor claims and tax authority claims up to the full value of the ELP liabilities.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Criminal offences under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and Gibraltar Money Laundering Regulations: unlimited fines and/or imprisonment on conviction, confiscation proceedings against ELP assets and the store, and suspension or revocation of the retail licence.

Director personal liability: Officers of the general partner who deliberately or negligently fail AML obligations bear personal criminal liability, travel bans requested by the Commissioner or the Financial Intelligence Unit, and asset-freezing orders over personal and corporate accounts.
