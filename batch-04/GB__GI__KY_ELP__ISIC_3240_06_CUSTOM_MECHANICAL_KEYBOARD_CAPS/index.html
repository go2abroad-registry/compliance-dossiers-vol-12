Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax); official portal: https://www.gov.gi/taxation

E-government filing portal

Non-resident entities establishing an Exempted Limited Partnership must first register the ELP with the Gibraltar Companies House / Registrar of Limited Partnerships. The partnership must then file Form TIN-01 (Application for Taxpayer Identification Number) with the Income Tax Office, attaching the Certificate of Limited Partnership Registration, the Exempted Status Certificate, constitutional/partnership agreement, and particulars of general and limited partners. The Income Tax Office issues a Gibraltar TIN in the format GI-TIN-XXXXXXX within 7–10 business days. The same TIN is used for partnership tax documentation, employer/labour obligations, and the customs electronic account. No statutory minimum capital or prior tax approval is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible / Territorial_Scope. The Exempted Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act 2010: the partnership itself is not a taxable person; each partner is separately assessed on their share of Gibraltar-source income. Non-resident partners are taxable solely on profits or gains deriving from Gibraltar, including income from manufacturing, storage, logistics, and sales facilitation carried out in Gibraltar. Foreign-source income is treaty-exempt from Gibraltar taxation. The ELP is not a Tax_Exempt_Zone vehicle; its 'exempted' status relates to legal compliance, not blanket tax exemption.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' system. However, the Income Tax Act 2010 permits an ELP to file a formal election to be treated as a company for tax purposes when all partners are corporate entities; the election, once made, is irrevocable and subjects the ELP to Gibraltar corporation tax at 12.5%. In the absence of such an election, the ELP remains pass-through. Hybrid mismatch elections may be available under Gibraltar’s OECD BEPS Action 2 implementing rules; such elections must be accompanied by Form HM-1 and substantiate a common tax treatment between Gibraltar and the partner’s residence jurisdiction to prevent double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None – No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax applies in Gibraltar. Gibraltar operates no general indirect consumption tax; only customs import duties, excise duties on alcohol/tobacco/hydrocarbons, and certain environmental screeds are imposed.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – Gibraltar levies no VAT/GST/SUT and therefore prescribes no registration threshold. Customs registration for import or export of physical goods is required regardless of value, but no monetary threshold triggers any indirect tax obligation. The local currency used for administrative purposes is the Gibraltar pound (GIP), pegged at par to GBP.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

As Gibraltar has no VAT/GST, no formal 0% export VAT mechanism or capital goods refund scheme exists. For customs and territorial tax purposes, the ELP must prove that manufactured keyboard keycaps physically depart Gibraltar and that the gross turnover from export sales is not attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment/profit motive. The absence of a Gibraltar indirect tax means there is no zero-rating to claim; however, if the buyer’s destination jurisdiction (e.g., UK or EU) grants import VAT relief or zero-rating for goods imported from Gibraltar, the exporter must supply a complete export evidence pack to satisfy that jurisdiction’s rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document or electronic export declaration lodged with Gibraltar Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or House Airway Bill evidencing physical export from Gibraltar terminal or warehouse
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (or SWIFT MT103 bank receipt) evidencing that export proceeds are repatriated to the ELP’s bank account
  • Commercial invoice with HS code 3926.90 / 8471.60, quantity, unit price, Incoterms 2020 and country-of-origin statement
  • Packing list and weight certificate
  • Copy of the ELP partner consent resolution identifying the export transaction for tax allocation purposes

No real-time fiscal clearance, API e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate exists in Gibraltar. Tax invoices must be issued and retained for a minimum of six years for income tax audit purposes, but they require no pre-clearance or government stamping. Customs transactions (import/export declarations) must be lodged through the electronic customs clearance system used by the Gibraltar Customs (currently the long-term Irish/GI custom declaration environment); the submission is electronic but no continuous transaction control API applies.

Export documentation that is false, forged, or materially inflated to claim a VAT zero-rating or territorial tax exemption in Gibraltar or abroad triggers intensive audit. Penalties include reopening tax assessments for up to six years (no time limit where fraud is proved), a tax-geared penalty of up to 200% of the under-assessed income under ss. 153–155 of the Income Tax Act 2010, statutory interest of 8% per annum, and forfeiture of the customs export privilege. Cross-border clawback proceedings by HMRC or EU customs authorities may recover any import VAT or duty refunded/relieved on the strength of the Gibraltar export evidence, plus statutory penalties and interest. Criminal referral for dishonest evasion of tax and money-laundering is applicable where remittance documentation is manufactured.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound is pegged at par to sterling and is freely convertible for ordinary current-account transactions. Gibraltar generally imposes no routine exchange-control approval for import payments, service payments, dividends, partner distributions, or repatriation of legitimate business profits. Capital-account movements are generally unrestricted, but banks may require evidence of source of funds, beneficial ownership, commercial purpose, tax status, and authorization under the partnership agreement. Profit or capital remittances may be delayed or rejected where the bank identifies sanctions exposure, insufficient substance, unexplained third-party funding, tax-evasion indicators, incomplete partnership documentation, or an unverified ultimate beneficial owner. A Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership is partnership-based rather than a conventional share-capital company; capital contributions and distributions should therefore be documented through the limited partnership agreement, contribution records, partner resolutions, and accounting ledgers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar has no general statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement, mandatory conversion ratio, or prescribed deadline requiring exporters to convert foreign-currency receipts into Gibraltar pounds or sterling. Export receipts may generally be retained in the original currency or converted at the account holder's discretion, subject to the bank's account terms, applicable payment-services rules, sanctions controls, tax records, customs documentation, and anti-money-laundering requirements. The business should reconcile each receipt to an invoice, customer, shipment or delivery record, currency, and accounting entry. No automatic exemption should be assumed for regulated payment providers or correspondent banks, which may impose their own settlement, reserve, or conversion requirements.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission, together with HM Government of Gibraltar. Gibraltar has no separate central bank and generally operates without exchange controls. Banks operating in Gibraltar may also be subject to UK regulatory requirements where the relevant institution, service, or payment activity is UK-regulated.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium to Low. An offshore digital EMI may provide payment accounts, safeguarding, card acceptance, or foreign-exchange services but is not a substitute for a Gibraltar commercial bank account and does not provide ordinary bank deposits unless separately licensed as a bank. Availability depends on the EMI's permitted countries, the ELP's registered office and operating substance, the partners' and beneficial owners' residency, supported currencies, payment-processor rules, and whether the EMI accepts Gibraltar entities. Stripe and Shopify Payments eligibility must be confirmed for the exact Gibraltar entity, merchant location, settlement bank, products, and customer geographies; platform acceptance does not establish tax residence or banking eligibility. EMIs may restrict third-party collections, high chargeback exposure, crypto-related flows, cash deposits, marketplace activity, and rapid pass-through transfers. Safeguarding, reserve, settlement, and termination risks should be addressed through a separate bank account and contingency payment provider.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar has not published a formal statutory Local File or Master File threshold; the Income Tax Office applies the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines. As a practical safe benchmark, contemporaneous Local File disclosures are expected where related-party sales or purchases exceed GIP 10 million per annum, and a Master File may be required where the consolidated group revenue exceeds GIP 750 million, aligning with the OECD Country-by-Country Reporting threshold. An advance pricing agreement may be requested for cross-border keycap sales to obtain certainty.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Prescribed Activities) Regulations 2019 apply to relevant activities including distribution and service centres. Manufacturing double-shot PBT keyboard keycaps is not a prescribed activity by itself, but the ELP's business model may be treated as a distribution and service centre if it purchases finished caps from an overseas affiliate and resells them online. In that case, the ELP must exercise direction and management in Gibraltar, perform core income-generating activities in Gibraltar, and maintain adequate employees, premises and board records. An annual Economic Substance return must be filed by 30 June following the tax year, failing which the entity is excluded from treaty benefits and subjected to penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Omission of E-commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under section 127 of the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, deliberate concealable understatement attracts a penalty of 200% of the underpaid tax, a criminal fine of up to £100,000, and imprisonment for a term of up to five years. In addition, the Gibraltar courts may order confiscation of the proceeds of crime under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.

Director personal liability: The general partner and each partner who participated in or who recklessly facilitated the concealment are jointly and severally liable for the civil tax and penalty. The corporate veil will be pierced where the ELP is used as a mere sham; directors and the general partner may face court-imposed travel bans, freezing orders, and personal restitution.
Shell Company and Sham Substance

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation under the 'substance over form' doctrine may shift taxable income to the economic owner. Under the Economic Substance Regulations 2019, non-compliance with management-and-control requirements attracts a fixed penalty of GIP 10,000 and a daily penalty of up to GIP 500 for continued default. Information will be automatically exchanged with HM Revenue and Customs under the UK-Gibraltar Tax Co-operation Agreement.

Director personal liability: The general partner is personally liable for unpaid Economic Substance penalties if the ELP fails to file the annual ESR return. The Gibraltar court may also make a disqualification order, preventing the individual from acting as a director or partner for up to 15 years, and may impose travel restrictions while the entity's tax liabilities are outstanding.
Abusive Transfer Pricing on Intercompany Keycap Sales

Statutory sanction: The Income Tax Office has authority to adjust the transaction to an arm's-length result and to impose additional tax on the understated profits. The taxpayer is liable for the underpaid tax, interest at 5% per annum, and a transfer-pricing penalty equal to 50% of the resulting underpaid tax. In particularly egregious cross-border arrangements, the UK's controlled foreign company rules may attribute the profits to a UK-resident shareholder.

Director personal liability: General partners or managing partners who sign related-party agreements without adequate pricing documentation are jointly and severally liable for the additional tax. A court may pierce the ELP's limited-liability shield, declare the general partner personally obligated, and issue a travel bar until the corrected tax liability is settled.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Failures

Statutory sanction: Under Gibraltar's AML legislation and the Beneficial Ownership (Access and Information) Regulations 2018, a failure to disclose beneficial ownership is punishable by a fine of up to GIP 100,000 and imprisonment for up to two years. The Registrar of Companies may strike the ELP off the register, and the courts may freeze all bank accounts linked to the ELP.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any compliance officer appointed for the ELP face personal criminal liability, including imprisonment. The court may order the forfeiture of personal assets, impose director disqualification, and issue a travel ban to prevent the individual from leaving Gibraltar before the AML investigation is concluded.
