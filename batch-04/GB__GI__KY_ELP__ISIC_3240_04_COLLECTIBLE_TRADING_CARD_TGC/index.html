Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, HM Government of Gibraltar – Customs and Income Tax Department (Income Tax Office), 6 Seca House, Bayside Road, Gibraltar; official government portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi (Income Tax online e-filing); corporate registry: Gibraltar Companies House (Registrar of Companies).

E-government filing portal

(1) Engage a Gibraltar-licensed registered agent and reserve the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) name with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies. (2) File the ELP registration statement under the Limited Partnerships Act 2020, submitting the limited partnership agreement, particulars of all general and limited partners, the Gibraltar registered office address, and a Gibraltar-resident general partner or authorized representative. (3) Upon issue of the certificate of registration, apply to the Income Tax Office for the partnership's tax reference number (TRN), i.e., the fiscal file reference. (4) Each partner liable to Gibraltar tax on Gibraltar-source income obtains an individual or company TRN; non-resident partners with exclusively foreign-source income are not required to file, but the ELP must record their allocation percentages in its administrative file. (6) If staff are engaged in Gibraltar, register as an employer for PAYE and social insurance. (7) Open the ELP bank account at a Gibraltar-licensed bank or e-money institution, completing AML/KYC on the collectible trading card publishing business.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Gibraltar (not the United Kingdom) is the sole taxing jurisdiction for this structure. Pass_Through_Eligible – Gibraltar ELP is statutorily fiscally transparent under the Limited Partnerships Act 2020 and the Income Tax Act: the ELP is disregarded for income tax and each partner is assessed on its allocated share of partnership income. Territorial_Scope – Gibraltar taxes only income arising in Gibraltar; foreign-source income is outside the charge. Tax_Exempt_Zone – non-resident partners with no Gibraltar-source activity have no Gibraltar income tax, no capital gains tax (none exists in Gibraltar), no inheritance tax, and no withholding tax on distributions; the 12.5% headline rate applies only to Gibraltar-source trading income allocated to partners.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; ELP transparency is mandatory under Gibraltar law, and there is no statutory entity-classification election for a single-member or multi-member partnership other than the profit-allocation terms of the partnership agreement. Partnerships may elect within the partnership agreement to allocate Gibraltar-source profits under Income Tax Act s.49 treatment, with assessment on partners as if the ELP did not exist. Hybrid mismatch elections under the OECD/G20 BEPS Action 2 framework (Gibraltar Taxation (Hybrid Mismatch) Rules) are available to align Gibraltar-transparent treatment with a foreign jurisdiction that treats the ELP as opaque. Legacy Qualifying/Exempt company elections under the former Income Tax (Exemption of Companies) Regulations are closed to new registrants; the go-forward regime is territorial taxation at 12.5% with full fiscal transparency.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / No GST / No Sales and Use Tax – Gibraltar is not part of the EU or UK VAT area and levies no general consumption tax; the sole indirect tax instruments are Customs and Excise Duties under the Customs Duties Ordinance (mostly 0% for manufactured goods, with excise duties on alcohol, tobacco and fuel) and Stamp Duty on real property and insurance. Collectible trading cards (ISIC 3240) are chargeable at 0% import duty; exports are entirely outside the Gibraltar indirect tax charge.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold applies in law. Every person or ELP importing goods into Gibraltar must register with the Gibraltar Customs Division regardless of turnover; no VAT/GST threshold exists because no such tax is imposed. A customs declarant code and business registration are required for the import of raw materials and the export of finished trading cards; remote sales to UK/EU consumers may separately trigger foreign VAT registration (UK VAT flat rate scheme or EU One-Stop-Shop) at destination.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a VAT construct – Gibraltar levies no VAT, so there is no 0% export VAT criterion and no VAT refund mechanism. For customs purposes, exports are duty-free: the ELP must lodge a valid electronic export declaration demonstrating that goods did not enter Gibraltar home use; where the ELP imported raw materials (security holographic cold foil, board, inks) under inward processing relief or duty suspension, the export declaration releases the guarantee and substantiates the relief. Because card sales to UK/EU/third-country distributors are final exports, no Gibraltar indirect tax is chargeable; buyer import VAT/customs duty is a destination-country matter.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic single administrative document lodged via the Gibraltar Customs e-Declaration system, with movement reference number and customs stamp)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from Gibraltar and delivery to the consignee (collectible card distributor, fulfillment center, or end customer)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank credit advices or SWIFT MT103 confirmation issued by the Gibraltar-licensed bank or e-money institution) evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or digital stamping mandate exists for domestic B2B/B2C sales; standard commercial invoices satisfy record-keeping obligations. Customs import and export declarations must be filed electronically through the Gibraltar Customs e-Declaration system (single administrative document equivalent) and carry a movement reference number. Income tax self-assessment returns must be filed online via the Income Tax Office portal, and the ELP must retain transactional data for the online card sales rails (Stripe and Authorize.Net) for six years.

Under the Customs Duties Ordinance, false or fraudulent export declarations attract forfeiture of goods, fines up to three times the duty value, and imprisonment up to two years; duty suspended on imported raw materials is retrospectively clawed back with interest if the corresponding export is not completed within the prescribed bond period. For income tax, if the Commissioner establishes that printing/publishing activity was performed in Gibraltar and mischaracterized as offshore, the Commissioner may reassess within six years (unlimited in fraud cases), impose penalties up to 100% of the tax underpaid, and pursue the general partner and the Gibraltar registered agent. Incorrectly claimed foreign-source characterization of distribution income also exposes licensed professionals to regulatory fines under the Financial Services and Markets Act and potential disqualification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP is fully convertible at the statutory 1:1 peg to GBP for both current-account and capital-account transactions. There are no exchange-control approvals, profit-remittance restrictions, repatriation hurdles, or maturity limits applicable to a Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP). Distributions to non-resident partners, capital repayments, redemption proceeds, and liquidation surpluses may be remitted freely. Practical constraints are infrastructural rather than regulatory: (i) GIP is not a SWIFT-clearing currency, so international settlement is booked as GBP at parity; (ii) Gibraltar is not SEPA-participating, so EUR and USD clearing require correspondent-bank conversion; (iii) USD card receipts from Stripe/Authorize.Net may be swept into multi-currency sub-accounts without mandatory conversion into GBP or GIP; (iv) UK-resident partners remain subject to UK taxation on their distributable share, but no Gibraltarian FX or repatriation consent is triggered.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil surrender regime. The UK Exchange Control Act 1947 was repealed in 1979 and never re-enacted in Gibraltar; no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or designated-account obligation applies to export proceeds. The ELP may retain 100% of export receipts in GBP, USD, EUR, or any other convertible currency, indefinitely. The only documentary duty is under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the Money Laundering Regulations 2019 (Gibraltar), requiring trade contracts, commercial invoices, and GB EORI customs declarations to be available for bank and GFSC review in support of the ELP's 'Low_Risk_Standard' (ISIC 3240) AML profile.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar does not maintain a domestic central bank or a dedicated foreign-exchange administration. Currency and FX affairs are anchored by the UK Crown: the Gibraltar pound (GIP), issued by HM Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934, is pegged at parity (1:1) to pound sterling, with the Bank of England as the anchor issuing institution. Prudential supervision of all banks, electronic money institutions, and payment institutions operating in Gibraltar is exercised exclusively by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019 (Gibraltar).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH, subject to structural pre-conditions. UK-authorised electronic money institutions (Wise, Airwallex, Ebury, Revolut Business) may service a Gibraltar ELP under the UK-Gibraltar post-Brexit financial services framework (Memorandum of Understanding, March 2019), which retains passporting of UK payment services into Gibraltar without a separate Gibraltar EMI licence. Legal and operational constraints: (i) no UK or Gibraltar EMI issues a GIP-denominated IBAN, so balances are held in GBP and converted at parity; (ii) Authorize.Net does not underwrite Gibraltar-domiciled merchants, requiring the ELP to establish a US-domiciled acquiring account (wholly-owned US subsidiary or US payment facilitator) before card receipts can be repatriated; (iii) EMIs mandate full CDD on every Limited Partner holding 10% or more and typically decline where the corporate General Partner is not the sole account signatory; (iv) the 'Low_Risk_Standard' classification for ISIC 3240 permits standard (not enhanced) CDD, enabling EMI onboarding in 3-5 business days once the partnership agreement, register of beneficial owners, and GP board resolution are delivered; (v) EMIs are a secondary rail only - they cannot issue documentary letters of credit, provide sterling cheque clearing, or hold capital escrow balances under Gibraltar trust law.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH, subject to structural pre-conditions. UK-authorised electronic money institutions (Wise, Airwallex, Ebury, Revolut Business) may service a Gibraltar ELP under the UK-Gibraltar post-Brexit financial services framework (Memorandum of Understanding, March 2019), which retains passporting of UK payment services into Gibraltar without a separate Gibraltar EMI licence. Legal and operational constraints: (i) no UK or Gibraltar EMI issues a GIP-denominated IBAN, so balances are held in GBP and converted at parity; (ii) Authorize.Net does not underwrite Gibraltar-domiciled merchants, requiring the ELP to establish a US-domiciled acquiring account (wholly-owned US subsidiary or US payment facilitator) before card receipts can be repatriated; (iii) EMIs mandate full CDD on every Limited Partner holding 10% or more and typically decline where the corporate General Partner is not the sole account signatory; (iv) the 'Low_Risk_Standard' classification for ISIC 3240 permits standard (not enhanced) CDD, enabling EMI onboarding in 3-5 business days once the partnership agreement, register of beneficial owners, and GP board resolution are delivered; (v) EMIs are a secondary rail only - they cannot issue documentary letters of credit, provide sterling cheque clearing, or hold capital escrow balances under Gibraltar trust law.
