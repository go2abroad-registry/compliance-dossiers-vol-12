Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Tax and Customs Directorate, HM Government of Gibraltar (portal: tax.gov.gi)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities obtaining a Gibraltar tax ID must first register with the Gibraltar Companies House under the Companies Act 2014 or the Limited Partnerships Act 1994, file a certified copy of the constitutional deed and registered foreign address, appoint a resident general partner or authorised representative, and then apply online to the Tax and Customs Directorate for a Tax Identification Number (TIN) using form TIN-APP-GI. The application must be accompanied by identification documents, proof of registered office in Gibraltar, and a declaration of intended economic activity. Processing typically completes within 5 to 10 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Gibraltar law treats an Exempted Limited Partnership as fiscally transparent by default for income tax purposes, with each partner taxed on their share of profits. There is no check-the-box election under Gibraltar domestic law; however, in cross-border contexts, the partnership may make a hybrid mismatch election under the Tax Treatment of Partnerships Rules to align characterisation with a foreign jurisdiction, subject to HMRC or other treaty partner classification. Where the ELP is one of a multi-jurisdictional group, a non-statutory transparent status declaration may be filed with the Tax and Customs Directorate to confirm no entity-level tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Purchase Tax and Import Duty (no VAT or GST is imposed)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT/GST registration threshold applies as Gibraltar does not operate a value added tax system. A registration threshold is only relevant for import duty registration where goods are imported commercially, triggering a customs registration requirement at any value.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

As Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST, the concept of export zero-rating does not arise. Services supplied by a Gibraltar ELP to non-Gibraltar clients (including international anti-dumping advisory services) are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation and are not subject to any output tax. No VAT zero-rating claim is required; instead, the ELP relies on the absence of a taxable supply and must evidence the recipient's non-resident status where treaty relief or withholding clauses are invoked.

  • Customs Export Declaration (if physical goods movement occurs, otherwise service contract declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt of offshore proceeds
  • Signed service agreement stating the export nature of the services
  • Gibraltar Tax and Customs Directorate service export schedule (where substantiation is sought)
  • Invoices and payment wire confirmations matching ISIC 7490 advisory services

Gibraltar does not mandate electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping for services. However, the Tax and Customs Directorate requires electronic filing of customs declarations via the Gibraltar Customs Electronic Declaration System where import duty applies, and certain high-risk service providers must maintain an electronic transaction ledger for AML compliance.

Fraudulent or unsupported export claims in Gibraltar attract penalties under the Customs Ordinance and Income Tax Act: a false declaration can trigger a penalty of up to 200% of the duty avoided, interest on any underpaid tax, criminal prosecution with fines not exceeding GBP 10,000 or imprisonment up to two years, and retrospective clawback of any relief or exemption obtained. For pass-through ELPs, clawback is assessed against the partners personally on a joint and several basis.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility without restriction: Gibraltar imposes no exchange control legislation, no ICO/CFIUS-style investment screening, and no capital approval regime. Current account and capital account transactions are freely convertible; the GIP/GBP peg is statutory and operationally seamless. Profit remittance from the KY ELP trade-defense consulting revenue to UK or Cayman partners is frictionless, with zero withholding tax, zero repatriation approval, and zero minimum holding period for USD, EUR, or GBP balances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender duty exists in Gibraltar. The Government of Gibraltar imposes no timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no retention requirement on export proceeds or fee income received under ISIC 7490 anti-dumping defense engagements. Receipts may be held indefinitely in any currency; conversion to GIP/GBP is voluntary. For UK Trade Remedies Authority (TRA) matters, no escrow of consulting fees is mandated, although segregated client accounts are recommended where a Gibraltar advocate is engaged as sub-consultant.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary anchor for sterling block) and Government of Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as sole licensing and prudential supervisor; the Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at absolute 1:1 parity with GBP under the Currency Notes Act 1934. No independent Gibraltar central bank exists; the GFSC applies UK-aligned AML/CFT rules via the AML (Cap 176) framework.

0% — Gibraltar does not levy withholding tax on dividends, profit distributions, or capital gains distributions made by an ELP, including distributions to non-resident partners.

For each Gibraltar tax year (1 January – 31 December): filing due no later than 30 June following the tax year-end, or 9 months after the end of the ELP's accounting period if a company partner adopts a non-calendar year; 30 days from service of a section 132 Income Tax Act 2010 notice where one is issued.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No bright-line statutory local-file threshold has been enacted; Gibraltar applies the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines through the Income Tax Act 2010. Prudent compliance benchmark: prepare and maintain a Local File when the aggregate value of controlled transactions (including management fees, cost-recharges, and IP-royalty charges to related anti-dumping defense affiliates) exceeds GBP 1,000,000 per accounting period, and a Master File where group revenue exceeds GBP 50,000,000; contemporaneous documentation is required to obtain penalty protection on any TP adjustment.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar economic substance rules (Economic Substance Regulations, adopted 2019 and amended) apply to tax-resident companies and partnerships engaged in geographically mobile relevant activities. A professional trade-remedy defense consultancy is treated as an ordinary commercial services activity requiring: (i) physical office or premises in Gibraltar; (ii) full-time employees proportionate to the activity; (iii) board/partner meetings held in Gibraltar with minutes; (iv) core income-generating activities (contract negotiation, dispute analysis, filing strategy, expert testimony management) performed in Gibraltar; (v) adequate annual operating expenditure in Gibraltar. A pure pass-through ELP with all non-resident partners and zero local substance forfeits the benefit of the tax-transparent regime upon challenge and may be reclassified as a resident shell subject to ESR penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Monetary penalty up to 200% of the tax undercharged plus fixed penalty of up to GBP 5,000; daily default fines; criminal conviction on indictment with a fine and imprisonment of up to 5 years; forfeiture of assets and interest at the statutory rate.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any corporate general partner's directors face joint and several liability for the ELP's unpaid tax where the return was submitted with their authority; civil restitution, personal asset seizure, director disqualification up to 15 years, travel bans on departure from Gibraltar, and custodial sentences for fraudulent evasion.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR financial penalty of up to GBP 100,000; daily penalty of up to GBP 1,000 for continued non-compliance; denial or revocation of a Gibraltar Tax Residence Certificate; administrative strike-off and dissolution of the ELP; automatic information exchange to the UK and other tax authorities triggering foreign tax assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner are personally liable for ESR penalties if they knowingly caused the ELP's failure; civil recovery of penalties from personal assets; disqualification orders; in cases of cross-border tax evasion facilitated by the shell, extradition and foreign criminal exposure in the UK or EU member state.
AML / Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: Fine on indictment of up to GBP 250,000 and/or imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering; civil penalties for AML control failures; freezing orders and confiscation orders over the ELP's accounts; liability to third-party victims.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Money Laundering Reporting Officer may be prosecuted personally; travel bans, asset freezes, and entry onto the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit's public warning list; the corporate general partner's license (if a fiduciary) is revoked with personal disqualification from regulated activity.
Customs / Trade Defense Fraud

Statutory sanction: Customs penalties of up to 3 times the value of the goods; forfeiture of goods and vessels; fines up to GBP 100,000; imprisonment of up to 7 years on conviction for fraudulent evasion; debarment from trade-remedy proceedings before the UK TRA and EU Commission.

Director personal liability: Individual lawyers, accountants, and directors involved in the fraud face aiding-and-abetting liability, personal confiscation orders, extradition to the importing state, and professional disqualification (striking off the roll of solicitors/accountants); travel bans and cross-border arrest warrants follow.
