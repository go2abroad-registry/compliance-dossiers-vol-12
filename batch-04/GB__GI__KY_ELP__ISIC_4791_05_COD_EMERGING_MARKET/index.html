Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar - Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax) and Companies Registry; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establish legal presence by first registering the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) with the Gibraltar Companies Registry under the Limited Partnerships Act 2010. Upon registration, the ELP and its non-resident partners obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) from the Income Tax Office by submitting the prescribed registration form, constitutional documents, registered office details, and identification of the general and limited partners. The TRN is used for all income tax dealings. If the ELP will directly import or export commercial goods, it must additionally obtain a Gibraltar Customs EORI number from HM Customs via the electronic customs registration system.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — Foreign entities establish legal presence by first registering the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) with the Gibraltar Companies Registry under the Limited Partnerships Act 2010. If the ELP will directly import or export commercial goods, it must additionally obtain a Gibraltar Customs EORI number from HM Customs via the electronic customs registration system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style 'check-the-box' regime. The ELP is transparent for income tax purposes by statutory default; profits and losses flow through to partners. The ELP may elect to be treated as an exempt entity approved by the Commissioner of Income Tax if its members are non-resident and the partnership avoids Gibraltar-source income, while preserving the ability to make a deemed-payment treaty election in an international context. Hybrid mismatch elections and anti-avoidance adjustments are addressed under Gibraltar's Income Tax Act 2010 and Taxation (International Tax Compliance) regulations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Customs and Excise Duty Regime (No VAT or GST; indirect taxation is limited to customs duty, excise duty, and purchase tax on specified imported goods)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT/GST (Gibraltar is outside the VAT area). Direct importers or exporters of commercial goods require an EORI number and Customs registration from the first customs transaction, with no monetary threshold. For cash-on-delivery retail trade administered through local logistics remittance, consolidated customs entries are required for shipments entering Gibraltar.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating in the VAT sense is inapplicable because Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST. Qualifying exports from Gibraltar to non-Gibraltar destinations are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation. For customs duty purposes, direct export of goods previously imported under a duty-suspension or duty-paid regime may qualify for duty drawback or relief where the physical export is completed within statutory time limits and substantiated with independent transport and payment documentation. Offshore service fees and supplies performed outside Gibraltar are treated as outside the charge to Gibraltar tax.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic CDS Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Mirror FX Settlement Record obtained via the local logistics remittance payment rail

No real-time domestic e-invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate applies in Gibraltar. Electronic customs declarations are mandatory via the United Kingdom Customs Declaration Service (CDS) integrated with Gibraltar Customs. Digital stamping is not used; however, cash-on-delivery and remittance records must be retained to support customs declarations and any potential goods valuation checks.

HM Customs and the Income Tax Office may conduct joint audit examinations of export transactions, payment rails, and FX repatriation records. Fraudulent export declarations, inflated refunds, false drawback claims, or delayed repatriation beyond the 180-day deadline expose the ELP to retrospective clawback of duty or tax relief, civil penalties of up to 100% of the amount evaded, daily default penalties, forfeiture of goods, and criminal sanctions including fines and imprisonment for responsible partners. Records must be retained for at least six years.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP and GBP are fully fungible under a parity peg of 1:1. Gibraltar maintains no exchange-control legislation: current-account payments, capital-account transfers, and profit/dividend remittances are unrestricted for both resident and non-resident partners. No prior regulatory approval is required for inbound capital subscription or outbound distributions. Multi-currency receivables (USD, EUR, PHP, VND, IDR, COP) may be freely held and converted; the only frictions are CRS/FATCA self-certification obligations and GFSC AML reporting duties on the ELP's registered agent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation deadline exists in Gibraltar. A mail-order/COD trading ELP may retain export receipts in any currency indefinitely; however, for tax-substance audit purposes under the Income Tax Act, the entity should maintain a daily sales-and-settlement ledger evidencing reconciliation of COD proceeds against inventory movements. Conversion into GIP/GBP for operational use occurs at the statutory 1:1 peg, and non-GBP conversion is unrestricted at market rates.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no autonomous central bank; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the sole financial services regulator, while the Government of Gibraltar (Ministry of Finance) exercises monetary and currency-issuance authority under the Currency Notes Act (Cap. 97), issuing the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at parity with GBP. The UK Bank of England does not regulate Gibraltar directly but operates the CHAPS/Faster Payments clearing infrastructure through which GIP/GBP flows are ultimately settled.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High, subject to two legal constraints. First, the EMI must hold UK FCA authorisation (Airwallex FRN 900792, Wise FRN 900507, Revolut FRN 900562) because Gibraltar is outside the EEA and UK EMI authorisation provides the cleanest legal basis for servicing a Gibraltar corporate. Second, the ELP must appoint a GFSC-regulated Gibraltar registered agent and maintain a substance file evidencing effective management at the Gibraltar office, satisfying the EMI's KYC test on place of incorporation vs. place of effective management. No Gibraltar capital controls block offshore multi-currency accounts; the practical blocker is the EMI's internal Risk-Committee acceptance of the cash-on-delivery e-commerce vertical in emerging markets, which is remedied by the evidentiary dossier below.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cash-on-delivery settlement model producing fragmented, small-ticket cash inflows from consumers in emerging markets (ISIC 4791), creating third-party payor and money-laundering typology flags.

Root cause: Cash-on-delivery settlement model producing fragmented, small-ticket cash inflows from consumers in emerging markets (ISIC 4791), creating third-party payor and money-laundering typology flags.

Defense evidence: Provide the full commercial contract set: signed logistics-partner agreements, last-mile COD collection reports with daily cash-out logs, automated reconciliation ledgers from Airwallex and the logistics API, and the GFSC-compliant AML/CTF Policy Manual. Attach a Gibraltar Chartered Accountant's procedures report confirming COD proceeds trace to genuine goods sold; supply a nine-month live transaction sample showing consistent ticket sizes, geographic concentration, and reconciliation tolerance below 2%; and implement segregation of duties between order fulfilment, collection, and treasury.
Exempted Limited Partnership with non-resident and possibly opaque partners (UK parent, Gibraltar ELP, emerging-market sales agents), raising UBO transparency concerns under POCA and Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.

Root cause: Exempted Limited Partnership with non-resident and possibly opaque partners (UK parent, Gibraltar ELP, emerging-market sales agents), raising UBO transparency concerns under POCA and Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.

Defense evidence: Deliver the complete ownership chain: certified register of partners, trust/company chart for each partner, Gibraltar Companies House certificate of registration, registered agent's statutory due-diligence file, FATCA/CRS self-certifications, and notarised partnership resolutions showing the consent of all general and limited partners. Disclose every UBO holding 5% or more, with passport copies, residential proof, and source-of-wealth declarations covering the contribution history since formation.
Settlement-corridor sanctions exposure: COD proceeds collected from high-risk geographies (e.g., Vietnam, Indonesia, Colombia cash networks) may accidentally touch sanctioned parties or be mischaracterised as illegal remittance flows.

Root cause: Settlement-corridor sanctions exposure: COD proceeds collected from high-risk geographies (e.g., Vietnam, Indonesia, Colombia cash networks) may accidentally touch sanctioned parties or be mischaracterised as illegal remittance flows.

High-velocity, low-value transaction throughput may be re-characterised by compliance as unlicensed money-service business activity rather than genuine e-commerce retailing.

Root cause: High-velocity, low-value transaction throughput may be re-characterised by compliance as unlicensed money-service business activity rather than genuine e-commerce retailing.
