Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), HM Government of Gibraltar; official tax eServices portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi (Income Tax e-Services / self-assessment module).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 – Register the ELP with the Gibraltar Companies Registry (Registrar of Companies) under the Limited Partnerships Act 2021 and obtain the Certificate of Registration. Step 2 – Submit the partnership deed, registered-office confirmation, and particulars of all general and limited partners to the Income Tax Office on the prescribed partnership/TIN application form. Step 3 – The Income Tax Office issues a unique partnership Tax Reference Number and, for each chargeable partner, an individual Gibraltar TIN; non-resident partners are registered without charge unless Gibraltar-source profits arise. Step 4 – Open a separate Customs registration/account at HM Customs only if the ELP imports/exports physical goods. Step 5 – Register as an employer for Gibraltar payroll withholding (Payroll Tax) if employees are engaged in Gibraltar.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

The ELP is treated as fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended); no election to be taxed as a corporation is available. The ELP may elect/designate a fiscal agent (designated general partner) for service of process and tax filings. Non-resident partners may apply for a formal territorial-scope determination confirming that no Gibraltar-source profits are attributable to the ELP. Where the ELP is treated as an opaque entity in another jurisdiction (hybrid/transparent mismatch), the Gibraltar Income Tax Act's anti-hybrid provisions may require filing an allocation statement or election with the Income Tax Office to align outcomes with the counterpart jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST (Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area); indirect taxation comprises Customs Import Duty, Purchase Tax and excise duties under the Customs (Consolidation) Ordinance.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – Gibraltar imposes no domestic VAT/GST and therefore sets no registration threshold. Importers of dutiable goods must register with HM Customs and operate a duty account from the first importation; no turnover threshold applies to that registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – no VAT/GST zero-rating or export refund mechanism exists in Gibraltar. Cross-border B2B enterprise SaaS subscriptions charged to non-resident customers are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxes. For income tax purposes, profits of the ELP arising abroad fall outside Gibraltar territorial charge provided the ELP does not trade through a permanent establishment in Gibraltar and the SaaS platforms/services are not supplied from a Gibraltar fixed base.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration and/or UK-Gibraltar transit document (only if physical goods are moved)
  • Carrier Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing shipment from/to Gibraltar
  • Executed B2B enterprise SaaS subscription agreement and service-level schedules
  • Commercial invoices and dry-tax status certificates (no VAT / out-of-scope statement)
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT credit advices evidencing receipt of B2B subscription proceeds from abroad
  • ELP Certificate of Registration, partnership deed, and beneficial ownership particulars for Customs/income-tax due diligence

Gibraltar does not mandate electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API-connected invoice stamping, or SAF-T-type digital reporting. Customs declarations must be lodged electronically through the Gibraltar Customs management/declaration system, with supporting transit documentation where goods move via the United Kingdom.

Because no export VAT refund is claimable, no VAT clawback arises; the equivalent compliance exposure is mischaracterisation of Gibraltar-source profits as foreign-source income or misuse of a lapsed 'exempt' status. The Income Tax Office may make discovery assessments outside the normal six-year window for up to 21 years where fraud or negligent non-disclosure is shown, add daily penalties and surcharges up to 100% of the understated tax (up to 200% for deliberate fraud), and charge statutory interest. Customs Ordinance offences for false export/import declarations expose the ELP and its general partner to fines, forfeiture of goods, and criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The GIP is pegged at statutory parity of 1:1 with GBP and is freely interchangeable within the jurisdiction. There are no exchange-control approvals, no foreign-investment screening for software export activities, and no restrictions on remittance of profits, capital, or partner distributions to non-resident partners, including the Cayman-domiciled ultimate parent. The ELP's pass-through tax classification generates no Gibraltar withholding tax on distributions to non-resident partners, provided the ELP does not trade at a Gibraltar source.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Gibraltar imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts. B2B SaaS subscription revenues may be retained in USD, EUR, or GBP in any convertible external bank account, subject only to bank-level documentation of the underlying commercial export transaction (master SaaS agreement, invoice, and payment reconciliation) for AML/CPE purposes.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) — statutory supervisor for banking, fiduciary, and payments activity; the Government of Gibraltar acting as currency-issuing authority for the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) under a 100% Bank of England note-backing arrangement; monetary anchor and oversight framework provided by UK HM Treasury.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High viability subject to structural safeguards. Gibraltar is outside the EEA e-money passporting zone, so EU/UK-authorised EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex) are treated as foreign-regulated payment services rather than Gibraltar deposit-taking institutions. Wise B2B and Stripe Billing are commercially feasible as target collection rails for enterprise SaaS because funds are segregated under EMI safeguarding rules, and settlement to the Tier-1 Gibraltar commercial bank is executed in GBP/EUR/USD. Legal constraints: (i) EMI balances do not constitute bank deposits and are not covered by the Gibraltar Depositor Compensation Scheme; (ii) the ELP must maintain a licensed commercial-bank account as its primary custody treasury layer; (iii) EMI rails must be limited to collection aggregation, vendor payments, and FX conversion, not long-term liquidity warehousing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from KY_ELP to the Gibraltar ELP should be executed through SWIFT MT103 or equivalent, from the Cayman-parent settlement bank to the Gibraltar Tier-1 account, using purpose code CAPT and narrative text: 'Capital contribution to Exempted Limited Partnership — GIP/GBP account funding'. No GFSC or central-bank prior approval is required because Gibraltar imposes no exchange-control regime. The receiving commercial bank will require a source-of-funds declaration, KY_ELP management or audited accounts, and an updated capital-contribution ledger of the Gibraltar ELP. USD-denominated wires must be converted to GIP/GBP at the bank's commercial rate; EUR-denominated funds may be held in a multi-currency operating wallet pending use. The wire must quote the ELP's full registered name and Gibraltar partnership registration number so the posting is accurately attributed to the partner's capital account.Disbursements execute through four accepted rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for GBP/EUR/USD supplier, contractor, and partner-distribution payments, using purpose codes PID for partnership distributions or PI for intercompany investment return, as applicable; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (SWIFT MT700/MT707) only where an enterprise SaaS contract includes on-premise licensing, implementation milestones, or system-integration deliverables requiring documentary trade settlement — being a supporting rail, not a default for pure SaaS; (3) UK Faster Payments, CHAPS, and SEPA credit transfers for low-value recurring payroll, VAT, corporate-tax, and UK/EU supplier obligations; and (4) Wise B2B as a low-cost fintech aggregation rail for B2B supplier refunds, freelance developer payments, and small vendor FX conversions. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% standard corporate rate (20% applies to utility and retail sectors; B2B enterprise SaaS falls within the 12.5% standard category).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%: Gibraltar domestic law imposes no withholding tax on dividends or on company distributions; because the ELP is tax-transparent, profit repatriation to partners follows the fiscal character of the underlying SaaS income and is not subject to Gibraltar WHT.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CbCR and Master File: MNE groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more; Local File: expected on an OECD-aligned basis where aggregate controlled transactions exceed GIP 1,000,000 in a chargeable period, and in all cases where the SaaS business has material intercompany licensing, distribution, or support charges.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations 2019 apply to relevant entities including an ELP; B2B SaaS operation is treated as a 'distribution and service centre' or 'headquarters' relevant activity. The ELP must demonstrate direction and management in Gibraltar, local employees, physical premises and operating expenditure proportionate to income; the pure equity holding exemption is available only for passive holdings and not for an active SaaS trade. Failure results in ESR penalties, mandatory exchange of information with the UK and other tax authorities, and potential strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 100% of the tax understated for deliberate inaccuracy, plus daily default penalties and statutory late-payment interest; criminal prosecution for dishonest evasion with fines at the court's discretion and/or up to 7 years imprisonment; restraint and confiscation of proceeds under the Proceeds of Crime Act.

Director personal liability: The general partner and acting directors are jointly and severally liable for partnership debts and civil restitution; travel bans, exit restrictions and freezing orders may be imposed; serious fraud leads to imprisonment and disqualification as a director in Gibraltar and the UK.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR notification and substance failure penalties: fixed penalty of GIP 10,000 plus up to GIP 10,000 per month of continuing default; automatic exchange of information with HMRC; the GTA may strike off the ELP and revoke its Gibraltar establishment status.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner who signed the false ESR certification are personally fined and disqualified; the Commissioner may apply for a travel prohibition and require a personal contribution to the ELP's liabilities.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: The GTA adjusts profits to an arm's length result, assesses additional tax at 12.5%, applies statutory interest and imposes an accuracy-related penalty of up to 100% of the understated tax; denial of DTA benefits may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign tax returns without exercising prudent diligence incur personal penalties; in egregious cases the general partner is personally liable for restitution of the underpaid tax and the transfer pricing penalty.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to GIP 250,000 for PSC register offences; regulatory restriction of Gibraltar bank accounts and access to Stripe and Wise payment rails; criminal money-laundering sanctions where the SaaS clients or subscription funds are of suspicious origin.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any shadow director are personally liable for AML fines; travel bans and property freezing orders apply during investigation; imprisonment of up to 2 years for PSC offences and up to 14 years for money laundering.
False International Marketing of Gibraltar Status

Statutory sanction: Regulatory fines under the Financial Services Act and consumer protection legislation of up to GIP 1,000,000 per contravention; coordinated disclosure to HMRC and the GTA; mandatory filing of corrected corporate and VAT positions; cease-and-desist orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner who signed misleading marketing or operated an unlicensed payment scheme are personally liable for consumer restitution; travel bans and disqualification orders apply; imprisonment of up to 7 years may be imposed for unlicensed financial activity in the UK.
