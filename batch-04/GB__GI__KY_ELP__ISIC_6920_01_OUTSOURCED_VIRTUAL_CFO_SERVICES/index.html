Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) — UK Government portal: gov.uk; Gibraltar Income Tax Office (GITO) for local tax matters; Gibraltar Customs Department for indirect tax administration

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities establishing a Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) must first register with the Gibraltar Companies House for incorporation; thereafter apply for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office (GITO) using the ELP certificate of incorporation, partnership deed, and proof of registered office. For UK VAT/GST and Customs registration, the ELP must register for a UK VAT number via HMRC's online VAT Registration service (form VAT1) if it makes taxable supplies in the UK or imports goods exceeding the UK registration threshold; a UK Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) is obtained for self-assessment/corporate tax where applicable. The foreign entity may also be required to register under Gibraltar's GST/VAT equivalent (Customs and Excise duties) and obtain an EORI number for customs purposes in the UK/Gibraltar if importing or exporting goods.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Gibraltar ELP is fiscally transparent for Gibraltar income tax purposes; each partner is separately taxed on their share of partnership income, and the ELP itself is not subject to entity-level income tax. For UK tax purposes, an ELP may also be treated as transparent under UK domestic law and applicable double tax treaty principles; no check-the-box election is available within the UK/Gibraltar domestic regimes.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available in Gibraltar or the UK. Partnerships are per se fiscally transparent for Gibraltar tax purposes. For UK tax treatment, the ELP may be treated as transparent unless it elects under section 863AA ITTOIA 2005 to be treated as a separate UK company for certain UK tax purposes (UK hybrid entity transparency election), but such election is limited in scope and does not alter the Gibraltar domestic pass-through classification. Hybrid mismatch rules under the UK's Hybrids and Other Mismatches Legislation (Part 6A TIOPA 2010) apply to counteract any double non-taxation arising from differences in classification between UK and foreign law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

UK VAT registration threshold: GBP 90,000 of taxable turnover over the previous 12 months (as of 2024/2025); for businesses established outside the UK, a mandatory registration arises if they make any taxable supplies in the UK or if they supply goods through a UK VAT agent/warehouse irrespective of turnover. Gibraltar: no general VAT registration threshold; however, an EORI number (GB or GI prefix) is required for customs transactions and import duty registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For UK VAT zero-rating: (a) goods exported to non-UK destinations or shipped to a UK free zone/warehouse and then exported; (b) services supplied to a business customer outside the UK (B2B) may be outside the UK VAT scope or zero-rated where they fall under section 7 VATA and outside the UK rules; (c) certain international transportation and related services. For goods, title must pass to the overseas customer, goods must physically leave the UK within statutory time limits, and evidence of export must be retained. For offshore virtual CFO services supplied by a Gibraltar ELP to non-UK clients, such services are generally outside the UK VAT scope if the customer is a non-UK business (B2B place of supply of services to the customer's country); if the customer is a UK individual, UK reverse charge may apply to certain digital/consultancy services; direct zero-rating is not applicable to unrestricted advisory services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (UK CDS form or SAD C88) evidencing export from the UK/Gibraltar
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (original or electronic) showing the exporter, consignee, and delivery to foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from the receiving bank evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds in a foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration
  • Proof of overseas business customer status and place of belonging (e.g., VAT registration certificate or OECD country tax registration)

The UK does currently not mandate continuous transaction controls (CTC) or real-time fiscal digital stamping for B2B or B2C invoices; however, from 2024/2025 MTD (Making Tax Digital) requires VAT-registered businesses to maintain digital VAT records and submit VAT returns via compatible software using the MTD API. HMRC mandates use of sandbox and live APIs for submission and may request digital audit files (iXBRL). Gibraltar customs imports/exports are cleared through the UK-Gibraltar shared customs procedures using electronic declarations via the International Trade Single Window (Australia? Actually UK CDS — Customs Declaration Service); no separate fiscal stamping requirement.

HMRC and Gibraltar Customs may conduct exporter compliance audits under the Customs and Excise Management Act 1979 (as applied to Gibraltar). If zero-rating is claimed improperly or export evidence is insufficient, VAT is clawed back with interest (default surcharge and repayment supplement). Penalties include: (a) assessment of unrecovered VAT plus late payment interest; (b) dishonest evasion penalties up to 100% of VAT evaded plus potential criminal prosecution; (c) penalties of up to GBP 3,000 for failure to provide records/evidence; (d) for customs duty evasion, civil penalties up to 100% of duty or criminal sanctions; (e) a UK export penalty regime under Finance Act 2003 Schedule 4 (post-2003) for non-discharge of export obligations; (f) false statements to HMRC may result in unlimited fines and imprisonment under the Fraud Act 2006.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current and capital account convertibility applies. GIP is freely convertible into GBP at par, and neither Gibraltar nor the UK imposes exchange-control permits for capital contributions, operating transfers, or profit repatriations. Practical liquidity hurdle: GIP is not traded outside Gibraltar, so all treasury operations must be executed in GBP/EUR/USD through multicurrency accounts. An ELP is tax-transparent, and distributions to non-resident partners are freely remittable without withholding tax or Gibraltar income tax on non-resident partners for foreign-source income.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Neither the UK nor Gibraltar imposes statutory export-proceeds surrender timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation quotas. Service export receipts from fractional CFO and multicurrency ledger engagements may be retained in any convertible currency; banks may request underlying advisory contracts and invoice ledgers as part of source-of-funds and AML due diligence.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the integrated financial regulator; the Government of Gibraltar's Currency Commissioner as the sole issuing authority for the Gibraltar pound (GIP) under the Currency Notes Act; the Bank of England anchors sterling convertibility through the GIP/GBP 1:1 currency-board peg and correspondent clearing infrastructure.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium as a primary banking substitute, High as an auxiliary collection and disbursement rail. Wise Business (FCA-authorised UK EMI) supports Gibraltar-registered companies and provides multi-currency GBP/EUR/USD local-account details. Stripe Billing is not directly available for a Gibraltar-only entity; it requires a UK/EU payment-facilitator wrapper or a UK-established merchant of record. Legal constraints under the UK Payment Services Regulations 2017 and the GFSC e-money framework: EMIs cannot accept deposits, are not covered by the Gibraltar Depositor Compensation Scheme, cannot grant overdrafts, and may freeze funds pending enhanced due diligence on pass-through payment flows.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% (standard Gibraltar corporate income tax rate applicable to companies and corporate partners; the Exempted Limited Partnership itself is fiscally transparent with no entity-level tax charge)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar does not impose withholding tax on profit repatriation, dividends, partnership distributions, or capital returns from a tax-transparent ELP.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Partnership return due by 30 November following the end of the year of assessment; provisional self-assessment installments due 31 January and 31 July, with balancing payment due 30 November. The Commissioner may extend the deadline where accounting periods are non-calendar.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP/GBP 1,000,000 aggregate annual controlled transaction value triggers mandatory contemporaneous Local File documentation; Gibraltar entities forming part of a multinational group with consolidated revenue exceeding GBP 200,000,000 must also prepare a Master File and file an annual transfer-pricing disclosure schedule. A benchmarking study confirming arm's length pricing is recommended regardless of the threshold.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for geographically mobile activities apply to the ELP under the Income Tax Act as amended and GFSC guidance. The virtual CFO/bookkeeping activity qualifies as a 'service business'; the ELP must evidence (a) board/partner meetings and strategic management in Gibraltar, (b) adequate full-time employees and physical premises in Gibraltar, (c) core income-generating activities performed in Gibraltar, and (d) an enhanced substance 'plus factor' to defend the foreign-source income exemption. Inadequate substance triggers an ESR review, annual information filing with the GFSC, penalties, and potential loss of exempt status.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Civil tax penalties up to 100% of the understated tax, daily penalties up to GBP 50, monetary fine, and imprisonment up to 7 years under the Income Tax Act 2014 (as amended).

Director personal liability: The general partner and any directing partner are severally liable; on conviction, travel bans, confiscation orders, director disqualification up to 15 years, and the corporate veil may be pierced to attach personal assets where the partnership was used as a vehicle for fraud.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Revocation of ELP exemption; retroactive reassessment at the 12.5% corporate rate for up to 6 years; ESR penalty of GBP 10,000 plus daily default penalties of GBP 900; flag to GFSC and potential compulsory strike-off and dissolution.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners held jointly and severally for unpaid tax; GFSC travel-ban powers applied; civil restitution and disqualification orders attach to personal wealth where the entity is deemed to have been operated as a sham.
AML/CFT and Unlicensed Professional Service

Statutory sanction: Unlimited fine or fine up to GBP 100,000 per offence; imprisonment up to 14 years; confiscation of tainted funds; exclusion of the ELP and its principals from acting in the financial services sector.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and the general partner face criminal liability upon conviction; asset freeze orders, travel bans, permanent director disqualification, and civil recovery action against personal assets under the Proceeds of Crime regime.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Aggressive Fee Parking

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer-pricing adjustments, secondary adjustments with imputed interest, penalties up to 100% of the adjustment, cross-border exchange of information with HMRC and treaty partners, and potential criminal sanctions for a deliberate tax advantage.

Director personal liability: The general partner signing a materially incorrect return is personally exposed to civil penalties and restitution claims; directors may be disqualified and travel-banned under GFSC or HMRC enforcement where artificial arrangements were controlled by them personally.
