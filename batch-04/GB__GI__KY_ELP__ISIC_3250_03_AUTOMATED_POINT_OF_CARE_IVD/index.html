Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office (ITO), HM Government of Gibraltar; official portal at https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation. Customs and indirect-tax matters are administered by H.M. Customs Gibraltar (Customs and Excise Division), operating within the UK-Gibraltar Customs Union Treaty framework.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident-owned Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP): (1) register the ELP with the Registrar of Companies (Companies House Gibraltar) under the Limited Partnerships Act 2021, electing whether to have separate legal personality; (2) file the income-tax registration application with the Income Tax Office within 30 days of establishment, identifying the general partner and all limited partners; (3) obtain the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) assigned to the partnership and the assessment file number for each taxable partner; (4) register the partnership with H.M. Customs Gibraltar for a customs identification number where goods will be imported or exported; (5) appoint a Gibraltar-resident registered office and tax representative where the partners are fully non-resident.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the ELP is statutorily transparent for Gibraltar income-tax purposes. Net profits are allocated to partners and taxed in their hands; the ELP itself is not chargeable to income tax. Resident individual partners are taxed at progressive rates up to 25%; resident corporate partners at the 12.5% corporation-tax rate. Non-resident partners are taxed only on Gibraltar-source income under the territorial system. Capital gains are not taxed for non-residents, and no entity-level charge arises on the ELP's export manufacturing profits.

Available election forms

Elections available: (a) election for separate legal personality (or absence of it) under the Limited Partnerships Act 2021; (b) election to be treated as a qualifying exempted fund or private investment partnership under the ELP regime; (c) hybrid-mismatch elections and corresponding-adjustment notifications under Gibraltar's OECD BEPS-aligned hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act Part on Hybrids), including the designated-taxpayer election where a mismatched deduction would otherwise arise. No US-style check-the-box election exists: partnership transparency is mandatory and follows the statutory legal form.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Indirect Tax Regime — Import Duty and Excise Duty only; no VAT/GST. Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and remains outside UK VAT under the post-Brexit UK-Gibraltar Customs Union Treaty. Export of goods from Gibraltar to any third-country destination is outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST turnover-based registration threshold exists. Income-tax registration is required only where the entity is chargeable to income tax (i.e., deriving Gibraltar-source profits). For goods movements, no threshold applies: every importer/exporter must hold a customs registration identification number from H.M. Customs Gibraltar. Excise and import duties attach selectively to specified goods (alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil and a limited list); IVD inputs and finished medical devices are generally duty-free.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is not applicable at law because Gibraltar levies no VAT/GST; there is therefore no zero-rate export VAT claim or refund mechanism. Export sales of microfluidic cartridges and immunoassay analyzers physically shipped from Gibraltar to foreign customers are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation. Where import duty was paid on inputs that are subsequently exported, the exporter may claim import-duty drawback or apply inward processing relief (suspension) under the Customs Duties Ordinance, subject to verification of export movement.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document) filed electronically with H.M. Customs Gibraltar, showing HS commodity codes, free-circulation status, value and destination
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and bill of lading or airway bill evidencing physical export from Gibraltar
  • Inward remittance certificate (bank SWIFT MT103 / TT confirmation) evidencing receipt of export proceeds on the contracted trade terms

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, API, or digital stamping mandate applies in Gibraltar. Standard commercial electronic or paper invoices are accepted for tax and customs purposes. No continuous transaction control (CTC), SAF-T, or mandatory online invoice pre-clearance regime is in force. Customs declarations are submitted electronically in SAD format via H.M. Customs Gibraltar's clearance system.

Gibraltar imposes no exchange-control repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure reflects the standard documentary-credit and trade-finance convention adopted as an internal audit control for proving receipt of export proceeds. H.M. Customs Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office may audit export contracts, physical movement records, shipping documents and related payment flows. Deficiencies or fraudulent claims trigger retrospective duty assessments (up to 6 years), clawback of duty drawback, forfeiture of goods, and civil penalties up to GBP 3,000 or three times the dutiable value under the Customs Duties Ordinance, with imprisonment for fraudulent evasion. Income-tax misstatements expose partners to penalties up to 100% of the underpaid tax plus interest; the General Anti-Abuse Rule permits recharacterisation and additional surcharge where export arrangements lack economic substance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains a fully convertible, open capital account regime with no exchange-control legislation. The GIP is pegged at par to GBP and is freely interchangeable with Bank of England notes and coins on a one-for-one basis. Both capital account transactions (equity injections, loan advances, liquidation distributions) and current account transactions (trade settlements, service fees, profit remittances) are fully convertible without central-bank approval. As an Exempted Limited Partnership with pass-through tax status, the ELP is not subject to Gibraltar withholding tax on distributions to non-resident limited partners under the Income Tax Act 2010 (Gibraltar), provided the partnership maintains the applicable economic substance conditions under the Taxes (Substance Requirements) Regulations 2020. The only practical hurdles to profit remittance are GFSC-licensed banks' internal de-risking policies and the Medium-Risk Enhanced AML categorisation attaching to this ISIC 3250 automated point-of-care IVD manufacturing scope, which require trade documentation and source-of-funds evidence for each remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender, repatriation, or mandatory conversion ratio in Gibraltar or under retained UK law. Exporters, including non-resident-owned ELPs, may retain 100% of export receipts in any convertible currency in accounts held at Gibraltar-licensed banks without any obligation to convert into GIP/GBP. For balance-of-payments and statistical collection, the Gibraltar Statistics Department may request supplementary trade data, and banks apply JMLSG-aligned transaction-monitoring reporting for wires above GBP 100,000 equivalent, but no foreign-currency holding caps, surrender deadlines, or conversion mandates exist. Prudent treasury practice for the IVD manufacturing entity is to consolidate export proceeds in a GBP/USD multicurrency operational account at the Tier-1 Gibraltar bank, hedge projected FX exposures under the permissible instruments described below, and maintain a documented reinvestment/remittance policy to satisfy Enhanced Due Diligence expectations.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (the United Kingdom's central bank) is the monetary authority underpinning the Gibraltar pound (GIP), which circulates in Gibraltar at a fixed 1:1 parity with pound sterling; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory regulator licensing and supervising Gibraltar-incorporated banks, payment services, electronic money institutions, and trust/corporate service providers, and enforces AML/CFT supervision under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Criminal Justice Act 2011 (as amended). Gibraltar is outside the European System of Central Banks; no domestic issuance of euro or US dollar is permitted without specific GFSC authorisation.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH when used as an ancillary FX conversion and low-value disbursement rail, but LOW as a primary corporate banking node. UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA)-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, ClearBank, and Tide can serve the UK-facing portion of the ELP's cash flows and provide cost-effective EUR/USD conversion below GBP 100,000; however, post-Brexit there is no automatic EEA/UK passporting regime into Gibraltar, so a UK EMI must either rely on a GFSC foreign-permission arrangement or serve only the non-Gibraltar side of the treasury. EMIs cannot accept deposits, cannot issue documentary letters of credit or SBLCs, cannot provide overdraft/trade-finance facilities, and are not eligible correspondent nodes for SWIFT MT700 LC settlement. The GFSC treats the KY_ELP structure as Medium-Risk Enhanced, so the core liquidity pool, LC issuance, and capital escrow must sit with a Tier-1 GFSC-licensed bank. Recommended architecture: one Gibraltar licensed bank as the primary clearing node, with a single UK/FCA EMI (Wise Business or ClearBank) integrated solely as an auxiliary FX layer for sub-threshold supplier payments in the European distribution network.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital contributions should be routed via SWIFT MT103 (single payment) or MT202 (cover payment for aggregated contributions) from the UK holding company's FCA-regulated bank to the ELP's dedicated capital-escrow sub-account at Trusted Novus Bank or Gibraltar International Bank. The remittance instruction must use Field 70 (Remittance Information) to state 'Capital contribution to Gibraltar exempted limited partnership - non-resident limited partner - ISIC 3250 IVD manufacturing' and include the ELP registration number and a unique capital-call reference matching the executed capital-call notice. Where the UK BOP enterprise survey framework applies, the transaction code should reflect direct investment equity ('capital contributed to an enterprise') rather than loan finance, and the beneficiary must receive a Receipt and Confirmation of Contribution (RCC) countersigned by the general partner. GFSC approval is not required for GBP/n-GIP conversion because the two currencies circulate at par; if the injection is denominated in USD or EUR, the receiving bank will apply a spot FX conversion or credit the foreign-currency deposit without statutory cap. The bank will verify the remitter against the AML/KYC file, and any contribution exceeding GBP 100,000 equivalent will trigger enhanced transaction monitoring requiring the evidentiary defense dossier described above.Settlement rails for the IVD manufacturing trade flows should combine: (1) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for open-account payments to European and UK component suppliers, customs brokers, logistics providers, and payroll, with the invoice and contract reference in Field 70; (2) commercial documentary letters of credit (sight or 30-90 day usance) issued via the Gibraltar bank's correspondent network and advised through SWIFT MT700, with MT707 amendments and MT734 refusals, for procurement of high-value microfluidic raw materials, immunoassay analyzer sub-assemblies, and precision instrumentation from Asian/US original-equipment manufacturers; (3) standby letters of credit (SBLC) only where a North American or EU distributor requires credit enhancement of the ELP's delivery obligations; and (4) an FX payment overlay that nets GIP/GBP operational inflows against EUR/USD disbursements under the permitted hedging instruments. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH when used as an ancillary FX conversion and low-value disbursement rail, but LOW as a primary corporate banking node. UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA)-authorised electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, ClearBank, and Tide can serve the UK-facing portion of the ELP's cash flows and provide cost-effective EUR/USD conversion below GBP 100,000; however, post-Brexit there is no automatic EEA/UK passporting regime into Gibraltar, so a UK EMI must either rely on a GFSC foreign-permission arrangement or serve only the non-Gibraltar side of the treasury. EMIs cannot accept deposits, cannot issue documentary letters of credit or SBLCs, cannot provide overdraft/trade-finance facilities, and are not eligible correspondent nodes for SWIFT MT700 LC settlement. The GFSC treats the KY_ELP structure as Medium-Risk Enhanced, so the core liquidity pool, LC issuance, and capital escrow must sit with a Tier-1 GFSC-licensed bank. Recommended architecture: one Gibraltar licensed bank as the primary clearing node, with a single UK/FCA EMI (Wise Business or ClearBank) integrated solely as an auxiliary FX layer for sub-threshold supplier payments in the European distribution network.
