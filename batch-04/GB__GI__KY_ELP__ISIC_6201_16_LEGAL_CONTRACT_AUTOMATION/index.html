Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement for a Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership; capital contributions are determined solely by the Limited Partnership Agreement. No escrow account or deposit of capital with the Registrar is mandated by law.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable for the initial virtual-office setup: no fire safety certification, structural load inspection, or physical workplace clearance is required where no staffed physical branch is established. If a physical Gibraltar office is later opened, compliance with the Occupational Safety and Health Act 1998 (Gibraltar), fire safety inspection, and landlord's structural load certification will become mandatory.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement for a Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership; capital contributions are determined solely by the Limited Partnership Agreement. No escrow account or deposit of capital with the Registrar is mandated by law.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar, Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax); portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation. Indirect/import duties are administered by HM Customs Gibraltar.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing an Exempted Limited Partnership must: (1) register the ELP with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Limited Partnerships Act 2015; (2) submit Form 430 to the Income Tax Office to obtain a Partnership Tax Reference Number for the annual partnership return; (3) ensure each partner obtains an individual Gibraltar Tax Reference only where Gibraltar-source income is reportable; (4) if the ELP imports or exports goods, apply to HM Customs Gibraltar for a Gibraltar Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number. There is no minimum capital requirement and non-resident ownership is freely permitted.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity establishing an Exempted Limited Partnership must: (1) register the ELP with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Limited Partnerships Act 2015; (2) submit Form 430 to the Income Tax Office to obtain a Partnership Tax Reference Number for the annual partnership return; (3) ensure each partner obtains an individual Gibraltar Tax Reference only where Gibraltar-source income is reportable; (4) if the ELP imports or exports goods, apply to HM Customs Gibraltar for a Gibraltar Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number. There is no minimum capital requirement and non-resident ownership is freely permitted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election is available. The Gibraltar Income Tax Act treats the Exempted Limited Partnership as fiscally transparent; profits and losses flow through to partners. No statutory hybrid mismatch election forms are prescribed; however, OECD/G20 hybrid mismatch principles are relevant where a partner jurisdiction applies conflicting classification rules. A partner-level election may only affect foreign tax credit computation or allocation covenants.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/SUT applies. Gibraltar operates a customs and excise duty system on imported goods and selected excise products.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 0 — no turnover-based VAT/GST threshold exists. Any importer or exporter of goods must register with HM Customs Gibraltar upon first commercial shipment; no VAT registration regime is in force.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is not applicable because Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST. Supplies of LegalTech SaaS to non-resident customers are outside Gibraltar indirect taxation. For goods, any customs duty relief or drawback is available only where the goods are exported outside Gibraltar within prescribed statutory time limits and are supported by corroborative transport documents.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/financial institution proof of export payment
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List

No mandatory real-time electronic invoicing, continuous transaction controls, digital stamping, or tax-authority API clearance is required. HM Customs Gibraltar accepts standard commercial invoices and operates an electronic export/import declaration service, typically filed by licensed customs agents.

For customs/export duty relief, inaccurate declarations attract repayment of duty relief plus civil penalties of up to 100% of the unpaid duty under the Customs and Excise Ordinance. For income tax matters, the Commissioner may reopen assessments within 6 years (12 years for deliberate or fraudulent conduct) and retrospectively claw back any relief or exemption granted. Fraudulent export claims or false repatriation documentation may additionally result in criminal prosecution and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates a fully convertible currency regime with no exchange controls. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at par to the British pound sterling (GBP), and GBP is also legal tender. Both current and capital account transactions are unrestricted. Profit remittances, dividends, and capital repatriation are freely permitted without prior approval or quantitative limits. No distinction is made between resident and non-resident accounts for convertibility purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion requirements on export proceeds. As a service-based jurisdiction, receipts from cross-border SaaS and digital services may be retained in any convertible currency. There is no timeline for repatriation or conversion; companies may hold foreign currency balances indefinitely. The only obligation is standard tax reporting under the Income Tax Act, which does not affect FX treatment.

Central bank supervision: Government of Gibraltar (Ministry of Finance) and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Gibraltar has a robust EMI licensing regime under the Financial Services Act 2019, allowing digital payment institutions to operate. However, offshore EMIs without a physical presence in Gibraltar face stringent licensing requirements and are subject to GFSC supervision. For a low-risk LegalTech SaaS, a licensed Gibraltar EMI (e.g., a local subsidiary) is viable, but pure offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK or EU-based) may be used for payment processing without a local license, provided they comply with PSD2 and cross-border passporting rules. The primary constraint is the need for a local banking partner for settlement and liquidity management.

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Inbound capital injections from shareholders or parent entities must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with clear purpose codes. For Gibraltar, the recommended purpose code is 'S1301' (Investment in equity capital) or 'S1302' (Investment in other capital) under the BPM6 classification. The remitter must include the company's full legal name, account number, and a reference to the capital contribution. Verification requires a board resolution authorizing the injection, a share subscription agreement (if applicable), and proof of the remitter's identity. The company may also use payment processors (Stripe, credit cards) for customer-facing transactions, but these are not considered clearing rails for B2B disbursements. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% standard rate of Gibraltar corporate income tax for Gibraltar tax-resident companies. The Exempted Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent: zero entity-level tax on the ELP itself; partners are assessed on their allocated profit shares (resident partners: 12.5%; non-resident partners: 0% on non-Gibraltar-source income).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividends or profit distributions paid by a Gibraltar company or ELP to non-resident partners; distributions from the ELP are treated as returns of allocated profit and capital, exempt from Gibraltar withholding tax and from Gibraltar income tax in the hands of non-resident partners.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

30 November following the end of the year of assessment (i.e., for the 31 December tax year, return due 30 November of the following year). Where the ELP's accounting period ends within a calendar year, the filing deadline is 30 November following the accounting period end. The wholly non-resident ELP files no tax return; the Registry Annual Return is due within 6 months of the ELP's registration anniversary.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: £1,000,000 (GBP) aggregated cross-border controlled transactions per annum — mandatory Local File required above this threshold; a Master File is required for group consolidated revenue exceeding £200,000,000 (GBP). Gibraltar applies the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines through the Income Tax Act 2010 arm's length provisions.

Economic substance scope: Applicable: the Gibraltar Economic Substance regime requires Gibraltar tax-resident entities carrying on relevant activities (banking, insurance, fund management, financing, leasing, headquarter, distribution, service centre, shipping, and IP holding) to demonstrate adequate substance through management and control, local premises, employees and control of core income-generating activities. Reporting is made via the Income Tax Office Economic Substance return (ES-GIB). An ELP managed and controlled outside Gibraltar with wholly non-resident partners is generally outside the scope; a UK-resident partner performing the LegalTech SaaS activities from UK premises will create a UK permanent establishment chargeable in the UK and ineligible for Gibraltar relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — undisclosed Gibraltar-source trading

Statutory sanction: Civil assessment on the full Gibraltar-source profits at 12.5%, plus interest on overdue tax at the prevailing statutory rate; late-filing fixed penalty of £100; daily surcharge of £60 per day up to 90 days; tax-geared penalties up to 100% of the understated tax; criminal fines up to £10,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years under sections 175 and 177 of the Income Tax Act 2010.

Director personal liability: The general partner is jointly and severally liable for the ELP's tax debts and penalties; any limited partner who participates in management loses limited-liability protection and faces personal liability. Directors of a corporate general partner are pursued personally via civil restitution, distraint, exit/travel bans, and imprisonment for fraudulent evasion.
Sham or Shell Arrangement — disguised UK permanent establishment

Statutory sanction: UK HMRC re-characterises the arrangement: the UK premises and activities constitute a taxable UK permanent establishment of the ELP; profits are charged to UK corporation tax at the applicable rate (19%–25%); penalties up to 100% of the understated tax, follower and accelerated payment notices, and IR35/offshore employment intermediary rules apply; the UK-Gibraltar DTA does not exempt UK-source activity.

Director personal liability: The directing mind (UK resident partner or director of the corporate general partner) faces personal liability under FA 2009 for unpaid UK tax of the ELP, director disqualification, travel bans enforced by HMRC, and criminal prosecution for cheating the public revenue; the corporate veil is pierced for deliberate misuse of the ELP structure.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Register non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Limited Partnerships Act 2011 and the Companies (Beneficial Ownership) Regulations 2019, fines up to £5,000 per default plus daily default fines; restrictions on entering into new contracts; administrative strike-off and cancellation of the ELP registration; the Income Tax Office may also lift tax transparency and assess the ELP as a resident limited company.

Director personal liability: The general partner is liable for AML fines; directors of a corporate general partner are subject to criminal penalties including imprisonment up to 2 years, disqualification from acting as a director, and removal from the Gibraltar Register of Directors.
Economic Substance failure — IP and fund-management substance

Statutory sanction: Re-characterisation of the ELP as a Gibraltar tax-resident enterprise with assessment on worldwide income at 12.5%, denial of treaty and zero-rate benefits, application of the UK CFC charge to UK-resident partners, and tax-geared penalties up to 100% of the additional assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner face personal liability for false economic substance declarations, fines up to £10,000, disqualification orders, travel restrictions, and criminal prosecution for making a false statement to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office.
