Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax), official portal: gibraltar.gov.gi (Government e-Services); indirect and customs duties are administered by Her Majesty's Customs Gibraltar (Ministry of Finance), official portal: gibraltar.gov.gi/customs

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Cayman Islands Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) as an external limited partnership with the Gibraltar Companies Registry under the Limited Partnerships Act 2010, submitting the certified partnership deed, register of partners, and Gibraltar registered office consent. Step 2: Apply to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number using the Business Tax Registration form, attaching the certificate of partnership registration, initial accounts or statement of capital, proof of Gibraltar business address, and CRS/FATCA self-certifications of all general and limited partners; if no partner is resident in Gibraltar, appoint a Gibraltar tax representative. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Statutory elections available include: (i) joint assessment election under the Income Tax Act whereby partners may elect to be jointly assessed on partnership profits instead of several assessment, while the default pass-through transparency remains; (ii) election for the reduced 5% qualifying small enterprise rate, instead of the standard 12.5% rate, for an ELP that elects corporate taxation and satisfies the qualifying conditions and statutory profit cap; (iii) remittance-basis election for resident non-domiciled individual partners; (iv) hybrid mismatch elections and disclosures under the Gibraltar Taxation (Hybrid Mismatch) Rules, including alignment elections to neutralise a double-deduction or reverse hybrid result arising from the differential treatment of the Cayman ELP; (v) group relief elections where a Gibraltar-resident corporate partner is a member of a Gibraltar tax group.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT or GST. Gibraltar is a British Overseas Territory excluded from both the UK VAT system and the EU VAT area; indirect taxation is limited to customs import duties, excise duties on alcohol, tobacco and fuel, and vehicle or consumption-specific duties, all administered by Her Majesty's Customs Gibraltar.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST and therefore no turnover registration threshold. Customs trader registration with HM Customs Gibraltar is mandatory from the first import or export of physical goods, with no minimum economic turnover. A customs guarantee or security may be required for transit declarations, temporary storage or bonded operations. Where the ELP makes taxable supplies in the United Kingdom, UK VAT registration is triggered at the statutory threshold of GBP 90,000 of taxable turnover per 12-month period; separate EU distance-selling thresholds and import VAT obligations may apply to physical movements of monitors into EU member states.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable at Gibraltar level. Exported ECG patient monitors are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation, so there is no Gibraltar VAT zero-rating claim or export refund to substantiate. Where the same goods transit the United Kingdom or an EU member state, for instance where the ELP imports the monitors into the UK and subsequently re-exports them, a UK zero-rating claim may be made under section 30 of the VAT Act 1994, or under Article 146 of the EU VAT Directive for an EU export, provided the goods physically depart the UK or EU within three months, the supplier retains official customs evidence such as a CDS export entry, transport documents such as CMR, bill of lading or air waybill, and export commercial documentation naming the Gibraltar ELP as exporter. A Gibraltar-based overseas trader may also reclaim UK input VAT under the statutory refund procedures for goods or services purchased in the UK for business purposes.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD release certificate) evidencing physical departure of the goods from the Gibraltar customs territory
  • Commercial invoice in English stating the HS 9018.12 or 9018.19 classification for ECG monitors, invoice number, Incoterms, unit prices, total declared value, and net and gross weights
  • Packing list and transport document, being a Bill of Lading, airway bill or CMR consignment note, identifying the ELP as exporter or consignor
  • Certificate of origin, such as EUR.1, Form A, or a statement of origin, where the ELP claims preferential tariff treatment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103/MT202 evidence showing export proceeds were repatriated and credited before the statutory deadline, used by the competent authorities to verify the arm's-length declared value and Gibraltar-source allocation
  • UK or EU transit accompanying document through the NCTS, and, where applicable, the UK CDS entry summary declaration or import entry for goods routed via the United Kingdom

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory B2B e-invoicing, clearance API, or digital stamping regime operates in Gibraltar. Invoicing follows ordinary commercial law requirements, namely a detailed English-language invoice with the supplier, recipient, description, quantity, unit price, Incoterms and an explicit statement that no VAT applies under the Gibraltar tax system. Customs declarations are filed electronically through the Gibraltar customs clearance system using the Single Administrative Document format; where goods transit the European Union or the United Kingdom, clearance is processed through the NCTS and the UK CDS/CHIEF ecosystems. There is no statutory requirement for an API-based integration with the tax authority for invoice clearance or stamping.

In Gibraltar, deliberate or negligent inaccuracies in customs or excise filings attract civil penalties of up to 100% of the duty evaded, forfeiture and seizure of the goods, and criminal liability under the Customs Ordinance with fines of up to three times the duty and imprisonment of up to five years. Where UK VAT zero-rating is separately claimed for an onward UK export, HM Revenue and Customs may issue assessments within the four-year statutory period, extended to twenty years where offshore matters or fraud are involved, charge default interest, and impose accuracy penalties under Schedule 24 to the Finance Act 2007 ranging from 15% to 200%, with up to 200% for deliberate and concealed behaviour. Joint and several liability can be imposed under section 77A of the VAT Act 1994 on parties participating in fraudulent evasion schemes, and criminal prosecution under section 72 of the VAT Act 1994 carries an unlimited fine and imprisonment of up to seven years. Under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act, false or fraudulent partnership returns and deliberate concealment of profits expose the partners to penalties of up to 150% of the tax under-assessed, interest, reopening of open assessment years, and criminal penalties of imprisonment of up to ten years for tax evasion; where an ELP is misused to disguise the economic activity of a single principal, the Commissioner may disregard the partnership structure and assess the principal directly.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound is pegged at par (1:1) to the UK pound sterling under a currency-board-style mechanism; full convertibility exists on both current and capital account. Dividends, profit distributions, and return-of-capital remittances from a pass-through Exempted Limited Partnership are unrestricted and require no regulator approval, licensing, or pre-declaration. Sterling circulates interchangeably in practice, so currency-convertibility risk for inbound capital and outbound trade proceeds is de minimis.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender of export proceeds and no mandatory conversion ratio applies. Gibraltar has maintained an open exchange-control regime since the United Kingdom repealed controls in 1979. Exporters of medical monitoring hardware may therefore retain receivables in any freely convertible currency, utilise multi-currency corporate accounts, and convert at commercial bank rates without any central-bank direction or surrender timeline.

Central bank supervision: Government of Gibraltar (Currency Notes Act 1934) for Gibraltar pound issuance, with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) responsible for prudential regulation and AML supervision of banks, EMIs, and trade-finance intermediaries. The UK HM Treasury retains sovereign currency-law competence, but neither HM Treasury nor the Government of Gibraltar operates an operative exchange-control authority over GIP/GBP transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium for operational cash-flow and low-value disbursement, Low for capital escrow and trade-finance clearing. UK-regulated EMI fintechs (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Currencycloud) may accept a Gibraltar ELP remotely and provide multi-currency collection/disbursement wallets. However, (i) EMI accounts are not deposit-taking bank accounts and hold no Gibraltar Deposit Guarantee Scheme protection; (ii) EMIs are generally unable to open, confirm, or settle commercial letters of credit; (iii) the structural opacity of a pass-through ELP is often treated as an elevated compliance red flag by EMI automated onboarding engines; and (iv) EMIs cannot act as the primary clearing infrastructure for high-value capital injection or LC-based trade settlements. The blueprint restricts EMIs to secondary collection wallets for small ticket export receivables, with daily sweeps to a tier-1 clearing bank.

Route the injection exclusively from the partner's own long-standing regulated-banking relationship, embed the remitter identity in the SWIFT MT103, and reference the capital-call notice and partnership deed payment clause.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from limited partners are routed via SWIFT MT103 (or CHAPS/SEPA for same-day GBP/EUR) directly to the ELP's capital escrow account at a Gibraltar tier-1 bank. The remittance must include the purpose code 'Capital Call [number] under ELP Partnership Agreement dated [date]' together with the subscribing partner's name. The receiving bank verifies each remitter against the onboarding UBO/partner KYC file and the register of contributions; no exchange-control approval is required. For multi-currency injection, the ELP maintains segregated GBP/EUR/USD sub-ledgers and executes a minuted net-conversion at bank board rates with an underlying FX contract where the volume exceeds daily operating need. The standard compliance rule is 100% remitter-to-partner identity to avoid third-party-payment rejection.Trade settlements are executed through two complementary clearing rails. For component-vendor purchases: irrevocable, confirmed at-sight commercial letters of credit are issued by the ELP's Gibraltar bank in favour of the vendor via SWIFT MT760, with presentation documents comprising commercial invoice, packing list, certificates of calibration, and transport/insurance papers, paid at sight by MT202/MT103. For advance and open-account payments: SWIFT GPI MT103 is used with a structured remittance containing 'L/C-ref / Invoice-ref / ISIC 3250 assembled patient monitors', with high-value GBP cleared through CHAPS, EUR through SEPA Credit Transfer, and USD through the bank's US correspondent (Fedwire/CHIPS lane, SWIFT GPI trackable). Each outbound tier is instrumented with a counterparty fallback (LC instead of open-account) and a disbursement calendar aligned to supplier payment terms to eliminate any appearance of round-tripping.Plain vanilla outright forward contracts (EUR/GBP and USD/GBP) for confirmed invoice receivables and supplier payablesFX swaps / rolling forward contracts covering periodic working-capital translation and rollover of maturity mismatchesNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) strictly limited to illiquid counter-party currencies outside the GBP core, where the export receivables are not deliverableCross-currency swaps (EUR/GBP) used only for matching EUR-denominated commercial LC flows against GBP functional-currency liquidity

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File / Master File are mandatory when the Gibraltar ELP's annual turnover exceeds GIP 50 million (GBP 50 million) and the aggregate value of controlled cross-border transactions exceeds GIP 5 million per annum. For CbC reporting, the parent group must consolidate to a turnover above GIP 750 million.

Economic substance scope: An ELP that carries on a relevant activity (e.g., financing, leasing, distribution, or holding) is subject to Gibraltar's Economic Substance Act. It must maintain a level of employees, premises, and expenditure proportionate to its activity, direct strategic decisions from Gibraltar, and provide annual substance evidence along with its tax return. Failure to meet the test leads to the issuing of a penalty and possible strike-off; in serious cases the ELP is not recognized for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act, a civil penalty of up to 100% of the unpaid tax, plus interest from the due date. Criminal prosecution may result in a fine of up to 200% of the evaded tax and imprisonment for up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the partnership veil and hold the general partner and any controlling limited partner jointly and severally liable for the tax and penalties if the incorrect return was signed knowingly. A travel restriction order may be imposed as a preventive measure.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Act 2020, a false declaration or failure to meet the substance test incurs a fine of £1,000,000. Continued breach leads to an application to strike the ELP off the register.

Director personal liability: Each general partner (or equivalent manager) is personally liable for making a false substance declaration; the court may impose a fine of up to £10,000 per declaration and disqualify the individual from holding any corporate office in Gibraltar for up to five years. For egregious cases, an order for restitution and asset freezing may be made.
AML – Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The Financial Services Act and Beneficial Ownership Regulations expose the entity to a regulatory fine of up to £2,000,000 for each contravention; criminal liability can attract imprisonment not exceeding 7 years for individuals.

Director personal liability: Where the MLRO is not appointed or is found to be willfully blind, the general partner is personally criminally liable. Sanctions include a fine on conviction, confiscation of the partner's gains, and restrictions on mental capacity to act as a director or partner within Gibraltar.
