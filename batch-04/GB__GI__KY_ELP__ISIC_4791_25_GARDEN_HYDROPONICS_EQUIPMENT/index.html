Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (official portal: taxation.gi)

E-government filing portal

The non-resident ELP must first be registered under the Exempted Limited Partnerships Act (Cap. 87) with the Registrar of Companies, who issues the certificate of registration. The ELP then files an application with the Income Tax Office, submitting the partnership deed, certificate of registration, and particulars of all general and limited partners. Upon verification, the Income Tax Office issues a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) to the ELP for administrative, customs and employer reporting purposes. Since the ELP is fiscally transparent, no Gibraltar TRN is issued to each foreign partner unless they derive Gibraltar-source income; partners use their home jurisdiction TINs for information reporting.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under the Income Tax (Partnerships) Rules, an ELP may make a binding election to be taxed as an entity for Gibraltar tax purposes only in exceptional cases involving hybrid mismatch avoidance. No check-the-box regime exists; however, the Income Tax (Anti-Hybrid) Rules provide a one-time aligning election for partnerships classified as transparent in Gibraltar but opaque in the UK or another partner jurisdiction, ensuring consistent treatment and preventing double deduction or DDNR outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax regime in Gibraltar; indirect taxation is limited to customs and excise duties on physical imports

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - Gibraltar levies no VAT/GST/SUT, so no registration threshold exists. Importers must appoint a licensed customs declarant or become an Economic Operator for customs purposes regardless of the value or volume of goods brought into Gibraltar.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Gibraltar has no VAT system under which export supplies can be zero-rated. Goods exported from Gibraltar are outside the scope of any Gibraltar consumption tax; no input VAT exists to reclaim. For customs purposes, export clearances are used only for statistical and duty-exclusion verification.

  • Gibraltar Custom House Export Declaration (electronic submission)
  • Commercial Invoice in triplicate
  • Packing List
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / CMR
  • Customs Procedure Code and duty-status supporting documents

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing, or digital stamping obligation exists in Gibraltar. The Income Tax Act requires ordinary invoices and records for business substantiation, but there is no government-linked API platform or B2B/B2C electronic invoicing mandate.

No VAT zero-rating clawback exists. However, fraudulent export declarations or misuse of customs procedures attract civil penalties of up to 100% of duty evaded, criminal fines up to £10,000, and imprisonment up to two years under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance 2014. Retrospective assessment may extend to six years for negligence and 20 years for deliberate false statements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies to both current and capital account transactions. There are no statutory exchange controls, prior approval requirements, or limits on the holding or repatriation of foreign currency. GIP circulates interchangeably with GBP; the territory accepts GBP, EUR, and USD freely in commercial banking. Profit remittance is unrestricted: the ELP is tax-transparent under the Income Tax Act 2010, non-resident partners are taxable only on Gibraltar-source income, and Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on distributions, interest, royalties, or dividends. The only practical hurdle for an e-commerce ELP is banking due diligence: the account-holding bank will require evidence of economic substance (registered office, local general-partner administration, minutes of partner meetings held in Gibraltar) to distinguish a genuine commercial treasury from a conduit arrangement. Since ISIC 4791 online retail via Stripe/PayPal is not a 'relevant activity' under Part 10A substance rules, no additional substance is statutorily mandated, but a substance memorandum is nonetheless advisable for seamless onboarding.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Gibraltar is a free-currency offshore special territory with zero mandatory surrender or conversion ratios for export proceeds. The ELP may retain all collections in GIP, GBP, EUR, or USD indefinitely, with no statutory repatriation timeline, no conversion quotas, and no central-bank filing obligations. Treasury decisions on currency mix are governed solely by the partnership agreement, commercial hedging policy, and the bank's facility covenants.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar operates without a domestic central bank. The Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Currency) issues the Gibraltar Pound (GIP), pegged at par to GBP under the Currency Notes Act 1934 (as amended). The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the prudential, AML/CFT, and conduct regulator for all authorized banks, e-money institutions, and payment service providers under the Financial Services Act 2019. HM Treasury and the Bank of England set UK monetary policy, which Gibraltar follows de facto through the currency peg. The UK–Gibraltar Treaty on the Exchange of Information and the post-Brexit financial regulatory partnership framework govern cross-border supervisory cooperation. No exchange-control authority exists in Gibraltar; the GFSC maintains the Authorized Bank register and enforces the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and Sanctions Act 2019.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a secondary collections/disbursements rail; LOW-MEDIUM as a primary account. Legal constraints: (i) UK-licensed EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, Currencycloud) lost automatic EEA passporting post-Brexit and require a GFSC notification or authorization route under the Financial Services Act 2019 when serving a Gibraltar-domiciled entity; (ii) the ELP must satisfy the EMI's internal EDD on offshore partnerships, including UBO disclosure and source-of-funds evidence; (iii) Stripe and PayPal gateway settlements may be received by an EMI only if the EMI holds valid e-money authorization in a jurisdiction the gateway recognizes and the account-holder legal name exactly matches the ELP; (iv) an EMI cannot issue GIP-denominated trade instruments, commercial letters of credit, or provide correspondent SWIFT clearing for high-value trade payments; (v) Tier-1 bank infrastructure therefore remains mandatory for capital escrow, LC issuance, and large MT103 disbursements. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: (i) partner injects via SWIFT MT103 from the partner's home bank to the ELP's Gibraltar Capital Escrow Account with structured remittance field 70 containing 'CAPT' (ISO 20022 capital contribution) or 'CFDI' (foreign direct investment) plus the reference 'GB__GI__KY_ELP__ISIC_4791'; (ii) the receiving bank's treasury operations verify the sender against the registered partner list before crediting; (iii) any contribution equal to or above GBP/EUR 10,000 triggers tier-2 source-of-funds scrutiny — the ELP pre-files the partnership contribution resolution with the bank's onboarding team; (iv) after verification, funds are swept from escrow to the operational account via internal transfer using purpose code 'CFDR' (direct investment, other); (v) Stripe and PayPal are never used for capital injections — those rails are strictly for customer collections; (vi) all Inbound MT103s must include the ELP's full legal name and registration number to avoid name-matching delays in the bank's sanctions engine.Clearing rails: (i) supplier trade settlements below USD 250,000 equivalent — SWIFT MT103 over correspondents (Citibank New York, HSBC London, or Deutsche Frankfurt) with structured beneficiary line, EORI/VAT number, and commercial invoice reference in field 70; (ii) open-account trade above USD 250,000 equivalent — Commercial Letter of Credit via MT700 issued by the Gibraltarian bank, or standby LC via MT760 where the supplier requires security; (iii) EU supplier payouts — SEPA Instant Credit or SEPA Credit Transfer via EBA Clearing through the bank's euro clearing alias (Banco de Sabadell branch network); (iv) UK/Gibraltar local expenses (warehouse leases, logistics, professional fees) — Faster Payments or CHAPS in GIP/GBP from the operational account; (v) Stripe/PayPal gateway settlement sweeps — batched ACH or SWIFT MT103 daily sweeps into the Tier-1 operating account with payment detail 'PSP Settlement'; (vi) partner profit distributions — MT103 with purpose code 'DIVD' only after the general partner executes a written distribution resolution and the ELP provides a tax-clearance self-certificate; (vii) every outbound MT103 must carry a unique invoice reference in subfield 70 pre-approved by the bank's transaction-monitoring engine to prevent frozen-funds incidents.FX Forward: plain-vanilla deliverable forward (GIP/GBP vs USD/EUR) up to 24 months with same-day settlement, documented under ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or local bank's FX Master Forward Confirmation.NDF (Non-Deliverable Forward): permissible only where a deliverable GIP offshore market is unavailable; in practice redundant due to the 1:1 GIP-GBP peg; offered only via London clearing under ISDA 1998 FX and Currency Option Definitions.Cross-Currency Swap (FX Swap): short-term liquidity swap-and-deposit transaction up to 12 months for cash shifting between GBP/EUR/USD accounts without incremental refinancing cost.Collared FX Forward (Range Forward): permissible hedging-only instrument where the bank's derivatives desk marks it as a hedge against known supplier invoices in EUR or USD.European-Style Currency Options: allowed on a case-by-case basis strictly as a hedge for a signed order pipeline; the ELP must execute a 'non-dealing, hedging-only' declaration and file a treasury policy memorandum with the GFSC-regulated bank upon request.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a secondary collections/disbursements rail; LOW-MEDIUM as a primary account. Legal constraints: (i) UK-licensed EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, Currencycloud) lost automatic EEA passporting post-Brexit and require a GFSC notification or authorization route under the Financial Services Act 2019 when serving a Gibraltar-domiciled entity; (ii) the ELP must satisfy the EMI's internal EDD on offshore partnerships, including UBO disclosure and source-of-funds evidence; (iii) Stripe and PayPal gateway settlements may be received by an EMI only if the EMI holds valid e-money authorization in a jurisdiction the gateway recognizes and the account-holder legal name exactly matches the ELP; (iv) an EMI cannot issue GIP-denominated trade instruments, commercial letters of credit, or provide correspondent SWIFT clearing for high-value trade payments; (v) Tier-1 bank infrastructure therefore remains mandatory for capital escrow, LC issuance, and large MT103 disbursements. Recommended structure: primary Tier-1 bank account plus one UK/EU-licensed EMI for low-value, high-frequency gateway payouts and contractor disbursements, segregated by a documented treasury policy.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No separate Gibraltar statutory monetary threshold applies to every ELP; OECD/Gibraltar transfer pricing rules require mandatory Local File documentation where the ELP's controlled transactions with related parties exceed GIP 1,000,000 in aggregate in an accounting period. Master File documentation is required when the consolidated group turnover of the ultimate parent exceeds EUR 750,000,000. Cross-border e-commerce transactions with connected distributors, overseas warehouses, and payment aggregators must be benchmarked regardless of quantum where a tax advantage is sought.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance (Information and Compliance) Regulations 2020 apply to ELPs carrying on a relevant activity in Gibraltar. Online retail of garden hydroponics equipment, where the ELP purchases goods and resells them through e-commerce channels, is capable of being characterised as a 'distribution and service centre' relevant activity. A non-resident ELP without staff, physical premises, or management decisions in Gibraltar must demonstrate a low-risk commercial profile; failure to provide such evidence may result in reclassification as a Gibraltar-resident relevant activity and imposition of the substance tests. All ELPs, irrespective of profit level, must maintain contemporaneous records of the place of key decision-making, storage, suppliers, customers, and banking flows.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Concealment of Gibraltar-Source E-Commerce Profits

Statutory sanction: The taxpayer is liable to the full amount of underpaid tax at 12.5%, interest at the prescribed penalty rate, and a civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded. Criminal tax evasion is punishable by an unlimited fine and/or imprisonment for up to 7 years, together with confiscation proceedings under the Proceeds of Crime Act.

Director personal liability: The General Partner's directors and the ELP's controlling partners may have the corporate veil pierced where the ELP was used to perpetrate tax evasion; they are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties and may be subject to travel bans, asset freezing orders, director disqualification, and custodial sentences.
Shell Company - Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Income Tax Office may deem the ELP a Gibraltar resident person, assess the entire foreign and Gibraltar-source income at 12.5%, and impose information penalties of up to GIP 50,000 per contravention plus GIP 500 per day. Continued default may lead to compulsory strike-off and dissolution.

Director personal liability: Directors of the General Partner are personally liable for penalties incurred as a result of knowingly permitting the partnership to operate without substance; they may be disqualified for up to 15 years and prohibited from acting as director or general partner of any Gibraltar entity.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration - Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust the controlled transaction to the arm's length result, increase assessable profits, impose additional tax at 12.5%, and apply a transfer pricing penalty of up to 100% of the under-reported profit. Interest accrues from the date the tax would have been payable.

Director personal liability: Officers and directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation or knowingly direct the ELP to enter non-arm's length arrangements are personally liable for civil restitution and may be subject to criminal prosecution for cheating the public revenue; travel restrictions and seizure of personal assets are available enforcement mechanisms.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach - Hidden Partners and Unclean Banking Flows

Statutory sanction: Breach of the Gibraltar Money Laundering Regulations attracts financial penalties up to GIP 250,000 per breach and daily default fines; criminal money laundering is punishable by up to 14 years' imprisonment and confiscation of all proceeds. The Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit may freeze bank accounts and payment processor balances.

Director personal liability: Directors of the General Partner are personally criminally liable where they fail to identify beneficial owners or knowingly permit the ELP to be used as an instrument of money laundering. In addition to imprisonment, they may face civil disqualification, personal confiscation orders, and travel bans pending final determination.
