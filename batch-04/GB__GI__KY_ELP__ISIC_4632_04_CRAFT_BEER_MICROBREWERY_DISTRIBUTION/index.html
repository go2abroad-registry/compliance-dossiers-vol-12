Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar Customs & Income Tax Department (Income Tax Office), 13 Europort, Gibraltar; principal online portal: https://www.tax.gov.gi; customs and excise clearance portal operated by Gibraltar HM Customs at https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/customs; corporate registry interface via Gibraltar Companies House (Registrar of Companies) at https://www.companieshouse.gi.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) must: (1) appoint a Gibraltar-resident general partner (typically a Gibraltar-licensed company or an individual named qualifying partner); (2) apply to the Registrar of Companies under the Exempted Limited Partnerships Act 2014 to be registered as an exempted limited partnership and obtain the Certificate of Exemption; (3) register the ELP for income tax at the Gibraltar Income Tax Office by filing Form P/ELP on which the partnership is allocated a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (GTRN); (4) file an annual partnership return (ITP) disclosing each partner's share of partnership profits; (5) register with HM Customs and the Collector of Customs for excise duty purposes, obtaining a Liquor Importer's Licence and, where applicable, a bonded warehousekeeper's licence for beer in transit or duty-suspension; (6) obtain a fiscal responsibility certificate via a Gibraltar tax agent where any general partner is a corporate entity; (7) apply to the Commissioner of Income Tax for a 'qualifying partnership' designation under Section 152A of the Income Tax Act 2010 so that non-resident limited partners benefit from Gibraltar's territoriality rule and are not chargeable to Gibraltar income tax on partnership profits derived otherwise than through a Gibraltar permanent establishment. Non-resident partners are not issued individual Gibraltar TINs unless they have a Gibraltar permanent establishment or a direct Gibraltar tax return obligation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Available elections under Gibraltar law: (a) election by the general partner under Section 152A of the Income Tax Act 2010 for the ELP to be treated as a qualifying partnership, rendering non-resident limited partners exempt on foreign-source and non-Gibraltar PE income; (b) annual computation election under Section 38B of the Income Tax Act 2010, available to qualifying Gibraltar-incorporated trading companies within the group, which caps annual tax liability at a statutory maximum to the extent the election is not disapplied by the EU/OECD rules; (c) irrevocable transparency election confirming the ELP's pass-through status for Gibraltar tax purposes; (d) notification election for capital-equity treatment of partners' capital contributions; (e) Gibraltar maintains no separate check-the-box mechanism equivalent to US Form 8832, and no hybrid-mismatch election under ATAD 2 (Gibraltar has not transposed ATAD 2 post-Brexit) although factual hybrid outcomes must nonetheless be reported to the Gibraltar tax authority under the international tax compliance framework.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar has no VAT and no GST; indirect taxation operates through the Customs Duties Ordinance, the Alcoholic Beverages Ordinance, and 'Purchase Tax' levied selectively on imported goods; beer distribution is principally captured by Alcohol Excise Duty on import (duty bands by original wort gravity and ABV); post-Brexit alignment with EU VAT is pending ratification of the UK-EU Treaty on Gibraltar, which would introduce a VAT-compatible regime on cross-border movements to Spain and the EU funnel.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover de minimis exists for excise goods: any person importing craft beer into Gibraltar must be registered with HM Customs as an importer and hold a Liquor Importer's Licence under the Alcoholic Beverages Ordinance, with statutory registration required at the first import (threshold effectively GIP 0); an excise warehousekeeper's licence is required where hazy-IPA kegs are held under duty suspension for onward export; a separate purchase-tax registration threshold of GIP 0 applies to taxable goods imported for domestic sale; there is no general Gibraltar VAT registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Gibraltar-registered exporter may claim duty-relief (effectively zero-rating) on craft beer exported from Gibraltar provided that: (i) the exporter is the holder of an excise suspension authorisation or an export licence under the Customs Duties Ordinance; (ii) the beer physically leaves Gibraltar on an approved direct-export route or as ship's stores; (iii) delivery occurs under an outcome-based movement with customs-supervised exit, including the EU/UK transit procedure via NCTS when routed through Spain or the UK; (iv) the foreign buyer is an arm's-length third party or foreign-affiliated distributor; (v) proof of arrival at the final foreign destination is furnished; and (vi) the exporter satisfies the documentary-completeness condition within 3 months of export. Income tax zero-rating applies analogously to offshore distribution services rendered by non-resident partners under the territoriality principle, where the service is arranged in Gibraltar but wholly performed abroad and derives no Gibraltar-source trading income.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (GD Ex Sim / SAD C200 with MRN where transit is used)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List with Incoterms 2020 and cold-chain terms
  • Bill of Lading, CMR or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Gibraltar
  • Certificate of Analysis and Brewers' Certificate of Consignment (SOFA/ABV declaration)

Gibraltar does not currently mandate real-time e-invoicing, digital stamping, or continuous transaction controls; customs clearance of craft beer is performed electronically through the Gibraltar Customs SEED goods-declaration system, with the Single Administrative Document (SAD) C200/C201 series required; movements into the EU/UK use NCTS transit procedures with an electronic Movement Reference Number (MRN) and, where the UK-EU Treaty is ratified, a fiscal e-marking and e-invoicing obligation for B2B alcohol distribution will apply; GST-style point-of-sale data reporting is not yet in force for distributors.

Export duty-relief claims are subject to review by Gibraltar HM Customs and the Customs & Income Tax Department. Statutory penalties include: (i) forfeiture and confiscation of the goods under the Customs Duties Ordinance; (ii) a financial penalty up to three times the duty evaded or GIP 5,000, whichever is higher, for false export declarations; (iii) retrospective clawback of all relieved excise duty with statutory interest of 1% per month compounded where goods are diverted to domestic consumption; (iv) licence revocation of the importer/exporter and disqualification of the fiscal representative; (v) additional income tax penalties under Section 74 of the Income Tax Act 2010 of up to 150% of tax under-assessed in cases where export documentation fraud affects the partnership's chargeable profits; (vi) criminal sanction of imprisonment up to 7 years for fraudulent misuse of export zero-rating; the retrospective assessment window for excise-duty clawback is 6 years, extending to 11 years where fraud or wilful neglect is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains a fully liberalised exchange-control environment. The GIP is pegged at par to GBP and is freely convertible on both current and capital account. Profit remittances by a non-resident Exempted Limited Partnership to foreign limited partners are unrestricted if the partnership does not carry on a trade or business in Gibraltar on a permanent establishment basis. No advance regulatory approval is needed for capital distributions, provided the partnership has filed its annual return, audited financial statements, and maintained current beneficial-ownership records with the GFSC Registry.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds applies. Foreign-currency receipts from craft beer sales can be held offshore or in Gibraltar-denominated accounts without exchange-control constraints. The ELP must, however, document all export proceeds with commercial invoices, bills of lading, and customs declarations to support tax-residency claims and satisfy GFSC AML/CFT record-keeping obligations.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the integrated financial regulator and foreign-currency supervisory authority; there is no separate central bank — Gibraltar pound (GIP) notes are issued by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934, and the Bank of England has no monetary authority in Gibraltar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for non-EU/offshore digital EMIs. Gibraltar is not part of the EU/EEA passporting regime post-Brexit; an e-money institution licensed solely in Malta, the UK, or an EU state cannot passport into Gibraltar without a separate GFSC e-money authorisation under the Financial Services (E-Money) Act 2011. Offshore digital EMIs without local physical presence and banking guarantee arrangements will not obtain Gibraltar sort codes or access domestic retail clearing circuits. A locally licensed EMI or a bank with e-money permission is required for meaningful operational viability.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar law does not set an express GIP monetary threshold for Local File preparation. The Commissioner may require transfer pricing documentation for any cross-border controlled transaction. As a prudential compliance standard, the ELP should prepare a Local File when aggregate related-party transactions exceed GIP 500,000 per annum and a Master File/Country-by-Country Report when the ultimate parent group meets the OECD threshold of GIP 750 million consolidated turnover.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to the ELP because distribution and service centre is a relevant activity. The ELP must maintain a physical office, employ personnel with decision-making authority, incur operational expenditure proportionate to the beer distribution turnover, manage commercial risks from Gibraltar, and file an annual ESR return. Passive pass-through status does not remove the ESR obligation; a failure to demonstrate economic substance can expose the ELP to penalties, reclassification of income, and exchange of information with foreign tax authorities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Failure to File

Statutory sanction: Best-of-judgment assessment by the Commissioner; interest from the due date; civil penalty up to 100% of the tax omitted where the failure is deliberate and concealed; daily default fines and suspension of the ELP's trading licence.

Director personal liability: The general partner and directors of a corporate general partner may be jointly and severally liable for restitution of diverted tax; the Supreme Court can issue a travel restriction order; conviction for cheating the public revenue carries imprisonment for up to 7 years.
Transfer Pricing Abuse / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length reallocation of adjusted profits to Gibraltar with tax at 12.5%; late payment interest; transfer pricing penalty of up to 40% of the resulting tax adjustment; the Commissioner may disallow claimed losses and impose daily record-keeping penalties.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly signs false transfer pricing documentation can be charged with fraudulent tax evasion; the corporate veil may be pierced for civil restitution of the adjusted tax; disqualification from managing a Gibraltar limited partnership for up to 15 years and a court-ordered travel ban.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR penalties up to GIP 100,000 per breach and GIP 250,000 for repeated non-compliance; the Registrar may strike off the ELP; HMRC and the Gibraltar Commissioner may exchange information, triggering a UK tax and economic substance inquiry.

Director personal liability: Officers in default, including the general partner, are personally liable for ESR penalties; where the exemption from corporate tax is obtained by deception, the corporate veil is pierced and partners are jointly and severally liable for all unpaid taxes; imprisonment may follow for fraud.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar AML legislation permits fines up to GIP 500,000, asset freezing orders, and confiscation; beneficial ownership register defaults attract penalties of up to GIP 10,000 plus GIP 1,000 per day; the regulatory authority may suspend the ELP from wholesale distribution.

Director personal liability: Individuals concerned in laundering proceeds of beer distribution face imprisonment for up to 14 years; banks may freeze ELP accounts; directors who knowingly permit AML failures are subject to civil restitution, travel bans, and disqualification from holding corporate office.
