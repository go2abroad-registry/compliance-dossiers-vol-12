Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Tax Return Portal and e-services via TransPaX; physically located at Portland House, Gibraltar)

E-government filing portal

A foreign Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) must first be established and registered with the Gibraltar Companies Registry. Following registration, the partnership (through its registered agent) must file an application with the Income Tax Office (ITO) for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) by submitting: (i) certified certificate of registration, (ii) partnership deed and amended deeds, (iii) details and identification documents of all partners, and (iv) registered office address proof. No separate VAT registration ID is issued because Gibraltar does not levy VAT. The TIN is used for all tax filings and domestic customs declarations.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) must first be established and registered with the Gibraltar Companies Registry. Following registration, the partnership (through its registered agent) must file an application with the Income Tax Office (ITO) for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) by submitting: (i) certified certificate of registration, (ii) partnership deed and amended deeds, (iii) details and identification documents of all partners, and (iv) registered office address proof. No separate VAT registration ID is issued because Gibraltar does not levy VAT. The TIN is used for all tax filings and domestic customs declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Gibraltar law treats ELPs as fiscally transparent by default, and no 'check-the-box' election mechanism exists under Gibraltar tax law. Partners are taxed individually on their share of profits, either as individuals or as companies. No hybrid mismatch election is separately available. For UK-connected partnerships, UK hybrid mismatch rules may apply at the partner level, but Gibraltar domestic law does not offer elective reclassification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No General VAT/GST – Limited Customs and Excise Duties under the Customs Ordinance

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – Gibraltar imposes no turnover-based indirect tax registration threshold. Businesses importing goods or carrying out customs operations must obtain a Business Registration Certificate from the Customs and Excise Department, which includes a local Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI-like) reference number.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable to Gibraltar domestic tax. Gibraltar does not levy VAT, so export zero-rating cannot be claimed locally. Sales of goods exported from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax. If the entity becomes voluntarily VAT-registered in the United Kingdom (which generally requires a UK establishment), UK VAT zero-rating for exports may apply subject to statutory evidence. In all other cases, the goods will be subject to import VAT and customs duties in the destination jurisdiction (e.g., EU or UK) at that jurisdiction's rates.

  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight)
  • Original Certificate of Origin (when preferential tariff treatment is claimed)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from the receiving bank (for export payment tracing)

None – Gibraltar has no statutory electronic invoicing mandate, no real-time fiscal clearance requirement, no API-based reporting obligation, and no digital stamping regime. Commercial invoices must be issued for legal and accounting purposes, but they are not cleared or pre-authorised by any tax fiscal system.

Fraudulent or abusive claims of export VAT zero-rating in the United Kingdom, where applicable, are subject to HMRC penalties up to 100% of the VAT evaded, with retrospective assessments extending back 4 years, and potential civil penalties for deliberate concealment. In Gibraltar, false export declarations or customs irregularities may result in seizure of goods, monetary penalties up to £10,000 per instance, suspension or revocation of customs registration, and criminal prosecution in cases of intentional tax evasion. No GB or GI export VAT refund mechanism exists independently of a valid VAT registration in a UK/EU jurisdiction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full convertibility on both current and capital account items for non-resident Exempted Limited Partnerships. No exchange control legislation restricts profit remittance, dividend-like distributions, or capital repatriation. An ELP may invoice in GBP, USD, or EUR, hold multi-currency bank accounts, and settle partner allocations offshore without prior treasury consent. No Gibraltarian withholding tax is imposed on distributions to non-resident partners, and no exchange-control approval is required for conversion from GIP to GBP or any fully convertible major currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratios for export proceeds in Gibraltar. Exporters are not required to surrender or repatriate trade receivables to the local banking system. Settlement currency may be retained in original form, kept in offshore or domestic multi-currency accounts, or used directly for supplier disbursements. If receipts are denominated in GIP, conversion to GBP at parity and onward wire transfer is processed by banks without GFSC or government approval.

Central bank supervision: There is no formal central bank in Gibraltar. Foreign exchange administration is absent; HM Government of Gibraltar acts as the monetary authority for note issuance, and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the banking, e-money, and investment regulatory authority. The Bank of England does not supervise Gibraltar banks. Sterling remains legal tender, with the Gibraltar pound (GIP) pegged at par to GBP and freely interchangeable subject to bank practice.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

The feasibility rating is Medium for full treasury reliance and High as a supplementary collection rail. Gibraltar-licensed and EEA/UK-authorized e-money institutions (EMIs) such as Stripe, PayPal, and select business EMI platforms may be used to capture online auto-parts card payments, but they are not considered substitutes for a licensed corporate bank account. Offshore digital EMIs without a local or EEA/UK license face viability constraints for capital escrow duties, trade-based lending, documentary credits, and FX counterparty approvals. The ELP should integrate EMIs solely as inbound payment gateways and settle surplus balances onward to a GFSC-regulated or UK bank account; no tax or AML advantage is gained by using an unlicensed digital-only bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections should be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 with unambiguous purpose codes such as 'Investment in share or partnership capital' or 'Direct investment contribution'. UK-origin capital should be sent via CHAPS in GBP or SEPA in EUR; US-origin capital should be sent via Fedwire or SWIFT in USD. The remitter name must correspond exactly to the partner name in the limited partnership agreement, and the payment reference must quote the ELP's registered number and the capital call notice number. Banks require the executed capital call notice, the board consent or general partner resolution, and confirmation that the funds are drawn from a bank account in the partner's own name. No physical FX surrender occurs, but the receiving Gibraltar bank may request the underlying source-of-funds evidence for any wire above its reporting threshold. For GIP-denominated contributions, the bank will apply parity with GBP; for direct USD or EUR contributions, the ELP should establish multi-currency accounts to avoid forced conversion.Outbound supplier payments for auto-parts inventory should use SWIFT MT103 bank transfers for open-account purchases, immediate settlement, and recurring disbursements to established suppliers. Commercial letters of credit must be reserved for high-value first-time supplier contracts or jurisdictions with higher documentary credit friction; those should be irrevocable, confirmed by a UK or Gibraltar bank, and payable by sight draft to maintain liquidity. Intra-Europe supplier payments are efficient via SEPA Credit Transfer in EUR; UK suppliers should be paid in GBP via Faster Payments or CHAPS; China and Asia suppliers should be paid in USD via SWIFT MT103 through a clearing bank with optional CNH settlement accounts where available. Card acquirer proceeds should be swept daily from Stripe and PayPal to the operational account to reduce counterparty concentration. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

10% flat rate for Gibraltar-incorporated companies on worldwide taxable profits; non-resident corporate partners are charged 10% only on Gibraltar-source profits. MNE groups with consolidated revenue ≥ €750 million are subject to OECD Pillar Two top-up to 15%. Individuals (including ELP individual partners) are taxed at progressive rates with a maximum marginal rate of 25% (plus social insurance).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar does not impose any withholding tax on dividend distributions made by a Gibraltar entity or by an ELP to its partners. Remittances of profit shares to partners are made gross.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CbCR/Master File: triggered at consolidated group revenue of €750 million (approximately GIP 645 million) under the Gibraltar Country-by-Country Reporting Regulations. Local File: no publicly gazetted GIP statutory threshold; the Commissioner requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for all controlled transactions (including ELP procurement, logistics, and management-fee arrangements with related parties) where the aggregate value of intra-group transactions exceeds GIP 1 million per annum, or earlier on enquiry. A full OECD Action 13-aligned Local File is recommended for any related-party transaction exceeding GIP 500,000.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar does not apply a codified Economic Substance Regulations statute in the BVI/UAE style, but the EU Code of Conduct Group and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) mandate substantive local presence for all registered entities. The ELP must demonstrate: a registered office in Gibraltar, a general partner or licensed tax representative resident in Gibraltar, board or partner meetings held in Gibraltar, locally maintained statutory records, and decision-making conducted in Gibraltar (minutes, bank mandates, and e-commerce supply contracts). CRS/FATCA registration and annual filings are compulsory. A 'brass-plate' ELP whose management and trading activities occur in the UK will be re-characterised: HMRC may assert a UK permanent establishment, apply the UK anti-hybrid rules (part 6A TIOPA 2010), and impose UK corporation tax on the e-commerce profits; the Gibraltar Commissioner may likewise challenge the pass-through status and assess the ELP on a standalone basis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Underdeclared E-Commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended): penalty up to 100% of the tax evaded, plus daily default penalties up to £60 per day, interest at 8% per annum on unpaid tax, and criminal fines up to £50,000 upon conviction; in serious cases the entity is struck off and the partnership agreement voided for tax purposes.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors who knowingly sign false returns are personally prosecuted. Courts may impose travel bans (bail condition or post-conviction order), freezing orders over UK/Gibraltar real estate, and proceeds-of-crime confiscation orders requiring personal restitution of the evaded tax plus costs; imprisonment up to seven years applies for fiscal fraud.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance — Sham ELP

Statutory sanction: The Gibraltar Commissioner may disregard the partnership and assess the ELP's worldwide trading profits directly, impose s.143 tax-geared penalties, and initiate compulsory strike-off under the Limited Partnerships Act 1991. HMRC can apply UK anti-hybrid and Transfer of Assets Abroad rules (ITA 2007 pt 13), resulting in UK tax on the full profit at 19–25% plus interest; penalties up to 100% of the UK tax.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners acting as shadow general partners become jointly and severally liable for the ELP's tax debts. Directors face corporate disqualification orders of up to 10 years under the Companies Act 2014, and UK courts may issue worldwide freezing orders and travel bans; the UK Crown Court can enforce a Gibraltar confiscation order against directors personally.
AML / Sanctions / Beneficial Ownership Transparency

Statutory sanction: CRS enforcement penalties up to £250,000 for deliberate non-filing of partner/CBC information; GFSC monetary penalties based on turnover; the Beneficial Ownership (Respective) Regulations require daily penalties for ongoing default; supply-chain fraud may lead to HMRC notice and the seizure of goods at the UK border; sanctions violations carry asset-freeze measures against the ELP’s Gibraltar bank accounts.

Director personal liability: The licensed general partner may lose its GFSC licence and be banned from holding offices; individuals who make false CRS declarations face up to two years' imprisonment; directors are personally liable for fines where they participated in the concealment and may be subjected to travel bans and extradition requests from EU/UK authorities.
Cross-Border VAT and Customs Fraud (EU Distance Selling)

Statutory sanction: EU member states may issue joint-and-several VAT assessments on the ELP's EU cross-border B2C revenue at the applicable standard rate (17–27%), plus penalties of 15–100% of the VAT amount; post-Brexit UK import VAT is due automatically and HMRC can enforce against the Gibraltar entity's UK assets; customs penalties may be up to 3 times the evaded duty.

Director personal liability: Where the limited partner participates in the EU sales or fulfilment decisions, the limited liability shield is pierced and the partner becomes personally liable for VAT/duty debts; EU tax authorities routinely issue travel bans and director disqualification orders in cross-border e-commerce fraud cases; imprisonment up to 10 years can apply for customs fraud.
