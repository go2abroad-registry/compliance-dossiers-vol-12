Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Limited Partnerships Act 2010 (Gibraltar), in particular ss. 4-9 (formation and registration of limited partnerships) and ss. 12-17 (general and limited partner rights, capital contributions and exempt status); Companies Act 2014 (Gibraltar), ss. 33-37 (name, registered office and corporate governance of the corporate General Partner); Finance (Income Tax) Act (Gibraltar), as amended, for the pass-through/tax-transparent classification of the ELP and its exemption declaration.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement are prescribed for a Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership; partners may agree capital contributions in cash, in kind or by service in the Limited Partnership Agreement, and such contributions remain uncalled until demanded; the agreed initial contribution must be stated in the Statement of Limited Partnership for registration purposes.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate an Exempted Limited Partnership with the Gibraltar Companies House (Registry of Companies) under the Limited Partnerships Act 2017; a Gibraltar-resident General Partner or licensed corporate service provider is mandatory, and the Registry issues the statutory registration number. Step 2: File the partnership particulars and notice of commencement with the Income Tax Office within 30 days of commencing business; the Income Tax Office issues the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (administratively linked to the registration number). Step 3: Gibraltar has no VAT/GST registration; however, for physical toy exports the ELP must obtain a customs trader reference from HM Customs and Excise, Gibraltar. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The ELP is fiscally transparent: partners are taxed only on their allocated share and only in respect of Gibraltar-source income. Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box election. Nevertheless: (1) the partnership agreement may elect to constitute the ELP as a private fund under Schedule 2 of the Limited Partnerships Act 2017, preserving transparency while conferring contractual separate personality; (2) non-Gibraltar partners may unilaterally elect different classification for their home tax systems, for example a US partner filing IRS Form 8832 to treat the ELP as an association taxable as a corporation, generating a potential hybrid mismatch; and (3) Gibraltar has adopted OECD/BEPS hybrid mismatch rules in the Income Tax Act 2010, so a partner may make an alignment election under UK TIOPA 2010 Part 6A where dual-inclusion income is anticipated.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: no turnover tax exists and therefore no registration threshold is applicable. United Kingdom: a non-established overseas seller of low-value imported goods (consignments not exceeding GBP 135) must register for UK VAT from the very first sale, with no de minimis threshold; other UK taxable supplies trigger compulsory registration at GBP 85,000 of annual taxable turnover (2024/25 rate). Online marketplaces may be deemed to make the supply and may account for VAT instead of the ELP.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported by the ELP from Gibraltar, or from a UK warehouse to a destination outside the UK and Gibraltar, are outside the scope of Gibraltar purchase duties and qualify for zero-rating for UK VAT purposes under VATA 1994 s.30(6), provided conclusive export evidence is obtained and preserved within 3 months of supply (or within the contractual 180-day FX settlement and documentary period). Services such as offshore marketing, graphic design and IP licensing supplied to non-UK taxable businesses are outside the scope of UK VAT under place-of-supply rules. The ELP must not zero-rate toys sold to UK consumers; such sales attract 20% UK VAT at checkout.

  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / shipping carrier log for postal or consolidated toy consignments
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (HS Chapter 95 collectible vinyl figures)
  • Proof of onward import into the destination jurisdiction (UK C88/SAD or EU import declaration where applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 credit advice evidencing export sale proceeds clearing through a Gibraltar-licensed bank or UK EMI account

Neither Gibraltar nor the UK mandates real-time fiscal clearance, government API receipt stamping, or mandatory digital invoice clearance. The UK requires MTD-compatible digital records and electronic VAT return filing via HMRC APIs; B2B e-invoicing may use XML/EDI or PDF but is not centrally cleared. Physical toy imports from Gibraltar into Great Britain must be declared electronically through the UK CDS (Customs Declaration Service) before release. Gibraltar export shipments use the Gibraltar Customs electronic manifest, and where no IOSS or UK parcel arrangement is applied, the consignee pays import VAT at delivery.

Gibraltar: fraudulent or careless export declarations are prosecutable under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance (Cap. 95) with penalties up to GIP 10,000 plus three times the duty or benefit reclaimed, forfeiture of toy inventory, and imprisonment not exceeding 2 years; false income tax statements attract penalties of 100% of tax undercharged. United Kingdom/HMRC: retrospective assessments may be made for up to 4 years, extended to 20 years for deliberate and concealed behaviour, under VATA 1994; penalties under Finance Act 2007 Schedule 24 reach 100% of the evaded VAT; criminal zero-rating fraud carries up to 7 years of imprisonment and unlimited fines under VATA 1994 s.72; and CEMA 1979 export misdeclaration penalties extend to three times the value of the goods or GBP 250,000, with clawback of any VAT refund paid plus default interest at 2.5% per month.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar is treated as an offshore special territory with full sterling-zone membership for currency purposes. There are no exchange-control restrictions on current account or capital account transactions. A Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership may freely receive foreign investment, maintain multi-currency accounts, convert GIP to GBP at the fixed one-to-one parity, and remit profits to non-resident partners without Gibraltar withholding tax or profit-remittance authorisation. The only practical convertibility frictions are correspondent-bank AML/KYC requirements, beneficial ownership disclosure, and compliance with UK or international financial sanctions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rule applies in Gibraltar. Export receipts may be retained indefinitely in any currency, at any licensed bank or regulated payment institution, with a mandatory conversion ratio of 0%. There is no 90-day, 180-day, or repatriation deadline. If export receipts are routed through a UK or EU payment institution, the entity should maintain underlying invoices and delivery evidence to prove commercial substance and to prevent the bank from reclassifying flows as royalty, gambling, or unexplained treasury transactions.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no independent central bank. Currency issuance is vested in the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934, with the Gibraltar pound (GIP) issued at a one-to-one peg to Pound Sterling and backed by Bank of England notes. Banking and AML supervision is administered by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC). HM Treasury and the Bank of England retain external monetary and sanctions-related sovereignty for the United Kingdom, and UK OFSI sanctions regimes are extended to Gibraltar by Order in Council.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for low-balance payment settlement and marketplace routing, but a fintech EMI is not a substitute for a licensed Gibraltar bank account. UK FCA-regulated EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Currencycloud may accept a Gibraltar-incorporated ELP as a third-country corporate client, provided the ELP can evidence local substance, a GFSC-registered office, a general partner with a verifiable identity, and a UK/Gibraltar operational address. The principal legal constraints are: (a) post-Brexit loss of EEA passporting, so the EMI must be willing to service Gibraltar from the UK; (b) GFSC classification/approval may be triggered if the EMI establishes a physical payment branch or solicits Gibraltar customers domestically; and (c) tax-transparent ELP structures may be rejected if beneficial ownership is unclear. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions should be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the investor's bank to the Gibraltar bank's SWIFT BIC, for credit to the designated Capital Escrow Account of KY_ELP. The remitter must populate field 70 with a clear narrative such as 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — KY_ELP — [ELP registration number] — [investor name]'. Where the sending bank requires a purpose code, use an internationally recognised capital transfer code such as CAPC or the sending bank's domestic Balance of Payments capital-transfer code; the exact code should be confirmed with the relationship manager to ensure the receiving bank does not classify the receipt as trading income. All inbound wires must be accompanied by the signed contribution deed, investor KYC, source-of-funds statement, and evidence of the underlying entitlement in the ELP. For inbound flows from mainland China, the PRC investor must complete SAFE ODI/NRA filings and route through a licenced Chinese bank; for UK-to-Gibraltar flows below GBP 1 million, CHAPS/Faster Payments may be used if the receiving bank is UK-connected, but SWIFT MT103 is preferred for audit trail and FX traceability.Outbound trade disbursements for factory deposits, raw materials, freight forwarding, agency commissions, and supplier settlements should use SWIFT MT103 for direct bank-to-bank payments, with invoices, purchase orders, and shipping documents retained as underlying trade evidence. The principal legal constraints are: (a) post-Brexit loss of EEA passporting, so the EMI must be willing to service Gibraltar from the UK; (b) GFSC classification/approval may be triggered if the EMI establishes a physical payment branch or solicits Gibraltar customers domestically; and (c) tax-transparent ELP structures may be rejected if beneficial ownership is unclear. Shopify Payments and Stripe are preferred primary rails for merchant collections, with an EMI used only for intercompany settlement and low-value payouts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A mandatory Local File is required when controlled transactions exceed GBP 2,000,000 in aggregate during a fiscal year. A Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds GBP 50,000,000 and at least one Gibraltar entity is engaged in controlled transactions, or when the controlled transaction amount exceeds GBP 2,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to Gibraltar tax-resident entities or permanent establishments carrying on relevant activities such as distribution, financing, holding, or intellectual property licensing. An ELP engaged in design, rotocasting, airbrush finishing, and online merchandising of vinyl art collectibles must demonstrate adequate premises, full-time employees, and expenditure proportionate to its income-generating activities unless it is fully tax-transparent and functions through a Gibraltar-resident general partner. Failure to meet economic substance results in a penalty of up to GBP 75,000 and potential strike-off or dissolution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Fines up to GBP 200,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years under the Income Tax Crimes Act; back taxes plus a penalty of up to 100% of the understated tax; asset forfeiture orders where applicable.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner of the ELP may be made personally liable for unpaid taxes; travel bans and passport surrender may be imposed; director disqualification for up to 15 years is available.
Shell Company with No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic substance levy of GBP 10,000 to GBP 75,000 per annum; potential strike-off under the Companies Act; denial of treaty benefits; additional penalties up to GBP 100,000.

Director personal liability: The general partner inherits personal liability for penalties and confiscation orders; directors may face travel bans, surrender of passports, and disqualification from managing any company for up to 10 years.
Money Laundering / AML Controls Failure

Statutory sanction: AML regulatory fines up to GBP 500,000; criminal imprisonment for up to 14 years under the Proceeds of Crime Act; asset freezing and confiscation orders; the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit may suspend trading activity.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers are personally liable for AML breaches; travel bans and seizure of passports apply in serious cases; extradition may be sought where funds originate from overseas criminal conduct.
Transfer Pricing Malpractice

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of chargeable profits to reflect the arm's length standard; interest and penalties up to 50% of the under-declared tax; additional ESR penalties if the entity fails to satisfy substance requirements.

Director personal liability: Directors may be surcharged personally for intentional transfer pricing mispricing; disqualification for up to 12 years; serious cases may attract criminal tax fraud charges leading to imprisonment.
