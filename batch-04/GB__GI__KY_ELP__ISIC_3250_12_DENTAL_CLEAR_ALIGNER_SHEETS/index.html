Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar - Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), official portal https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi; HM Customs Gibraltar/Custom House for customs and consumption duties; Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) Companies Registry for limited partnership registration, portal https://www.fsc.gi; HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) for UK VAT and GB EORI when applicable, portal https://www.gov.uk.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) must (1) submit the prescribed limited partnership registration application (together with the executed partnership agreement, register of limited partners and consent to act of the general partner) to the GFSC Companies Registry to obtain the ELP registration number; (2) following incorporation, submit the partnership notice/return and the ELP's constitutional particulars to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office to obtain the Gibraltarian Tax Identification Number (TIN) for the partnership and to identify each partner's TIN; (3) register the non-resident corporate/individual partners in the Income Tax Office's partner database so that the statutory pass-through assessment is applied at partner level; (4) where goods or services are supplied into Great Britain, apply to HMRC for UK VAT registration (Form VAT1) and a GB EORI number for customs declarations; (5) appoint and maintain a licensed Gibraltar registered office/registered agent, which remains responsible for filings, books and records, and communications from the Tax Office.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 and the Limited Partnerships Act, an ELP is tax-transparent and has no separate income-tax personality; it is not itself assessed on profits. Each partner is separately chargeable on its agreed share of ELP income: corporate partners are subject to the flat 10% Gross Income Based Tax (GIBT) where profits are Gibraltar-source; individual partners are assessed under progressive individual rates; non-resident partners enjoying non-Gibraltar-source income are generally outside the scope of Gibraltarian tax, subject to source/territorial rules.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box election or Code-choosing regime. The transparency of the ELP is statutory; however, partners may (i) elect in the partnership agreement and by notice to the Commissioner the profit-sharing/basis-of-assessment method (including capital gains allocations) for partner-level taxation; (ii) apply for an advance ruling confirming no entity-level taxation and the territorial scope of non-resident partners; (iii) secure 'qualifying fund' exemption treatment when the ELP is constituted as a regulated fund, and (iv) invoke the relevant double-taxation agreement or OECD hybrid-mismatch re-characterisations (where imported into Gibraltar law) to prevent dual tax relief or double non-taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar: no national VAT, GST or sales-and-use tax; Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and levies only customs/import duties and selected purchase/excise duties under the Customs Duties Ordinance. UK VAT (20%) and destination-country EU import VAT are the relevant external levies for merchandise consumed in those territories, including dental clear aligner e-commerce supplies.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP nil (no Gibraltarian VAT/GST to register for; every importer must, however, obtain a customs trader registration with HM Customs Gibraltar without turnover condition). For UK nexus: the non-established ELP supplying goods/services into Great Britain must register for UK VAT when the value of taxable supplies exceeds GBP 90,000 in a rolling 12-month period (deregistration at GBP 88,000); specified digital/electronically supplied services to UK consumers exceed GBP 8,818; low-value imported consignments not exceeding GBP 135 attract a nil de-minimis where the marketplace/overseas seller is the deemed supplier, so VAT registration is required from the first relevant sale.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar: zero-rating is conceptually inapplicable because there is no domestic VAT; exported dental aligner sheets and related services are outside the scope of Gibraltarian indirect taxation where they leave Gibraltar for a foreign territory and are not sold for local consumption. UK VAT context: a UK VAT-registered ELP may claim 0% VAT on (a) goods exported from Great Britain to non-UK customers provided title passes, the goods are physically exported within the permitted period, and the supplier holds valid commercial, transport and customs export evidence; and (b) B2B services (including digital aligner design/trimming) supplied to non-UK business customers, which are outside the scope of UK VAT or fall under the recipient's reverse-charge in the EU Member State.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar export entry or UK CDS declaration with MRN / SAD C88) evidencing the physical export of the clear aligner sheets
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or courier despatch record proving movement of the goods to the foreign customer's destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or corresponding bank/SWIFT credit confirmation evidencing receipt of the export sales proceeds by the ELP within the prescribed repatriation period

No real-time fiscal clearance, API invoice authorization, or digital stamping applies in Gibraltar; e-invoicing is not mandated domestically. Where the ELP becomes UK VAT-registered, HMRC's Making Tax Digital regime requires VAT accounting records in functional-compatible software and VAT return submission via HMRC's API platform (JSON payloads). For customs, goods exported/imported via the UK require the Customs Declaration Service (CDS), a GB EORI and corresponding electronic export declarations; EU-destination sales may trigger the destination state's e-invoicing/clearance obligations (including the future EU ViDA continuous transaction controls and the CTS/ADR regimes).

HMRC scrutinises export zero-rating claims through proof-of-export verification and commodity-code/ETA checks; absence of compliant evidence leads to VAT assessment at 20%, interest, penalties of 30%-100% for careless/deliberate conduct, and retrospective clawback of all refunded sums for the open assessment period; fraudulent chains attract criminal prosecution and unlimited financial liability. Under Gibraltarian customs law, false export declarations, undervaluation, or misuse of export exemptions result in seizure and forfeiture of the goods, duty-recovery assessments and penalties of up to 100% of evaded duty, together with criminal sanctions; abuse of territorial pass-through treatment at partner level may also trigger partner-level GIBT assessments at 10%-50% and late-filing penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Capital and current account items are fully convertible. The Gibraltar pound is pegged at par to GBP sterling (1 GIP = 1 GBP) and GBP notes and coin circulate freely alongside GIP, eliminating convertibility friction for UK-linked flows. There are no exchange-control approvals, no restrictions on inward or outward capital movements, no limits on profit distribution or capital repatriation, and no withholding at source on distributions by an Exempted Limited Partnership to non-resident partners. The ELP's pass-through eligibility permits profits to be allocated directly to partners outside Gibraltar without Gibraltarian corporate tax impeding the remittance. The residual legal hurdles are documentary and AML-driven: the paying bank will require the partnership deed, GFSC beneficial-ownership register transcript, current capital account ledger and partners' tax-residency certificates before executing large or recurring outward remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, conversion, domiciling or repatriation requirements attach to export proceeds in Gibraltar. There is no mandated timeline for bringing export receipts onshore, no percentage conversion ratio, and no requirement to route receipts through a central-bank settlement account. The ELP may lawfully retain sales proceeds in any convertible currency at any GFSC-licensed bank. In practice, however, the relationship bank will apply the GFSC AML/CFT Guidance Notes, which require the ELP to evidence commercial provenance of all inbound trade funds, including invoices, shipment documentation, waybills, customs declarations and, for platform receipts, periodic Stripe settlement statements.

Central bank supervision: Not applicable as an independent monetary authority. The Government of Gibraltar (Minister for Financial Services, Gaming and Economic Development) issues Gibraltar pound (GIP) currency notes under the Currency Notes Act, while the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) acts as the prudential supervisory authority for all licensed banks, payment institutions and financial services firms in Gibraltar. No foreign-exchange-control authority or reserves-management central bank exists in the jurisdiction.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High for UK-licensed EMIs and fintech treasury platforms (Wise Business, Revolut Business and similar) that onboard Gibraltar corporate clients on a discretionary cross-border basis; Low for EU/EEA-licensed EMIs that formerly relied on EEA passporting, because Gibraltar is a third country outside the EEA financial services passport regime following the UK's exit from the EU. Legal constraints include: (i) UK EMIs may not passport into Gibraltar and instead accept Gibraltar entities under their own money-laundering regulations, typically requiring a regulated-introducer certificate; (ii) GIP is not a clearing currency supported by most EMIs, so the ELP's EMI accounts are held in GBP, EUR or USD at the EMI's sponsoring UK or EU bank; (iii) offshore pass-through structures are treated as higher-risk by EMI compliance teams unless the GFSC beneficial-ownership extract, partnership deed and capital contribution schedule are disclosed; and (iv) platform-origin payment flows (Stripe and card acquiring) attract enhanced transaction-monitoring, which can be satisfied only by full reconciliation of merchant settlement statements to the sales ledger. The EMI route is therefore appropriate for low-value customer-collection sweeps, while the GFSC-licensed tier-1 banks remain the mandatory rail for escrow, trade finance, FX hedging and partner distributions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner capital injections into the Gibraltar ELP must be effected by SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer in GBP, EUR or USD to the ELP's operational account at a GFSC-licensed bank. The payment reference must read exactly: 'Capital contribution — [ELP legal name] — capital call no. [x]' and must be accompanied by the signed capital call notice and an update of the partnership register showing the contributing partner's interest. For GBP transfers originating in the UK, CHAPS or Faster Payments is permissible only where the receiving Gibraltar bank participates in the UK payment infrastructure or maintains a UK correspondent account; otherwise the transfer must route through SWIFT MT103 via a UK clearing intermediary. No Gibraltar exchange-control approval is required, but the remitting bank will apply UK and EU sanctions screening and may demand source-of-funds evidence for contributions above GBP 10,000 or equivalent. For large injections, the ELP must have the funds-receipt ledger, GFSC beneficial-ownership extract and partnership capital schedule ready for verification to avoid holds.Trade disbursements for raw materials (multilayer thermoplastic sheets, polyurethane films and medical-grade packaging), automated trimming equipment and subcontract manufacturing should be cleared using four rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for international supplier payments in USD and EUR, quoting the invoice, pro-forma and bill-of-lading references; (ii) irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit subject to UCP600 for high-value capital-equipment imports, issued by the Gibraltar relationship bank and advised through the supplier's confirming bank; (iii) UK Faster Payments and BACS for domestic UK suppliers where the ELP maintains a UK settlement account; and (iv) documentary collections on a documents-against-payment or documents-against-acceptance basis for recurring low-to-mid value trade obligations. Stripe and platform-card settlement proceeds are swept into the operational account in GBP or EUR and then redeployed to trade payables; the ELP must maintain a funds-flow reconciliation map linking each Stripe payout batch to the corresponding supplier payment to satisfy GFSC AML review and the medical-device regulatory audit trail. Customer-collection rails (Platform_Credit_Card and Stripe) govern receipts only; all supplier disbursements must clear through the banking rails described above.Vanilla deliverable FX forwards — permitted for GBP/EUR, GBP/USD and EUR/USD tenors up to 12 months, executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or bank-documented forward contract, with cash-collateral cover lodged at the relationship bank; no speculative or uncovered forward positions are permitted.FX swaps (principal and interest exchanged in two currencies) — permitted solely for liquidity re-alignment between Stripe GBP/EUR settlement balances and the ELP's USD procurement and equipment-financing obligations, subject to treasury board approval and the bank's credit facility; cross-currency interest-rate swaps are confined to fixed-rate equipment financings.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) — available only against genuinely non-convertible or restricted-settlement currencies (for example RMB, INR or BRL) where the underlying exposure is a verifiable trade payable documented by invoices and contracts; no NDF is permitted or necessary for GBP/EUR/USD transactions because all three currencies are fully deliverable and GIP is pegged at par to GBP; speculative or uncovered NDF trading is prohibited.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium-High for UK-licensed EMIs and fintech treasury platforms (Wise Business, Revolut Business and similar) that onboard Gibraltar corporate clients on a discretionary cross-border basis; Low for EU/EEA-licensed EMIs that formerly relied on EEA passporting, because Gibraltar is a third country outside the EEA financial services passport regime following the UK's exit from the EU. Legal constraints include: (i) UK EMIs may not passport into Gibraltar and instead accept Gibraltar entities under their own money-laundering regulations, typically requiring a regulated-introducer certificate; (ii) GIP is not a clearing currency supported by most EMIs, so the ELP's EMI accounts are held in GBP, EUR or USD at the EMI's sponsoring UK or EU bank; (iii) offshore pass-through structures are treated as higher-risk by EMI compliance teams unless the GFSC beneficial-ownership extract, partnership deed and capital contribution schedule are disclosed; and (iv) platform-origin payment flows (Stripe and card acquiring) attract enhanced transaction-monitoring, which can be satisfied only by full reconciliation of merchant settlement statements to the sales ledger. The EMI route is therefore appropriate for low-value customer-collection sweeps, while the GFSC-licensed tier-1 banks remain the mandatory rail for escrow, trade finance, FX hedging and partner distributions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation is mandatory where cross-border controlled transactions of the Gibraltar ELP — thermoforming tolling fees, automated trimming services, intra-group machinery leases and aligner-design software royalties — exceed GBP 200,000 in aggregate in the chargeable period; Master File and country-by-country reporting obligations apply where the ultimate parent consolidates group revenue of GBP 750 million or more; below the threshold, arm's-length benchmark studies should still be retained for defensive record-keeping.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar implements Economic Substance Requirements aligned with international standards for geographically mobile activities, namely banking, insurance, fund management, financing, leasing, headquarters and distribution services. Manufacturing of multilayer clear-aligner sheets through thermoforming and automated trimming is not a listed geographically mobile activity, so a separate ESR filing is not triggered by the manufacturing trade. Nevertheless, the ELP must demonstrate real presence — a physical local factory, payroll, board meetings held in Gibraltar and management decision records — to preserve fiscal transparency, protect the UK-Gibraltar DTA benefit and avoid recharacterisation as a shell arrangement under the principal-purpose test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Fraudulent evasion of income tax under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act is punishable by an unlimited fine and imprisonment for up to 7 years, in addition to full recovery of the undercharged tax, statutory interest and a tax-geared penalty of up to 100% of the amount understated; the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 additionally permits confiscation and forfeiture of the resulting benefit.

Director personal liability: The General Partner and any shadow directors are exposed to piercing of the corporate veil where the ELP is a mere façade; joint and several civil liability for the unpaid tax applies to the extent profits were diverted or retained; courts may impose travel bans, director disqualification for up to 15 years and civil restitution orders.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of pass-through treatment and reclassification of the ELP as a taxable unit assessed at 12.5% on the full Gibraltar-source receipts; withdrawal of DTA relief with interest and a 100% penalty; referrals to the Registrar for strike-off; HMRC may apply UK CFC and general anti-abuse rules to impute the profits to UK partners.

Director personal liability: General Partner liability for deemed taxes where distributions were made while insolvent; civil recovery of diverted profits; travel restraint orders pending investigation; disqualification under the Companies Act and referral to the Financial Crime Unit.
Anti-Money Laundering Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar), failure to disclose suspicious activity is punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine; tipping-off carries up to 2 years; the Financial Intelligence Unit may impose civil penalties of up to GBP 5 million and obtain account freezing orders against the ELP's bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Individual directors and the General Partner may be prosecuted for money laundering with a maximum sentence of 14 years' imprisonment; confiscation orders, travel bans and exclusion from regulated activity follow upon conviction.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Adjustment of chargeable profits by the Assessor at the 12.5% rate with late-payment interest; tax-geared penalties of up to 100% of the adjustment; fixed penalties of up to GBP 30,000 for each missing documentation category; referral to international exchange of information frameworks.

Director personal liability: The signatory General Partner who certified a false transfer-pricing declaration is personally liable for the penalty and interest; director disqualification for up to 15 years applies; civil recovery extends to assets stripped from the partnership before settlement.
DTA and Tax Residency Certificate Abuse

Statutory sanction: Withdrawal of treaty relief retroactively; assessment of the entire benefit plus statutory interest; application of the principal-purpose test under the UK-Gibraltar DTA; penalties up to 100% of the tax undercharged and criminal referral for false statements under oath.

Director personal liability: Contempt proceedings for false affidavits; personal restitution orders; travel bans pending criminal inquiry; imprisonment for up to 7 years for perjury; disqualification of the General Partner from acting as a director of any Gibraltar undertaking.
