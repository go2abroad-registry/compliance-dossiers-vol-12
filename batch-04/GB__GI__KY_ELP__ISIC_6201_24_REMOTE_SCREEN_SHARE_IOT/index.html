Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office (ITO), HM Government of Gibraltar, 231 Main Street, Gibraltar GX11 1AA; electronic services and registration portal: https://egov.gibraltar.gov.gi. Indirect tax and export controls are administered separately by HM Customs of Gibraltar (Gibraltar Customs & Excise), with digital filing through the Gibraltar Customs Single Window (GCSW).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Limited Partnerships Act 2016 by filing the partnership statement, appointing a Gibraltar-resident registered office and, where required by the Act, a Gibraltar-resident general partner or licensed manager. Step 2: The Companies Registry automatically transmits the incorporation record to the Income Tax Office, which opens a partnership tax file without a separate application and issues a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) to the ELP; the TRN notice is served on the registered office. Step 3: Each non-resident limited partner must submit the ITO 'Non-Resident Partner Registration Form' together with certified constitutional documents, partnership agreement extract, foreign tax identification number, and identity/beneficial-ownership verification to obtain a partner-specific TRN. Step 4: Where the ELP makes any taxable supplies in Gibraltar, or supplies digital services to Gibraltar consumers, apply to HM Customs of Gibraltar for a Gibraltar VAT registration number; non-resident suppliers must register from the first supply without relying on the ordinary turnover threshold. Step 5: Appoint a Gibraltar tax agent if the ELP has no place of management in Gibraltar, execute the required power of attorney, and complete all subsequent filings through the egov.gibraltar.gov.gi portal. Typical processing: TRN auto-issued within 2-5 working days of incorporation; VAT registration processed in 10-15 working days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The ELP is fiscally transparent for Gibraltar income tax purposes under the Income Tax Act 2010: no entity-level taxation is imposed on the partnership; each partner is assessed on their agreed share of the partnership profits. Gibraltar-resident partners are taxable on their worldwide share at the applicable individual (up to 28%) or corporate (standard 10%-12.5%) rates, subject to the OECD Pillar Two 15% top-up for in-scope multinational groups. Non-resident partners are taxable in Gibraltar only on Gibraltar-source income or profits attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment. The ELP itself is not a Gibraltar tax resident unless its central management and control is exercised in Gibraltar; the default offshore management profile of the ELP therefore supports pass-through treatment with no Gibraltar entity-level charge.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not maintain a US-style check-the-box election. Available elections and statutory mechanisms for the ELP: (a) default partnership transparency election, with the partnership required to file an annual partner allocation return; (b) an election, made with the Commissioner's consent, for the ELP to be taxed as an assessable company where the arrangement displays corporateness, subject to the Income Tax Act 2010 provisions on 'partnerships treated as companies'; (c) an election under the Income Tax (Exemptions) Regulations for a qualifying private investment fund or certified fund to exempt non-resident investors' offshore investment income from Gibraltar tax; (d) hybrid mismatch self-declarations and reporting under the Income Tax (Hybrid Mismatch) Rules implementing OECD BEPS Action 2, including any reverse-hybrid classification of the ELP where partner jurisdictions diverge; and (e) in respect of US-connected investors, supplementary US Form 8832 classification elections coordinated with the Gibraltar 'reverse hybrid' declaration; the ELP may also elect into the UK 'surrounding territory' treatment only to the extent a UK permanent establishment is created, which is not anticipated under the remote screen-share/IoT delivery model.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

10%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 100,000 (Gibraltar Pound at parity with GBP; approximately USD 125,000) in taxable turnover in any rolling 12-month period for suppliers established in Gibraltar. No threshold applies to non-established suppliers of digital, electronic, and IoT telemetry services to private consumers in Gibraltar: such suppliers must register from the first supply. Supplies of exported remote screen-share and unattended-device telemetry services fall outside the Gibraltar VAT charge and do not count toward the registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating or outside-scope treatment applies as follows: (a) goods: physical telemetry devices, routers, and pre-configured IoT gateways exported from Gibraltar are zero-rated upon removal from Gibraltar territory evidenced by an electronic customs export declaration; (b) B2B services: supplies of remote desktop access and unattended-machine telemetry software to business customers belong where the recipient is established under place-of-supply rules aligned with EU VAT Article 44, so no Gibraltar VAT arises; (c) B2C electronic services: supplies to consumers outside Gibraltar fall outside the Gibraltar VAT charge, with any foreign VAT (EU OSS/IOSS, UK VAT, or non-EU sales tax) administered by Stripe or Paddle as Merchant of Record; and (d) input VAT recovery: recoverable only to the extent attributable to taxable local activities; no Gibraltar VAT refund arises on exports because exports are not charged to Gibraltar VAT. Claims must evidence the recipient's VAT/TIN status, the delivery and performance location through telemetry and session logs, and the absence of any Gibraltar-based personnel or fixed establishment involved in performance.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/EAD) filed electronically through the Gibraltar Customs Single Window, with unique export consignment reference, for physical telemetry gateways, routers, dongles, and pre-provisioned IoT hardware
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (original or electronic telex release) evidencing physical removal from Gibraltar for any goods component
  • Inward Remittance Certificate in the name of the ELP or original FX settlement statement from the receiving bank/EMI evidencing export consideration credited within the statutory repatriation window
  • Commercial Invoice in the name of the ELP reflecting Incoterms, HS/CN codes, ISIC 6201 activity description, and SOC code
  • B2B export contract or master subscription agreement together with the customer's VAT registration number, GSTIN, or equivalent tax identification certificate
  • Machine-verifiable service delivery log: timestamped remote desktop access session records, unattended-device telemetry manifests, IP-address geolocation data, and edge-node usage reports substantiating the export of digital services
  • Merchant of Record settlement reports from Stripe and Paddle reconciling each transaction, platform fees, and any VAT/GST collected on behalf of the ELP

Gibraltar does not operate a real-time continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing clearance regime; there is no fiscal digital stamping, no SI-SII-equivalent API, and no mandatory government-cleared e-invoice exchange. However: (i) all VAT invoices issued by a Gibraltar-registered supplier must contain the statutory VAT invoice elements prescribed under the Value Added Tax Act 2019; (ii) cross-border B2B e-invoices should conform to PEPPOL BIS 3.0 to ensure interoperability with buyer-side fiscal systems; (iii) customs export declarations for any physical telemetry hardware, gateways, or screen-sync dongles must be filed electronically via the Gibraltar Customs Single Window, utilising the electronic export declaration (SAD/EAD) with AES-style messaging; and (iv) for pure digital services, the ELP must retain machine-readable delivery logs, IP-geolocation evidence, session timestamps, and Merchant of Record (Stripe/Paddle) transactional APIs as the functional substitute for physical clearance.

HM Customs of Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office may audit zero-rating and outside-scope claims for up to six years from the end of the relevant accounting period, extended to twenty years in cases of fraud, wilful default, or careless conduct. Unsupported or contrived zero-rating claims trigger: (a) immediate assessment of Gibraltar VAT at the 10% standard rate with statutory interest from the date of supply; (b) civil penalties of up to 100% of the VAT undercharged, mitigated only by prompt voluntary disclosure under the Civil Penalty Regulations; (c) criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion of VAT under the Value Added Tax Act 2019 and the Criminal Procedure Act, carrying unlimited fines and imprisonment; (d) retrospective clawback of input VAT previously recovered in respect of the related overheads; and (e) reclassification risk whereby the Income Tax Office disregards the ELP's pass-through status if the 'export' structure is found to be a sham, with consequential cross-reporting to UK HMRC and the partners' home tax authorities under the exchange of information framework.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar imposes no exchange-control legislation and maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at 1:1 to GBP and circulates alongside Bank of England notes, eliminating fiat convertibility friction for GBP-settled flows. Profit remittance, partner distributions, and capital repatriation to non-resident partners are unrestricted and require no central-bank licence or exchange-control approval. USD and EUR conversions are executed freely through licensed banks at market rates; the only practical hurdle is standard AML source-of-funds documentation for large outward remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender period, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no repatriation deadline applies to export or software-licensing proceeds. Receipts from ISIC 6201 remote-access licensing may be retained indefinitely in original currency (USD, EUR, GBP) or converted at the entity's discretion. No authorised-dealer confirmation or central-bank notification is required; only normal reporting thresholds under the GFSC AML/CFT framework and UK-sanctions screening apply.

Central bank supervision: Government of Gibraltar - Commissioner of Currency (GIP currency issuance); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (banking supervision and AML/CFT oversight); Bank of England (monetary anchor via GIP:GBP 1:1 parity and lender of last resort access via UK clearing relationships)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner capital injections must arrive by SWIFT MT103 (USD/EUR/GBP) or UK CHAPS for same-day GBP, referencing the ELP's bank account and a clear purpose description, e.g. 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - LIMITED PARTNERSHIP' or 'LP CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION'; where the bank's systems require a regulatory purpose code, use the BoP capital-transfer class (e.g. 'M03' equity investment) while the free-text field should name the ELP. Standard documentary pack for any single injection over GBP 250,000 or USD 350,000: general-partner resolution/capital call, executed subscription form, source-of-funds briefing, and identity confirmation of the remitting partner. SEPA is not a native Gibraltar rail; EUR injections are cleared via TARGET2 through the bank's EU correspondent, while USD is cleared via the bank's New York correspondent (Fedwire/CHIPS).Supplier and trade settlements use SWIFT MT103 for ordinary correspondent-cleared invoices; MT700 documentary letters of credit are recommended for procurement contracts above GBP 100,000 to add payment certainty for IOT hardware/telemetry suppliers; UK Faster Payments or CHAPS is available for same-day GBP vendor payments via the bank's UK agency arrangement; USD vendor payments via ACH or Fedwire through US correspondents; EUR payments via SEPA/TARGET2 where the counterpart is in the EEA. No Gibraltar exchange-control approval is triggered on any outbound payment. Stripe and Paddle remit aggregate net revenue via non-negotiable MT103 or ACH transfers from their merchant-of-record treasury entities; remittance advice should separately state gross subscription, platform fees, and VAT/GST rows to permit compliant seller-of-record and VAT accounting by the ELP.GBP/USD deliverable FX forward contracts (tenors up to 12 months) with Gibraltar-licensed or UK clearing banksEUR/GBP deliverable FX forwards and FX swaps for hedging Paddle EUR subscription settlements and EU IOT supplier costsVanilla over-the-counter GBP/USD and GBP/EUR FX options under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or short-form FX master agreement with margin supportGBP/USD cross-currency swaps restricted to matched long-term partner capital injections or qualifying debt refinancing

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Medium - UK and EU electronic-money institutions cannot passport into Gibraltar post-Brexit; only GFSC-authorised payment institutions may hold local customer funds. Cross-border EMIs (Airwallex, Wise Business, Revolut Business, neobanking platforms) may on-board the ELP on a bilateral merchant-customer basis for auxiliary disbursements and FX conversion, but they are not substitutes for a GFSC-licensed deposit-taking bank and present counterparty concentration risk. The target rails Stripe and Paddle operate as foreign merchant acquirers / merchants of record, not Gibraltar EMIs; both accept Gibraltar-registered software businesses subject to standard scheme rules, PCI DSS, and enhanced KYC on aggregate payout flows.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% corporate income tax rate applies to Gibraltar-resident companies; the Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) is fiscally transparent and is not itself assessed as a corporation unless separately registered as a company under the Companies Act.
