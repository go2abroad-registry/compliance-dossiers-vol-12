Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar — Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax; official portal: https://www.ito.gi and https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi) together with HM Customs Gibraltar / Customs & Excise Department (import duty, excise duty, and export declarations operated through the UNCTAD Asycuda World single-window).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the ELP with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Limited Partnerships Act 2013 (no minimum capital; non-resident ownership permitted). Step 2: Within 30 days of incorporation, the registered agent files the partnership registration particulars with the Income Tax Office to obtain the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN). Step 3: A non-resident ELP may obtain the TIN using a Gibraltar registered office address and a local agent; no branch or physical presence is required. Step 4: Register with HM Customs as an indirect exporter using the same TIN/business registration number to obtain access to Asycuda World for export declarations. Step 5: Obtain Food Business Registration/Approval from the Gibraltar Environmental Agency (Food Safety Act 2005) for wholesale food handling — this is a separate statutory license outside the tax registration infrastructure. Step 6: No VAT ID is required because Gibraltar does not operate a VAT/GST system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box or equivalent entity-classification election exists in Gibraltar. Partnership tax transparency is statutory (Income Tax Act 2010, section 6): the ELP is fiscally transparent and the partners are taxed individually in their country of residence on their distributive share of Gibraltar-source income only; non-resident partners have no Gibraltar tax liability on non-Gibraltar-source export profits. For hybrid mismatch purposes, the Income Tax (Hybrid and Branch Mismatch Rules) 2019 apply and require the ELP to make prescribed filings where a structured arrangement with UK/EU partners would otherwise achieve a double deduction or deduction/no-inclusion outcome. No unilateral election to decouple local classification from foreign classification is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar does not levy VAT, GST, or sales and use tax. The applicable indirect tax system is Import Duty and Excise Duty under the Customs Ordinance (Cap. 155); exports to third-country destinations are outside the scope of duty.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A (GIP) — no VAT/GST turnover threshold exists. Commercial export of food products requires: (i) Food Business Registration/Approval from the Environmental Agency (no turnover threshold), and (ii) Customs Trader registration with HM Customs/Gibraltar for electronic export declarations (no turnover threshold).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from Gibraltar to a non-Gibraltar destination are outside the scope of Gibraltar import/excise duty, achieving de facto 0% indirect taxation; no Gibraltar VAT is chargeable and no VAT refund mechanism exists in Gibraltar. To substantiate duty-free export treatment and support any foreign buyer-side zero-rating or drawback claim, the exporter must prove (i) the goods departed Gibraltar in the same condition within a commercially reasonable window, (ii) risk passed to the overseas buyer (FCA/CFR/CIF), and (iii) the buyer is not a Gibraltar resident. For animal-fat content (tallow-based hotpot base bricks), an Export Health Certificate issued by the Gibraltar Veterinary/Environmental Authority must accompany the consignment where the destination jurisdiction requires it; this certificate strengthens zero-rating substantiation. If UK VAT is incurred on services or goods in the UK pipeline, the Gibraltarian entity may recover UK VAT under the VAT Refund Scheme for Non-Established Taxable Persons (VAT 65A) only if the costs relate to UK taxable activities, not to exports outside the UK.

  • Customs Export Declaration (signed Single Administrative Document lodged and receipted through Asycuda World by HM Customs Gibraltar)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (dated, endorsed, and evidencing physical movement from the Port of Gibraltar/Harbour to the overseas consignee; supported by commercial invoice and packing list)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank FX confirmation (SWIFT MT103 or Letter of Credit settlement advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign exchange within 180 days)

Gibraltar has no real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital invoicing mandate. Export declarations are submitted electronically via the UNCTAD Asycuda World customs single-window; customs brokers may integrate via Asycuda web services/API for batch pre-clearance. Invoices must be retained in original form for at least 6 years under the Income Tax Act 2010 record-keeping rules. No independent electronic B2G clearance platform applies to food exports.

False or inflated export declarations expose the ELP to: (i) forfeiture of the goods and fines up to the full value of the goods plus imprisonment of up to 2 years under the Customs Ordinance (Cap. 155); (ii) penalties of up to 100% of the tax undercharged and interest at the statutory rate under the Income Tax Act 2010 for fraudulent or negligent claims on the partnership return, with retrospective clawback of any tax relief or duty drawback claimed on export proceeds; and (iii) cross-border penalties and clawback if the same goods are used to support an overseas VAT zero-rating or refund claim — e.g., HMRC may assess penalties up to 100% of the understated UK VAT under Finance Act 2007, Schedule 24, and the UK–Gibraltar mutual assistance and Exchange of Information arrangements enable full information sharing, bank account discovery, and enforcement of foreign tax judgments against the ELP and its partners.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The GIP is fully and freely convertible into GBP at parity with zero statutory friction; Gibraltar imposes no exchange controls on current-account, capital-account, dividend, or partnership-distribution flows. The Exempted Limited Partnership, registered under the Limited Partnerships Act 2018 as a transparent vehicle, may hold multi-currency balances (GBP, USD, EUR, CNY) without restriction. Profit repatriation to UK-resident limited partners is subject to no Gibraltar withholding tax on distributions. The only meaningful convertibility constraint is bank-level AML/CDD screening applied at the point of credit and debit; there are no sovereign limits on conversion amounts, holding periods, or transaction frequency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None apply in Gibraltar. There is no statutory obligation to surrender export or trading proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriation into GIP/GBP. The ELP may retain USD, EUR, and CNY balances discretionarily in segregated settlement accounts. If any trade proceeds are ultimately routed to or liquidated through a UK bank account, the UK bank may apply statistical reporting requirements under the IMF Balance of Payments regime; this is an informational matter, not a surrender or conversion mandate.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar maintains no independent central bank. Currency issuance of the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is vested in HM Government of Gibraltar pursuant to the Currency Notes Act 1934, operating under a 100% sterling-backing currency-board arrangement with a fixed 1:1 GIP/GBP peg. Prudential supervision of banking institutions is discharged by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services (Banking) Act 1996 and the Financial Services Act 2019. The Bank of England, FCA and PRA exercise no direct regulatory jurisdiction in Gibraltar; however, GBP clearing and correspondent relationships for a Gibraltar-incorporated ELP are ultimately routed through UK-based settlement banks.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-Low. Post-Brexit, UK FCA-licensed electronic money institutions (Airwallex, Wise, Revolut Business) no longer have EU/EEA passporting rights into Gibraltar and most jurisdiction-risk frameworks treat Gibraltar as outside the FCA Overseas Business Regime; a Gibraltar-incorporated ELP is typically declined or subject to enhanced due diligence, with a materially reduced probability of successful onboarding for USD/CNY rails. A GFSC-licensed EMI under the Electronic Money Act 2017 can provide GBP/EUR float and card issuance, but its correspondent clearing depth for USD and CNY is thin. Recommended architecture: the GFSC-regulated bank remains the sole primary clearing institution for trade and escrow activity; an EMI may be used only as a secondary low-value collection channel, never for LC operations or capital custody.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital calls are funded via SWIFT MT103 from UK-resident limited partners into the ELP's operational account at Gibraltar International Bank. Field 50K states the remitting LP's legal name; field 59 identifies the ELP by registered name and Gibraltar Companies Registry number; field 70 must state 'Partnership capital contribution - capital call dated [X]'. The credit is provisional until the bank receives the executed capital call notice, subscription agreement, and GFSC registration extract; any single credit exceeding GBP 100,000 triggers a source-of-wealth file. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Related-party transactions exceeding GIP 1,000,000 in an accounting period require a contemporaneous Local File. A Master File is required for Gibraltar members of multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of GIP 50,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar’s Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to the ELP’s distribution activity. The ELP must ensure its principal purpose for generating distribution income from export wholesale supply is supported by physical presence in Gibraltar, including a registered office, employees, and board/partner management meetings held in Gibraltar. For a corporate general partner, its directors must also hold regular board meetings in Gibraltar. Failure to satisfy these substance requirements exposes the entity to penalties and mandatory exchange of information with the ultimate beneficial owner’s tax authority.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Understatement of Gibraltar-Source Profit

Statutory sanction: Recovery of unpaid tax plus interest at 3% above the Gibraltar base rate; a fine of up to double the tax evaded; and criminal prosecution under Section 85 of the Income Tax Act punishable by up to 5 years’ imprisonment.

Director personal liability: The general partner is jointly and severally liable for the full tax adjustment and civil restitution; on criminal conviction, travel bans and surrender-of-passport orders may be made; the directors may be disqualified from managing any Gibraltar entity for 10 years.
Shell Company / ESR Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The Economic Substance Regulations impose a penalty of up to GIP 200,000 per failure; continued failure may lead to a default assessment based on deemed profits or an order striking the ELP off the Register of Limited Partnerships. Relevant information is automatically exchanged with the partners’ residence authorities under a Competent Authority Agreement.

Director personal liability: The corporate general partner’s officers are personally liable for the ESR penalty; the Registrar may impose a travel ban on such officers pending disclosure; providing false substance documentation constitutes an offence carrying imprisonment of up to 2 years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: A transfer-pricing adjustment of additional taxable profit at the 12.5% rate, a penalty of 40% of the under-assessed tax, and daily late-payment penalties; referral to the Inland Revenue Special Compliance Office for criminal investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be held jointly liable for the tax deficiency if the transfer pricing act was willful; injunctions can freeze personal assets pending audit, and courts may issue travel bans where the deficiency exceeds GIP 500,000.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, money laundering carries imprisonment up to 14 years on indictment and unlimited fines; non-compliance with the Companies (Registers of Beneficial Ownership) Act incurs a fine of GIP 50,000 plus daily default penalties.

Director personal liability: General partners face confiscation orders, criminal fines, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 7 years for knowingly participating in money laundering; Gibraltar-based property may be restrained by the Supreme Court.
