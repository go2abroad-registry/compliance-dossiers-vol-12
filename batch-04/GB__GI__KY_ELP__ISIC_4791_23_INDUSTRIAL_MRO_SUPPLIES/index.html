Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement under Gibraltar law for an Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP); initial capital is set solely by the Limited Partnership Agreement and must be received in cash or in kind pursuant to that agreement; practical minimum initial cash contribution of US$50,000 is recommended to evidence commercial substance for bank account opening.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification by the Gibraltar Fire and Rescue Service for any warehouse or office premises; occupancy and structural clearance from the Development and Planning Commission for premises above 280 m2; workplace clearance under the Gibraltar Health and Safety at Work Act 2008 including documented risk assessment and staff fire safety training records.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement under Gibraltar law for an Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP); initial capital is set solely by the Limited Partnership Agreement and must be received in cash or in kind pursuant to that agreement; practical minimum initial cash contribution of US$50,000 is recommended to evidence commercial substance for bank account opening.

Pro-Forma First Board Resolution Draft

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar — Income Tax Office (ITO), acting through the Commissioner of Income Tax; 13 Corral Road, Gibraltar; primary portal: www.ito.gov.gi (Self-Online Assessment / SOLA); auxiliary registries: Companies House Gibraltar (www.companieshouse.gi) for overseas partnership registration and the Customs & Excise Division for indirect-tax/customs matters.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Legal-status registration: the Cayman ELP must register as an overseas limited partnership under Gibraltar's Limited Partnerships Act 2019 at Companies House Gibraltar, filing the Cayman certificate of registration, the partnership agreement, a declaration of the general partner and a Gibraltar registered-office address; no minimum capital is imposed. Step 2 — Taxpayer registration: the ELP applies to the Income Tax Office on Form ELP-Reg for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) within 30 days of commencing Gibraltar-source activity, appointing a resident agent (fiscal representative) and disclosing all partners and their jurisdictions. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the ELP is fiscally transparent by default under Part IX of the Income Tax Act 2010: the partnership itself bears no tax and each partner is assessed on their share of Gibraltar-source profits. Territorial_Scope — only income accruing in or derived from Gibraltar is within the charge; capital gains of non-resident partners and income of a partnership not carrying on a Gibraltarian trade are outside the charge. Tax_Exempt_Zone — not applicable: no statutory exempt-zone or exempt-company status remains in Gibraltar (legacy exempt companies were grandfathered only until 30 June 2014); however, genuine non-residence effectively operates as an exemption.

Available election forms

Check-the-box-style election: a partnership may irrevocably elect under the Income Tax Act 2010 to be taxed as if incorporated, causing entity-level taxation at 12.5% on Gibraltar-source profits and triggering access to the Participating Interest Exemption (PIE) on qualifying dividends and disposal gains; the election is filed with the ITO before the start of the relevant tax year (1 January). Hybrid-mismatch alignment: Gibraltar applies OECD BEPS Action 2-based hybrid rules requiring the ELP to reconcile its fiscal transparency with the classification applied to each partner in their home jurisdiction, with mandatory annual classification statements; the ELP may also request an advance ruling from the ITO on whether a partner's home-country check-the-box or hybrid treatment creates a deductible/no-inclusion outcome, which is disallowed where mismatches are present.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales-and-Use-Tax in force — Gibraltar is excluded from the UK VAT Act 1994 and sits outside the EU VAT area. The operative indirect-tax system is a customs/import-duty regime under the Customs Ordinance (Cap. 109) and Excise Duties Ordinance, supplemented by motor vehicle purchase tax; a General Consumption Tax (GCT) aligned with EU VAT norms has been proposed but is not enacted as of the effective date.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — there is no turnover threshold for a VAT/GCT registration because no VAT/GCT exists. Indirect-tax registration is instead an import-event trigger: the ELP must obtain an importer identification code and, where recurrent, a customs deferment/security account with the Gibraltar Customs & Excise Division prior to the first importation of MRO hardware; there is no minimum monetary threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in current law — with no VAT, there is no 0% export rating and no input-VAT refund mechanism. Customs neutrality for exports is available instead: goods physically removed from Gibraltar (third-party shipments, direct exports or re-exports of previously warehoused imports) are outside the import-duty charge when the ELP presents the authorised export declaration and transport evidence; inward processing relief (IPR) and customs warehousing may suspend duty on MRO hardware imported for onward international distribution. Should a future GCT be enacted, zero-rating will apply to export sales of goods and to qualifying ancillary logistics services for non-Gibraltar customers, conditioned on proof of removal from Gibraltar. The 180-day export-proceeds benchmark is retained as a prudential compliance convention; Gibraltar has no statutory FX-repatriation deadline since exchange controls were abolished.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from Gibraltar

None — Gibraltar imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, API e-invoicing, continuous-transaction-control, or digital-stamping mandate; UK Making Tax Digital does not extend to Gibraltar. Customs declarations are cleared electronically through the UNCTAD ASYCUDA World system. Voluntary B2B e-invoicing is permitted; a future GCT, if enacted, is expected to adopt EU VIDA-standard structured e-invoicing requirements.

Export zero-rating fraud is not a distinct offence absent VAT; enforcement operates through customs and income-tax anti-avoidance rules. Under the Customs Ordinance (Cap. 109), false declarations, forged removal evidence or fraudulent duty-suspension claims trigger forfeiture of goods, civil penalties of up to treble the value of the goods, and imprisonment of up to three years. The Income Tax Office may reopen returns within six years (unlimited in cases of fraud or neglect), reclassify artificially structured partnership profits as Gibraltar-source, and assess tax at 12.5% with interest and penalties of up to 100% of the tax under the Intentional Default provisions. Partner-level clawbacks apply where profit is shifted to non-residents without substance, invoking the OECD principal-purpose test and Gibraltar's hybrid-mismatch rules; missing or inconsistent documentation negates duty-neutrality and recaptures import duty, plus administrative surcharges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

There are no exchange controls in Gibraltar on either the current account or capital account. The GIP is fully convertible into GBP at a fixed 1:1 rate, and GBP banknotes are legal tender. For a pass-through ELP, profit distributions, return of partner capital, and trade settlements to non-resident partners involve no convertibility permission, no preferential approval, and no withholding; the only practical constraint is the correspondent bank's jurisdiction-risk policy on the partners' country of residence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Gibraltar legislates no mandatory surrender, repatriation timeline, or conversion ratio for export proceeds. Proceeds from cross-border B2B MRO catalogue sales may be retained in foreign currency or converted freely; there is no obligation to route proceeds through a designated domestic account.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no central bank of its own. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Note Act 1934 and is pegged at par with the pound sterling; the Bank of England retains monetary authority over GBP. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the competent prudential authority and AML/CFT supervisor for credit and financial institutions in Gibraltar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium feasibility with restricted utility. Post-Brexit, Gibraltar is outside the EEA payment passport, so FCA-authorised UK EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex) generally decline British Overseas Territory partnerships or offer only settlement-account service without local GBP sort codes. Direct EMI onboarding is possible only where the EMI holds a safeguarded e-money licence acceptable to GFSC, or through a Gibraltar-licensed investment/corporate services firm. Recommended posture: use the tier-1 bank as the treasury backbone and an EMI solely as a pay-out channel for small B2B card refunds, with daily zero-balancing sweeps back to the operating account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Funds flow only through the partner's own bank account to the ELP's designated account via SWIFT MT103, stating in Field 70: 'ELP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — CAPITAL CALL [REF] DATED [DATE]'. For GBP payments within the UK use CHAPS; for EUR use TARGET2 via SEPA-compliant creditor identifiers; for USD use Fedwire through the bank's correspondent. The receiving bank will verify that the beneficiary account name exactly equals the registered ELP name against the Certificate of Limited Partnership. Capital contributions are free of capital duty or stamp duty in Gibraltar; the ELP's accountant must post the contribution to the partners' capital accounts and, where funds are contributed in non-GBP, the conversion is executed at the bank's prevailing mid-market rate. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Gibraltar Year of Assessment (YA) runs 1 July to 30 June. A return must be filed within 12 months of the end of the YA in which the ELP's accounting period falls. For accounts ended 31 December 2024 (YA 2025), the return is due by 30 June 2026. Payment of any assessed liability is due on the statutory payment date preceding assessment; interest runs from the due date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where the Gibraltar entity's annual turnover exceeds GIP 10,000,000 or the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds GIP 1,000,000; Master File mandatory where the consolidated group revenue of the UK parent group exceeds GIP 50,000,000; below these thresholds a contemporaneous 'preparedness' file must still evidence arm's-length pricing on request.

Economic substance scope: The Income Tax (Substance) Regulations 2019 apply to every Gibraltar-resident entity engaged in a 'relevant activity'. The B2B MRO catalogue e-commerce operation falls within the 'distribution and service centre' category. To be treated as tax-resident and not as an artificial shell, the ELP must demonstrate in Gibraltar: (a) direction and management exercised at board level, (b) sufficient business premises, (c) qualifying employees on the Gibraltar payroll, (d) adequate operating expenditure proportional to the activity, and (e) performance of the core income-generating activity. A 'pure equity holding' ELP benefits from reduced substance requirements, but any active trading activity removes that concession. Where the partnership is factually managed and controlled from the UK by the parent, the correct position is to file as a UK-resident structure and refrain from claiming Gibraltar residence benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded under the Income Tax Act (Cap 183); criminal conviction for fraudulent evasion of income tax carrying a fine of up to GIP 100,000 and/or imprisonment up to 7 years; confiscation order for the full benefit derived under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.

Director personal liability: Civil restitution against the General Partner; joint and several liability for the underpaid tax where dishonesty is proven; disqualification order under the Companies Act 2014 for up to 15 years; travel ban and asset freezing order ancillary to confiscation proceedings.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement / Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance penalty of GIP 10,000 to GIP 100,000 per assessment year; revocation of the Gibraltar Tax Residence Certificate; recharacterisation of the structure as UK tax-resident; UK GAAR counteraction with double tax assessments in both territories.

Director personal liability: Strike-off of the ELP and forfeiture of its assets as bona vacantia; personal assessment on the UK directors as de facto shadow partners; criminal sanction for knowingly making a false declaration of residence and substance.
Transfer Pricing / Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment increasing Gibraltar assessable profits; interest at 8% per annum on the underpaid tax; penalty of 50-100% of the additional liability where the taxpayer cannot demonstrate reasonable care in preparing documentation; potential UK corresponding adjustment creating a second exposure.

Director personal liability: Directors' breach of fiduciary duty for authorising reckless pricing; clawback of distributions paid out of misstated profits; disqualification for wrongful trading where the TP adjustment renders the ELP insolvent; personal liability of the General Partner under the Partnership Act for partnership debts.
AML / Suspicious Activity Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: Money laundering conviction punishable by imprisonment up to 14 years and/or an unlimited fine; regulatory fine of up to GIP 200,000 for SAR contravention; freezing and confiscation of the ELP's bank accounts and inventory; withdrawal of the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission licence, where applicable.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability of the compliance director and Money Laundering Reporting Officer; travel restrictions while investigation is pending; disqualification and significant personal fines; potential extradition exposure under UK-Gibraltar criminal cooperation arrangements.
Abusive Pass-Through Filings / False Non-Residence Claim

Statutory sanction: Denial of Gibraltar non-residence status; assessment of the ELP as a Gibraltar-resident taxpayer on its worldwide income; simultaneous UK assessment on the parent; penalties of up to 200% of the evaded UK tax under Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007 for deliberate behavior; cross-border enforcement of both revenue claims.

Director personal liability: HMRC enforcement against the nominated General Partner; unlimited liability for de facto control and management; personal tax assessments on the UK resident directors for the economic benefit obtained; criminal prosecution for dishonest conduct involving deliberate tax avoidance.
