Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Revenue Department, HM Government of Gibraltar, Gibraltar Income Tax Office, e-Services portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity established as a Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) must file a completed Tax Registration Form (Form 6) with the Commissioner of Income Tax, attaching the executed Limited Partnership Agreement, a certified copy of the Registration Certificate issued under the Limited Partnerships Act, and proof of identity and place of business for each general and limited partner. A local tax representative must be appointed for non-resident partners. Upon scrutiny and approval, the Commissioner issues a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) and, where applicable, a separate Partner TIN schedule for income allocation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under the Income Tax Act 2010, an ELP is fiscally transparent unless any partner (corporate or natural) is itself a taxable entity; no check-the-box election exists for the partnership as a whole. Partners may individually elect treatment under any applicable tax regime for their residency. No hybrid mismatch election is available in Gibraltar domestic law; however, partners should review the OECD MLI and UK-Gibraltar arrangements for allocation and anti-hybrid adjustments.

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST (Gibraltar is outside the EU VATE regime; customs and excise duties apply to imports of physical goods)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not Applicable - Gibraltar does not levy value added tax or a general consumption tax; no indirect tax registration threshold exists for services, including trade finance and factoring transactions.

Not Applicable - Gibraltar does not impose VAT/GST and therefore has no export zero-rating mechanism. Cross-border financial services (factoring and reverse factoring) are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax. For physical goods, customs export formalities may apply but no VAT refund arises in a zero-VAT jurisdiction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP is pegged at par to GBP and fully convertible. Gibraltar maintains a wide-open capital account with no exchange-control permits, quotas, or prior approvals for current-account flows (factoring proceeds, discount fees, commissions, interest) or capital-account flows (capital calls, loan repayments, partner distributions, dissolution proceeds). Profit remittance is unrestricted in law; the only practical hurdles are documentary: evidence of the underlying invoice ledger, proof of pass-through accounting for the ELP, source-of-funds clearance of receiving limited partners, and CRS/FATCA reporting schedules. Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on distributions to non-resident partners of a fiscally transparent exempted limited partnership, provided the ELP maintains its non-resident partner status and files the annual return; a nil-withholding tax opinion and a partner tax-residence schedule are the standard cross-border remittance evidence bundle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Neither the United Kingdom nor Gibraltar imposes any statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation or mandatory conversion ratio. The ELP factor may retain 100% of export-proceeds collections in the invoice currency (GBP, USD, EUR) with no repatriation timeline and no conversion requirement. Statutory timeline: nil; mandatory conversion ratio: 0%. Residual controls are limited to OFSI/OFAC/UN/EU asset-freeze screening under the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018; a factoring ELP is therefore free to pre-fund suppliers in original invoice currency and carry a natural open FX position subject to its internal hedging policy.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the licensing, prudential, and AML/CFT supervisory authority for Gibraltar; the Government of Gibraltar (HM Government of Gibraltar Currency Notes Board) as issuer of Gibraltar pound (GIP) banknotes under the Currency Notes Act 1934 on a 100% Bank of England note cover basis; the Bank of England as the ultimate monetary authority of the sterling-pegged currency area; HM Treasury Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) as the sanctions administration authority for the United Kingdom and Gibraltar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH for ancillary disbursement rails using UK FCA-authorised EMIs (Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Modulr, ClearBank) and Gibraltar-licensed payment/e-money institutions (e.g., Flexbase Technologies) as liquidity vestibules; MEDIUM for using a UK-centred fully digital neobank as the primary treasury account, because post-Brexit there is no EEA passport into Gibraltar and the EMI must onboard the ELP as a cross-border corporate client at the UK headquarters; LOW for offshore non-EEA/non-UK unregulated digital EMIs, whose balances carry counterparty default risk and are frequently rejected by GFSC banks on correspondent 'feeder-fintech' AML grounds. Legal constraints: (1) an e-money float is not a bank deposit and is outside the Gibraltar Deposit Guarantee Scheme and UK FSCS; (2) an EMI balance exceeding GBP 85,000 triggers daily-sweep and backup-bank obligations under prudent GFSC-managed treasury policy; (3) crypto-native EMI rails are acceptable only for T+1 fiat sweeps and must never hold escrow of trade receivables. Mandatory design: factor collections flow to a GFSC tier-1 bank for finality, and EMI accounts are used solely for vendor microdisbursements and payroll-type payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: all capital injections are wired from each limited partner's own bank account (never from third-party EMI settlement accounts) via SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the ELP's GFSC bank capital-acceptance account. Purpose coding: ISO 20022 purpose code 'CAPT' (capital contribution) for GBP and EUR legs, or 'INTC' for intra-group loans, mapped by the receiving bank to UK BOP6 category 'Foreign Direct Investment — Equity Capital' where required; the remittance narrative must match the LPA capital-call clause (e.g., 'LP-2 CAP CALL 3, GBP 1,250,000, LPA cl. 7.2'). Verification is two-step: (1) pre-funding — source-of-wealth pack, sanctions screens, and LP bank reference; (2) post-funding — bank credit reconciliation against the capital-call ledger and an auditor's movement schedule. For EUR inflows routed through SEPA, the SEPA Credit Transfer must carry the same purpose narrative and must originate from the LP's own identified account. Inbound injections above GBP 500,000 are reported on the Gibraltar FDI annual survey submitted via the GFSC statistical gateway.Outbound settlement is rail-segmented by currency and instrument: (1) GBP corridor — CHAPS same-day value for payments above GBP 100,000 and Faster Payments for vendor microdisbursements, both carrying the SWIFT MT103 equivalent reference; (2) EUR corridor — Gibraltar participates in SEPA (GIB country code), so EUR payables settle via SEPA Credit Transfer/SEPA Instant at zero SWIFT charge for SEPA-zone counterparties; (3) commercial documentary credits — supplier payments against purchase orders are made by irrevocable Commercial LC (MT700 issue / MT710 confirmation) with sight or usance drafts against compliant presentation of invoices, bills of lading, and inspection certificates; (4) interbank cover payments use SWIFT MT202 for correspondent-funded legs. For reverse-factoring, suppliers are paid at issuance under an MT103 with purpose code 'TRAD' or 'INVC' and reference 'FACTOR-PRE-ADV', while at maturity the buyer's payment is swept through the escrow account and disbursed under a covering letter referencing the factoring agreement. All disbursements are matched to the approved obligor matrix and concentration limits and archived with the underlying invoice ledger.Deliverable FX outright forwards (GBPUSD, EURGBP, USDCNH) up to 12-month tenors executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement and Credit Support Annex with a GFSC-bank or rated counterparty; hedge notional capped at 110% of forecast affected receivables under a hedging-only treasury policy.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted-convertibility receivables (CNY, INR, BRL, IDR) versus USD/GBP, referenced to representative fixing rates (CNH CNY fix, Reuters NDF fixings, central-bank reference rates), cleared through a prime-brokerage client-clearing structure acceptable to the GFSC bank.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CCS/XCCY) to hedge the basis between USD/GBP warehouse funding and the EUR/USD/GBP receivable currency, with tenors matched to the ageing profile of factored receivables and periodic reset schedules.Vanilla FX options and zero-cost collar/risk-reversal overlays, permissible as an overlay on the forward layer; premiums and settlements are recorded in the treasury file and the instruments may not be used speculatively beyond economic hedge notional.FX spot and FX swap/roll transactions settled via CLS (Continuous Linked Settlement) for G10 pairs (USD, EUR, GBP, JPY) to eliminate settlement risk on collections and disbursements executed on the same SWIFT value date, with daily internal netting under the general-partner-approved hedging mandate.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH for ancillary disbursement rails using UK FCA-authorised EMIs (Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud, Modulr, ClearBank) and Gibraltar-licensed payment/e-money institutions (e.g., Flexbase Technologies) as liquidity vestibules; MEDIUM for using a UK-centred fully digital neobank as the primary treasury account, because post-Brexit there is no EEA passport into Gibraltar and the EMI must onboard the ELP as a cross-border corporate client at the UK headquarters; LOW for offshore non-EEA/non-UK unregulated digital EMIs, whose balances carry counterparty default risk and are frequently rejected by GFSC banks on correspondent 'feeder-fintech' AML grounds. Legal constraints: (1) an e-money float is not a bank deposit and is outside the Gibraltar Deposit Guarantee Scheme and UK FSCS; (2) an EMI balance exceeding GBP 85,000 triggers daily-sweep and backup-bank obligations under prudent GFSC-managed treasury policy; (3) crypto-native EMI rails are acceptable only for T+1 fiat sweeps and must never hold escrow of trade receivables. Mandatory design: factor collections flow to a GFSC tier-1 bank for finality, and EMI accounts are used solely for vendor microdisbursements and payroll-type payments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar has no statutory Local File or Master File monetary threshold in the Income Tax Act. As a prudent compliance benchmark for this advisory: a Local File should be prepared where cross-border related-party factoring or financing transactions exceed GIP 1,000,000 annually; a Master File is recommended where worldwide group revenue exceeds GIP 50,000,000. Supporting documentation must be made available to the Commissioner on request.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations 2019 apply to an ELP carrying on a relevant activity in Gibraltar. Cross-border trade factoring and reverse factoring constitute financing-and-leasing business for ESR purposes. The ELP must exercise mind and management in Gibraltar, perform core income-generating activities locally (credit underwriting, factoring-book risk management, funding and discounting decisions), maintain adequate physical premises, full-time employees and local operating expenditure, and file an annual economic substance return. Failure to satisfy the doctrine creates a statutory presumption of a shell company and exposes the ELP to penalties of up to GIP 120,000 plus daily compounding penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 100% of the tax undercharged; monthly interest at 1.5%; assessments may be raised without a time limit in cases of fraud; on indictment, an unlimited fine and imprisonment. The Gibraltar Income Tax Office may also invoke mutual assistance and exchange-of-information mechanisms with HMRC and other treaty partners.

Director personal liability: General partners and their directors who knowingly participate are personally exposed to civil restitution orders, asset freezes, travel bans and imprisonment for fraud; the corporate veil is disregarded where the ELP is used as a mere facade.
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance (ESR Failure)

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance Regulations penalties up to GIP 120,000, daily compounding penalties of up to GIP 10,000, potential strike-off from the Gibraltar register, revocation of any required GFSC licence, and automatic exchange of information with foreign tax authorities, which may trigger reverse-hybrid and CFC adjustments.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners face disqualification orders, personal assessment for penalties, travel restrictions, and adverse inference of tax evasion in source jurisdictions.
Unlicensed Financial Services Activity and AML Breach

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction, a fine of up to GIP 500,000 and/or imprisonment; on indictment, an unlimited fine and imprisonment of up to 7 years. The GFSC may impose additional supervisory penalties and remedial orders; AML failures attract civil penalties and asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Every director or officer of the general partner who consented or connived is personally criminally liable; courts may impose confiscation orders, travel bans, prohibition from acting as a director, and, for serious trade-based money-laundering, extradition exposure.
Treaty Shopping and Abuse of DTA Benefits

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterisation under the Gibraltar general anti-abuse rule, tax-geared penalties up to 100% of the undercharged tax, and coordinated joint investigations with HMRC under the UK-Gibraltar DTA exchange-of-information provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors who structure or approve abusive arrangements face personal penalty assessments, travel bans, and criminal negligence proceedings where false or misleading documentation was lodged with the tax authority.
Trade-Based Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment of up to 14 years under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, civil asset forfeiture, financial sanctions enforcement, imposition of AML fines, and mandatory reporting obligations to the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Any person knowingly concerned at director or general-partner level is personally liable, with asset seizure, travel restrictions, extradition exposure, and automatic disqualification from holding financial-services positions.
