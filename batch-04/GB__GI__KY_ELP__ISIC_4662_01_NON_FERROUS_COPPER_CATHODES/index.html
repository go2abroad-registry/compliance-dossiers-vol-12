Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (ITO) in conjunction with Gibraltar Companies House (Registry) for entity registration. The ITO is the sole corporate income tax administrator; customs and excise matters are administered by the Customs & Excise Division of HM Government of Gibraltar. Primary e-services portal: www.gibraltar.gov.gi (online forms for TIN application and customs declarations).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the ELP with Gibraltar Companies House under the Limited Partnerships Act 2012 (online incorporation filing, submit partnership deed, register of partners). Step 2: Upon issuance of the Certificate of Registration, file Form ELP-1 'Application for Tax Reference Number' with the Income Tax Office. Step 3: Annex certified copies of the Certificate of Registration, Registered Details Form, partnership agreement, and declarations of residence of general and limited partners (including beneficial-owner disclosure under the Beneficial Ownership (Access and Security) Regulations). Step 4: ITO issues the Tax Reference Number (TRN) and, where all partners are non-resident and no Gibraltar-source income is expected, a confirmation of non-resident tax status. Processing typically 5–10 working days.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible. Under Section 3(1) Income Tax Act, an ELP is transparent unless capitalised as an entity for tax purposes; each partner is separately assessed on allocable Gibraltar-source income. If no income arises in Gibraltar and management and control are outside Gibraltar (e.g., trading through LME brokers and offshore arbitrage counterparties), the ELP has no Gibraltar income-tax liability. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. Under Section 3(1) Income Tax Act, an ELP is transparent unless capitalised as an entity for tax purposes; each partner is separately assessed on allocable Gibraltar-source income. If no income arises in Gibraltar and management and control are outside Gibraltar (e.g., trading through LME brokers and offshore arbitrage counterparties), the ELP has no Gibraltar income-tax liability. Not an Entity_Level_Taxation regime.

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box. Available elections and rulings: (i) Non-resident/exempt status election under Income Tax Act s.7 (qualifying entities wholly owned by non-residents); (ii) advance tax ruling from the Commissioner of Income Tax confirming partnership transparency and allocation of profits; (iii) hybrid-mismatch election under OECD Action 2 implementing provisions to align deduction/timing outcomes with the UK parent regime; (iv) election under the Limited Partnerships Act that the ELP has separate legal personality (affects legal form, not tax transparency).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST in Gibraltar. The indirect tax regime consists of: (1) Import Duty under the Customs Duty (General) Regulations and Gibraltar Tariff (aligned to the UK Global Tariff for third-country goods); (2) Excise Duty on hydrocarbons, alcohol and tobacco; (3) Stamp Duty on certain documents and property transfers. Exports are exempt from duty. Post-Brexit, Gibraltar applies its own customs tax boundary distinct from the UK.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Gibraltar levies no VAT/GST and therefore sets no economic-turnover registration threshold. Any person (including a foreign ELP) importing goods for the first time must register with the Customs & Excise Division as an importer/presenter of goods (no minimum value threshold; however, low-value consignments under GIP 135 may qualify for customs de minimis relief).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — Gibraltar has no VAT, so no 0% export VAT rate is claimable. Exports of copper cathodes from Gibraltar are outside the scope of indirect tax. For customs purposes, goods which are imported into Gibraltar and subsequently physically exported may be relieved of import duty under Inward Processing Relief (suspension) or obtained post-export Duty Drawback; relief is available to an authorised trader, requires prior customs approval, and proof of physical export plus receipt of sale proceeds within the prescribed time.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar electronic export declaration with unique MRN)
  • Bill of Lading (originally endorsed, evidencing shipment of Grade A copper cathodes)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 (evidencing receipt of export consideration within 180 days)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (LME-specified grade, lot numbers and net copper weight)
  • LME Warrant or Transferable Warehouse Receipt (evidence of title in copper cathodes)
  • Certificate of Origin / EUR.1 (where preferential origin or duty drawback is claimed)
  • Proof of Physical Exit (port outturn report / vessel departure certificate)

No real-time e-invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate (no CTC or digital stamping regime) in Gibraltar. Import/export declarations must be filed electronically via the Gibraltar Customs electronic filing system, with the Export Accompanying Document and movement references generated automatically. Invoices issued by the ELP must satisfy Income Tax Act record-keeping rules (retention six years) and commercial authenticity requirements of LME/LC documentation.

Because no VAT/GST applies, export zero-rating fraud per se is not prosecuted; however, false customs declarations, abuse of inward-processing/drawback relief, or trade mis-invoicing of copper cathodes triggers retrospective duty recovery plus interest (1.5% per month), penalties of up to 100% of duty avoided or GIP 10,000 (whichever is higher), forfeiture of goods, and criminal liability under the Customs Ordinance. The Commissioner of Income Tax may issue an amended assessment for up to 6 years (unlimited in fraud cases) where export proceeds are diverted or not repatriated, with a 50% uplift for deliberate concealment. AML investigators may apply the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 to trading profit and impose account-freezing orders.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar Pound (GIP), pegged at par with British Pound Sterling (GBP). Both current account and capital account items are fully convertible with no exchange control restrictions. Profit remittances, capital distributions, loan repayments and partnership redemptions may be executed freely in GIP, GBP, USD, EUR or any freely convertible currency, subject only to bank-level AML/CFT documentary diligence and, where applicable, the Gibraltar Economic Substance (ES) filing obligations of the ELP.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil. Neither Gibraltar nor the United Kingdom imposes a statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation or mandatory conversion ratio. Physical copper cathode export receipts may be retained indefinitely in the original transaction currency. The effective surrender ratio is 0% and there is no prescribed holding or repatriation window. Conversion to GBP or other currencies is at the ELP's commercial discretion, subject to normal bank dealing rates.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for the United Kingdom and, through sterling parity, for Gibraltar); the Financial Services Commission (Gibraltar) ('GFSC') acts as the licensing, prudential and AML/CFT supervisory authority for Gibraltar-licensed banks and payment firms. Gibraltar maintains no separate central bank or foreign exchange administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-LOW as a primary clearing rail; MEDIUM-HIGH as an ancillary treasury rail. Post-Brexit, EEA and UK digital e-money institutions (EMIs) no longer benefit from passporting into Gibraltar. A third-country EMI must obtain GFSC authorisation under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the E-Money Regulations, or operate through a Gibraltar-licensed branch. Legal constraints include safeguarding account requirements, transaction caps for high-value metal settlements, and the practical incompatibility of EMI rails with LME warrant value transfers and documentary Letters of Credit. EMI rails are usable for minor freight, insurance and logistics disbursements but not for core copper cathode settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections are executed via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for intermediary settlement) from the contributing partner's bank to the ELP's segregated GIP/GBP or USD account at the Gibraltar bank. The remittance memo must identify the purpose code 'CT' (Capital Transfer) or the bank's prescribed 'Capital Contribution' narrative and a clear reference to the partnership agreement article governing capital commitments. Verification requires (i) the certified capital contribution schedule, (ii) an extract of the Limited Partnership Agreement evidencing capital commitment, (iii) source-of-funds documentation from the contributing partner, (iv) confirmation of unencumbrance, and (v) where the contribution exceeds bank thresholds, an ECDD file and confirmation of Economic Substance filing status.Core clearing rails for outbound trade settlements: (1) documentary Letters of Credit issued under UCP 600 by the Gibraltar bank in favour of overseas copper suppliers, payable against presentation of LME warehouse warrants, bills of lading, certificates of origin, assay certificates and weight lists; (2) direct SWIFT MT103 payments for settled spot purchases, including LME warrant value-transfer settlement through LME Clear-approved clearing members and warehouse-to-warehouse warrant transfers; (3) standby Letters of Credit and bank guarantees to LME-approved warehouse operators and logistics providers; (4) MT101 batch customer transfer requests for recurring freight, insurance, storage and inspection disbursements. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 5,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions in a fiscal period

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations 2019 (as amended) apply, and the wholesale physical copper cathode arbitrage activity falls within the 'distribution and service centre' relevant activity. The ELP must demonstrate, through its corporate general partner and any Gibraltar employees/agents, proportionate premises, staff and direction-and-control presence in Gibraltar. Reduced substance requirements do not apply to a trading partnership of this nature unless the partnership is a pure equity holding entity, which it is not.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: Penalty up to 200% of the tax evaded (minimum GIP 100), statutory interest, criminal prosecution with fines up to GIP 500,000, and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner face civil restitution for unpaid tax; the ELP veil may be pierced when the partnership is used as a deliberate tax evasion mechanism; travel bans, passport surrender, and imprisonment up to 7 years may be imposed.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment with additional tax at 10%, interest, and an accuracy penalty up to 100% of the tax underpaid; the filing may be treated as fraudulent where APAs or contemporaneous documents are fabricated.

Director personal liability: Signing officers who approve non-arm's-length arrangements face personal penalties up to GIP 100,000, director disqualification, and imprisonment up to 5 years; the corporate veil is disregarded where the structure is a sham, leaving the directors personally liable for restitution.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Economic Substance penalties up to GIP 50,000 per breach, daily default penalties up to GIP 1,000, automatic exchange of information with HMRC and OECD, and strike-off from the Gibraltar Registry.

Director personal liability: A general partner and its directors cannot rely on limited liability where economic substance is absent; civil recovery for penalties, travel restrictions, asset freezes and criminal prosecution of the controlling directors are available.
AML / Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Fines up to GIP 500,000 under the Criminal Justice Act 2011, confiscation of proceeds of crime, prohibition orders on holding regulated functions, and compliance monitoring by the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: Directors, the nominated MLRO and the general partner are personally liable for AML/sanctions failures; penalties include confiscation of personal assets, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 10 years; the corporate veil will be pierced where AML controls are deliberately ignored.
