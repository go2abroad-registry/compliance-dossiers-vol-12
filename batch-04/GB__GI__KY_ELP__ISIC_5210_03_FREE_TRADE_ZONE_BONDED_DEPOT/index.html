Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar, 200 Town Range, Gibraltar GX11 1AA; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax-office (e-filing via ITO e-Business). Complementary authorities: Companies House Gibraltar (Registry) for ELP incorporation; HM Customs Gibraltar (Gibraltar Border and Coastguard Agency) for bonded warehouse licensing and customs declarations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the ELP with Companies House Gibraltar under the Limited Partnerships Act 1925 (as amended), filing the partnership statement and register of general partners; obtain the Partnership Registration Number. Step 2: File the prescribed Partnership Tax Declaration form with the Income Tax Office within 30 days of establishment, together with the ELP constitutional documents and the nominated Gibraltar-resident general partner or tax officer. Step 3: Obtain the Gibraltar Partnership Tax Reference Number (TRN) issued by the Commissioner, used for annual partnership returns and partner reporting. Step 4: Identify each partner (including non-resident corporate or natural persons); partners deriving Gibraltar-source income must obtain their own individual or corporate TIN, while partners with no Gibraltar-source income may apply for a Non-Resident Clearance Certificate (NRCC) to substantiate nil filing obligations. Step 5: Register the ELP with HM Customs for an Economic Operator Code and, if acting as warehouse keeper, apply for a Bonded Warehouse Licence under the Customs and Excise Ordinance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership is mandatorily fiscally transparent: no entity-level income tax is imposed; each partner is assessed on their share of Gibraltar-source income only (territorial scope). The ELP has no minimum capital requirement and non-resident ownership is fully permitted.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box election; transparency is the statutory default and no election to be taxed as a company is available. Available statutory elections and mechanisms: (i) application for exempt limited partnership status under the Limited Partnerships Act 1925, confirming that profits arising outside Gibraltar are not liable to tax and that no tax return is required for foreign-source activities; (ii) election by the ELP general partner under the Income Tax Act 2010 to file a composite partnership return allocating taxable Gibraltar-source income among partners by agreed ratio; (iii) non-resident partner clearance certificate (NRCC) election to evidence territorial exemption from filing; (iv) for multinational groups, hybrid mismatch elections filed in accordance with the EU Anti-Tax Avoidance Directive rules as transposed in Gibraltar; and (v) joint election with HM Customs for duty-suspension bonded warehouse treatment rather than manufacturing or processing status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None — Gibraltar imposes no VAT, GST, or general Sales and Use Tax. Indirect taxation comprises Customs Import Duty and Excise Duty on alcohol, tobacco, and petroleum products, plus Stamp Duty on specified instruments; the bonded warehouse/free trade zone operates under duty suspension.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A — no VAT/GST turnover threshold applies in Gibraltar. Registration with HM Customs as an Economic Operator (customs code) and a Bonded Warehouse Keeper's Licence are mandatory without any minimum turnover for any person storing third-country goods in the Free Trade Zone bonded depot. A duty bond or financial guarantee equal to suspended duties must be lodged with HM Customs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT, hence no formal export zero-rating certificate is issued. Instead, the bonded depot operates a duty-suspension free zone regime: customs and excise duties are deferred while goods remain in the depot or are re-exported to a customs territory outside Gibraltar; goods released for free circulation in Gibraltar attract duty at the point of removal. Where a UK VAT zero-rating claim is relevant (goods supplied by a UK VAT-registered party and exported from the depot directly to the UK customer), the exporter must evidence that the goods were exported from the United Kingdom's customs territory within 30 days of supply and satisfy UK VAT Act 1994 Schedule 8 Group 1 conditions, supported by the Gibraltar customs export record.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar electronic SAD/EX1 equivalent, lodged and accepted by HM Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (issued by the carrier confirming physical export from Gibraltar)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in a Gibraltar-licensed bank within the repatriation deadline)
  • Commercial Invoice and detailed Packing List
  • Bonded Warehouse Withdrawal Permit (customs release note from the depot documenting termination of duty suspension)
  • Certificate of Origin (when preferential tariff treatment or UK/EU free trade agreement claims are made)

No real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping of invoices is required by the tax authority. Mandatory electronic customs clearance applies: all declarations for admission to, storage in, and removal from the bonded depot (including re-export) must be lodged via the Gibraltar Electronic Customs Declaration System in SAD format; warehouse inventory and stock movement records must be maintained electronically and transmitted to HM Customs on a per-shipment basis (or weekly for low-volume keepers). No statutory e-invoicing API mandate exists, but declared export invoices must be retained in the ITO e-Business file for audit.

HM Customs and the Income Tax Office may conduct joint audits of bonded warehouse operators, customs agents, and partner records. Fraudulent export claims, false declarations, or unlawful removal of duty-suspended goods attract civil penalties up to the greater of GIP 500,000 or 300% of the duty suspended or evaded; goods and vehicles are liable to seizure; retrospective assessments may extend beyond 10 years where fraud, neglect, or false declarations are established; warehouse keeper licences are revocable with immediate effect with publication of a blacklist; criminal convictions carry imprisonment of up to 7 years. In cases involving UK VAT zero-rating fraud connected to depot exports, HMRC may raise a clawback assessment for the full VAT amount plus a penalty of up to 100% of the VAT and interest at the statutory rate. General partners may be personally liable for duty clawbacks where the ELP is insolvent or where the partnership veil is pierced for fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both the capital account and current account are fully convertible; GIP is freely exchangeable into GBP at 1:1 backed by UK sterling assets. No exchange-control approvals are required for inward capital contributions, trade payments, or outward distributions; the UK Exchange Control Act restrictions were repealed in 1979 and Gibraltar has never imposed separate controls. Profit remittance is unrestricted: a Gibraltar ELP is tax-transparent, non-resident partners pay no Gibraltar tax on foreign-source income, and there is no withholding tax on partnership distributions. The only operational frictions are GFSC-driven AML source-of-funds verification under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and sanctions screening on SWIFT traffic.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, compulsory conversion, or repatriation timeline applies to export proceeds in Gibraltar or the United Kingdom. A bonded-depot operator may retain receivables in any invoiced currency within multi-currency accounts, including USD, EUR, and GBP. Customs duty suspension under the Customs Ordinance and Free Trade Zone regulations is independent of foreign-exchange rules; however, full TBML-compliant trade documentation must be retained to support clearance of incoming proceeds.

Central bank supervision: Government of Gibraltar, acting through the Ministry of Finance under the Currency Notes Act 2011, is the currency-issuing authority for the Gibraltar Pound (GIP), pegged at parity to GBP. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory prudential and conduct regulator for all banks, electronic money institutions, and payment service providers under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2021. The Bank of England and the Prudential Regulation Authority have no direct supervisory jurisdiction over Gibraltar-incorporated Exempted Limited Partnerships.

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as primary transaction bank; MEDIUM as an auxiliary low-value payments rail. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions are received via SWIFT MT103 with ISO purpose code CAPC and the remittance narrative expressly stating the partnership name, contributing partner reference, and 'partnership capital contribution - no trade proceeds'. Alternatively, UK remitters may use UK Faster Payments through the Gibraltar bank's UK settlement account for GBP amounts below one million. A capital call notice and registered-agent certificate are required for any tranche above GBP 50,000; multi-currency injections are converted at the daily interbank rate plus a 0.25%-0.40% spread, and no GFSC or Bank of England prior approval is required.Trade settlements use SWIFT MT103 with ISO purpose code TRAD and SWIFT GPI tracking for open-account supplier payments; MT700 irrevocable documentary credits for high-value bonded inventory purchases, with full-set clean on-board bills of lading, warehouse warrants, commercial invoices, and packing lists as presenting documents; local Gibraltar credit clearing for domestic counterparties; and UK Faster Payments via the bank's agency account for small-value disbursements. Customs duties, storage, and Free Trade Zone authority fees are settled through the customs deferment direct debit arrangement.Outright deliverable FX forwards (USD/GBP, EUR/GBP, CNY/GBP) with tenors up to 24 months, matching bonded-trade receivables and payables under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement or FX Master Agreement with a licensed Gibraltar bank; the ELP is a Non-Financial Counterparty below the UK EMIR clearing threshold, so no central clearing or margin obligation applies.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on CNY/CNH, INR, NGN, and EGP for counterparties operating under local exchange controls, cash-settled in GBP and confirmed via SWIFT MT300.Cross-currency swaps (GBP/USD, GBP/EUR) for balance-sheet matching of foreign-currency capital commitments and transformation of trade receivables into the functional currency, permitted strictly under the ELP's hedging mandate in the Limited Partnership Agreement and subject to GFSC derivatives record-keeping.Vanilla FX options, both purchased calls and puts on USD/GBP and EUR/GBP, for contingent bid-offer trade flows, with option premiums remitted via MT103 using the CFDS purpose narrative.FX swap combinations (spot-start plus matched forward) as short-dated liquidity management instruments for tri-party inventory conversion inside the bonded depot; all transactions must be contemporaneously evidenced by underlying trade documents to sustain hedge accounting and pass-through tax treatment.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW as primary transaction bank; MEDIUM as an auxiliary low-value payments rail. A non-Gibraltar EMI authorized only by the UK FCA or another EEA regulator cannot passport into Gibraltar post-Brexit without separate GFSC licensing under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2021. Gibraltar-licensed EMIs, such as Transact Payments Limited, predominantly provide BIN sponsorship and card-scheme services rather than full commercial treasury accounts. Global digital EMIs (Revolut Business, Wise, Airwallex) typically decline bonded-warehouse and Free Trade Zone traders classified as Medium Risk Enhanced, citing trade-based money-laundering policy; the ELP should rely on these rails exclusively for small, name-matched disbursements below GBP 5,000.

The Gibraltar year of assessment runs 1 July to 30 June. The return must be filed by 30 November immediately following the close of the year of assessment; where the ELP uses a non-30 June accounting reference date, the return is due within five months of its accounting period end.
