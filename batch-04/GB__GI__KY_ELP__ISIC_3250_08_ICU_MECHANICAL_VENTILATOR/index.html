Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (ITO), operated via the HMGoG e-Tax Portal (https://egov.gibraltar.gov.gi) and administered under the Income Tax Act 2010 as amended. The ITO issues the Gibraltar Registration Number (GRN/TIN) for direct tax and customs matters; indirect taxation is administered by HM Customs Gibraltar (Custom House).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) must complete the following: (1) file the Partnership Registration Form with the Gibraltar Companies House (Registrar of Companies) under the Limited Partnerships Act 2016, obtaining a Partnership Registration Certificate; (2) apply to the Income Tax Office for a GRN/TIN using Form IF-2025-ELP-NR, attaching the Partnership Deed, Certificate of Limited Partnership, and proof of registered office in Gibraltar; (3) submit details of all general and limited partners, including each partner's home jurisdiction TIN, to satisfy the Tax Information Exchange and Beneficial Ownership (Legal Entities) regime; (4) receive the GRN/TIN letter confirming the ELP is registered, that it is treated as tax transparent, and that no Gibraltar corporate income tax applies absent local source trading income or local-source property income; and (5) complete separate registration with HM Customs Gibraltar for import/export duty and Intrastat/GB customs reporting where goods physically transit Gibraltar or are cleared through the Port of Gibraltar. The ELP must also provide the GI003-BO file naming the ultimate controlling person. Registration is completed within 5–10 business days; no minimum capital requirement applies.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible (fiscally transparent partnership). Gibraltar applies territorial taxation: partnership profits derived by non-resident partners are not taxable in Gibraltar unless attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment or local-source property/rental income. Distributions to non-resident partners are exempt from Gibraltar income tax. The ELP itself is not a taxable person; partners are returned on their respective jurisdictions' tax returns. The 12.5% profits tax rate applies only to Gibraltar-resident companies; the ELP has no entity-level profits tax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (fiscally transparent partnership). Gibraltar applies territorial taxation: partnership profits derived by non-resident partners are not taxable in Gibraltar unless attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment or local-source property/rental income. Distributions to non-resident partners are exempt from Gibraltar income tax. The ELP itself is not a taxable person; partners are returned on their respective jurisdictions' tax returns. The 12.5% profits tax rate applies only to Gibraltar-resident companies; the ELP has no entity-level profits tax. A Gibraltar tax residence certificate is not ordinarily available to a non-resident ELP.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a 'check-the-box' election equivalent to US Subchapter S or IRS Entity Classification Regulations. However, the ELP may make the following statutory elections: (1) an election under s.45 of the Income Tax Act 2010 for the partnership to be conclusively treated as fiscally transparent for all Gibraltar tax purposes, binding on partners; (2) a deemed permanent establishment election in writing to the ITO, electing to be assessed on a territorial basis at a reduced allocable amount where local trading activities are conducted; (3) a hybrid mismatch election under the Gibraltar (Tax Treaties and Hybrid Mismatch) Regulations to re-characterise the ELP as a separate taxpayer for treaty purposes where a double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion mismatch arises; and (4) an irrevocable election to opt out of the Gibraltar Companies (Surcharge) rules if the partnership elects to be treated as an 'offshore company' solely for customs facilitation. No US-style check-the-box return is filed, as Gibraltar does not participate in IRS Form 8832 domestic elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/SUT applies in Gibraltar. Gibraltar is excluded from the UK VAT Act 1994 and the EU VAT Directive (except to the limited extent of the EU-UK Withdrawal Agreement transitional provisions). The indirect tax nexus is governed by the Import Duties Act 1998, the Customs Duties Ordinance, and the Excise Duties Ordinance applying a single territorial customs tariff on the physical import of goods into Gibraltar. There is no sales and use tax, no transfer tax on goods, no service tax, and no digital services tax in Gibraltar.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 0 (nil). Gibraltar levies NO VAT/GST, and therefore no economic turnover threshold applies for VAT registration; no Gibraltar VAT number is obtainable. For customs duty purposes, any person or entity importing or exporting goods through Gibraltar (including the ELP) must register with HM Customs Gibraltar when the cumulative value of goods imported or exported in a rolling 12-month period reaches GIP 5,000, at which point a Customs Registered Exporter (CRE) Code is mandatory; a separate Customs Warehouse licence is required for bonded storage, subject to a bank guarantee of GIP 25,000 minimum. The ELP manufacturing critical care ventilators in Gibraltar for export is not required to register for VAT anywhere in Gibraltar, but must file monthly customs Intrastat/EU-import declarations only when physical goods enter Gibraltar; if goods are drop-shipped and never enter Gibraltar customs territory, no Gibraltar customs registration arises.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar does not have a VAT zero-rating regime. Instead, the ELP applies export duty zero-rating (0% export tariff) under the Export (No. 2) Regulations pursuant to the Import Duties Act 1998. To qualify for 0% export duty and to obtain a Certificate of Export (D.35) verified by HM Customs, the ELP must establish all of the following: (1) the buyer is a non-Gibraltar resident (foreign country, EU Member State, or UK mainland destination), evidenced by a purchase order and export contract (Ex Works, FOB Gibraltar, or CIF terms); (2) genuine physical exportation occurs within 90 days of payment from Gibraltar customs territory — for drop-shipment to the UK/EU, the ELP must evidence that the goods never enter Gibraltar and be wholly outside Gibraltar at the moment of sale; (3) the ventilator goods are classified under HS Code 9018.90 (parts/accessories) or 9019.20 (mechano-therapy appliances; ventilation apparatus), attract 0% duty, and bear valid CE/UKCA marking for medical devices under the UK MDR 2002/618 and Gibraltar Medicines Act 2020; (4) proceeds of export are received in accordance with Gibraltar Exchange Control Notice EX-2024-08; and (5) the ELP holds a valid Customs Exporter Licence (class X). Export zero-rating is not automatic — a written approval from the Comptroller of Customs must be obtained before shipment. Services related to the ventilators (e.g., offshore engineering, commissioning, remote diagnostics performed from Gibraltar) are not subject to any Gibraltar indirect tax because no VAT/GST applies; if invoiced to a UK mainland customer, they fall outside the scope of UK VAT where the Gibraltar supplier is not registered for UK VAT, provided the place of supply rules are met.

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping for B2B/B2G invoices. No national e-invoicing API clearance platform exists. Invoices of the ELP must nevertheless comply with the Gibraltar Customs (Counterinsurance) and Foreign Trade Regulations: (1) issue English-language invoice showing 'GIBRALTAR – NO VAT' (NIL rated); (2) file customs declarations electronically via the HM Customs Electronic Data Interchange system (G-CDS Web Portal) for any physical shipment entering or leaving Gibraltar, using CN-23/SAD style digital declarations; (3) for goods routed through the UK to Northern Ireland or EU Member States, the ELP must connect to the UK CDS (Customs Declaration Service) via a customs broker with CHIEF/CDS API integration, submitting customs valuation and commodity codes using the UK Integrated Online Tariff; (4) export declarations are required before goods are laden, using SAD Form C88/E-EX, transmitted via the CDS API with consignment references. Sanitary and Medical Device Regulations 2023 apply, but no fiscal clearance is involved. A post-clearance electronic archive of all invoices, declarations, packing lists, and customs release notifications must be retained for at least 6 years.

HM Customs and the Income Tax Office jointly audit export transactions of ELP medical-device manufacturers under the Foreign Trade and Exchange Control Act. Penalties for false export declarations or sham zero-rating claims include: (1) criminal conviction for fraudulent exportation — up to GIP 250,000 fine (or 7 years' imprisonment; ELP may be struck off); (2) civil seizure and forfeiture of all goods and the conveyance carrying them, including the ELP's bank funds in equal amount, under s.20 Customs Duties Act; (3) retrospective duty recovery — Comptroller may 'claw back' the 0% duty benefit for ten years and charge the amount that would have been payable at the standard rate (effective tariff 12.5% for some manufactured goods), plus interest of 2% per month (compound) and a 100% surcharge if any false As. certificate, Bill of Lading, or Inward Remittance Certificate is tendered (WP send). (4) the Income Tax Office may re-characterise export rebates/duty-free proceeds as a distribution taxable to Gibraltar-resident partners at 12.5% (plus combined additional payments) where the ELP maintained a Gibraltar permanent establishment; (5) for failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days, daily penalties of 0.05% of the unremitted amount apply, subject to GIP 50,000 cap; the consequence is automatic forfeiture of the export zero-rating and a redetermination of all prior export declarations within that financial year; (6) where the ELP's beneficial owner is the KY Exempted Limited Partnership ('KY_ELP'), HM Customs may pierce the structure under the Beneficial Ownership Act 2023 to recover penalties from partners, and the Comptroller can deny the export refund until a full audit of the entire 10-year export ledger confirms the genuineness of all shipments, Bills of Lading, and inward remittances. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) automatically receives whistleblower/whisteblower reports under the Tax Information Exchange Notice, and cross-border cooperation with UK HMRC and EU MS CTC via the OECD MAP platform is mandatory, extending the clawback audit limitation period to 12 years for ventilator ventilators sales tied to government procurement of strategic stock supplies.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The GIP is fully convertible into GBP at par with no exchange-control restrictions on current or capital account transactions. Gibraltar abolished exchange controls in 1979 and maintains a liberalised capital account: non-resident partners may inject capital, repatriate profits, and liquidate partnership interests without prior central-bank approval. In practice, international settlements are executed in GBP, USD, or EUR because the GIP is not a freely traded offshore currency; GIP balances are held within the territory and are 100% sterling-backed. Profit remittance from the Exempted Limited Partnership is unrestricted, subject only to standard AML/CFT verification and the partnership's own tax-transparent status.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation exists in Gibraltar. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no prescribed foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, and no requirement that export receipts be credited to a Gibraltar account. Proceeds from ICU mechanical ventilator sales may be retained in the invoicing currency (typically GBP, USD, or EUR) in any licensed bank account worldwide; however, the GFSC's AML/CFT Handbook requires that export payments to the ELP be accompanied by underlying trade documentation for audit and sanctions-compliance purposes.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the integrated financial supervisor; HM Government of Gibraltar as the sole note-issuing authority for the Gibraltar pound (GIP) under the Currency Notes Act 1934; monetary policy anchored to the Bank of England (United Kingdom) via the statutory GIP/GBP parity peg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium viability with restrictive legal constraints. Gibraltar regulates Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Gibraltar E-Money Regulations; post-Brexit, UK and EEA EMIs no longer benefit from passporting rights into Gibraltar. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise or Revolut cannot serve as the primary settlement rail for this ELP because they lack a GFSC licence and cannot provide government-procurement LC facilitation. A locally licensed EMI may provide ancillary collection and supplier-disbursement accounts, but it cannot support UCP600 LC confirmation, documentary collections, or trade-finance limits. Therefore, a GFSC-licensed EMI is viable only for low-value operational float, not for the core treasury function.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol: non-resident partners inject capital via SWIFT MT103 wires denominated in GBP, USD, or EUR, using the ISO 20022 purpose code reflecting foreign direct investment into a partnership ('EQUI' for equity-like capital contributions or the BOP-equivalent direct-investment category). The bank matches each inbound wire against the ELP's statutory capital ledger, the registered partnership deed, and the filed Economic Substance Return. Verification requires a certified copy of the capital-call notice, partner resolution, and the escrow account mandate. For GIP-denominated injections the funds must first clear in GBP and be converted at par into GIP upon receipt at the Gibraltar account; no exchange-control approval is required. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium viability with restrictive legal constraints. Gibraltar regulates Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Gibraltar E-Money Regulations; post-Brexit, UK and EEA EMIs no longer benefit from passporting rights into Gibraltar. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise or Revolut cannot serve as the primary settlement rail for this ELP because they lack a GFSC licence and cannot provide government-procurement LC facilitation. A locally licensed EMI may provide ancillary collection and supplier-disbursement accounts, but it cannot support UCP600 LC confirmation, documentary collections, or trade-finance limits. Therefore, a GFSC-licensed EMI is viable only for low-value operational float, not for the core treasury function.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 10,000,000 annual consolidated group turnover and GIP 1,000,000 aggregate annual value of controlled transactions - the de minimis thresholds under the Gibraltar Income Tax (Transfer Pricing) Rules 2019. Where either threshold is exceeded, a contemporaneous Local File must be prepared and retained. A Master File is additionally required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding EUR 750,000,000, consistent with BEPS Action 13. The arm's-length principle applies to all controlled transactions irrespective of threshold.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar enacted the Economic Substance Regulations 2019 under the Income Tax Act and Companies Act. The designated relevant activities are banking, insurance, shipping, fund management, financing and leasing, headquarters, distribution and service centres, and intellectual-property holding. Manufacturing of turbine-driven critical-care invasive ventilators (ISIC 3250) is NOT a relevant activity. Nevertheless, the ELP must file an annual ESR return declaring non-relevance and maintain a physical registered office in Gibraltar. If the ELP additionally participates in any listed activity, for example providing financing to group procurement vehicles or carrying distribution of imported components, it must satisfy the core income-generating activity test locally: physical premises, full-time employees in Gibraltar, board meetings in Gibraltar, and adequate operational expenditure. For a non-resident ELP, failure to evidence these elements upon enquiry can trigger re-characterisation as resident, bringing all worldwide profits within the 12.5% CIT charge.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Income Tax Act 2010: tax-geared penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax assessed for deliberate concealment; daily default penalties of GIP 100 per day up to GIP 30,000; late-payment interest at 8% per annum; on conviction on indictment, an unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 5 years; on summary conviction, a fine up to GIP 10,000 and/or imprisonment up to 12 months.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any partner acting in management are personally and jointly liable for civil restitution of evaded tax and penalties once the corporate veil is pierced; courts may issue travel bans, worldwide freezing orders, and confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; individuals face director disqualification for up to 15 years under the Companies Act 2014.
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar ESR regime: fixed penalty of GIP 10,000 for a missing ESR return; GIP 100 per day for continued default; revocation of the No Tax Liability Certificate; retrospective residence-based reassessment at the 12.5% CIT rate with interest; strike-off of the ELP by the Registrar of Companies; contractual losses arising from exclusion from Government tender frameworks for medical devices.

Director personal liability: Gibraltarian courts will lift the partnership veil where the ELP is a mere façade; general and limited partners who exercise control are treated as shadow directors and may be ordered to contribute personally to unpaid third-party debts; travel bans and disqualification orders are routinely issued against nominee directors.
AML/CFT and Banking Integrity Violations

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar) and AML/CFT Regulations: on conviction on indictment, imprisonment up to 10 years and an unlimited fine for laundering; imprisonment up to 5 years for tipping off; regulatory financial penalties at the GFIU discretion linked to the pecuniary advantage obtained; loss or suspension of the EMI banking licence relationship; revocation of the statutory medical-device trading licence.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners, and compliance officers face personal criminal liability where wilful blindness is proven; courts may impose travel bans, order confiscation of personal assets connected to the proceeds stream, and permanently debar individuals from regulated financial activities in Gibraltar.
Transfer Pricing, False Records, and Document Destruction

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's-length price, producing additional CIT at the applicable 10%/12.5% band plus interest at 8% per annum; fixed record-keeping penalty of GIP 5,000 plus daily penalties of GIP 100 per day; tax-geared penalty of up to 100% for concealed adjustments; criminal prosecution for forgery and falsification of books under the Crimes Act 2011.

Director personal liability: The general partner who signs or causes the false accounts to be prepared is personally liable for fraudulent trading under the Companies Act 2014; courts can order personal contribution orders over assets, impose disqualification from management, and issue travel restrictions pending full civil recovery; deliberate destruction of records converts the default into tax-evasion category with the highest custodial exposure.
