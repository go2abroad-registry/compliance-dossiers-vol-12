E-government filing portal

Step 1 — Formation: Apply to the Registrar of Companies under the Exempted Limited Partnership Act 2014 (filing the limited partnership deed naming the general partner and the registered office) to obtain the statutory ELP registration number and certificate. Step 2 — Tax registration: Within 30 days of formation, file the prescribed partnership tax-registration application (Income Tax Office form) together with the ELP certificate, partnership deed, schedule of partners (name, address, tax residence and status as exempt/non-resident), and a declaration signed by the general partner; the Commissioner issues the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TIN) for the ELP. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — default tax-transparent treatment under section 18 (Partnerships) of the Income Tax Act 2010: the ELP is not chargeable to tax as an entity; each partner is personally assessable on its distributive share of partnership profits. Territorial Scope — Gibraltar taxes income arising in Gibraltar and foreign income received in Gibraltar; offshore VHP raw-cane-sugar arbitrage profits arising from FOB/CFR/CIF sales between third-country ports are outside the Gibraltar source charge for non-resident partners. Tax Exempt Zone — an ELP certified under the Exempted Limited Partnership Act 2014 is statutorily treated as not carrying on business in Gibraltar in respect of its offshore trade, so its non-Gibraltar trading income remains outside the charge without any entity election.

Available election forms

(1) Opaque election (Gibraltar 'check-the-box' analogue): the general partner may, by written notice to the Commissioner under section 18 of the Income Tax Act 2010, elect to tax the ELP as if it were a company; the election is irrevocable absent the Commissioner's consent and applies the 12.5% standard corporate rate (10% or 8% for regulated financial-services income where applicable). (2) ELP offshore-exemption election: application under the Exempted Limited Partnership Act 2014 for an exemption certificate confirming that offshore income is excluded from assessment. (3) Partner basis-of-assessment election between the actual-year and preceding-year basis. (4) Non-resident-partner territorial-status declaration excluding non-Gibraltar income from any remittance charge. (5) Hybrid-mismatch defensive election: filing under the Income Tax (Anti-Avoidance) Act 2011 to disclaim reverse-hybrid treatment and align the ELP's tax characterization with the residence-country classification of each partner. All elections are made in writing to the Commissioner; Gibraltar operates no automated online check-the-box portal.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full convertibility on both the capital account and the current account; its application of the repealed Exchange Control Act leaves no restriction on subscription of ELP capital, loan financing, trade advances, or profit and dividend remittances by non-resident partners. The practical hurdles to conversion are documentary rather than regulatory: clearing banks under correspondent relationships with US and EU banks will demand trade contracts, tax clearance certificates, and UBO attestations before routing lump-sum distributions. Since the entity is a pass-through ELP, outward remittance of trading profits enjoys no withholding tax, but the bank's Money Laundering Reporting Officer will impose a 'crystallised source-of-funds' threshold above GBP 100,000. VHP raw sugar settlements denominated in USD or alternative currencies can be converted freely to GBP/EUR without mandatory conversion to Gibraltar Pound.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts; proceeds from physical VHP raw cane sugar exports may be retained indefinitely in USD, GBP, EUR or any other admissible currency, in either domestic or offshore accounts. There is no deadline for repatriation under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, the Income Tax Act 2010, or GFSC guidance. Nevertheless, the UK-Retained EU Wire Transfer Regulation requires full payer/payee identification data on any MT103 or MT202 wire crossing the GI border, with value date records maintained for five years, and any receipt exceeding GBP/EUR 1,000 equivalent that lacks a stated trade or capital purpose code may be blocked by the receiving bank pending KYC clarification.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission is the sole prudential regulator for banking and money transmission services; the Bank of England, acting for the United Kingdom, is the de facto central monetary authority for Gibraltar without a separate local central bank; the Government of Gibraltar issues Gibraltar Pound notes under a statutory currency agreement at 1.000:1 parity with GBP sterling. FX administration is non-statutory, and no exchange-control ministry exercises prior approval over foreign currency transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM-LOW for this commodity treasury. Legally, Gibraltar-recognised Fintech EMIs and licensed UK electronic money institutions may hold a current account for the ELP and execute SWIFT wires; however, they cannot issue documentary letters of credit, discount trade bills, or provide revolving trade facilities, rendering them unsuitable as the primary clearing rail for ISIC 4631 vessel-based raw sugar purchases. Offshore digital EMIs without a Gibraltar or UK FCA passport are generally rejected by tier-1 GI banks as correspondent back-to-back counterparties, and most mainstream EMI onboarding logic will decline a non-UK-resident Cayman/Gibraltar ELP because natural-person beneficial owners of limited partners cannot be disclosed on the EMI's standard form. Use of Wise Business or Revolut Business is authorised only for ancillary disbursements such as surveyor fees, insurance premiums, and registry charges, capped at GBP 50,000 per transaction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Injection routing must follow the SWIFT MT103 standard from the limited partner's home banking jurisdiction, denominated in the same currency as the trade contract (USD for international VHP sugar contracts, GBP for UK-linked clearing), credited to the ELP's operational account with the Gibraltar tier-1 bank. Field 70 (Remittance Information) must recite 'INVESTOR CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION & TRADE ADVANCE FOR RAW VHP CANE SUGAR — ISIC 4631 — [ELP NAME]', and the ELP must upload its executed Capital Contribution Agreement, underlying trade contract page, and initial balance-sheet allocation via the bank's file-transfer portal before the first wire. The provider bank of the LP must CFX-match the beneficiary legal name exactly; any USD wire originating from a Brazilian, Thai, or Indian sugar-origin bank above USD 100,000 triggers pre-advice to the GI AML desk 48 hours before crediting. No GFSC or Gibraltar foreign exchange approval is required for the inbound flow. Where seller accepts a documentary collection, use DP/DA collection through the same clearing corridor. Legally, Gibraltar-recognised Fintech EMIs and licensed UK electronic money institutions may hold a current account for the ELP and execute SWIFT wires; however, they cannot issue documentary letters of credit, discount trade bills, or provide revolving trade facilities, rendering them unsuitable as the primary clearing rail for ISIC 4631 vessel-based raw sugar purchases. Offshore digital EMIs without a Gibraltar or UK FCA passport are generally rejected by tier-1 GI banks as correspondent back-to-back counterparties, and most mainstream EMI onboarding logic will decline a non-UK-resident Cayman/Gibraltar ELP because natural-person beneficial owners of limited partners cannot be disclosed on the EMI's standard form. Use of Wise Business or Revolut Business is authorised only for ancillary disbursements such as surveyor fees, insurance premiums, and registry charges, capped at GBP 50,000 per transaction.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% for corporate partners under Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010, s.20. The Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) is tax transparent; no income tax is assessed at partnership level. Each partner is assessed on its allocated share of chargeable income. Non-resident partners are chargeable only on Gibraltar-source income received in or remitted to Gibraltar.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividends or partnership distributions paid to residents or non-residents. The ELP is not a withholding agent; distributions to limited partners are paid gross and represent profits already assessed at the partner level.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000: annual aggregate cross-border related-party transactions above this threshold trigger mandatory Local File and Master File preparation aligned with OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines under Income Tax Act s.20B. Below the threshold, a concise contemporaneous arm's length evaluation must still be retained and produced on request.

Economic substance scope: The Income Tax (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019/2020 impose a substance test on entities carrying on relevant activities, including distribution and service centres. A trading ELP must demonstrate in Gibraltar: physical office premises, at least one full-time executive who directs chartering, hedging, and sugar sales from Gibraltar, board meetings where strategic decisions are taken, and adequate operating expenditure. Failure: financial penalty from GIP 20,000 to GIP 100,000; for high-risk jurisdiction categories, additional sanctions and possible criminal referral. A non-resident ELP with no physical activity in Gibraltar falls outside the test, but a nil-return claim invites a Commissioner's enquiry; contemporaneous evidence of offshore management is mandatory.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration of Foreign-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Unpaid tax, interest at 8% per annum, civil penalties up to 200% of the tax under Income Tax Act ss.147-150; criminal prosecution with unlimited fine and confiscation order equal to the value of profits hidden.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who sign inaccurate returns face personal tax liability where the veil is pierced, travel restriction orders, and imprisonment of up to 7 years for income tax fraud.
Shell Company and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Financial penalty of GIP 20,000 to GIP 100,000 under the Income Tax (Substance) Regulations; high-risk activity escalation; compulsory strike-off of the partnership and revocation of tax exemptions.

Director personal liability: Disqualification as a director for up to 15 years, asset freezing orders, and personal liability for the penalized entity's tax where the corporate veil is pierced for abuse of the transparency regime.
Trade-Based Money Laundering and AML Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 s.45: failure to report suspicious activity carries up to 5 years' imprisonment and an unlimited fine; confiscation orders cover the full value of laundered sugar sale proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors of the GP who consented or connived are criminally liable; travel bans are routinely imposed pending investigation; civil restitution may be ordered to victims under civil conspiracy principles.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment restoring profit to Gibraltar, additional tax at 12.5%, interest at 8% per annum, and penalties up to 100% of the tax avoided; disallowance of excess intercompany interest above the 30% EBITDA cap.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize false intercompany pricing are personally liable for the additional tax and costs; criminal sanctions apply as accessories when the mispricing is intentional.
