Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For an Exempted Limited Partnership acting as a Single Family Office (ISIC 6430), a virtual office is generally not accepted as the sole registered office. The ELP must maintain a physical registered office in Gibraltar, managed by a licensed registered agent or registered general partner, which must be a real, active location capable of receiving legal process and statutory notices. A serviced office or managed agent address is permissible where the registered agent maintains the physical premises and holds original books, records, and UBO registers. However, the GFSC Enhanced AML risk tier applicable to private investment holding companies means the registered office must demonstrate operational substance documentation, board or partnership meeting records, and an identifiable physical presence. A purely virtual mailbox with no staff or records management is not permissible and will trigger enhanced filing review.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, HM Government of Gibraltar; operating under the Income Tax Act 2010 and the Taxation (International Cooperation) Act; primary e-portal: Gibraltar Tax Online eServices at www.gibraltar.gov.gi and the Income Tax Office portal; customs and indirect tax function is handled by the Gibraltar Customs & Border Agency under the Customs (Duty and Regulations) Act and the Purchase Tax Act (Cap 171).

E-government filing portal

For a Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) used as a Single Family Office: (1) register the ELP deed and statement of purpose with the Registrar of Companies at Gibraltar Companies House under the Limited Partnerships Act; (2) the General Partner (GP) must then file the partnership registration and declaration of transparency with the Income Tax Office to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (GRN), typically using Form ELP-P1; (3) submit an annual Partnership Return (Form ELP-200) reporting allocable profits and the residence status of each partner; (4) the ELP must maintain a registered office and a Gibraltar-resident registered agent; non-resident general and limited partners are permitted with no minimum capital requirement; no financial services license is triggered purely for passive family investment holding activity; standard GRN issuance takes approximately 3 to 10 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the ELP is fiscally transparent for Gibraltar income tax purposes; no entity-level tax is imposed on the partnership; taxable profits are allocated to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement; non-resident partners are taxable in Gibraltar only on Gibraltar-source income, while an investment-holding SFO with underlying assets outside Gibraltar and no taxable local activities will normally have nil Gibraltar tax liability; territorial scope is recognised and the ELP must demonstrate sufficient economic substance to preserve non-residency of income.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box system, but the ELP filing an explicit transparency declaration achieves automatic pass-through classification; UK HMRC will generally respect the ELP as a transparent partnership under the UK partnership code, providing the partnership mismatch regime is not triggered; an election may be filed under the UK Hybrid and Other Mismatches Regulations (SI 2015/1637) to align the UK and Gibraltar classifications and prevent dual deduction or double non-taxation; individual family members may also elect for the Category 2 tax regime to take advantage of Gibraltar's remittance basis; the ELP itself cannot elect for corporate taxation except in the rare case of registration as a GBC under the Gibraltar Companies (Taxation) regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Purchase Tax (Cap 171) and Customs Import Duties; Gibraltar is not part of the EU VAT area and does not levy a general VAT or GST; the ELP's passive investment management and custody services are outside the scope of Purchase Tax, while only specified tangible goods (such as automotives, fuel, alcohol, tobacco and certain luxury items) are subject to Purchase Tax when entered for consumption in Gibraltar.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general economic turnover threshold applies to financial or holding services; importers, manufacturers and wholesalers of specified Purchase Tax goods must register with the Gibraltar Customs & Border Agency regardless of turnover (nil threshold). For the ELP's activities as a single family office, no Purchase Tax or customs registration is required unless it imports physical taxable goods into Gibraltar for local use; the practical service-related registration threshold for non-taxable financial services is therefore nil and effectively outside the indirect tax net.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar does not operate a VAT system; therefore zero-rating of exports is de facto achieved because exported goods are relieved from Purchase Tax and import duty when they are proven to be removed from Gibraltar. Financial and investment services supplied by the ELP to non-resident family entities, including cross-border securities custody, private banking wire transfers and asset management, are wholly outside the charge to Gibraltar Purchase Tax and are treated as nil-rated or not taxable for indirect tax purposes. To claim relief on any physical exported asset, the ELP must show that the goods left Gibraltar in an unaltered state within ninety days of supply, that there was no local consumption, and that the purchaser is outside Gibraltar; services must be rendered to a non-resident recipient and not enjoyed in Gibraltar.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD Form C88 / GIB-EXP1)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill (original negotiable document or electronic house bill endorsed to the non-resident buyer)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank confirmation showing export proceeds credited to a Gibraltar-designated account within the statutory 180-day window)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List (itemised HS-6 codes, stated Purchase Tax status and export valuation)
  • Certificate of Origin or declarant-certified EU/Gibraltar origin statement (only where preferential tariff treatment is claimed)

Gibraltar does not impose a real-time fiscal clearance, electronic invoicing clearance, or digital stamping mandate; there is no continuous transaction control system analogous to EU CTC frameworks. However, the Gibraltar Customs & Border Agency requires electronic submission of export and import declarations using the Single Administrative Document (SAD) through the Gibraltar electronic clearance system; licensed customs brokers use API-based batch submissions, but there is no government QR code or fiscal e-invoice authentication for ordinary commercial invoices; all export and re-export documentation must be retained electronically for at least six years.

Incorrect or fraudulent export claims expose the ELP to retrospective clawback of Purchase Tax or duty relief, plus civil penalties up to 150% of the tax at risk and forfeiture of goods under the Customs (Duty and Regulations) Act; deliberate evasion is a criminal offence under the Gibraltar Economic Crime and Corruption Act 2018 carrying imprisonment of up to ten years. Where export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day deadline without valid commercial justification, the Commissioner may disallow the zero-rating treatment and assess Purchase Tax on a taxable value including any mark-up. For income tax purposes, the six-year discovery period applies for reopening assessments, with compound interest and penalties up to 100% of the tax deficiency for deliberate concealment; additionally, the UK HMRC may apply the Diverted Profits Tax at 25% and the partnership hybrid mismatch rules if the ELP arrangement creates double non-taxation for a UK-connected family office.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom and Gibraltar maintain a fully liberalised capital and current account regime. There are no exchange controls under the Exchange Control Act 1947 (repealed 1979) and no Gibraltar equivalent. The Gibraltar pound is freely convertible into GBP at par, and the UK, Gibraltar, USD, and EUR float freely in the foreign exchange market. A Cayman Islands Exempted Limited Partnership structured as a single-family office faces no statutory restriction on inward capital subscription, dividend or capital distributions, interest remittance, or repatriation of disposal proceeds. The only frictions are bank-level AML/KYC checks, fiscal substance requirements for Gibraltar tax residency, and the requirement to report certain cross-border flows to the Bank of England's International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or centralised holding period applies to export receipts or any other foreign currency proceeds in either the United Kingdom or Gibraltar. A Cayman ELP undertaking private investment holding may retain earnings in any eligible currency without conversion obligation. The sole regulatory duty is informational: inward or outward direct investment flows exceeding GBP 5,000 in a single transaction must be reported to the Bank of England through the ITRS, generally by the resident bank on the customer's behalf, with the relevant BPM6 purpose code. No surrender, sale, or approval is required.

Central bank supervision: Monetary sovereignty is vested in the Bank of England and HM Treasury of the United Kingdom; the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1935 and is pegged at par to GBP. Banking supervision and financial services authorisation in Gibraltar are exercised by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019. The UK Financial Conduct Authority and Prudential Regulation Authority continue to regulate UK-based counterparties and clearing banks for cross-border sterling flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary capital escrow and securities custody rails; Medium for low-value operational disbursement and FX payments. Gibraltar has a domestic Electronic Money Institution (EMI) licence under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2019, and UK-authorised EMIs may operate on a cross-border services basis into Gibraltar under the UK temporary permissions regime. Nonetheless, EMIs are not permitted to conduct securities custody, private banking, or lending activity, and their payment accounts generally lack the settlement depth and correspondent-bank access needed for family-office grade wires. Correspondent banks frequently reject or delay transactions where a Cayman-registered ELP is the ultimate beneficiary of an EMI account. An EMI is therefore suitable only as a supplementary treasury tool, never as the primary clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is injected from the family principals' private banks via SWIFT MT103 messages into the ELP's designated escrow or operational account at its GFSC-licensed Gibraltar bank. No prior exchange-control approval is required. The sending bank must populate Field 50K with the remitting partner's name exactly as held in KYC records and Field 59 with the registered ELP name; the purpose code should follow BPM6 classification using 'EQU' for equity-type capital contribution or 'INV' for investment in a non-resident enterprise. The Gibraltar receiving bank will require a capital call notice, board resolution of the general partner, and the partnership agreement to distinguish contributed capital from shareholder loans. Any transaction exceeding GBP 5,000 equivalent is reportable to the Bank of England under the ITRS, and the bank may also require a third-party authorisation letter if the remitting and receiving party names differ from the partnership register.Trade settlement is executed through SWIFT MT103 for low-value direct payments, MT202 for interbank cover to correspondent banks, and MT700 or MT707 for commercial letters of credit issued or amended by the GFSC-licensed bank. High-value sterling payments clear over CHAPS, euro payments over TARGET2, and US dollar payments over CHIPS or Fedwire via a New York correspondent. For securities custody transactions, the custodian bank receives DVP settlement instructions on SWIFT MT541/MT543 and disburses funds only against simultaneous delivery of securities. Every outgoing payment carries a bank-assigned purpose code, such as 'SECPUR' for securities purchase, 'INTR' for intragroup transfer, or 'CAPRET' for capital return, ensuring compliance with ITRS reporting, GFSC recordkeeping, and the family office's Investment Policy Statement.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX forwards (1-month to 24-month tenors) against GBP, USD, EUR, and CHFNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for restricted or emerging-market currencies, such as INR, KRW, and BRL, cash-settled in GBP or USDCross-currency swaps (fixed-for-fixed and floating-for-fixed) for long-term balance-sheet hedging of multi-currency family assetsFX swaps for short-term liquidity management between operating and custody accounts, consistently settled through CLS or bilateral clearing

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% standard corporate rate; Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) is tax-transparent unless an election is made to be taxed as a company, in which case the 12.5% rate applies to taxable profits

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (Gibraltar does not levy withholding tax on dividends paid by an ELP or a company, regardless of the recipient’s residence)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File required if the Gibraltar entity's turnover exceeds GBP 10 million and related-party transactions exceed GBP 1 million per category; smaller entities must demonstrate arm’s length pricing in their tax records even if no formal file is required

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) introduced via the Taxes (Economic Substance) Regulations 2019 apply to entities conducting relevant activities; a single family office engaged primarily in passive equity investment is categorised as a 'pure equity holding entity' and may be exempt from substance requirements if it has no activities other than holding shares and receiving dividends; however, if the SFO provides any management or advisory services, it must demonstrate economic substance in Gibraltar: (i) active and board management in Gibraltar, (ii) adequate physical presence, and (iii) qualified staff proportionately engaged; annual ESR return required even for exempt entities, with penalty for non-compliance: GBP 50,000 plus GBP 500 per day for continued failure, and possible striking-off for persistent non-compliance

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 200% of the understated tax; criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act and the Criminal Code (Gibraltar) with fines up to GBP 250,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years

Director personal liability: Directors or general partners can be personally liable for penalties if they were knowingly involved in the evasion; in addition, a travel ban may be imposed and assets seized under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015
Shell Company / Economic Substance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of GBP 50,000 and daily penalty of GBP 500 per day (capped at GBP 300,000); the Registrar may strike off the ELP from the Register if non-compliance continues for 6 months; forfeiture of any tax benefits claimed

Director personal liability: Directors (or general partners) are jointly and severally liable for the penalties if the entity fails to pay; the Court may issue a disqualification order against directors for up to 15 years and enforce personal catch-ups of unpaid corporate taxes
Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: Fines imposed by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, up to GBP 200,000 per breach and imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering; also criminal penalties under the Financial Services (Investment and Fiduciary Services) Act 2018

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners who fail to comply with AML obligations can be held criminally and civilly liable; travel bans and freezing orders may be imposed; imprisonment up to 2 years for non-disclosure of suspicious activity
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Adjustment by the Income Tax Officer to taxable profits, plus interest and penalties of up to 100% of the additional tax; under the Transfer Pricing Rules (Gibraltar), the taxpayer must prepare adequate documentation; failure to produce documentation on demand triggers a fixed penalty of GBP 10,000 and additional penalties of up to GBP 50,000

Director personal liability: Directors who sign unaudited tax returns containing misleading transfer pricing could face personal liability for penalties if they acted negligently or with intent; in egregious cases, the general partner or director may be subject to recovery of unpaid corporate taxes and could face a criminal charge of fraudulent evasion of tax
