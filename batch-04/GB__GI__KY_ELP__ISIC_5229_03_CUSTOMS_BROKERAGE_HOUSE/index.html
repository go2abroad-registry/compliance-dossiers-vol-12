Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

H.M. Government of Gibraltar — Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax) for direct taxation; H.M. Customs of Gibraltar (Customs and Excise Department) for indirect taxation and customs administration. Primary portals: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi (Income Tax e-filing via the iTax portal; customs declarations lodged through the UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS) as adopted by Gibraltar).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) under the Exempted Limited Partnerships Act 2019 at the Gibraltar Companies Registry, obtaining the ELP registration certificate and registered number. Step 2: Register the ELP for direct tax with the Income Tax Office via Form Reg-01 (Application for Tax Registration) within 90 days of formation; as the ELP is fiscally transparent, it is registered as a notional partnership unit and each partner obtains or is covered by a Gibraltar Taxpayer Identification Number (GTIN). Step 3: Apply to H.M. Customs of Gibraltar for a Gibraltar Economic Operator Registration (GEOR) number and a Customs Brokerage Licence under the Customs and Excise Management Ordinance, submitting the ELP statement of place of effective management and the licensed general partner's details. Step 4: For movement of goods under the Common Transit Convention, obtain an NCTS transit guarantee and, where required, a UK-issued EORI number (GB/EORI) through the HMRC customs interface.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (fiscally transparent for Gibraltar income tax; no entity-level Gibraltar corporation tax where the ELP is engaged in non-Gibraltar-sourced customs brokerage activity; non-resident partners are not chargeable on foreign-source shares under the territorial regime of the Income Tax Act 2010).

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists. Statutory transparency under s.7 of the Income Tax Act 2010 is automatic. Available administrative and statutory elections: (i) election to exclude separate legal personality under the Exempted Limited Partnerships Act 2019, preserving look-through classification; (ii) application to the Commissioner of Income Tax for a Section 7 Confirmatory Ruling certifying that the ELP's receipts from offshore clients are not Gibraltar-source trade income; (iii) election under the Income Tax (Partnership) Regulations for the ELP to adopt a bespoke accounting period and apportion profits among non-resident partners on a taxable-amount certificate basis; (iv) for UK-resident corporate partners, election under the UK Income Tax (Trading and Other Income) Act 2005 s.863D to be assessed on the arising basis on ELP profit shares; (v) hybrid mismatch alignment is achieved through Income Tax Office rulings rather than a domestic check-the-box regime, with OECD BEPS Action 2 responses documented as deduction/credit alignment elections subject to the Commissioner's anti-arbitrage guidance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Import and Consumption Duties Regime (no VAT/GST/Sales Tax applies in Gibraltar; customs and excise duties are charged at importation, and re-export or transit movements are executed under duty suspension).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — there is no Gibraltar VAT and therefore no turnover-based VAT registration threshold. Any economic operator importing, exporting, or moving bonded goods through Gibraltar must be registered with H.M. Customs regardless of annual turnover or transaction value. Duties are assessed in Gibraltar pounds (GIP) according to the Gibraltar Customs Tariff, which is aligned with the UK Global Tariff; a separate ad valorem or fixed excise duty applies to alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oils, and certain environmental goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

As Gibraltar imposes no VAT, zero-rating is not the operative legal concept. Export relief is instead granted as 'export/re-export outside the course of a consumption entry' under the Customs Duties Ordinance, provided: (i) goods are physically removed from Gibraltar's customs territory to a foreign destination within 180 days of importation or removal from a customs warehouse; (ii) the ELP files a complete electronic Export/Re-Export Declaration; (iii) sale consideration is evidenced by a bank remittance certificate or inward credit advice; (iv) the underlying export transaction is commercially genuine, supported by bills of lading and, in indirect-representative engagements, a notarised customs power of attorney and an indemnity bond. Offshore-bound services rendered by the customs brokerage house are non-Gibraltar-source income to the extent the territorial deeming provision in s.5(2)(c) of the Income Tax Act 2010 is displaced by contemporaneous substance and transfer-pricing documentation.

  • Gibraltar Export/Re-Export Declaration (electronic CDS entry obligatory)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing lawful physical export from Gibraltar
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List signed by the licensed customs broker
  • Inward/Outward Remittance Certificate or Bank Credit Statement evidencing collection of the export consideration
  • Customs Agent Power of Attorney and endorsed ELP Customs Brokerage Licence

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal e-invoicing, clearance stamping, or API-based digital stamping for domestic B2B invoices. For customs purposes, all import, export, and transit declarations must be lodged electronically through the Gibraltar Customs Declaration System operating on the UK CDS platform (with transitional CHIEF fallback), integrated with the New Computerised Transit System (NCTS) for T1/T2 movements. Licensed customs brokers must utilise the Automated Broker Interface (CDS API/Web Services) and the Trader's Digital Account for duty settlement. Excise goods are separately controlled through the Excise Movement and Control System (EMCS) and require digitally stamped banderoles under the Excise Duties (Stamp-Marking) Regulations; no analogous digital stamping applies to ordinary brokerage services.

H.M. Customs may conduct post-clearance audits within three years of release, extended to six years in cases of fraud or dishonest evasion. Where export relief is abused through phantom exports, circular cargo, overvaluation, or failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day deadline, the applicable consequences include: forfeiture of goods and, in aggravated cases, of the conveying vessel; a penalty of up to three times the value of the goods under the Customs and Excise Management Ordinance; statutory interest on the unpaid import duty; revocation of the customs broker's licence; and criminal liability carrying imprisonment of up to seven years. The Income Tax Office may additionally recharacterise un-repatriated proceeds as Gibraltar-source brokerage commission, re-assess all ELP partners, and impose a penalty of up to 100% of the tax undercharged under s.152Y of the Income Tax Act 2010, together with publication in the Tax Defaulters' Gazette. Duty clawback may be enforced jointly and severally against the ELP and its licensed general partner through the posted bond security.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at par to Sterling and is fully convertible. Gibraltar has no exchange control regulations; the UK's Exchange Control Act 1947 was effectively repealed and Gibraltar permits unrestricted current and capital account transactions. Profit remittance and capital repatriation are free of official barriers, subject only to anti-money laundering (AML) reporting thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender or conversion of export proceeds under Gibraltar/UK law. Export receivables may be held in any freely convertible currency and are not subject to repatriation deadlines or forced conversion to GIP or GBP.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority) and Government of Gibraltar (currency issuance under Gibraltarian Currency Notes Act); the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) serves as prudential regulator for banks and payment services.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High. Licensed EMIs in the EEA (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise, Airwallex) will accept a Gibraltar ELP under a remote onboarding flow, provided the entity has a registered office and a Gibraltar resident agent. However, due to the Medium-Risk Enhanced AML classification of customs brokerage, EMIs may impose restrictive transaction ceilings, require pre-funded client accounts, and mandate a physical meeting at the EMI's correspondent banking partner for high-volume settlement activity. Legal constraint: EMIs cannot provide licensed trust or escrow services under Gibraltar law, but can clear GBP/EUR/USD via local correspondent banking.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Back-to-back receipt/disbursement flows through offshore escrow accounts perceived as money-value transmission without an electronic money institution license

Root cause: Back-to-back receipt/disbursement flows through offshore escrow accounts perceived as money-value transmission without an electronic money institution license

Defense evidence: Evidence that the ELP operates solely as an agent for principal trade clients via written mandates, formal invoicing with a clear service fee, copies of customs declarations (Entry Summary Declaration ENS / Exit Summary Declaration EXS) matching payables/receivables, and confirmation that no customer funds are held in the treasury account prior to instruction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from non-resident partners must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 to the ELP's designated operational capital account with a Gibraltar clearing bank. The referencing code should be 'GBP/GIP' with purpose code 'GIB S.P. 20' (or 'CIP' for capital investment) and must be accompanied by a board resolution of the general partner approving the capital call. The beneficiary must be a licensed bank under GFSC AML supervision and the partner's originating bank should provide a fully verified sender/receiver chain (Ordering Customer included) to ensure a clean audit trail.For customs brokerage trade settlements, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for standard wire transfers to EU and UK clearing houses, plus commercial letters of credit (Documentary Credit) where larger bonded customs or excise payments exceed GBP 500,000. For local GIP/GBP obligations, CHAPS expedited same-day clearing is used; within the EU/EEA, SEPA (both SCT and INST for EUR) provides low-cost 10-second clearing for receivables from EU customs brokers. All outgoing disbursements are reconciled against an export/import customs declaration database and are subject to enhanced counterparty screening against OFAC and EU sanctions lists.Forward FX contracts (up to 12 months tenor) with licensed Gibraltar/UK banks for converting EUR/USD receipts into GIP or GBP.Cross-currency swaps, permitted for matched trade finance obligations and only on delivery of a written underlying trade contract.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) available for exotic currency exposures (e.g., USD to a GIP stable value proxy), but only for non-deliverable currencies not acceptable for physical settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 10,000,000 consolidated group revenue triggers Country-by-Country Reporting; Local File and Master File are mandatory when aggregate controlled cross-border transactions exceed GIP 2,000,000, or when any single controlled transaction exceeds GIP 500,000.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar economic substance requirements apply to entities conducting prescribed business activities. Customs brokerage and bonded transit management are treated as service-centre or headquarters activities where core income-generating activities must occur in Gibraltar: licensed premises, adequate full-time employees, operating expenditure proportionate to revenue, partnership management meetings in Gibraltar, and substance not outsourced to a single foreign affiliate. Failure to demonstrate economic substance may cause reassessment of profits to the foreign jurisdiction, denial of Gibraltar tax residence, and imposition of ESR penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Filing

Statutory sanction: Unlimited fine, repayment of underpaid tax plus 8% interest, imprisonment up to 7 years, forfeiture of assets, and public disclosure of the penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be personally assessed for 100% of the evaded tax through piercing of the corporate veil; travel bans, asset freezing orders, and disqualification for up to 15 years may be imposed.
Shell Company and Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax residence, reassessment at foreign-source rates, ESR fine up to GIP 500,000, and cancellation of the customs broker licence.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors of corporate general partners face personal civil restitution, disqualification, and a 5-year re-entry ban to Gibraltar.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 5% of the affected transaction value plus a fixed penalty of GIP 100,000; transfer pricing adjustments may apply with back interest for six years.

Director personal liability: A director signing a false transfer pricing declaration is personally liable for 100% of the resulting tax deficiency and may be disqualified from managing a Gibraltar customs broker for 10 years.
AML and CFTPayment Rails Breach

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and Terrorist Financing Act 2011 penalties: imprisonment up to 14 years, confiscation of proceeds, revocation of any financial services permission, and immediate suspension of the customs broker licence.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners, and the Money Laundering Reporting Officer are criminally liable where suspicion was concealed; personal asset freeze, travel ban, and extradition may follow.
Unauthorised Customs Brokerage and Licence Violation

Statutory sanction: Fine up to GIP 250,000, closure of the ELP trading account, forfeiture of all commission derived from unlicensed activities, and suspension of the brokerage licence.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are jointly and severally liable for all duties collected and customs penalties incurred; repeated breach attracts an automatic 10-year disqualification from regulated customs activities.
