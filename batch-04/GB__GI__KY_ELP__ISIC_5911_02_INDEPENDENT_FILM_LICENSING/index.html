Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), Ministry of Finance; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation. Customs and indirect taxation is administered separately by HM Customs Gibraltar (Customs Duty Division), which operates jointly with the UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS) infrastructure.

E-government filing portal

The ELP must obtain a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) by submitting the prescribed partnership registration form (available on the Income Tax Office portal) within 30 days of formation. Required attachments: certified copy of the Limited Partnership Agreement / Exempted Limited Partnership deed; full register of general and limited partners (name, address, profit-share, nationality); and contact details for the Gibraltar resident agent or tax representative. Non-resident partners may appoint a Gibraltar resident agent for service of process. The Income Tax Office issues the TIN within approximately 5–10 business days and may issue a written ruling confirming pass-through treatment and the territorial scope of taxation applicable to the ELP.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope: the ELP itself is tax-transparent and no entity-level tax is charged; each partner is assessed in their own name on profits 'accruing in or derived from Gibraltar' and on foreign-source income received in or remitted to Gibraltar. Pure foreign-source film licensing royalties that are neither accrued in Gibraltar nor received/remitted into Gibraltar fall outside the charge to Gibraltar income tax. Non-resident partners acting through no Gibraltar permanent establishment are not subject to Gibraltar tax on such royalties.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Applicable elections and rulings include: (a) an application to the Commissioner of Income Tax for binding confirmation of partnership tax transparency under the Income Tax Act; (b) a territorial-source immunity ruling for film royalty income deriving from foreign distribution territories; (c) election for the general partner to act as the representative partner responsible for filing and payment on behalf of the ELP; and (d) compliance and disclosure obligations under the UK Hybrid Mismatch Arrangements (Part 6A TIOPA 2010) for UK corporate investors holding an interest in the ELP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar has no value added tax (VAT), goods and services tax (GST), or sales and use tax. Indirect taxation is limited to customs and excise duties on physical imports under the transitional EU Common Customs Tariff regime. Cross-border film licensing royalties, copyright assignment proceeds, and offshore services fall wholly outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST and therefore no turnover threshold for indirect tax registration exists. Entities importing physical goods into Gibraltar must register with HM Customs Gibraltar for an EORI (Economic Operator Registration and Identification) number before filing customs declarations; this is a fixed registration, not turnover-based.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating is inapplicable because Gibraltar has no VAT. For physical exports of film media (e.g., archival masters, merchandise), customs duty suspension applies when goods leave the Gibraltar customs territory under a valid export declaration; no partial exemption or input-tax refund mechanism exists. For intellectual property licensing, the operative relief is income-tax territorial exclusion: the ELP must ensure the licensing rights are exploited exclusively outside Gibraltar, contracts are negotiated/managed by non-Gibraltar partners (or a permitted central administration), and royalty receipts are not received into or remitted to Gibraltar, or, alternatively, rely on a territory-specific exemption ruling from the Commissioner.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document via CDS) — required only for physical film-media or merchandise shipments; not applicable to pure IP licensing.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement — required only for physical media shipments; digital licensing deliveries must instead retain authenticated digital delivery manifests and sales reports.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice) evidencing full gross royalty receipt, supported by foreign withholding tax certificates where the licensee's jurisdiction deducted source tax.

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal e-invoicing, API-based tax clearance, or digital tax stamping. Import and export declarations are made electronically through the UK-Gibraltar CDS/CHIEF customs platform using the Single Administrative Document; no continuous transaction controls or government-approved invoicing software are required for film licensing activities.

Gibraltar has no VAT refund clawback mechanism; penalties instead arise under the Income Tax Act. Fraudulent or careless claims to territorial exclusion or exemption trigger: reassessment of undercharged tax; statutory interest; civil penalties of up to 100% of the tax undercharged for careless returns and up to 200% for deliberate concealment; and criminal fines or imprisonment upon conviction. The Commissioner may reopen assessments for up to 6 years in ordinary cases, 12 years for negligent non-disclosure, and 21 years for fraud or offshore concealment. Where royalties initially treated as non-remitted foreign-source income are subsequently traced into Gibraltar, retrospective taxation applies to the earlier years together with penalties.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Both capital and current account transactions are fully convertible. The United Kingdom's Exchange Control Act 1947 was repealed by the Exchange Control (Repeal) Act 1979, and its territorial application to Gibraltar was abolished concurrently; Gibraltar today maintains no exchange-control legislation. GIP is exchangeable at par with GBP, and USD and EUR may be held freely without restriction. Profit remittance and royalty outflows from an Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) to non-resident partners are unrestricted, subject only to the GFSC-administered anti-money-laundering framework (including the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, record-keeping and suspicious activity reporting) and to any applicable UK financial sanctions extended to Gibraltar. No balance-of-payments approval or conversion permit is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation applies to Gibraltar-resident ELPs. Export proceeds, including foreign-currency film-licensing royalties received from USD or EUR licensees, may be retained in any currency indefinitely; there is no prescribed repatriation timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio (0% surrender requirement). The licensed bank applies its internal treasury policy on netting and currency position-keeping, and the ELP may freely instruct settlement currency without GFSC intervention.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no independent central bank. The Government of Gibraltar - Ministry of Finance (Note Issue Department) issues Gibraltar Pound (GIP) banknotes at a fixed 1:1 peg with GBP, and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) exercises prudential regulation over banks and payment institutions. Foreign-exchange control authority effectively rests with HM Treasury (United Kingdom) only insofar as UK sanctions and financial restrictions are extended to Gibraltar by Order in Council; no Gibraltar-specific exchange-control licence or conversion permit exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: HIGH for UK-licensed EMIs passported into Gibraltar under reciprocal UK-Gibraltar financial-services arrangements; LOW for unlicensed offshore digital EMIs. Feasibility is constrained post-Brexit because EEA EMIs lost automatic passporting into Gibraltar. A Gibraltar-licensed or UK-licensed EMI may operate an e-money wallet for operating float and GBP Faster Payments transit, but EMIs generally cannot issue documentary letters of credit or hold escrow in the name of a distribution counterparty. EMI rails are therefore supplemental; the primary treasury relationship must remain with a full authorised bank such as NatWest International, GIBank or J. Safra Sarasin.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route partner contributions as urgent bank-to-bank wires: SWIFT MT103, or UK Faster Payments/CHAPS in GBP, from the partner's own account to the ELP's designated capital-escrow sub-ledger. Remittance narrative must state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION - [ELP FULL NAME] - PARTNER [NAME] - ELP AGREEMENT [DATE]' and, for EUR SEPA transfers, carry the standard payment-purpose category for capital transfer or, for subsequent income flows, royalty and licence fees. The receiving bank will require a signed capital call, the register of limited partners entry, and a source-of-funds declaration for any wire above GBP/USD/EUR 100,000. No central-bank approval, surrender or foreign-investment notification is required in Gibraltar; the bank records the contribution as partnership equity and will not impose GIP conversion on multi-currency balances.Use SWIFT MT103 for direct payments to licensors, collection agents, dubbing/post-production suppliers and profit participants. For distribution minimum guarantees or milestone-based licensing deals, established clearing rails include: (a) irrevocable documentary Letters of Credit (UCP 600) opened by the licensee's bank and advised or confirmed via MT700/MT710, payable against presenter's delivery certificate, chain-of-title opinion, invoice and inspection certificate; (b) standby Letters of Credit (ISP98) as performance security for licence advance recoupment; and (c) MT202 cover payments for nostro settlement in USD/EUR. GBP disbursements may also use CHAPS/Faster Payments. All outbound wires should cite the invoice or contract reference and, where distribution counterparties reside in treaty countries, the applicable withholding-tax treaty basis. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File documentation is required where aggregate transactions with connected persons exceed GIP 1,000,000 in a tax year. A Master File is required where the multinational enterprise group's consolidated turnover exceeds GIP 1,000,000,000 (approximately EUR 750,000,000).

Economic substance scope: Applicable. Gibraltar's Economic Substance Regulations 2019, as amended, apply to all resident entities including ELPs carrying on geographically mobile activities. Independent film licensing and copyright distribution may qualify as 'intangible property activity' or 'distribution and service centre' activity. The ELP must demonstrate (i) effective management and control in Gibraltar, (ii) an adequate number of qualified employees and physical premises in Gibraltar, (iii) core income-generating activities performed in Gibraltar, and (iv) substantive operating expenditure proportionate to the income. Non-compliance exposes the ELP to financial penalties up to GIP 250,000, return amendments, and potential strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: On conviction on indictment: imprisonment up to seven years and/or an unlimited fine; on summary conviction: imprisonment up to two years and/or a fine up to £100,000. The Commissioner may add a tax penalty of up to 100% of the underpaid tax, plus interest and late payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: A general partner or director of a corporate partner who knowingly authorises the false return is jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the unpaid tax, the evasion penalty, and any confiscation order. Travel bans and imprisonment up to seven years may be imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Penalty under the Economic Substance Regulations of up to GIP 250,000; the Commissioner may set aside treaty benefits, disallow TP documentation relief, strike off the ELP, and issue a formal adverse determination.

Director personal liability: General partners are personally liable for unpaid penalties if the ELP fails substance requirements. Directors of a corporate general partner may be disqualified, subjected to civil restitution claims, and, if fraudulent concealment is proved, subjected to travel bans and imprisonment.
Anti-Money Laundering / Counter-Terrorist Financing Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and Financial Services Act 2018: civil penalties up to £125,000 per breach; criminal fines on indictment are unlimited; imprisonment up to two years; regulatory suspension or cancellation of the registered agent's licence.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and designated officers who consent to or connive in money-laundering conduct are criminally liable. Personal confiscation orders may be made, travel bans may be imposed, and civil restitution for losses caused by the breach may be ordered.
Misdeclaration of Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Under the Beneficial Ownership Regulations and the Exempted Partnerships Act: fine of up to £5,000 for each default plus a continuing daily penalty of £500; the register may be flagged for criminal investigation and the ELP may be struck off.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any director of a corporate partner that serves as general partner are liable for such penalties. Deliberate concealment is a criminal offence carrying imprisonment up to two years, in addition to civil restitution and travel-ban measures.
