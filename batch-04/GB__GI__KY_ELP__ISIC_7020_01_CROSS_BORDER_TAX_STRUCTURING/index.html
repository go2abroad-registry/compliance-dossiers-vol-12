Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar - Income Tax Office, Gibraltar Taxation Directorate; official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership ('ELP') must complete Parts 1 and 2 of the relevant Income Tax Act registration forms, file the partnership constituent deed and partner details with the Income Tax Office, and obtain a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN). The Registrar of Companies issues the company/partnership registration number as a prerequisite. The TIN is issued for the partnership itself for administrative filing; however, taxable profits are assessed in the hands of the partners.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible: the ELP is fiscally transparent by default; Gibraltar-source income flows through to partners. Non-resident partners with no Gibraltar-source income are generally outside Gibraltar income tax. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the ELP is fiscally transparent by default; Gibraltar-source income flows through to partners. Non-resident partners with no Gibraltar-source income are generally outside Gibraltar income tax. The ELP itself is not a separate taxable person, but it may be subject to specific filing duties and annual return obligations.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a check-the-box election. Partnerships are automatically pass-through unless specifically converted to a taxable corporate vehicle. Certain hybrid entity elections are constrained by domestic law and the OECD/G20 BEPS recommendations regarding hybrid mismatch arrangements; no elective tax transparency regime exists for corporate entities, and no hybrid transfer pricing election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar does not levy VAT, GST, or a general sales and use tax. Importations may attract customs duties under the Gibraltar Customs Duties legislation, and certain selected services or goods may be subject to local excise-like taxes.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - there is no VAT or GST registration threshold. Customs duty registration is mandatory for any person importing or exporting goods under the Customs (Gibraltar) legislation, subject to specific exemptions for low-value consignments.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as Gibraltar has no VAT system. Exports of goods from Gibraltar are outside the scope of any Gibraltar VAT or sales tax. For customs purposes, zero-rating does not exist; customs duties may be relieved if goods are exported from Gibraltar under customs warehousing or inward processing procedures, subject to statutory conditions.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document - SAD)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Gibraltar
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer evidence where proceeds are received (where required for customs or anti-money-laundering verification)

No real-time e-invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate exists in respect of indirect taxation. Commercial and electronic invoicing are permitted, but the Gibraltar Customs and Border Agency may require electronic submission of export/import declarations through the Customs Handling of Import and Export Freight (CHIEF) system or successor systems for customs clearance; no digital stamping or continuous transaction controls are imposed.

Although Gibraltar has no export VAT-zero-rating regime, customs misdeclaration and attempted evasion of customs duties on export goods may attract penalties up to 100% of the value of the goods, forfeiture of goods, and criminal prosecution. Any fraudulent claim for a customs relief or duty drawback may be retroactively withdrawn by the Gibraltar Customs and Border Agency and additional penalties may be imposed. For income tax purposes, a non-resident partner's claim that Gibraltar-source income is not taxable is scrutinised against place-of-management and source rules; misdeclaration may result in assessments, interest, and penalties up to 150% of the tax under-assessed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The GIP is fully convertible at par with GBP, and neither the UK nor Gibraltar maintains exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. A pass-through Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) may invoice and hold foreign currency and remit profits to non-resident partners without statutory conversion or withholding hurdles. Practical impediments are confined to bank-side enhanced due diligence, CRS/FATCA reporting, and documentary proof of economic substance and source of wealth.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. There is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export or service fee receipts in Gibraltar or the UK. Advisory fee income under ISIC 7020 may be retained in the invoicing currency indefinitely; repatriation timing is a commercial decision driven by cash flow and hedge requirements.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (UK monetary authority); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as prudential and AML/CFT supervisor; Government of Gibraltar as issuing authority for the Gibraltar pound (GIP) under the Currency Notes Act 1934, with GIP pegged at par to GBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: High (conditional). UK- or Gibraltar-authorised electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) provide viable multi-currency virtual account rails for a low-risk advisory ELP, provided the EMI holds authorisation under the UK Electronic Money Regulations 2011 or the Gibraltar Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act and the ELP's beneficial owners complete video KYC. Legal constraint: third-country EMIs without GFSC or UK FCA authorisation cannot lawfully hold GIP/GBP client balances, and receiving banks may reject payments originating from Cayman-domiciled partnership structures. Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Newly formed ELP with no operating history and no audited financial statements

Root cause: Newly formed ELP with no operating history and no audited financial statements

Defense evidence: Notarised partnership agreement, Gibraltar registration certificate, detailed business plan, initial capital source documentation, introducer letter from a regulated UK or Gibraltar accountant, and a 12-month transactional forecast.
Pass-through structure involving Cayman (KY_ELP) or other offshore tax-haven domiciles

Root cause: Pass-through structure involving Cayman (KY_ELP) or other offshore tax-haven domiciles

Defense evidence: Economic substance assessment prepared under the Gibraltar Economic Substance Act 2018, updated CRS/FATCA self-certifications, tax residency confirmation, organisational chart evidencing real commercial activity in Gibraltar, and transfer-pricing documentation.
High-velocity cross-border incoming payments from unrelated counterparties

Root cause: High-velocity cross-border incoming payments from unrelated counterparties

Defense evidence: Signed advisory contracts, issued invoices, deliverables evidence (reports, engagement emails), professional bank references for the ELP's partners, and sanctions/PEP screening clearance records.
Nominee general partner or opaque beneficial ownership structure

Root cause: Nominee general partner or opaque beneficial ownership structure

Defense evidence: Registered agent confirmation letter, UBO declaration schedule, proof of identity of all partners holding 25% or more beneficial interest, independent source-of-wealth report, and express disclosure that the nominee arrangement is approved by the GFSC-licensed registered agent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections must be transmitted by SWIFT MT103 using the receiving bank's standing settlement instructions (SSI). The field 70 narrative must state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION – [ELP NAME]'. The remitter must append a capital call notice, executed partner resolution, source-of-funds evidence, and valid FATCA/GIIN and CRS status certifications. For injections from a Cayman parent, a legal opinion confirming the ELP's valid capacity and good standing should accompany the transfer. Amounts exceeding GBP 10,000 trigger automated sanctions and enhanced due diligence screening in the UK clearing chain; routing via a UK clearing anchor bank minimises delay, freezing, or return risk.Clearing rails for fee settlements and trade disbursements are: SWIFT MT103 through SWIFT GPI for real-time tracking; SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant for EUR payments to European counterparties; UK Faster Payments for GBP payments below GBP 1 million; CHAPS for GBP payments above GBP 1 million; and SWIFT MT202/COVER for high-value non-urgent interbank transmissions. Commercial letters of credit are not suitable for pure advisory services under ISIC 7020; for milestone-based tax structuring contracts, a standby letter of credit or bank guarantee may support performance obligations. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% under Gibraltar domestic law; no withholding tax is imposed on distributions paid by a Gibraltar-included entity. For a pass-through ELP, profit remittances to partners are treated as distributive shares, not dividends, and carry no Gibraltar withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed on or before 30 November following the end of the chargeable accounting period; for a standard calendar-year ELP, the deadline is 30 November of the following tax year.

Box 6 Taxable profits, loss relief claimed, and distributable partnership share allocations. Because the ELP is pass-through, allocate profit shares to partners on each partner's tax basis; do not treat the ELP as a taxable person. Late filing attracts an initial penalty of GI£100, a GI£50 per-day default penalty up to a maximum of GI£1,000, plus denial or delay of loss relief and potential assessment inflation; deliberate non-filing may be treated as tax evasion with penalties up to 150% of the tax evaded and criminal sanctions.
Box 7 Withholding tax declarations, DTA relief claims, and qualifying participation exemption elections. Attach the Gibraltar Certificate of Tax Residence and Form GI-DTA when invoking a double taxation agreement; specify beneficial owners. Late filing attracts an initial penalty of GI£100, a GI£50 per-day default penalty up to a maximum of GI£1,000, plus denial or delay of loss relief and potential assessment inflation; deliberate non-filing may be treated as tax evasion with penalties up to 150% of the tax evaded and criminal sanctions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is required when annual turnover equals or exceeds GI£10 million and intra-group connected-party transactions equal or exceed GI£1 million; a Master File is required when the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent equals or exceeds GI£750 million.

Economic substance scope: The Commercial (Economic Substance) Act 2020 applies to Gibraltar tax-resident entities, including exempted limited partnerships, carrying on relevant activities. The ISIC 7020 advisory scope is assessed as a headquarters/service-centre relevant activity if material decision-making occurs in Gibraltar; the entity must maintain adequate employees, physical premises, annual expenditure, and core income-generating activities in or directed from Gibraltar, and must file an annual substance declaration.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declarations

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 150% of the tax evaded, interest on all overdue tax, and a criminal fine at the discretion of the court.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate may be personally liable for civil restitution, are subject to travel bans pending collection, and may be imprisoned for up to seven years.
Shell Company or Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Commercial (Economic Substance) Act 2020 penalties of up to GI£20,000 per offence, daily additional penalties of GI£2,000, and striking-off of the entity.

Director personal liability: The court may lift the corporate veil, impose personal responsibility for outstanding substance penalties, and issue director disqualification orders for up to ten years.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties up to GI£100,000 per breach and regulatory revocation of the required statutory license; criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal fines, asset forfeiture, travel bans, and imprisonment of up to seven years for money laundering; continued office-holding is prohibited.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Mandatory transfer pricing adjustment plus penalties up to 100% of the additional tax arising from the adjustment; interest accrues on the differential.

Director personal liability: Directors may be required to make good any corporate tax shortfall if they signed misleading transfer pricing certificates; persistent failure leads to disqualification and travel restraining orders.
Non-Filing and Obstruction

Statutory sanction: Daily default fines and a maximum civil penalty of GI£5,000; the tax authority may issue a best-of-judgment assessment based on third-party data.

Director personal liability: A director who obstructs or conceals records may be held personally liable for the resulting tax assessment, subjected to a travel restriction, and imprisoned for up to two years.
