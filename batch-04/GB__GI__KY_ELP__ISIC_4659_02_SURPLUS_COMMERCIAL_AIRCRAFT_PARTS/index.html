Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar — Income Tax Office (ITO), Suite 771, Europort, Gibraltar (official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation; e-services portal for VAT and income tax returns: https://www.ito.gov.gi). Customs administration: Gibraltar Customs & Excise, operating within the UK-Gibraltar customs union and utilising HMRC's Customs Declaration Service (CDS) for import/export declarations.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident Exempted Limited Partnership must: (1) complete registration with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) Registrar of Limited Partnerships under the Limited Partnerships Act 1991, appointing a Gibraltar-registered general partner and a registered office in Gibraltar; (2) file an Application for Tax Registration (form TIN/TR-1) with the Income Tax Office, submitting the partnership deed, GFSC certificate of registration, partner identity details, and registered office address; (3) receive the Gibraltar TIN (statutory unique reference number) from the ITO, which serves for both income tax and VAT purposes; (4) separately apply for VAT registration with the ITO under the Value Added Tax Act 2019 where the ELP will make taxable supplies in Gibraltar or import goods; (5) where export/import activity occurs, obtain a GB-prefixed EORI (Economic Operator Registration and Identification) number issued by HMRC for use within the UK-Gibraltar customs union and register on the CDS platform. No minimum capital is required for ELP formation; processing time is typically 5–10 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the Exempted Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010: income and gains are attributed proportionately to partners, with non-resident partners enjoying exemption or territorial (remittance-based) treatment on foreign-source trading income derived from surplus commercial aircraft parts distribution; the ELP may be a separate taxable person for Gibraltar VAT purposes only where it conducts a taxable activity in Gibraltar, and transactions involving goods that never enter Gibraltar remain outside the scope of Gibraltar VAT.

Available election forms

(1) Default pass-through election: each partner is assessed individually on their share of ELP income as if the partnership did not exist (Income Tax Act 2010, Part III, sch. 6); (2) non-resident corporate partner election to be treated under the 'exempt/qualifying company' equivalent regime for ordinary-course-of-trade foreign-source income, yielding 0% Gibraltar taxation; (3) non-resident individual partner election for 'Category 2 (High Net Worth)' territorial taxation on Gibraltar-source income only; (4) VAT grouping election under the VAT Act 2019 (analogous to UK VATA 1994, s.43) enabling the ELP to be grouped with a Gibraltar-established group member; (5) hybrid mismatch election is unavailable as an avoidance tool — Gibraltar's anti-hybrid rules, aligned with UK domestic implementation under the UK-Gibraltar tax cooperation framework, deny deductions for transaction structures exploiting a US 'check-the-box' disregarded classification of the ELP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Value Added Tax (VAT) under the Gibraltar Value Added Tax Act 2019, administered by the Commissioner of Income Tax as the Gibraltar VAT authority; import and export duties levied under the Customs Ordinance 2010 within the UK-Gibraltar customs union; no separate GST or sales-and-use tax regime exists.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 0 — no economic turnover threshold: any person carrying on a taxable business activity in Gibraltar, including an importation of goods or a supply of taxable goods/services in Gibraltar, is mandatorily registrable from the first taxable supply; a non-established (foreign) entity is required to register if it supplies goods or services within the scope of Gibraltar VAT, with no de minimis exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because the Gibraltar VAT standard rate is 0%, export zero-rating operates as a legal designation (VAT Act 2019, sch. 6) that secures the availability of input VAT recovery and confirms no VAT charge arises. Eligibility criteria: (1) goods must be physically removed from Gibraltar (including consignment to the UK as a separate VAT territory, or to the EU/third countries) within 3 months of supply; (2) the ELP must hold positive export evidence — a UK CDS/SAD C88 export declaration with Movement Reference Number, transport documentation (bill of lading, air waybill, CMR) naming the ELP as consignor/seller, and a commercial invoice referencing the certified civil-aircraft rotables/avionics contract; (3) for offshore/merchanting trading where the surplus components are never brought into Gibraltar, the supply is outside the scope of Gibraltar VAT, and zero-rating is not required provided title-transfer, inspection, and warehousing documentation substantiates foreign-source status; (4) ancillary services (logistics, inspection supervision, certification brokerage) are zero-rated when supplied to a non-Gibraltar business recipient or directly linked to the exported goods; the ELP must be VAT registered and maintain a fully indexed documentation trail for the claim.

  • Customs Export Declaration — UK CDS/SAD C88 entry with valid Movement Reference Number (MRN) and commodity code (HS 8803/8807) for civil aircraft parts
  • Bill of Lading (original or seaway bill) or Air Waybill evidencing despatch from Gibraltar or a UK consolidation hub, showing the Gibraltar ELP as shipper/consignor with consignee and logistics chain matching the purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) issued by the Gibraltar receiving bank confirming export proceeds credited to the ELP's account, aligned to the invoice value/currency and settlement via Escrow_Aviation or SWIFT wire
  • Commercial invoice and packing list cross-referencing the airworthiness certification (EASA Form 1 / FAA 8130-3) and the registered scope ISIC 4659 / NAICS 423860 for surplus certified rotables and avionics

Gibraltar imposes no real-time electronic fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control mandate for B2B invoices (no SAF-T filing, no state-administered e-invoicing clearance platform). Mandatory requirements: sequential VAT invoice numbering, display of Gibraltar VAT registration number, issue within 30 days of supply, 6-year retention of records, and electronic VAT return filing via the ITO e-services portal. For goods movements, customs declarations must be lodged electronically through HMRC's CDS on behalf of Gibraltar traders, requiring a GB-prefixed EORI number; Authorised Economic Operator (AEO) status is available to facilitate expedited clearance of aviation spares, but no mandatory digital stamping applies.

Gibraltar VAT and customs authorities, co-operating with HMRC under the UK-Gibraltar customs union, subject export zero-rating claims to documentary verification. Where evidence is absent or fraudulent: (1) retrospective assessments claw back unsubstantiated input VAT, recharacterise the supply as domestic, deny input credit, and impose interest on the amounts recovered; (2) civil penalties apply of up to 100% of any VAT sought for deliberate inaccuracies plus a further GIP 1,000 per false invoice, aligning with the UK Finance Act 2007 / HMRC Schedule 24 penalty framework; (3) false customs declarations attract penalties of up to three times the value of the goods and forfeiture under the Customs Ordinance 2010; (4) criminal liability for fraudulent evasion of VAT/customs export controls is an indictable offence punishable by imprisonment of up to 7 years and unlimited fines; (5) given the Medium_Risk_Enhanced AML rating, trade-document inconsistency triggers Suspicious Activity Reports and enhanced due diligence by the Gibraltar bank under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, with cross-border information exchange available under the UK-Gibraltar Double Taxation Agreement and OECD CRS, exposing the ELP and its partners to clawback, penalty, and licence revocation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar imposes no exchange controls. Both current-account transactions (trade in surplus aircraft parts, freight, brokerage commissions) and capital-account transactions (partnership capital contributions, loan repayments, profit distributions, and liquidation surplus) are fully convertible in GBP, USD, EUR, and all freely usable currencies. The GIP is legal tender in Gibraltar, redeemable at par with GBP, and no surrender, licensing, or prior-approval requirement applies to inward or outward remittances by non-resident limited partners of the ELP. Practical remittance frictions are limited to targeted sanctions screening (OFSI, UN, EU under the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018 as extended to Gibraltar) and to the ELP's banking provider's own source-of-funds due diligence. No Gibraltar withholding tax, dividend tax, or distribution tax applies to ELP profit allocations remitted overseas because the ELP is fiscally transparent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory obligation exists to surrender or convert export proceeds in Gibraltar or the UK. The ELP may lawfully retain 100% of USD and EUR export receipts from the distribution of certified civil-aircraft rotables and avionics surplus, hold them in offshore or onshore accounts, and deploy them for trade settlements without mandatory conversion ratios, repatriation floors, or time limits. Compliance duties are record-keeping and reporting: each export shipment must be supported by an HMRC CDS export declaration; the ELP must maintain audited annual financial statements revaluing foreign-currency balances; and any cross-border aggregate flow that reaches the materiality threshold in the bank's UK BoP reporting instructions must be attributed with a correct purpose code to permit Bank of England statistical reporting. The GFSC requires an annual MLRO report and audited accounts for the ELP's banking relationship.

Central bank supervision: Monetary policy and sterling anchor for Gibraltar rest with the Bank of England; the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act 1934 at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory regulator for banking, electronic money, payment services, and investment-related FX business under the Financial Services Act 2019, and administers the AML/CFT licensing framework set out in the GFSC AML/CFT Guidance Notes and the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar). There is no separate central bank or exchange-control authority in Gibraltar; residual FX control functions are absorbed by HM Treasury/OFSI sanctions jurisdiction and by Bank of England balance-of-payments reporting obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a collections and treasury overlay, LOW-MEDIUM as a standalone settlement rail. A GFSC-licensed electronic money institution under Part VII of the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Regulations 2019 can onboard the Gibraltar ELP fully remotely, with virtual IBANs in GBP, USD, and EUR. UK-authorised EMIs (Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex) lost automatic passporting into Gibraltar after Brexit; they would require the ELP to maintain a UK branch or to rely on the EMI's cross-border servicing licence, which many core correspondent banks treat as elevated risk for a Medium_Risk_Enhanced ISIC 4659 aviation-parts trading profile. Legal constraints include: EMI balances are safeguarded e-money, not bank deposits; EMIs cannot issue commercial documentary credits, cannot integrate aviation escrow rails, and typically impose a practical transaction ceiling of approximately GBP 500,000 annual throughput before enhanced AML review. The recommended architecture is a GFSC-licensed EMI for customer collections and instant FX conversion, overlaid by a tier-1 Gibraltar bank for clearing, LC issuance, and escrow finality.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing protocol: SWIFT MT103 single-beneficiary credit from the partner's bank to the ELP's segregated operational or capital escrow account at Gibraltar International Bank. USD wires clear via the Gibraltar bank's New York correspondent on Fedwire/CHIPS, GBP via CHAPS, and EUR via TARGET2. Every credit is tagged with the UK BoP purpose code 'D.4 Direct Investment Equity Capital' or the corresponding creditor reference 'CAP-<date>-<call number>'. The receiving bank obtains in advance the capital-call notice, the executed subscription or partnership-deed amendment, and FATCA/CRS status of the remitter. Wires between GBP 50,000 and GBP 500,000 trigger a five-business-day MLRO confirmation window; wires above GBP 500,000 additionally require a source-of-wealth memorandum prepared by a licensed fiduciary and a bank-to-bank SWIFT pre-advice. No GFSC or HMRC authorisation is required for the capital injection itself, but the ELP must record the contribution in its statutory accounts and file the annual CRS return.Outbound settlement uses a five-rail hierarchy: (1) SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for direct supplier payments, instructed via GIB's New York, London, and Frankfurt correspondents to minimise intermediary deductions; (2) SWIFT MT202 COVER for interbank reimbursement of correspondent claims; (3) commercial documentary Letters of Credit under UCP600, confirmed by a global top-50 bank for high-value rotable purchases, with presentation of the airworthiness certificate (EASA Form 1 or FAA 8130-3), commercial invoice, packing list, and air waybill or FIATA FCR; (4) Escrow_Aviation closed-loop releases where funds are dispatched only upon serial-number verification and delivery-confirmation proof; (5) CHAPS for same-day GBP disbursements and TARGET2 for euro settlements. The ELP maintains a beneficiary whitelist, dual-signature entitlement limits, and a monthly reconciliation of disbursements against purchase orders and escrow release records.FX Forward (outright, deliverable GBP/USD and GBP/EUR, tenor 1 to 12 months) — permitted under GFSC conduct standards when executed with a licensed Gibraltar bank or its dedicated FX desk and fully covered by the underlying aviation-parts trade contract.FX Swap (spot-forward or forward-forward) — permitted for rolling settlement of USD payables matched against EUR/USD aviation-part receivables.European-style FX Options (zero-premium collar and participating forward, tenor up to 12 months) — permitted exclusively for hedging surplus USD conversion receivables; exotic, leveraged, or barrier structures are prohibited.Cross-Currency Interest Rate Swap (fixed-to-fixed USD vs GBP) — permitted only where the notional equals an outstanding commercial loan on a specific aircraft-part inventory purchase and the ELP General Partner has passed the bank's appropriateness assessment.Non-Deliverable Forward (NDF) — legally permissible in Gibraltar solely for non-convertible or restricted currencies; GIP/GBP is freely convertible at par, so NDFs are neither necessary nor recommended for this entity's functional-currency flow.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a collections and treasury overlay, LOW-MEDIUM as a standalone settlement rail. A GFSC-licensed electronic money institution under Part VII of the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Regulations 2019 can onboard the Gibraltar ELP fully remotely, with virtual IBANs in GBP, USD, and EUR. UK-authorised EMIs (Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex) lost automatic passporting into Gibraltar after Brexit; they would require the ELP to maintain a UK branch or to rely on the EMI's cross-border servicing licence, which many core correspondent banks treat as elevated risk for a Medium_Risk_Enhanced ISIC 4659 aviation-parts trading profile. Legal constraints include: EMI balances are safeguarded e-money, not bank deposits; EMIs cannot issue commercial documentary credits, cannot integrate aviation escrow rails, and typically impose a practical transaction ceiling of approximately GBP 500,000 annual throughput before enhanced AML review. The recommended architecture is a GFSC-licensed EMI for customer collections and instant FX conversion, overlaid by a tier-1 Gibraltar bank for clearing, LC issuance, and escrow finality.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at ELP level (tax-transparent pass-through); 12.5% standard Gibraltar corporation tax if a corporate partner is Gibraltar tax resident.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% statutory dividend withholding tax in Gibraltar; distributions from an Exempted Limited Partnership are treated as profit shares and are not subject to Gibraltar withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are mandatory when annual turnover exceeds GBP 5,000,000 or connected-party cross-border transactions exceed GBP 2,000,000 per annum; contemporaneous benchmarking is required for transactions involving surplus aircraft parts, spare rotables, and avionics inventory.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to AIFs? with 'relevant activities', including distribution and service centre businesses. An ELP engaged in surplus commercial aircraft parts trading must demonstrate economic substance through management and direction from Gibraltar, adequate local employees, physical premises, and proportionate operating expenditure. A mere letter-box operation or board seat held by an overseas nominee will fail the ESR and expose the partnership to registration strike-off and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: Finance penalty of up to GBP 50,000 or 100% of tax evaded, plus daily penalties for continued non-compliance; deliberate concealment may lead to criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to 7 years and forfeiture of proceeds.

Director personal liability: The general partner and directors of a corporate general partner may be personally liable for civil restitution and joint and several tax liabilities where fraud is proven; travel bans, asset freezing orders, and imprisonment of responsible officers may be imposed.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Strike-off from Gibraltar Companies Registry, cancellation of the ELP's registration, fixed penalty of GBP 300 plus GBP 100 per day for ESR non-compliance, and disqualification of the general partner from acting in Gibraltar.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally disqualified for up to 15 years, jointly and severally liable for partnership liabilities if the entity is deemed a façade, and subject to contempt proceedings and travel bans.
Anti-Money Laundering Breach

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to GBP 5,000,000 per breach under Gibraltar's AML/CFT regime; criminal sanction of up to 14 years’ imprisonment for money laundering; regulatory rebuke, licence revocation, and prohibition from banking services.

Director personal liability: The appointed compliance officer and general partner may face personal criminal liability, disqualification, freezing of personal assets, and travel bans; piercing the Limited Partnership veil is permitted where AML misconduct makes the partnership a mere alter ego.
Unlicensed / Uncertified Aircraft Parts Distribution

Statutory sanction: Under Gibraltar and UK aviation supply-chain legislation, penalties include fines up to GBP 1,000,000, forfeiture of the aircraft parts and associated escrow funds, suspension or revocation of trading licences, and criminal prosecution for falsifying aviation documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors and the ELP general partner may be prosecuted personally, face civil restitution to buyers or insurers, travel bans, disqualification from aviation supply chain registration, and imprisonment up to 10 years.
