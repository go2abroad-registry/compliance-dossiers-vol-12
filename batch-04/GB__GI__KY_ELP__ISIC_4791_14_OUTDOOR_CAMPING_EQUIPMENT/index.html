Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar, 4th Floor, New Harbours, Europort, Gibraltar; official portal: www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax. Partnership formation is filed at the Gibraltar Companies House Registry (www.companieshouse.gi), which administratively interfaces with the Income Tax Office for tax reference issuance.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the ELP under the Limited Partnerships Act 2021 at the Gibraltar Companies House Registry, naming the general partner and all limited partners. Step 2: The Registry transmits registration particulars to the Income Tax Office, which issues a unique Tax Reference Number (TRN) for the partnership. Step 3: Each partner files a non-resident/resident status declaration with the Income Tax Office; non-resident partners deriving no Gibraltar-source income require no further substantive filing, though an annual Economic Substance Notification (Form ESN) is mandatory if the ELP is in scope of the Economic Substance (Companies and Partnerships) Act 2020 (relevant activities include distribution and service centres and headquarters). Step 4: Upon a chargeable event, the ELP files a partnership return; each partner reports their allocated share under their personal/corporate TRN. A general partner performing local management (e.g., bookkeeping or inventory handling in Gibraltar) requires substance commensurate with that activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box election; pass-through status is statutorily automatic for all limited partnerships under the Limited Partnerships Act 2021 and the Income Tax Act 2010, with no entity-level income tax on the ELP. The partnership deed may freely determine profit/loss sharing ratios, which control the tax attribution to each partner for Gibraltar purposes, subject to the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) in Part XIX of the Income Tax Act 2010 and the OECD hybrid mismatch framework adopted under Gibraltar legislation for cross-border arrangements involving the UK or EU Member States.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST operates in Gibraltar; indirect taxation is limited to customs duties under the UK-Gibraltar common customs union and excise duties on a narrow list of commodities (alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oils). Online retail of outdoor camping equipment falls entirely outside the domestic indirect tax base.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — there is no VAT registration threshold because Gibraltar does not impose VAT on any supply of goods or services. Excise duty registration is required only for businesses producing, importing, or distributing the specified dutiable commodities; outdoor camping equipment (HS Chapters 63 and 95) is not a designated commodity. For non-EU and non-UK export sales, customs export declarations are required where goods physically transit the UK customs territory via the UK Customs Declaration Service (CDS).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — Gibraltar levies no VAT, so there is no 0% export VAT mechanism to claim or substantiate. Cross-border sales of camping equipment supplied from Gibraltar are treated as outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation, and the importer of record in the destination jurisdiction accounts for local import VAT/GST and duties. To protect the non-resident pass-through position of the partnership, the ELP should preserve evidence that title, dispatch origin, and settlement are offshore, thereby confirming the absence of Gibraltar-source retail profit and the non-applicability of the Gibraltar 12.5% corporate rate to any corporate general partner.

  • Commercial Invoice citing Incoterms 2020 and HS Chapter 95 / 63 commodity classification
  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/C88 lodged via UK CDS) where goods transit the UK customs territory
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or courier consignment note evidencing physical export from Gibraltar
  • Payment settlement statements from Stripe/PayPal evidencing each sale event
  • Bank credit confirmation (equivalent of Inward Remittance Certificate) evidencing receipt of consideration

Gibraltar mandates no real-time fiscal clearance, no VAT e-invoicing, and no digital stamping regime. Payment rails (Stripe/PayPal) are treated as ordinary commercial records, not fiscal clearance channels. Goods movements to non-UK destinations require a SAD/C88 declaration through UK CDS (self-filed or via a licensed customs agent), and commercial invoices must be retained electronically in an auditable format for six years. No foreign tax authority pre-clearance or API-based fiscal interface applies to the ELP's e-commerce platform.

Where export documentation is fabricated or profits are artificially diverted to avoid Gibraltar tax, the Commissioner of Income Tax may raise discovery assessments (unlimited time limit in cases of fraud, six years otherwise), impose civil penalties of up to 100% of the tax understated plus statutory interest, and claw back reliefs or refunds previously granted. False customs declarations attract penalties under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance, and fraudulent conduct may lead to criminal prosecution with imprisonment. The Income Tax Office actively cross-references bank flows, payment gateway records, and declaration data under exchange of information arrangements; the 180-day receipt deadline in this dossier reflects the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission AML/CFT expectation and standard banking practice for export settlements rather than a statutory income tax requirement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates a fully convertible current and capital account regime. The Gibraltar pound is pegged at par to GBP, and there are no exchange controls, approval requirements, capital repatriation caps, or mandatory waiting periods applicable to non-resident partners. An Exempted Limited Partnership is treated as tax-transparent for Gibraltar income tax purposes, and profit distributions to non-resident partners can be remitted without Gibraltar withholding tax provided the underlying income is not Gibraltar-source. Banks may nonetheless apply internal anti-money-laundering checks on the economic substance and source of funds of distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory obligation to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds within any prescribed timeline. The ELP may hold export receipts in multicurrency accounts at Gibraltar or UK clearing banks, apply them directly to supplier disbursements, or convert them through licensed counterparties at prevailing market rates. No mandatory conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (sterling monetary authority and settlement anchor) together with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), which supervises banks and payment institutions under the Gibraltar Financial Services (Banking) Act and related FSC rules. The Government of Gibraltar is the statutory issuer of Gibraltar pound notes and coins, which circulate at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP; Gibraltar does not maintain a separate central bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium-High as a secondary clearing rail; Low as the sole treasury banking solution. A Gibraltar-registered Exempted Limited Partnership may open accounts with UK-FCA or EEA-regulated e-money institutions using remote KYC, but those institutions are not licensed Gibraltar banks. EMIs cannot extend overdraft credit and must hold client funds in safeguarded trust accounts; consequently they are suitable only for payment-rail settlement, expense routing, and Stripe/PayPal sweeps. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

10% flat corporation tax rate for all Gibraltar-resident companies charged under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Gibraltar imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions by a Gibraltar company to any shareholder, resident or non-resident. Distributions from an ELP to its partners are similarly free of Gibraltar withholding tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be delivered to the Commissioner of Income Tax within 7 months of the end of the accounting period, and the resulting assessment is due and payable within 30 days of the date of assessment. Extensions are available only by prior written approval of the Commissioner.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing follows the corporate return deadline regime: delivery within 7 months of the ELP's accounting reference date; partnership-level assessment is raised on the precedent or general partner.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GBP 20,000,000 - Mandatory Local File applies where the Gibraltar entity's aggregate annual controlled transactions exceed GBP 20 million, or where any single controlled transaction exceeds GBP 5 million. Master File obligations apply to multinational groups with consolidated global revenue above GBP 750 million. Transactions below these thresholds must still be supported by written benchmark evidence if challenged.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance (Companies and Partnerships) Regulations 2020 apply to an ELP that is tax resident in Gibraltar and carries on a relevant activity, including distribution and service centre business. Internet retailing of outdoor camping equipment falls squarely within this class. The entity must maintain in Gibraltar: a physical place of business, adequate full-time employees, proportionate operating expenditure, and the core income-generating activities must be conducted or directed in Gibraltar. Evidencing factors include board or partner management meetings held in Gibraltar, procurement and logistics decisions taken by Gibraltar staff, and maintenance of inventory or commercial records in Gibraltar. Non-compliance triggers a formal determination, financial penalties of GBP 10,000 for a first failure and GBP 20,000 for continued non-compliance, loss of the tax residence certificate, automatic exchange of information reports to foreign authorities, and potential strike-off for persistent breaches.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to 150% of the undercharged tax plus statutory interest from the due date; criminal prosecution on indictment carries unlimited fines and imprisonment for up to 7 years; confiscation orders may reach the full evaded amount and connected assets under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners, and shadow managers who consented to or connived in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the entity's unpaid tax and penalties. Courts will pierce the ELP veil where the partnership was used as a facade; travel bans, seizure of passports, worldwide asset freezing orders, and director disqualification orders for up to 15 years are routinely imposed.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Determination of economic substance failure; penalty of GBP 10,000 for the first reporting period and GBP 20,000 for each subsequent period; mandatory exchange of information with the UK HMRC and EU member state authorities; striking off and dissolution of the ELP for persistent non-compliance.

Director personal liability: The general partner (and any corporate director of a corporate general partner) is personally liable for the substance penalties and for misrepresentation to third parties; disqualification and personal liability for debts incurred while trading without substance are ordered where the arrangement is held to be a sham.
Money Laundering and AML/CFT Breach

Statutory sanction: Civil financial penalties of up to GBP 500,000 or 10% of annual turnover under the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission AML regime, whichever is greater; criminal conviction for money laundering carries fines and imprisonment for up to 14 years; payment processors may be directed to terminate the merchant account.

Director personal liability: Natural persons acting as MLROs, compliance officers, and directing minds may be criminally prosecuted even where the partnership is the registered customer; confiscation orders attach to personal assets; travel bans and extradition cooperation with UK and EU authorities apply.
Transfer Pricing Frauds and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment increasing chargeable income, secondary adjustments for any deemed distributions or loans, interest charged at statutory rates, and penalties ranging from 30% to 100% of the adjusted tax deficiency where documentation is absent or the arrangement is deliberately mispriced.

Director personal liability: The general partner of the ELP is jointly and severally liable for the adjusted partnership tax; a corporate director who signed false TP documentation faces personal civil restitution, disqualification, and, where the mispricing amounts to cheating the revenue, prosecution for fraud and imprisonment.
