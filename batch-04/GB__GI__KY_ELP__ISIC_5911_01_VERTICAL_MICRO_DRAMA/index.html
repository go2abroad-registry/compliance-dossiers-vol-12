Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (official taxation portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation; customs and excise are administered by HM Customs Gibraltar)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity establishing a Gibraltar Exempt Limited Partnership (ELP) must instruct its Gibraltar-licensed registered agent to file the limited partnership registration with the Gibraltar Companies Registry (Registrar of Companies). After issuance of the ELP certificate, the agent applies to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) by submitting the certificate of registration, details of the general and limited partners, and confirmation of the registered office in Gibraltar. A VAT ID is not procurable because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST system. Non-resident partners obtaining a Gibraltar TIN is optional and is generally done only to evidence territorial tax status or satisfy bank/KYC requirements. No statutory tax registration threshold applies to an ELP that maintains valid exempt status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The ELP may elect exempt status under the Gibraltar Limited Partnerships Act and the associated Income Tax (Exempt Limited Partnerships) rules, which removes the partnership from Gibraltar corporation tax while preserving pass-through treatment for partners. There is no US-style check-the-box election and no hybrid mismatch election in Gibraltar; the sole material election is the exempt-status application filed with the Registrar and accepted by the Income Tax Office. Partners remain taxed in their own jurisdictions, with non-Gibraltar-source income excluded from Gibraltar taxation under the territorial/source rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No national VAT, GST, or general sales-and-use tax applies in Gibraltar; indirect taxation is limited to customs duties and excise on physical imports of specified goods, neither of which attaches to digital micro-drama streaming services

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 0 — no VAT/GST registration threshold exists because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST. A customs/excise registration may be required for physical importers of goods, but that is unrelated to the digital streaming activity. Suppliers of digital services to consumers located outside Gibraltar have no Gibraltar indirect-tax registration requirement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for export purposes is not a Gibraltar VAT concept because Gibraltar is outside the UK VAT and EU VAT systems. Gibraltar VAT does not exist and therefore no VAT is chargeable on export goods or offshore services. For income tax purposes, revenue derived by the ELP from the global distribution of vertical micro-dramas will be treated as non-Gibraltar-source income if the underlying services are performed outside Gibraltar, the contracts are concluded outside Gibraltar, and the relevant streaming platform revenues are booked and collected abroad. This territorial treatment operates as a de facto zero-rating shield for the ELP, subject to substantive evidence and absence of Gibraltar management-and-control activities.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (or applicable cargo/exit declaration where physical goods are involved)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from Gibraltar to the foreign destination (for goods; not applicable to pure digital streaming)
  • Commercial invoice for the streaming rights or platform service fees
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank credit statement evidencing receipt of foreign-sourced revenue in the ELP account
  • Signed platform agreement and subscriber/revenue reports demonstrating the place of supply and non-Gibraltar performance
  • ELP exempt-status certificate and partner residence statements

Gibraltar imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory e-invoicing API, and no digital stamping regime. Commercial invoices are sufficient for Gibraltar tax and customs purposes. If the Gibraltar ELP supplies services into the UK or another VAT jurisdiction, the counterparty's local e-invoicing or digital reporting rules may impose practical formatting requirements, but they are not Gibraltar fiscal clearance mandates.

Deliberate submission of false documentation, artificial diversion of Gibraltar-source revenue, or breach of ELP exemption conditions may trigger reassessment by the Income Tax Office for up to six years, or without limit in cases of fraud. Penalties can reach up to 150% of the tax underpaid plus interest; criminal fines and imprisonment are possible for materially false declarations. Customs penalties apply separately if export documents are falsified or exemptions are abused. The ELP exemption can be retrospectively revoked, causing full corporation-tax liability on previously exempt profits and exposure for the general partner.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The United Kingdom and Gibraltar operate an entirely liberalised capital and current account regime with no exchange controls. GBP, GIP, USD and EUR are freely convertible for current account transactions, capital movements, profit distributions and partnership withdrawals. No central bank approval or exchange permit is required for the repatriation of profits from a Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP), provided the distribution is made in accordance with the limited partnership agreement and the beneficial ownership of the payee has been verified under AML/CFT standards. Post-Brexit, Gibraltar is outside the EU single market, so EU capital-movement directives no longer apply, but this creates no convertibility restriction; the only practical hurdle is the banking counterparty's internal KYC and source-of-funds verification for a non-resident ELP.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Neither the Bank of England nor the GFSC imposes any statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio or surrender obligation on export proceeds or subscription receipts. Foreign currency receivables from app-store payouts and content-licensing contracts may be retained in foreign-currency accounts held at a Gibraltar-licensed bank or a UK-regulated bank, provided the balances are reflected in the ELP's books and all inbound receipts are traceable through a regulated financial institution for audit and CRS reporting purposes.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (United Kingdom); the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the licensing and supervisory authority for financial services in Gibraltar, while the Government of Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP), which is pegged at par to the pound sterling (GBP).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium with material legal qualification. Gibraltar does not participate in the UK FCA's post-Brexit temporary permissions regime, and EU passporting rights have been extinguished; therefore, a UK or EU electronic money institution (EMI) cannot passport its licence into Gibraltar. Offshore EMI-only accounts (e.g. a fintech with no local regulated branch) are generally rejected for ELP structures because regulators require clear evidence of the EMI's authorisation, the safeguarding of client funds in a Tier 1 bank, and full transparency of the ELP's beneficial ownership. A workable structure is a GFSC-regulated or UK FCA-regulated EMI with explicit Gibraltar servicing capability, used solely as a payment conduit for low-value creator payouts and app-store settlement forwarding, with the principal treasury balance held at a Gibraltar Tier 1 commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions must be routed as SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from the limited partner's regulated bank directly to the ELP's operational account at a Gibraltar Tier 1 bank. Each remittance shall carry the ISO 20022 purpose code EQUI (equity investment contribution) or CAPC (capital contribution) in the structured remittance information field; for intra-group funding, the code ICAG (intercompany agreement and payment) may be used provided a signed intercompany agreement accompanies the transfer. The ELP must hold on file a board resolution or general-partner resolution authorising the capital call, the executed subscription schedule, and a source-of-funds declaration from the remitting partner. All inbound wires must be verified against the bank's payment-screening thresholds and matched to the ELP's internal capital account ledger before funds are classified as available treasury liquidity.Trade settlements are executed through the following compliant clearing rails: (i) SWIFT MT103 for single cross-border payments above USD 10,000, with a full remittance narrative identifying the underlying content-licensing, creator-settlement or production-service invoice; (ii) Irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit governed by UCP600 for milestone-based production financing, issued by the Gibraltar bank in favour of the production vendor; (iii) SEPA Instant for EUR-denominated creator and influencer payouts routed through a licensed EMI; and (iv) CHAPS or BACS for UK-domestic settlement of salaries, residuals and operating expenses. Recipient banking details must be static, verifiable accounts at regulated banks or GFSC/UK FCA-authorised e-money institutions; payments to unverified virtual-asset wallets or unregistered third-party payees are blocked by policy. 10% for taxable companies; 0% for exempt companies; 12.5% standard rate historically referenced — current effective headline for active trading companies is 10% under the Income Tax (Consequential Amendments) provisions; partnerships are fiscally transparent with members taxed at their personal or corporate marginal rates.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% withholding tax on dividends paid by Gibraltar companies / partnerships to non-resident partners, because Gibraltar does not impose dividend withholding tax under domestic law. Exempt companies by default pay no withholding.

Within 10 months and 15 days after the end of the fiscal year / basis period (e.g., for a 31 December year-end, filing and payment by 14 October of the following year). The Commissioner may grant extensions. Penalties apply for late submission.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar has no statutory local-file threshold expressed in a single published amount; the Income Tax Act applies the arm's length principle to all controlled transactions, and the GITO requires contemporaneous documentation for transactions exceeding GBP 1,000,000 in aggregate annual value, with simplified documentation for transactions between GBP 200,000 and GBP 1,000,000. Master File is expected for groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million (CbC-reporting threshold). For a micro-drama streaming business, TP documentation must cover intercompany content licensing, user-acquisition services, payment-processing cost-sharing, and management fees.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies Economic Substance for Resident Companies (ESRC) and the Income Tax (Economic Substance — Reporting) Rules. All Gibraltar-resident entities, including ELPs considered 'resident' for tax purposes, that carry on a 'relevant activity' (distribution and service centre business, holding company business, financing, leasing, IP holding, and headquarters) must satisfy the core income-generating activities test, directed and managed in Gibraltar, with adequate physical presence, employees, and expenditure. For a vertical micro-drama business holding IP rights, the key requirement is that strategic content acquisition, the licensing/distribution decision-making, and streaming-app management take place in Gibraltar. The Commissioner may levy a penalty of up to £100,000 plus an additional £1,000 per day for continued noncompliance, and may exchange information with the UK HMRC under automatic exchange of information agreements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Under Gibraltar's Income Tax Act, a person who knowingly furnishes a false return is liable on summary conviction to a fine not exceeding £5,000 and/or imprisonment for up to 12 months; on indictment, an unlimited fine and/or imprisonment up to 7 years. Civil penalties are up to 100% of tax undercharged plus interest. The GITO may issue discovery assessments, and cross-border revenue matching with Apple, Google, and Stripe payment processors exposes the discrepancy.

Director personal liability: Partnership general partners and any shadow directors are personally liable for the partnership's tax debts if the ELP is used as a vehicle for fraud or is a sham. The court may pierce the ELP veil, make the general partner personally liable for unpaid tax, impose director disqualification orders valid in Gibraltar and the UK, issue travel bans where assets exceed £100,000, and order civil restitution. Imprisonment for fraud is up to 7 years. Under UK Proceeds of Crime Act 2003 (as extended to Gibraltar), a person found to have laundered the proceeds of tax evasion faces up to 14 years' imprisonment.
Failure to File Economic Substance Return / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Failure to file an ES return is a strict liability offence. Fines: initial £10,000 per relevant activity; for continued failure, £1,000 per day; maximum penalty for non-compliance with substance requirements is £1,000,000 per relevant activity. The GITO may also require a statutory declaration that the ELP is not a shell; if the declaration is false, a criminal offence arises.

Director personal liability: The general partner (often a corporate GP) and any individual exercising management control may be prosecuted personally. The court may impose a travel ban, prevent the person from leaving Gibraltar until the outstanding penalties are paid, and order forfeiture of partnership assets. Directors who permit the shell operation may be disqualified for up to 15 years under Gibraltar's Companies Act equivalents, and are jointly and severally liable for the penalties.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Underpricing of Content Rights

Statutory sanction: The Income Tax Office may impose a TP adjustment, charge additional tax at up to 100% of the difference, and apply interest. Under the UK's Diverted Profits Tax, where the ELP is managed from the UK, a 25% tax may apply on diverted profits, and HMRC may issue a penalty notice under Schedule 36 Finance Act 2008 of up to £3,000 per failure plus daily penalties. Criminal penalties for fraudulent transfer pricing documents can reach 7 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors of the corporate GP who knowingly signed false transfer pricing documentation or transfer pricing declarations are personally liable for the resulting penalties and may face a personal assessment equal to the diverted profit. Courts will pierce the veil where the arrangement lacks any commercial substance, imposing joint and several liability, asset freezing orders, and international travel bans pending settlement.
Failure to Register for UK Tax / CFC Attribution

Statutory sanction: If the ELP's place of effective management is found to be the UK, it may be treated as a UK tax resident and liable to UK corporation tax on worldwide profits at up to 25%, with penalties up to 200% of the unpaid tax for deliberate concealment. UK CFC rules may attribute Gibraltar profits to UK corporate partners at the UK corporate rate. Failure to register for UK corporation tax carries a penalty of up to £500 per HMRC notice.

Director personal liability: The UK directors of the corporate partner, or the individual exercising substantial influence over the ELP from the UK, are exposed to HMRC's senior accounting officer certification regime; a deliberate failure to establish proper tax accounting arrangements carries a personal penalty of £5,000. In cases of dishonest conduct, disqualification orders, travel bans, and criminal liability under the Corporate Manslaughter and Corporate Homicide Act (in cases of gross management failure) may be asserted.
AML, IAP, and Payment Processing Compliance Failures

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Proceeds of Crime Act and the AML/CFT Handbook: failure to register as a relevant financial business is a criminal offence, with fines up to £1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years. For banks/EMIs, the GFSC may revoke the payment institution licence, issue a public statement, and impose a financial penalty up to €5,000,000 for breach of AML obligations. Unauthorised payment-services business under the Financial Services Act can attract a fine and up to 2 years' custody.

Director personal liability: Senior management who consent to or connive at money laundering schemes face personal criminal liability: confiscation orders under the POCA, director disqualification, travel bans pending asset disclosure, and imprisonment up to 14 years. The GFSC routinely enforces against responsible individuals at a personal level, ordering restitution of illicit gains and costs.
