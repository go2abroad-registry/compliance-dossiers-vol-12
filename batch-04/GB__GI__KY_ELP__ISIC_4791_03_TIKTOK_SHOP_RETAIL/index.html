Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax) under the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended) for direct taxes, and HM Customs Gibraltar (Customs and Excise Division) for VAT; official access via the Government of Gibraltar portal at www.gibraltar.gov.gi and the online tax/VAT filing systems.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the ELP under the Limited Partnerships Act 2015 (as amended) with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies to obtain the statutory registered number. Step 2: File the partnership particulars and Form A1 with the Income Tax Office; submit the ELP deed, registered-office certificate, general partner details, and partner ownership schedule to obtain the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN), issued within approximately 5 business days. Step 3: Where taxable turnover exceeds or is expected to exceed the VAT threshold, apply for a Gibraltar VAT identification number online via HM Customs Gibraltar (Form V1), appending bank references, projected supply data, and the ELP deed; the VAT certificate issues within 15 working days. The TRN and VAT number constitute the statutory TIN/VAT ID pair; a resident tax agent or responsible general partner must be designated for compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the ELP is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act 2010; profits and losses are attributed pro rata to the general and limited partners; territorial taxation limits Gibraltar exposure for non-resident partners without Gibraltar-source income or a permanent establishment.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists in Gibraltar; fiscal transparency is mandatory for the ELP. Available elections include: (a) designation as an exempt private fund under the Limited Partnerships Act 2015; (b) appointment of the responsible general partner or a resident tax representative for filings; (c) partnership-agreement election to consolidate partnership results into one partner's computation where commercial conditions meet the group/consortium criteria; and (d) treaty-tie-breaker considerations for hybrid entities under the UK-Gibraltar double taxation arrangement. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; BEPS Action 2 automatic adjustments apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Value Added Tax (VAT)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 85,000 annual taxable turnover in Gibraltar pounds (compulsory registration within 30 days of exceeding; voluntary registration permitted), subject to cross-border nexus: TikTok Shop retail sales to UK/EU consumers are further tested under online-marketplace deemed-supplier and distance-selling thresholds in the destination state.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to physical goods exported from Gibraltar to a destination outside the Gibraltar customs territory within three months of supply, and to qualifying international services supplied to customers outside Gibraltar, provided that: title passes to an overseas buyer; the ELP retains conclusive evidence of export (customs entry, transport contract, courier tracking, and merchant settlement records); the TikTok Shop order and payment data identify the overseas destination; and the supplier files the zero-rated supply on its VAT return. Refund of residual input VAT is obtained through the periodic VAT return process.

  • Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Gibraltar electronic export entry (outward declaration) lodged with HM Customs Gibraltar
  • Commercial invoice referencing the TikTok Shop order ID and ISIC 4791 goods description
  • TikTok Shop Payout settlement statement and Wise B2B FX confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Packing list with courier airway bill or bill of lading proving physical departure from Gibraltar
  • Buyer location evidence (delivery address and transaction records) preserved for six years

No CTC (continuous transaction control), real-time fiscal clearance, API stamping, or e-invoice authorisation is currently mandated in Gibraltar; VAT invoices may be issued in standard electronic or paper format and must be retained for six years. VAT returns and supporting records must be lodged electronically through HM Customs Gibraltar's online system, with digital-preservation obligations aligned to OECD record-keeping standards.

Zero-rating claims are subject to retrospective reassessment by HM Customs Gibraltar for up to six years, with extended assessment windows in cases of deliberate fraud; defective or missing export evidence triggers denial of zero-rating, clawback of any input VAT claimed, civil penalties up to 100% of the VAT at risk, interest, and criminal prosecution for evasion, with joint and several liability extending to the general partner and tax agent. The Income Tax Office cross-references export declarations, TikTok Shop payouts, and Wise B2B settlement records to detect hybrid-mismatch and circular-funds fraud; fraudulent refunds are recoverable with sanctions including disqualification of the ELP's general partner.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged 1:1 with GBP; both current account and capital account payments are fully convertible with no exchange-control quotas. Profit remittances from an Exempted Limited Partnership are treated as pass-through distributions at the partner level and face no statutory repatriation tax or FX conversion barrier. The practical compliance hurdle is not convertibility but the transparent demonstration of underlying trade substance, source of funds, and platform settlement documentation under GFSC AML/CFT standards.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rules exist in Gibraltar. Export and social-commerce redemption proceeds may be retained in any permitted currency, although GFSC-regulated banks apply enhanced transaction monitoring to USD and multi-currency inflows. For any single inbound remittance exceeding GBP-equivalent EUR 10,000, the bank requires a supporting commercial document such as a TikTok Shop settlement statement or commercial invoice; there is no mandatory conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as banking and financial services regulator; Bank of England as the ultimate monetary authority for sterling; Government of Gibraltar issues Gibraltar pound banknotes at par with GBP.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Gibraltar tax/assessment year runs from 1 July to 30 June. A CT-1 return notice is issued after the year of assessment; the return must be filed no later than 31 December of the calendar year in which the tax year ends. For the assessment year 1 July 2024 to 30 June 2025, the filing deadline is 31 December 2025. Non-resident ELPs are subject to the same statutory deadline with no administrative extension.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Gibraltar Local / Master File documentation obligation is triggered where the Gibraltar entity belongs to a multinational group with consolidated annual revenue exceeding GIP 750,000,000 / EUR 750,000,000. In addition, a country-by-country report threshold applies at the same group revenue level. A de minimis local-file requirement is applied for related-party transactions: Gibraltar operations with aggregate controlled transaction value exceeding GIP 10,000,000 per tax year must contemporaneously document the controlled transactions, commercial rationale, selection of transfer-pricing method, and arm's length benchmarking.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime under the International Tax Co-operation (Economic Substance) Act 2019, as amended. Any ELP deriving relevant income from e-commerce, distribution and service centre activity carried on in or from Gibraltar must satisfy the core income-generating activities (CIGA) test in Gibraltar: maintain a physical office, have adequate staff, incur sufficient local expenditure, and ensure strategic decisions are taken by partners or directors meeting in Gibraltar. The ELP must file an annual economic substance declaration with the Commissioner; a low-risk social-commerce entity with real personnel and inventory management will generally pass, but a pass-through ELP used solely as an invoice-milking shell is subject to substance failure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties of up to 200% of the tax evaded, plus interest at 8% per annum. Criminal conviction on indictment carries a fine of up to GIP 250,000 and/or imprisonment up to six years; proceeds of tax fraud may be confiscated under the Proceeds of Crime Act.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where the ELP is used as a sham or cloak for fraud. The general partner, the directors of the corporate general partner, and the ultimate beneficial owner are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, may face asset-freezing orders, travel bans, and up to six years' imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: A formal ESR finding of non-compliance triggers financial penalties of up to GIP 100,000, a daily default penalty of GIP 2,000, cancellation of the ELP's registration at the Gibraltar Companies Registry, and a mandatory disclosure to the UK revenue authorities and OECD exchange of information network.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are personally liable for knowingly making false substance declarations. Courts may issue director disqualification orders, international travel restrictions, and impose personal restitution orders over diverted profits.
Transfer Pricing Adjustment and BEPS Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner will reconstruct the transaction at arm's length, assess additional tax at 12.5% plus an offshore penalty equal to 100% of the understated profit, and charge 8% interest. Non-filing of country-by-country or master-file documentation attracts a penalty of GIP 10,000 per month of delay, with unlimited fines on conviction.

Director personal liability: Officers who sign or direct false transfer-pricing documentation bear civil liability for the alignment of intra-group pricing; criminal liability arises where documents are fabricated. Director disqualification, confiscation orders, and up to three years' imprisonment may be imposed for wilful misconduct.
Money Laundering / Sanctions and AML Breach

Statutory sanction: Sanctions under the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering framework carry civil penalties up to GIP 500,000 per breach, asset-freezing and forfeiture orders; where money laundering is proven, imprisonment up to seven years, unlimited fines, and publication on the Gibraltar FIU sanctions register.

Director personal liability: The general partner and directors of any corporate partner are personally liable for AML failures. Individual officers face criminal prosecution, permanent disqualification from regulated activities, travel bans while investigations are active, and personal civil liability for damages arising from the proceeds of crime.
Unauthorised Deposit-Taking / EMI Licence Intrusion

Statutory sanction: Offence under the Financial Services (Banking) Act and Electronic Money Regulations, punishable on conviction by a fine of up to GIP 1,000,000 and/or imprisonment up to five years; the GFSC may issue a winding-up petition and require repatriation of all client funds.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner are personally and jointly liable for debts arising from unlawful deposit-taking. Personal asset-confiscation orders, disqualification from holding office in a regulated financial entity, five-year travel bans, and imprisonment are typical outcomes.
