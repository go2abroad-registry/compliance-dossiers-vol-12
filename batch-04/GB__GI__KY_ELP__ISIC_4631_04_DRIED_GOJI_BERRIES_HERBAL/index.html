Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax of Gibraltar and HM Customs Gibraltar (HM Government of Gibraltar), with HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) as the relevant authority for UK-nexus matters. Official portals: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/ (Income Tax Office and Customs) and https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/hm-revenue-customs.

E-government filing portal

A Gibraltar-registered Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) must apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) on Form TRN/APP, attaching the certificate of registration issued by the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies, the executed limited partnership agreement, passport copies and proof of address for each partner, and a compliance certificate signed by the General Partner. Where the ELP is non-resident in Gibraltar, a locally appointed tax liaison officer is required to accept service of tax documents; the TRN is typically issued within 5–10 working days. If the ELP has a UK permanent establishment or UK-source trade income, it must apply separately to HMRC for a UK Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR) and, where required, a UK VAT registration number (VRN).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Gibraltar applies statutory fiscal transparency to limited partnerships: the ELP itself is not a taxpayer, and each partner is assessed on their share of the ELP's Gibraltar-source income. There is no US-style check-the-box election. The ELP may, however, with the unanimous consent of the partners and approval of the Commissioner, elect for centralised assessment (income computed and reported at the ELP level through the General Partner, with the tax liability then allocated pro rata to the partners). Hybrid mismatch participants should file UK hybridity declarations under TIOPA 2010 Part 6A where a partner is UK-resident to prevent disproportionate release or non-inclusion arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Purchase Tax regime in lieu of VAT/GST; narrow customs excise and import duties apply

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar has no VAT/GST and therefore no turnover-based indirect-tax registration threshold. Any trader making customs declarations must hold a valid HM Customs Gibraltar registration, and wholesale botanical trading requires a Business Licence (fee banded by annual turnover). If the ELP holds UK inventory and makes taxable UK supplies, the UK VAT registration threshold is £90,000 (VAT Act 1994, Schedule 1), with mandatory digital registration via HMRC for taxable turnover above the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The ELP may claim zero-rated (out of scope) treatment for dried goji berries (HS 0813.40) and herbal botanicals (HS 1211) supplied to non-Gibraltar customers where: (i) the goods are physically exported from Gibraltar within 90 days of the supply; (ii) the ELP retains freight-forwarding evidence and the customs export declaration; (iii) the buyer is outside Gibraltar and the transaction is a genuine arm's length commercial sale; and (iv) export proceeds are received through a regulated bank. Foodstuffs for human consumption are conditionally relieved from Gibraltar Purchase Tax, and botanical material for pharmaceutical use may enter a customs duty-suspension regime pending actual export.

  • Customs Export Declaration / electronic lodgement receipt from HM Customs Gibraltar
  • Commercial Invoice (three copies) and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or CMR consignment note evidencing physical export
  • Inward Foreign Currency Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 matching the invoiced value
  • Phytosanitary Certificate and Certificate of Origin (non-preferential) for the herbal consignment

Gibraltar does not currently mandate real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction controls, or digital stamping of invoices. Statutory invoices must contain the supplier name, Gibraltar TRN, invoice date and number, buyer identification, quantity, price, and a statement of the applicable Purchase Tax treatment. Customs declarations are lodged electronically with HM Customs Gibraltar, and UK–Gibraltar movements require parallel UK export declarations plus, where applicable, Postponed VAT Accounting for importers into Great Britain.

HM Customs Gibraltar and the Commissioner's audit unit perform post-clearance reviews of export zero-rating claims, with the ELP's General Partner jointly and severally liable for any tax found due. Non-compliant claims attract interest of 7.5% per annum, penalty surcharges of up to 100% of the tax evaded, detention and forfeiture of the goods, revocation of the business licence, and criminal prosecution for willful evasion, carrying fines of up to £10,000 per transaction and/or imprisonment of up to 7 years; refunds and zero-rate benefits are retrospectively clawed back for a six-year assessment period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility prevails. Both the Gibraltar pound and GBP Sterling circulate freely as legal tender, and all major currencies (USD, EUR, CNH, GBP) may be held in ELP accounts without Bank of England or GFSC approval. There are no exchange-control licences, no prior-authorisation steps, no profit-remittance hurdles, and no withholding or local-tax gate on distributions by a tax-transparent Exempted Limited Partnership to non-resident partners. The only compliance encumbrances are FATCA/CRS reporting, UK-Gibraltar automatic exchange of information obligations, and standard anti-money-laundering documentation. Post-Brexit, Gibraltar is treated as a third country for EU capital-movement statistics but remains free of any quantitative restrictions on cross-border transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar law imposes no mandatory surrender, conversion ratio, or statutory repatriation timeline for export receipts. The ELP may retain USD, EUR, GBP, or CNH export proceeds indefinitely in multi-currency accounts. Recommended treasury policy: convert export receipts into the ELP functional currency within 90 days of invoice settlement to minimise FX revaluation volatility; maintain full CRS/FATCA records for each receipt; and ensure all proceeds relate to verifiable goods movements (dried goji berries classified under HS 1211/1212).

Central bank supervision: N/A - Gibraltar does not operate an independent central bank or foreign-exchange administration. Financial supervision is exercised by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the GFSC AML/CFT Handbook. Note issuance is managed by the Government of Gibraltar through the Currency Commissioner, issuing the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at a statutory 1:1 parity with GBP Sterling; monetary stability is anchored to the Bank of England's currency framework without a formal lender-of-last-resort relationship.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-low viability as a primary treasury rail. Gibraltar is not directly integrated into UK Faster Payments or SEPA Instant schemes, so offshore digital EMIs (Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) can only operate via a UK FCA-authorised sponsor or a GFSC-licensed entity; post-Brexit EU passporting no longer applies. These EMIs are suitable as a secondary collection and small-ticket disbursement overlay (multi-currency virtual IBANs, GBP sort-code accounts) but cannot issue documentary letters of credit, provide trade finance, originate SWIFT MT202 cover payments, or clear GIP-denominated instruments. Legal constraint: adoption of an EMI without UK FCA authorisation or GFSC licensing is unlawful for the payment leg; the GFSC bank account must remain the primary settlement account for all bulk trade settlements.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% for Gibraltar companies; an Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) is tax-transparent and is not itself a taxable person. Partners are assessed individually on their share of partnership profits in accordance with the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - No Gibraltar withholding tax on dividends, partnership distributions, or allocations made by an ELP to any partner. Distribution of profits to non-resident or foreign corporate partners is wholly free of Gibraltar WHT.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar has adopted transfer pricing legislation aligned with OECD TPG via the Income Tax (Transfer Pricing) Regulations 2019. Mandatory documentation applies to transactions between associated enterprises where the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds GIP 1,000,000 (approximately EUR 1.1m) per annum; Local File preparation is compulsory where related-party trading volume crosses this threshold or where the Commissioner issues a notice. Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated turnover exceeds EUR 50 million and the Gibraltar entity engages in controlled transactions of GIP 1,000,000 or more. For a mid-sized ELP wholesale operation with related-party sourcing or distributor sales, a proportionate Local File should be maintained even if below the threshold to mitigate penalty risk during an audit.

Economic substance scope: Yes. The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to all entities including Partnerships, effective July 1, 2021. The ELP must demonstrate core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Gibraltar for 'relevant activities' such as distribution and service centres if it derives income from those activities. A wholesale dried-goji and herbal export business that conducts procurement, logistics, sales, and management outside Gibraltar must prepare an annual economic substance return and justify that direction and management (board meetings, strategic decisions) occurs in Gibraltar. Pure 'letterbox' or 'shell' trading entities with no physical presence, no employees, and no substantive activities are in breach and subject to ESR sanctions. Conversely, an active ELP with a real overseas trading operation and no Gibraltar CIGA may be outside the ESR scope if the activity is not 'relevant' or if it is a pure non-resident trading vehicle; this must be documented robustly.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Deliberate Understatement / Omitting Sales)

Statutory sanction: Fine up to GBP 10,000 per offense (or unlimited on indictment), additional penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded, interest at 8% per annum, seizure of assets, and criminal prosecution in the Gibraltar Supremes Court. Offender's name may be published by the Income Tax Office.

Director personal liability: The General Partner (if corporate) and any participating Limited Partner who consents to or facilitates evasion lose limited-liability protection; directors of the corporate General Partner may be personally assessed for the penalty, subjected to travel bans, restrained from leaving Gibraltar, and sentenced to imprisonment of up to 7 years. Personal civil restitution of taxes plus penalties is owed jointly and severally.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance in Gibraltar (ESR Breach)

Statutory sanction: ESR non-compliance: civil penalty of GBP 10,000 per year of non-compliance; repeated default from year 2 attracts GBP 20,000 per year; final default leads to striking-off from the Companies Registry and automatic disqualification of General Partner or its directors. Tax assessments may be reopened to attribute profit to Gibraltar.

Director personal liability: Directors of the General Partner may be personally liable for penalties knowingly paid out of distributable reserves or when the entity is insolvent. Court may issue director disqualification orders (up to 15 years), freeing of assets, and, in cases of fraudulent security, personal contribution orders. Travel bans and contempt of court for non-attendance are available.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Absence of Arm's Length Documentation

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustment (upward assessment) of profits under the Income Tax (Transfer Pricing) Regulations 2019, with late payment interest; penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax liability where the misdeclaration is deliberate; statutory fines of GBP 3,000 per failure to produce documentation; default interest from the due filing date.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve fictitious transfer pricing arrangements may be treated as a manager participating in a tax-default; personal liability for penalties arises under the joint and several rules. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent tax evasion may follow. A travel ban and asset freeze may be imposed where the evasion exceeds GIP 100,000.
AML / Proceeds of Crime Act (POCA) Failure (Banking Red Flags)

Statutory sanction: Under Gibraltar's Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and AML/CFT legislation: fines up to EUR 5 million or 10% of annual global turnover; criminal prosecution for money laundering carrying up to 14 years imprisonment; freezing orders on the ELP's bank accounts; regulatory sanctions against the EMI and the partnership including license suspension.

Director personal liability: General partner directors are 'relevant individuals' under the AML/CFT framework; personal fines up to GBP 2 million per director; disqualification; criminal conviction and imprisonment for up to 14 years; travel bans and seizure of personal assets. Directors may not hide behind corporate veil where they controlled the suspicious transactions.
False Exempt Status / Tax Misrepresentation to Third Parties

Statutory sanction: False statement penalty: up to GBP 3,000 per offense; tax previously avoided becomes payable with interest; the structure loses pass-through protection and is reclassified as a Gibraltar taxable partnership with full assessment; financiers may invalidate credit agreements.

Director personal liability: The director(s) who signed a false declaration of exemption may be criminally liable for fraud (Theft Act), subject to imprisonment of up to 10 years, personal restitution obligation, and a travel ban. The corporate veil is pierced under the Fraud Act 2006 concepts extended to Gibraltar.
