Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Commissioner of Income Tax (Income Tax Office), 231 Main Street, Gibraltar GX11 1AA; primary portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation. Entity registration is separately administered by the Gibraltar Companies House under the Companies Act 2014 and the Limited Partnerships Act 1875.

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity must (i) appoint a local registered agent and maintain a physical registered office in Gibraltar; (ii) file the ELP registration particulars with the Companies House and obtain a Certificate of Registration; (iii) using that certificate, submit Form GIV (Partnership Information Return) to the Income Tax Office to obtain a Gibraltar Identifier (GIB ID) for the partnership and, where required, tax reference numbers for each partner; (iv) for customs purposes, apply for an Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number via HM Customs Gibraltar using the Customs Online Declaration System; (v) where the partnership conducts no taxable activity in Gibraltar, file a nil return annually. The full onboarding cycle is typically 5 to 7 business days.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity must (i) appoint a local registered agent and maintain a physical registered office in Gibraltar; (ii) file the ELP registration particulars with the Companies House and obtain a Certificate of Registration; (iii) using that certificate, submit Form GIV (Partnership Information Return) to the Income Tax Office to obtain a Gibraltar Identifier (GIB ID) for the partnership and, where required, tax reference numbers for each partner; (iv) for customs purposes, apply for an Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number via HM Customs Gibraltar using the Customs Online Declaration System; (v) where the partnership conducts no taxable activity in Gibraltar, file a nil return annually. The full onboarding cycle is typically 5 to 7 business days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Gibraltar domestic law provides no check-the-box election. An Exempted Limited Partnership is statutorily tax-transparent by default: all income, gains, losses and expenses flow through to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement. There is no statutory mechanism to elect entity-level taxation for a Gibraltar ELP. Hybrid mismatch treatment is not subject to a domestic election; instead, the law applies the OECD hybrid mismatch framework only where a foreign jurisdiction (most notably the United Kingdom under TIOPA 2010 Part 6EA) re-characterises the ELP. No additional electoral miracle is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

N/A – Gibraltar does not operate a VAT, GST or general sales/use tax. Indirect taxation is limited to customs duties, excise duties, and stamp duties.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover-based VAT/GST registration threshold exists. Any importer, exporter, or warehouse proprietor must obtain an EORI number from HM Customs Gibraltar; there is no minimum economic-activity threshold. A customs deferment guarantee may be required for duty suspension.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST to zero-rate. For customs duty purposes, goods are relieved from duty when they are exported from Gibraltar and provided the customs export declaration is accepted. Drawback relief is available for duty-paid imported goods subsequently exported within 12 months under the Customs Duties Act 2020. Offshore services of a creative agency, including TikTok creator management and global MCN services, are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax and require no fiscal zero-rating mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Export Declaration / Single Administrative Document) filed with HM Customs Gibraltar
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and underlying creator management contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103/bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds in a Gibraltar or partner jurisdiction account
  • Certificate of Origin where preferential tariff treatment is claimed

Gibraltar imposes no real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or digital stamping mandate. Electronic invoices are permitted if they contain the statutory fields (date, supplier, recipient, description, price, applicable duty/rate) and are retained for six years. There is no API-based tax administration gateway or invoice pre-clearance system.

Gibraltar has no statutory exchange-control deadline; however, for this dossier the 180-day substantiation window is adopted as the standard documentary-evidence benchmark. Where export evidence is missing or falsified, HM Customs Gibraltar may claw back duties plus interest and apply penalties under the Customs Duties Act 2020: a fine up to £250,000 and/or imprisonment up to 7 years for fraudulent declarations. For income tax, failure to preserve records under Section 49(2) of the Income Tax Act 2010 attracts a penalty of £300 plus £60 per day, and the Commissioner may disallow reliefs, require a re-opening of prior returns, and apply up to 50% penalties for negligence or deliberate error.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full current and capital account convertibility. The Gibraltar pound is pegged at parity to sterling and fully backed by Bank of England reserves. Inward equity or debt capital, operating revenues and profit remittances are not subject to exchange control approval. The practical hurdles are prudential only: the receiving bank requires audited or management accounts, a current tax clearance (e-letter) for distributions to non-resident partners, and evidence that funds do not arise from regulated or restricted activities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio and no prescribed holding period. MCN operators and creators may retain foreign-currency invoiced income in any convertible currency and freely disburse it to overseas creators, partners or vendors.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) and the Government of Gibraltar Financial Secretary, with the Bank of England as the monetary anchor for the Gibraltar pound and sterling-convertible currency issuance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No de minimis threshold has been gazetted; all cross-border controlled transactions (including intercompany management fees, intellectual property licensing to the Gibraltar branch, and creator payout allocations) must satisfy the arm's length standard under section 45 of the Income Tax Act 2010. A Local File equivalent, formatted per OECD Chapter V, must be produced within 30 days of a written demand from the Commissioner, irrespective of transaction value; a Master File is required above GIP 10,000,000 of controlled turnover.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to entities undertaking 'relevant activities', which include distribution and service centre business, IP holding, financing, and head office functions. A TikTok MCN branch that merely books global commission income without a Gibraltar board that manages and controls the activity, full-time resident staff whose headcount is proportionate to revenue, and physical premises in Gibraltar will be deemed to have failed the substance test; penalties escalate on repeated failure and lead to compulsory strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 100% of underpaid tax plus interest at 8% per annum; on conviction, unlimited fine and imprisonment up to seven years under section 135 of the Income Tax Act 2010.

Director personal liability: The General Partner and responsible officers of the Cayman ELP are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; the court may order civil restitution, asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment of the controlling mind.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of the tax advantage under the General Anti-Abuse Rule (section 54A) plus a 50% GAAR penalty; ESR fines of GIP 100,000 for the first non-compliance period and up to GIP 300,000 for subsequent periods; compulsory strike-off under the Companies Act 2014.

Director personal liability: Directors and the ELP's General Partner face personal liability for the denied advantage and penalties; the Registrar may impose director disqualification orders, and criminal travel bans apply where assets are removed from Gibraltar.
AML / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Money laundering carries a maximum penalty of 14 years' imprisonment and unlimited fines; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission may impose administrative fines up to GIP 2,000,000 on the corporate entity and issue freezing orders over its bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Each officer is individually culpable under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; confiscation orders attach to personal assets, and the court may impose personal imprisonment alongside the corporate penalty.
Transfer Pricing Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment to the ELP's chargeable profits with interest at 8% per annum and a penalty of up to 100% of the resulting tax; false documentation is a criminal offence carrying imprisonment up to seven years.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the adjusted tax where the ELP is thinly capitalised and unable to pay; exposure remains for 10 years after the relevant accounting period, and non-compliant directors are disqualified from managing Gibraltar entities.
