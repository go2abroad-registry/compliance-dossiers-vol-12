Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, Government of Gibraltar (https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation)

E-government filing portal

Foreign entities must apply for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) via the Income Tax Office. For VAT, registration is required if taxable supplies exceed the statutory threshold. The ELP itself is not a taxable entity for income tax; partners are taxed individually. However, for VAT purposes, the ELP may need to register as a taxable person if it makes taxable supplies in Gibraltar.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods to destinations outside Gibraltar are zero-rated. Services provided to non-residents may also be zero-rated if the recipient is outside Gibraltar and the benefit is enjoyed outside Gibraltar. Evidence of export must be retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate. E-invoicing is accepted but not mandatory. No digital stamping or API-based clearance requirements are in force.

Fraudulent claims for zero-rating may result in penalties up to 200% of the tax evaded, interest, and potential criminal prosecution. Retrospective clawback applies for up to 6 years from the date of the claim.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound is fully convertible in law and practice, pegged one-to-one with GBP, and UK banknotes are legal tender in Gibraltar. Both current and capital account transactions are free of exchange-control restrictions for non-resident and offshore structures, subject to AML/KYC, sanctions screening, CRS/FATCA reporting and the submission of supporting commercial documentation. Profit remittances and distributions from an Exempted Limited Partnership may proceed without exchange-control approval or Gibraltar withholding tax, provided the partnership is not carrying on a taxable Gibraltar trade and beneficial-ownership filings are current.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory surrender or compulsory conversion requirement for export proceeds. Receipts from overseas e-commerce sales may be retained in the original currency, converted at freely negotiated bank rates, or used directly to settle supplier liabilities. Banks nevertheless require the underlying trade documentation for payment validation and will file suspicious transaction reports where the commercial rationale is not evident.

Central bank supervision: HM Government of Gibraltar acting through the Financial Services Commission (FSC), with the Bank of England serving as the monetary anchor for the Gibraltar pound (GIP), which is pegged at par to GBP; Gibraltar has no independent central bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-to-high for Gibraltar-licensed electronic-money institutions, but low for EU/EEA EMIs because Gibraltar no longer benefits from EEA financial-services passporting after Brexit. A Gibraltar-licensed EMI may offer online payment processing, virtual IBANs and card acquiring for the maternity-supplies e-commerce business, but may not hold the senior escrow deposit or execute large trade payments. A fully regulated Gibraltar bank of record remains necessary for capital escrow, FX settlement and supplier disbursement instructions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from investors are routed by SWIFT MT103 into the ELP's segregated Capital Escrow Account at a Gibraltar clearing bank, using GBP, EUR or USD denomination according to the LP capital schedule. The payment purpose code must state 'Capital contribution to limited partnership' or 'Direct investment into trading entity'; the instructing LP must supply a signed capital-contribution notice, the partnership agreement, source-of-funds questionnaire, recent bank statements, and confirmation that the remitting party is not a sanctioned or PEP-restricted person. No exchange-control approval or surrender certificate is required; the receiving bank will verify the payment against the signed drawdown notice before releasing funds to operational working capital.Supplier settlements are executed by SWIFT MT103, MT202COV or documented commercial letters of credit from the Gibraltar bank through its London or EU correspondent clearing network. Sterling payments to UK suppliers utilise CHAPS or Faster Payments; euro disbursements use SEPA-capable euro clearing correspondents or TARGET2; US-dollar payments clear via Fedwire through a New York correspondent. Trade documentation, confirming invoices, bills of lading or delivery receipts must accompany higher-risk payments. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File where annual turnover exceeds GBP 10 million or controlled transactions exceed GBP 1 million; Master File where group turnover exceeds GBP 50 million; the ITO may demand documentation below these thresholds where the arm's length principle is at risk.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations 2019 apply if the ELP undertakes 'distribution and service centre activity' or holds intellectual property; the ELP must demonstrate adequate staff, physical office and partner/board meetings in Gibraltar. Third-party online retail via PSPs is lower risk but must not be operated as a pure pass-through shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Assessment of underpaid tax plus interest; penalty up to 100% of the tax; on conviction a fine of up to GBP 20,000 and imprisonment of up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: The General Partner and shadow directors risk personal civil restitution for diverted partnership receipts, director disqualification up to 15 years, travel bans while tax warrants are outstanding, and imprisonment for fraudulent trading.
Shell Company and Deficient Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR penalties: initial fine up to GBP 100,000 and possible strike-off or deregistration; refusal of the ITO tax residence certificate; exchange of information with HMRC under the UK-Gibraltar arrangement.

Director personal liability: The General Partner and all general partners can be made jointly and severally liable for the ELP's unpaid tax to the extent of assets distributed within 12 months; travel bans may issue pending investigation.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Breaches

Statutory sanction: Beneficial ownership default: fine up to GBP 500 per day; Proceeds of Crime Act 2015: on conviction an unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 5 years for the General Partner as relevant nominated officer.

Director personal liability: De facto directors face personal criminal liability, travel bans and confiscation or restraint orders under the Criminal Finances Act 2021 (UK) applied to Gibraltar; civil restitution to fraud victims may be ordered.
Transfer Pricing and Anti-Hybrid Mismatch

Statutory sanction: Arm's length adjustment to profits, disallowance of related deductions, penalty up to 100% of the under-declared tax plus interest; anti-hybrid rules deny the deduction if the payment is not included in the payee's ordinary income within 12 months.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the pricing arrangement can be assessed for the penalty and disqualified for 10-15 years; if the TP adjustment creates insolvency, the General Partner owes a fiduciary duty to creditors for the deficiency.
