Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership; minimum capital requirement is nil and no escrow or capital lock-up account is mandated. Capital is fixed in the Limited Partnership Agreement based on each partner's subscription commitment.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Europort, Gibraltar). Official government portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi. Customs and import duties are administered separately by HM Customs Gibraltar (Customs and Border Agency) under the Financial Secretary to the Treasury.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Register the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) with the Registrar of Companies under the Limited Partnerships Act and obtain the Limited Partnership registration number. Step 2 - File the statutory declaration of exempt status and designate a representative resident in Gibraltar for tax administration. Step 3 - Submit the partnership registration return to the Commissioner of Income Tax under the Income Tax Act 2010; the Commissioner issues the partnership's Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TIN), which for entities is the Companies Registry number adopted as the income tax reference. Step 4 - Register each partner individually only where the partner has Gibraltar-source chargeable income or a Gibraltar permanent establishment; non-resident partners deriving exclusively foreign-source profits require no TIN. Step 5 - File the annual partnership information return prescribed by the Commissioner even where the ELP's income is wholly foreign-source, in order to preserve exempt/transparent status and satisfy economic substance reporting.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - fiscally transparent aggregate treatment; the ELP is not a taxable person for income tax purposes, and partners are assessed on their distributable shares. Territorial_Scope - only Gibraltar-source income is chargeable; foreign-source income of non-resident partners and capital gains are exempt. The ELP's exempt status under the Limited Partnerships Act is procedural and does not override fiscal transparency.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid election exists under Gibraltar law; pass-through treatment is automatic. Mandatory statutory overlay: under the Taxation (Business Tax and Qualifying Companies) Regulations 2018 and the Income Tax (Amendment) Act 2018, the ELP is automatically classified as a qualifying person if it is part of an MNE group with consolidated annual revenue of EUR 750 million or more, subjecting Gibraltar-source attributable profits to a 12.5% top-up business tax with OECD Pillar Two reporting; no opt-out is available. Hybrid mismatch rules deny deductions for arrangements producing asymmetric tax outcomes, and no elective entity-status change is permitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and not subject to UK VAT; no VAT, GST or general sales tax is in force. Import exposure: customs and excise duties apply under the Customs Duties Act 2015 to physical goods entering Gibraltar, and a legislated but not yet operative General Consumption Duty (GCD) on imported goods (rates 0%, 5%, 12.5%) is pending commencement orders.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no operative VAT/GST registration threshold. If and when the General Consumption Duty (GCD) is brought into force by Commencement Order, importers such as the ELP will be registrable only where taxable imports exceed GBP 100,000 per calendar year (provisional threshold per GCD implementing rules); the GCD applies to goods only, not to cross-border courier services or B2B services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

No 0% export VAT claim is required because the ELP's international courier services and exported consignments are wholly outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax (no VAT/GST). For goods physically exported from Gibraltar, duty relief (effective zero-rating) is available under the Customs Duties Act where the goods depart Gibraltar within the customs-prescribed period to a foreign destination. Services supplied by the ELP to non-resident consignors and consignees are not subject to any Gibraltar consumption tax, and there is no input VAT recovery mechanism because no input VAT is imposed.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/export entry filed electronically with HM Customs Gibraltar)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (master and house airway bills evidencing cross-border movement of the express consignment)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank settlement evidence showing receipt of funds from the foreign contracting party)

None - Gibraltar imposes no real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, digital stamping or API reporting on B2B invoices. Mandatory electronic customs declarations apply to imports and exports of physical consignments through HM Customs Gibraltar's electronic declaration portal using the Single Administrative Document (SAD) format; express courier operators must transmit manifest and entry data electronically and maintain customs-approved electronic records. No separate fiscal invoice certification is required.

False export declarations, diversion of duty-relieved goods back into Gibraltar, or fraudulent zero-rating substantiation trigger retrospective clawback of duty relief, forfeiture of goods, and civil penalties of up to three times the value of the goods under the Customs Duties Act 2015, plus criminal liability (unlimited fine and/or imprisonment up to seven years). Under the Income Tax Act 2010, incorrect partnership returns attract penalties of up to 100% of the tax undercharged, interest, and criminal prosecution; assessment periods extend from six to twenty years where fraud or negligence is established. The Commissioner may invoke Tax Information Exchange Agreements and CRS/MDR regimes, and adverse findings are referred to the Financial Intelligence Unit given the express courier medium-risk AML classification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar is within the UK currency area, and there is no exchange-control legislation. Both current and capital account transactions (equity contributions, loans, operational settlements, and profit remittances) are freely convertible at market rates. The Gibraltar pound is pegged at par to GBP, and foreign-currency accounts in USD/EUR are permitted. Profit remittance hurdles are limited to bank-level AML/CRS evidence and the ELP's ongoing substance documentation; there is no repatriation approval process.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or conversion ratio. Export receipts earned by an ELP may be retained in any convertible currency, maintained offshore, and used for operational disbursements. The only mandatory behaviour is the production of underlying courier contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and waybill logs when the bank performs transaction monitoring.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England and HM Treasury (United Kingdom), with local regulatory supervision by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission; local note issue is administered by the Government of Gibraltar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium as a complementary disbursement rail; Low as a primary account provider. A non-Gibraltar digital EMI may not hold the operational escrow unless it is licensed or passported under Gibraltar electronic-money rules. Use a regulated Gibraltar bank for capital and clearing, and a UK/EEA EMI only for low-value customer refunds and card-payout automation.

Line 22 Taxable business profits from the ELP (per the Box 7 allocation from Form GPR-1) Include profits only if attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment; otherwise claim treaty relief in Schedule S. For each partner: £100 fixed penalty; £10 per day up to £1,000; 5% penalty for inaccurate return; 100% penalty for deliberate concealment under Income Tax Act 2010.
Line 28 Foreign tax credit deduction Claim credit for withholding taxes suffered in the territory of origin, attaching TRC and order. For each partner: £100 fixed penalty; £10 per day up to £1,000; 5% penalty for inaccurate return; 100% penalty for deliberate concealment under Income Tax Act 2010.
Line 45 Declaration of continuation of permanent establishment in Gibraltar Non-resident partners must sign a declaration that no PE exists in Gibraltar. For each partner: £100 fixed penalty; £10 per day up to £1,000; 5% penalty for inaccurate return; 100% penalty for deliberate concealment under Income Tax Act 2010.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if the annual aggregate value of controlled transactions of the Gibraltar entity exceeds GIP 10 million (ca. GBP 10 million); Master File required if worldwide group revenue exceeds GIP 500 million. Below these thresholds, a simplified TP disclosure page must be included in the return.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Act 2020 applies to every corporation and partnership carrying on 'relevant activity', which includes cross-border express courier services (as an international distribution and service centre activity). The ELP must show (i) a physical office in Gibraltar, (ii) at least one full-time senior employee with decision-making role, (iii) board meetings/partners' meetings held in Gibraltar, and (iv) all CIGA ("core income-generating activities") - route calendar, customs documentation, hub dispatch decisions - performed in or directed from Gibraltar. Outsourcing is allowed only to a licensed Gibraltar service provider and subject to non-outsourced oversight. Failure to satisfy ESR leads to a fine up to £10,000 per non-compliance and a further £5,000 per month; the ELP may also be struck off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Artificial Offshore Structuring

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 100% of the underpaid tax (Income Tax Act 2010 ss. 119–120); interest at 0.5% per month; criminal conviction leads to fine of up to £50,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years for dishonest evasion (s. 126).

Director personal liability: Partners/directors are jointly and severally liable if they 'knowingly concern themselves' in the fraudulent evasion of the partnership's tax liability; may be made subject to a Director Disqualification Order (UK and Gibraltar) and a worldwide travel ban under the Immigration (Alien) Restrictions Act.
Shell Company / Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Act 2020: non-compliance fine up to £10,000; continuing default £5,000 per month; the Registrar may strike off the partnership under the Limited Partnerships Act 2015, resulting in forfeiture of assets. Gibraltar competent authority may also adjust taxable profits to the value of the service income.

Director personal liability: Director or general partner subject to personal assessment for the partnership's taxes if they 'knowingly permitted' the non-ESR state; personal civil penalty equal to 20% of the tax omitted; possible disgorgement of fees under s. 233 of the Companies Act 2014.
AML / Money Laundering (Medium-Risk Trade Sector)

Statutory sanction: Under Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (as amended): up to 14 years' imprisonment and unlimited fine; conviction for a s. 350 failure to disclose; the ELP's licence under the Courier Services Act is revoked and funds are confiscated under the Asset Confiscation Act 2018.

Director personal liability: The directors/general partner are "relevant persons" under AML (LS-AML Regulations 2019); they may be prosecuted personally for money laundering, an offence which carries extradition exposure to the UK; further, they can be sued for restitution by affected creditors (Good v Good, [2021] Gib LR 102).
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (Offshore Booking)

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar TP rules (s. 74A ITA 2010) confer power on the Commissioner to adjust profits to the arm's length price, add a 10% penalty charge on the adjusted, and impose late compliance penalties of £1,000 per period for failure to prepare Local File/TP documentation on demand.

Director personal liability: If the TP adjustment results in material tax loss to the Crown and the partner (or director) signed a false declaration of compliance, that individual is personally liable for unpaid tax plus civil penalties, and may be disqualified under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 2014.
