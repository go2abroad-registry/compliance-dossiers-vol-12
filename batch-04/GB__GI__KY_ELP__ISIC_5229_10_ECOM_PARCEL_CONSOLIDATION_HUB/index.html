Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar, 32 Governor's Lane, Gibraltar GX11 1AA (official portal: www.gibraltar.gov.gi); indirect tax and customs: HM Customs of Gibraltar, Town Range, Gibraltar (operating the Gibraltar Trade Single Window, GTSW). Statutory basis: Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended), Customs Duties Act 2010 (as amended), Limited Partnerships Act 2015.

E-government filing portal

STEP 1: File a Statement of Registration with the Registrar of Companies (Companies House Gibraltar) under the Limited Partnerships Act 2015, expressly electing Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) status in the partnership deed and registration particulars. STEP 2: Obtain the Certificate of Registration confirming ELP status; this certificate constitutes the entity's statutory existence and tax-transparency trigger. STEP 3: Apply to the Income Tax Office for the ELP's Tax Reference Number (TRN) by submitting the partnership return registration form, certified copy of the partnership deed, Certificate of Registration, and a declaration identifying the general partner, all limited partners, profit-sharing ratios, and the designated 'authorised partner' responsible for tax filings. STEP 4: Each non-resident partner must obtain a personal/corporate Gibraltar TRN prior to deriving any Gibraltar-source income; partners without Gibraltar-source income may be recorded by country-of-residence TIN. STEP 5: If goods will cross the Gibraltar frontier, register separately with HM Customs Gibraltar for a Gibraltar EORI number and, where applicable, a customs comprehensive guarantee. Typical end-to-end completion: 2 to 4 weeks. No minimum capital, no economic-substance filing at registration, no statutory license required for the parcel-consolidation scope.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible / Territorial_Scope. The ELP is fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act 2010: it is not a separate taxable person and pays no Gibraltar corporation tax. Each partner is chargeable on its agreed share of partnership profits. Non-resident partners are taxable only on Gibraltar-source income (income attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment, Gibraltar land, or Gibraltar employment); all foreign-source income is outside the charge. There is no Gibraltar capital gains tax, no wealth or inheritance tax, and no withholding tax on distributions or interest paid by the ELP. Tax_Exempt_Zone status does not apply to this ELP.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists; tax transparency is mandatory. The sole election is the irrevocable designation as an ELP made at registration under ss.21-24 of the Limited Partnerships Act 2015. The partners may elect, through the authorised partner, to file a single composite partnership return covering all partners' shares, or each partner may file separately. No Gibraltar hybrid-mismatch election is available; Gibraltar has not domestically enacted OECD BEPS Action 2, but the general anti-avoidance rule (s.42 Income Tax Act 2010) can recharacterize arrangements lacking commercial substance. UK-CTA-2010-Part-6A hybrid rules may apply at the level of any UK-resident partner to neutralize a UK/Gibraltar hybrid mismatch.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / No GST / No Sales and Use Tax. Gibraltar is excluded from the EU VAT territory (Protocol 3 of the 1972 Act of Accession; Article 28 TFEU, position continued post-Brexit) and imposes no value-added or general consumption tax. Indirect taxation on the parcel-consolidation business is limited to customs import duties and excise duties (alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oils) under the Customs Duties Act 2010, as amended. Freight, sorting, consolidation, and line-haul services are outside the scope of all Gibraltar indirect taxes.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A — there is no VAT registration threshold or VAT registration mechanism in Gibraltar. For customs purposes, registration with HM Customs Gibraltar for a Gibraltar EORI number is mandatory for any economic operator importing or exporting goods through Gibraltar, with no monetary threshold. Low-value non-commercial consignments may qualify for de minimis duty relief; the parcel hub itself acts as a logistics service provider and is not generally the importer or exporter of record.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

N/A under Gibraltar law because Gibraltar imposes no VAT, and therefore no 0% VAT zero-rating or refund mechanism exists. Goods exported from Gibraltar attract zero customs duty and no Gibraltar consumption tax; services supplied by the ELP to non-resident e-commerce merchants are outside the scope of Gibraltar taxation. Where the ELP's services are received by UK or EU established merchants, the customer's jurisdiction determines VAT via reverse charge (UK VATA 1994 s.7A; EU VAT Directive art.196), with no Gibraltar collection obligation. In the direct-injection parcel model, UK/EU import VAT and customs obligations fall on the importer of record in the UK/EU, not on the Gibraltar ELP.

None. Gibraltar has no real-time fiscal clearance regime, no mandatory B2G e-invoicing, no digital stamping, and no tax-authority API clearance requirement. Customs declarations are lodged electronically through the Gibraltar Trade Single Window (GTSW) using the Single Administrative Document (SAD); this is a customs clearance and statistical gateway only, not a fiscal e-invoicing mandate. Ordinary commercial invoicing obligations apply under contract law.

No export zero-rating clawback or VAT fraud penalty can arise given the absence of VAT. Customs risks: false or negligent declarations to HM Customs Gibraltar attract forfeiture of goods, recovery of duty underpaid, and monetary penalties up to the statutory maximum under the Customs Duties Act 2010. Income-tax risks: abuse of the ELP pass-through structure or concealment of Gibraltar-source income allows the Income Tax Office to raise assessments within six years (twenty years in cases of fraud or neglect), charge interest at the statutory rate, and impose penalties up to 100% of the tax undercharged. The general anti-avoidance rule (s.42 Income Tax Act 2010) may strip the pass-through benefit where the ELP lacks commercial substance or has been interposed mainly to obtain a tax advantage. Non-resident partner relief is available only where no Gibraltar permanent establishment exists and income has no Gibraltar source.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains a currency-board-style regime in which the Gibraltar pound is freely convertible at a fixed 1:1 rate into pound sterling. There is no legal distinction between capital-account and current-account convertibility for balance-of-payments purposes. Profit remittance by an Exempted Limited Partnership to non-resident limited partners is unrestricted, subject only to standard anti-money-laundering verification, tax residence certificates, and the absence of any unpaid Gibraltar tax liability. Foreign-currency accounts may be held without central-bank approval, and no exit tax, withholding tax, or repatriation licence is required.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Gibraltar imposes no statutory surrender obligation on export proceeds, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for repatriation. The effective mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent; exporters and e-commerce parcel hubs may retain foreign currency in licensed domestic bank accounts or regulated escrow wrappers, with standard suspicious-transaction reporting thresholds under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 applying.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the licensing and conduct regulator; monetary anchor provided by the Bank of England for sterling, with no standalone Gibraltar exchange-control department.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High as an ancillary liquidity rail. A Gibraltar-incorporated ELP may use UK FCA-regulated electronic money institutions such as Airwallex for multi-currency receivables, supplier payments, and parcel linehaul settlement through Faster Payments, CHAPS, and SEPA. Legal constraints: the EMI cannot serve as the sole capital escrow account for statutory, audit, or GFSC-supervised purposes; the EMI must complete its own KYB/AML onboarding of the ELP; and any residual balance must ultimately be settleable into a licensed Gibraltar domestic bank. Recommended structure is a dual-banking model: domestic Gibraltar bank for capital preservation and statutory escrow, plus Airwallex virtual accounts for high-velocity transaction flows.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

10% standard corporation tax on trading profits; profits classified as passive income (interest, IP royalties, rents, dividends) are taxed at the 20% passive rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividends or partnership profit distributions paid to non-resident partners or shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed within 12 months after the end of the accounting period; for a 31 December year-end the statutory filing deadline is 31 December of the following calendar year. Final corporation tax is due and payable 9 months and 1 day after the accounting period end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Master File and Local File where the multinational group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds GIP 750 million (OECD BEPS Action 13 threshold); a Gibraltar Local File is also required where related-party transactions exceed GIP 1 million per annum.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations apply to Gibraltar tax-resident entities carrying on relevant activities, which include distribution and logistics support services, headquarters and service-centre functions, and IP exploitation. The parcel consolidation hub must demonstrate: (i) effective management and direction from Gibraltar; (ii) adequate full-time local employees, premises, and operating expenditure proportionate to the sorting and linehaul activity; (iii) core income-generating activities performed locally, including parcel sorting supervision, linehaul contracting, customs compliance management, and payment-rail reconciliation; and (iv) an annual economic substance return filed with the Registrar of Companies and Income Tax Office. Non-compliance attracts financial penalties and, for significant breach, mandatory strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Deliberate Concealment of Gibraltar-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded plus interest at 8% per annum; criminal conviction on indictment attracts a fine of up to GIP 50,000 and imprisonment of up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Where the ELP is deemed a sham or facade, courts pierce the corporate veil; the general partner and its directors are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of unpaid tax, asset freezing orders, travel bans, disqualification, and imprisonment for fraudulent conduct.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Regulation Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR financial penalty of GIP 10,000 per breach escalating to GIP 100,000 for significant failure, compulsory strike-off of the ELP, and an adverse transfer pricing re-determination of the hub's taxable profits.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner face disqualification orders of up to 5 years, personal penalty notices for continuing ESR breach, and direct liability to discharge the partnership's unpaid tax where the entity is treated as an artificial arrangement.
AML/CTF Breach in Payment Rails and Cross-Border Flows

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 with unlimited fines and imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering; Financial Services Commission administrative penalties of up to GIP 2 million for regulatory compliance failures.

Director personal liability: Directors who consent to or negligently permit AML failures face personal criminal liability, civil asset forfeiture, travel bans during investigation, and exclusion from acting as director or general partner of any regulated Gibraltar entity.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to arm's length profit, statutory interest on underpaid tax at 8% per annum, penalty of up to 100% of the adjustment amount, and issuance of compulsory documentation orders.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are personally liable where transfer pricing documentation is falsified; if the arrangement constitutes fraudulent trading under Gibraltar insolvency law, they face personal restitution, disqualification, and custodial penalties.
Recurring Late Filing, Non-Payment, and Dissolution Fraud

Statutory sanction: Daily default penalties, interest accrual, distraint and enforcement proceedings against partnership assets, criminal sanctions for fraudulent winding-up, and statutory tax certificates enabling direct collection against the general partner's assets.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner receive personal liability notices for tax debts arising from negligence or fraudulent avoidance; courts may impose travel bans, forfeiture orders, director disqualification, and custodial sentences of up to 7 years for deliberate evasion.
