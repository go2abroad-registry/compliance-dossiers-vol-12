Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar; official Government portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi. Import duties and excise matters are administered by the Customs and Excise Department, HM Customs Gibraltar.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership must: (1) instruct a licensed Gibraltar corporate service provider and maintain a registered office in Gibraltar; (2) file the partnership registration application under the Gibraltar Limited Partnerships legislation with the Registrar of Companies and obtain a Gibraltar partnership registration number; (3) register the partnership for tax at the Income Tax Office by submitting the prescribed partnership registration particulars, thereby obtaining a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number; (4) identify each general and limited partner, supplying the partner's foreign TIN or an explanation where no TIN is held; and (5) if importing or holding duty-suspended or excise goods, register separately with the Customs and Excise Department. There is no separate Gibraltar VAT registration or VAT ID because Gibraltar does not operate a VAT/GST system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. The Exempted Limited Partnership is tax-transparent by default under Gibraltar income tax legislation; each partner is separately chargeable on their distributive share of partnership profits. Statutory and administrative elections are limited to the tax treatment of non-resident partners' Gibraltar-source income, the allocation of profits under the partnership agreement, and anti-hybrid/compliance elections required to respect OECD BEPS Action 2 and prevent double non-taxation. The partnership cannot elect to be taxed as an entity-level company without changing its legal form.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar_No_General_Consumption_Tax_No_VAT_GST_or_Sales_Use_Tax

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT/GST purposes; there is no turnover-based indirect-tax registration threshold in Gibraltar. Registration obligations arise under customs/excise law for businesses importing or holding excisable goods, and not from ordinary ghost-kitchen or food-delivery revenues. If the Gibraltar entity separately carries on a UK taxable business, UK VAT registration may be required at the standard GBP 85,000 turnover threshold or sooner for non-established businesses.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Gibraltar because there is no VAT/GST regime and therefore no statutory zero-rating claim exists for goods or services. Goods physically exported from Gibraltar are outside the domestic excise duty base only when the applicable customs export procedure is completed. Offshore services supplied to non-resident recipients are not subject to Gibraltar consumption tax. If the entity is separately registered for UK VAT through a UK fixed establishment, UK export zero-rating rules may apply to qualifying goods and services under UK legislation.

  • Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate

Gibraltar does not impose a mandatory electronic-invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API connectivity, or digital stamping regime for domestic supplies. Ordinary delivery and food-service invoices may be issued without fiscal authorisation. Customs import/export declarations must be filed electronically with HM Customs Gibraltar, and platform settlement data from UberEats, DoorDash or similar operators should be retained as contemporaneous books-and-records evidence.

Because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST, there is no VAT export-clawback mechanism; however, false or incomplete customs declarations can result in recovery of unpaid duty, penalties of up to 100% of the duty value, forfeiture of goods, and criminal prosecution. Fraudulent misdescription of export transactions may also reopen Gibraltar income tax assessments beyond the normal six-year window, with unlimited time limits in cases of deliberate non-disclosure. Intercompany payments connected with export or platform revenues should be supported by contemporaneous contracts, transfer-pricing documentation, and foreign currency repatriation evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar issues its own currency, the Gibraltar pound (GIP), legally pegged 1:1 to the British pound sterling (GBP), with GIP and GBP banknotes circulating interchangeably. Both current-account flows (trade settlements, delivery-platform payouts, royalties, management fees) and capital-account flows (partner capital contributions, profit distributions, redemption/repurchase of partnership interests) are fully convertible without prior authorisation, retention limits, or central bank approval. Profit remittance is unrestricted; only standard FATCA/CRS reporting, GFSC transaction monitoring, and sanctions screening duties apply. Non-resident partners may hold, settle, and remit in GBP, EUR, or USD without any mandatory conversion ratio or balance-sheet conversion mandate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Gibraltar and the United Kingdom impose no statutory surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline on export or operating receipts. All delivery-platform settlements (UberEats, DoorDash) may be retained in the receipt currency; any voluntary conversion to GBP/EUR is governed solely by commercial treasury policy and documented in the partnership's cash-management and risk framework.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of England (monetary anchor for sterling and ultimate lender-of-last-resort for the Gibraltar pound) and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) (prudential and AML/CTF supervisor of all banks and payment institutions in Gibraltar). Gibraltar has no separate Central Bank or Foreign Exchange Administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium. GFSC-authorised Electronic Money Institutions and UK-FCA-authorised EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, specialised fintech cash managers) are viable as clearing-only sub-ledgers for rapid cross-border settlement of ghost-kitchen payouts, but they cannot serve as the primary regulated custody rail for the Capital Escrow Account because they are not licensed deposit-takers. Legal constraints: EMI funds must be safeguarded under e-money regulations and are not protected by the Gibraltar Deposit Guarantee Scheme; EMI accounts require an upstream top-up and downstream sweep from a Tier 1 licensed bank; and GFSC AML/CTF rules require a regulated local introducing agent to complete the beneficial-ownership and source-of-funds dossier. Feasibility rating: Medium - high utility for operational float, low utility for statutory capital escrow.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All partner capital injections must be routed by SWIFT MT103 to the Tier 1 Capital Escrow Account or Operational Account, with Field 70 narrative: 'Capital Contribution to Gibraltar ELP - [Partnership Legal Name]', and, where supported by the correspondent bank, ISO 20022 payment purpose code CAPC (capital payment) or INTC (intra-company transfer) as a fallback. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar does not impose statutory dividend withholding tax on distributions made by a Gibraltar entity, including an ELP, to its partners.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GBP 10,000,000 — mandatory Local File is triggered where annual gross turnover of the Gibraltar taxpayer exceeds GBP 10,000,000; a Master File is required when the multinational group’s consolidated annual revenue exceeds GBP 50,000,000, and Country-by-Country notification is due when the group exceeds GBP 750,000,000 in consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Applicable. The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations 2019 require resident companies and partnerships engaging in relevant activities to maintain core income-generating activities, premises, employees, board meetings and operating expenditure in Gibraltar. A virtual-brand ghost-kitchen model falls within the ‘distribution and service centre’ relevant activity where order aggregation, delivery logistics, brand management and customer settlement occur in Gibraltar. The ELP must document staffing, premises, service contracts and decision-making; failure renders the arrangement a shell company and triggers denial of tax residence benefits, recharacterisation and civil sanctions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Underdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Civil tax-geared penalty of 100% to 200% of the additional tax due; statutory interest at base rate plus 2% per annum; late-filing surcharges up to GBP 1,000; criminal prosecution under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act carrying an unlimited fine and imprisonment for up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: The general partner and every person concerned in the management of the ELP may be assessed personally where the partnership is treated as an artificial arrangement. Courts may pierce the partnership veil, impose joint and several restitution orders, issue travel bans for tax absconders, and disqualify individuals from acting as directors or general partners for up to 15 years.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may deny the territoriality benefit by asserting a Gibraltar permanent establishment or chargeable presence, recharacterise circular payments, impose fines under the Economic Substance Regulations of up to GBP 500,000, and strike the partnership from the Gibraltar Register. Professional enablers may be fined under the general anti-abuse framework.

Director personal liability: The general partner and limited partners participating in management face personal liability for unpaid tax and penalties. Directors or managers may be subject to disqualification, civil recovery of assets exported from Gibraltar, and travel restrictions where unpaid tax exceeds statutory thresholds.
Money Laundering / AML Control Failure

Statutory sanction: Breach of the Gibraltar Criminal Justice (Money Laundering) Act and the Proceeds of Crime Act exposes the ELP to fines up to GBP 1,000,000, asset forfeiture, confiscation of platform balances, and suspension or revocation of the statutory ghost-kitchen operating licence.

Director personal liability: Individual officers or general partners may be imprisoned for up to 14 years for money laundering offences; directors may be ordered to pay compensation for funds lost to suspicious transactions; travel bans and international asset-freezing orders may be imposed pending criminal investigation or extradition proceedings.
