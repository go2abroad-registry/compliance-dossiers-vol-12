Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar, Income Tax Office (ITO), Ministry of Finance, 221 Main Street, Gibraltar; official e-services portal: HM Government of Gibraltar Tax E-Services (https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax); entity registration counterpart: Gibraltar Registrar of Companies (Companies House Gibraltar) and HM Customs and Excise Department for customs/EORI matters.

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) that is non-resident and does not carry on a trade in Gibraltar has no automatic obligation to register for Gibraltar Income Tax. If the ELP commences commercial inspection/testing activity with a Gibraltar presence (office, employees, or local agency), it must: (1) register the non-resident partnership as an Overseas Limited Partnership with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Limited Partnerships Act 1996 within 21 days of establishing the branch; (2) obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference (GTR) by submitting Form 10 (Statement of Liability to Income Tax) to the Income Tax Office, together with the partnership deed, Cayman registration certificate, and, if prepared, audited financial statements, no later than 30 days after commencement of commercial activity; (3) obtain a Business Licence from the Gibraltar Business Licensing Authority under the Business Licensing Act 1985, as the commercial inspection and QC agency constitutes a licensable business; (4) register with HM Customs and Excise for a Gibraltar EORI number if any physical goods movement occurs; and (5) file an annual partnership return (Form P/S Statement) to the ITO even if a nil outcome is expected.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible with Territorial_Scope: under the Income Tax Act 2010, partnerships are fiscally transparent; the ELP itself is not a taxable person for income tax purposes, and each partner is assessable on their distributable share of Gibraltar-source profits only. Non-resident partners deriving income wholly outside Gibraltar are outside the territorial scope. The ELP remains a separate legal person for customs, contractual, and liability purposes.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. Available mechanisms include: (i) an application to the Commissioner of Income Tax under section 11(2) of the Income Tax Act 2010 for a partnership to be treated as a separate taxable entity where it is treated as such in its jurisdiction of incorporation; (ii) alignment of tax transparency status under the Taxation (Hybrid Mismatch Rules) Act 2019 (OECD BEPS Action 2), restricting inconsistent treatment between Gibraltar and the Cayman Islands; (iii) partner-level elections on Form P for the basis of assessment and loss relief; and (iv) for non-resident partners, an election to file a Form N return for their Gibraltar-source allocable profits to obtain a tax clearance certificate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST in Gibraltar; indirect taxation comprises Import Duty, Excise Duty (alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oils), and customs-related levies administered by HM Customs and Excise, Gibraltar.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. For customs duty purposes, every importer or exporter of commercial goods must obtain a Gibraltar EORI number; there is no minimum revenue threshold. Excise duty registration is required only for producers/importers of alcohol, tobacco, and fuel, which is not relevant to a pre-shipment inspection and QC agency.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in the VAT sense, because Gibraltar does not levy VAT. Exports of goods from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Import Duty (duty-free) provided the goods physically depart Gibraltar under an approved customs export declaration. For services (on-site factory pre-shipment quality control, inspection, and audit services rendered to a non-resident principal), the fees are excluded from Gibraltar-source income to the extent the testing and inspection work is performed outside Gibraltar; any portion of the service physically performed in Gibraltar remains taxable under the territorial scope. Duty drawback is available only where imported goods are subsequently re-exported in an unchanged state under the Customs Drawback Regulations.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/C1) lodged via the Gibraltar Customs Declaration System
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) evidencing foreign currency receipt and conversion
  • Commercial Invoice and packing list
  • Inspection/QC report and underlying service contract
  • Duty suspension authorization or drawback certificate where relief is claimed

Gibraltar imposes no real-time electronic invoicing, no fiscal clearance mandate, no digital stamping, and no Continuous Transaction Control (CTC) regime. Commercial invoices are governed by ordinary Income Tax Act record-keeping obligations (records must be retained for 6 years from the end of the relevant accounting period). Electronic customs declarations must be filed through the Gibraltar Customs Declaration System (CDS) aligned to the UK tariff, but there is no continuous tax control framework or mandatory API-based reporting to the ITO.

Although there is no VAT zero-rating clawback, fraudulent customs export declarations to obtain duty relief or drawback are prosecuted under the Customs and Excise Act 1962, with forfeiture of goods, penalties up to three times the value of the goods, and imprisonment of up to two years. Misrepresentation to support an income exclusion is revenue fraud under the Income Tax Act 2010, leading to additional assessments, interest, penalties up to 100% of understated tax, and a maximum fine of £3,000. The Commissioner may retrospectively unwind pass-through attributions within 6 years for negligent filings, with no time limit where fraud is established. Duty drawback claims are subject to a four-year post-clearance audit window with a 10% post-clearance demand surcharge.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP is fully convertible at 1:1 to GBP with no capital account restrictions for non-resident partners. The ELP may freely hold, settle, and remit in GBP, EUR, and USD. Profit repatriation is unrestricted, subject only to standard GFSC-regulated banking verification. No statutory requirement exists to convert foreign currency receipts into GIP. As a pass-through entity, the ELP is transparent for Gibraltar income tax; partners are assessed only on their profit share where Gibraltar-source income arises. No VAT applies in Gibraltar.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Gibraltar imposes no surrender, retention, repatriation timeline, or mandatory conversion ratio on export or foreign-currency receipts. The quality-control agency's foreign-currency proceeds (GBP, EUR, USD, CNH) may be held indefinitely in non-resident accounts without regulatory penalty.

Central bank supervision: Financial Services Commission (GFSC) – the integrated regulator for banking and financial services in Gibraltar. The Government of Gibraltar is the issuer of the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) under the Currency Notes Act 1934, with GIP circulating at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP. No separate central bank exists; prudential cooperation is maintained through HM Treasury, the Bank of England, and the FCA under the Constitution of Gibraltar 2006 and related supervisory memoranda.

Box 3 (Accounting Information) Statement of total assessable profits or losses from the Gibraltar trade, including gross fee income from factory inspection, testing, and QC services. Only include profits attributed to a Gibraltar permanent establishment or to services physically carried out in Gibraltar. Late filing: immediate penalty of £100 plus £10 per day for each day of continued non-compliance, up to maximum £1,000; inaccurate return without reasonable excuse: up to 100% of tax understated; non-filing after assessment: up to £2,000 and potential criminal conviction.
Box 4 (Adjustments for Tax Purposes) Addbacks and deductions to accounting profit to arrive at assessable profit, including transfer pricing adjustments and disallowed expenses. Non-resident partners must ensure no expense deduction exceeds arm's length limits in respect of transactions with related parties. Late filing: immediate penalty of £100 plus £10 per day for each day of continued non-compliance, up to maximum £1,000; inaccurate return without reasonable excuse: up to 100% of tax understated; non-filing after assessment: up to £2,000 and potential criminal conviction.
Box 5 (Tax Loss Carry-Forward Claim) Claim for loss relief for losses arising in previous chargeable years, capped at a 5-year carry-forward period. Losses can be claimed by the partnership and allocated to partners; non-resident partners should verify that the losses are attributable to Gibraltar-source activities. Late filing: immediate penalty of £100 plus £10 per day for each day of continued non-compliance, up to maximum £1,000; inaccurate return without reasonable excuse: up to 100% of tax understated; non-filing after assessment: up to £2,000 and potential criminal conviction.
Box 6 (Double Tax Relief / DTA Claim) Foreign tax credit or treaty relief claimed against Gibraltar tax on the same income. Attach Gibraltar DTA claims and any certificate of foreign tax paid; partner-level treaty entitlement is assessed individually. Late filing: immediate penalty of £100 plus £10 per day for each day of continued non-compliance, up to maximum £1,000; inaccurate return without reasonable excuse: up to 100% of tax understated; non-filing after assessment: up to £2,000 and potential criminal conviction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File / Master File applies where aggregate turnover exceeds GIP 10 million and related-party transactions exceed GIP 1 million in a tax year; for an ELP operating a factory inspection agency, this is measured at the combined activity level of all partners.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Act 2019 applies. Factory inspection and QC testing constitutes 'geographically mobile activity' only if management is influenced by intangible property; in standard factory inspection agency arrangements, the activity is treated as 'pure equity holding' or 'corporate services' only if no substantive local operations. The ELP must demonstrate adequate officers, premises, and expenditure in Gibraltar to avoid being considered a 'jurisdiction non-resident company'. For non-resident partners, no ESR arises on the respective partner's profit share, but if the ELP has a commercial presence in Gibraltar, an ESR notification must be filed annually.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion & Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: On conviction: fine up to GIP 100,000; imprisonment up to 7 years; confiscation order of up to twice the tax lost; revocation of any tax clearances.

Director personal liability: Directors, and ELP general partners, may be jointly and severally liable for restitution of unpaid tax and interest; travel bans and asset freezing orders under Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; imprisonment for up to 10 years for fraudulent evasion of income tax.
Shell Company Arrangement / Wholly Artificial Structure

Statutory sanction: Under Economic Substance Act 2019: penalties up to GIP 10,000 for non-compliance, GIP 20,000 for continuing default, and possible strike-off/winding-up; under general anti-abuse rules (GAAR), assessment adjustments and penalties up to 100% of the tax advantage.

Director personal liability: General partners who fail to maintain economic substance face disqualification as director; civil liability for the ELP's unpaid tax; courts may issue a director's personal compliance order and a worldwide asset freezing injunction.
AML / POCA Breach

Statutory sanction: Under Proceeds of Crime Act (Gibraltar) 2015: unlimited fines and imprisonment up to 14 years for failing to disclose suspicious activity; regulatory fines by the GFSC up to GIP 2 million.

Director personal liability: General partner (a natural person) is treated as a 'criminal' and subject to custodial sentence; the ELP's assets may be confiscated, and directors' personal bank accounts can be frozen at the first stage of investigation.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: TP adjustment disallowing deductions up to the arm's length deviation; penalty of 20% of the tax differential; late documentation penalty up to GIP 5,000.

Director personal liability: Directors as tax decision-makers may be jointly liable for the TP tax adjustment if they failed to take reasonable care; disqualification orders for repeated non-compliance.
