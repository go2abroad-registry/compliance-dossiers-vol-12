Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the ELP is fiscally transparent; partners are taxed on their distributive shares of Gibraltar-source income; a non-resident ELP with no Gibraltar-source profits is outside the charge on territorial-scope grounds; no Tax_Exempt_Zone status applies.

Available election forms

Gibraltar law provides a statutory election for a limited partnership to be treated as a body corporate for income tax purposes; absent that election, default pass-through treatment applies. No US-style check-the-box election and no domestic hybrid-mismatch election are available; the EU ATAD is not directly applicable in Gibraltar. The election should be made before the first return filing and evaluated alongside the ELP's non-residence position.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - Gibraltar does not levy VAT, GST, or sales/use tax; indirect taxation is confined to Customs Import Duty and Excise Duty on physical goods. Digitally delivered PC/console game publishing via Steam and PlayStation Store is not within any Gibraltar indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - there is no indirect tax registration threshold in Gibraltar for digital services. Import duty is triggered only at the border upon physical importation into Gibraltar and is not turnover-threshold based.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating is inapplicable because Gibraltar has no VAT. For income tax purposes, Steam and PlayStation Store publishing revenue falls outside Gibraltar tax on territorial grounds where the ELP is non-resident, all relevant activity occurs outside Gibraltar, and no Gibraltar-source profits arise. The ELP should evidence that contracting, platform operations, and service performance are wholly outside Gibraltar; no refund mechanism is required.

Non-Gibraltar VAT obligations (e.g., EU OSS or UK VAT on digital sales) may arise from customer locations but are outside the Gibraltar regime.

UK-Gibraltar exchange-of-information arrangements may support retrospective adjustments if platform payouts flow through the UK or EEA without adequate source documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility applies. GIP is fixed at 1:1 to GBP, and GBP is legally tender alongside GIP; there are no surrender, approval, quota, or license requirements for converting USD, EUR, GBP/GIP, or JPY. Inward capital contributions (LP drawdowns), outward partner distributions, platform net-revenue remittances, and trade payments are free of central-bank authorization. The only convertibility friction is documentary: banks require accurate SWIFT purpose codes (e.g., 'CAPT' for capital contributions, 'TRAD' for trade, 'ROYC' for royalties) and a valid KYC/EDD file. Profit and partnership-distribution remittance from Gibraltar is unencumbered; withholding and reporting obligations are limited to CRS/FATCA and any U.S. tax documentation demanded by Valve/Sony, not to currency controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None. Neither Gibraltar nor the United Kingdom imposes any statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender requirement on export proceeds, including digital-game net revenue remitted by Steam/Valve and PlayStation/Sony. The ELP may leave USD, EUR, or JPY proceeds unhedged, retain them offshore, or convert to GBP/GIP at its treasury discretion. No 30-day, 90-day, or 180-day repatriation window applies, and no central-bank post-transaction reporting is triggered by retaining foreign currency. The only residual obligations are normal tax reporting (CRS/GATCA), accounting treatment of foreign exchange gains/losses, and, if U.S. platform payments are involved, holding a valid IRS Form W-8BEN-E to prevent backup withholding.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar does not maintain a separate central bank or foreign exchange administration. The integrated financial regulator is the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC), which licenses and supervises banks and executes the AML/CFT framework. The Gibraltar Pound (GIP) is issued by the Government of Gibraltar (HM Government of Gibraltar) under the Currency Notes Act 1934 and circulates at par with GBP. Monetary-policy anchor and ultimate sterling liquidity assurance rest with the Bank of England, while operational currency issuance sits with the Government of Gibraltar (Financial Secretary/Currency Commissioner). For FX-control matters, the applicable regime is the UK/Gibraltar post-1979 liberalized sterling area: no exchange control legislation remains in force.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File required where annual related-party transactions exceed GBP 5,000,000 or gross revenue exceeds GBP 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) 2020 apply to entities carrying on relevant activities, including IP-holding and distribution; video game publishing via Steam and console platforms is characterised as a distribution/IP-related activity. The ELP must demonstrate economic substance in Gibraltar proportionate to its online activity through physical presence, staff, and management control. If the ELP is managed and controlled outside Gibraltar or possesses no local substance, it may file an annual Economic Substance Return (ESR-01) showing no relevant activity within Gibraltar, but failure to file or substantiate triggers penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Income Tax Act 2010; upon conviction, a fine up to £500,000 and/or imprisonment up to 7 years; unpaid tax reassessed without limitation, plus interest and surcharges up to 100% of the tax due.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where the general partner knowingly authorises or participates in the evasion; directors or managers are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes, criminal restitution orders, and personal penalties; travel restrictions and seizure of assets may be imposed in the context of a tax fraud investigation.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of £10,000 under the Economic Substance Regulations 2020; continued default attracts a daily penalty of £2,000; the partnership may be struck off, and information is automatically exchanged with HMRC and other EU/OECD member tax authorities.

Director personal liability: The Regulations empower the Gibraltar Registrar to impose penalties on the general partner and any designated person; court proceedings may additionally result in disqualification orders and a personal contribution order against the general partner for the ELP's penalties and unpaid taxes.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the Criminal Justice (Money Laundering) Act, non-compliance can result in an unlimited fine, imprisonment up to 2 years, and revocation of the ELP's registration; platform payout agreements may be suspended or terminated by Valve or Sony.

Director personal liability: Where the general partner knowingly launders proceeds of tax fraud or evades AML obligations, personal criminal liability attaches with confiscation orders and travel bans; the partner remains personally liable for civil restitution and potential sequestration of personal assets.
