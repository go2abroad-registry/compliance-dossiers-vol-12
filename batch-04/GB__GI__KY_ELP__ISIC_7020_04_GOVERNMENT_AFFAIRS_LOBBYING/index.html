No statutory minimum paid-up capital. Partners may contribute cash or assets at the times and in the amounts stated in the partnership agreement; capital drawdowns are funded by wire into the ELP's partner capital account, and no escrow account is prescribed by Gibraltar law.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar - Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office (ITO); partnership registration administered by the Gibraltar Registry, Companies House Gibraltar. Primary portals: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation (Income Tax e-Services) and https://www.gibraltarcompanieshouse.gi

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The foreign general partner files the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) registration application with the Gibraltar Registry under the Limited Partnerships Act 2014, submitting the partnership deed, registered office consent, and particulars of the general partner. Step 2: The Registry issues the LP registration number, which the ITO converts into a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN/Assessment Reference Number) via the CRS/TIN registration procedure. Step 3: The ELP applies for a Trade, Business and Occupation (TBO) licence for the activity of government-relations advisory and cross-border policy consulting. Step 4: Each partner obtains their own Gibraltar TIN and files CRS/FATCA self-certifications; non-resident partners without Gibraltar-source income are outside the charge to Gibraltar income tax but must maintain compliant AEOI records with the ITO.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Gibraltar law; an ELP is mandatorily fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act 2010, with each partner assessed on their share of partnership profits. Election mechanisms available: (i) General Partner may elect ELP 'exempted' status under the Limited Partnerships Act 2014 to avoid Companies Act filing and audit publicity requirements; (ii) under the Income Tax (Hybrid Mismatches) Rules 2017, the entity may make a conforming election to treat a non-resident partner's share as transparent/conduit where double non-taxation arises from conflicting foreign classification; (iii) a non-resident partner may elect the remittance-basis style relief for offshore-source profits when the partnership is not carrying on a trade in Gibraltar.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

15%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST turnover registration threshold exists because Gibraltar does not levy VAT. Registration with HM Customs (Gibraltar Customs & Consumer Tax Unit) is mandatory for every importer of goods into Gibraltar, without an exempt turnover threshold; businesses supplying locally listed consumer-taxable services must also register for Consumer Tax remittance. Professional advisory and lobbying services exported to non-resident clients are outside the scope of the tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT export zero-rating mechanism. Consumer Tax is imposed only on goods imported for home use; goods in customs transit, under warehousing arrangements, or subsequently re-exported are relieved from Consumer Tax. For the ISIC 7020 cross-border lobbying and policy-advisory services, the supply is an exported service outside the scope of Gibraltar Consumer Tax. Eligibility conditions: (i) the client is a non-resident of Gibraltar; (ii) the service is performed wholly outside Gibraltar or the benefit is enjoyed abroad; (iii) invoicing and payment are in a foreign currency; and (iv) the ELP holds a valid TBO licence and maintains contemporaneous engagement contracts, attendance records, and foreign tax-residence certificates of clients.

  • Gibraltar Export Entry / Customs Re-Export Declaration (single administrative document)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export (for goods) or service-delivery log (for advisory services)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency consideration)
  • Commercial Invoice and detailed service contract with TINs of both parties
  • Client foreign tax-residence certificate (CRS self-certification form)
  • TBO licence and current ELP registration certificate

No real-time fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping mandate for B2B e-invoicing in Gibraltar. Gibraltar does not form part of the UK's Making Tax Digital (MTD) HMRC regime for income tax. Invoices must be chronologically retained for 6 years and satisfy the ITO's prescribed invoice content (seller/buyer names, TINs, description, date, and currency). Customs import/export declarations are lodged via the Gibraltar customs electronic entry processing system without a Peppol or RTIR API requirement.

Misdeclaration of export/re-export relief or mischaracterising services performed in Gibraltar as exported triggers: forfeiture of goods and a customs demand of up to three times the value of goods under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance; income tax penalties up to 200% of the tax under-assessed plus interest for fraudulent evasion, and up to 100% for careless error, under the Income Tax Act 2010; criminal fines and imprisonment up to 7 years for fraudulent evasion; retrospective clawback extending 6 years for careless errors and without limit for fraud; and enhanced audit referral by the ITO to the GFSC for money-laundering risk, given the Medium_Risk_Enhanced AML tier applicable to government-affairs lobbying. Repatriation of export receipts must be evidenced within 180 days, failing which the ITO may recharacterise the transaction as Gibraltar-source revenue and deny the outside-scope treatment.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains no routine exchange control formalities. The Gibraltar pound circulates at parity with the UK pound sterling and is freely convertible into major currencies on the open market. Both current account transactions (retainer fees, consulting income, royalties) and capital account flows (partnership capital contributions, profit distributions, loan repayments) are treated as unrestricted. No central bank pre-approval, exceeding-participation license, or currency surrender requirement applies. In practice, convertibility hurdles arise only from bank-level AML/KYC due diligence and from adherence to UK, EU, or UN/OFAC sanctions regimes affecting counterparties in the target business scope.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation for any Gibraltar-licensed or Gibraltar-resident entity, including an Exempted Limited Partnership engaged in cross-border government affairs advisory and lobbying. No mandatory conversion ratio, repatriation deadline, or foreign-exchange escrow requirement is imposed on receipts from foreign clients. For statistical and record-keeping purposes, the Financial Intelligence Supervision Unit of the GFSC may receive transaction reports under the Proceeds of Crime Act (Gibraltar) 2015 when a transfer is flagged as suspicious or exceeds internal reporting thresholds, but this does not affect availability of funds.

Central bank supervision: Financial Services Commission (Gibraltar) as the statutory regulator for banking and financial services, supported by the Government of Gibraltar's Currency Notes Act 1934 framework and HM Treasury of the United Kingdom, which retains ultimate sterling-convertibility and sanctions coherence authority for the Gibraltar pound (GIP).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a non-primary ancillary rail only. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK-authorised EMI, EU passported institution, US money-transmission operator) cannot hold or move funds as a licensed 'e-money institution' in Gibraltar after the UK's withdrawal from the EEA unless licensed under the Financial Services Act (Gibraltar) 2019 or passporting under a third-country designation. A non-Gibraltar EMI may lawfully service the ELP where the account is held only for passing re-invoicing of retainer flows, provided that no funds remain outstanding beyond the clearing cycle and the partnership maintains the real place of management and substance in Gibraltar. Domestic banks refuse to treat the EMI as a compliance-grade treasury artery; as such, an offshore EMI should never be the sole clearing rail for underlying 7020 government-affairs payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions to the ELP from non-resident investors or partners are routed using the standard SWIFT MT103 wire protocol. The instructing bank should include a field-72 purpose text reading 'GI/KY_ELP Capital Contribution – ISIC 7020' and an ISO 20022 purpose code equal to 'CACC' (current account access) or 'BEXP' (business expenses) for advisory working capital. The beneficiary bank must be a GFSC-licensed tier-1 commercial bank in Gibraltar holding a segregated 'Capital Escrow Account' in the ELP's name. The bank's reconciliation unit matches each inbound wire against an issuance-specific capital call note containing the exact contribution amount, the partner reference, the invoice or call date, and the underlying partnership clause. If the inbound currency is not GIP or GBP, the bank executes the FX conversion at the prevailing spot, maintaining an FX contract reference attached to the transaction. For security, the ELP treasury must require the sending bank to include a UETR (unique end-to-end transaction reference) and a country-specific regulatory originator reference such as UK-to-GIB or EU-to-GIB originator information under the Funds Transfer Regulation (FTR) remittance information rules.Outbound trade and advisory disbursements from the ELP must settle via one of three regulated rails: (i) for sub-consultants and lone advisers earning retainers, a high-speed SWIFT MT103 wire to a specified overseas bank account, with field 70 stating 'ISIC 7020 retainer / policy-analysis disbursement' and ISO purpose code 'PRF' (professional and consulting fee) rather than 'GOOD'; (ii) for larger multi-invoice settlement structures to a foreign research contractor, a clean documentary commercial L/C (issuing a standby LC where no physical goods are shipped) is permissible, and the confirmation bank should be a rated financial institution; (iii) for UK/EU disbursements, use UK CHAPS or Single Euro Payments Area (SEPA) Credit Transfer with the ELP's Gibraltar IBAN and the beneficiary's EUR account. The same treatment applies to payments to registered lobbyists or trade-association representatives retained for legitimate stakeholder advocacy. All outbound payments must carry a fully populated remittance remitter name, be tracked by the SWIFT traffic type MT103 (no cover payment MT202 is allowed without an underlying MT103), and be queued through the same GFSC-regulated bank relationship to preserve audit continuity.Onshore deliverable FX forward on GIP/GBP versus USD, EUR, CHF, and JPY, permitted when the ELP holds a paper contract with a licensed counterparty in GibraltarNon-deliverable forward (NDF) on offshore-traded or soft currencies of emerging-market trade-policy client countries (e.g., INR, EGP, NGN), provided that the underlying settlement occurs outside a GIP-issued clearing actCross-currency interest rate swap (CCIRS) matching partnership capital injections in USD/EUR to retainer receivables in GBP/GIP, reducing both basis and duration riskFX swap (rollover/spot-forward combination) for temporary treasury liquidity management, where the full notional is required to repay the onward leg within the same clearing cycleEuropean-style currency swaption, only available where the banking relationship permits GFSC-approved derivative conduct and the ELP can evidence genuine hedging of a commercial cashflow, not speculative undertaking

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when the aggregate value of cross-border related-party transactions (including payment for services, IP, and financing) exceeds GBP 1,500,000 in the accounting period. Master File is required when the consolidated group turnover exceeds EUR 50,000,000, or when the group has related-party cross-border transactions exceeding GBP 15,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Companies and Partnerships) Act 2019 applies to all Gibraltar entities, including ELPs. A lobbying advisory business is not a pure geographically mobile activity, but it must still demonstrate: (i) the partnership is managed and directed in Gibraltar, (ii) its core income-generating activities (client solicitation, policy analysis, stakeholder meetings) are performed by employees physically present in Gibraltar, and (iii) its assets, expenditures, and board/partner decisions are proportionate to its revenue. Failure to satisfy substance requirements may result in sanctions and information exchange with the partner's tax authority.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Understatement

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties up to 100% of the tax underpaid plus interest at 8% per annum; criminal prosecution may carry a fine of up to GBP 20,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: As the ELP is a limited partnership, the general partner bears unlimited liability for the partnership's obligations. A general partner who knowingly participates in evasion is personally liable for all civil penalties and may be subject to a travel ban preventing departure from Gibraltar, along with freezing orders on personal assets.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Act, the Commissioner may levy a penalty of GBP 10,000 for initial non-compliance and GBP 100,000 for continued non-compliance. The Registrar may strike the ELP off the register, and the entity name may be published on the public 'non-compliant entities' list.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any other partner in control may be held directly liable to satisfy the penalties out of their own estates. Persistent failure can lead to disqualification from acting as a general partner in any Gibraltar entity and to the enforcement of a personal financial guarantee.
Anti-Money Laundering / Corruption (Public Officials)

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, the maximum penalty for a corporate breach is GBP 5 million, and in serious cases, an unlimited fine. Individual officers may face up to 14 years' imprisonment for money laundering. The Gambling Supervision and AML authorities may also revoke the ELP's passport for business expectations and impose a public censure.

Director personal liability: The general partner and every limited partner participating in management with knowledge of irregularity are treated as 'senior management' and are personally liable for criminal fines and restitution. Courts may impose travel bans, asset freezing orders, and UK extradition requests if the offence is linked to a UK-licensed public body.
