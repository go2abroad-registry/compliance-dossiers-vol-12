Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar, Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax, under the Income Tax Act 2010) for direct taxes; HM Customs Gibraltar (under the Customs Duties Act and the Purchase Tax Act) for indirect taxes. The Exempted Limited Partnership is registered with the Gibraltar Companies Registry, a department of the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission. Official portals: www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax-office, www.hmcustomsgibraltar.gi, www.fsc.gi.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the ELP registration statement with the Gibraltar Companies Registry under the Limited Partnerships Act 2019, appointing a general partner (typically a Gibraltar-resident professional services firm) and at least one limited partner, and stating the registered office. Step 2: Obtain the Gibraltar partnership identification number from the Registrar. Step 3: Within 30 days of commencing business or first receiving Gibraltar-source income, file Income Tax Form 1 (Registration of a Person for Income Tax) with the Income Tax Office, which issues a Tax Reference Number (TRN). Step 4: Where central management and control is outside Gibraltar, file a non-resident partnership declaration and, if desired, a look-through transparency election. Step 5: Apply to HMRC/HM Customs Gibraltar for a UK EORI number (GB prefix) for any physical import or export of data centre equipment. Step 6: Register as an employer for payroll withholding in Gibraltar only if local staff are engaged.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible_Territorial_Scope_Tax_Exempt_Zone_Eligible - The ELP is not a body corporate; income is attributed to partners on a look-through basis once the transparency election is made. A non-resident ELP (central management and control outside Gibraltar) is taxed only on Gibraltar-source income; wholly foreign-source receipts fall outside the territorial scope. No minimum capital is required and 100% non-resident ownership is allowed, enabling the ELP to operate with a nil or zero assessment in practice.

Available election forms

(a) Look-Through Transparency Election under the Income Tax (Partnerships) Rules 2019: partners are treated as if they carried on their proportionate share of the ELP business directly. (b) Corporate-Style Opt-Out: the ELP may elect to be assessed on a gross-income basis (12.5% for financial or professional activities, 10% for utilities and 20% for monopoly-type businesses), which is often chosen by inbound institutional investors unable to accept flow-through of Gibraltar-source character. (c) Hybrid Mismatch Alignment: a UK-resident partner can file an election under the UK-Gibraltar tax cooperation framework so that the ELP is treated as transparent in only one jurisdiction, eliminating the mismatch under TIOPA 2010 Part 6. (d) Grandfathered exemption: an ELP migrating from Cayman retains an existing exemption certificate until its expiry; new ELPs cannot obtain a full tax-exempt certificate after the 2020 gross-income reform.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Purchase Tax and Customs Duty (no general value-added tax or GST; services are outside the scope)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - Gibraltar imposes no VAT or GST and therefore has no registration threshold. A UK EORI number (GB prefix) is mandatory for physical imports and exports of colocation hardware; the purchase tax attaches only to imported or locally manufactured goods at the point of import or sale, with most rates at 0% and no tax on the colocation service itself.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar does not operate a VAT zero-rating regime. Cross-border services such as data centre colocation, rack-space leasing, and bare-metal hosting are not enumerated supplies under the Purchase Tax Act and are therefore outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax. For physical hardware, zero-rating can be secured only through Inward Processing Relief or customs warehousing, ending with a removal from Gibraltar customs territory evidenced by an export declaration. No purchase-tax refund mechanism exists for services; customs duty relief may be claimed under section 12 of the Customs Duties Act.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed via HMRC CDS/GECS (EXP-PL return with item-level server serial numbers)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (master and house copies showing container or rack identifiers)
  • Commercial Invoice stating HS codes, unit prices, and INCOTERMS
  • Packing List with rack-level itemised description of each server platform
  • Inward Processing Relief authorisation reference (where duty-suspended hardware is re-exported)
  • Certificate of Origin (only if a preferential origin claim is made under the UK-Gibraltar trade arrangement)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT MT103 advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or continuous transaction control regime applies in Gibraltar. Invoicing must comply with the UK-derived Commercial Practices (B2B Invoicing) Regulations, including minimum content, retention, and 60-day payment rules. Customs clearances are filed through HMRC's Customs Declaration Service (CDS) and the Gibraltar e-Customs System (GECS), with EU-style Entry Summary Declaration safety-and-security data; no SAF-T file or CTC exchange is required.

Fraudulent zero-rating or false IPR claims trigger: (1) a treble-duty penalty under the Purchase Tax Act, equal to three times the evaded tax, plus forfeiture of the equipment; (2) a civil penalty of up to GIP 500,000 under the Customs Duties Act; (3) retrospective clawback assessments for six years where re-imported goods are traced into Gibraltar consumption; (4) criminal liability, including up to two years' imprisonment for the general partner on conviction for fraudulent evasion; (5) parallel HMRC proceedings in the UK under the UK-Gibraltar joint enforcement framework, which may re-characterise the supply as UK VAT-able where the UK parent is the effective supplier, creating a matching assessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies to both current-account and capital-account transactions. Gibraltar imposes no exchange-control licensing, no distinction between capital and current account for prior approval, and no central-bank consent requirement for profit remittances, partner distributions, dividend payments, or capital repayments. GIP deposits are convertible at par into GBP, and multi-currency GBP/USD/EUR accounts may be opened freely. Residual friction is limited to AML/KYC verification of beneficial owners, source-of-funds documentation, and the commercial purpose of the data-center colocation and leasing operation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Gibraltar maintains no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no prescribed timeline for retention or repatriation of export receipts. Cross-border rents and colocation service invoices may be retained in the original invoicing currency or swept into GBP/USD/EUR treasury accounts at the ELP's discretion.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory banking and foreign-exchange conduct authority; Her Majesty's Treasury (United Kingdom) provides the monetary anchor through the one-for-one sterling peg of the Gibraltar pound (GIP). Gibraltar has no independent central bank and no separate FX-control administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-Medium for offshore digital EMIs not integrated into the Gibraltar regulatory perimeter. Gibraltar is outside the EEA, and UK or EU e-money licences do not automatically passport into Gibraltar. A digital EMI must hold a GFSC licence or operate through a GFSC-licensed payment institution to serve a Gibraltar-domiciled ELP. DLT-based payment firms may be regulated under Gibraltar's Distributed Ledger Technology framework, but they cannot replace a licensed commercial bank for capital escrow, tenant settlement, or liquidity management. The recommended structure is to use a licensed Gibraltar bank as the primary clearing anchor and fintech EMIs only as secondary receivables collection applications.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File are required where Gibraltar gross turnover of the ELP/group exceeds GIP 10,000,000, or where aggregate related-party transactions, including management fees, leasing charges, loans and service agreements, exceed GIP 1,000,000 per accounting period; simplified annual TP disclosure is required for related-party transactions above GIP 250,000.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations apply to any ELP carrying on a 'relevant activity' such as headquarters, distribution, service-centre, financing, or holding-company functions. A data-center colocation business will be scrutinised if the leasing and hosting services are provided to related group entities. The ELP must maintain registered office premises in Gibraltar, hold partner/board meetings locally, keep accounting records in Gibraltar, and employ adequate staff and assets commensurate with its activity. Pure, actively-operated third-party colocation performed by sufficiently resourced staff in Gibraltar generally satisfies the substance test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty up to 100% of the tax evaded plus interest at 9% per annum; criminal fine not exceeding GIP 250,000 and/or imprisonment for up to five years.

Director personal liability: Partners and directors who knowingly authorise or sign a false return are jointly and severally liable to the Commissioner; the court may impose director disqualification, travel bans, seizure of assets, and imprisonment.
Shell Company and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic substance penalty of GIP 10,000 plus GIP 500 per day for continued non-compliance; risk of strike-off, loss of tax residence benefits, and nullification of treaty/withholding tax relief.

Director personal liability: The limitation of liability shield is broken; the corporate general partner's directors may be personally liable to creditors and regulators, disqualified, and made subject to travel bans and asset freezing orders.
Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalisation Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may make transfer pricing adjustments, assess additional tax at 12.5%, add default interest at 9% per annum, and impose penalties up to 100% of the understated tax.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner can be held personally liable where they signed, knew, or caused to be submitted materially inaccurate transfer pricing documentation; criminal liability may follow for fraudulent evasion.
Anti-Money Laundering and Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Forfeiture of assets, criminal fines, regulatory penalties on the AML compliance officer, and imprisonment up to 14 years under the Proceeds of Crime Act; additional civil liability for negligent monitoring.

Director personal liability: Senior managers and beneficial owners are exposed to civil restitution, director disqualification, extradition, travel bans, and asset freezes; the corporate veil is pierced where the ELP is used as a vehicle for money laundering.
