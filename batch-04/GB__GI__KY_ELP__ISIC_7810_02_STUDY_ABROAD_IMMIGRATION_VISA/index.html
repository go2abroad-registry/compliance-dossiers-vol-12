Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Companies, Companies House Gibraltar, Suite 561, Europort, Gibraltar (online portal: egov.gibraltar.gov.gi).

Governing statute

Limited Partnerships Act 2015 (Laws of Gibraltar), ss. 4-16 (registration, legal personality, statement of particulars) and s. 27 (records); complemented by the Companies Act 2014 (Gibraltar) in respect of the corporate general partner, and the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar) for AML registration obligations.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form LP-1 (Application for Registration of a Limited Partnership) together with Form LP-1A (Statement of Particulars of the Exempted Limited Partnership).

Incorporation processing time

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum capital for a Gibraltar ELP; the Statement of Particulars must declare the capital contribution or callable commitment of each limited partner. The Registrar requires no statutory escrow account, but client monies held for tuition or visa fees must be segregated in a licensed Gibraltar bank client escrow account under GFSC supervisory guidance.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate under the Fire Safety (Commercial Premises) Regulations (Gibraltar) with annual inspection by the Gibraltar Fire and Rescue Service; occupancy load capped at 8 persons per 100 m² with fire compartmentation and 30-minute FD30 doors; weekly alarm test log and two fire drills per year. Occupational clearance: Health and Safety at Work Act 2004 (Gibraltar) compliance certificate, five-yearly DSE workstation assessment, first-aid kit and AED if premises exceed 500 m², and a structural engineer's confirmation of 3.0 kN/m² live load for the office floor plate.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) via Government Gateway; Gibraltar-specific registration through the Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Tax Fax: +350 200 44600; online portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation)

E-government filing portal

A foreign Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) seeking a Gibraltar tax ID must first apply for a Certificate of Compliance from the Companies House Gibraltar or the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as relevant, then register for Gibraltar Income Tax using Form ELP-1 or the corporate online registration portal. The partnership must provide certified constituent documents, a registered office address in Gibraltar, the proposed UK/Gibraltar tax residency status, partner details, and a statement of intended activities. HMRC registration is not required unless the ELP has a UK permanent establishment or taxable UK source income; otherwise the ELP should register for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (G-TIN) only. For VAT, if the ELP makes taxable supplies in Gibraltar or the UK, it must register with HMRC (for UK VAT, threshold GBP 90,000) or the Gibraltar Commissioner of Income Tax (for Gibraltar VAT currently aligned to UK VAT rules).

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) seeking a Gibraltar tax ID must first apply for a Certificate of Compliance from the Companies House Gibraltar or the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as relevant, then register for Gibraltar Income Tax using Form ELP-1 or the corporate online registration portal. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

A foreign ELP may elect to be treated as transparent for Gibraltar income tax purposes under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act (ELP provisions), provided it files a partnership return and no partner is a Gibraltar tax resident carrying on a trade through the partnership. The ELP is not subject to Gibraltar tax at entity level; partners are taxed on their share of profits. No US-style check-the-box election is available in Gibraltar; however, hybrid mismatch elections under UK CTA 2010 Part 6A may affect dual-inclusion or deduction/non-inclusion outcomes if the ELP is treated as transparent in Gibraltar and opaque in another jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

VAT

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 90,000 for UK VAT registration (HMRC); Gibraltar applies a zero-rate regime for most services, with no general VAT registration threshold applicable to a non-resident ELP unless it makes taxable supplies in Gibraltar or imports goods into Gibraltar; threshold for non-established taxable persons: nil (mandatory registration upon first taxable supply)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The ELP may zero-rate supplies of services related to international student placement and visa advisory if the recipient belongs in a third country (outside the UK and Gibraltar) and the services fall within the B2B export rules (place of supply of services to a business customer is where the customer belongs) or the B2C exceptions for land-related, admission-related, or consultancy services. Goods exports are zero-rated if they are removed from the UK/Gibraltar to a destination outside the UK/EU customs territory within the relevant time limit (usually 3 months for direct export, 12 months for indirect export) and the exporter retains official Customs Export Declaration evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / SAD or CHIEF/CDS printout)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation evidencing receipt of export consideration from the foreign customer
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Proof of payment of any export-related fees (e.g., visa consultation fee receipts)
  • Foreign customer tax identification number and non-UK/non-Gibraltar residency evidence

No real-time fiscal clearance mandate in Gibraltar; e-invoicing is voluntary but HMRC requires electronic submission of VAT returns (VAT 100) and Making Tax Digital (MTD) compatibility for UK VAT registrants. Gibraltar follows UK-style MTD to the extent aligned. No API-based continuous transaction controls or digital stamping regime is imposed by the Gibraltar tax authority; GST/VAT digital clearance is limited to standard periodic VAT returns filed electronically through the Gibraltar Tax e-Filing portal or HMRC MTD API for UK VAT.

Fraudulent or unsupported zero-rating of exports triggers VAT reassessment plus a penalty of up to 100% of tax evaded under VATA 1994 s.60 (civil evasion penalty in the UK; Gibraltar applies analogous penalty provisions). Late or absent Foreign Exchange repatriation within 180 days may lead to deemed non-export and VAT recovery, plus interest of up to 8% per annum and criminal prosecution for dishonest conduct, including imprisonment of up to 7 years for fraud (UK CTA 2010 s.105 and VATA 1994 s.72; Gibraltar Misuse of Drugs and Fraud Acts equivalent). Retrospective clawback covering 4 years (or 20 years in the case of deliberate fraud) is possible, and the ELP and its general partner may be jointly and severally liable for any clawback, penalties, and interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP and GBP are fully convertible on both current and capital account; no exchange-control restrictions apply to Gibraltar or the United Kingdom following the repeal of the Exchange Control Act 1947. There are no statutory barriers to profit repatriation or distribution to non-resident limited partners; Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on such distributions. However, the GFSC AML/CFT regime requires a payment-conduct review, and any transaction touching UN, UK OFSI, EU or Gibraltar-sanctioned persons or jurisdictions is blocked. The ELP must maintain audited records evidencing that distributions represent partnership profits, not undisclosed capital or client money.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No requirement exists in Gibraltar or the UK to surrender, repatriate, or convert export proceeds. The consultancy may retain 100% of its foreign-currency fee income in designated multi-currency accounts without mandatory conversion ratios. For the ISIC 7810 service model, receivables are classified as cross-border business services; only Bank of England statistical balance-of-payments reporting is triggered, and no enforced conversion timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the prudential regulator of licensed banks and e-money institutions. The Commissioner of Currency of the Government of Gibraltar issues Gibraltar pound (GIP) banknotes under the Currency Notes Act 1934 at mandatory 1:1 parity with GBP; monetary policy follows the Bank of England, and HM Treasury retains ultimate monetary sovereignty.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: MEDIUM (restricted). UK-licensed EMIs may service Gibraltar entities only through the reciprocal UK-Gibraltar authorisation framework, and most digital EMIs (Revolut, Wise, Airwallex, Currenxie) exclude Exempted Limited Partnerships classified as Medium-Risk Enhanced because the partnership UBO documentation exceeds automated onboarding. The compliant architecture is to open the primary Escrow at a Tier-1 Gibraltar bank and attach one EMI solely for operational settlement rails, never as custodian of client money, with a full source-of-funds audit trail preserved.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where the Gibraltar entity's controlled transactions exceed GBP 1,000,000 in an accounting period; Master File is mandatory where the group consolidated annual turnover exceeds GBP 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regime (ESR) applies to entities carrying on relevant activities including headquarters, distribution, and service centre operations. A tax-resident ELP must satisfy the ESR test by maintaining substantial activities, Gibraltar-based decision-making, employees, premises, and annual ESR filing. A non-resident ELP centrally managed outside Gibraltar is generally out of scope, but must evidence non-residence to avoid classification as a shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — deliberate under-declaration of chargeable profits and omission of revenue

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under the Income Tax Act 2010: fine of up to 110% of the tax evaded, imprisonment up to seven years, and a civil penalty equal to 100% of the under-assessed tax.

Director personal liability: General partners are jointly and severally liable for unpaid partnership tax; officers knowingly contributing to evasion face personal asset freezes, travel bans, directorship disqualification for ten years, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Inadequate Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR penalties: warning notice, initial penalty of GBP 20,000, escalating to GBP 100,000 for continued default; the Commissioner may re-calculate tax at 12.5% on the entire foreign-source profit together with interest and late-payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners face civil restitution orders, freezing of personal assets, a five-year directorship ban, and if fraud is established, criminal conviction with up to five years' imprisonment.
Money Laundering / Unlicensed Immigration and Visa Practice

Statutory sanction: Contravention of the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the Financial Services (Investment and Fiduciary Services) Act 2015 attracts fines up to GBP 500,000 per transaction and imprisonment up to ten years; the regulator may issue asset forfeiture orders and cancel the ELP's exempt registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are personally liable for penalties; fallout includes bankruptcy proceedings, travel bans, extradition within Commonwealth channels, and imprisonment for failure to comply with AML remedial orders.
