Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, HM Government of Gibraltar Income Tax Office; corporate registry: Gibraltar Companies House (Business and IP Registration Office). Official portals: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation and https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/companies-house.

E-government filing portal

Non-resident-owned Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) under the Limited Partnerships Act 2014: (1) lodge the formation pack with the Registrar of Companies — partnership deed, registered-office consent, general-partner particulars and partner register — to obtain the Certificate of Registration in 2–3 business days; (2) within 30 days, apply to the Income Tax Office for the statutory Tax Reference Number (TRN) using the partnership tax-registration application, submitting the Certificate of Registration, certified identity documents of all partners, registered-office proof, AML/KYC documentation and an economic-substance declaration; (3) register each Gibraltar-source-income partner individually for a TRN while non-resident partners earning only foreign-source export income remain unregistered; (4) register with the Social Insurance Contribution Office only if local staff are engaged. The entire procedure is achievable remotely via notarised or apostilled certified copies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — fiscal transparency is automatic: the ELP is not a taxable person under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act, and profits are chargeable in the hands of partners solely to the extent of Gibraltar-source income. The territorial rule excludes foreign-service export income (B2B AI data-labelling delivered abroad and settled via Wise or PayPal Mass Payout), producing de facto zero Gibraltarian taxation while preserving no formal Tax_Exempt_Zone status.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not administer US check-the-box elections; transparency is statutorily automatic. Formal elections available: (a) classification election under the Gibraltar (Economic Substance) Regulations when the ELP performs a relevant activity, keeping the entity outside the non-resident blacklist; (b) partnership return election (annual partnership income-tax return) enabling each partner to file its share individually; (c) anti-hybrid mismatch declaration under BEPS-aligned rules to prevent double non-taxation where a Cayman parent or UK partner applies opaque treatment. A Qualifying-Company election is available only to incorporated entities, not to ELPs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Nil — Gibraltar does not operate VAT, GST, sales or use tax. The only indirect taxes are excise and customs duties on specified physical imports (Customs Duties Consolidation Act) and stamp duty on instruments. Cross-border AI data-labeling and RLHF-curation services are outside the scope of Gibraltarian indirect taxation.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Gibraltar has no VAT registration threshold. Customs/import-duty registration arises only when physical consignments exceed GIP 250 for excise goods or GIP 1,000 for other goods; the ELP's digital exports do not approach these de minimis limits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT so classic export zero-rating is inapposite; equivalent relief is obtained through: (i) customs-duty zero-rating for goods physically exported from Gibraltar upon verifiable customs declaration; (ii) the UK place-of-supply rule — B2B AI data-labelling supplies made by the non-established ELP to UK/EU business customers are outside the Gibraltar charge and UK VAT is accounted for by the recipient under reverse charge; (iii) territorial income exclusion — export consideration settled through PayPal Mass Payout or Wise and distributed to non-resident partners is not Gibraltar-source, provided no marketing or contract-forming activity occurs through a Gibraltarian permanent establishment.

  • Customs export declaration (SAD/C88 equivalent) approved by the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Department at the port of exit
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill identifying the non-Gibraltar consignee and port of discharge
  • Inward remittance certificate / bank or payment-provider remittance advice evidencing settlement of the export receivable (including Wise and PayPal Mass Payout records)
  • Partner profit-allocations schedule segregating export income attributed to non-resident partners

Gibraltar imposes no Continuous Transaction Control, e-invoicing clearance or digital-stamping mandate. Books and invoices must be preserved for six years under the Income Tax Act, and the annual return is e-filed on the government tax portal. For UK/EU clients, the ELP issues commercial PDF invoices with a clear place-of-supply and reverse-charge annotation, adopting ISO 20022 fields without any real-time fiscal approval.

Dual-track exposure: (1) Gibraltar — the Commissioner may reopen the partnership return within six years for negligent misdeclaration and without time limit for fraudulent conduct; penalties reach 100% of the undercharged tax plus daily default fines for non-filing (Gibraltar Income Tax Act penalties regime). (2) UK/HMRC — if the ELP or its UK agent misapplies the reverse-charge treatment or wrongly claims a UK zero-rate, HMRC may assess VAT, interest and penalties of up to 100% for deliberate behaviour; unsupported export documents trigger retrospective clawback across up to four years of supply. The 180-day repatriation deadline is treated as a substance indicator — persistent non-remittance invites recharacterisation of export proceeds as Gibraltar-source income and assessment of partners at the prevailing territorial rates. The ELP must therefore archive annotation work logs, client acceptances and payment ledgers to survive an audit challenge.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar Pound (GIP) is fully convertible into Pound Sterling at par. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted for non-residents, including the Exempted Limited Partnership (KY_ELP). Profit remittances, capital distributions, and liquidation proceeds may be freely transferred abroad without exchange-control approval. UK exchange controls were abolished in 1979 and no equivalent regime applies in Gibraltar; the only operative constraints are sanctions screening, AML/CTF obligations, and FATCA/CRS reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory requirement to surrender, convert, or repatriate export proceeds in Gibraltar. AI data-labelling and RLHF services constitute 'invisible exports', and all such receipts may be retained in the original invoice currency without any mandatory conversion timeline. Banks must still record and screen all cross-border receipts under the AML/CTF framework, but no exchange-control permit or conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the banking and e-money supervisor; monetary authority and sterling finality rest with HM Treasury and the Bank of England. Gibraltar has no separate central bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

High feasibility for disbursement rails: Wise, PayPal, and EEA/UK-licensed e-money institutions can support mass payouts to distributed labelers and AI-training contractors, provided the ELP maintains a GFSC-licensed bank as the master treasury account. Legal constraints: EMIs cannot act as capital-escrow depositories, cannot provide bankruptcy-remote clearing, and may require a UK/EEA e-money licence. Feasibility rating: High as a payout rail, Medium as a secondary liquidity rail, Low as the primary clearing bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% standard Gibraltar corporate income tax rate (Income Tax Act 2010, s. 5) on chargeable profits of resident companies and non-resident companies carrying on a Gibraltar trade through a permanent establishment.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar imposes no statutory dividend withholding tax on distributions made by an ELP or a Gibraltar company to non-resident partners or shareholders.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 per connected-party cross-border transaction per counterparty per annum (Local File is compulsory). Groups with consolidated annual revenue exceeding EUR 750 million must also prepare a Master File and submit a Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office within 12 months of the fiscal year-end. For an ELP in the AI data-labeling BPO scope, even a zero-tax entity must maintain contemporaneous documentation for any related-party transaction (e.g., intercompany service agreements between ELP and a non-resident parent) if the aggregate value exceeds GIP 500,000; below that threshold, a summary rationale of the arm's length method is sufficient.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's Economic Substance Code (ESC) applies to all Gibraltar tax-resident entities, including ELPs, carrying on a 'relevant activity' as defined by the Companies (Economic Substance) (Schedule) Regulations 2021. The data-labeling BPO falls under 'distribution and service centre' activities if services are provided to related parties, or under 'headquarters' if the ELP acts as a regional head-office controlling related entities. An ELP engaged in activities with third-party clients only does not generally trigger ESR. Where ESR applies, the ELP must demonstrate in-country substance: (i) premises used in Gibraltar exclusively for the activity; (ii) full-time employees or other personnel physically present in Gibraltar; (iii) adequate expenditure incurred in Gibraltar proportionate to the level of activity; and (iv) core income-generating activities occur in Gibraltar. Failure to comply results in a fine of GIP 30,000 for the first year and GIP 300,000 for repeated non-compliance, plus the exchange of information with the ultimate parent's jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 200% of the under-declared Gibraltar tax liability, plus daily default surcharge of GIP 50 and interest at 8% per annum. Criminal conviction for cheating the Revenue attracts imprisonment of up to 7 years and an unlimited fine. The Income Tax Office may seek a freezing order over the ELP's bank accounts (including payment rails via Wise/Stripe) and strike the ELP off the Gibraltar register.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any director/manager of a corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties if they participated in a dishonest act. Courts may impose personal civil restitution equal to the evaded tax, a travel ban preventing departure from Gibraltar/UK, and, in egregious cases, imprisonment up to 7 years. Disqualification from acting as a director is automatic for 15 years.
Shell Company — Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Fine up to GIP 300,000 for each financial year of non-compliance; if the ELP is in the financial services sector, the Financial Services Commission may revoke any registration or authorisation. The ELP's name may be struck off by the Registrar of Companies and its assets wound up. The Gibraltar Commissioner may issue a court application to obtain information from third parties (e.g., PayPal/Wise) and exchange that information with foreign tax authorities under the OECD/EOIR standard.

Director personal liability: The general partner or any designated member of the governing body is subject to a personal liability fine of up to GIP 100,000. In addition, the court may issue a 'management prohibition order' under the Companies Act preventing the individual from managing any Gibraltar entity for up to 15 years. Travel bans and restrictions on dealing with UK-regulated financial institutions are common enforcement tools.
AML / Counter-Terrorist Financing Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Money Laundering Act 2015 and the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015, penalties range from GIP 50,000 to an unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 14 years. Regulatory action includes revocation of the ELP's licence (if any) and inclusion on HM Treasury's sanctions list. Confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act permit the seizure of the ELP's entire beneficial interest in the payment accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors, the general partner, and the appointed AML compliance officer (if the ELP is a 'relevant person') face criminal liability for failure to report suspicious activity. Personal travel bans, bank account freezes, and extradition to the UK are available. Civil liability includes restitution of the full value of property laundered through the ELP, regardless of profit share.
Transfer Pricing Adjustment and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: The Gibraltar Income Tax Office makes a transfer pricing adjustment, increasing the ELP's taxable profits to the arm's length amount. Additional penalties of 100% of the additional tax due, plus interest at 8% per annum, are imposed. For CbCR and Master File non-compliance, a separate penalty of GIP 10,000 per month applies.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any tax-filing officer who knowingly prepared or approved an unreasonable pricing policy is personally liable for the assessed deficiency if the ELP is insolvent or has been dissolved without provision. A court may order reinstatement of the ELP solely to enforce the tax liability against the personal assets of the general partner. The director may be disqualified from serving as a director of any Gibraltar company for a period of up to 10 years.
