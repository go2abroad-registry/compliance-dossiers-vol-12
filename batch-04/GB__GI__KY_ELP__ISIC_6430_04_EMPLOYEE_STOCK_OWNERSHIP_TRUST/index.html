Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital. Capital contributions are determined solely by the Partnership Agreement and may be fixed, unfixed, or contingent. No escrow account is required by the Limited Partnerships Act 2016 for ELP formation; however, if the ESOP administrator holds employee plan funds or client money, such funds must be segregated in a client bank account and held subject to applicable financial services client-money rules.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification required from the Gibraltar Fire and Rescue Service under the Fire Safety Act (Cap 195) for the office premises, including emergency lighting, extinguishers, escape routes, and fire risk assessment. Occupational clearance under the Health and Safety at Work Act (Cap 167) requires a workplace risk assessment, first-aid provision, structural load approval for office use via Building Control, and compulsory employers' liability insurance. The registered agent shall maintain the occupancy load plan and annual fire inspection records.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar, with digital filing via the Gibraltar Tax e-Services portal; ancillary authority for indirect duties is the HM Customs & Excise Division of Gibraltar.

E-government filing portal

A foreign promoter forming the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) must: (1) submit the registration application to the Registrar of Companies under Gibraltar's limited-partnership legislation, including the statement of the partnership (Gibraltar registered office, registered agent, general partner details and capital contribution schedule); (2) upon issue of the partnership certificate, file the tax registration application (TRN application) with the Income Tax Office, appending the registration certificate, the executed limited partnership deed and the CRS/FATCA self-certifications of all partners; (3) obtain the Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) issued to the partnership; (4) record the non-resident filing status of each foreign partner for direct assessment purposes, as the ELP is fully transparent; and (5) if any physical goods are to be imported, obtain a Gibraltar Customs Code from the Customs & Excise Division. No minimum capital is required, and a licensed Gibraltar professional must remain retained.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: Gibraltar treats the ELP as statutorily tax-transparent under the partnership-assessment provisions of the Income Tax Act 2010. The ELP files for information and assessment purposes only; tax is charged directly on each partner in respect of its agreed share of partnership profits. A passive ESOP-holding structure with foreign-resident partners holds no Gibraltar source income and no permanent establishment risk, and distributions from the ELP carry no Gibraltar withholding tax.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or deemed-company election exists in Gibraltar; transparency is mandatory. Practical available elections include: (i) forming or converting the vehicle as a private-fund limited partnership under the modernised limited-partnership regime, preserving transparency with reduced administrative filings; (ii) electing that the partnership itself be assessed on a single annual return filed in the partnership name, with each partner's share itemised in one assessment notice; and (iii) in hybrid-mismatch scenarios, an election to align the ELP's allocation period with a corporate investor's accounting period under the ATAD-compliant hybrid financial-instrument rules, eliminating deduction/non-inclusion mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No_VAT_System: Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and levies no VAT, GST or sales-and-use tax; the only indirect taxes are General Compensation Duty on imported goods, excise duties on fuel, alcohol and tobacco, and insurance premium tax on Gibraltar-risks policies.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable: since Gibraltar does not levy VAT or GST, no economic threshold triggers indirect-tax registration. Any importer of goods must complete customs formalities regardless of value and hold a Gibraltar Customs Code; excise licensing applies only to alcohol, fuel and tobacco operations. For direct tax, every partnership must obtain a TRN irrespective of revenue, while individual foreign partners are assessed only on Gibraltar-source income.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Gibraltar: without a VAT system there is no 0% export VAT claim and no refund mechanism. Goods physically exported from Gibraltar fall outside the scope of import duties, since General Compensation Duty is levied only upon importation, and cross-border ESOP administration, equity-transfer and dividend-collection services are outside the scope of any Gibraltar indirect tax. Any UK VAT consequences are governed separately by UK VAT law.

  • Gibraltar Export Accompanying Document (EAD) / Single Administrative Declaration, applicable only to physical exports from Gibraltar
  • Commercial invoice and asset-level packing list evidencing the export or the out-of-scope financial-service supply
  • Executed ELP partnership deed and schedule of partners' profit shares supporting the partnership assessment basis under the Income Tax Act 2010
  • Inward remittance evidence, including SWIFT MT103 records, evidencing receipt of ESOP dividend, subscription or share-sale settlement proceeds
  • Tax-residency self-certifications (CRS/FATCA forms) for each foreign-resident partner supporting non-Gibraltar-source treatment

Not applicable: Gibraltar imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous-transaction-control e-invoicing and no digital stamping. Commercial invoices in paper or PDF format satisfy all customs and income-tax documentary requirements; no fiscal-device mandate applies, and the Tax e-Services portal is used exclusively for returns, assessments and status checks.

Gibraltar has no VAT zero-rating regime to claw back; enforcement focuses on customs and income tax. Customs: false export or import declarations trigger forfeiture of goods and civil penalties of up to three times the duty evaded under the Customs and Excise Ordinance, in addition to criminal liability for fraudulent evasion. Income tax: an incorrect claim of foreign-source exemption, or an artificial allocation of ESOP income, attracts retrospective reassessment within six years, interest on unpaid tax at a monthly rate, tax-geared civil penalties typically up to double the tax undercharged, and daily default fines for any unfiled return or notice.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility applies. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) circulates at a 1:1 parity with GBP, and the UK exchange-control regime is not extended to impose restrictions on Gibraltar. Current-account and capital-account transactions, including ESOP trust capital injections, share redemptions, dividend distributions and profit remittances, may be freely converted in GIP, GBP, USD, EUR or any other currency without prior permission. Remittance profits are not subject to withholding or a remittance tax in Gibraltar; only worldwide tax compliance for the parent and beneficiaries remains.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or conversion ratio applies to export receipts or foreign earnings in Gibraltar. The ESOP trust is not required to surrender, repatriate, convert or ring-fence inbound settlement amounts. A financial services licensee must, however, retain complete records of all receipt proceeds and report suspicious transactions to GFSC under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the AML/CFT Guidance Notes. The absence of exchange surrender rules does not exempt the trust from corporate tax record-keeping obligations under Gibraltar's Income Tax Act.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (UK monetary authority) is the ultimate sovereign issuer; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the delegated banking and foreign-exchange regulatory supervisor. Gibraltar has no separate central bank and no separate foreign-exchange administration.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Gibraltar is not an EEA member and does not recognise the UK or EEA e-money passport. A UK-authorised EMI cannot branch into Gibraltar without a GFSC licence; an offshore digital EMI may be used only as a payment gateway feeding into a licensed Gibraltar bank account and cannot independently hold GIP e-money or provide a definitive clearing account. For ESOP payroll and equity transfer rails, pure fintech EMI accounts are not acceptable unless backed by an approved GFSC credit institution and a formal parent-company guarantee.

Late filing triggers an initial penalty of GIP 300, plus GIP 40 per day for each subsequent day of default, up to a maximum of GIP 12,000; a further 5% of any additional tax due may be applied for understating income.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 aggregate value of controlled transactions (Local File); GIP 10,000,000 total controlled transactions for a Master File

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Regulations 2020 (Gibraltar) apply. An ESOP Trust administrator is classified as a 'Distribution and Service Centre' Relevant Activity and must satisfy the economic substance test by operating (a) actual management and control in Gibraltar, (b) sufficient staff and physical presence, and (c) adequate expenditure localised in Gibraltar. Non-resident partners investing passively are not necessarily affected, but the ELP itself must demonstrate the substance if it invoices service fees or holds controlled entities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Gibraltar's Income Tax Act, conviction with intent to defraud may result in a fine of up to 150% of the tax evaded plus a non-negotiable penal tax of GIP 10,000, and imprisonment for up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where a general partner or trustee knowingly participated in the evasion; responsible directors/partners face joint and several civil restitution, surrender of passports, travel bans, and disqualification from managing tax-transparent entities for up to 5 years. In aggravated cases, custodial sentences are imposed.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance (Companies and Limited Partnerships) Regulations 2020, a first contravention attracts a penalty of GIP 50,000; continued non-compliance results in a further daily penalty of GIP 5,000 and potential strike-off from the Gibraltar Companies Registry.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any person exercising control are personally liable for the penalties if they failed to ensure substance compliance; the court may additionally issue personal trading prohibitions and freeze assets, and the Gibraltar regulator may refer the matter to the Financial Intelligence Unit.
AML / Financial Crime

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar), acts of laundering carry a maximum fine of GIP 2,000,000 and/or 14 years' imprisonment; failure to conduct prescribed CDD under the AML/CFT Regulations may Additionally attract fines up to GIP 500,000 and revocation of any registration.

Director personal liability: The ELP’s general partner, trustees, and the board are designated as 'relevant individuals' for AML obligations; personal liability arises for failure to prevent money laundering, leading to civil asset forfeiture, prohibition on holding any fiduciary office for 10 years, and placement on the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission's 'enforcement and disqualified persons' register.
