Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax) under the Ministry of Finance for direct taxation; Her Majesty's Customs, Gibraltar (Customs and Excise Department) for customs, import duty and excise obligations. Official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi (taxes section); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) electronic registry is the entry point for ELP formation and statutory filings.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Gibraltar Exempt Limited Partnership ('ELP') is first incorporated/registered with the GFSC under the Limited Partnerships Act 2015. For direct tax, the general partner must file the partnership registration particulars with the Income Tax Office and apply for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN); each partner's identity, beneficial ownership and residence details must be disclosed. No VAT registration is obtainable because Gibraltar does not impose VAT/GST. The ELP must separately register as a Customs Declarant/Economic Operator with Gibraltar Customs if it imports or exports commercial goods. Standard supporting documents: partnership agreement, certificate of formation, registered-office certificate from a licensed local service provider, and partner residency/beneficial-ownership certificates.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible – the ELP is fiscally transparent under Gibraltar income tax law; the partnership itself is not an entity-level taxpayer. Each partner is chargeable on their respective share of Gibraltar-source profits (Income Tax Act 2010, as amended). Non-resident partners are taxable in Gibraltar only if a trade is carried on in Gibraltar and profits arise there; offshore franchise royalties and syrup-export co-ordination income may fall outside Gibraltar taxable income where the ELP lacks a Gibraltar trade nexus and complies with territorial-source rules. — A foreign-owned Gibraltar Exempt Limited Partnership ('ELP') is first incorporated/registered with the GFSC under the Limited Partnerships Act 2015. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – the ELP is fiscally transparent under Gibraltar income tax law; the partnership itself is not an entity-level taxpayer. Each partner is chargeable on their respective share of Gibraltar-source profits (Income Tax Act 2010, as amended). Non-resident partners are taxable in Gibraltar only if a trade is carried on in Gibraltar and profits arise there; offshore franchise royalties and syrup-export co-ordination income may fall outside Gibraltar taxable income where the ELP lacks a Gibraltar trade nexus and complies with territorial-source rules.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists under Gibraltar law. The ELP is transparent by default; a partnership-agreement election that confers separate legal personality on the ELP does not convert it into a taxable entity for Gibraltar direct tax. Hybrid-mismatch counteraction rules aligned with OECD BEPS Action 2 and ATAD principles apply: a foreign-jurisdiction election to treat the ELP as opaque will be disregarded where it creates deduction/no-inclusion outcomes in the franchise royalty and syrup supply structure. A consolidated partnership return may be filed for administrative convenience, but assessed liability remains at partner level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / GST / Sales and Use Tax in Gibraltar (Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area); the applicable indirect-tax regime is import duty and excise duty under the Customs Ordinance, supplemented by stamp duty on relevant documents.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A – there is no VAT/GST registration threshold because no such tax exists. A person making commercial imports into Gibraltar must be registered as a customs declarant with Gibraltar Customs regardless of turnover; each import is subject to an electronic customs entry and payment of any applicable duty.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is legally inapplicable because Gibraltar does not impose VAT/GST. For customs purposes, goods physically exported from Gibraltar to destinations outside the UK–Gibraltar customs territory are not chargeable to Gibraltar import duty on departure; duty-paid goods previously imported and subsequently re-exported may qualify for duty drawback. Offshore B2B services supplied by the ELP (franchise licensing, brand royalties, syrup-supply chain management) are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax and may be excluded from Gibraltar direct tax as non-Gibraltar-source income, provided no trade is carried on within Gibraltar and the ELP meets applicable economic-substance and territoriality requirements.

  • Gibraltar Customs export declaration (electronic entry/SAD equivalent with export release number)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical removal from Gibraltar and, where relevant, export outside the UK–Gibraltar Single Customs Territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT MT103 or equivalent evidencing receipt of franchise royalty and export-sale proceeds within 180 days
  • Franchise/master licence agreement, royalty invoice schedule and transfer-pricing documentation for the syrup-supply chain
  • Duty-paid import entry and drawback claim reference where re-exporting goods previously imported into Gibraltar
  • Beneficial ownership and partner tax-residence certificates substantiating the pass-through claim and hybrid-mismatch audit trail

No real-time e-invoicing, fiscal digital stamping or government clearance mandate exists for domestic supplies or services. Import/export entries are filed electronically through the Gibraltar Customs electronic entry-processing system; post-Brexit UK–Gibraltar Single Customs Territory applies to qualifying goods movements between Gibraltar and the UK. B2B invoices and royalty invoices must be retained in original audit-ready form but do not require pre-clearance or digital stamping.

False export, duty-drawback or offshore-service claims are subject to intensive audit scrutiny. Customs penalties under the Customs Ordinance include forfeiture of goods and monetary penalties of up to 200% of duty evaded, plus criminal prosecution for fraudulent declarations. Income-tax misdeclaration — such as booking Gibraltar-source franchise income as offshore royalties to avoid tax — attracts retrospective clawback (up to 6 years; extended to 20 years for fraud or neglect), surcharges up to 100% of the tax understated, interest, GAAR counteraction, and possible cancellation or disqualification of the ELP's registration. Non-resident partner documentation and economic-substance evidence are reviewed under UK–Gibraltar and OECD exchange-of-information arrangements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility for current and capital account items; no exchange controls, no prior approval thresholds, and no withholding taxes on profit remittance or capital repatriation from a tax-transparent Gibraltar ELP. GIP and GBP circulate interchangeably at par; free conversion between GIP, GBP, EUR and USD is permitted, with only standard bank-level reporting. The ELP may denominate franchise agreements, trade contracts and bank accounts in any reserve currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender, forced conversion ratio, or retention timeline applies to export proceeds or service revenues in Gibraltar. Franchise royalty receipts and syrup-trade proceeds may be held indefinitely in their original currency; the only obligation is routine balance-of-payments statistical disclosure by regulated credit institutions for settlements at or above EUR 10,000 equivalent.

Central bank supervision: Commissioner of Currency, H.M. Government of Gibraltar (currency issuance and maintenance of the Gibraltar pound at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP under the Currency Note Act 1934); banking, e-money and FX conduct supervision vested in the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019 and Banking Act 2019.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium-High. UK-authorised EMIs such as Wise Business, Revolut Business and Currencycloud routinely accept Gibraltar-registered entities under the UK-Gibraltar financial services continuity framework; non-UK EMIs without GFSC authorisation are not viable. EMIs may not accept deposits or extend credit, and all safeguarded funds must be held with a GFSC-licensed credit institution. Treasury cap: GBP 150,000 per EMI, swept daily to Barclays or Gibraltar International Bank to preserve purpose-code audit trails.
