Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital; the ELP is capital-flexible and partnership capital is fixed solely by the Limited Partnership Agreement. No escrow, blocked account, or pre-funding arrangement is mandated by Gibraltar law. Contributions may be made in cash, in kind, or by service; the liability of each Limited Partner is statutorily capped at its agreed capital contribution.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar - Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), official portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax; customs administration: HM Government of Gibraltar Customs (https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/customs).

E-government filing portal

A foreign Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) seeking to operate in Gibraltar must file an Application for Registration (Form G1) with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office, accompanied by the executed partnership deed, Gibraltar registered office address, certificate of foreign registration, full particulars of all general and limited partners, an appointed licensed resident tax representative where applicable, and a declaration of the place of central management and control. The Commissioner issues a Partnership Tax Identification Number (PTIN) upon registration and, for an ELP managed and controlled outside Gibraltar, a Certificate of Non-Residence confirming that Gibraltar tax is not imposed at entity level. Any change in partners, governing law, registered address, or central management and control must be notified to the Income Tax Office within 30 days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. The ELP is mandated to be fiscally transparent; each partner's worldwide share of partnership income is subject to tax in the partner's own jurisdiction. A statutory non-residence determination may be applied for under the Income Tax Act where central management and control is outside Gibraltar. Hybrid mismatch filings and classification self-assessments are required under the Income Tax (Anti-Avoidance) Rules where any partner jurisdiction imposes entity-level taxation on the partnership.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST; Gibraltar import duty, excise duty, and stamp duty regime applicable within the customs territory of Gibraltar

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; Gibraltar levies no VAT or GST, therefore no de minimis turnover threshold triggers indirect tax registration. Any entity undertaking import or export physical movements must register with HM Government of Gibraltar Customs as a declarant and obtain a local Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) reference; customs registration is mandatory regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating as a VAT concept is not applicable because Gibraltar does not levy VAT on brokerage services or goods deliveries. For physical exports of soybeans and yellow corn, the export is relieved from Gibraltar import duty and excise tax where the goods are placed under customs supervision and permanently removed from the customs territory of Gibraltar within the prescribed export timeframe. The ELP's brokerage commission for international agri-commodity vessel carriage is outside the scope of Gibraltar income tax where the services are performed outside Gibraltar, no income arises in Gibraltar, and the partnership holds a valid Certificate of Non-Residence.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed through the Gibraltar Electronic Customs System
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the ELP or its delegated agent
  • Packing List and cargo weight list
  • Bill of Lading evidencing port-to-port ocean carriage
  • Certificate of Origin for the agricultural commodities
  • Phytosanitary Certificate for grain cargo, where required by the destination jurisdiction
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or Swift/Letter of Credit payment evidence demonstrating export proceeds repatriation
  • Bulk vessel charter party and brokerage commission statement

No domestic electronic invoicing clearance, real-time tax approval, or digital fiscal stamping applies to brokerage or agri-commodity trading invoices. Customs export declarations for soybeans, yellow corn, and other bulk agricultural cargo must be filed electronically in the Gibraltar Electronic Customs System, which applies risk-based selectivity; no open-API fiscal clearance is imposed on commercial B2B invoices.

The Gibraltar Income Tax Office and HM Government of Gibraltar Customs maintain joint audit powers over export transactions. Any fraudulent claim of tax-free export status, under-declaration of customs consignment value, or artificial diversion of grain export proceeds triggers retrospective assessment with a standard six-year look-back period, unlimited in cases of fraud. Penalties include civil fines up to twice the unpaid tax or duty, forfeiture of the relevant goods and carrying vessel, criminal prosecution of the responsible partners, revocation of the ELP's non-residence certificate, and retrospective clawback of all allegedly exempt brokerage income plus compound interest at statutory late-payment rates.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full current-account and capital-account convertibility in both the United Kingdom and Gibraltar. No exchange-control legislation restricts inbound equity contributions, shareholder loans, trade receipts, profit distributions, or repatriation to Cayman Islands UBOs. GIP trades at a pegged 1:1 to GBP with no central-bank approval threshold. USD and EUR transactions are unrestricted and may be held freely in multicurrency accounts. Residual frictions are limited to correspondent-bank sanctions screening, enhanced KYC on the Cayman ELP, and CRS/FATCA reporting duties; no licensing or FX-permission bottleneck exists for the ISIC 4631 grain brokerage mandate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. Gibraltar imposes no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no surrender requirement for export proceeds or brokerage commissions received in foreign currency; receipts may be retained, converted, or reinvested at the trader's discretion. The United Kingdom abolished exchange-control restrictions under the Exchange Control Act 1947 repeal in 1979. A purely statistical Bank of England Balance of Payments survey may require threshold reporting for aggregate annual cross-border receipts exceeding GBP 10,000,000, but this is informational and does not affect disposition of funds.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the prudential banking and payment-institution supervisor; the Government of Gibraltar issues Gibraltar Pound (GIP) banknotes under the Currency Notes Act 1934 (as amended) at a statutory 1:1 parity with GBP; the Bank of England is the anchor monetary authority for the sterling area and provides the monetary-policy backstop for the GIP/GBP peg. The UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) regulates UK-authorized EMIs accessible to the structure; the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU-G) supervises AML/CFT obligations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

High viability as a supplementary collections and disbursement rail for low-value brokerage commissions, operating expenses, and FX conversion, using UK FCA-authorized electronic money institutions (Airwallex, Ebury, Currencycloud, Wise Business, Revolut Business). Legal constraints: on the post-Brexit, UK-authorized EMIs cannot automatically passport into Gibraltar; the ELP must either open via the UK corporate trading arm or select a GFSC-authorized EMI. EMIs are structurally incapable of issuing documentary letters of credit, providing trade credit, or hosting formal escrow against LC enforcement; therefore the primary trade settlement must remain with GFSC-licensed banks, with EMIs limited to sub-GBP 250,000 fast-payment flows and day-to-day treasury virtual accounts.
