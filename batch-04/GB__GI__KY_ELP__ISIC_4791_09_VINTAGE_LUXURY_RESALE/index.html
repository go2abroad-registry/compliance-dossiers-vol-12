Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow account requirement for an Exempted Limited Partnership. The ELP must however maintain a registered office in Gibraltar, have a General Partner that is either resident in Gibraltar or a licensed corporate services provider, and file an income tax exemption application with the HM Commissioner of Income Tax to obtain deemed non-residence status.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate from the Gibraltar Fire and Rescue Service under the Fire Prevention Act, covering smoke detection, Class ABC extinguishers, and evacuation plans for any office or warehouse; structural load inspection certificate from the Building Surveying Department for mezzanine or high-density storage racks; workplace clearance from the Gibraltar Safety at Work Inspectorate under the Health and Safety at Work Act, including the risk assessment, fixed electrical installation inspection (5-year cycle), and means of egress certification.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office of HM Government of Gibraltar and Gibraltar VAT Office / Customs Division; online portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/hm-government-of-gibraltar (Income Tax) and https://vat.gov.gi (VAT registration and filing).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) formed under Gibraltar's Limited Partnerships Act 2010: (1) file the limited partnership registration statement with the Gibraltar Companies House to obtain the Registration Number; (2) apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar tax reference number (GIT number) via the Business Tax Registration form; (3) if the ELP makes taxable supplies connected with Gibraltar (including distance sales to Gibraltar consumers or the domestic consignment margin scheme), apply for VAT registration on the Gibraltar VAT portal once actual or expected taxable turnover in a rolling 12-month period exceeds the threshold; (4) non-resident ELPs with no Gibraltarian source of income may remain outside the VAT net and merely declare tax-transparent status. Required documents: certificate of registration, partnership agreement, proof of registered office, director/general partner identification, and business plan.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For a non-resident Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) formed under Gibraltar's Limited Partnerships Act 2010: (1) file the limited partnership registration statement with the Gibraltar Companies House to obtain the Registration Number; (2) apply to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar tax reference number (GIT number) via the Business Tax Registration form; (3) if the ELP makes taxable supplies connected with Gibraltar (including distance sales to Gibraltar consumers or the domestic consignment margin scheme), apply for VAT registration on the Gibraltar VAT portal once actual or expected taxable turnover in a rolling 12-month period exceeds the threshold; (4) non-resident ELPs with no Gibraltarian source of income may remain outside the VAT net and merely declare tax-transparent status. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The Gibraltar ELP is fiscally transparent by default under the Income Tax Act 2010, with all income, gains, deductions, and credits flowing through to partners in proportion to their profit shares. The ELP may elect, by written notice to the Commissioner of Income Tax, to be charged to tax as if it were a company (per s.159 of the Income Tax Act 2010), converting to entity-level taxation. This serves as the Gibraltar functional equivalent of a check-the-box election. Hybrid mismatch positions must be assessed against the Taxation (Hybrid and Mismatch Arrangements) Rules implementing OECD BEPS Action 2, particularly where a partner resides in an EU or UK jurisdiction with different entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

12.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GBP 85,000 taxable turnover in a rolling 12-month period (Gibraltar VAT Regulations 2020); voluntary registration permitted. Distance sales to Gibraltar consumers are subject to an annual cross-border threshold equivalent to GBP 85,000, above which the ELP must register for Gibraltar VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Gibraltar VAT-registered ELP in the online consignment and secondhand luxury goods retail sector may zero-rate: (i) supply of goods physically exported from Gibraltar to a destination outside Gibraltar or deemed exported on evidence of removal, including goods sold to consumers in the UK or EU (consumption outside Gibraltar); and (ii) supply of most services to a business customer established outside Gibraltar (place of supply of customer) or to a non-business customer where the service is connected with overseas property or performed outside Gibraltar. Consignment sales of vintage luxury goods sold through marketplaces may alternatively fall under Gibraltar's second-hand goods margin scheme, limiting VAT to the margin on the sale. The zero-rating claim requires documentary proof of export within three months, the customer's identity and overseas address, and full consideration paid by or on behalf of the overseas buyer.

  • Gibraltar Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document or electronic equivalent)
  • Commercial Invoice / Consignment Note describing the luxury item, serial number, authenticity certificate, and value
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from Gibraltar to an overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank SWIFT confirmation evidencing receipt of foreign currency funds into the ELP's bank account within the repatriation deadline
  • Packing list, originals of authenticity/provenance documentation, and KYC/AML due diligence records for the overseas buyer

Gibraltar does not operate a mandatory real-time Continuous Transaction Control (CTC) clearance or digital stamping regime. All VAT invoices must however be issued through compliant electronic or paper systems, retain sequential numbering, contain the statutory VAT invoice data points, and be stored electronically for six years. VAT returns (Form VAT 102) must be filed online through the Gibraltar VAT portal by the 20th day following the end of each quarterly tax period. The customs export process uses the Gibraltar Electronic Customs System for export declarations, with Customs Handling Import and Export Freight (CHIEF) or its successor CDS for goods moving between Gibraltar and the UK/EU.

The Gibraltar Commissioner of Income Tax may disallow zero-rating and assess VAT at 12.5% plus default interest if export evidence is incomplete or funds are not repatriated within 180 days without valid commercial justification. Fraudulent abuse of export zero-rating incurs penalties up to 100% of the VAT evaded for deliberate and concealed conduct, or 70% for deliberate but not concealed conduct; criminal prosecution may result in fines and imprisonment of up to seven years under the Customs (Penalties and Miscellaneous Provisions) Act and the VAT legislation. The ELP and its general partner are jointly and severally liable for clawback amounts, and the Commissioner may also levy assessments on the underlying consignor if benefits inure to that person.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility exists for both current-account and capital-account transactions. No Gibraltar or UK exchange-control statute restricts remittance of profits, dividends, capital, or liquidation proceeds payable to non-resident partners of an Exempted Limited Partnership. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is legal tender only in Gibraltar and is pegged at par to GBP; most banking operations are settled in GBP, EUR, or USD. Practical hurdles are bank-driven rather than statutory: disbursement of partner capital and profit share requires a current tax residency certificate, CRS/FATCA self-certification, and evidence of economic substance; any payment involving sanctioned jurisdictions is prohibited by HM Treasury/OFSI regulations. EU AMLD-derived AML obligations impose source-of-funds verification for remittances above the equivalent of EUR 10,000.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio apply to export proceeds in the UK or Gibraltar. Currency earned from cross-border online luxury resale may be retained in the original currency or converted at the operator's election; there is no surrender obligation to the Central Bank or Government of Gibraltar. The residual obligations are reporting only: (i) overseas inward payments above EUR 10,000 trigger UK Money Laundering Regulations record-keeping and Suspicious Activity Report thresholds; (ii) settlement flows routed through Stripe and Escrow_Pay must be reconciled and disclosed to the bank; (iii) CRS and Gibraltar Automatic Exchange of Information reporting applies to partner income attributable to export profits.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority and sterling clearing oversight); HM Treasury and Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) (financial sanctions and FX-related controls); Government of Gibraltar (issue of Gibraltar pound notes pegged 1:1 to sterling); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) (prudential supervision of Gibraltar-licensed banks and electronic money institutions). There is no standalone central bank or exchange-control authority; foreign exchange policy follows the UK regime via the pegged Gibraltar pound.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a secondary settlement rail, but LOW as the sole treasury account. Gibraltar-licensed or UK FCA-authorised EMIs (Airwallex, Wise Business, Currencycloud, Revolut Business) can open accounts remotely for a Gibraltar ELP and integrate with Stripe and Escrow_Pay sweeps. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not deposit-taking banks, so client funds are not protected by the Gibraltar Deposit Guarantee Scheme or the UK FSCS; (ii) safeguarding arrangements require client money to be held in segregated accounts at an approved bank; (iii) EMIs routinely reject or restrict e-commerce merchants with third-party consignor payout structures and high turnover velocity unless a full merchant KYC file, authentication framework, and AML risk assessment are provided; (iv) all EMI traffic is subject to HM Treasury sanctions screening; (v) the ELP must still maintain at least one conventional bank account with a Gibraltar-licensed bank to satisfy substance expectations and transaction-banking requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner capital contributions must be routed via SWIFT MT103 (or CHAPS/Faster Payments for sterling within the UK) with the remittance string stating the purpose as equity contribution or capital injection under the OECD/EU balance-of-payments classification (direct investment category) and referencing the ELP's Gibraltar company number plus an internal verification code. The receiving bank will place a hold until (i) the partnership deed and register of beneficial owners are verified; (ii) CRS/FATCA self-certifications for all partners are filed; (iii) a source-of-funds questionnaire with supporting evidence (bank statements, sale agreement, audited accounts) is delivered; and (iv) HM Treasury sanctions screening is clear. Crypto-asset or unregulated instrument transfers are not acceptable for capital injection; only regulated fiat wires from the partner's own account are compliant.Three compliant clearing tiers are available: (1) domestic GBP payouts to UK and Gibraltar consignors and service providers via Faster Payments for sub-1m value and CHAPS for high value; (2) cross-border settlement to vendors, authenticators, and logistics partners via SWIFT MT103 with invoice-referenced purpose codes for merchandise trade, accompanied by commercial invoice and authentication certificates; (3) commercial Letters of Credit for high-value vintage assets where the bank examines transport documents, authentication reports, insurance, and the consignment contract before payment. Platform-generated payouts from Stripe and Escrow_Pay must be swept daily to the operational account and matched against the sales ledger; each beneficiary must be pre-vetted under the vendor KYC file and OFSI sanctions screening.Outright FX spot trades in GBP/EUR/USD major pairs under a pre-agreed treasury dealing mandate.Outright FX forwards (GBP/EUR, EUR/USD, GBP/USD) with tenors up to 12 months, requiring bank credit-line approval but no regulatory licence.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for illiquid or restricted currencies arising from buyer-side refunds or vendor payouts in non-convertible markets.Cross-currency swaps to hedge sustained multi-currency settlement mismatches between escrow balances and GBP-denominated operating costs.Structured FX collars and participating forwards, subject to an internal derivatives governance policy and general-partner approval.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM-HIGH as a secondary settlement rail, but LOW as the sole treasury account. Gibraltar-licensed or UK FCA-authorised EMIs (Airwallex, Wise Business, Currencycloud, Revolut Business) can open accounts remotely for a Gibraltar ELP and integrate with Stripe and Escrow_Pay sweeps. Legal constraints: (i) EMIs are not deposit-taking banks, so client funds are not protected by the Gibraltar Deposit Guarantee Scheme or the UK FSCS; (ii) safeguarding arrangements require client money to be held in segregated accounts at an approved bank; (iii) EMIs routinely reject or restrict e-commerce merchants with third-party consignor payout structures and high turnover velocity unless a full merchant KYC file, authentication framework, and AML risk assessment are provided; (iv) all EMI traffic is subject to HM Treasury sanctions screening; (v) the ELP must still maintain at least one conventional bank account with a Gibraltar-licensed bank to satisfy substance expectations and transaction-banking requirements.
