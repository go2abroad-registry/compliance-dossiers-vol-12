Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax); portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax. Companies Registry Gibraltar (limited partnership registration); portal: https://www.egibl.gi. E-government filing portal

A foreign Exempted Limited Partnership is generally tax transparent, so the ELP itself is not the statutory income taxpayer. The ELP must first be registered with the Gibraltar Companies Registry. If the partnership has a Gibraltar permanent establishment, carries on trade in Gibraltar, or derives Gibraltar-source income, a Form IT-REG tax registration application must be filed with the Income Tax Office, attaching partner names, foreign tax identification numbers, and the partnership agreement. No VAT ID is required because Gibraltar has no VAT/GST. For physical import/export of goods, the ELP must additionally open an HM Customs Gibraltar trader account; this is not required for cloud, database, and API monitoring services supplied as exports.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The default status is pass-through transparency. The partners may elect for entity-level taxation only in limited circumstances provided under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act; this election must be evidenced in the partnership agreement and notified to the Commissioner of Income Tax. Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Hybrid mismatch rules aligned with OECD BEPS Action 2 apply, and no election is available to opt out of a mandatory mismatch adjustment where the ELP is disregarded.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax; Gibraltar applies customs duties, excise duties, and import/export controls on goods only.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A — there is no VAT/GST turnover threshold in Gibraltar. HM Customs Gibraltar trader registration is required for import/export of physical goods regardless of value; exported digital services are not within any indirect-tax registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable for VAT because Gibraltar does not impose VAT. Export of physical goods may qualify for customs duty relief or draw-back only if the goods are exported from Gibraltar and the prescribed export declaration and proof of export are furnished. The ISIC 6201 services covered by this dossier are exported services falling outside the Gibraltar indirect-tax base; no VAT zero-rating claim or VAT refund is necessary.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar electronic declaration or Single Administrative Document)
  • Commercial invoice describing goods or services and foreign recipient
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank/MT103 payment evidence for offshore consideration
  • Signed service agreement or purchase order defining the non-Gibraltar place of supply

No national real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping mandate exists in Gibraltar for B2B services. Commercial invoices and electronic accounting records are accepted. Goods declarations are filed through HM Customs Gibraltar electronic customs declaration procedures, but developer infrastructure, database, and API monitoring cloud services are not subject to fiscal clearance requirements.

False export-relief, duty-drawback, or zero-rating claims are treated as tax evasion: civil penalties up to 200% of tax or duty at risk, interest, seizure of goods, retrospective clawback of relief where goods are not actually exported or are diverted into Gibraltar consumption, and criminal fines or imprisonment for fraudulent documentation. For exported cloud services, artificial offshore-billing arrangements are recharacterized under Gibraltar's general anti-abuse provisions and hybrid mismatch rules, restoring Gibraltar-source income to the partnership and exposing partners to additional assessments, penalties, and clawback of any improperly obtained relief.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is fully convertible to GBP and other major currencies. Both current and capital account transactions are unrestricted. There are no exchange control regulations, no prior approval requirements for profit remittance, and no restrictions on the repatriation of capital, dividends, or interest. The jurisdiction operates a free capital movement regime consistent with EU directives (as retained under the UK-Gibraltar relationship).

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirements exist. Exporters are permitted to retain all foreign currency proceeds in any designated account, and there is no mandatory conversion to GIP or GBP. The timeline for repatriation is unrestricted; however, standard banking practice requires proper documentation for cross-border receipts to satisfy AML/CFT obligations.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the primary regulatory authority for financial services and foreign exchange administration; monetary policy is aligned with the Bank of England (BoE) due to the Gibraltar pound (GIP) being pegged 1:1 to GBP.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High. Gibraltar has a robust fintech ecosystem and a dedicated regulatory framework for Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2011. EMIs licensed in Gibraltar (or passported from the UK/EEA under the temporary permissions regime) can provide multi-currency accounts, virtual IBANs, and cross-border payment rails. However, for an Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) with a cloud infrastructure business, the primary constraint is the EMI's ability to support high-volume B2B transactions and integrate with payment gateways like Stripe and Mercury. Legal constraints include the need for the EMI to be fully licensed and to conduct enhanced due diligence on the ELP's ultimate beneficial owners (UBOs). The ELP should maintain a fallback relationship with a tier-1 bank to mitigate concentration risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant wire transfer protocols: Inbound capital injections from the parent entity (KY_ELP) or investors must be routed via SWIFT MT103 with a clear purpose code. For Gibraltar, the appropriate purpose codes are: 'G' for general capital contributions, 'D' for direct investment, or 'S' for services (if the injection is structured as a loan). The bank will require a copy of the partnership agreement and a board resolution (or equivalent) authorizing the capital call. Verification includes source of funds documentation and a signed declaration of the economic purpose. For transfers from the UK, use the UK's purpose code 'A' (for investment) or 'B' (for loans). All transfers must be in GIP, GBP, EUR, or USD; the bank will convert at prevailing rates. No prior approval is needed from the GFSC, but the bank may conduct enhanced due diligence for amounts exceeding €10,000.Clearing rails for trade settlements: For B2B payments to suppliers and vendors, the primary rail is SWIFT MT103 for international wire transfers. For high-value or recurring payments, consider using a commercial letter of credit (LC) via the issuing bank, especially for cross-border procurement of cloud infrastructure hardware. For intra-EEA payments, use SEPA (Single Euro Payments Area) if the counterparty is in the EEA, with the IBAN and BIC. For USD payments, use the Fedwire or CHIPS network via correspondent banks. The ELP should maintain multi-currency accounts to minimize conversion costs. For subscription-based revenue from customers, integrate with Stripe Billing (which uses card networks and local acquiring) and Mercury ACH (for US-based clients). Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

10% (statutory corporate rate under the Income Tax Act 2010; an Exempted Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent and partners are assessed on their allocable share, the 10% rate applying only where profits derive from Gibraltar sources)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Gibraltar imposes no withholding tax on dividends or partnership distributions; a distribution by the ELP to a non-resident partner exits Gibraltar entirely free of source withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: £1,000,000 annual aggregate of cross-border controlled transactions triggers contemporaneous Gibraltar Local File documentation; Master File and group documentation are expected where worldwide group turnover exceeds £50,000,000. The Gibraltar Income Tax Office endorsed the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines through the 2018 Gibraltar Transfer Pricing Guidelines and applies substance-over-form comparability analysis.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Companies and Partnerships) Regulations 2019 apply because data/API monitoring and developer infrastructure constitute 'provision of services' and 'distribution centre' relevant activities. The ELP must maintain adequate physical premises, full-time employees, operational expenditure, and board/mind-and-management presence in Gibraltar proportionate to the activity. Annual ESR filing is mandatory irrespective of the 0% tax outcome; non-compliance triggers rebuttable-presumption exchange of information with UK HMRC and the EU Code of Conduct Group.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — deliberate omission or misdeclaration of Gibraltar-source income

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to 100% of the underpaid tax plus 4% interest under ss.77–79 of the Income Tax Act 2010; criminal prosecution with an unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 5 years for false statements in tax returns.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are personally liable for fraudulent returns; Gibraltar courts will pierce the ELP veil to order civil restitution; travel bans and asset-freezing orders may issue under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 together with disqualification for up to 20 years.
Shell or sham economic substance arrangement

Statutory sanction: ESR financial penalties up to £10,000 per failure plus £1,000 per day of continued contravention; compulsory strike-off and revocation of the exemption; exchange of information with UK HMRC and publication on registries of shell entities.

Director personal liability: Individual general partners and directors of the corporate general partner face personal fines, disqualification, and liability to creditors; the Commissioner can notify border enforcement agencies, triggering a travel ban pending full economic-substance remediation.
AML and beneficial ownership violation (Money Laundering Regulations 2019, Proceeds of Crime Act 2015)

Statutory sanction: Civil penalties up to £1,000,000 under the Money Laundering Regulations 2019; criminal penalties of an unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 7 years; suspension of payment processing and closure of Gibraltar bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners are 'relevant persons' with personal criminal exposure; courts will freeze personal bank accounts and impose travel bans; extraterritorial U.S. OFAC and UK sanctions lists may mirror the restraint orders.
Transfer pricing and information-return non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Denial of the intercompany deduction plus primary and consequential transfer pricing adjustments with interest; financial penalty of £3,000 plus £600 per day of continued non-compliance; exchange of information and country-by-country reporting penalties enforced in partner states.

Director personal liability: The Commissioner may assess the corporate general partner jointly and severally; director indemnity is voided for non-arm's-length de facto distributions; personal surcharges up to £50,000 per director and referral for director disqualification.
