Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax); online portal https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi; import duty and customs registry administered by HM Customs and Excise, Gibraltar.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP): (1) register the ELP at Companies House Gibraltar under the Limited Partnerships Act and obtain the LP registration number and certificate; (2) file the partnership tax return (P-Return / Form IT-1) with the Income Tax Office within 30 days of registration, submitting the constitutional documents, certified partner details, and the Gibraltar registered-office address; (3) the Income Tax Office issues a six-to-seven digit Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN), which is also used for FATCA/CRS reporting; (4) if the ELP moves goods, obtain a Customs Business Trading (CBT) number from HM Customs and Excise (no VAT ID exists because Gibraltar has no VAT); (5) non-resident partners with no Gibraltar-source income are not chargeable and receive nil assessments.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible (Territorial_Scope): the ELP is fiscally transparent with no entity-level charge; partners are assessed individually, and non-resident partners are taxable only on income accruing in or derived from Gibraltar. — For a foreign-owned Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP): (1) register the ELP at Companies House Gibraltar under the Limited Partnerships Act and obtain the LP registration number and certificate; (2) file the partnership tax return (P-Return / Form IT-1) with the Income Tax Office within 30 days of registration, submitting the constitutional documents, certified partner details, and the Gibraltar registered-office address; (3) the Income Tax Office issues a six-to-seven digit Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN), which is also used for FATCA/CRS reporting; (4) if the ELP moves goods, obtain a Customs Business Trading (CBT) number from HM Customs and Excise (no VAT ID exists because Gibraltar has no VAT); (5) non-resident partners with no Gibraltar-source income are not chargeable and receive nil assessments.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (Territorial_Scope): the ELP is fiscally transparent with no entity-level charge; partners are assessed individually, and non-resident partners are taxable only on income accruing in or derived from Gibraltar.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election exists; Gibraltar limited partnerships are compulsorily transparent and cannot elect entity-level taxation. Available statutory elections and mechanisms: (i) tax accounting year-end election by notice to the Commissioner; (ii) advance ruling / advance pricing arrangement confirming treatment of cross-border cold-chain storage fees and exclusion of foreign-source income; (iii) OECD/G20 and EU ATAD2-aligned anti-hybrid rules (Income Tax (Anti-Hybrid) Regulations 2019) that counteract hybrid mismatch outcomes; (iv) no election to apply UK VAT is available because Gibraltar is outside the UK VAT area.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax in Gibraltar; indirect taxation limited to Gibraltar import/customs duty and selective excise duties

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general indirect tax threshold; zero-rate VAT/GST is absent. Any importer/exporter must register for a Customs Business Trading (CBT) number and use a licensed customs broker; import duty applies principally to alcohol, tobacco, and motor fuel, while commercial goods such as cold-chain equipment are generally duty-free. Currency: Gibraltar pound (GIP), pegged 1:1 to GBP.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT zero-rating mechanism. In its place: (i) goods physically exported from Gibraltar are relieved from import/customs duty under in-transit, temporary admission, and re-export/transshipment procedures; (ii) refrigerated storage supplied to a non-resident customer is outside the scope of Gibraltar consumption taxes, and for income-tax purposes the fees are excluded from charge if the contract is concluded abroad, services are performed for a non-resident counterparty, and no profit is attributable to a Gibraltar permanent establishment; (iii) UK VAT may be reverse-charged by the UK business recipient, leaving the Gibraltar ELP without UK VAT liability.

  • Customs export declaration (Single Administrative Document / electronic export entry) identifying the consignee and destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list with cold-chain specification (temperature range 2-8 degrees Celsius for pharmaceuticals)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export and reefer-container integrity
  • Outturn certificate and continuous temperature-logger record from the controlled cold-storage facility (ISIC 5210)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank MT103 SWIFT proof of payment from the foreign buyer
  • Certificate of origin where a preferential duty claim is made, plus pharma GDP/GMP health attestations for regulated perishable pharmaceuticals

No electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping mandate exists. Paper or PDF invoices satisfy Income Tax Act record-keeping; customs declarations are filed electronically through the Single Administrative Document (SAD) / national customs entry system. The UK-Gibraltar arrangements do not create a VAT filing obligation.

Export reliefs are audited by the Income Tax Office and HM Customs; assessments may be reopened up to 6 years, extending to 21 years in cases of fraud or wilful default. False export claims trigger: (i) recovery of underpaid duties/taxes with interest; (ii) civil tax penalties up to 100% of tax avoided under the Income Tax Act 2010; (iii) customs penalties and seizure/forfeiture of goods with duty penalties up to three times the duty evaded under the Customs Ordinance; (iv) criminal prosecution with imprisonment for fraudulent declarations; (v) retrospective clawback of relief where goods are not exported within the prescribed period or foreign-exchange proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound is fully convertible in both the capital and current accounts; there is no exchange-control legislation, no prior approval regime for foreign direct investment, and no restriction on the repatriation of profits, capital, or tax-transparent partnership distributions to non-resident limited partners. The statutory 1:1 GIP/GBP peg, supported by a 100% foreign-exchange backing requirement for notes, effectively eliminates convertibility or transfer risk for a Gibraltar exempted limited partnership. Remittances of warehousing fee income earned from EU or UK pharmaceutical clients may be denominated in GBP, EUR, or USD at commercial discretion; however, banks will require that underlying invoices and contracts evidence commercial substance in the refrigerated logistics sector to distinguish income flows from mere tax-driven pass-through movements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or prescribed repatriation timelines applicable in Gibraltar. Export earnings derived from cross-border cold-chain and refrigerated warehousing services may be retained indefinitely in the original invoicing currency inside the ELP's operational bank account, and there is no obligation to convert such proceeds into Gibraltar pounds or any other currency within any defined period. The only practical constraint is the correspondent-bank policy of the receiving Gibraltar institution, which may require documentary evidence of the underlying trade invoice where aggregate foreign-currency inflows exceed a bank-defined threshold, but no such threshold is imposed by regulation.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar does not maintain a resident central bank or independent monetary authority. The Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the competent authority for authorisation and prudential supervision of banks under the Financial Services (Banking) Act 2019 and the Financial Services (Banking) Regulations 2020, and also discharges AML/CFT supervision under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015. Currency issuance for the Gibraltar pound (GIP), which is exchangeable at par and at no cost with the British pound sterling (GBP), is effected through the Royal Gibraltar Post Office under the Currency Notes Act 1934, with HM Government of Gibraltar retaining denomination and monetary design authority. For purposes of foreign exchange policy and clearing settlement, Gibraltar relies upon the Bank of England's sterling framework and the UK Faster Payments and CHAPS systems, although no formal delegation of bank supervision exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium. Licensed UK and EEA electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Monese, and multi-currency digital banking platforms generally decline m for Gibraltar-resident exempted limited partnerships owing to correspondent-bank restrictions, restricted jurisdictional risk appetites, and the absence of a Gibraltarian e-money passport. GFSC-licensed electronic-money and payment-institution firms do exist locally but offer limited overseas clearing reach, no documented lucidity on ISIC 5210 trade payment flows, and no capital-escrow or partnership-distribution functionality, making them unsuitable as a primary treasury rail for this ELP. A Gibraltar-licensed EMI may however be used as a secondary sub-account for receiving low-value cold-chain monitoring fees, provided the EMI maintains Tier-1 UK correspondent clearing and accepts the ELP's GFSC-regulated status as a de-risking mitigant. Any unregulated offshore digital EMI is non-viable and would constitute an AML compliance defect for the General Partner.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from non-resident partners must be remitted from each partner's own bank account in the partner's country of tax residence using SWIFT MT103 or, for larger contributions above EUR 350,000, MT202 with cover, labelled with the receiving reference 'Capital Contribution of [Partner Name] to Exempted Limited Partnership [ELP_REF]'. The sender bank must apply the ISO 2022 transaction purpose code 'FUND' (Financial Grant) or 'EQUI' (Equity participation) and include the Limited Partnership Agreement date in field 72 of the MT103. Gibraltar banks will deposit the funds into a segregated Capital Escrow Account, which cannot be drawn down until the bank receives an attorney's due-diligence certificate, the executed subscription schedule, and a copy of the GFSC or Gibraltar Companies House acknowledgment of the partnership registration. There is no FX conversion obligation on inward injections, but where a partner remits in USD, the bank will convert into GBP for the escrow ledger only if the Partnership Agreement defines capital in GBP; a Strategic-Controlled Escrow held in USD is permissible when the operating currency of the refrigerated logistics venture is USD.Two clearing rails apply for trade settlements. First, SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers are used for operating disbursements of the cold-storage business, including refrigerated warehouse lease instalments, electricity and REEFER energy costs, cold-chain monitoring subscriptions, freight forwarding, and invoices of temperature-validated packaging suppliers; these transfers must carry ISO 20022 purpose codes SUPP, TRCP, or P/L as appropriate, and the Gibraltar paying bank will require a digital copy of the underlying invoice for transactions above GBP 10,000. Second, Commercial Letters of Credit, arranged through the bank's trade finance department, settle large supply contracts for pharmaceutical-grade cold-storage equipment; documents to be presented are a clean bill of lading or CMR, a pharma-cold-chain certificate of inspection, invoice, packing list, and a temperature-log attestation, conformed to UCP 600. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File documentation is required when controlled transactions exceed GBP 900,000 in a fiscal year; Master File documentation is required where consolidated group turnover exceeds GBP 60,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue above EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar’s Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to ELPs engaged in relevant activities such as distribution and service centres, financing and leasing, holding companies, intellectual property, or shipping. Cold-chain warehousing is generally classified as a physical logistics activity and not a relevant activity unless provided as part of an integrated group distribution operation; in that event, annual ESR returns and substance indicators (premises, employees, management, and core income-generating activities in Gibraltar) are mandatory.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Imprisonment up to 7 years under Gibraltar’s Criminal Justice Act 2011; monetary penalty of up to 200% of the tax evaded; asset seizure; cancellation of tax clearance and withdrawal of the cold-storage licence.

Director personal liability: The general partner of an ELP is jointly and severally liable for partnership tax liabilities; directors who sign false returns face personal civil restitution, travel bans, and disqualification from acting as directors for up to 10 years.
Shell Company Arrangement and Failure of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: ESR breach penalties of up to GBP 10,000 per return plus GBP 1,000 per day of continued non-compliance; automatic recharacterisation of fees as Gibraltar-source taxable profits; denial of treaty benefits and mandatory CRS exchange of information.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be personally assessed for the unpaid tax under the Tax Collection and Management Act; a 5-year travel ban and prohibition from holding any fiduciary office may be imposed.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Breach

Statutory sanction: Fine up to GBP 2 million and imprisonment up to 14 years under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; confiscation of warehouse assets; suspension or permanent revocation of the statutory storage licence.

Director personal liability: The general partner and designated compliance officers are personally liable for AML failures; individual fines up to GBP 75,000 and injunctions may be issued; the corporate veil is pierceable where the ELP is used as a conduit for laundering logistics payments.
Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment with interest at 1.5% per month; penalty equal to 40% of the additional tax where documentation is absent or inadequate; publication on the TCD’s tax defaulters list.

Director personal liability: Operating directors who approve knowingly distorted invoices are treated as joint tortfeasors; personal liability can extended to the settled tax difference plus penalties; European and UK asset-seizure orders may be obtained through the Gibraltar courts.
