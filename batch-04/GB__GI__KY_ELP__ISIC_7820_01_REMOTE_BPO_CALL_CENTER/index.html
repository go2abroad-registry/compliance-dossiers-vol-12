Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar, Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax, Ministry of Finance); online portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation with e-services filing; Customs matters: Gibraltar Customs Department (import duty only).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the ELP with the Gibraltar Companies Registry (Registry of Companies) under the Exempt Limited Partnerships Act 2014, appointing a Gibraltar-resident general partner and registered office. Step 2: Within 30 days of registration, file the income tax registration application (Partnership Registration Form) with the Income Tax Office. Step 3: Submit the partnership deed, registered office certificate, general partner particulars, and a signed non-resident declaration for each limited partner. Step 4: Obtain the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) and activate the ITO e-services account. Step 5: File annual partnership information returns; non-resident partners with no Gibraltar-source income require no individual TIN and fall outside the charge to income tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible: the ELP is statutorily transparent; each partner is assessed in their own name on their share of partnership profits. Territorial scope applies: only income arising in Gibraltar (e.g., BPO service revenue generated by a Gibraltar-based call center) is chargeable; ELPs performing services wholly outside Gibraltar and earning non-Gibraltar-source income are outside the tax net. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the ELP is statutorily transparent; each partner is assessed in their own name on their share of partnership profits. Territorial scope applies: only income arising in Gibraltar (e.g., BPO service revenue generated by a Gibraltar-based call center) is chargeable; ELPs performing services wholly outside Gibraltar and earning non-Gibraltar-source income are outside the tax net. No automatic tax-exempt zone status exists beyond the territorial exclusion.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists; transparency is mandatory for Gibraltar ELPs. Available mechanisms: (1) UK-connected partners may file the UK CTA 2009 s.59C look-through declaration or, where eligible, the s.59D election to align opaque/transparent treatment; (2) CRS/GATCA self-certification of entity classification to avoid hybrid partnership mismatch; (3) written election to align the partnership basis period with a partner's accounting year; (4) no new exempt-company elections available (legacy exempt status abolished).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax in Gibraltar; only residual Import Duty on a limited basket of dutiable goods (alcoholic beverages, tobacco, mineral hydrocarbons). Offshore BPO services (ISIC 7820) are outside the scope of any Gibraltar indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. Import Duty registration with Gibraltar Customs is required only for importers of duty-liable goods (no monetary threshold); BPO service providers have no indirect-tax registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - the absence of VAT/GST means offshore BPO services are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect tax; there is no 0% VAT claim or refund mechanism. For direct tax, the ELP must evidence that services are performed and managed from Gibraltar to sustain a Gibraltar-source position; any claim that profits are non-Gibraltar-source requires demonstrable offshore delivery, management, and decision-making substance.

  • Customs Export Declaration (N/A for pure services; required only for physical goods movements)
  • Service Agreement / SOW for multilingual helpdesk and outbound tele-sales outsourcing
  • Commercial invoice and time-stamped service delivery records (call logs, agent rosters, CRM reports)
  • Inward remittance certificate / SWIFT or Wise B2B payment confirmation
  • Economic substance file (Gibraltar premises lease, payroll records, board/partner decisions, AML/KYC file)

None. Gibraltar imposes no real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or digital stamping mandate. Service invoices follow English commercial practice; invoices and supporting records must be retained for six years for income tax audit purposes.

No VAT clawback applies. The Income Tax Office may reassess under the Income Tax Act 2010 within the standard six-year window from the end of the year of assessment, extended to 21 years for fraud or neglect. Anti-avoidance powers (ITA 2010 s.17) permit counteraction of excessive tax advantages. Penalties: up to 100% of under-assessed tax for deliberate concealment, 50-70% for deliberate non-concealment, up to 30% for careless errors; daily default fines and interest on late tax; criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment for fraudulent returns. ELP partners subject to UK or EU hybrid-mismatch rules additionally face counterpart counteraction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at parity to GBP under a currency-board arrangement. There are no exchange controls on current-account or capital-account transactions. Profit remittances by a Cayman Exempted Limited Partnership are fully convertible without surrender requirements, exchange-control approval, or conversion restrictions. The only practical hurdles are (i) production of a UK/Gibraltar tax opinion to satisfy beneficial-owner documentation and (ii) compliance with anti-hybrid rules that could disallow treaty benefits or create UK attribution issues; none materially impede currency conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar and the United Kingdom impose no statutory surrender timelines, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation windows for export proceeds. BPO service receipts from call-center contracts may be freely settled in GIP, GBP, EUR, USD, or other currencies. Only FATCA/CRS reporting, suspicious transaction reporting, and standard AML documentation apply; no surrender obligation exists.

Central bank supervision: HM Treasury (UK fiscal authority), Bank of England (monetary anchor), Gibraltar Currency Commissioner (issue and gold/sterling backing for GIP), and Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (prudential supervision and AML/CTF compliance).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Medium-to-Low. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise Business, Currencycloud, Mistertango) may accept a Cayman ELP only where the ELP demonstrates a real UK/Gibraltar operational nexus, clean source of funds, and audited financials. EMI sub-accounts are not equal to bank accounts, often restrict currency rails to SEPA/local GBP clearing, and apply wider FX spreads. They are viable only for small-volume ancillary disbursements and payouts to remote agents; core treasury flows must remain with Tier 1 licensed banks.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% statutory Gibraltar withholding tax on dividends paid by resident companies. Distributions by an Exempted Limited Partnership are not dividends under Gibraltar law and are not subject to any Gibraltar withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be maintained when the Gibraltar ELP or its associated enterprises complete aggregate related-party controlled transactions exceeding GBP 1,000,000 in an accounting period, or when the Gibraltar entity's annual gross revenue exceeds GBP 10,000,000. A Master File is required where the ultimate multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds GBP 50,000,000. Even below these bright-line thresholds, sufficient comparable evidence and a written transfer-pricing policy must be available because Gibraltar adopts OECD BEPS Action 8-10 principles and the UK-Gibraltar exchange framework permits challenge of remote BPO fee arrangements.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies Economic Substance Requirements derived from EU/OECD harmful-tax practice standards and the Gibraltar Economic Substance Guidance. An ELP engaged in a relevant activity, including a service-center or distribution and service-center activity through a remote BPO/call-center, must be managed and controlled in Gibraltar. Core income-generating activities must be performed by adequate, full-time employees physically present in Gibraltar, with suitable premises and proportionate operating expenditure. Call-center activities outsourced outside Gibraltar must be monitored and controlled from Gibraltar; outsourcing to a related party outside Gibraltar may deny the substance benefit. Failure to meet these requirements triggers notification obligations, substantive penalties, and loss of tax residence certificate or treaty access.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through False Non-Residence or Ghost BPO

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the ELP as a Gibraltar-resident taxable person; assessment of 10% corporation tax on the full undeclared profit; interest at 8% per annum; civil penalties of up to 200% of the tax evaded; criminal prosecution for fraudulent tax evasion with fines unlimited and imprisonment up to seven years.

Director personal liability: The general partner, shadow directors, and any limited partner exercising management control lose partnership law protection. Courts may pierce the ELP veil, impose joint and several liability for unpaid tax, issue travel bans, asset freezing orders, disqualification orders of up to 15 years, and confiscation of proceeds derived from offshore BPO operations.
Transfer-Pricing Fraud or Artificial Markup Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Transfer-pricing adjustment to the arm's length profit; denial of the related-party expense in the paying jurisdiction; secondary adjustment treating the excessive charge as a hidden distribution; penalties of up to 100% of the adjusted tax; enhanced monitoring and information exchange under the UK-Gibraltar tax information agreement.

Director personal liability: Officers who sign or cause false TP returns may be personally liable through fraudulent trading provisions. The corporate veil of a limited-partner vehicle can be pierced; directors may face personal restitution, disqualification, travel bans, and imprisonment for knowingly providing false statements to tax authorities.
Shell Company / Artifical Economic Substance Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar ESR notification penalties of up to GBP 10,000; continued noncompliance penalties of up to GBP 100,000; refusal of tax residence certificate; strike-off cancellation from the Gibraltar Register; the entity may be characterized as a shell and placed on an international blacklist, resulting in denial of DTA/TIEA benefits.

Director personal liability: The directors and the general partner cannot hide behind limited liability or partnership form where the arrangement is a sham. The court may order personal contribution to the ELP's tax debts, impose civil punitive fines, bar the individuals from serving as directors for a defined period, and restrict travel while tax investigations are pending.
AML, CTF, and Payment-Rails Misuse

Statutory sanction: Violation of Gibraltar's AML/CFT Regulations and Proceeds of Crime Act triggers unlimited fines, suspension or revocation of financial-service permissions, forced closure of bank accounts, confiscation of funds, and public censure. Where the misusing entity is judged to be a conduit for money laundering, criminal asset forfeiture may reach the ELP's entire revenue stream.

Director personal liability: The general partner, compliance officer, MLRO, and beneficial owners face criminal liability with imprisonment of up to seven years for money-laundering facilitation. Personal fines are imposed in addition to corporate penalties, and the corporate veil is inapplicable to knowing AML misconduct; travel bans and cross-border freezing orders are routinely issued.
Permanent Establishment and UK Tax Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: UK HMRC may assert a UK permanent establishment under multilateral DTA principles, assess corporation tax on the profits attributable to UK activity, impose interest and penalties, and initiate a mutual agreement procedure against Gibraltar. Penalties may increase to 100% of the UK tax for careless or deliberate errors.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow partners who knowingly permit a UK PE without disclosure may be held jointly liable for the UK tax debt under civil evasion provisions. UK director disqualification, personal financial penalties, and imprisonment for deliberate tax evasion may follow; the ELP's claimed non-resident status will be disregarded.
