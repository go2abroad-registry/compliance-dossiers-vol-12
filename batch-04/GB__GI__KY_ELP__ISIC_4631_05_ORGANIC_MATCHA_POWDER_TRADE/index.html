Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Her Majesty's Government of Gibraltar, Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, 6 Moreton Place, Gibraltar (https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation); VAT registration is administered by HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) for UK VAT purposes where applicable, with Gibraltar's customs and indirect tax regime aligned under the EU-Gibraltar arrangements and UK post-Brexit framework.

E-government filing portal

A foreign constituent (including an Exempted Limited Partnership) must apply to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office on Form GILP/ELP application for an employer/income tax reference and, where the ELP elects or is deemed to have a taxable presence, a Gibraltar tax identification number (TIN). The ELP must file an annual return and election under the Income Tax Act 2010 (Gibraltar). For VAT/customs purposes, the ELP must obtain a GB Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) number beginning with GB from HMRC if importing goods into Great Britain or Northern Ireland; registration for UK VAT is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed £90,000 (or voluntary earlier). Gibraltar has its own indirect tax system (GST-like purchase tax/customs duties) separate from UK VAT; for goods physically entering Gibraltar, registration with the Gibraltar Customs Department and a Gibraltar EORI/trader number is required. The Foreign Account Information Exchange and Companies House registration of the ELP must be current before any tax ID is issued.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign constituent (including an Exempted Limited Partnership) must apply to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office on Form GILP/ELP application for an employer/income tax reference and, where the ELP elects or is deemed to have a taxable presence, a Gibraltar tax identification number (TIN). The ELP must file an annual return and election under the Income Tax Act 2010 (Gibraltar). The Foreign Account Information Exchange and Companies House registration of the ELP must be current before any tax ID is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The ELP is statutorily transparent under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010 (Part 4), and may elect to be treated as opaque only in limited circumstances with Commissioner consent. The ELP may form a Gibraltar 'tax group' for VAT/CT purposes. UK check-the-box is not applicable to non-US entities; however, the ELP may avail itself of the UK/Gibraltar tie-breaker provisions under the UK-Gibraltar taxation agreement for residence. The partnership may also elect to be treated as an 'offshore' entity for Gibraltar tax certificates, enabling the issue of a certificate of residence/tax residence. Hybrid mismatch elections must be reviewed under the UK Hybrids Mismatches Rules (Taxation (International and Other Provisions) Act 2010) if the ELP is a hybrid entity used in a UK group; no standalone Gibraltar election exists for hybrid mismatches except the transparent/opaque designation in the partnership agreement.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

20%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

UK VAT registration threshold: GBP 90,000 (taxable turnover in a 12-month period); Gibraltar Purchase Tax registration: nil threshold on importation of goods into Gibraltar; nominal rate generally 0% except specified items

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under UK VAT Act 1994 s.30, zero-rating applies to: (1) export of goods from the UK to a destination outside the UK (including export to Gibraltar treated as a foreign destination for UK VAT purposes) provided the exporter retains evidence of export within 3 months of the time of supply; and (2) certain services directly linked to exported goods or delivered to overseas recipients. For goods traded from or through Gibraltar, Gibraltar does not impose VAT; exports from Gibraltar are generally outside the charge to Gibraltar purchase tax/customs duty, provided customs declarations evidence removal from the territory. Zero-rating is available only where the ELP is acting as principal or disclosed agent in the export chain and holds the requisite commercial, transport and customs evidence identifying the goods and destination.

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD/C88; UK CDS Export Declaration; for goods from Gibraltar, the Gibraltar Customs Bill of Entry/Export Declaration or EU SAD 309), Bill of Lading (where sea freight) or Air Waybill, commercial/tax invoice, packing list, inward remittance certificate (bank/foreign exchange proof of payment from the overseas buyer)
  • Evidence of physical departure within the three-month time limit (port departure stamps or carrier electronic departure notification)
  • Proof of export instructions and ownership transfer (contracts, letters of credit, SWIFT messages)

No mandatory real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping applies in the UK or Gibraltar for B2B cross-border matcha powder trade. UK VAT returns and certificates are filed electronically via HMRC Making Tax Digital (MTD) using MTD-compatible software; inbound customs declarations use the Customs Declaration Service (CDS) with only limited electronic clearance, no physical fiscal stamp. Gibraltar uses the Customs Management System (GCMS/CustomsWare) for electronic import/export declarations; no continuous transaction-level fiscal clearance API is imposed for ordinary wholesale foodstuffs.

HMRC may impose a denial of zero-rating, VAT assessment for unpaid output VAT at 20%, interest, and penalties under VAT Act 1994 (up to 100% of VAT lost for deliberate and concealed conduct). Fraudulent export zero-rating claims attract civil evasion penalties (s.60/61 VAT Act 1994) and criminal sanctions including up to 7 years' imprisonment under the Customs and Excise Management Act 1979 (CEA 1979) where fraudulent evasion is proved. Gibraltar independently imposes penalties under Gibraltar Income Tax Act and Purchase Tax legislation for false declarations and customs offences, including forfeiture of goods and fines up to £3,000 or three times the tax value. HMRC may retrospectively claw back VAT refunds for missing or defective export evidence up to 4 years (extendable to 20 years in cases of dishonest conduct), and Gibraltar customs may raise assessments within 6 years. The 'MTIC' (Missing Trader Intra-Community) and both-side foreign exchange repatriation rules require full documentary chain of title; failure to repatriate proceeds within 180 days in stipulated FX accounts may recharacterize the transaction as domestic supply and reverse zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at a 1:1 fixed parity with GBP; both currencies circulate interchangeably and are fully convertible for all current-account trade transactions, including inbound payments for matcha wholesale. Capital-account convertibility is unrestricted for a Cayman ELP: no exchange-control approval, profit-remittance licence, or withholding consent is required for distributions to non-resident partners. Practical hurdles are limited to (i) AML source-of-funds verification on the escrow account receiving partner capital and (ii) tax-reporting gateways (TIEA / FATCA) that impose transparency obligations on the Gibraltar paying agent rather than any convertibility restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rules, mandatory conversion ratios, or repatriation timelines apply in either the UK or Gibraltar. Export receipts from matcha sales may be retained in any freely convertible currency (GBP, USD, EUR, JPY). The Exchange Control Act 1947, as effectively repealed for ordinary trade, imposes no conversion obligation. However, OFSI-administered sanctions screening remains mandatory for each counterparty, and banks may impose daily reporting thresholds for unusually large aggregations of export receipts.

Central bank supervision: The Bank of England (HM Treasury Monetary Authority) sets UK monetary policy, while the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory licensing and prudential regulator for all banks operating in Gibraltar. HM Treasury, acting through the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI), administers the UK-Gibraltar financial sanctions and exchange-control supervision framework applicable to GBP-denominated clearing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: MEDIUM. UK or EU-licensed digital EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise) may open remote GBP/EUR operating accounts for a Gibraltar-registered ELP; however, they cannot take deposits in Gibraltar and are not covered by the Gibraltar Depositor Compensation Scheme. A foreign EMI is not passported into Gibraltar unless separately authorized by the GFSC under the Financial Services Act 2019 (DLT/EMI framework), and USD inward wires must transit the EMI's UK/EU settlement bank, creating slower clearing and higher correspondent-fee drag than a tier-1 Gibraltar bank. The EMI channel is viable only as an operational sweep and expense account; the Capital Escrow Account for partner injections must remain at a GFSC-licensed bank.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return for an accounting period ending 31 December must be filed no later than 31 December of the following year. For any other accounting period, the return is due within 30 days after the issuance of the Commissioner’s electronic notice, or by 31 December of the following year, whichever is earlier.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File is mandatory when the aggregate annual value of controlled transactions (sales, purchases, fees, financing) with related parties exceeds GIP 1,000,000. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required only where the ultimate parent group has consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million (or GIP equivalent).

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (updated 2023) apply to any Gibraltar tax-resident entity carrying on a ‘relevant activity’, including distribution and logistics services. The matcha powder trading ELP must maintain in Gibraltar: a physical business premises, qualified local employees, core income-generating expenditure, and strategic management meetings. If the ELP claims non-resident tax classification, it must demonstrate that all decisions and core activities occur abroad. Failure to satisfy substance tests leads to financial penalties and, for repeated failures, strike-off and dissolution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: A monetary penalty equal to 100% of the tax evaded, together with daily interest at 3% above the official Bank of England rate. Criminal conviction for tax evasion carries an unlimited fine and imprisonment for up to seven years.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners who knowingly assisted in the evasion are jointly and severally liable for the entity’s tax liabilities. A court may issue travel bans, freezing orders, and director disqualification orders lasting up to fifteen years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Initial penalty of GIP 10,000; continued non-compliance attracts a further daily penalty of GIP 1,000 and ultimately an application to strike off the ELP from the Gibraltar Companies Register. Tax residence may be retrospectively denied, making the ELP non-resident and exposing income to foreign tax.

Director personal liability: Directors or governing partners who fail to comply are personally liable for the substance penalties and may be disqualified from acting as a director or manager in Gibraltar for up to five years. Foreign owners may be personally assessed for unpaid Gibraltar tax if the corporate veil is pierced.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to GIP 50,000 per violation for AML systems failures. The Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit may impose civil freezing orders and require revocation of the ELP’s bank relationships. Criminal money laundering charges carry imprisonment of up to fourteen years and unlimited fines.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors face personal criminal liability for suspicious transactions routed through the ELP if they knew or suspected the proceeds of crime were involved. Travel bans, asset seizure, and prohibition from managing a financial institution may be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner may make a transfer pricing adjustment increasing chargeable profits, add tax at 12.5% on the adjusted amount, and charge late-payment interest. A careless or deliberate return error leads to an additional penalty up to 50% of the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: If the mispricing is directed by a beneficial owner or shadow director, that individual is personally liable for the underpaid tax and penalty. In egregious cases, the Supreme Court of Gibraltar may pierce the ELP veil and impose restitution obligations on the parent entity.
