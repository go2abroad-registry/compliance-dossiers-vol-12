Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar – Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax), 1 Leousson Place, Gibraltar; online portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax.

E-government filing portal

Register the Exempted Limited Partnership with the Gibraltar Companies House through a licensed corporate services provider. Following entity registration, file the partnership registration particulars and constitutional documents with the Income Tax Office, including the registered office address, identity of the general partner, and registered agent confirmation. The Income Tax Office issues a Gibraltar tax reference number (TIN) for the ELP before taxable activity commences; the ELP must display its TIN on invoices and tax filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – the ELP is fiscally transparent; income is taxed at the partner level. Non-resident partners are within Gibraltar taxation only to the extent income is Gibraltar-source or remitted/treated as arising in Gibraltar (territorial scope).

Available election forms

No Gibraltar equivalent of the U.S. check-the-box election is available; ELPs are categorically transparent. The ELP may elect, through its general partner, to file a consolidated partnership return rather than partner-by-partner attestations, and may designate the treatment of hybrid instruments or hybrid entities under OECD/G20 BEPS action item 2, subject to the Gibraltar Income Tax Act and U.K.-compatible controlled foreign company rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None – Gibraltar does not levy VAT, GST, or sales and use tax. Indirect taxation is limited to customs and excise duties under the Gibraltar Customs Ordinance.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – no VAT/GST registration threshold exists because Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST. For income tax purposes, any entity deriving Gibraltar-source income must register regardless of turnover; no de minimis threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar has no VAT zero-rating regime. Export of goods is outside the domestic customs/excise duty chain where the goods physically leave Gibraltar and are properly reported to the Gibraltar Customs Department. Export of SaaS/SEO analytics services, being digital services, is not subject to any Gibraltar indirect tax and therefore requires no 0% VAT claim or refund procedure.

  • Customs Export Declaration submitted to the Gibraltar Customs Department
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the ELP
  • Packing List
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank FX receipt evidencing cross-border payment and repatriation within 180 days
  • ELP tax registration reference and registered agent confirmation

No real-time fiscal clearance, government e-invoicing platform, API clearance, or digital stamping requirement exists in Gibraltar. Commercial invoices must be retained for at least six years and, for customs relief claims, must be filed with the Gibraltar Customs Department export declarations.

The Gibraltar Customs Department and Income Tax Office may jointly audit export substantiation retroactively for up to six years. Failure to document export flow results in deemed taxable revenue or loss of customs relief, with clawback of duties and benefits. False customs declarations may attract penalties under the Customs Ordinance, including fines up to £3,000 plus 200% of the evaded duty and potential criminal liability. For income tax purposes, fraudulent export claims attract interest at the statutory rate and penalties up to 150% of the additional tax due.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates a fully liberalized exchange control environment. There are no statutory restrictions on current account transactions, capital account flows, or dividend/profit remittance for non-resident partners of an Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP). GIP is fully convertible into GBP at par; an ELP may lawfully hold, receive, settle, and remit GBP, EUR, and USD without prior central bank approval. Profit remittance hurdles: none, provided the ELP maintains its non-resident-status governance and settles its local tax obligations (if any) accurately under the Income Tax Act; Gibraltar does not impose withholding tax on distributions to non-resident partners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no currency surrender or forced conversion requirements. There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts, subscription payments, or SaaS licence proceeds received in foreign currency (USD/EUR). An ELP may retain all export earnings in original currency or convert freely through GFSC-licensed banks and payment institutions.

Central bank supervision: HM Government of Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) exercises banking regulatory and anti-money laundering supervision. No separate Gibraltar central bank exists; the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act and is legally pegged one-to-one with the pound sterling (GBP). Monetary policy for both GIP and GBP is effectively set by the Bank of England (BoE), and GIP notes circulate alongside GBP banknotes in Gibraltar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium. Offshore digital e-money institutions (EMIs) licensed in the EEA cannot automatically passport into Gibraltar post-Brexit unless they hold a separate GFSC licence or operate through a Gibraltar-licensed agent. UK FCA-authorized EMIs also do not have an automatic right of establishment or cross-border service provision in Gibraltar. A GFSC-licensed EMI would have to be locally incorporated or hold, in practice, a regulated banking or payment institution licence, which erodes the cost advantage of an offshore EMI. For recurring subscription revenue in the MarTech segment, use Stripe and Paddle as merchant rails for outbound payment collection, and keep treasury balances in a GFSC-regulated tier-1 bank rather than an EMI.

To avoid misinterpretation, instruct the sending bank to use a purpose code of 'SCIR' (Settlement of Investment in Kind) or commonly available 'TRRC' (Bank Transfer Remittance) in the optional 71G field. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File are mandatory when the Gibraltar entity participates in related-party transactions exceeding £1,000,000 in the accounting year, or when the ELP’s gross turnover exceeds £6,000,000. A country-by-country report is required where the multinational group has consolidated revenue of at least €750,000,000 and a Gibraltar constituent entity.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Companies and Partnerships) Regulations 2019 apply to relevant activities, including cross-border SaaS and IP analytics. The ELP must demonstrate that core income-generating activities (direction, management, oversight and coordination) occur in Gibraltar, with a physical presence, at least two resident employees and expenditure proportional to the income. For a purely passive Cayman-style ELP, the tax exemption is conditional on satisfying the reduced substance test or the full substance test if the analytics tool is actively developed through employees in Gibraltar.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false declarations

Statutory sanction: A revised assessment for the tax evaded, civil penalties of up to 300% of the underpaid tax, daily default surcharges and criminal prosecution for fraudulent tax evasion carrying imprisonment up to 10 years and unlimited fines.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors of the corporate GP are jointly and severally liable for all penalties caused by their wilful default; courts may issue travel bans, freezing orders, and director disqualification orders up to 15 years; personal assets may be seized under civil asset recovery.
Shell company / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: An ESR compliance notice may subject the ELP to penalties from £20,000 to £100,000 and require re-filing. Continuing failure results in loss of exempt status, a 10% tax charge on all offshore profits, and strike-off by the Registrar of Companies.

Director personal liability: Directors signing a false economic substance declaration face a fine of £50,000 and disqualification; the corporate general partner is liable to restore the benefit; if the structure is found to be an illegal shell, directors may be held personally accountable for restitution to the tax authority.
AML and beneficial ownership breaches

Statutory sanction: Civil fines up to £1,000,000 per breach under the Proceeds of Crime Act; revocation of the EMID/payment-account registration; criminal liability with imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering using corporate structures.

Director personal liability: Directors and designated officers are personally exposed to fines, imprisonment, travel restrictions and asset forfeiture; the beneficial owner is also exposed to a national court order identifying them by name and freezing all UK and Gibraltar assets.
Failure to file and pay

Statutory sanction: Immediate late-filing penalty of £100, plus £100 per day (max £3,000); tax-geared penalty of 50% of the tax that would have been due; cancellation of the partnership’s registration for persistent default.

Director personal liability: When the default is caused by the corporate general partner, its directors become personally liable for the penalty if the corporate GP is dormant or unsuitable for payment; the Income Tax Commissioner may secure prohibition on opening a bank account and targeted travel ban on the director.
