Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Government of Gibraltar, Income Tax Office (Tax Registry) — official portal https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax-office

E-government filing portal

A foreign constituent of an Exempted Limited Partnership must apply to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (GTIN) using Form GIB-TIN-ELP. The application is filed by the registered agent, accompanied by a certified copy of the Certificate of Limited Partnership, the registered office consent letter, a schedule of partners showing non-resident status, and a declaration of the intended place of management. The GTIN is issued within seven working days and must be used for all payroll, corporation tax, and business levy filings.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign constituent of an Exempted Limited Partnership must apply to the Gibraltar Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (GTIN) using Form GIB-TIN-ELP. The application is filed by the registered agent, accompanied by a certified copy of the Certificate of Limited Partnership, the registered office consent letter, a schedule of partners showing non-resident status, and a declaration of the intended place of management. The GTIN is issued within seven working days and must be used for all payroll, corporation tax, and business levy filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

An Exempted Limited Partnership may file an irrevocable election for tax transparency under section 45B of the Income Tax Act 2010; a hybrid mismatch election can additionally be made in relation to UK/Gibraltar disregarded payments. Election forms are filed with the Income Tax Office through the registered agent and are binding on all partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax in Gibraltar; indirect taxes are limited to customs duties on imported goods and a business levy payable on payroll/social insurance.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Gibraltar does not impose VAT or GST. Customs registration is required for importers whose inward consignments exceed GBP 1,000 per shipment; there is no economic-turnover VAT threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Since Gibraltar has no VAT, the concept of zero-rating is displaced. For UK VAT registrants, 0% export VAT applies to goods dispatched from the UK to non-UK destinations and to certain cross-border services under UK VAT Act Schedule 8; for the Gibraltar entity, similar effect is achieved by excluding non-Gibraltar income from the territorial scope of Gibraltar income tax rather than by VAT zero-rating.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration
  • Bill of Lading / House Airway Bill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate from the receiving bank
  • Local Clearance Statement issued by HM Customs Gibraltar

No real-time fiscal clearance or API-based digital stamping is mandated in Gibraltar. Invoices must satisfy the content requirements of the Income Tax (Accounts and Records) Regulations and be retained for six years. For UK/VAT-linked transactions, HMRC's Making Tax Digital for VAT will require API submission if UK VAT registration is held.

Where a UK VAT registration exists, fraudulent misuse of zero-rating attracts a 100% VAT clawback plus penalty up to 30% of potential lost revenue and criminal liability under the Fraud Act 2006. In Gibraltar, material misdeclarations in corporate tax filings incur a penalty of GBP 300 per return, daily default penalties of GBP 50, and interest on underpaid tax at 8% per annum; deliberate falsification of export substantiation may be referred to the Royal Gibraltar Police Economic Crime Unit.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar operates an open and effectively fully convertible currency regime. The Gibraltar pound is pegged at par with sterling and there are no exchange controls applicable to current account transactions, profit repatriation, dividends, partnership distributions, or capital movements. Capital account convertibility is equally unrestricted for non-resident Exempted Limited Partnerships, subject solely to compliance with GFSC AML/KYC regulations and any suspicious transaction reporting. No prior central bank approval or profit remittance licence is required; practical hurdles are limited to bank-level source-of-wealth verification and tax residence documentation for the ELP and its limited partners.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio to Gibraltar pounds, and no time-limited obligation for repatriation of export receipts. Export proceeds arising from the logistics fleet dispatch SaaS operations may be retained in any freely convertible currency, held in multicurrency corporate bank accounts, or reinvested offshore without central bank approval. The only mandatory obligations are standard commercial record-keeping under GFSC AML regulations and accurate accounting treatment for tax purposes, including transfer pricing documentation where connected-party cross-border transactions are involved.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (as the ultimate sterling monetary authority); Government of Gibraltar (for the issue of Gibraltar pound banknotes and coinage); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) (for banking licensing, AML/CFT supervision and foreign exchange conduct oversight)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for UK-regulated EMI/Fintech providers; Low for unregulated offshore digital EMIs. Legal constraints are substantial: Gibraltar is a separate legal jurisdiction from the UK, and an EMI authorised only by the UK Financial Conduct Authority does not automatically enjoy passported e-money rights into Gibraltar. To lawfully hold client funds or provide payment accounts to a Gibraltar ELP, the EMI must either obtain a Gibraltar-level licence or operate solely through a GFSC-licensed bank as an undisclosed agent of the ELP. An offshore digital EMI without a recognised EEA, UK, or Gibraltar licence is not viable as a primary treasury rail due to GFSC restrictions on unlicensed deposit-taking, AML classification of non-bank lenders, and inability to issue compliant GBP or EUR sort codes for corporate treasury operations. Hybrid solutions using Stripe Billing as a payment facilitator are permitted only for front-end collection, with net settlement routed into a GFSC-regulated bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant capital injections must be made solely through the formal banking system using SWIFT MT103 or equivalent ISO 20022 payments. The remitting bank must transmit the payment from the funding limited partner's own account in the exact legal name of that partner; third-party remittances are prohibited. The wire must carry full purpose-code clarity: for equity and partnership capital use ISO 20022 external purpose code EQUI, with field 70 remittance information stated as 'Limited Partner Capital Contribution — ELP name — Pursuant to Partnership Agreement dated [date]'. If banking portals require a local standard, the bank's onboarding officer should map EQUI to the bank's internal purpose code for direct investment or capital subscription; the same code must be used consistently on the contribution notice, capital account ledger, audited financial statements and GFSC record. Documentation evidencing the business purpose (telematics infrastructure and fleet route optimisation software development) must be held by both remitting and receiving banks; proof of payment should never be labelled as goods, software licence sales, or general services to avoid misclassification as revenue and accidental taxable income.Outbound trade and operating disbursements should be cleared over: SWIFT MT103 for supplier payments outside Gibraltar and EEA, especially fleet hardware vendors in Asia and North America; SWIFT MT202 for bank-to-bank treasury transfers; commercial irrevocable Letters of Credit issued by a GFSC-regulated bank for high-value fleet hardware procurement where the supplier demands documentary security; CHAPS or Faster Payments for urgent GBP settlements in the United Kingdom through a UK correspondent account; SEPA Credit Transfer for EUR-denominated payments to EEA logistics partners; domestic GIP payments via regional clearing through the account-holding bank. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File are required where the partnership group's Gibraltar turnover equals or exceeds GBP 10,000,000 or cumulative cross-border related-party transactions exceed GBP 1,000,000. The pilot ELP operates below these thresholds, but contemporaneous OECD-compliant transfer pricing documentation and a benchmarking study remain advisable to satisfy both HMRC and the Gibraltar Income Tax Office.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to entities carrying on relevant activities, including holding-company, intellectual property, and distribution services. A tax-transparent ELP falls outside the strict corporate ESR net where no partner is Gibraltar-resident; however, HMRC applies substance over form and will re-characterise the ELP as UK tax-resident where central management and control are exercised in the UK. Minimum substance for treaty protection requires a Gibraltar registered office, a bank account, locally maintained books and records, and, if the ELP itself is taxable, board meetings conducted in Gibraltar with minutes evidencing strategic decisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act: civil penalty of up to 100% of the tax undercharged plus statutory interest; criminal prosecution with fines up to GBP 100,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years. HMRC may apply parallel sanctions under the Finance Act 2021 failure-to-prevent-facilitation offence with unlimited corporate fines.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for fraudulent trading: the general partner and any shadow director who signed false returns are jointly and severally liable for the full tax loss; confiscation orders under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; director disqualification for up to 15 years; travel bans and extradition under the UK-Gibraltar mutual legal assistance protocol.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Cancellation of the ELP's tax residence certificate; reassessment of the entire profit at 10% in Gibraltar; denial of UK-Gibraltar Double Taxation Arrangement relief; administrative strike-off under the Limited Partnerships Act 2010; GFSC penalties for false beneficial ownership declarations.

Director personal liability: General partners are personally liable for the tax shortfall arising from the sham substance structure; freezing injunctions may be granted over personal and corporate assets; director disqualification and removal of the corporate services provider under GFSC disciplinary action; HMRC may pursue the UK parent under CFC and GAAR anti-avoidance rules.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment increasing taxable profits by 20% to 35% of the distorted margin, late-payment interest at 8% per annum, and penalties of 30% to 70% of the adjustment. The UK CFC charge may re-attribute diverted profit to the UK parent where the ELP lacks substantive people functions in Gibraltar.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners who instigate the avoidance face penalties under the UK GAAR (Part 5, Finance Act 2013) and CTA 2010 Part 15; joint and several indemnity claims by other partners for uncovered tax, interest, and penalties; travel restrictions and asset disclosure orders ancillary to civil recovery proceedings.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: GFSC administrative fines of up to GBP 5,000,000 and public censure; imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering or tipping-off under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; the AML supervisory levy applies to all ELPs maintaining a Gibraltar bank account regardless of trading activity.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any compliance officer bear personal criminal liability for consent and tipping-off failures; civil recovery orders over the property in question; UK POCA 2002 restraint orders over worldwide assets; disqualification of the appointed accountant; and travel bans while asset tracing and confiscation investigations remain open.
