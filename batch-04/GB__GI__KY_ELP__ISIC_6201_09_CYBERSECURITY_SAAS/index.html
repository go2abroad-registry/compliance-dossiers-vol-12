Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) of the United Kingdom; Government Gateway portal (www.gov.uk/topic/dealing-with-hmrc) with separate registration through the Gibraltar Income Tax Office for local direct taxes, and the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Department for indirect taxes.

E-government filing portal

An Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) formed in Gibraltar must first obtain a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number by registering with the Gibraltar Income Tax Office on the basis of its partnership registration certificate. Because Gibraltar is a Crown Dependency with its own fiscal autonomy but not part of the UK VAT territory for most purposes, the ELP typically remains outside UK VAT registration unless it has a UK establishment. For UK VAT, a foreign entity may register through HMRC online using Form VAT1 if it makes taxable supplies in the UK and exceeds the £90,000 threshold or makes distance sales to UK consumers exceeding £90,000 per annum. For Gibraltar VAT/GST analogues, the ELP should register for Consumption Tax if making local taxable supplies; registration is effected via the Gibraltar Customs and Excise Department using the partnership's Gibraltar TIN and company/partnership documentation including the ELP certificate and register of partners.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible; ELP is tax transparent in Gibraltar, so each partner is assessed directly on their share of Gibraltar-source income; non-resident partners are not subject to Gibraltar income tax on foreign-source income and, depending on control and management, the ELP may not be Gibraltar-taxable. Territorial Scope applies: Gibraltar taxes only profits arising in or derived from Gibraltar. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; ELP is tax transparent in Gibraltar, so each partner is assessed directly on their share of Gibraltar-source income; non-resident partners are not subject to Gibraltar income tax on foreign-source income and, depending on control and management, the ELP may not be Gibraltar-taxable. Territorial Scope applies: Gibraltar taxes only profits arising in or derived from Gibraltar. For UK tax purposes, the ELP may be treated as a corporation if it has a UK establishment or if the partnership is managed in the UK, but absent UK permanent establishment it files no UK tax return; for US federal tax purposes, the ELP may be 'check-the-box' elective.

Available election forms

No direct Gibraltar check-the-box election exists; classification follows the formal legal status as a partnership under Gibraltar law. Partners resident in the UK may make a UK partnership return disclosure; in the United States, the ELP can file Form 8832 to elect tax entity classification (partnership vs corporation) if treated as an eligible entity, though as a foreign partnership it is normally disregarded/per se partnership. Gibraltar has no hybrid mismatch election, but entities must comply with UK hybrid mismatch rules (Taxation (International and Other Provisions) Act 2010 and Finance Act 2016) where the ELP has UK partners, and Gibraltar enacted Economic Substance and Tax Information Exchange rules. The ELP may also elect to be treated as a 'qualifying partnership' if all partners are non-Gibraltar resident and the ELP has no local taxable income, avoiding filing obligations beyond declarations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Consumption Tax (a VAT-like tax administered by HM Customs Gibraltar); UK VAT for cross-border supplies to or from the UK mainland; digital services VAT rules under UK and EU frameworks as applicable.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Gibraltar: No general consumption tax registration threshold is publicly established because Gibraltar's Consumption Tax is a selective/import duty and excise regime; the standard rate is 0% for most services and 0% on exports, with positive rates limited to selected goods such as tobacco, alcohol, fuel, and vehicles. UK VAT: compulsory registration threshold is £90,000 taxable turnover (from 1 April 2024), and distance sales threshold is £90,000. The ELP providing B2B SaaS cybersecurity services from Gibraltar to UK businesses generally falls under the reverse charge (place of supply is the UK customer's jurisdiction), so no UK VAT registration is needed unless the ELP has a UK establishment or makes domestic-rated supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Gibraltar does not levy VAT on exported services, and UK VAT zero-rating does not generally apply to B2B services supplied to business customers, which are instead outside the scope of UK VAT under the reverse charge. For UK VAT purposes, claims for zero-rating on exported goods require the exporter to hold valid evidence of export; for services supplied to non-UK businesses, the supplier must establish the customer is a taxable business in another country and that the place of supply is outside the UK. For cybersecurity SaaS sold by a Gibraltar ELP to non-Gibraltar customers, the place of supply for B2B is the customer's country, and the ELP should obtain the customer's VAT registration number or equivalent tax reference and maintain evidence of customer business status to support non-Gibraltar treatment if a local VAT registration is challenged.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Single Administrative Document / electronic export declaration if goods) — not applicable to pure SaaS services
  • Bill of Lading or airway waybill — only for physical exports, not software-as-a-service
  • Signed written contract or service agreement identifying the B2B customer and jurisdiction
  • Customer's valid VAT/GST registration number in the destination country or equivalent tax ID
  • Invoices showing zero-rated/outside-scope status and place of supply rules applied

No real-time electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate applies in Gibraltar for B2B SaaS exports. UK domestic e-invoicing is not mandatory for B2B invoices, although all VAT invoices must be issued within 30 days and retained digitally. Making Tax Digital (MTD) requires digital record-keeping and quarterly VAT returns via HMRC-recognised API software for any business VAT-registered in the UK; there is no continuous transaction controls or digital stamping in Gibraltar. For Gibraltar excise and customs import procedures, electronic declarations (GDDS/CHIEF replacement) may apply but do not extend to services.

In Gibraltar, penalties for incorrect excise/consumption tax declarations and for assisting tax evasion are up to £3,000 per contravention plus interest and potential criminal sanctions under the Customs and Excise Acts; for income tax, fraudulent or negligent returns attract penalties from £250 plus tax avoided, up to 100% of the tax. In the UK, incorrect VAT returns and improper zero-rating claims expose the taxpayer to HMRC assessment, interest, and penalties of up to 100% of the VAT lost (Schedule 24 Finance Act 2007), and dishonest conduct can trigger criminal prosecution under the Fraud Act 2006 and VAT Act 1994 section 72, with potential imprisonment. Retrospective clawback of input VAT or reversal of zero-rating applies where adequate evidence is not held within the required time limits; HMRC may deny relief for exports not substantiated within 6 months of the supply date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound is pegged at parity to Sterling, and the jurisdiction maintains no material exchange-control restrictions. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely convertible for resident and non-resident persons. Profit remittances, partnership distributions, dividends, interest, and liquidation proceeds may be effected in any convertible currency without prior central-bank approval. The Exempted Limited Partnership qualifies for pass-through treatment and is not subject to withholding on distributions to non-resident partners; there are no documented hurdles on inward or outward remittance, except statutory AML/CFT reporting obligations on banks and fiduciaries for each wire transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. All export and services receipts, including SaaS subscription fees and penetration testing fees from overseas customers, may be retained in the currency of receipt or freely converted into GBP, EUR, USD, or any other acceptable major currency. Banks may require a standard payment-information form for statistical purposes but cannot compel compulsory conversion or surrender.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (for United Kingdom monetary and prudential policy, with jurisdictional guidance) and HM Government of Gibraltar Treasury (for Gibraltar pound note issue and exchange control administration)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Medium-High for UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA)-authorised Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) that operate under the UK Electronic Money Regulations 2011 and can lawfully passport or service clients in Gibraltar via the Gibraltar-UK financial services relationship. Feasibility is Medium for EU-authorised EMIs because Gibraltar is no longer within the EU single market and access requires separate UK/Gibraltar licences or a specially structured payment agent arrangement. Legal constraints: The use of offshore EMIs is permissible only if the EMI is FCA-authorised or Gibraltar-licensed, holds client funds in segregated safeguarding accounts, and completes full know-your-customer (KYC) and source-of-funds verification on the ELP's ultimate beneficial owners and general partner. Use of an unregulated EMIs or a non-EEA 'payment aggregator' without a regulated banking or e-money licence is a compliance red flag and is therefore not recommended for high-volume cross-border treasury operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections into the ELP's Gibraltar account should be routed exclusively through regulated banking systems using SWIFT MT103 beneficiary wires or, for UK originated payments, Faster Payments or CHAPS via a UK bank account. Beneficiary account name must exactly match the ELP legal name, and the payment instruction must state a purpose code and free-text remittance details such as 'Capital Contribution under the Limited Partnership Agreement' or 'Loan for Working Capital'. For non-EUR/GBP/USD wires, the paying institution should pre-clear foreign-currency conversion rates and legally obtain underlying source-of-funds evidence. Acceptable inbound methods for Stripe or platform-settlement funds include an auto-reconciliation transfer from the Stripe Treasury/Stripe balance account into the bank operational account, provided that the account reference and source identify the settlement title. Electronic payment institutions may not be used as primary capital rails unless they are FCA-authorised and maintain segregated accounts.Outbound trade settlements and partner distributions are cleared via (i) SWIFT MT103 for international supplier payments and non-UK disbursements, (ii) Faster Payments Service / CHAPS in GBP for UK counterparties and urgent processing, (iii) SEPA Instant Credit Transfer for EUR payments where supported by the Gibraltar bank, and (iv) Commercial Letters of Credit or Bank Payment Obligation for high-value contractual staged payments to software infrastructure vendors or cybersecurity subcontractors. All outbound disbursements from the ELP operational account must be accompanied by a distribution minute or board approval and the corresponding economic substance rationale. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12.5% (Gibraltar corporate income tax rate); however, an Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) is required to be treated as fiscally transparent unless an election under the Exempted Limited Partnerships Act is made to be taxed as a body corporate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (Gibraltar imposes no dividend withholding tax; distributions from an ELP are generally treated as partnership profit splits and therefore not subject to entity-level tax; no withholding is applied on outgoing distributions to non-resident partners).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,200,000 (aggregate cross-border controlled transactions in an accounting period). If the threshold is exceeded, the ELP must prepare and retain a Master File and Local File, including the notional profit allocation of the SaaS business, identity of associated enterprises and a functional analysis.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to all ELPs carrying on 'relevant activities'. A cybersecurity SaaS business is primarily an 'IP business' where internally developed scanning/penetration-testing software produces the main profit, or a 'distribution and service centre' if the activities consist of purchasing licences and delivering services. The ELP must meet the 'adequate substance' test: board meetings and senior decision-making in Gibraltar; physically present employees and consultants proportionate to the IP and service functions; sufficient annual operating expenditure in Gibraltar; and active direction and management of the commercial risk within Gibraltar. An annual ESR notification and substance report must be filed through the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies; failure to do so triggers automatic information exchange with UK and EU tax authorities and a denial of all treaty and pass-through benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Civil penalty of 100% of tax understated and fixed fine up to GIP 50,000; criminal conviction under the Income Tax (Penalties and Enforcement) Act may impose imprisonment up to 5 years, an unlimited fine, and confiscation orders over assets derived from the evasion. Interest on unpaid tax accrues at 8% per annum.

Director personal liability: Each partner or director who participated in or knew of the concealment is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. Courts will pierce the ELP veil where the compliance function was delegated to a nominee service provider; directors may face personal restitution orders, an immediate travel ban during criminal investigation, and a disqualification order of up to 15 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Regulations 2019, a first failure attracts a GIP 50,000 penalty; continuing failure after 12 months attracts GIP 100,000 per month and the Registrar may strike off the ELP. The Commissioner will share the non-cooperation file with the UK HMRC and all EU Member States through exchange of information mechanisms.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner cannot rely on the ELP's separate personality if the general partner was a sham or entirely under the director's control. The court may order civil restitution of all profits earned through the defect, freeze personal bank accounts in Gibraltar, issue a travel ban, and bar the director from serving on any Gibraltar-licensed company for a maximum of 15 years.
AML / Economic Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Sanctions and Money Laundering Act 2018 carries a criminal fine capped at GIP 250,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years. The Financial Services Commission may also revoke the ELP registration, require disgorgement of the fees earned from the sanctioned client, and place an ancillary penalty order at 10 times the transaction value.

Director personal liability: Criminal liability is personal and not limited by the ELP structure; directors, general partners and the compliance officer are all 'responsible persons' under the AML framework. Conviction triggers automatic confiscation of personal assets, a travel ban until all confiscation and mutual legal assistance requests are resolved, and permanent disqualification from regulated activities across the UK and Gibraltar.
