Competent tax authority

H.M. Government of Gibraltar, Income Tax Office (Commissioner of Income Tax); official portal: https://www.income-tax.gov.gi. Customs and indirect tax registry: Gibraltar Customs and Excise Department, H.M. Government of Gibraltar (https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/customs).

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate the ELP under the Limited Partnerships Act 2019 via the Gibraltar Companies Registry and obtain the LP registration number. 2) Within 30 days of registration, submit the partnership tax-registration return to the Income Tax Office, attaching the Certificate of Registration, the partnership agreement, and KYC documentation for all general and limited partners. 3) The Income Tax Office issues a Gibraltar TIN (GRN) for the ELP and opens its taxpayer profile; each partner individually holds or obtains a TIN. 4) Non-resident partners require no Gibraltar TIN unless they derive assessable Gibraltar-source income, but must file opening and nil-return notifications where required by statute. 5) For UK-bound imports, the ELP must additionally obtain a UK EORI number (and XI EORI for Northern Ireland) from HMRC; for exports from Gibraltar, apply for customs registration via the Tradeport/ICES electronic system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the Gibraltar ELP is fiscally transparent by default under the Income Tax Act 2010 (as amended); each partner is assessed on their agreed profit share and the partnership itself bears no income tax. Territorial scope: non-resident partners are chargeable only on Gibraltar-source income; offshore trading profits of a foreign-owned ELP with no Gibraltar source are outside the Gibraltar tax charge.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box election. A limited partnership may, by written notice to the Commissioner of Income Tax, elect opaque treatment (taxation as a separate person) where the vehicle is a recognised fund or where commercial necessity exists; the default remains transparency. ATAD-aligned hybrid mismatch rules apply to any election or structure exploiting jurisdictional classification differences, including reverse hybrid vehicles, and operate by way of deduction-denial and inclusion countermeasures. No standardised hybrid election form is prescribed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No Gibraltar VAT/GST/Sales-and-Use Tax; indirect taxation is limited to customs import duties and excise duties under the Customs Duties Ordinance, Cap. 92. UK import VAT (VATA 1994) applies separately at the point of importation into the United Kingdom.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST and therefore prescribes no registration threshold or minimum turnover. Any commercial trader importing or exporting goods must register with Gibraltar Customs and Excise through Tradeport/ICES irrespective of turnover. Separately, the ELP must register for UK VAT immediately (no threshold) if it acts as the deemed seller of low-value consignments (goods worth GBP 135 or less) of FPV drones into Great Britain or Northern Ireland.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable within Gibraltar. As Gibraltar levies no VAT, goods exported from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation; no zero-rating claim or export VAT refund mechanism exists. Exported drone goods leave Gibraltar free of customs and excise duty subject to export evidence. For UK-bound private consumers, FPV racing drones are standard-rated at 20% UK import VAT, and the ELP (as deemed seller for goods valued at GBP 135 or less) must charge and account for that VAT to HMRC rather than claim zero-rating.

None in Gibraltar. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control (CTC) e-invoicing, or digital stamping mandate. Invoices need only comply with the record-keeping requirements of the Income Tax Act 2010 and must be retained for six years. Any UK VAT invoice issued by the ELP must meet HMRC's standard VAT-invoice content rules; no tax-authority clearance or API pre-approval is required.

Gibraltar: false customs declarations and export misstatements are penalised under the Customs Duties Ordinance (Cap. 92) and the Customs and Excise Management and Duties Ordinance, with civil penalties of up to treble the duty evaded, forfeiture of goods and conveyances, and criminal sanctions for dishonest conduct; no VAT clawback risk arises because no Gibraltar VAT is charged. UK-side exposure: where the ELP is registered for UK VAT on low-value drone consignments, HMRC may raise assessments for undeclared output VAT (VATA 1994 s.73), impose misdeclaration penalties of up to 100% (VATA 1994 s.63), charge default interest, and pursue criminal penalties under CEMA 1979 s.167 for fraudulent evasion. No Gibraltar foreign-exchange repatriation deadline applies because Gibraltar has no exchange-control regime; audit emphasis is on accurate customs valuation, correct HS classification, and full discharge of UK import VAT obligations.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains zero statutory exchange controls for both current-account and capital-account transactions. The GIP is freely convertible at par with GBP, and a non-resident-owned Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) may hold and disburse denominated balances in GIP, GBP, USD, EUR, and CNY without a prior central-bank license. Current-account flows (e-commerce retail receipts settled through Stripe and PayPal, supplier invoices for carbon-fiber sheets and brushless motors) are fully unrestricted. Capital-account flows (partner capital contributions, loan injections, profit distributions to non-resident partners) are likewise unrestricted from an exchange-control perspective; however, GFSC-regulated banks apply UK-equivalent purpose-code and AML source-of-funds verification prior to booking large foreign transfers. Profit-remittance hurdles are minimal: a Gibraltar-resident ELP is transparent for tax purposes and no Gibraltar withholding tax is imposed on partnership distributions to non-resident partners, but the remitting bank will require an executed partner distribution resolution, a current register of limited partners, and evidence that the ELP has not triggered Gibraltar Economic Substance reporting obligations for the relevant accounting period. If the ELP is treated as managed and controlled outside Gibraltar, no Gibraltar corporate-tax liability attaches to foreign-source drone-trading income; nevertheless, the partners should carry passports and tax-residency certificates to avoid a bank rejection of the distribution as prohibited royalty or commission pass-through.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for the surrender of export proceeds in either Gibraltar or the United Kingdom. The ELP's offshore retail export revenues realized through Stripe, PayPal, and open-account trade settlements (USD, EUR, GBP, and CNH) may remain in segregated multi-currency accounts indefinitely, and no requirement exists to convert such proceeds into GIP or GBP. The only practical constraints are AML-oriented: the GFSC and the UK Financial Policy Committee expect the ELP's bank to match incoming export settlements against shipping manifests and commercial invoices within the bank's transaction-monitoring cycle. The ELP should therefore retain its e-commerce order ledger, courier tracking evidence, and supplier purchase orders in a file that can be delivered to the relationship manager within five business days of any banking query. For annual tax filing, HM Government of Gibraltar does not require a remittance-basis declaration for foreign-trading ELPs, but the partnership must include a schedule of gross foreign-trading revenue in its annual confirmation to the Gibraltar Income Tax Commissioner to maintain its non-resident partner status.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the integrated regulator of banking and payment services, acting with HM Government of Gibraltar Finance Centre; the Bank of England functions as the ultimate monetary and settlement authority because the Gibraltar pound (GIP) is a distinct territorial currency issued under the Gibraltar Currency Notes Act but pegged and redeemable at par with Sterling (GBP). No separate Gibraltar central bank is constituted; GIP banknotes and coins are issued by HM Government of Gibraltar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: HIGH-TO-MEDIUM with material legal constraints. A UK FCA-authorised EMI (Revolut Business, Wise Business, Payoneer) or a Gibraltar-licensed payment institution may open a virtual-IBAN multi-currency account for a Gibraltar-registered ELP. Post-Brexit, no EEA passport extends automatically into Gibraltar; nevertheless a UK EMI can lawfully service the ELP as a remote client using an FCA-permitted payment service and UK sort-code settlement via Faster Payments, provided the EMI's onboarding policy accepts partnerships. The likely blocker is the 'KY_ELP' offshore partnership designation, which generates enhanced due diligence as a transparent structure; the ELP must submit its Gibraltar partnership registration, the full register of limited partners, a signed GP resolution, and an economic-substance narrative showing actual drone trading from a Gibraltar registered office. Stripe and PayPal are the target payment rails and, as the commercial layer, are independent of the EMI layer: Stripe officially supports Gibraltar-registered companies (GI) for card acquiring and PayPal offers a Gibraltar merchant contract; both require a bank account or an EMI virtual IBAN for settlement. Legal constraints: (a) EMIs do not offer FSCS deposit protection, so working capital above the safeguarding threshold must be swept to a Tier-1 bank; (b) the EMI must not be used as the capital-escrow custodian because GFSC AML rules attribute custody to the regulated Gibraltar bank; and (c) a pure money-transmission profile is a red flag, so the ELP should maintain separate commercial-invoice documentation for every settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for partner capital injections into the Gibraltar ELP: (a) the remitting partner must execute a capital-contribution deed and deliver a copy of the GP resolution approving the funding; (b) the wire must be sent by SWIFT MT103 from the partner's bank to the Gibraltar beneficiary bank, with beneficiary field 59 showing the full ELP legal name and the designated Capital Escrow sub-ledger reference, and with field 70 (remittance information) citing the contribution deed number and the phrase 'Capital Subscription - Partner Number [X]'; (c) the sending bank must attach a BoE-recognised UK purpose code, normally the BOP code CAP for capital injection into a non-financial entity or ISO 20022 purpose code B020/B021, to avoid auto-rejection by the receiving bank's FX-monitoring engine; (d) if the injection is in USD or CNY, the relationship manager should be notified in advance so that the receiving Gibraltar bank can either hold the currency in a multi-currency account or convert at the interbank spot rate with a treasury confirmation memo, avoiding any surprise retail-level FX spread; (e) for injections exceeding GBP 50,000 in a single tranche, the ELP must provide a clean 12-month bank statement from the partner's foreign bank and a signed declaration that no third-party funds exceed 5 percent of the contribution, matching GFSC beneficial-ownership guidance; and (f) capital-escrow funds remain in the escrow ledger until a GP-approved drawdown notice moves them to the operational account, preserving an auditable capital log.The ELP will operate three clearing rails for outbound trade disbursements. First, for routine supplier settlements (carbon-fiber sheet suppliers in China, brushless-motor factories, ESC and FPV-goggle manufacturers), execute an open-account SWIFT MT103 transfer from the Gibraltar operational account, stating the supplier's full legal name and account number in field 59, the proforma invoice number and contract date in field 70, and attaching a PDF commercial invoice delivered by separate authenticated email under the bank's trade-service agreement. Second, for new suppliers, high-value purchase orders above GBP 50,000, or Asian counterparties demanding documentary security, issue a Commercial Letter of Credit under UCP600 via SWIFT MT700 through NatWest International or Barclays Gibraltar; the LC will require presentation of a clean Bill of Lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and, where applicable, an inspection certificate for the drone components, with Incoterms 2020 terms Ex-Works Shenzhen or FOB. Third, for UK and EU operational disbursements (warehousing, fulfilment, accounting fees, HMRC/Gibraltar tax), use GBP Faster Payments for UK creditors and SEPA Instant Credit for EUR creditors, both of which can be initiated from the EMI layer if the operational account is fed by the Tier-1 bank. Best practice for the FPV drone business: keep retail settlement currency aligned to the sale country (GBP for UK, EUR for EU, USD for North America) and convert only once a month through the bank's treasury desk, preserving the multi-currency liquidity buffer for future supplier payments.Permissible deliverable FX forwards (GBP/USD, GBP/EUR, USD/CNH) with maturities of 1 to 12 months, executed under a bilateral confirmation with a GFSC-authorized Gibraltar/UK bank and limited to hedge-only notional amounts matching forecast e-commerce settlements and purchase-order exposure.Permissible offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in CNH/USD and CNH/GBP for hedging CNY-denominated supplier costs when the ELP's bank does not maintain an onshore CNY clearing line; such NDFs must be documented under an EMTA master agreement and approved by the GP treasury memo, with settlement in GBP upon fixing.Permissible plain-vanilla FX options and zero-cost collars (GBP/USD) to limit adverse settlement-currency volatility from Stripe and PayPal USD flows, with pre-trade authorization from the GP and a notional cap of three months' projected sales revenue.Permissible cross-currency swaps (GBP/CNH or GBP/USD) where the ELP's annual import volume from Asia exceeds GBP 1,000,000; the swap is executed under an ISDA 2002 schedule with a Gibraltar/UK bank and subject to UK EMIR trade reporting through the Gibraltar trade repository.Permissible static FX position-keeping via segregated multi-currency current accounts (GBP, USD, EUR, CNY), using natural netting and inter-account transfers rather than derivative leverage, provided all balance movements are disclosed in the ELP's monthly treasury report and are consistent with GFSC low-risk standards.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: HIGH-TO-MEDIUM with material legal constraints. A UK FCA-authorised EMI (Revolut Business, Wise Business, Payoneer) or a Gibraltar-licensed payment institution may open a virtual-IBAN multi-currency account for a Gibraltar-registered ELP. Post-Brexit, no EEA passport extends automatically into Gibraltar; nevertheless a UK EMI can lawfully service the ELP as a remote client using an FCA-permitted payment service and UK sort-code settlement via Faster Payments, provided the EMI's onboarding policy accepts partnerships. The likely blocker is the 'KY_ELP' offshore partnership designation, which generates enhanced due diligence as a transparent structure; the ELP must submit its Gibraltar partnership registration, the full register of limited partners, a signed GP resolution, and an economic-substance narrative showing actual drone trading from a Gibraltar registered office. Stripe and PayPal are the target payment rails and, as the commercial layer, are independent of the EMI layer: Stripe officially supports Gibraltar-registered companies (GI) for card acquiring and PayPal offers a Gibraltar merchant contract; both require a bank account or an EMI virtual IBAN for settlement. Legal constraints: (a) EMIs do not offer FSCS deposit protection, so working capital above the safeguarding threshold must be swept to a Tier-1 bank; (b) the EMI must not be used as the capital-escrow custodian because GFSC AML rules attribute custody to the regulated Gibraltar bank; and (c) a pure money-transmission profile is a red flag, so the ELP should maintain separate commercial-invoice documentation for every settlement.
