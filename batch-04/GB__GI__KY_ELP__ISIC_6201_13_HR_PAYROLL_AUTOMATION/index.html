Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, Department of Tax and Customs, HM Government of Gibraltar, 32-38 Stag Lane, Gibraltar GX11 1AA; online portal 'eTax' (https://etax.gi); entity registration via the Registrar of Companies, Gibraltar Companies House, 14 Market Street, Gibraltar. Note: Gibraltar is a British Overseas Territory with a fiscally distinct regime; HMRC (UK) and the Gibraltar Income Tax Office are separate competent authorities.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: register the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) with the Registrar of Companies under the Gibraltar Limited Partnerships Act 2019, submitting the ELP application, the statutory declaration that the partnership will not carry on business in Gibraltar, and evidence that at least one general partner is a Qualifying Person (a company incorporated in Gibraltar, a non-Gibraltar company registered under the Gibraltar Companies Act, or a Gibraltar-licensed professional firm). Step 2: the Registrar issues the Limited Partnership registration number; no separate Gibraltar tax identification number is mandatory for the ELP while its exempted declaration stands and it derives no Gibraltar-source income. Step 3: if any partner becomes Gibraltar-resident and taxable, or the ELP incurs Gibraltar withholding, employer or payroll obligations, apply to the Income Tax Office for a Tax Reference Number, enrol in eTax, and file under the Income Tax Act 2010; each non-resident partner remains subject to TIN and reporting rules of its own country of residence. Step 4: file the annual ELP return and the exemption declaration with the Registrar each year, and notify the Commissioner immediately if any Gibraltar business activity commences.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible, subject to territorial-scope limitation. The ELP is fiscally transparent under Gibraltar law: no entity-level income tax is imposed on the partnership; partners are assessed only on their share of Gibraltar-source income. Non-Gibraltar-source income, including global payroll automation and staff-compliance SaaS fees from non-resident clients, falls outside the charge to Gibraltar tax, producing in substance a tax-exempt-zone outcome where the ELP continuously satisfies the no-Gibraltar-business condition and annual filing obligations.

Available election forms

Available elections: (a) the ELP exemption election under the Limited Partnerships Act 2019, electing exempted status and accepting the statutory prohibition on Gibraltar business; (b) for United States federal tax purposes, the IRS check-the-box election on Form 8832 to classify the ELP as a partnership or as an association taxable as a corporation, the default being partnership status because the ELP has general partners with unlimited liability; (c) hybrid-mismatch documentation: where the ELP is transparent in Gibraltar but opaque or differently classified in another jurisdiction, the Gibraltar anti-hybrid rules (aligned with ATAD) require the partners to confine deduction and non-inclusion benefits and to document the dual treatment contemporaneously; (d) no check-the-box equivalent exists in Gibraltar domestic tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None: Gibraltar imposes no VAT and no GST. Indirect taxation is confined to customs import duties, excise duties and stamp duty under the administration of the Department of Tax and Customs. The UK VAT system (20% standard rate) does not extend to Gibraltar; a Gibraltar ELP supplying automated payroll software to overseas business customers is outside any Gibraltar indirect-tax charge. For B2B supplies to UK customers, the UK reverse charge applies to the customer; for B2C digital supplies to UK consumers, the ELP may be separately obliged to register for UK VAT with HMRC.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A: no VAT or GST registration threshold exists in Gibraltar because no such tax is chargeable. The UK registration threshold (GBP 90,000 from 1 April 2024) is irrelevant to a Gibraltar domestic supplier absent a UK business establishment. Registration with Gibraltar Customs becomes mandatory only where the ELP imports dutiable goods into Gibraltar; that registration arises from specific import transactions, not from any economic-turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in VAT terms because Gibraltar has no VAT and therefore no zero-rating or refund mechanism for exported services. Under the territorial income-tax system, the ELP's exported payroll and compliance services are not derived from Gibraltar, are not chargeable to Gibraltar income tax, and require no refund claim. The ELP must preserve its exempted status, avoid a Gibraltar permanent establishment, and maintain contemporaneous evidence of the non-resident location of clients. For any physical goods exported from Gibraltar, exports are outside customs duty, and drawback or end-use relief may apply to imported inputs incorporated in exported goods.

  • Gibraltar Customs export declaration (only where physical goods are exported; not applicable to pure SaaS services)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, with commercial invoice, for any goods consignment
  • Executed SaaS master subscription and service agreement evidencing the non-resident customer and its place of business
  • Bank statements and SWIFT or ACH inward-remittance records showing settlement by non-resident clients
  • ELP annual return and exemption declaration filed with the Registrar of Companies

None: Gibraltar maintains no real-time electronic fiscal clearance, no SAF-T or PEPPOL obligation, no API-based continuous transaction control, and no digital stamping for services invoices. The ELP may issue standard commercial invoices under generally accepted invoicing practice. Where goods are imported or exported, declarations are transmitted electronically through the Gibraltar Customs electronic system, with the Department of Tax and Customs retaining access to declaration data and supporting documentation.

Gibraltar imposes no exchange controls and no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day period functions as the internal substantiation benchmark against which inward remittances are matched to underlying invoices and contracts. If the ELP breaches its exemption conditions, carries on business in Gibraltar, loses its Qualifying Person general partner, or makes false declarations, the Commissioner of Income Tax may withdraw exempted status, raise retrospective assessments on the Gibraltar-source income of the ELP and its partners (12.5% rate for corporate partners and progressive gross-income-band rates for individuals), and impose statutory interest, civil penalties for non-filing or fraudulent conduct, and criminal liability for tax evasion or false statements. The Gibraltar General Anti-Abuse Rule and anti-hybrid measures allow recharacterisation of artificial arrangements and the clawback of any relief previously granted in reliance on the pass-through or territorial treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains an open capital and current account with unrestricted convertibility between GIP, GBP, EUR, USD, and all major freely traded currencies. There are no exchange-control approvals, quotas, or prior-authorisation mechanisms for inbound or outbound current or capital transactions. Profit remittances, partnership distributions, and liquidation proceeds of an Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) are freely payable to non-resident partners without withholding or repatriation restrictions by virtue of the ELP's pass-through classification and Gibraltar's territorial income-tax framework. The only operational frictions are commercial-bank KYC/AML checks, OFSI/UN/EU sanctions screening, and enhanced due diligence for FATF-listed jurisdictions; these do not constitute legal convertibility barriers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

None applicable. The United Kingdom and Gibraltar impose no mandatory surrender, compulsory conversion ratio, or statutory repatriation timeline on export proceeds or cross-border service receipts. SaaS subscription fees and payroll-platform revenues may be retained indefinitely in any currency denomination. The binding obligations are limited to: (i) complete and accurate record-keeping for tax and AML purposes under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar); (ii) statistical reporting to the Bank of England's International Transactions Reporting System (ITRS) where cross-border transaction thresholds are met; and (iii) compliance with the UK Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018, as extended to Gibraltar by the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018 (Overseas Territories) Order 2020, including freezing and surrender of proceeds involving sanctioned persons or jurisdictions.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the integrated prudential regulator and supervisor of banks, electronic money institutions, and payment service providers; Bank of England as the monetary authority underpinning GBP stability; HM Treasury / Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) as the authority extending and administering financial sanctions to Gibraltar. The Commissioner of Currency of the Government of Gibraltar exclusively issues the Gibraltar pound at a fixed 1:1 peg to GBP. Gibraltar has no independent central bank exercising exchange-control or monetary-policy functions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium-High conditional viability. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant partner capital injections use SWIFT MT103 with field 70 remittance information populated as 'Capital contribution pursuant to Limited Partnership Agreement dated [date]' together with the partner's capital-account reference. Statistical classification follows the Bank of England ITRS as 'Direct Investment – Equity Capital (Inward)'. The receiving tier-1 Gibraltar bank requires, before credit confirmation: executed LP Agreement, board/partner capital-call resolution, UBO register extract, sanctions-screening evidence, and source-of-funds documentation for each contributing partner. Funds settle to the ELP's segregated operational account in the ELP's legal name; no commingling is permitted. No Gibraltar exchange-control approval is required, but the bank's AML function will verify the originator bank and jurisdiction and may impose enhanced EDD for non-EEA partners.Payroll and staff-compliance disbursements clear via: (i) SWIFT MT103 (single) or MT101 (batch) for non-EEA and non-GBP corridors, using ISO 20022 pain.001 instructions with payroll-batch identifiers; (ii) CHAPS for same-day high-value GBP (e.g., HMRC settlements, senior payroll) and Faster Payments for low-value UK payroll runs; (iii) SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant for EUR-denominated payroll in EEA jurisdictions; (iv) ACH (NACHA) and BACS/Faster Payment Direct Debit for recurring UK-US payroll volumes, through the ELP as registered Originator or via an FCA-licensed PSP; (v) Commercial Letters of Credit are unsuitable for payroll obligations and are reserved for ancillary hardware/infrastructure procurement where SWIFT MT700 and deferred-payment LC terms would apply; (vi) mandatory pre-release OFSI/UN/EU sanctions filtering and beneficiary-account verification to prevent payments to sanctioned employees or contractors. Settlement risk is mitigated by segregated client-fund safeguarding (where applicable), daily camt.053 statement reconciliation, and multi-currency liquidity buffers sized against the 13-HR payroll automation trigger.Deliverable FX forwards (outright) for GBP/EUR/USD payroll exposures, executed under an ISDA 2002 Master Agreement with CSA margining through a GFSC-licensed or FCA-supervised bank counterpartyNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) strictly limited to non-convertible or quota-restricted emerging-markets currencies used in payroll corridors, documented by master confirmation and subject to OFSI complianceCross-currency swaps and FX swaps for tenor-matched conversion of partner capital contributions and surplus liquidity, with EMIR (Gibraltar) trade-reporting, clearing, and margin requirements verified

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-High conditional viability. The ELP may utilise GFSC-licensed electronic money institutions under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2010 or, more practically, UK FCA-authorised and EEA-authorised EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, MultiPass, Payoneer, Airwallex) for high-velocity payroll disbursements, provided each EMI holds client-fund safeguarding, is regulated in an FATF-equivalent jurisdiction, and does not engage in unauthorised deposit-taking or lending. Legal constraints: (i) the ELP cannot itself conduct payment services or issue e-money without a Gibraltar payment-services licence; (ii) use of offshore EMIs does not displace GFSC-derived AML/CTF obligations, including payee verification; (iii) GDPR compliance and lawful data-transfer mechanisms are required when the EMI processes employee personal data; (iv) core treasury balances should remain in tier-1 commercial banks to mitigate counterparty and continuity risk inherent to younger EMIs.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold published; OECD BEPS-aligned documentation recommended whenever connected-party transactions arise; practical threshold: annual related-party transactions exceeding GBP 1,000,000 or qualifying entity meeting OECD large multinational thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); any Gibraltar tax-resident entity carrying on 'relevant activities' (including service centre functions, financing, or headquarters) must demonstrate substance: local premises, local full-time employees, and local decision-making. An ELP with central management in Gibraltar will be captured.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Financial penalties up to 100% of tax evaded; daily penalties; criminal prosecution with fines and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors/general partners personally liable for knowing participation; civil restitution, travel bans, and custodial sentences possible.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: ESR penalties of GBP 10,000 to GBP 50,000 plus daily penalties; possible cancellation of tax residence.

Director personal liability: Disqualification orders; personal civil liability for penalties; travel restrictions.
AML / Proceeds of Crime

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under Proceeds of Crime Act 2015; imprisonment up to 14 years; unlimited fines.

Director personal liability: Personal forfeiture orders; travel bans; extradition under Gibraltar-UK arrangements.
Transfer Pricing / Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Adjustments to taxable profit; interest and penalties up to 100%; cross-border information exchange.

Director personal liability: Liability where fraudulent trading is established; court orders imposing personal restitution and potential travel bans.
