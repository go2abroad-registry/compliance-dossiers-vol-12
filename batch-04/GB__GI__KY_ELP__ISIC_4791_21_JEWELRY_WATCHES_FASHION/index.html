Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Gibraltar law does not provide a US-style 'check-the-box' election. The ELP is fiscally transparent by default under the Income Tax Act 2010, with each partner assessed on their allocable share. An ELP may apply for a private tax ruling ('protective assessment') from the Commissioner of Income Tax confirming the exclusion of non-Gibraltar-source income of non-resident partners. For hybrid mismatch purposes, the partnership may align its tax character with the partner jurisdictions under the Gibraltar hybrid mismatch rules (Income Tax Act 2010 and administrative guidance), allowing the ELP to be treated as either transparent or opaque in relation to a particular payment to prevent double non-taxation or mismatched deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST — Gibraltar applies Customs Duty and Excise Duty under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance; the jurisdiction is outside the European Union VAT area and outside the UK VAT regime.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Gibraltar does not impose a domestic VAT/GST, and therefore there is no local turnover threshold for indirect tax registration. However, where the ELP holds inventory in the United Kingdom or EU consumer markets, foreign VAT registration thresholds must be monitored (UK VAT registration threshold GBP 90,000; EU Union OSS distance-sales threshold EUR 10,000). The Gibraltar Customs Department requires import/export reporting and, where applicable, EORI registration for all cross-border goods movements.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a domestic Gibraltar concept because Gibraltar does not levy VAT/GST. By analogy and in respect of any UK or EU VAT registration the ELP holds, zero-rating applies to the export of goods to customers outside the VAT jurisdiction, provided the goods are physically removed from the jurisdiction within the statutory time limit (usually 90 days for UK VAT) and the exporter retains proof of export. For the online merchandising of fashion jewelry and fine timepieces, the ELP may also zero-rate supplies of services connected to exported goods (e.g., international delivery and insurance) and, if eligible, claim a foreign VAT refund or input VAT credit using the cross-border EU refund mechanism (e.g., Article 171 VAT Directive procedures) or the applicable bilateral refund treaty.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD / UK CDS declaration) showing commodity codes for jewelry and timepieces
  • Commercial Invoice (itemized description, HS/CN code, value, currency, incoterm)
  • Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from the shipping jurisdiction
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 or payment processor settlement statement proving receipt of foreign exchange proceeds
  • Proof of Identity and Authorization of the purchaser (for AML/CTF and high-value goods compliance)
  • Certificate of Origin (if preferential tariff treatment is claimed in the destination market)
  • Insurance certificate or valuation certificate for high-value timepieces and fine jewelry

No mandatory domestic electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping regime exists in Gibraltar. Exports of jewelry, watches, and fashion goods from Gibraltar to third countries must be declared electronically through the UK/Gibraltar customs electronic lodgement systems (Customs Declaration Service or national export system). For foreign VAT registrations (UK/EU/other), the ELP must comply with the relevant B2B e-invoicing obligations of the destination jurisdiction, including PEPPOL or other mandated formats, should the customer or market require it.

Gibraltar imposes no exchange-control or statutory foreign-currency repatriation deadline; the 180-day parameter reflects the evidentiary benchmark used by tax authorities to validate genuine export proceeds and to resist reclassification as domestic or unidentified income. Fraudulent or abusive zero-rating claims (including falsified export documentation, over-declared values, or sham export transactions) expose the ELP and its general partner to retrospective VAT clawback, interest, penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded, monetary penalties under the Customs and Excise Duties Ordinance, forfeiture of goods, criminal prosecution, and disqualification of the general partner. In the UK, deliberate VAT evasion on exported goods attracts a penalty of 30% to 100%, potential withdrawal of the VAT registration number, and publication of defaulters.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility without foreign-exchange controls. The GIP is legally interchangeable with GBP at par, and multi-currency accounts in EUR, USD, GBP, and CNY are freely permitted. No exchange approval, residency condition, or capital-export permit is required to repatriate profits, make partner distributions, or remit capital from a Gibraltar-permitted entity; the absence of Gibraltar withholding taxes further eliminates remittance friction. The regime is subject only to standard AML transaction reporting and source-of-wealth documentation obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirements apply in Gibraltar or the United Kingdom to export proceeds derived from online retail of fashion jewelry and fine timepieces. Export receipts may be retained indefinitely in the original settlement currency (USD, EUR, GBP, CNY) in unrestricted multi-currency accounts. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no collection moratorium, and no obligation to route proceeds through a domestic bank; however, financial institutions must file suspicious activity reports on transaction thresholds consistent with GFSC AML guidance and UK aligned counter-terrorism financing reporting.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary anchor for sterling) together with HM Government of Gibraltar, which issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP) at a fixed 1:1 parity with GBP; prudential and conduct regulation is vested in the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019 and the Banking Act 2015.

0% — Gibraltar does not impose withholding tax on dividends or on typical profit distributions from an ELP. Distributions to non-resident partners are not subject to Gibraltar tax unless the ELP is deemed to be carrying on a trade in Gibraltar.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File preparation is required when the ELP's annual gross turnover exceeded GIP 20 million (≈ GBP 20 million) OR when the aggregate value of cross-border controlled transactions exceeded GIP 1 million in a fiscal period. Master File and Country-by-Country Reporting capacity apply to groups with consolidated group revenue of ≥ GIP 200 million.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar has enacted Economic Substance Regulations (both domestic and those required by the EU Code of Conduct Group). The ELP, when operating an e-commerce retail business in jewelry, watches, and fashion, must demonstrate substantive economic activity in Gibraltar if the trade generates Gibraltar-source profits. Substantivity includes: (1) a registered office and operational premises; (2) qualified full-time employees (two or more) managing and directing the business; (3) local bank accounts; (4) board/partner meeting minutes held in person in Gibraltar; and (5) adequate accounting records kept in Gibraltar. Passive holding entities are not automatically caught by the highest substance test, but active e-commerce is classified as a relevant activity (distribution and service centre) and would require real presence. Failure to meet the economic substance test converts the ELP into a liable entity for Gibraltar corporate income tax and exposes the entity to penalties and forced tax assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Concealment

Statutory sanction: Criminal conviction under Section 105 of the Gibraltar Income Tax Act. Fine up to GBP 1,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years. The court may additionally order payment of 100% of the evaded tax plus interest. HM Customs can issue a user condition order prohibiting movement of assets.

Director personal liability: Partners, shadow partners, and managing individuals are jointly and severally liable for restitution. Personal travel bans, asset freezing orders, and confiscation orders may be issued. Upon criminal conviction, a commercial disqualification order may apply for up to 15 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Under the Economic Substance Regulations, the failure to satisfy the economic substance test results in a civil penalty of GIP 100,000 plus daily default penalties of GIP 5,000 per day. In addition, the Commissioner may ignore the tax-transparent status and assess a notional 12.5% tax on the ELP's chargeable profits, with interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners owe a statutory duty to ensure the ELP satisfies the substance obligations. A contravention creates personal liability for the civil penalty and personal disqualification from acting as a director or partner in Gibraltar. Travel bans and removal of benefited status (e.g., right of residence) can also be imposed.
AML / CDD Failure in E-commerce Payment Processing

Statutory sanction: Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 (Gibraltar): fines up to GIP 500,000 or 10% of the annual turnover derived through the retail process. The Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit (GFIU) may freeze all bank and payment processor balances, and criminal confiscation of the ELP's estate may be ordered. Money laundering offences carry custodial sentences up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and the general partner of the ELP are criminally liable if they knowingly or negligently allow the ELP to be instrumental in money laundering. This may lead to personal imprisonment, individual fines, seizure of personal assets, and a statutory prohibition on holding a financial services license or acting as a beneficial owner.
Inaccurate or Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act, a civil penalty of GBP 10,000 is imposed for each incorrect or misleading tax return, plus 100% of the tax undercharged. Where fraud or negligence is proven, the maximum criminal fine is GBP 500,000 and/or imprisonment for up to three years. Any late payment triggers the 3% surcharge regime.

Director personal liability: In addition to the entity-level penalties, every designated partner or director who signed the return may be held personally liable for the penalty. If the error is the product of an unqualified tax advisor, the partner may still face liability for negligent mismanagement of the ELP, but may seek contribution from the advisor in contract/tort.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar applies OECD transfer pricing rules. Absent valid Local File, the Commissioner can adjust the ELP's profits to an arm's length outcome and impose a penalty ranging from 50% to 120% of the additional tax paid. A specific failure to keep documentation triggers an additional fixed penalty of GIP 10,000 per reportable transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally accountable for the preparation and submission of transfer-pricing documentation where thresholds are exceeded. Failure to do so may give rise to personal fines and removal from the registry, as well as the application of a senior accounting officer (SAO) sanction for unsuitable tax compliance arrangements.
