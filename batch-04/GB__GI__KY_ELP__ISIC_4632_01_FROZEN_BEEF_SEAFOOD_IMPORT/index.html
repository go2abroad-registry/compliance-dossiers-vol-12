Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax), 6-10 Parliament Lane, Gibraltar; portal: www.ito.gov.gi. Corporate registration: Registrar of Companies, Cyprus House, 71/71a Irish Town, Gibraltar (www.companieshouse.gi). Customs and EORI registration: HM Customs Gibraltar.

E-government filing portal

1) Register the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Limited Partnerships Act (Cap. 216) via a Gibraltar-resident general partner or licensed local agent; obtain the Certificate of Registration and Partnership Number. 2) Submit Income Tax Form 1 (Application for Registration) to the Commissioner of Income Tax to obtain the partnership TIN and individual Gibraltarian TINs for each partner (non-resident partners receive a non-resident TIN series). 3) Obtain an EORI number from HM Customs Gibraltar for import/export declarations. 4) File annual partnership returns (Partnership Return under the Income Tax Act 2010) and, if trading locally, payroll tax returns. Foreign entities without a permanent establishment in Gibraltar file a nil or non-resident return; no statutory audit is required for the ELP unless partners are Gibraltar residents or the partnership is insolvent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope - The ELP is fiscally transparent; partners, not the partnership, bear taxation, and only on income accruing in or derived from Gibraltar. Non-resident partners are outside the charge for foreign-source trading income; Gibraltar levies no capital gains tax (except land-development gains), no wealth tax, and no withholding tax on dividends or interest. No general tax-exempt zone exists, but qualifying company status and territorial sourcing rules confer de facto exemption on offshore income.

Available election forms

Gibraltar does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime. Available statutory elections: (1) Election for transparent partnership treatment of the ELP under the Income Tax Act 2010 (mandatory default, with partners electing to be assessed jointly or individually); (2) Election for territorial scoping of partner income as Gibraltar-source vs. foreign-source per the Income Tax (Sourcing of Income) Rules; (3) Election by a qualifying non-resident partner to be excluded from Gibraltar tax on deemed distributions from an ELP; (4) Hybrid mismatch elections under the Income Tax (Hybrid and Mismatch) Rules 2017, applying ATAD-based alignment to avoid double non-taxation; (5) Election to file a consolidated Partnership Return instead of separate partner returns where all partners are Gibraltar-resident.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Customs and Excise Duties only - Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and levies no VAT, GST, or sales and use tax; imports are liable to the Gibraltar customs tariff, excise (limited to alcohol, tobacco, and mineral oils), and compulsory import licensing for meat and seafood consignments.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST registration threshold exists in Gibraltar. All commercial importers must register with HM Customs Gibraltar and hold an EORI number before the first import. The frozen meat and seafood wholesale activity additionally requires an import licence from the Gibraltar Competent Authority (Veterinary and Environmental Health) and a mandatory electronic customs entry per shipment regardless of turnover; the effective threshold is zero.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating as a VAT concept does not exist because Gibraltar imposes no VAT. Equivalent relief is granted under the customs tariff: goods imported into and subsequently re-exported from Gibraltar are relieved from customs duty under inward-processing, duty-suspension, or end-use re-export rules. To claim duty-free treatment or a refund of customs duty paid on re-export of frozen beef and seafood, the trader must demonstrate that (a) the goods were not sold or consumed in Gibraltar, (b) they were physically exported within the authorised period, and (c) the import and export transactions are covered by a single commercial cycle with matching HS chapters 0202 (frozen beef) and 0303/0304 (frozen pelagic fish and fillets).

  • Customs Export Declaration (SAD C88 or ASYCUDA export entry) bearing the approved exit date from Gibraltar or a UK/EU transit port
  • Bill of Lading (or Sea Waybill / CMR for overland transit through Spain) evidencing physical carriage to the third-country destination
  • Import Entry (SAD C88) and the inward-processing or end-use authorisation reference supporting duty relief
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SWIFT/TT credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin matching the frozen beef and seafood consignment
  • Health and phytosanitary clearance certifying compliance with destination-country import rules

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoice stamping, or continuous data transmission mandate applies in Gibraltar; electronic invoicing is voluntary and recognised under the Electronic Commerce Act. Customs clearance is mandatory through Gibraltar's electronic declaration platform (ASYCUDA World / Gibraltar Customs Management System), requiring a Single Administrative Document (C88) entry, risk-based inspection, and release upon the Customs Entry Acceptance Number. For movements via the UK or the EU, the UK-EU customs treaty framework requires electronic export and import summary declarations.

HM Customs Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office audit traders under the Customs Ordinance (Cap. 104) and the Income Tax Act 2010. Penalties for fraudulent export relief claims include forfeiture of goods, duty clawback of up to three times the duty evaded, and criminal fines up to GBP 5,000 with imprisonment up to two years. In tax audits, misstated export documentation is treated as fraudulent misdeclaration: the Commissioner may reopen assessments for six years (ten years for fraud) and impose additional tax of up to 100% of the underassessment together with statutory interest. The Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and import-licence revocation apply to trade-based money-laundering schemes using false bills of lading or circular remittances.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is legally pegged at 1:1 to GBP, and GBP banknotes are freely accepted alongside GIP. Current and capital account transactions are fully convertible with no general exchange control approvals; profit remittances and capital repatriations are unrestricted. The only practical hurdles are transaction-based AML/KYC verification, sanctions screening, and documentary compliance under the UK Global Tariff where physical imports clear through customs.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio applies under Gibraltar or UK law. Exporters may retain receipts in any freely convertible currency and are not obliged to convert into GIP or GBP. Records of all export proceeds must be maintained for at least five years under the Proceeds of Crime Act and FATF-aligned record-keeping rules. This inbound frozen beef and seafood import structure has no export surrender exposure.

Central bank supervision: HM Treasury / Bank of England (UK monetary authority) together with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the statutory regulator for banking, payments and investment services; Gibraltar has no separate domestic central bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium with material legal constraints. A Gibraltar ELP may maintain an operational balance with an FCA-authorised UK EMI or EEA-authorised EMI for ancillary payment flows, but such EMI cannot be the core treasury provider for this high-volume frozen meat and seafood import business because it cannot issue documentary credits, provide SWIFT correspondent settlement, or support cold-chain trade documentation. Post-Brexit passporting into Gibraltar is not automatic; the EMI must rely on UK/Gibraltar transitional arrangements or local GFSC licensing, and is typically prohibited from undertaking banking activities such as accepting deposits from the public. Use of an EMI should be limited to pre-funded supplier payments with no cargo title routing.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (Gibraltar does not impose any domestic withholding tax on distributions or profit shares paid by an Exempted Limited Partnership to its partners)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be filed by 30 November following the end of the relevant year of assessment (1 July to 30 June). For an accounting period ending on 30 June, the filing deadline is 30 November of the following calendar year, providing a 12-to-17-month window depending on the period end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 aggregate connected-party transactions during the accounting period triggers the requirement to prepare and retain a Local File; GIP 10,000,000 combined group turnover triggers the need for a Gibraltar-compliant Master File. The Gibraltar Income Tax Office may also issue a formal TP notification for transactions below these thresholds in exchange pricing of commodities or cold-chain logistics.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) under the Income Tax (Economic Substance) Regulations 2020 apply. Importation and wholesale distribution of frozen meat and seafood constitutes a 'Distribution and Service Centre' relevant activity, requiring the ELP to demonstrate (a) direction and management in Gibraltar, (b) adequate employees and physical premises in Gibraltar, and (c) adequate expenditure attributable to the core income-generating activity. A file must be submitted using the ESR return appended to the CT-1; failure to comply exposes the ELP to fines and migration of tax residence to the partners' jurisdictions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and deliberate under-declaration

Statutory sanction: On conviction: a fine up to GIP 150,000 and an additional penalty of up to 100% of the evaded tax (capped at 150% of net tax deficiency); a further daily fine of up to GIP 1,000 for continued failure to correct.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where the director or general partner authorised the evasion. Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties, subjected to a travel ban, disqualification from acting as a director for up to 15 years, and imprisonment for up to 7 years if criminal fraud or false accounting is proved.
Economic Substance Regulations non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Fixed penalty of GIP 1,000 per return; additional daily penalty of GIP 500 per day for non-compliance beyond 30 days; potential loss of Gibraltar tax residence for the ELP, exposing partners to tax in their home jurisdictions.

Director personal liability: If the director or general partner is the person who knowingly caused the false ESR declaration, they are personally liable to a fine of GIP 5,000 and, following conviction, imprisonment for up to 2 years. The court may also issue a disqualification order preventing them from acting as a director of any Gibraltar company or partnership.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and trade-based fraud

Statutory sanction: Fines of up to GIP 250,000 and forfeiture of assets; freezing of bank accounts and revocation of the financial services licence of the operative trading entity; prohibition on using Swift TT and LC settlement rails for 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners, and compliance officers are individually prosecutable. They may face imprisonment of up to 14 years under the Criminal Evidence (Money Laundering) Rules, personal monetary compensation to the victim for the amount laundered, and an automatic entry into the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission's prohibition register.
Transfer pricing and connected-party documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 10% of the net tax liability arising from the adjustment, plus a fixed penalty of GIP 10,000 for non-documentation. Where transfer pricing adjustment exceeds GIP 2,000,000, a further fine of GIP 50,000 per year of assessment.

Director personal liability: The general partner or director who signed the CT-1 without verifying TP documentation becomes personally liable for the adjusted under-taxation, and in matters of gross negligence the court may impose a civil penalty equal to twice the tax underpaid. In aggravated cases, a concealment penalty of 100% of tax due and a director ban of 10 years apply.
