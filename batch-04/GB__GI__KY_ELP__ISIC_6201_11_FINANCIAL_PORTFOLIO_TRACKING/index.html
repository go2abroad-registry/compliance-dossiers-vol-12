Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Gibraltar Tax and Customs Duty Division, HM Government of Gibraltar (https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation)

E-government filing portal

A foreign entity seeking to register for Gibraltar taxation obtains a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number by filing the prescribed registration application with the Commissioner of Income Tax, including the entity's constitutional documents, registered office details, particulars of partners or members, and evidence of economic substance or activity. The application is processed through the Income Tax Office under the Income Tax Act 2010 and the Taxes (Administration) Act 2010; upon approval, the entity receives a statutory GRN (Gibraltar Reference Number) used for all tax filings, VAT registration, and customs purposes. Non-resident entities with no fixed establishment or taxable supply connection are not normally required to register, but a non-resident company or partnership with Gibraltar-source income, a permanent establishment, or a place of effective management must register and disclose the relevant particulars.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign entity seeking to register for Gibraltar taxation obtains a Gibraltar Tax Identification Number by filing the prescribed registration application with the Commissioner of Income Tax, including the entity's constitutional documents, registered office details, particulars of partners or members, and evidence of economic substance or activity. The application is processed through the Income Tax Office under the Income Tax Act 2010 and the Taxes (Administration) Act 2010; upon approval, the entity receives a statutory GRN (Gibraltar Reference Number) used for all tax filings, VAT registration, and customs purposes. Non-resident entities with no fixed establishment or taxable supply connection are not normally required to register, but a non-resident company or partnership with Gibraltar-source income, a permanent establishment, or a place of effective management must register and disclose the relevant particulars.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Gibraltar law does not operate a check-the-box election comparable to the U.S. classification regime. An Exempted Limited Partnership registered as KY_ELP is statutorily treated as fiscally transparent unless it elects, or is deemed to have elected, to be treated as taxable under the Qualifying Partnership regime. For hybrid mismatch purposes, an ELP may elect to be treated as a separate taxable person for indirect tax and certain anti-avoidance provisions, but for income tax it remains transparent unless it carries on a trade or business through a permanent establishment in Gibraltar. Cross-border hybrid entity elections are assessed under the U.K. and Gibraltar tie-breaker rules and the OECD hybrid mismatch framework where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar VAT and indirect purchase tax regime (integrated with EU-derived import controls, but distinct from U.K. VAT)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold because Gibraltar does not levy a local VAT on domestic transactions; however, any person importing goods into Gibraltar, or supplying services deemed to be supplied in Gibraltar for customs duty or Purchase Tax purposes, must register for the relevant transactional taxes where turnover or import volume exceeds the de minimis limits set by the Purchase Tax Ordinance and Customs Duty Ordinance. The practical threshold for purchase tax registration is nil for taxable importers, but an economic activity test applies to foreign digital service providers with no established presence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Given Gibraltar's zero-rated indirect tax system for exported services, a financial portfolio tracking software provider may claim a zero-rating or non-taxable treatment on supplies made to customers located outside Gibraltar, provided the recipient, the effective use, and the place of supply satisfy the export rules under the Gibraltar Purchase Tax and retained EU place-of-supply provisions. Offshore services concerning financial information and software-as-a-service are treated as exported if the recipient is a non-resident business or a private consumer outside Gibraltar, the supply is not effectively used or enjoyed in Gibraltar, and the supplier retains proof of the export.

  • Customs Export Declaration or Intrastat-equivalent dispatch record for any physical media or server equipment
  • Commercial invoice or digital export contract containing customer identity and non-Gibraltar place of supply
  • Electronic evidence of delivery or cloud-service activation outside Gibraltar (e.g., server logs, IP addresses, platform activation records)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank transfer confirmation evidencing settlement in foreign currency
  • Written confirmation of the recipient's business status and country of establishment for B2B zero-rating claims
  • Annual statement of export supplies submitted to the Income Tax and Customs Duty Division

Gibraltar does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing, or digital stamping for the software-as-a-service and financial portfolio tracking sector. Invoices must be issued in accordance with retained EU invoicing rules for cross-border B2B and B2C transactions, including identification of the supplier, the customer, the nature of the supply, the date, the consideration, and any applicable purchase tax or customs duty reference. No pre-clearance e-invoicing gateway exists for ISIC 6201 services; however, records of all digital exports must be retained in an electronically accessible format that permits audit by the Commissioner of Income Tax and HM Customs.

Any false or unsupported zero-rating or export exemption claim is subject to a clawback of the unpaid Purchase Tax, plus interest from the due date, and a tax-related penalty of up to 100% of the tax evaded where the misstatement is deliberate. Misrepresentation of export status for software services may additionally trigger misuse of the Gibraltar digital services facilitation regime, with penalties for incorrect documentation of up to GBP 10,000 per transaction for a first offence and disqualification from zero-rating for a period of up to 24 months. The Commissioner may retrospectively assess up to six years for innocent errors and twelve years for deliberate concealment, and may require a bond or guarantee from the taxpayer before granting future zero-rating clearance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains no exchange-control legislation. The Gibraltar pound is fixed at parity with pound sterling and is freely convertible into GBP at 1:1. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are fully open; there are no general restrictions on inward or outward remittances, no mandatory Central Bank approvals, no profit-repatriation barriers for an Exempted Limited Partnership, and no withholding at the ELP level. Because the ELP is treated as pass-through, cash distributions to non-resident partners can be remitted freely, subject only to underlying bank AML/KYC evidence, sanctions verification, and the tax rules of the partners' countries of residence. Multi-currency balances in USD, EUR, or GBP are permissible; conversion occurs at commercial rates with a GFSC-licensed bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Gibraltar. There is no statutory deadline for surrendering export receipts, no minimum conversion percentage, and no requirement to route export proceeds through the Central Bank or any designated domestic account. Export proceeds and SaaS/IAP-related sale settlements may be retained in the currency received, in GIP/GBP, or converted into any acceptable clearing currency at the commercial discretion of the ELP, subject only to banking documentation and AML/CFT record-keeping.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary anchor); HM Government of Gibraltar (issuer of Gibraltar pound notes and coin); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as banking, AML/CFT, and financial services supervisor. Gibraltar has no separate domestic central bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary transaction inflows and low-value disbursements, but Low as a principal treasury rail for the ELP. Offshore digital EMIs may be used only if the institution is appropriately authorised under a recognised e-money regime and can pass the GFSC-licensed bank's correspondent-risk checks. Legal constraints: Gibraltar payment services and electronic-money activities are prudentially supervised; an unlicensed or purely offshore EMI cannot provide the settlement finality, capital-escrow integrity, FX capacity, and clearing membership required for partner distributions or material vendor payments. Stripe and Apple IAP may settle into an EMI or bank account, but the core operating and capital accounts should remain with a GFSC-licensed commercial bank or a fully authorised UK/EU bank with a formal Gibraltar servicing arrangement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound partner capital must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the partner's own bank account to the ELP's GFSC-licensed operating or capital-escrow account. The remittance instruction should include the bank-specific purpose code for 'partnership capital contribution / equity participation' (for example the bank's internal code for capital inflow, commonly CAPC or EQUI) and a free-text narrative identifying the ELP's name, the registered number, and the capital contribution transaction. The beneficiary bank will require the general partner's capital-contribution resolution, the updated partner register, source-of-funds evidence, and confirmation that no third-party intermediary is the ultimate remitter. Because Gibraltar does not apply surrender rules, incoming USD or EUR can be placed in a multi-currency account or converted to GIP/GBP through the bank's treasury desk. Sanctions screening, PEP screening, and FATF-based beneficial-ownership transparency are mandatory.Routine vendor, cloud-cost, salary, and licensing payments must be executed over SWIFT MT103 where the counterparty is outside Gibraltar, or over SEPA/CHAPS/Faster Payments if a UK or EU account is held. The return must be filed within 12 months after the end of the basis period and no later than 31 December of the year following the year of assessment; if the Revenue issues a notice to file under section 23 of the Income Tax Act, the return is due within 30 days of service of the notice.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 of aggregate connected-party revenue in the accounting period requires a full Gibraltar Local File; Master File and country-by-country reporting apply where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000. Transactions below GIP 1,000,000 require only a unilateral transfer pricing declaration within the tax return.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Guidelines require the ELP to be managed and controlled in Gibraltar, maintain a registered office and local bank account, employ adequate staff, and perform its core income-generating activities in the territory. Because the ELP is tax-transparent, the substance test applies to the partnership and, where relevant, the general partner. Provision of cloud-based portfolio tracking and payment-pairing services must be evidenced by developer contracts, server logs, board minutes, and payroll records. Failure to satisfy core income-generating activities in Gibraltar triggers automatic loss of QELP status, a 12.5% tax charge, and penalties; a pure letterbox entity is rebutted as a shell arrangement under the EU and OECD anti-harmful-tax-practice framework.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Penalty regime of the Gibraltar Income Tax Act: fines up to GBP 100,000 plus accuracy penalties of 100% of the evaded tax; aggravated offences attract summary prosecution and asset forfeiture under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.

Director personal liability: The general partner and its directors can be pursued civilly for the joint tax debt on wrongful trading principles; the Gibraltar Supreme Court may impose director disqualification orders, prohibitory travel bans, and imprisonment of up to 7 years for fraudulent tax evasion.
Shell Company / Artificial Arrangement Doctrine

Statutory sanction: Loss of exempt status; retrospective 12.5% tax charge on profits; penalties up to 150% of the tax understated; the corporate services provider is also fined and potentially struck off.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are personally liable for tax evasion debts; courts will pierce the corporate veil, freeze bank accounts, and order personal contribution orders without limitation, including seizure of foreign-held assets through anti-money laundering machinery.
AML and Terrorist Financing Failures

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission Regulations: civil penalties up to EUR 100,000 or 10% of annual turnover, unlimited criminal fines, licence withdrawal, and confiscation orders.

Director personal liability: Senior officers are personally liable under the approved-person rules; travel restrictions, ancillary orders, extended bans on financial-services roles, and imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering are applicable.
Transfer Pricing Contravention and Unsupported Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The Revenue may adjust profitability to arm's length, disallow the QELP exemption, and impose a corrective tax rate of 12.5% on the entire Gibraltar-source profit; secondary penalties of up to GBP 50,000 apply for non-disclosure.

Director personal liability: In transfer pricing disputes, directors of the general partner face personal contribution orders for the additional tax; failure to correct the record under the Tax Administration and Practices Act founds personal liability of up to treble damages in civil proceedings.
