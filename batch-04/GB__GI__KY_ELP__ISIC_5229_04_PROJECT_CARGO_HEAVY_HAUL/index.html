Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office (Office of the Commissioner of Income Tax), HM Government of Gibraltar, acting for direct taxes; HM Customs Gibraltar (Customs and Revenue Department), acting for customs and indirect taxes. Official portal: Gibraltar eGov Tax E-Services (https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi) and the HM Customs Gibraltar electronic declarations interface.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible with territorial scope: the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) is fiscally transparent; partners are taxed individually on their profit shares, and, being non-resident, the ELP is chargeable only on income arising in or derived from Gibraltar, leaving non-Gibraltar project-cargo income outside the Gibraltar tax net.

Available election forms

Gibraltar law does not provide a check-the-box or elective entity-level taxation regime; under the Income Tax Act 2010 partnerships are statutorily transparent, and no election can render the ELP assessable as a company. The ELP may elect, through its nominated partner, the accruals basis of accounting and may designate a representative taxpayer for procedural compliance. No local hybrid-mismatch elections are available; the OECD Multilateral Instrument and the Gibraltar General Anti-Abuse Rule neutralise artificial hybrid-mismatch outcomes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar does not operate a VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax; indirect taxation is effected through Gibraltar customs import duties, excise duties, and the Purchase Tax under the Purchase Tax Ordinance, administered by HM Customs Gibraltar. UK VAT does not extend to Gibraltar.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable (GIP nil threshold): Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST registration turnover threshold. Purchase tax attaches at importation or on the first supply in Gibraltar, and any importer or supplier of taxable goods must hold an HM Customs Gibraltar customs operator / client registration regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar purchase tax; where duty-paid imported goods are exported within the prescribed period, the exporter may claim a customs duty drawback under the Customs Ordinance. Zero-rating or export relief is available to the ELP where: (i) the OOG project cargo is physically removed from Gibraltar to a destination outside Gibraltar; (ii) the export is unconditional and evidenced by electronic customs export declarations and transport documents; (iii) the sale is made to an overseas buyer or end-user for use wholly outside Gibraltar; (iv) consideration is paid from non-Gibraltar sources; and (v) the goods have not entered Gibraltar home-consumption, unless held under duty-suspension or bonded-warehouse arrangements. Services supplied to non-residents for use entirely outside Gibraltar are not subject to Gibraltar purchase tax and are evidenced by contract, invoicing, and actual-use documentation rather than a refund mechanism.

  • Gibraltar Customs electronic export declaration (export entry / SAD-format where applicable)
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill (with AWB/CMR for multimodal OOG heavy-haul movements)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and out-of-gauge project-cargo stowage certificate
  • Delivery orders, port discharge certificates, or consignee receipts evidencing foreign destination
  • Certificate of Origin or Form A / movement certificate where preferential origin is claimed
  • Inward or outward remittance certificate or bank statement evidencing receipt from the non-Gibraltar counterparty

Gibraltar imposes no mandatory real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, API stamping, or digital duty-stamp regime. Customs and purchase-tax accounting is effected through electronic import and export declarations in the HM Customs Gibraltar clearance system; invoices must be issued in accordance with ordinary commercial law, and customs entries must be filed electronically, but no government API integration or real-time invoice validation is required.

HM Customs Gibraltar and the Commissioner of Income Tax subject export claims to substantive audit scrutiny. False zero-rating or drawback claims attract forfeiture of goods, civil penalties of up to treble the value of the goods or the duty involved, and criminal prosecution under the Customs Ordinance and the Income Tax Act 2010, including penalties up to 100% of the under-assessed tax plus interest. Retrospective clawback of duty drawback, cancellation of duty-suspension facilities, revocation of customs operator registration, and personal liability of the general partner or nominated partner for fraudulent or negligent statements are also enforceable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility for the Gibraltar pound against GBP and all major reserve currencies. There are no exchange controls, no prior approval requirements for profit or capital remittance, and no restrictions on overseas investment by Gibraltar corporate entities. Convertibility is assured by the currency peg to GBP, and all GIP balances are convertible to GBP at par without administrative intervention.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Gibraltar imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rules. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, no repatriation deadline, and no requirement to sell export receipts to the central bank or any designated dealer. Exporters may hold, disburse, or reinvest receipts in any freely convertible currency without FX surrender or reporting obligations beyond standard AML/CTF record-keeping.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019, with currency issuance and central banking functions exercised by HM Government of Gibraltar through the Currency Notes Ordinance (Gibraltar pound pegged 1:1 to GBP).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-Low. Only Gibraltar-licensed Electronic Money Institutions under the Financial Services Act 2019 can maintain local payment accounts. UK or EEA EMIs cannot passport into Gibraltar post-Brexit; such foreign fintechs may provide card issuing or collection support only through GFSC-compliant wrappers. Primary treasury liquidity must reside in a licensed Gibraltar bank to ensure substance and regulatory credibility.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 1,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions per tax period; a Local File must be maintained where the ELP's intra-group expenditure or revenue exceeds GIP 1,000,000, and a Master File is required where the multinational group consolidated revenue exceeds GIP 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar applies Economic Substance Regulations to resident entities conducting relevant activities. Pure out-of-gauge heavy project cargo logistics will normally fall outside the specified 'relevant activities' unless the ELP carries on distribution or service-center functions. An ELP claiming non-resident partner treatment must nonetheless demonstrate that central management and control, major overhead costs, and key logistics decision-making are not situated in Gibraltar, and must retain board minutes, crew manifests, project files, and bank records evidencing the physical location of operations.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Income Tax Act 2010, penalties not exceeding 100% of the undercharged tax plus late interest at up to 10% per annum; criminal prosecution may result in an unlimited fine and/or imprisonment for up to seven years.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the ELP veil where the general partner or de facto director signs a false return. Directors/general partners become jointly and severally liable for restitution of the tax shortfall, may be subjected to travel bans and asset seizure, and may receive disqualification orders from holding Gibraltar company or partnership offices.
Artificial Allocation to Non-Residents

Statutory sanction: Gibraltar's anti-avoidance legislation empowers the Commissioner to counteract the tax advantage and impose a penalty of 30% of the counteracted tax, rising to 100% in non-disclosure cases, plus statutory interest from the end of the accounting period.

Director personal liability: The general partner who knew, or ought to have known, of the artificial allocation is personally liable for the counteracted tax and penalties. Repeated violations expose directors to civil recovery proceedings, travel restrictions, and imprisonment for cheating the public revenue.
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The Revenue may reprice the controlled transactions and adjust taxable profits, causing additional tax, a penalty not exceeding 100% of the deficit, and interest at up to 10% per annum; the burden of proof shifts adversely to the taxpayer when documentation is absent.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify non-arm's length transfer prices without a reasonable basis are jointly liable for assessed uplift. In egregious cases, a director may be made personally liable for penalties and subjected to a travel ban pending settlement.
Anti-Money Laundering and Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under Gibraltar's Proceeds of Crime Act and AML regulations, persons involved in laundering logistics funds face civil forfeiture, unlimited fines, disqualification, and imprisonment of up to 14 years; corporate fines may be imposed on the ELP in addition to seizure of the project assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners are natural persons for AML purposes; the corporate veil is disregarded, and the court may order personal restitution, asset freezes, travel bans, and penal sentences where the individual knowingly facilitated or deliberately ignored the money-laundering arrangement.
