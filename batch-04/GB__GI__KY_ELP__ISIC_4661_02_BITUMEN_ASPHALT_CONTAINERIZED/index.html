Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP): (i) register the limited partnership with Gibraltar Companies House to obtain the partnership registration number; (ii) file the tax registration application with the Income Tax Office to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN); (iii) submit the partnership deed, general partner details, registered office address, and a declaration of source of income and place of effective management; (iv) if an exemption election is sought for non-resident partners, file the applicable exempt partnership status declaration with the Commissioner of Income Tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible (tax-transparent under Gibraltar Income Tax Act; non-resident partners are chargeable only on Gibraltar-source income; a non-resident ELP with no Gibraltar-source income is outside the charge to tax) — For a foreign-owned Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP): (i) register the limited partnership with Gibraltar Companies House to obtain the partnership registration number; (ii) file the tax registration application with the Income Tax Office to obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN); (iii) submit the partnership deed, general partner details, registered office address, and a declaration of source of income and place of effective management; (iv) if an exemption election is sought for non-resident partners, file the applicable exempt partnership status declaration with the Commissioner of Income Tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (tax-transparent under Gibraltar Income Tax Act; non-resident partners are chargeable only on Gibraltar-source income; a non-resident ELP with no Gibraltar-source income is outside the charge to tax)

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election is available in Gibraltar. Gibraltar law applies transparent treatment to limited partnerships that satisfy the statutory definition; an exempt partnership election may be lodged with the Commissioner of Income Tax. Hybrid mismatch adjustments under Gibraltar's OECD BEPS Action 2 rules apply where the ELP is a constituent entity of an MNE group; limited derogation elections are available for small partnerships where statutory thresholds are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar has no VAT/GST or retail sales tax; import duties are chargeable only at importation under the Customs Ordinance

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST exists; therefore no turnover-based indirect tax registration threshold is triggered

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - Gibraltar does not levy a VAT/GST on exports. Containerised shipments of drummed and bagged penetration grade bitumen leaving Gibraltar attract no Gibraltar export tax; import VAT or excise duty, if any, is triggered by the importation into the destination jurisdiction. Retention of the full export file is nevertheless essential to support the customer's VAT recovery and any zero-rating in the counterparty jurisdiction.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (Computerised Transit System / XML manifest)
  • Original Bill of Lading or multimodal transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / SWIFT MT103 matching the contract
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (ISIC 4661 drummed penetration grade bitumen)
  • Certificate of Origin where a preferential origin claim is made

No real-time e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, digital stamping, or tax-API invoice mandate exists in Gibraltar. Any indirect tax obligation arises only in the destination jurisdiction (e.g. EU import VAT on entry).

No Gibraltar indirect-tax clawback is possible because no export VAT is chargeable. However, false export declarations, misdescription of bitumen grade, or abuse of customs procedures are penalised under the Customs Ordinance through seizure of goods, financial assessments up to treble the duty evaded, criminal sanctions against partnership officers, and potential de-registration or credibility challenges in the destination jurisdiction.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar and the United Kingdom maintain full convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. There are no foreign exchange controls, no restrictions on profit remittance, and no prior approval requirements for dividends, partnership distributions, or capital repatriation. The ELP is tax-transparent, and distributions retain the character of the underlying trade proceeds. Currency conversion is free at market rates through licensed banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export receipts in Gibraltar or the United Kingdom. Export proceeds may be held in the invoiced currency, converted freely, or repatriated without a central bank declaration. Only standard customs reporting under the UK Trade Tariff and the Common Transit Convention applies to containerized exports of bitumen.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) is the statutory financial regulator; the Government of Gibraltar issues Gibraltarian pound notes at parity with GBP under the Currency Notes Act; HM Treasury and the Bank of England exercise oversight through the Crown dependency relationship, but there is no separate Gibraltar central bank and no exchange control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is Medium-High for a Gibraltar-based ELP. Offshore digital EMIs authorised by the GFSC under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act or by the UK FCA under the E-Money Regulations may operate within the EEA under passporting rights. Legal constraints include maintaining segregated client funds, complying with anti-money laundering (AML) identification of all limited partners, and accepting that some correspondent banks may stringently review commodity trading flows. A GFSC-licensed EMI is viable for collections and supplier disbursements below £50,000 per transaction, while larger bitumen containerised trade settlements should be routed through tier-1 commercial banks with documentary credit capability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the partner's home jurisdiction into the Gibraltar ELP operational account. The wire must include a clear purpose code: for capital contribution use 'CAPT' where available or ISO 20022 'Capital Transfer'; for intercompany funding use 'CLRS'; for trade advances use 'ADVA'. Verification is completed through an inbound credit advice matched against the executed capital call and board resolution. No central bank notification is required in Gibraltar; the bank will request source-of-funds evidence for amounts above EUR 10,000 equivalent, including banker's reference, financial statements, or partnership capital account certificate.Supplier settlements are executed using SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfers of containerised asphalt payments, and by documentary commercial letters of credit issued under UCP 600 where the vendor requests deferred payment or conformity assurance. The standard rail is: Gibraltar ELP account issues payment order to bank; bank transmits SWIFT MT103 with ICC Purpose Code 303A for wholesale trade; settlement is made in GBP, USD, or EUR according to contract. For LC operations, the Gibraltar bank issues an irrevocable LC in favour of the bitumen supplier, with presentation documents comprising bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and quality/weight certificate. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Gibraltar publishes no codified de minimis threshold; prevailing professional practice: mandatory Local File for controlled cross-border transactions exceeding £1,000,000 per year (bitumen intercompany purchase/resale margin); Master File and CbCR required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue exceeding £750,000,000. Contemporaneous benchmarking is expected where the ELP transacts with affiliate suppliers or buyers.

Economic substance scope: The Gibraltar Economic Substance Act (2019/2020) covers entities carrying on relevant activities, including distribution and service-centre operations, where the entity is tax resident in Gibraltar. A pass-through ELP with control and management outside Gibraltar and with no Gibraltar-source income is outside the substance net; the corporate general partner and the licensed registered agent must, however, be able to evidence non-residence, no Gibraltar meetings, no local staff, and no storage or warehousing of bitumen in Gibraltar. If Gibraltar tax residence is inadvertently established, the ELP must demonstrate full-time local staff, physical premises, and core income-generating activities (procurement, risk management, invoicing) located in Gibraltar.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Civil: penalty up to 200% of the tax evaded plus statutory interest at 8% per annum. Criminal: fine up to £250,000 and imprisonment of up to 5 years under the Income Tax Act 2010; proceeds may be confiscated under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are jointly and severally liable for abetting evasion; imprisonment up to 5 years, unlimited personal confiscation orders, travel bans, and director disqualification orders of up to 15 years.
Shell Company / Sham Substance Structure

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization of the ELP as a sham; denial of pass-through treatment; strike-off by the Registrar of Companies; penalty of up to £5,000 per annum for false filing; best-of-judgment assessment at 12.5% on deemed Gibraltar profits.

Director personal liability: Directors and the registered agent face civil restitution to the Revenue, director disqualification for up to 15 years, mandatory disclosure obligations under tax transparency regulations, and personal liability for debts if the partnership trades while falsely representing substance.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment to arm's length profit plus interest at 8%; penalty of 100% of the additional tax assessed; secondary adjustments treated as deemed distributions; documentation-failure penalty up to £10,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate transfer-pricing certifications expose themselves to penalty proceedings for knowingly furnishing false documents; liability for the secondary adjustment can extend to shareholders and the corporate general partner.
AML and Sanctions Violation

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2015: unlimited fine and imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering; GFSC regulatory fine of up to £1,000,000 or 5% of annual turnover for systemic controls failures; freezing orders and asset forfeiture.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner with actual knowledge or suspicion face criminal prosecution for money laundering; travel bans, seizure of personal assets, and extradition under UK-Gibraltar mutual legal assistance arrangements are available.
Repeat Non-Filing and Tax Default

Statutory sanction: Escalating fixed penalties up to £1,000 plus daily surcharges of £25; automatic best-of-judgment assessment; revocation of the ELP pass-through election; publication of the ELP on the tax defaulters list.

Director personal liability: The corporate general partner and the appointed tax representative become jointly and severally liable for all unpaid Gibraltar tax, interest, and penalties; the Commissioner may apply for a travel ban and issue international arrest requests for principals.
