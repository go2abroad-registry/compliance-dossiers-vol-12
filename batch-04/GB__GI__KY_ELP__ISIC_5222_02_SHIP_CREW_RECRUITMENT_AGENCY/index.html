Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar, 32 Europort, Gibraltar GX11 1AA; official portal www.ito.gov.gi (digital filing via Income Tax e-Services/IRIS). Customs and indirect duties: HM Customs Gibraltar.

E-government filing portal

A Cayman Islands Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) establishing tax identity in Gibraltar must: (1) file an Application for Tax Identification Number (TIN) and Registration for Non-Resident Entities with the Income Tax Office, executed by the General Partner, accompanied by the Cayman Islands Certificate of Registration, the Limited Partnership Agreement, and a certified copy of the registered office address in Cayman; (2) submit Form T-37 'Application for Tax Treatment Certificate' under s.12(5) Income Tax Act 2010 to elect fiscally transparent treatment so each partner's share of income/loss is taxed as if derived directly; (3) where the ELP operates through a Gibraltar-registered branch or maintains a fixed place of business, register under the Business (Registration of Businesses) Act 1999 with the Registrar of Businesses; (4) if employing crew through a Gibraltar payroll, register as an employer with the Income Tax Office and the Director of Social Insurance for payroll withholding; (5) obtain a Gibraltar Economic Substance notification/online filing reference if the ELP's activity falls within a 'relevant activity' under the Income Tax (Economic Substance) Act 2018 (crew recruitment to third-party shipowners generally falls outside the defined relevant activities, but the filing must be made for the record); (6) upon completion, the Income Tax Office issues a permanent TIN within 15–30 working days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Gibraltar law does not provide a domestic 'check-the-box' regime. The operative election is the s.12(5) Income Tax Act 2010 Tax Treatment Certificate, which fixes the ELP's transparent classification for all Gibraltar tax purposes and binds the Commissioner. For hybrid mismatch exposure under the Income Tax (Anti-Hybrid) Regulations 2019 (ATAD implementation), the ELP must align its Cayman classification (opaque per Cayman law) with the Gibraltar transparent treatment to prevent a 'reverse hybrid' result where one or more UK/EU-resident partners obtain a double deduction or non-inclusion. No separate CFC or PE participation exemption election applies at the partnership level; however, a non-resident partner may elect the remittance basis to confine Gibraltar taxation to locally remitted income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT or GST in force in Gibraltar. Indirect taxation comprises import duties and excise taxes under the Customs & Excise (General) Act 1999 and Customs & Excise (Goods) Act 2020, plus stamp duties on specific instruments. A General Consumption Tax (GST) at 12.5% has been announced but remains deferred and is not law. The jurisdiction stands outside the UK VAT area.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. A business carrying on any activity in Gibraltar must register under the Business (Registration of Businesses) Act 1999 irrespective of turnover. If the deferred GST is enacted, the proposed registration threshold mirrors the UK model at GIP/GBP 85,000 of taxable supplies. Employers must register for payroll withholding as soon as any employee is engaged.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating does not apply as Gibraltar has no VAT. The functional equivalent for income tax is the territorial 'offshore income' exemption: a Cayman ELP deriving crew recruitment and crewing management fees from non-resident shipowners in respect of vessels trading wholly outside Gibraltar is outside the charge to Gibraltar income tax under s.8 Income Tax Act 2010, provided: (i) the services are negotiated and performed outside Gibraltar or by personnel not resident in Gibraltar, (ii) no Gibraltar permanent establishment or fixed place of business is maintained, (iii) the vessel is not registered in Gibraltar and is beneficially owned by non-Gibraltar persons, and (iv) the income is not credited to a Gibraltar bank account of the ELP (if the ELP itself opens a Gibraltar account, the 'remittance' of service fees into that account may trigger chargeability depending on partner residence). The ELP should obtain a Tax Treatment Certificate evidencing transparency and, where applicable, a certificate from HM Customs Gibraltar that the services constitute offshore supplies. No indirect tax refund mechanism exists.

  • Customs Export Declaration (only where tangible ancillary goods, e.g., vessel stores, marine equipment, are exported from Gibraltar)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill (for physical exports; for pure services, the equivalent evidence of cross-border supply),

No real-time fiscal clearance, electronic-invoice stamping, continuous transaction control (CTC), or mandatory API clearance regime operates in Gibraltar. Invoices and supporting records must be retained for 6 years and produced to the Income Tax Office on audit request. Import and export declarations must be filed electronically through HM Customs Gibraltar's customs declaration system, and any goods exported for crew supplies or vessel stores may require electronic Customs Summary Declarations. There is no digital stamping or fiscal API authentication for service invoices.

The Income Tax Office may reopen assessments for up to 6 years in cases of negligent misdeclaration and without time limit for fraudulent or wilful default. Where the offshore/zero-rating position is denied, the Commissioner will assess the ELP's Gibraltar-sourced income and may impose: failure-to-notify penalties up to GBP 300 plus GBP 60 per day; late payment interest at 8% per annum or such alternative base rate as published; accuracy penalties up to 100% of the tax understated; and, for deliberate concealment, additional daily penalties up to GBP 10,000. Under the Income Tax (Economic Substance) Act 2018, a relevant entity failing the economic substance test for a relevant activity may incur a penalty of GBP 100,000 per tax year, cumulative daily penalties, and ultimately strike-off or winding-up. Aggressive avoidance schemes involving the ELP may additionally attract the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) with counteraction of the tax advantage, and the general partner may be held jointly and severally liable for partnership-level penalties. Fraudulent claims of offshore status with intent to evade tax are criminalised under the Income Tax Act 2010 and the Criminal Justice Act 1998, carrying fines and imprisonment.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Full convertibility on both current and capital account. GIP is a 1:1 pegged note issue with no exchange-control restrictions or floating-rate conversion friction. A Gibraltar ELP that is non-resident for tax purposes may hold USD, EUR, GBP or GIP accounts freely, and profit distributions to non-resident partners face zero Gibraltar withholding tax; the sole practical hurdle is the bank's source-of-funds (SOF) and source-of-wealth (SOW) verification of underlying partners, not any statutory convertibility ceiling.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable — zero surrender or mandatory conversion requirement. Gibraltar repealed exchange controls and imposes no statutory timeline for surrendering export receipts: all seafarer-staffing service fees (a service export under ISIC 5222) may be retained in any currency without conversion into GIP. The only compliance duty is bank-reported classification of the receipts under the UK/Gibraltar BOPS ITRS reporting framework, filed by the bank rather than by the ELP.

Central bank supervision: The Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Currency under the Currency Notes Act 1934) issues the Gibraltar pound (GIP), which is pegged at 1:1 with sterling and fully backed by Bank of England notes. There is no standalone Gibraltar central bank: monetary policy is set by the Bank of England, and prudential supervision of banks and EMIs is conducted by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) under the Financial Services Act 2019.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium — Gibraltar-licensed and UK-regulated EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud, and GFSC-authorised EMIs under the Financial Services Act 2019) are viable for high-volume multi-currency crew payroll disbursements and offer remote onboarding, but they are not substitutes for a Tier 1 Gibraltar bank where capital-escrow, substance, or trade-finance rails are required. Legal constraint: an ELP may not use an EMI as an unregulated money-transmission box, and the EMI's safeguarding-account regime obliges treasury to maintain a segregated reconciliation ledger; therefore use a dual-rail architecture (Tier 1 bank for escrow and commercial LC; EMI for payroll disbursement micro-payments).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route partner capital via SWIFT MT103 from the partner's home bank to the Tier 1 Gibraltar escrow account, embedding in Field 70 the narrative 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION — GIBRALTAR ELP — ISIC 5222' plus the partnership's unique reference. For GBP injections, use CHAPS same-day clearing; for EUR, use SEPA/SWIFT with the creditor's IBAN. The bank classifies the credit under BOPS/ITRS direct-investment category (partner capital into a partnership) rather than trade revenue; the ELP must provide the CSP letter and partner resolution naming the account and purpose to avoid a return-wire or suspicion flag.Primary rail is SWIFT MT103 for seafarer contract wages and MT202 COV for interbank transfers where sub-agents execute regional payouts; commercial letters of credit (UCP 600 / ISBP 745) are used only where shipowners pre-finance crew mobilisation through the Banco Sabadell trade desk. Payroll executions operate on segregated multi-currency payroll sub-accounts using a two-layer model (shipowner→agency gross settlement, then agency→sub-agent→seafarer) fully reconcilable to MLC 2006 wage statements; SEPA/CHAPS rails may be used only for intra-UK/EEA beneficiary wallet configurations and never bypass the SWIFT audit trail.Deliverable FX forward contracts (GIP/GBP, USD, EUR) for locked-cost projection of crew wage liabilitiesFX swaps for treasury liquidity repositioning between USD receivables and EUR/GBP payroll drainsCross-currency swaps for multi-year ship-management agreements with long-dated foreign-currency revenue streamsPlain-vanilla FX options (call spread/collar structures) to protect the agency's commission margin against adverse EUR/USD and GBP/USD movesNDFs are not market-practicable for GIP (peg to GBP); for other pairs, GFSC-authorised banks will only allow NDFs with an underlying crew-payroll exposure and documented trade rationale

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium — Gibraltar-licensed and UK-regulated EMIs (Wise Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud, and GFSC-authorised EMIs under the Financial Services Act 2019) are viable for high-volume multi-currency crew payroll disbursements and offer remote onboarding, but they are not substitutes for a Tier 1 Gibraltar bank where capital-escrow, substance, or trade-finance rails are required. Legal constraint: an ELP may not use an EMI as an unregulated money-transmission box, and the EMI's safeguarding-account regime obliges treasury to maintain a segregated reconciliation ledger; therefore use a dual-rail architecture (Tier 1 bank for escrow and commercial LC; EMI for payroll disbursement micro-payments).

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at ELP entity level because the Exempted Limited Partnership is fiscally transparent; corporate partners are subject to 12.5% on their attributable share of Gibraltar-source profits, and resident individual partners are subject to progressive rates up to 35%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. Gibraltar does not impose any withholding tax on dividends, partnership profit distributions, or capital distributions made by an ELP to its partners.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 500,000 in gross related-party revenue or GIP 150,000 in individual connected-party transactions triggers mandatory contemporaneous documentation. A Local File must be prepared and available on request; a Master File and Country-by-Country report must be maintained if the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue exceeds GIP 25,000,000 and cross-border covered transactions exceed GIP 5,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Requirements) Act 2020, as amended, applies to Gibraltar ELPs carrying on maritime crew management, shipping, or service-business activities. The ELP must demonstrate active direction and management in Gibraltar, adequate physical premises, qualified seafarer-recruitment staff, board or partner meetings with minutes, and a proportionate flow of operating expenses. A pure payroll-routing shell without operational substance will fail the ESR test and is subject to financial penalties, compulsory exchange of information to the United Kingdom, and possible strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Under-Declaration of Gibraltar-Source Crew Agency Income

Statutory sanction: Assessment to recovered tax plus interest at 8% per annum; civil penalty up to 200% of the under-declared tax; criminal conviction on indictment resulting in a fine up to GIP 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any directing partner are treated as officers of the ELP. Fraudulent evasion pierces the limited-liability shield, exposing directors and partners to joint and several civil restitution, personal asset seizure, travel bans, disqualification from managing companies in Gibraltar for up to 15 years, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Artificial Structure Under Economic Substance Rules

Statutory sanction: Failure to meet the economic substance test attracts an initial penalty of GIP 20,000 and monthly default penalties of GIP 10,000 per relevant activity. The Commissioner may also strike off the ELP and transmit the substance assessment report to the United Kingdom and competent tax authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners who knowingly permit the ELP to operate as a shell may be personally assessed for unpaid tax, disqualified under the Companies Act for up to 15 years, and subjected to cross-border information exchange, with civil liability for the full tax loss caused by the artificial arrangement.
Money Laundering Through Crew Payroll Disbursements

Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 criminalises laundering the proceeds of criminal conduct with a maximum penalty of 14 years' imprisonment and an unlimited fine; the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission may also revoke the AML registration and freeze bank accounts.

Director personal liability: General partners and compliance officers faces personal criminal exposure, personal confiscation orders, asset freezing, travel restrictions, and disqualification. Any attempt to rely on the ELP veil to shield AML misconduct will be rejected by the courts; individuals who participate in or consent to the laundering act are joint tortfeasors.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Sham Intercompany Charges

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner of Income Tax has power to make transfer-pricing adjustments applying the arm's length standard; the adjustment will be assessed with interest at 8% per annum and penalties up to 100% of the additional tax. Dishonest misuse of connected-party contracts may also trigger criminal fraud sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false intercompany service agreements or issue inaccurate TP declarations are personally liable for adjusted tax and penalties where the ELP is unable to pay. The court may pierce the limited partnership veil for fraud, impose director disqualification, and order personal restitution of the repatriated profits.
