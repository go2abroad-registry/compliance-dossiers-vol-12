Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Commissioner of Income Tax) — autonomous tax authority distinct from UK HMRC; company and partnership registration conducted through the Gibraltar Companies House Registry (www.csra.gi); tax registration and filing administered via the Income Tax Office portal (www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) with the Gibraltar Companies House Registry to obtain the statutory partnership registration number. Step 2: File the ELP constitutive deed, Gibraltar registered office address, certified partner particulars, and general partner details with the Income Tax Office on Form 01 (Application for Tax Registration). Step 3: Receive the Gibraltar Tax Identification Number (TIN) and partnership tax reference. Step 4: Submit the annual ELP declaration confirming that no trade or business is conducted in Gibraltar other than holding passive investments, and that all partners are non-resident; this maintains the territorial exemption and removes the partnership and its non-resident partners from the Gibraltar tax net.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Gibraltarian Exempted Limited Partnerships are fiscally transparent under the Income Tax Act 2010; each partner is assessed individually on their share of partnership profits. Non-resident partners are chargeable only on Gibraltar-source income. Foreign-source income, including international Ro-Ro shipping profits derived wholly outside Gibraltar, falls outside the charge to Gibraltar tax; an ELP with all non-resident partners is effectively tax-exempt.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists. Available statutory elections: (1) Partnership separate-assessment election (Ninth Schedule, Income Tax Act 2010) enabling each partner to be assessed directly on their profit share; (2) International Shipping Operator election under s.14A Income Tax Tax Act 2010, permitting a qualifying ship operator to elect into a tonnage-based fee regime for internationally-sourced shipping profits in lieu of income tax; (3) Discretion of the Commissioner to deem a partnership taxable as an opaque company where the partnership structure is established chiefly to secure a tax advantage.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST — Gibraltar levies no value-added tax or goods and services tax; indirect taxation comprises customs import duties, excise duties, and minor consumption levies under the Customs Ordinance and the Assessment of Duties Ordinance. Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT and EU Customs Union.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. Any economic operator importing goods into Gibraltar must register with the Customs Division as an importer of record (nil monetary threshold), and businesses providing taxable goods in Gibraltar may require a trading licence; customs registration is documented in GIP/GBP.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating is inapplicable because Gibraltar imposes no VAT. Physical exports of goods from Gibraltar are free of export customs duty. For direct tax, the s.14A Income Tax Act 2010 International Shipping regime applies to a deep-sea Ro-Ro car carrier: profits from international traffic operations of a qualifying ship — registered in Gibraltar or an approved register, with strategic and commercial management exercised in Gibraltar — are exempt from income tax and replaced by an annual tonnage fee.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic GEMS declaration including vessel particulars and cargo line items)
  • Ro-Ro Cargo Manifest and Stowage Plan (vehicle units loaded and consignee details)
  • Bill of Lading or Seaway Bill (contract of carriage and title evidence)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank SWIFT MT103 credit evidence (export proceeds repatriation)
  • Certificate of Registry and International Tonnage Certificate (evidence of qualifying ship status for the s.14A shipping exemption)

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory national e-invoicing, or digital tax-stamping regime applies to commercial invoices in Gibraltar. Customs import and export declarations are filed electronically through the Gibraltar Electronic Manifest System (GEMS), which requires pre-arrival and pre-departure cargo manifest lodgement and electronic clearance of declarations; however, there are no continuous transaction control (CTC) or government-approved invoicing API mandates.

No VAT export-refund clawback mechanism exists given the absence of VAT. Under the Income Tax Act 2010 (ss.113–121), fraudulent or negligent misstatements in a shipping exemption or partnership transparency claim trigger reassessment with a 6-year statutory look-back (unlimited in cases of fraud), interest on underpaid tax at 8% per annum, and civil penalties of up to 100% of the tax evaded for fraud or 50% for negligence. Customs export misdeclarations attract seizure or forfeiture of goods, monetary fines under the Customs Ordinance, potential revocation of the qualifying ship election, and retrospective withdrawal of the tonnage fee benefit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The GIP is fully convertible and pegged at parity to GBP. Both capital and current account transactions are freely convertible, with no exchange-control permission required for equity contributions, intercompany loans, freight earnings, dividends, or partnership distributions. A non-resident Exempted Limited Partnership may hold GIP, GBP, USD, and EUR accounts, and convert between them at commercial bank rates. Profit repatriation is subject only to standard CRS/FATCA reporting, AML/CFT vetting, and the requirement that the underlying Ro-Ro shipping activity be genuine and commercially substantive. There is no statutory restriction on maintenance of foreign-currency balances outside Gibraltar.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no mandatory export-proceeds surrender rules in Gibraltar. Ro-Ro freight invoices denominated in USD, EUR, or other foreign currencies may be retained indefinitely in offshore accounts without conversion to GIP or GBP. No statutory timeline applies for the realisation or repatriation of export earnings, and no prescribed conversion ratio is imposed. Banking counterparties may request supporting charter parties, bills of lading, and freight invoices for AML/trade-compliance purposes, but this is not an exchange-control requirement.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no separate central bank; monetary policy is effectively anchored through the Bank of England because the Gibraltar Pound (GIP) is pegged at 1:1 to Pound Sterling. Banking and AML/CFT supervision are exercised by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission, while HM Government of Gibraltar retains the legal authority to issue Gibraltar currency notes and coins under the Gibraltar Currency Notes Act.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ancillary payment conversion and small operational disbursements; Low as a primary treasury rail for capital escrow, documentary letters of credit, or high-value Ro-Ro trade settlements. Post-Brexit Portuguese, UK, and other EU/EEA electronic money institutions no longer benefit from automatic passporting into Gibraltar; an offshore digital EMI must hold a relevant labour license in Gibraltar or an express recognition from the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission. Many digital EMIs also lack the correspondent banking infrastructure to execute SWIFT MT700 trade instruments or receive USD clearing through US correspondent banks. Use a licensed traditional bank as the primary clearing entity and an EMI only for low-value, non-documentary payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from investors must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the investor's own bank account to the ELP's segregated capital escrow account at the Gibraltar clearing bank. The remitter name in field 50K must match the investor's KYC file, the beneficiary field 59 must carry the full legal name of the ELP, and field 70 should state 'CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION / ELP SUBSCRIPTION' with the task reference. Where the correspondent bank requires a purpose code, use the ISO 20022 purpose code 'EQUI' for equity-like capital or the applicable direct-investment code under the receiving country's BOP/transaction monitoring framework. The verification pack must include the executed capital subscription deed, updated partnership register, capital call notice, source-of-funds evidence for each investor, and a signed compliance declaration. Inbound funds should never be commingled in the general operating account before completion of the bank's internal 'source of funds' clearance.Outbound settlement for Ro-Ro operations must use a combination of SWIFT MT103 for direct customer credit transfers, MT202 COV for correspondent bank cover in USD/EUR where direct delivery is unavailable, MT700 for irrevocable commercial letters of credit covering bunker suppliers or port terminal services, and MT760 for standby letters of credit securing charter or terminal agreements. For immediate cleared USD payments, route via a New York correspondent using CHIPS/Fedwire; for GBP, use CHAPS through a UK clearing bank; for EUR, use TARGET2 via a eurozone correspondent. All disbursements must carry the underlying invoice or contract reference in field 70, and the bank's sanctions-screening engine must validate every beneficiary before release.Plain-vanilla deliverable FX forwards (GIP/USD, GIP/EUR, GBP/USD, EUR/USD) with tenors matched to freight receivables and bunker payments.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in currencies where physical delivery is impractical due to convertibility constraints or correspondent-market limitations.Cross-currency swaps (fixed-float and float-float) documented under ISDA 2002 with a credit support annex, used to align USD/EUR/GBP cash flows of vessel financing, charter hire, and operating expenditure.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For an accounting period ending on 31 December, the return must be filed by 30 September of the following tax year. The Gibraltar tax year runs 1 July to 30 June, so returns are due no later than six months after the fiscal year-end.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Due on 30 June each year for partnerships with a registered calendar-year accounting reference date; a 28-day extension may be granted by the Registrar for cause shown.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File must be prepared where the Gibraltar-resident entity's aggregate controlled transactions with related parties exceed GIP 500,000 in a fiscal year. A Master File is required where the multinational group's global consolidated revenue exceeds GIP 15,000,000 and the local entity's controlled transactions exceed GIP 1,000,000. The ELP must also maintain, within 30 days of a notice, contemporaneous documentation evidencing the arm's-length nature of any service fee, charter hire, loan, or hedging agreement with affiliates.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Code, issued under section 49 of the Taxation (Anti-Avoidance) Act 2019, applies to every Gibraltar-resident undertaking, including an ELP, that carries on a relevant activity. RoRo vessel chartering and deep-sea transport constitute 'shipping' as a relevant activity. The ELP must demonstrate directed and managed in Gibraltar: the majority of board meetings are held in Gibraltar, the general partner is fully resident and accessible, and the core income-generating activities (route planning, pricing, vessel chartering, and crewing decisions) physically take place at a sufficient scale in Gibraltar. If the ELP is tax-transparent but its partners are non-resident and central management is outside Gibraltar, it will not meet the economic substance test and may incur penalties or be treated as a non-Gibraltar resident.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Upon summary conviction, a fine not exceeding GIP 10,000 for each count; upon indictment, a fine up to GIP 250,000. Any tax evaded plus interest at 12% is recoverable, and the court may impose an additional penalty of up to 150% of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors, shadow directors, and the general partner of the ELP are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution. Where the evasion is aggravated, the court may issue a travel ban confiscating passports until all fines are satisfied, and a term of imprisonment not exceeding seven years.
Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: A written warning from the Gibraltar Income Tax Office and a mandatory filing of a corrective economic substance report. Repeated non-compliance may attract a fine of up to GIP 50,000 and automatic withdrawal of the ELP's tax residence certificate.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any individual who knowingly controls the shell may be disqualified from holding Gibraltar directorships for up to five years, and face personal liability for the full amount of any tax that would have been charged if the ELP had not been artificially interposed.
AML/CFT Deficiencies

Statutory sanction: Penalty for money-laundering offence of up to GIP 500,000 and confiscation of any proceeds. The Financial Services Commission may suspend or revoke the ELP's shipping licence and freeze the partnership bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Every general partner who failed to prevent the offence is criminally liable; directors may be personally fined up to GIP 100,000 and imprisoned for a term not exceeding fourteen years for laundering proceeds associated with the shipping business.
Misrepresentation of Residency

Statutory sanction: A declaration under the Gibraltar Companies Act that the ELP has ceased to be resident in Gibraltar for tax purposes. This retroactively subjects all previously untaxed profits to a 12.5% levy with interest. The Registry may also dissolve the entity after issuing two default orders.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed a false declaration may be personally prosecuted for perjury, punishable by up to three years' imprisonment and disqualification from serving as an officer of any Gibraltar company or partnership.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: An upward adjustment to chargeable income equal to the undercharge deducted, plus interest and a penalty of 100% of the additional tax. The Gibraltar Commissioner may disregard the artificial transaction under the Taxation (International and Other Provisions) Act.

Director personal liability: Where the TP manipulation is a flagrant part of a cross-border tax-avoidance scheme, the general partner is liable for the additional tax and penalties as a primary taxpayer, even if the ELP is fiscally transparent; personal guarantees will be enforced.
