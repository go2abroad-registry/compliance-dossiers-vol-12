Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office (ITO), HM Government of Gibraltar, 8 Casemates Square, Gibraltar; e-services portal https://www.ito.gov.gi. E-government filing portal

A non-resident-owned Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) must: (1) register with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies under the Limited Partnerships Act 2015 to obtain a Limited Partnership registration number; (2) submit Form TRN application to the Income Tax Office together with the certificate of registration and a full schedule of general and limited partners, each partner's legal name, jurisdiction of tax residence, and agreed profit-share ratios; (3) obtain a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) for the ELP; (4) file annual information/returns to the ITO confirming that all partners remain non-resident in Gibraltar and that no profits are attributable to Gibraltar-resident persons; and (5) register separately with the Gibraltar Customs Department to obtain a customs declarant code before lodging electronic export declarations. Non-resident partners are not subject to Gibraltar income tax on their distributive shares; the ELP, though registered, files nil tax returns where all persons holding an interest are non-resident.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under the Income Tax Act 2010, Gibraltar partnerships are taxed as fiscally transparent pass-through vehicles by default; the ELP itself is not a separate taxable person and each partner is assessed on their share of profits in their own jurisdiction. The Limited Partnerships Act 2015 permits the partnership agreement to set bespoke allocation ratios, and the partners may apply to the Commissioner of Income Tax for an advance ruling confirming non-residence-based non-assessment, nil withholding on distributions, and the absence of Gibraltar chargeable profits. No check-the-box hybrid election exists in Gibraltar law; however, a hybrid mismatch election may be documented in the partnership agreement where a partner's home jurisdiction treats the ELP as opaque, and the ITO will accept reasoned position papers supporting single-taxation or exemption outcomes consistent with OECD BEPS Action 2.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Gibraltar Customs Duty (Import Duty) regime — no VAT, no GST, no sales/use tax. Exports of goods from Gibraltar are outside the scope of Gibraltar indirect taxation and attract zero-rate treatment by territorial exception.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable: Gibraltar does not operate a VAT/GST system, so no economic-turnover VAT threshold exists. Any person engaging in commercial import or export transactions through Gibraltar must register with the Gibraltar Customs Department for a customs declarant code regardless of turnover; personal consignment duty exemptions apply only to non-commercial movements below the published de minimis value.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Because Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST, the EU-style 'zero-rating with refund' mechanism is not operative. An export is instead excluded from Gibraltar's indirect tax net by territoriality: prilled urea and potassium chloride exported by the ELP must physically leave the Gibraltar customs territory under a customs-approved export procedure, be sold to a buyer resident outside Gibraltar, and be adequately evidenced. Eligibility requires that no subsequent re-import into Gibraltar occurs unless properly declared, and that the export proceeds are received through formal banking channels in the ELP's operating account.

  • Electronic Customs Export Declaration (Gibraltar Single Administrative Document equivalent, lodged via Customs EDI)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the urea/potash consignment from Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (SWIFT MT103/MT950) evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Certificate of Origin (optional; retained for buyer-country preferential tariff claims)

Gibraltar mandates the electronic lodging of customs declarations through the Gibraltar Customs electronic data interchange (EDI) system; goods moved to or from the port of Gibraltar are subject to real-time risk screening and customs clearance at the port of exit. No real-time fiscal clearance of B2B invoices, no continuous transaction controls, no SAF-T file obligation, and no digital stamping/API-based invoice authorisation requirement applies to wholesale exporters of agrochemical commodities. Exporter records may be retained electronically but must be produced to the Commissioner of Income Tax or Customs Comptroller within 72 hours on demand.

The Customs Ordinance (Cap. 113) treats false export declarations, misdescription of goods, or fraudulent evasion as criminal offences: goods are subject to forfeiture and seizure, plus monetary penalties of up to GIP 10,000 per irregularity and imprisonment up to six months on summary conviction. Under the Income Tax Act 2010, any improperly claimed exemption, relief, or drawback is repayable to the Crown with statutory interest and a penalty of up to 100% of the tax under-assessed; for deliberate or concealed conduct, the maximum civil penalty doubles and criminal sanction may be pursued against both the general partner and the ELP itself. Given the Medium_Risk_Enhanced AML classification of agrochemical commodity trading, the Gibraltar FIU and the Income Tax Office will jointly scrutinise round-tripping, invoice discounting, and diversion of urea/potash to sanctioned end-users; repeated zero-rate or customs duty abuses may trigger retrospective clawback for up to six years and publication of the settlement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The UK and Gibraltar constitute a fully liberalised, open-account currency regime with zero exchange controls on either capital or current account transactions. Non-resident partners of an Exempted Limited Partnership may freely inject capital, repatriate profits, and convert GIP/GBP into USD or EUR for commodity settlements, provided the ELP has not elected onshore tax characterisation under Gibraltar's Income Tax Act. The only practical profit-remittance hurdles are the standard CRS/FATCA transparency filings, source-of-wealth documentation at the banking layer, and the absence of any partnership-level withholding tax, which ensures fully tax-transparent upstream distributions to non-resident partners. No central bank approval, exchange-control licence, or pre-funding notification is required for any inward or outward cross-border payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export-proceeds surrender obligation under UK or Gibraltar law. The ELP must retain all export receipts in the original invoicing currency (typically USD) and may hold them indefinitely in designated multi-currency trading accounts without any mandatory conversion into GIP/GBP and without any prescribed repatriation window. For enhanced due diligence purposes only, the receipting bank will require a copy of the underlying sale and purchase agreement, bill of lading, commercial invoice, and quality certificates to evidence the genuine physical charter of prilled urea and potassium chloride.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary anchor) with the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the local banking and AML/CFT supervisor; Gibraltar has no independent central bank and the Gibraltar pound (GIP) circulates at par with GBP under the Currency Notes Act 1934.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Gibraltar-licensed e-money institutions are numerically thin, while UK FCA-authorised EMIs (for example Revolut Business, Wise, and Airwallex) will generally accept a Gibraltar ELP for remote onboarding. The governing legal constraint is that an EMI cannot hold capital escrow balances, cannot issue documentary letters of credit, and cannot provide credit collateralisation; EMI functionality is restricted to e-money accounts, payment initiation, and small-value disbursements. For the fertiliser physical-charter trade, EMI rails are viable only for working-capital supplier pre-payments below USD 50,000 per transaction, whereas all capital-injection funds, LC-backed settlements, and freight payments above that threshold must clear exclusively through a Tier-1 licensed bank. Multiple EMIs decline unregulated partnership vehicles with non-resident limited partners unless the General Partner supplies a consolidated beneficial-ownership register and the underlying trade contract pack; therefore the EMI layer should be treated as a disbursement accelerator, not as the primary treasury spine.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant protocol for limited-partner capital injections: each non-resident LP originates a SWIFT MT103 from its own verified bank account to the ELP's Capital Escrow Account at Gibraltar International Bank, with SWIFT field 70 populated as 'LPC CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION [date]' and the ISO 20022 purpose code field set to 'DCGT' for capital contribution into an investment vehicle. Prior to funds release, the bank must receive the executed capital call notice authorised by the General Partner, a certified extract of the partnership agreement listing the contributing LP and contribution schedule, and an independent source-of-wealth report for any single contribution exceeding GBP 25,000. Incoming funds are locked in the escrow account for a five-business-day validation window, screened against HMT/OFSI and UN consolidated sanctions lists, and only then released to the Operational Trading Account for trade settlement. Third-party payments made for the benefit of a limited partner are rejected and recycled to the originator, prohibiting any capital-account funding through undisclosed intermediary accounts.Priority clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for supplier pre-payments, freight and handling charges, port demurrage, and inspection fees up to the counterparty's credit-capped limit; (2) irrevocable documentary Commercial Letter of Credit issued under UCP 600 (sight or usance 30 to 90 days) by Gibraltar International Bank or Barclays, payable against presentation of bill of lading, certificate of quality, certificate of origin, phytosanitary certificate where applicable, and the commercial invoice; (3) MT103 with MT202 cover for supplier-agent commissions only where the underlying chartering or distribution agreement expressly evidences such commissions. All outbound disbursements are matched against the trade-contract repository before release, and any payment to a jurisdiction outside the EEA, Switzerland, or the UK is pre-screened against OFAC, HMT, and denied-party lists. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: MEDIUM. Gibraltar-licensed e-money institutions are numerically thin, while UK FCA-authorised EMIs (for example Revolut Business, Wise, and Airwallex) will generally accept a Gibraltar ELP for remote onboarding. The governing legal constraint is that an EMI cannot hold capital escrow balances, cannot issue documentary letters of credit, and cannot provide credit collateralisation; EMI functionality is restricted to e-money accounts, payment initiation, and small-value disbursements. For the fertiliser physical-charter trade, EMI rails are viable only for working-capital supplier pre-payments below USD 50,000 per transaction, whereas all capital-injection funds, LC-backed settlements, and freight payments above that threshold must clear exclusively through a Tier-1 licensed bank. Multiple EMIs decline unregulated partnership vehicles with non-resident limited partners unless the General Partner supplies a consolidated beneficial-ownership register and the underlying trade contract pack; therefore the EMI layer should be treated as a disbursement accelerator, not as the primary treasury spine.
