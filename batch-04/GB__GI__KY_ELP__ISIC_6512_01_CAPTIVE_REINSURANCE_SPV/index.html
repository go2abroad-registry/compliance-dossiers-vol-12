Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Commissioner of Income Tax, Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Ministry for Finance); official portal: HM Government of Gibraltar eServices (gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation). Insurance regulatory authority: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the ELP with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies (Companies House Gibraltar) and obtain the limited partnership certificate of registration. Step 2: Apply to the GFSC for a captive reinsurance licence under Gibraltar insurance legislation and the Solvency II prudential framework; the regulated undertaking (the ELP itself or, where the licensing framework requires, its corporate general partner) must satisfy fit-and-proper, governance, and head-office requirements. Step 3: Notify the Income Tax Office of commencement of business within 30 days and obtain Gibraltar Tax Reference Numbers (TRNs) for the general partner and any limited partners chargeable to Gibraltar tax; the ELP is transparent and is not separately assessed. Step 4: For a qualifying captive, file a written election with the Commissioner of Income Tax under the Income Tax (Captive Insurance) Rules to apply the alternative deemed-profit basis of assessment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Gibraltar has no check-the-box regime; partnership transparency is mandatory, with each partner assessed individually on Gibraltar-source income. Statutory elections: (i) qualifying captive insurers may elect the alternative basis of assessment, under which taxable profit is deemed to be 19% of net written premiums (effective tax charge of approximately 2.375% at the 12.5% corporate rate), in lieu of ordinary territorial assessment; (ii) no new tax-exempt company status has been available since 31 December 2010; (iii) hybrid-mismatch and OECD BEPS-aligned anti-avoidance rules constrain elective outcomes and cross-border deduction/credit arbitrage.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT or GST regime. Gibraltar is excluded from the EU VAT territory and is outside the UK VAT and insurance premium tax systems; indirect taxation is confined to customs and excise duties on specified goods (e.g., alcohol, tobacco, fuel) and stamp duty on certain documents.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Gibraltar imposes no VAT/GST and therefore has no economic turnover threshold or indirect tax registration requirement; customs and excise obligations arise only upon importation of dutiable goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Gibraltar levies no VAT, GST, sales and use tax, or insurance premium tax, so no export zero-rating or input-tax refund mechanism exists. Services supplied from Gibraltar, including captive reinsurance and underwriting services, are outside the scope of any Gibraltar indirect tax, and exports of goods from Gibraltar are not subject to export duties.

  • Customs export declaration (Gibraltar customs control documentation)
  • Transport document (bill of lading, airway bill, or CMR consignment note)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward remittance certificate (not applicable; Gibraltar has no exchange controls and does not require FX repatriation certification)

No real-time fiscal clearance, digital stamping, API tax clearance, or B2B e-invoicing mandate exists in Gibraltar. Mandatory electronic filing applies to GFSC Solvency II regulatory returns and Income Tax Office corporate/partnership returns through the Gibraltar Government eServices platform.

No VAT zero-rating clawback risk exists because no VAT regime is in force. Misuse of the captive deemed-profit election, artificial segregation of underwriting income, or submission of falsified documentation may result in: (i) reassessment on actual Gibraltar-source profits at the 12.5% corporate rate; (ii) interest and late-payment penalties; (iii) penalties of up to 100% of tax avoided under the Income Tax Act 2010 for negligent or fraudulent conduct; (iv) criminal prosecution for deliberate tax evasion; and (v) GFSC administrative sanctions, including fines and suspension or revocation of the captive reinsurance licence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

GIP, GBP, USD, and EUR are freely convertible on both current and capital account; there are no exchange-control restrictions, surrender requirements, or conversion quotas on any transaction of the captive reinsurance ELP. Reinsurance premium income, claims proceeds, parental capital injections, loans, and partnership distributions may be remitted without limitation or licensing. The ELP, being transparent, incurs no Gibraltar withholding tax on distributions to non-resident partners; Gibraltar-source profits attributable to a permanent establishment are assessable at the standard corporate rate (currently 10%, subject to OECD Pillar Two top-up rules where applicable). The only material convertibility constraints are prudential: GFSC insurance rules require that the captive's eligible own funds and technical reserves be held in approved assets with an EEA/UK-regulated credit institution, and bank-level enhanced due diligence applies to all source-of-funds flows. Profit remittance is unrestricted once the annual audited return and GFSC solvency reporting are submitted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Gibraltar maintains no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no export-proceeds surrender obligation for any trade or reinsurance receipts. The ELP may freely invoice and retain premium income in GIP, GBP, USD, EUR, or any other trade currency, invest surplus liquidity per the GFSC-approved investment policy, and deploy funds for claims without conversion into local currency.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar has no central bank and no separate foreign exchange administration. The Gibraltar Pound (GIP) is issued by HM Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act and circulates at a fixed 1:1 parity with the Pound Sterling; the Bank of England acts as the de facto monetary anchor. Prudential, insurance, and AML/CFT supervision is exercised solely by the Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC). The UK Exchange Control Act 1947 was repealed in 1979, and no exchange-control permit regime has existed in Gibraltar since then; therefore no central bank approval or GFSC consent is required for any cross-border treasury movement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium for ancillary low-value premium collection and expense payments; Low for statutory reserve and own-funds custody. Gibraltar authorises domestic electronic money institutions under the Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act, and post-Brexit the UK-Gibraltar statutory passporting framework permits FCA-authorised UK EMIs to operate into Gibraltar without a separate licence, so high-volume small-ticket premium flows could be routed through a digital EMI for reconciliation efficiency. However, GFSC Solvency II-equivalent rules restrict eligible assets backing insurance liabilities to regulated credit institutions; unrated EMIs cannot hold regulatory capital, technical reserves, or unimpaired own funds, and EMIs do not provide the SWIFT bank-level clearing and correspondent-bank network required for true cross-border claim settlement. A GFSC-licensed digital bank (e.g., Xapo Bank Ltd) may support onboarding and operational payments, but should not serve as the sole custody bank for the captive's statutory reserves.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Parent-entity capital is routed by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank) with purpose code CAPC in field 70 for partnership capital contribution, or INSU where the inflow is premium; GIP/GBP legs clear via CHAPS through the corresponding UK clearing relationship, USD legs via Fedwire/CHIPS, and EUR legs via TARGET2. Before any debit, the remitting parent must pre-submit source-of-funds evidence: audited financials, board resolution approving the contribution, and the GFSC-approved capital plan; the GFSC-regulated receiving bank will freeze or reject funds lacking the reference to the captive licence number and the specific capital call. The statutory own-funds threshold set out in the licence must be fully funded in the capital escrow account before activation of treasury services, and a minimum liquidity buffer must remain at the principal clearing bank. Funds may not be routed via an EMI, unlicensed money-service business, or crypto exchange, and any conversion of USD or EUR to GIP/GBP must occur within the regulated bank's FX desk with a retained dealing ticket for regulatory audit.Claims and treaty settlements are executed over SWIFT gpi using MT103 with end-to-end tracking; GBP payments to UK and Gibraltar beneficiaries clear via CHAPS same-day; EUR payments settle via TARGET2 or SEPA Credit Transfer; USD payments clear via Fedwire for high-value wires and CHIPS for netted settlements. Large claims (above GBP 5 million) are prefunded from the technical-reserves trust account with a matching MT199 advice, and each instruction carries the treaty identifier and claims bordereau reference to satisfy GFSC conduct-of-business records rules. Fronting carriers are collateralised by UCP600-compliant standby letters of credit issued by the principal clearing bank in favour of the cedant; commercial letters of credit under ISP98 are used only where the reinsurance treaty expressly permits deferred payment. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File is mandatory where controlled cross-border transactions, including ceded premiums, claims, commissions, management fees and financing transactions, exceed GIP 1,000,000 in a chargeable period. A Master File is required where the ultimate parent group exceeds GIP 50,000,000 consolidated turnover. Country-by-Country Reporting applies at the OECD threshold of EUR 750,000,000 consolidated group turnover where the Gibraltar entity is an appointed constituent entity of a multinational group.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar's Economic Substance Requirements apply to an ISIC 6512 captive reinsurance SPV. The ELP must demonstrate directed and managed status in Gibraltar: local physical premises, duly appointed directors or general partner committees, board meetings in Gibraltar deciding underwriting and risk strategy, and adequate full-time employees performing core insurance activities in Gibraltar. Annual substance notification, audited accounts, regulatory capital records and core income-generating activity evidence must be filed with the GFSC and the Income Tax Office.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: On conviction, an additional assessment equal to 100% of the avoided tax, tax-geared penalties up to 200%, daily late payment interest, and a criminal fine not exceeding GIP 500,000 or such greater sum as the court may order under the Income Tax (Assessment) Act.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the ELP veil where the general partner or beneficial owners used the partnership as a sham; personal civil restitution, asset forfeiture, travel bans, disqualification from directorships and imprisonment of up to five years apply.
Shell Company / Deficient Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Economic substance penalties of up to GIP 100,000 per chargeable period, mandatory adjustment of the tax return to treat purported Gibraltar profits as beneficially owned elsewhere, GFSC licence suspension or revocation, and potential strike-off of the ELP.

Director personal liability: General partners and shadow directors are jointly and severally liable for unsatisfied tax and penalties; travel bans, directorship disqualification up to 15 years, and confiscation orders may be imposed where the entity is found to be an offshore shell.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner will adjust the controlled transactions to the arm's length standard, disallow related-party deductions, assess additional corporation tax at 10%, and impose transfer pricing penalties of up to 100% of the adjustment plus interest. Non-compliant documentation is treated as an aggravating factor.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation are personally exposed to restitution claims by the group or liquidators, unfair prejudice proceedings, penalty orders, and imprisonment for fraudulent accounting where deliberate mispricing is proved.
AML and Unlicensed Reinsurance Activity

Statutory sanction: Breach of the Financial Services (Insurance) Act and Proceeds of Crime legislation attracts a supervisory fine up to GIP 1,000,000, licence revocation, civil recovery of the proceeds of crime, and on conviction imprisonment for up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: The beneficial owner, general partner and compliance officers face personal criminal liability for money laundering, travel restrictions and asset freezing orders; limited partnership liability is pierced where AML failures allowed the captive vehicle to be used as an instrumentality of crime.
