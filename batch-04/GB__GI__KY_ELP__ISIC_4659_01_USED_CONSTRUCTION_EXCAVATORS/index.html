Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Income Tax Office, HM Government of Gibraltar (Gibraltar Tax & Customs) – portal: https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/taxation-and-customs

E-government filing portal

A foreign-owned Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership (ELP) must first register with the Gibraltar Registrar of Companies and obtain a Gibraltar registered partnership number. The ELP then applies to the Income Tax Office for a Gibraltar Tax Reference Number (TRN) and, if seeking exempt status, files the exempted limited partnership application under the Exempted Limited Partnerships Act 2019. For General Services Tax (GST), the ELP must register through Form GST-RG once its taxable turnover is expected to exceed the GIP 100,000 threshold, and it may be required by the Customs Department to obtain a Gibraltar Economic Operator Registration (EORI-like) customs identifier for export clearances. Non-resident partners are not issued a Gibraltar TRN unless they derive Gibraltar-source income; each partner uses its domestic TIN for reporting under applicable tax treaties.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

The Exempted Limited Partnerships Act 2019 and the Income Tax Act 2010 confer an automatic pass-through classification: the ELP is tax-transparent and is not an entity-level taxpayer. No separate US-style check-the-box election exists in Gibraltar, but the ELP may elect to be treated as 'exempted' and thus outside Gibraltar income tax. For GST purposes, the ELP may elect to register voluntarily or remain registered under the second-hand goods margin scheme; upon export, it must elect the zero-rated export treatment on the GST return and retain the required evidence. Hybrid mismatch elections, where available to partners, follow the rules of the partner's residence jurisdiction.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

1.5%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

GIP 100,000 (Gibraltar Pounds) of taxable supplies in any 12-month period; imports of exempted or zero-rated goods are outside the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of new or used excavators and rollers from Gibraltar is zero-rated under the General Services Tax (GST), provided the physical goods leave Gibraltar, title passes to a non-resident buyer, and the ELP has documentary proof of export and payment. Ancillary freight, insurance and inspection services are zero-rated if they are supplied to the exporter in direct connection with the exported machinery.

  • Original signed commercial invoice and packing list
  • Electronic Customs Export Declaration (e-SAD EX) cleared by Gibraltar Customs
  • Ocean/air waybill of lading (B/L or AWB) evidencing physical export from Gibraltar
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or SWIFT MT103 evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Certificate of origin or certificate of conformity for used heavy machinery, where applicable

Gibraltar does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping system for GST invoicing. GST returns are filed electronically through the Gibraltar Tax & Customs online portal (GST-ONLINE). Export goods must be pre-cleared via the electronic Single Administrative Document (e-SAD) in the Gibraltar Customs electronic system; the invoice must include the e-SAD export reference and the statement 'Zero-rated export' to support the 0% GST treatment.

Gibraltar Customs and the Income Tax Office will audit export zero-rating claims, particularly for high-value used construction equipment. If a zero-rated export is found to be unsupported or fraudulent, the GST is clawed back at 1.5% with interest from the date of supply. Civil penalties may amount to up to 100% of the tax evaded; fraudulent documentation attracts criminal penalties, loss of exempted status, and disqualification of the registered agent, with personal liability of the general partner.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar is part of the sterling monetary area and maintains full, unrestricted convertibility on both current and capital account. There are no exchange controls, administrative barriers, or approval requirements for trade settlements, capital contributions, profit distributions, loan repayments, or liquidation remittances. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is pegged at par to the pound sterling (GBP) and is redeemable on demand; entities may freely hold, convert, and remit GBP, EUR, USD, or other major convertible currencies. Profit remittance from a Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership is not subject to Gibraltar withholding tax or exchange-control authorisation, though partners may be taxable in their own jurisdictions based on domestic rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory export proceeds surrender obligation, mandatory conversion ratio, or prescribed repatriation timeline in either Gibraltar or the United Kingdom. A trading entity may retain all export receipts in any convertible currency without time-limited disposal requirements. For balance-of-payments statistical purposes, UK and Gibraltar banks may collect purpose codes and transaction-level data from MT103 messages, but this creates no duty to sell foreign currency or remit proceeds within any defined period.

Central bank supervision: Bank of England (monetary authority for the sterling area); HM Treasury (United Kingdom fiscal and monetary policy authority); Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) as the prudential and conduct regulator for banks, payment institutions, and investment firms in Gibraltar. The Government of Gibraltar issues Gibraltar pound currency notes under the Currency Notes Act; no separate Gibraltar central bank exists.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Limited viability as a primary treasury rail. UK-regulated electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut Business, and Currencycloud may onboard a Gibraltar ELP with complete beneficial-ownership documentation, but their utility for used heavy-machinery trade is constrained by the absence of commercial letter-of-credit origination, bank guarantee issuing, and segregated credit-authorised escrow product. Post-Brexit, EEA EMI passports do not automatically extend to Gibraltar; a provider must hold a Gibraltar payment-services licence or be separately authorised under the UK Financial Services and Markets Act with a UK branch. EMIs can be used as secondary collection points in minor currencies, but core liquidity and trade settlement should remain with a GFSC-authorised bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from partners or external investors must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the remitter's bank to the ELP's dedicated capital or operational account at a GFSC-authorised Gibraltar bank. The SWIFT field 70 (remittance information) must clearly state 'PARTNERSHIP CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION – [LEGAL ENTITY NAME] – [CAPITAL ACCOUNT REFERENCE]' to avoid ambiguous crediting. No exchange-control approval is required in Gibraltar or the UK. The correspondent bank may request a balance-of-payments purpose code; the standard code for inward direct investment or partner loan should be provided in accordance with Bank of England reporting guidance where the underlying remittance passes through a UK clearing bank. To satisfy AML and tax substantiation, the bank should receive a capital account statement signed by the general partner, a copy of the partnership deed, and source-of-funds evidence for the contributing partner, such as audited financial statements, tax returns, or a bank reference letter.Outbound trade disbursements for used construction excavators should be settled through (i) irrevocable commercial letters of credit opened by the buyer's bank and confirmed or advised by a Gibraltar or UK clearing bank; (ii) direct SWIFT MT103/MT202 open-account payments only upon presentation and matching of the applicable tax invoice, shipping mark, and pre-shipment inspection report; (iii) documentary collections (D/P or D/A) when the seller retains title control; and (iv) automated treasury-to-bank APIs for recurring partner distributions or intra-group allocations. Payments must be strictly referenced to the relevant proforma invoice, and banks will require a copy of the export customs declaration, bill of lading, and insurance policy for post-transaction verification. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: GIP 5,000,000 aggregate annual connected-party goods and financing transactions; above this threshold a formal Gibraltar Local File must be filed with the ITO together with the return, and a Master File is required where the consolidated group exceeds the OECD threshold; below the threshold a summary transfer pricing policy memorandum is still expected for the used machinery resale model.

Economic substance scope: Gibraltar Economic Substance Regulations 2020 (as amended) apply. Wholesale remarketing of used construction excavators constitutes a relevant distribution and service centre activity. The ELP must demonstrate for each financial year: management and control exercised in Gibraltar; core income-generating activities performed in Gibraltar; adequate qualified full-time employees engaged in Gibraltar; adequate physical premises in Gibraltar; and local operating expenditure proportionate to income. An annual economic substance notification and assessment must be submitted; failures attract financial penalties and potential strike-off.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Civil restitution of the full tax evaded, a tax-geared penalty of up to 200% of the understated liability, statutory interest, unlimited criminal monetary fines on conviction, and public debarment from Gibraltar corporate service provision.

Director personal liability: Directors of the General Partner and the controlling partners are criminally liable; they face personal civil liability for restitution of evaded tax, asset freezing orders, travel bans, and imprisonment for up to 7 years.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: ESR financial penalty of up to GIP 100,000 per failure, daily default fines of up to GIP 2,500 per day, cancellation of the ELP's Gibraltar registration, and public disclosure of the penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors of the General Partner who sign the substance returns face personal fines of up to GIP 50,000; knowing participation in an artificial structure triggers disqualification as a director for up to 15 years.
AML and Money Laundering Offences

Statutory sanction: The Proceeds of Crime Act 2015 and the Gibraltar Money Laundering Regulations 2019 impose unlimited fines, confiscation of the full property laundered, and imprisonment for up to 14 years; enhanced due diligence and electronic fund transfer reports are mandatory.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners and partners face personal criminal liability, seizure of personal assets under confiscation orders, travel bans, denial of access to the EEA payments network, and extradition to the requesting state.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: ITO transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's length margin, additional tax at 12.5%, a penalty of up to 100% of the adjustment for negligent documentation, daily penalties, and interest from the original filing date.

Director personal liability: Where mispricing is directed by the General Partner's board, directors are jointly and severally liable for the adjustment and can be prosecuted for furnishing false tax computations.
False DTA and Tax Residence Certificate Declaration

Statutory sanction: Revocation of treaty relief, reopening of assessments beyond the ordinary time limits in fraud cases, a penalty of up to 200% of the tax undercharged, and referral to the Gibraltar Financial Intelligence Unit for money-laundering predicate offence review.

Director personal liability: Signatories to the false declaration face personal criminal liability for false statements, travel bans pending trial, and imprisonment of up to 7 years for tax perjury offences.
