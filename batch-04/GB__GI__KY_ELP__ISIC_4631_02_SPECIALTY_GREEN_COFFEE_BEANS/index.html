Governing statute

Limited Partnerships Act 1926 (Cap. 222) of Gibraltar, as amended by the Limited Partnerships (Amendment) Act 2021; sections governing the registration, designation, and exemption of limited partnerships (ss. 4-8 and the exemptions regime); partnership pass-through taxation treatment under the Income Tax Act 2010 (Gibraltar).

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or security deposit requirement for a Gibraltar Exempted Limited Partnership (minimum_capital_requirement: false). Contributions may be in cash, in kind, or by loan/promissory note, recorded in the Limited Partnership Agreement with a capital-call procedure; funds may be drawn down as commercial exigency requires.

Funding & unlock path: No clearance required for a paper-trading ELP with no physical premises or employees. If a registered office or warehouse is staffed: (1) fire safety certification from the Gibraltar Fire and Rescue Service — escape routes, alarm testing, portable extinguishers, emergency lighting, and occupancy load certificate; (2) structural clearance from the Building Control Office (Gibraltar Development & Planning Commission) — racking dead/live loads, wind and seismic/rockfall assessments for storage structures; and (3) occupational safety clearance under the Safety at Work Act 2006 (Gibraltar) — written risk assessment, first-aid provision, statutory welfare facilities, and display of health and safety notices.

Industrial Access / EIA & Civil Defense Approval Exempted / Not Required

Not applicable — no industrial or manufacturing license is triggered in Gibraltar for the wholesale import and container distribution of unroasted green coffee beans (ISIC 4631). Category: No statutory operating license is required for pure trading. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (Territorial_Scope: the ELP itself is not subject to entity-level taxation; each partner is assessed only on income accruing in or derived from Gibraltar; foreign-source trading income of non-resident partners falls outside the Gibraltar tax charge)

Available election forms

Pass-through transparency is the statutory default for partnerships under the Gibraltar Income Tax Act 2010. The Commissioner of Income Tax may, under s.52 and the Classification of Businesses Rules, direct that a partnership be treated as non-transparent on a case-by-case basis. A transparent ELP may elect into the Qualifying Company or International Headquarters regimes, providing elective 0%-2% tax on qualifying foreign-source income. Partners may individually elect non-resident tax status to exclude non-Gibraltar income. No US-style check-the-box election exists; however, hybrid mismatch elections under the anti-hybrid rules may apply where the ELP has partners resident in a jurisdiction recognising entity-level classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST — Gibraltar Import Duties regime (Customs Act 2010 and Excise Duties Act); Gibraltar is outside the EU VAT area and operates no sales or use tax

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

N/A — Gibraltar imposes no VAT registration threshold. Import duty is chargeable solely at customs entry; green coffee (HS 0901.11) enters Gibraltar at 0% tariff duty, and no turnover-based indirect-tax registration obligation applies to the ELP

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

As Gibraltar levies no VAT, zero-rating applies by analogy only to import duty: green coffee imported into Gibraltar is subject to a 0% tariff; where coffee is deposited in a licensed bonded warehouse and subsequently re-exported, the import duty remains suspended and is fully relieved upon removal for export under the Customs Act 2010. For income-tax purposes, export sales of green coffee contracted, invoiced and completed outside Gibraltar by non-resident ELP partners are outside the Gibraltar territorial charge, provided no profits accrue in or are derived from Gibraltar; all export proceeds should be receivable through Gibraltarian or international banking channels traceable to the ELP's Trade Finance/LC infrastructure.

  • Gibraltar Customs Export Declaration (electronic SAD declaration)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill for airfreight)
  • Inward Remittance / Foreign Exchange Repatriation Certificate
  • Certificate of Origin (for re-export documentary compliance)
  • ELP Tax Reference Number and Customs Identification Number (CIN) evidence

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, continuous transaction control, or digital stamping mandate exists in Gibraltar. Ordinary commercial invoices, packing lists and purchase records constitute compliant VAT-free documentation. Import/export declarations are filed electronically through the Gibraltar Customs SAD/EDIFACT system via a licensed customs agent; a UK EORI is required only for goods movements between Gibraltar and the UK customs territory; no proprietary software API is mandatory for ELP non-customs operations.

HM Customs Gibraltar and the Income Tax Office conduct risk-based audits reconciling export declarations, Bills of Lading, warehouse removal records and inbound Swift/LC payments. An untrue export or relief declaration triggers Customs Act 2010 penalties including goods seizure and forfeiture, revocation of bond licences, and income-tax assessments up to 200% of the undercharged tax plus interest. Duty relief and zero-rating are retrospectively clawed back over a maximum 7-year assessment window, with no time limit in cases of fraud or deliberate evasion. Criminal conviction carries imprisonment of up to 7 years and automatic disqualification of the general partner or tax agent involved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Gibraltar maintains full free convertibility for both current and capital account transactions. There are no exchange control laws, no profit remittance restrictions, and no prior approval requirements for foreign-currency operations. The ELP may receive capital injections, settle trade payables, and distribute profits in any major convertible currency (GBP, EUR, USD, CHF) subject only to standard anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing, CRS, and FATCA reporting obligations. The primary practical hurdle is evidentiary: banks will require robust commercial-substance documentation for high-value commodity trades and cross-border remittances, but no statutory foreign-exchange barrier exists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export-proceeds surrender rules exist in Gibraltar. There is no mandatory timeline, no conversion ratio, and no requirement to bring export receipts into the GIP/GBP system. Green-coffee export and re-export proceeds may be retained in the invoiced currency, held in multi-currency accounts, or freely converted through GFSC-licensed banks. The only compliance duty is to maintain complete accounting and tax records consistent with the ELP's beneficial-ownership and economic-substance filings.

Central bank supervision: Gibraltar Financial Services Commission (GFSC) — acting as the integrated regulator for banking and investment; there is no separate central bank in Gibraltar. The Gibraltar pound (GIP) is issued by the Government of Gibraltar under the Currency Notes Act and operates at a fixed 1:1 parity with the British pound (GBP); the Bank of England retains the ultimate monetary policy framework for the United Kingdom and its Overseas Territories.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Licensed Gibraltar electronic-money institutions (EMIs) and select UK FCA-authorised EMIs can provide the ELP with a cost-efficient treasury rail for low-value settlement, multi-currency accounts, and online FX conversion. However, they do not provide documentary trade finance or letter-of-credit issuance, which is critical for containerised green-coffee import disbursements. Legal constraints: any EMIs used must hold valid authorisation under Gibraltar's Financial Services (Electronic Money) Act 2011 or an equivalent UK/EEA authorisation recognised under Gibraltar law. Unauthorised offshore digital EMIs are non-viable and will be rejected by GFSC-regulated banks at the clearing and counterparty level, triggering account de-risking.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injections shall be executed via standard SWIFT MT103 value-dated remittances from the partner's own bank account to the ELP's designated GFSC-licensed operating or escrow account. The remittance instruction must quote the beneficiary as exactly stated in the ELP banking mandate, include the reference 'KY_ELP Capital Injection', and, where required by the remitting bank's jurisdiction, carry the appropriate capital-transfer purpose code (for UK banks the common category is 'P20' or the equivalent corporate investment/trade-related working-capital designation as mandated by the remitting bank). Before settlement, the receiving bank will require a source-of-funds certificate signed by the remitting partner, copies of the partner's bank statements for the prior three months, and a board/general-partner resolution evidencing the capital contribution as approved under the limited-partnership agreement. The funds must originate from a named, cleared bank account and never from an unlicensed virtual-asset exchange or a third-party aggregator.Trade disbursements for green-coffee import shall use two primary clearing rails: (i) direct SWIFT MT103 wire transfers in the settlement currency for open-account supplier arrangements, with each payment instruction embedding the supplier invoice number, purchase-order number, and if applicable the LC number; and (ii) irrevocable documentary letters of credit issued under the UCP600 by the ELP's banking partner for large container and contract-grade purchases, with the ELP as the applicant and the exporter as the beneficiary. Where payments must be made to origin-country agents or stevedoring providers, batch MT103 wires against validated shipping and storage statements are acceptable. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

10.00% (standard Gibraltar corporation tax rate on chargeable profits under the Income Tax Act 2010; a 20% rate applies only to regulated electricity, fuel, water, telecoms and utility activities, outside the scope of green coffee distribution)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The statutory Year of Assessment runs from 1 July to 30 June. The return must be delivered on or before the filing date specified in the Commissioner's notice of assessment, which in standard practice is 30 November immediately following the 30 June year-end. An ELP adopting a 31 December accounting period must file its Long Form return, audited accounts and TP schedules no later than 9 months after the accounting period end, i.e., 30 September of the following year, unless an extension is granted under Section 71 of the Income Tax Act 2010.

Deadline: · Frequency:

The declaration is due within the period fixed by the Commissioner's notice, normally 30 November following the end of the Year of Assessment, or 30 days from the date of the Commissioner's request, whichever is earlier; extensions are not automatic for non-resident declarants.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Gibraltar Local File is mandatorily required where the aggregate value of controlled transactions (green coffee purchases from associated traders, intercompany financing, brokerage commissions, management charges and logistics fees) exceeds GIP 500,000 per accounting period. A Master File is expected where the multinational group undertakes cross-border related-party transactions exceeding GIP 50 million or where consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 750 million, which also triggers Country-by-Country Reporting. For the low-risk distribution of exchange-traded green coffee, the Gibraltar Income Tax Office nevertheless requires a contemporaneous CUP benchmark to be retained once any controlled transaction exceeds GIP 100,000.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance (Companies and Foreign Partnerships) Regulations 2019 apply because distribution and service centre activity is a relevant activity; the ELP performing the core income-generating activity of coffee trading must demonstrate that it is directed and managed in Gibraltar, holds adequate premises, employs proportionate numbers of Gibraltar-based staff, incurs adequate local operating expenditure, and files an annual Economic Substance return. If the ELP is tax resident outside Gibraltar by reason of effective place of management abroad, it may claim the non-resident exemption, but a pure offshore booking entity with no substance in either Gibraltar or the partner's residence jurisdiction will be treated as non-compliant and its benefits of territoriality may be stripped.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through false pricing documents

Statutory sanction: Civil: penalty up to 100% of undercharged tax plus interest. Criminal: on conviction on indictment, a fine up to GIP 50,000 or treble the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 7 years; the ordinary 6-year limitation period is extended to 20 years in cases of wilful default; assessments may be reopened regardless of earlier determinations.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner face joint and several liability for evaded tax and penalties under the fraudulent trading provisions of Gibraltar's Companies Act, civil restitution orders, asset freezing injunctions, travel bans, passport surrender and disqualification from acting as a director for up to 15 years.
Shell company and economic substance failure

Statutory sanction: Fines under the Economic Substance Regulations of up to GIP 100,000 plus GIP 250,000 for continued non-compliance; information automatically exchanged with HMRC and other competent authorities; striking-off of the corporate general partner; removal of the ELP from the Gibraltar register; foreign jurisdictions may disregard the ELP's separate existence and recharacterise its tax residency.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be personally charged with the substance penalties where they knew the ELP was a shell; courts may impose personal charging orders, disqualification orders and cross-border travel embargoes; indemnification by the partnership is void as contrary to public policy where fraud is established.
AML and beneficial ownership non-disclosure

Statutory sanction: Under the Proceeds of Crime Act and the Beneficial Ownership (Register) Act, civil penalties of up to GIP 100,000 per offence; criminal fines and imprisonment of up to 5 years for deliberate non-disclosure; confiscation of shipment proceeds traceable to the non-compliant transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner may be subjected to travel bans, seizure of passports, personal confiscation orders and money-laundering prosecution where they knowingly caused the ELP to process funds without adequate beneficial-ownership transparency; indemnity cover is unavailable for criminal fines.
Transfer pricing and documentation default

Statutory sanction: The Commissioner will apply Section 22 of the Income Tax Act 2010 to adjust profits to the arm's length result, assess the differential at 10% with interest from the filing date, and impose a penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax; the 5-year loss carryforward is forfeited in respect of any disallowed amount; persistent default triggers disclosure to the UK's HMRC under the exchange of information provisions.

Director personal liability: Where a director knowingly signed a false Schedule TP transfer pricing declaration, that director becomes personally liable for the differential tax and penalties under the wilful default doctrine; reliance on a professional advisory opinion will not shield a director who possessed actual knowledge of circular pricing and artificial markups.
Failure to register, notify chargeability and file returns

Statutory sanction: Initial penalty of GIP 500; daily default penalty of GIP 50 per day imposed by court order; the Commissioner may raise a best-of-judgment assessment enforceable through court proceedings; the 20-year extended assessment period applies where chargeability is never notified; the ELP may be struck off the register of limited partnerships.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are personally assessed for the unpaid corporation tax and penalties if the ELP's assets are insufficient and the director knew or ought to have known of the filing obligation; such assessments are provable in insolvency and may trigger automatic director disqualification and personal bankruptcy proceedings.
